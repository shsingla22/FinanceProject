# Balance-Sheet Patterns That Deliver ≥ 20% Stock CAGR — All Horizons

**Universe:** Nifty 500 (all 500 constituents)
**CAGR target:** ≥ 20% forward stock-price compound annual growth
**Horizons evaluated:** 3 years, 5 years, 7 years (forward from base year T)
**Predictor framing:** signals are computed using ONLY the balance sheet
data through fiscal year-end T. The forward CAGR is then measured
from T → T+horizon using year-end stock prices.

**'Met target'**: ✅ Y = forward CAGR ≥ 20%; ❌ N = forward CAGR < 20%.

---

## TL;DR — The answer

After testing 35 tight balance-sheet signals (3- and 4-year persistence,
multi-condition stacking, low-leverage filters) at the strict ≥20%
CAGR target across all 3 horizons, **exactly ONE pattern reaches the
≥80% probability bar**:

| Horizon | Pattern | n | Hit rate | Avg realized CAGR |
|---------|---------|--:|---------:|------------------:|
| **5 years** | **"Fixed Assets > 30% YoY for EACH of the last 3 years"** | 11 | **81.8%** | **+34.2%** |

For 3-year and 7-year horizons, no pattern reaches 80% at the
strict 20% CAGR target (best ~64-69%). The 5y horizon is the
**sweet spot** — long enough for the capex investment cycle to play
out, short enough to avoid the mean-reversion seen at 7y.

### The 2 failures — why they missed

- **AJANTPHARM Mar 2018 → Mar 2023 (+5.5%)**: the balance sheet
  showed perfect sustained-capex (FA > 30% YoY for 3 consecutive
  years), but Indian pharma broadly underperformed 2018-23 due to
  regulatory pressure on US generics and multiple-compression. The
  pattern correctly identified a fundamentally strong company; the
  sector cycle dominated.
- **AFFLE Mar 2021 → Mar 2026 (+5.8%)**: caught Affle at its
  post-COVID adtech valuation peak (100x+ P/E). The balance sheet
  was strong but **entry valuation was rich** — multiple-contraction
  to ~50x P/E dragged returns even as fundamentals held.

## Section 3 — Why this pattern works at 5y but not 3y or 7y

Looking at the data, the same FA-growth signals show **horizon
non-monotonicity**:

| Signal | 3y hit rate | 5y hit rate | 7y hit rate |
|--------|------------:|------------:|------------:|
| FA > 30% YoY for 3 yrs | 64.3% | **81.8%** | (n too small) |
| FA > 25% YoY for 3 yrs | 60.7% | (varies) | 60.0% |
| FA > 20% YoY for 4 yrs | 65.0% | 64.3% | (n too small) |
| FA & Reserves both > 20% YoY for 3 yrs | 68.8% | 63.6% | (n too small) |

**3-year horizon (too short)**: heavy capex needs time to deliver
revenue and margin gains. A company that just spent 3 years in heavy
capex mode may still be in the "build" phase at T+3, not yet
harvesting. Market sentiment about the capex spend can swing either
way over 3 years.

**5-year horizon (sweet spot)**: by T+5, the capacity is operational
and contributing to revenue. The earnings expansion shows up in P&L,
which the market re-rates. Stocks compound at 30%+ on average.

**7-year horizon (mean-reversion zone)**: by T+7, the growth has
matured. Initial multiple expansion has happened; subsequent CAGR
moderates as base effects compound (a stock at 10x its starting price
cannot easily 10x again in the next 7 years). Hit rate for ≥20% CAGR
target drops because hurdles get harder.

This is **why the 5-year horizon is special** for heavy-capex signals.

## Section 4 — Applying this today (June 2026)

To use the winning pattern to screen for high-conviction 5-year
compounders right now:

### Step 1 — For each Nifty 500 company, pull Fixed Assets at:
- Mar 2023, Mar 2024, Mar 2025, Mar 2026

### Step 2 — Compute 3 YoY growth checks:

```
FA[Mar 2026] / FA[Mar 2025] > 1.30   ← must be > 30%
FA[Mar 2025] / FA[Mar 2024] > 1.30   ← must be > 30%
FA[Mar 2024] / FA[Mar 2023] > 1.30   ← must be > 30%
```

A company qualifies if all three checks pass.

### Step 3 — Forward expectation

Based on the 11 historical matches:
- **81.8% probability of compounding the stock at ≥20% per year over
  the next 5 years**
- **Average expected CAGR: +34%**
- **Median: +33%**
- Best-case in history: +79% (DIXON 2020 → 2025)
- Failure mode: valuation-driven misses (AFFLE 2021) or sector
  cycle pressure (AJANTPHARM 2018)

### Step 4 — Risk overlay (the failures suggest these filters)

The 2 historical failures point to two soft overlays:

1. **Valuation discipline**: don't apply the pattern when the
   company's P/E is at a 2σ premium to its 5-year historical average.
   The Affle 2021 failure was entirely valuation-driven.
2. **Sector diversification**: even a strong BS pattern can be
   defeated by a sector cycle (Ajantpharm 2018 / Indian pharma cycle).
   Hold qualifying names across at least 4-5 industries.

### Step 5 — Sample size honesty

With only n=11 historical observations:
- 95% Bayesian credible interval on the "true" hit rate (uniform
  prior): roughly 53% to 96%
- So the 81.8% historical rate could be anywhere in that range
  for the next 11 occurrences

Treat this as a high-conviction **prior** for further fundamental
research on screen winners, not as a guaranteed trading rule.

## Section 5 — Caveats

1. **Sample size is small (n = 11)**. The 95% Bayesian credible
   interval on the true probability is wide (53-96%).
2. **Survivorship bias**: the Nifty 500 today excludes failures and
   delistings — the hit rate would likely be 5-15 pp lower in a
   survivorship-bias-free universe.
3. **Indian bull market overlap**: 2018-2021 base years had
   favorable forward windows.
4. **Multiple testing**: 105 (signal × horizon) combinations were
   tested. At a 5% false-positive rate, ~5 spurious patterns are
   expected. The single 81.8% pattern with n=11 sits in the marginal
   zone — interpret with appropriate skepticism.
5. **Sector concentration**: 4 of the 7 unique companies (excluding
   the 2 failures) are in just 3 sectors (Consumer Goods, Construction,
   auto-parts). The pattern may overstate the universe of qualifying
   companies in less-cyclical sectors.
