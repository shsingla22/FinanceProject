# Does the method hold on the wider universe? Top 1,250 vs top 750 — April 2020 → September 2026

The live skill now screens the NiftyTotalMarket constituents ∪ the 1,250
largest NSE companies by market cap. This note replays the identical
engine on a point-in-time version of that universe — the top 1,250 by
market cap each month (exit past rank 1,500), ETFs excluded, IPOs seasoned
three months, built from NSE bhavcopies with no lookahead — and sets it
beside the same replay on the top 750 (exit past 900). Both start with
₹100 all in cash on 2020-04-01, ten equal slices, daily stops, weekly
ratchets, Angel One charges on every order and capital-gains tax settled
every 1 April. "Net" is after all of that; the Nifty 50 figure is
pre-cost, pre-tax. (The historical index constituent lists are not
available, so the market-cap ranking stands in for the union; today the
two overlap on 746 of the index's 750 names.)

Widening the universe cut the net result by more than half and took the
system from nine points **above** the Nifty to eight points **below** it.
The ladder watch still helps on the wider universe (₹181 against ₹159),
but it cannot rescue it.

## Where the trades came from on the wider universe

| Group (rank on the buy date) | Buys | Closed | Mean return | Median | Winners |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Top 750 names | 97 | 93 | +6.2% | −3.7% | 43% |
| Rank 750–1,500 names | 123 | 116 | +3.9% | −3.9% | 38% |
| *For reference: the top-750-only run* | *214* | *204* | *+10.5%* | *−1.1%* | *46%* |

- **The small caps took more than half the book.** 123 of 220 buys were
  companies ranked below 750. They returned +3.9% a trade against +10.5%
  for the trades the top-750 run made.
- **They also displaced the good trades.** Signals are funded loudest
  first, and a small cap with a thin volume baseline produces huge
  multiples (20×, 50×) on modest turnover, so it jumps the queue. 9,665
  signals went unfunded on the wider universe against 4,149 on the top
  750 — 4,374 of them top-750 names that the narrow run would have had
  room for. Even the top-750 trades that *did* get funded on the wider
  universe were a worse selection (+6.2% against +10.5%), because the
  loud small caps kept taking the slots on the best weeks.
- **The best single trade was a small cap (+281%),** and the worst
  (−40%) a large one. Individual small caps do run; the book of them
  does not.

## What this says about the live universe

The live skill was widened to the top 1,250 on 26 September so that names
like Macpower and Bluspring would be screened at all. On the evidence
here, screening them and *funding them ahead of larger names* is what
costs the return. Two ways to keep the coverage without the cost, both
measurable on this archive before anything changes live:

1. **Screen 1,250, fund 750 first.** Rank each week's signals so that
   top-750 names take the slots before any smaller name, the way fresh
   signals now go before promoted ones. Small caps would still appear in
   the report and on the ladder watch.
2. **Go back to 750.** The simplest option, and the one the six-year
   record supports today.

Nothing has been changed in the skill. The live universe stays at 1,250
until you decide.
