# Jindal Steel & Power Ltd. (JINDALSTEL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: METALS*

Jindal Steel (listed as JINDALSTEL, formerly Jindal Steel & Power Limited) is an Indian integrated steel maker that turns coal and iron into finished steel products at two large plants — Raigarh and Angul (Odisha) — and sells them mainly to Indian construction and industrial buyers, with some exports. It makes money by selling steel tonnage: roughly 2.0–2.3 million tons a quarter in recent periods, generating about INR 15,000 crores of quarterly gross revenue, with profit driven by the gap between the realized steel price per ton and the cost per ton (dominated by coking coal and thermal coal). Its product basket runs from TMT rebar for construction to hot-rolled coil, plates and newer cold-rolled/coated products, and it also earns from by-products and from a power plant and overseas mining subsidiaries. Management is in the middle of a very large expansion program — about INR 47,043 crores announced, INR 32,925 crores spent by December 2025 — that takes steelmaking capacity to 15.6 million tons a year.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Long products / TMT (rebar for construction)** — Construction steel bars sold into India's building and infrastructure market; the calls treat TMT pricing as demand-sensitive and seasonally weak in the monsoon, and it is one of the two main pillars of the product basket.
- **Flat products — Hot Strip Mill (HRC)** — Hot-rolled coil from the Angul Hot Strip Mill, commissioned in 29 months and ramped from a standing start to about 450 kt in a single quarter against a ~3 million ton plan; it is the biggest growth driver of volumes, though management says HRC carries the lowest realization and margin in the basket.
- **Plates** — Heavy steel plate made at the Raigarh plate mill; mentioned mainly as a facility whose maintenance shutdowns move quarterly production.
- **Value-added flats — CRM and colour coating (CCL)** — Cold rolling and a 0.2 million tons per annum colour-coating line commissioned in January 2026, described as broadening the product portfolio and supporting higher margins.
- **Steelmaking assets at Angul (blast furnaces, BOF, coke ovens, DRI)** — The upstream iron- and steel-making chain being built out — BF-2, BOF-2 and BOF-3 (3 million tons per annum, due Q4 FY26), plus coke oven batteries and a DRI unit; commissioning and ramp-up of these is the main swing factor in production, cost and capex in the calls.
- **Captive coal mines** — Own coal mines such as Utkal C (started) and Utkal B1 (opened, overburden removal underway) that feed the plants and reduce dependence on bought coal, which the calls repeatedly flag as the biggest cost item.
- **Power** — A 1,050 MW power plant (SBPP) acquired through the insolvency process and turned around, with two 525 MW modules commissioned/synchronized to the grid in Q3 FY26 and January 2026.
- **Overseas subsidiaries (Mozambique, South Africa, Australia)** — Foreign operations that in Q1 FY23 contributed roughly INR 334 crores, INR 84 crores and INR 24 crores of EBITDA respectively; later calls describe them mainly in terms of whether their losses are shrinking, so they are small relative to the Indian steel business.
- **By-products and other operating income** — Sales of process by-products alongside steel; the calls note these did not keep pace with steel revenue in Q3 FY26 because more output was consumed captively, which dragged down average selling price per ton.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Policy repeatedly and directly sets JSPL's economics: the May 2022 export duty cut domestic and export realizations ~15-16% and volumes 12-28% in a single quarter; the 2026 safeguard duty (12%/11.5%/11%) props up pricing; NMDC administered iron ore prices, captive coal blocks (Utkal B1/C) and their environmental clearances gate raw-material cost; general elections delayed the Angul mega-project and slurry pipeline; and management explicitly thanks the government for import bans on Q&T plates and for defence and metro-rail demand.
