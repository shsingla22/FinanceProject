**Checks that improved:**

- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Results are traceable to disclosed mechanisms rather than narrative — the 14.7% ROE is decomposed into cost of funds, opex and credit cost movements at low leverage — though the eloquence of the commentary outruns what is a good-not-exceptional 4.5% ROA business facing rising credit costs. | The long view had said: Skill is real (opex down 161bps, consistent guidance delivery), but a material part of the improving spread came from exogenous repo cuts (cost of funds down 90bps year-on-year) while credit cost nearly doubled and Stage 2 ECL went 6%→16%, so eloquent commentary and headline ROE gains should not be read as pure execution.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: The credit cycle looks past its worst (flows contained, rollback momentum better) but management simultaneously guides to hardening rates, imported fuel inflation and softening demand, so there is no identifiable upturn ahead. | The long view had said: The company is positioned into a deteriorating credit cycle, not near its bottom — credit cost rose from 0.80% to 1.45%, Stage 2 ECL was lifted from 6% to 16%, login-to-disbursal fell from 42% to 34%, and management flags household DSR at ~14% with inflation, rate and oil headwinds ahead.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: The pitch is ease and availability of credit rather than certainty commanding a premium, and customers demonstrably shift to rivals on price and loan size, so trust does not clearly outrank price. | The long view had said: Customers — largely first-time property borrowers — buy access and certainty of getting a loan at rates near 18%, indicating price is secondary to reliable availability, and the model deliberately preserves in-person assessment as the trust-building step.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Weak → Poor. Now: Management explicitly characterises its own product as an undifferentiated commodity with no price-setting ability, and competitors freely poach business with better rates and larger loan amounts. | The long view had said: No monopoly-like position — small-ticket LAP and gold loans are contested products where competitors actively undercut on price and loan amount, and management describes itself as a price taker.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. Now: Growth spend is branch build-out well ahead of guidance (46 branches added in FY26 vs 25 guided, 251→256, target 275) funded from a 32% CRAR balance sheet while ROE improved to 14.7%, though opex rose 36bps quarter-on-quarter as costs precede benefits. | The long view had said: Branch network (the NBFC equivalent of growth capex) rose from 135 to 256 over the period — 46 added in FY26 versus 25 guided — while returns improved (ROA 4.31%→4.53%, ROTE 10.77%→14.73%) and opex/AUM fell 161bps against 150bps promised, with further expansion explicitly gated on branch-level profitability.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: The retail/micro-business customer base is served with convenience and secured collateral, which is a sensible match, but neither an intangible brand nor any switching-cost lock-in is present. | The long view had said: The customer base is retail/micro-enterprise and the benefit offered is correctly matched to it — accessibility, local relationship and speed rather than corporate TCO arguments — though the relationship lacks retail intangible pricing power or lock-in.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: The model is built on physical proximity and direct origination — 256 branches across small towns with distribution cited as a core driver of returns — but that reach is replicable and did not prevent competitive losses this quarter. | The long view had said: Convenience and proximity are the core proposition — 256 direct-model branches taking credit into small towns with localized underwriting, in-house sourcing and digitized servicing, explicitly framed as making loans easy where customers are.

*Unchanged: Candor; Gain in market share; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Disciplined stewards; Technology advantage; Intangible benefits; Independent, long-term and tenacious; People matter (talent development); Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Asset turn; Barriers to entry and rationality; Out of limelight; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Distribution advantage; Geographical expansion; Structural end-market growth; Security by obscurity; Share donators.*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Unknown on the long view, Unknown on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Management itself frames the product as undifferentiated, saying lenders are price takers and the lowest-cost producer wins — and Q1 FY27 showed rivals winning volume on cheaper pricing or bigger ticket sizes, with login-to-disbursal conversion falling from 42% to 34%. Defence rests on cost position rather than brand or product protection, though so far that defence is working. (The long view had said: Management itself defines the product as undifferentiated and the firm as a price taker whose only defence is being the low-cost producer; yields have no headroom ('I do not think that there's opportunity to increase or inch it up beyond from what we are already charging'), yields actually fell y-o-y in Q1 FY27, and competitors are already undercutting on price and loan size. That is precisely the Nobel Biocare setup — a not-complex product, low end-customer brand awareness, and challengers able to profit at lower prices.)

**That worsened:**

- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Across all four calls policy actions directly reset revenue and asset quality: the Karnataka ordinance wrecked a 'superb portfolio' and management says it takes 3 quarters to damage and 3-4 more to restore; the April 2026 gold-loan eligibility norms shrank disbursal sizes; and an RBI circular on collateral for sub-Rs.20 lakh MSME loans halved co-origination from 20% to 10% of disbursals in one quarter. (The long view had said: No subsidy or government-contract revenue, but the regulator visibly resets economics: an RBI circular effective 1 April 2026 barring banks from taking collateral for MSME loans up to Rs 20 lakh cut co-origination — ~20% of disbursals and book — to 10% in a single quarter, and new gold eligibility norms shrank loan amounts. Funding cost is also repo/policy-driven (cost of funds down 83-90 bps y-o-y purely on the repo cycle).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
