### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 3 weakened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Bunty Bubli is described in both Q1FY26 and Q4FY26 as the highest-selling liquor brand in India with leadership held in UP and Uttarakhand, and the name supports partnership/adjacency launches (Amrut, Bacardi, own IMFL brands); but there is no A&P disclosure and the majority of revenue (chemicals, biofuel to OMCs) carries no brand at all. (The long view had said: Real regional brand equity in liquor — Bunty & Bubbly the largest-selling brand in the country, Amazing/Soulmate/MaQintosh stretched across vodka, whisky and flavour adjacencies with 21-122% growth in FY26 — but the heritage and 'world-class quality' story belongs to partner Amrut, and the chemicals/biopharma businesses are unbranded B2B.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Management repeatedly points to a new Performance Chemicals portfolio with a 'strong pipeline' and incremental capacity additions, plus branded nutraceuticals at Ennature, but no R&D spend, share of industry R&D, or named-product revenue contribution is ever disclosed in the FY26 calls. (The long view had said: A consistent, delivered innovation narrative — a 2022 pipeline of '20 products across six segments', the ICIS Global Innovation Award with Unilever/LanzaTech, the new specialties plant commissioned and targeted at INR150-200 crore in year one, carbon-smart materials, bio-based amines, dibasic esters — but R&D spend is never disclosed or quantified and gross margins are commodity-level (group EBITDA 15.5%), so innovation is not funded by high margins.)

**That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Nothing in the four FY26 calls touches attach rates, AMC/service revenue, renewals, installed base or customer advances as a funding source; the business is sold as bulk chemical, ethanol and liquor volumes. (The long view had said: Across all 16 calls the revenue discussion is volumes, feedstock costs, EBIT margins and government ethanol allocations; no attach rates, AMC/service revenue, renewals, deferred revenue or customer advances are ever mentioned.)
- **Toll Roads**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The Q1 and Q3 FY26 calls describe chemicals pricing as a spread game against crude-based MEG with customers walking when the gap widens, plus 'structural overcapacity' — the opposite of a small-share-of-cost, no-negotiation input. (The long view had said: The performance-chemicals side shows some toll-road traits — multi-year co-development with global majors and sticky niche customers (Aug 2022: oilfield product commercialised for a US major; Jun 2026: first green bio-based amine with L'Oreal after 3-4 years of co-development, 'niche customers have continued to buy glycols from us') — but the bulk of the group (biofuels, glycols, spirits) is price-taking commodity volume with no certification or standards anchoring.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The calls themselves describe the core assets as replicable and already replicated — industry ethanol capacity has caught up with the blending mandate and chemicals faces structural global overcapacity — so a funded entrant faces no time, data or location barrier. (The long view had said: Some articulable barriers exist — brownfield distillery capacity with multi-feedstock flexibility, state liquor licences and excise-anchored leadership in UP/Uttarakhand, and multi-year customer co-development — but management itself flags surplus national ethanol capacity, structural global overcapacity and competition against Reliance's EO price, and FY26 ROCE of only 11.9% is not the return signature of a hard-to-replicate franchise.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 5 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: The Chemicals/BSPC segment is explicitly framed in commodity-cycle terms across all four FY26 calls — crude-linked MEG pricing, soft PET demand, global structural overcapacity, and FY26 chemicals revenue down 10.4% — while war-driven crude spikes were described as swinging their relative competitiveness. Exposure is bounded because ~two-thirds of revenue (Potable Spirits, Bio-Fuels) is consumption/flow-linked and group EBITDA margin rose steadily FY22 9.6% → FY26 15.5% rather than swinging. (The long view had said: Across all 16 calls management frames performance through commodity price decks — coal up 2x, ethanol, freight up 5-6x in FY23, crude/MEG, grain, DDGS prices — and margins swung from 8.4% EBITDA in Q1FY23 to 17.1% in Q4FY26 largely on feedstock spreads plus a war-driven crude move; the chemicals segment also sits in a globally over-supplied petrochemical chain. Mitigants are real but partial: the potable spirits and biofuels segments are operations/consumption-linked flow demand, and the 3x3 feedstock strategy plus countercyclical grain-distillery capex during the 2022 cost shock has lifted EBITDA margin steadily from 9.6% (FY22) to 15.5% (FY26).)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: A meaningful slice of earnings runs through a handful of named relationships: the Clariant JV (IGL sells it ethylene oxide and books the return as 'other income' — enough that Q4 EBITDA margin moves from 17.1% to ~20.8% when included), brand tie-ups with Amrut and Bacardi, and a named oilfield-chemicals customer whose business went on hold. Ethanol offtake is also into the OMC blending channel rather than a broad customer base. (The long view had said: Repeatedly named single relationships drive results: OMC tender allocations are the sole channel for biofuels, the Clariant JV supplies 'the most significant part of our other income' (worth ~370bps of Q4FY26 margin), the Amrut licence and Bacardi packaging tie-up underpin premium spirits growth, and a single oilfield customer going quiet cut exports. Spirits volume is also geographically concentrated in UP and Uttarakhand.)
- **Fashion Risk**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: The Potable Spirits franchise rests on repeat-purchase country liquor with entrenched state market leadership rather than a trending product or celebrity moment — 23.7 million cases in 9MFY26, up 5% — and no call credits growth to a fad. The premium/IMFL push adds some taste exposure but is small relative to the country-liquor base. (The long view had said: A meaningful part of spirits growth is credited to trending launches and novelty SKUs — Amazing Vodka, Zumba Lemoni, Bunty Vodka in Jeera/Cranberry/Jamun flavours, Dhurandhar Whisky, city- and state-specific commemorative editions — demand that rests on taste and can move without warning. Risk is bounded by breadth rather than one big bet, and by a large repeat-purchase regular/country-liquor base.)
- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No call in the window shows a competing platform threatening the installed base, and no analyst raised disruption; instead management describes a continuous small-scale innovation cadence — new formulations, a Performance Chemicals pipeline, multi-feedstock ethanol capability (rice, corn, molasses), branded nutraceuticals — which is the value-adding rather than perilous form of innovation. (The long view had said: The ethanol-blending demand base is exposed to vehicle-technology shifts — management itself flags the global move to EVs over blending, and the next leg of demand depends on flexi-fuel vehicles — while the chemicals franchise is a bio-route challenger to entrenched petrochemical EO/MEG capacity rather than the incumbent. Offsetting this is a steady cadence of small-scale, value-adding innovation (specialty amines, oilfield chemicals, carbon-smart materials, first green bio-based amine with L'Oreal, ICIS Global Innovation Award with Unilever/LanzaTech).)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No volume erosion attributed to changing habits appears in the window; the shifts management cites — premiumisation in spirits, and demand for bio-based/green ingredients and decarbonisation in chemicals — run in the company's favour, and the portfolio is being pivoted toward them (Amrut premium brands, branded nutraceuticals, plant-based MEG). (The long view had said: The spirits demand base is migrating — consumers moving from blended whisky to malts, and toward premium/luxury — and the industrial side depends on a sustainability preference for bio-based over petro inputs; management is pivoting rather than defending, with the Amrut single-malt tie-up, city/state special editions and a full price-ladder portfolio. Nicotine extraction for smoking-cessation therapeutics is itself a bet on health-attitude shift.)

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); New Entrants (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
