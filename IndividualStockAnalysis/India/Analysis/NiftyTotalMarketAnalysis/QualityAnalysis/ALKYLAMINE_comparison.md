### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 6 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 5 weakened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Cost to Replicate**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The only articulable barriers are process technology on a new import-substitute molecule and integrated scale in aliphatic amines; against that, the calls show ~150,000t of methylamine capacity against 80-90,000t demand with Aarti Drugs entering, and acetonitrile economics depending on a government anti-dumping duty — barriers a funded entrant has demonstrably crossed within a few years. (The long view had said: The evidence directly contradicts a replication barrier: Aarti Drugs built and commissioned a methylamine plant within the observed window, capacity overhang now exceeds demand by ~60,000t, and the company's defence in acetonitrile and isopropylamine is government anti-dumping duty rather than anything a funded entrant could not rebuild.)

**That weakened:**

- **Toll Roads**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: In May 2026 management explicitly framed the product as a trivial share of customer cost that customers absorbed when ammonia doubled — classic toll-road economics — but the same calls show four-player capacity overhang, Chinese dumping and reliance on anti-dumping duty, so pricing is set by competition rather than by irreplaceability. (The long view had said: Management explicitly describes itself as a trivial line item in customer cost structures when justifying pass-through of the FY26 ammonia spike (May 2026 call), and pharma/agro customers qualify grades (acetonitrile approvals for peptide APIs). But it fails the rational-oligopoly leg: methylamines has four domestic players against ~150,000t capacity for an 80–90,000t market, and Chinese aggression set prices for three years.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: FY26 management claims share was retained and 'perhaps even slightly increased' and expects acetonitrile share gains post anti-dumping duty, but FY26 volumes actually fell ~1% and the 10-15% normal growth guidance was missed, so any gain is unquantified and comes in a shrinking market. (The long view had said: FY26 management claims share held or slightly gained, and acetonitrile share rose after the anti-dumping duty took effect (May 2026); but that gain came from regulatory protection rather than competitive strength, and the long-run 10–15% volume growth guidance repeated in 2022 and 2024 degraded to flat FY26 volumes and a 5–10% forward outlook.)
- **Brand Strength**: QUANT SIGNAL → NO FIT. Why now: Pricing and volumes are explained entirely by demand-supply balance, imports and competitor capacity; no heritage, A&P spend, premium positioning or brand-led share language appears anywhere in the last-12-month calls. (The long view had said: all 2 computable checks point this way but the calls offer no evidence — numbers alone are only ever a screening hint, never a confirmed fit.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No discussion of licences, AMCs, subscriptions, attach rates, installed base or customer advances in any of the four calls; the business is described purely as tonnage of amines sold. (The long view had said: the calls are silent and the numbers are mixed (1 of 3 checks supporting).)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Only isolated cost datapoints (coal at Rs 9,000-10,000/MT, 80% of power cost from steam) appear; there is no evidence of a cost culture, itemised savings programme or deliberate low-price-plus-quality positioning. (The long view had said: There is a genuine, repeated cost culture — a dedicated process engineering group grinding yields and specific consumptions lower every year (Nov 2024), solar addition and high-temperature boilers/turbines to cut power cost (Nov 2022) — but scale economics are modest, plants run at 60–85% utilisation, and low prices are imposed by Chinese imports rather than chosen as a strategy.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (QUANT SIGNAL); Forward Integrators (QUANT SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The core products are largely undifferentiated: management concedes customers accept acetonitrile from either production route, and Chinese material has held prices 'very stable at a low level' with no brand or switching barrier to defend. The response is to build differentiated higher grades and a niche import-substitute molecule rather than to defend a price premium. (The long view had said: The products are largely undifferentiated commodities where cheaper supply wins: management concedes both acetonitrile production routes are "acceptable to the customers," declines to make DMF purely "because the imports are much cheaper," and has lost monoisopropylamine volumes to Chinese material for years while awaiting duty protection. Defence rests on tariff protection and cost, not brand or complexity.)

**That worsened:**

- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Industry economics are being reset by two sets of entrants discussed defensively in every call: Chinese producers dumping acetonitrile and pushing agrochemical intermediates/derivatives into India, and a fourth domestic methylamines player, Aarti Drugs, commissioning capacity into an already long market. Management attributes the FY26 volume shortfall partly to Chinese aggression and describes an explicit capacity overhang. (The long view had said: Methylamines now has a fourth player after Aarti Drugs commissioned its plant, against roughly 150,000 tons of capacity for an 80,000-90,000 ton market — management concedes a structural "overhang" and that methylamines will be "a long item for some time." Separately, Chinese producers redirected from the US turned aggressive across the portfolio, at times selling below cost of production, which is what drove the acetonitrile duty petition.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
