*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Toll Roads**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management explicitly frames its amines as a trivial share of customer cost in the May 2026 call, which let it pass through a doubling of ammonia cost without immediate demand loss; but the toll-road conditions fail on the oligopoly side — Nov 2025 describes ~150,000t capacity against 80–90,000t methylamine demand, a fourth entrant (Aarti Drugs), and Chinese dumping, so customers are not captive. (The long view had said: Management explicitly frames itself as a tiny, non-negotiated line in customers' cost base in May 2026, and the amines/acetonitrile markets are few-player, but the alleged toll is repeatedly broken by Chinese import pressure and a fourth methylamine entrant (Aarti), so customers do not behave as captive.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: May 2026 claims share was retained or slightly increased and Nov 2025 expects acetonitrile share gains from the anti-dumping duty, but the gains are unquantified and come alongside flat-to-negative FY26 volume, with the acetonitrile benefit dependent on a tariff rather than competitive strength. (The long view had said: FY26 management claims share was retained and 'perhaps even slightly increased', and acetonitrile share rose after the anti-dumping duty, but methylamine volumes were lost to a fourth entrant and monoisopropylamine share has been under Chinese pressure for years pending government protection — gains lean on duty protection rather than persistent competitive share capture.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No discussion of licences, AMC/service contracts, subscriptions, attach rates, renewals or customer advances in any of the four calls. (The long view had said: No discussion of service/AMC contracts, subscriptions, attach rates, renewals, installed base or customer advances across any of the eight calls; the business is described purely as tonnage sales of amines.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Cost items are discussed only when asked (coal ~Rs.9-10/kg, steam ~80% of power/fuel cost in Nov 2024); there is no itemised savings programme or cost-culture narrative to judge. (The long view had said: There is a persistent, itemised cost culture — a dedicated process engineering group grinding yields and specific consumption every year, solar capacity, high-temperature boilers/turbine for steam (Nov 2022, Nov 2024) — but no evidence of scale-driven low price plus differentiation translating into high asset turns or defended returns; margins compressed instead.)

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 2 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: A fourth domestic methylamines producer (Aarti Drugs) commissioned in FY26 into an already long market, and structurally lower-cost Chinese supply — by-product-route acetonitrile plus imported agrochem derivatives that displace demand upstream — is discussed defensively in every call, requiring an anti-dumping petition as the response. (The long view had said: Two structurally different competitive resets are discussed defensively and repeatedly: Chinese producers selling at or below cost across acetonitrile, methylamine derivatives and monoisopropylamine and redirecting US-blocked volumes into Asia; and Aarti Drugs commissioning a fourth domestic methylamine plant into a market with ~80-90kt demand against >150kt capacity. Management's response has been duty petitions and accepting lower prices to defend volume rather than a structural answer.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The product is explicitly commoditised — customers accept either production route, and cheaper Chinese material took share and held acetonitrile prices 'very stable at a low level', with prices improving only marginally even after the duty because 'the Chinese are dropping the prices further'. Management's own view is that 'the pressure on pricing may not be from the customers as much as from competition', i.e. price, not quality, sets the outcome. (The long view had said: The products carry no brand protection at the end customer and buyers switch on price alone: management concedes that for acetonitrile 'both routes are acceptable to the customers', that DMF is not worth making because imports are cheaper, and that Chinese material sold below cost of production dragged ethyl and methyl spreads to two-decade lows. The premium is defended mainly by petitioning for duties, which is the weakest form of protection.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
