# Balkrishna Industries Ltd. (BALKRISIND) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: AUTOMOBILE*

Balkrishna Industries (brand name "BKT") is an Indian company that makes rubber tyres, with its main business being large off-highway tyres (OHT) for farm tractors and agricultural equipment and for earth-moving, mining, industrial and construction machinery. Most of what it makes is exported and sold through distributors and dealers rather than to vehicle makers — replacement-market sales run about 70-73% of volumes, with OEMs about 25-28% — and roughly 44-49% of sales come from Europe, 16-19% from the Americas, 21-29% from India and the rest from other markets. It makes money by manufacturing tyres in India (plants at Waluj and Bhuj) and selling them abroad, so profits swing with rubber and crude-linked raw-material costs, sea-freight rates and the euro-rupee exchange rate (it hedges the euro, e.g. INR 85-91 in earlier years versus about INR 97 by Q3 FY26); quarterly standalone revenue was around INR 2,200-2,700 crores with EBITDA margins in the 19-25% range across the calls. Alongside tyres it runs a carbon black plant that supplies its own factories and sells to outside customers, and from FY26 it has started moving into on-highway tyres — truck-bus radials, two-wheelers and, planned, passenger-car radials.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Off-Highway Tyre (OHT) business** — The core business — agricultural tyres (about 59-63% of the category mix) plus OTR, industrial and construction tyres (about 34-37%), sold worldwide mainly to the replacement market under the BKT brand; management describes it first on every call and it drove the highest-ever quarterly and annual volumes in FY26.
- **Agricultural segment** — The largest product category within OHT, tyres for farm equipment, contributing 63% of sales in 9M FY23 and 59% in H1 FY25.
- **OTR / industrial / construction segment** — Tyres for earthmoving, mining, industrial and construction machines, the second-biggest category at 34% in 9M FY23 and 37% in H1 FY25; the first phase of the FY25 tyre capex was directed at expanding this OTR range.
- **Carbon black business** — An in-house plant making carbon black — a key tyre raw material — used captively and also sold to outside customers (third-party sales were about 9% of overall business in FY26), with capacity taken to 265,000 MTPA in December 2025 and heading to 360,000 MTPA, including higher-value 'advanced'/specialty grades approved for plastics, inks, paints, pressure pipes and power cables.
- **On-Highway business** — A new and still small adjacency started in FY26 — truck-bus radial tyres launched February 2026, a re-launch of two-wheeler tyres for India, and passenger car radials (PCR) planned for FY27 — supported by a new distribution network and Ranveer Singh-fronted marketing, and management lists scaling it up as one of three company priorities.
- **Captive power plant** — A power plant at the Bhuj site, expanded from 40 MW to 64 MW in February 2026, run alongside the carbon black operation for what management calls an 'energy circularity' model that supports cost and efficiency rather than being a separate revenue line.

### The story in depth

Balkrishna Industries is, at its core, a low-cost specialist that sells a comparable-quality off-highway tyre at a deliberate 10-15% discount to the big Western brands, and almost everything good about the company flows from that one idea. The discount is affordable because the company makes its own inputs - carbon black at 265,000 MTPA and 64 MW of captive power at Bhuj - and because it has built roughly 360,000 MTPA of capacity and thousands of low-volume tyre sizes and moulds over decades. That combination shows up in the quality work as steady economics (about INR 21 of operating profit per INR 100 of sales, with margins moving only about 3.9 points a year) and as genuine barriers to entry from capital intensity and product complexity. The pattern screen reads the same facts as a clear 'Low Price Plus' fit, a 'Brand Strength' fit on the back of heavy and sustained marketing (IPL, all five Women's Premier League franchises for 2026, Ranveer Singh, a formal brand repositioning), and a partial 'Cost to Replicate' fit - real, but bounded, because the moat is scale and integration rather than anything a rival could never buy.

The awkward part is that the same discount strategy that creates the strengths also creates the biggest vulnerabilities, and the records say so directly. Being the cheaper alternative to Tier-1 brands is itself the 'good enough goods' attack, which leaves the company symmetrically exposed to cheaper Chinese and regional supply coming at it from below - hence that risk sitting on watch rather than low. It also caps pricing power: the screen rates that pattern only partial because price increases are reactive pass-through of raw-material and tariff costs (1-2% in Q3 FY25, 3-5% in FY26 with about 2% more planned) rather than pricing ahead of inflation, and the company cut average selling price 6% in a quarter when freight fell. FY26 makes the point plainly - record volumes but flat revenue, meaning growth came from selling more, not from selling better. Selling entirely through distributors and dealers rounds this out: it makes 'friendly middleman' only partial and 'forward integrators' a clear no, and the channel visibly holds leverage, since the company had to share US tariff costs with its own distributors. Market share gains are asserted more than demonstrated, with management declining to claim share shifts and US volumes down about 30% year on year on tariffs.

Underneath all of this sits a cyclical business, and cyclicality is the one risk rated elevated: FY23 destocking cut volumes 5% with EBITDA margin at 19.1%, FY25 recovered to 25.6%, and FY26 nine-month volumes were down 1% with EBITDA down 10% at 22.7%. What keeps the overall risk picture safe rather than fragile is that 70-73% of volume is replacement demand tied to actual farming, construction and mining activity rather than new equipment purchases - which is also why the consumer-preference, fashion, technology and customer-concentration risks all come out low, and why resilience reads as sound. Management earns credit for behaving consistently through this: no acquisitions, modest leverage (net debt of INR 895 crore against INR 3,154 crore of cash), an unbroken INR 4 per quarter dividend through the downturn, and heavy capex sustained counter-cyclically - INR 2,800 crore spent in FY26 with a further INR 2,000 crore approved out to FY29. But that spending is exactly where the doubts concentrate. The quality scorecard is weakest on growth, capital allocation and return on capital, about 111 days of cash sits trapped in the working capital cycle, and the money is going into on-highway categories - truck radials launched February 2026, a two-wheeler relaunch, passenger car radials by end-2026 - where the company is itself the new entrant, against a market leader it has said it will not undercut. That tension between a proven, defensible core and an unproven, capital-hungry expansion is what produces the overall verdict of 61 out of 100, or decent.

The single most useful way to hold this company in mind is that its cheapness is simultaneously its moat and its ceiling. It defends the core off-highway niche well enough that competitors have not broken its pricing, and management reports the Tier-1 price gap has held. But it also means every input cost, freight rate, tariff and duty change passes through the profit and loss rather than being absorbed by pricing strength, and it means the 2030 vision of INR 5,000 crore of on-highway revenue has to be won on the same low-price terms in a market where the incumbent is larger and the company has no channel of its own beyond the roughly 90 primary distributors it is now building.

## What to watch

- Whether the on-highway push (truck radial, two-wheeler, passenger car radial) starts producing revenue and returns proportionate to the INR 2,800 crore spent in FY26 and INR 2,000 crore approved to FY29 - the 'capex for growth' check and the weak return-on-capital and growth scores hinge on this.
- Any sign the 10-15% discount to Tier-1 brands has to widen, or that cheaper Chinese and regional supply is undercutting it - that would turn the 'good enough goods' risk from watch to real and break the 'low price plus' pattern that carries the whole business model.
- A repeat of FY26's pattern of record volumes with flat revenue - the negative price and mix flagged in the 'pricing, mix and volume' check would confirm that 'pricing power' is not just partial but absent.
- Resolution of the unsettled policy items named under government dependency: the US tariff position that already cut US volumes about 30%, the EU duty and free-trade-agreement outcome, and the twice-deferred EUDR timeline.
- Movement in the roughly 111-day working capital cycle, especially if the new categories and the ~90 new primary distributors stretch it further while a cyclical downturn is underway.
