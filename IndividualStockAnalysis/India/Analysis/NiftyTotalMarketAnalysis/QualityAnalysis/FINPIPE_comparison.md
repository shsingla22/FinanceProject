**Checks that improved:**

- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Poor → Neutral. Now: 50,000 tons of capacity was commissioned during the year and a further brownfield phase at Ratnagiri/Masar is being worked on internally, but incremental returns look poor since FY26 volumes actually fell to 332,736 MT from 347,982 MT and no board approval or greenfield decision exists yet. | The long view had said: Capex has been flat at ~INR100-250cr for four years and is explicitly maintenance/debottlenecking, with capacity of 520,000t against only 332,736t of FY26 sales, so there is no growth capex and no incremental ROIC to earn.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Good. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹16 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±2.8 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹16 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±5.9 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Candor** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Management voluntarily quantified the Q4 inventory gain at INR35-40cr, warned unprompted that current margins will moderate to low double digits, and flagged Middle East supply risk rather than only the price upside. | The long view had said: Management is forthright on spreads, weak April demand and lost share, but evasive on the recurring questions that matter — margin drivers, the inventory accounting and the idle cash — deflecting to 'a combination of factors'.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Purely organic growth funded from a INR2,563cr net cash balance, no acquisitions, no leverage, and capacity added at existing sites through a weak-demand year — textbook counter-cyclical, low-risk stewardship. | The long view had said: Entirely organic, no M&A, and a consistently net-cash balance sheet (INR2,563cr surplus, AA+ rating upgrade), though the discipline shades into inertia since the cash is neither invested counter-cyclically nor returned.
- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: After direct input costs, the company keeps ₹42 of every ₹100 of sales — pricing power customers are happy to pay for. And from the calls: Gross margin reached 42% versus 30% a year earlier and management says it consciously holds price for profitable growth, indicating real brand-backed pricing latitude — though demand is not steady, being both seasonal and price-elastic. | The long view had said: After direct input costs, the company keeps ₹42 of every ₹100 of sales — pricing power customers are happy to pay for. And from the calls: Management itself characterises the business as pass-through with realizations tracking PVC prices, so there is no image- or taste-driven pricing power and demand is highly price-elastic.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: An analyst raised industry consolidation and weaker converters do suffer without backward integration, but Finolex itself lost volume through the year, so any donated share is not visibly accruing to it. | The long view had said: Consolidation is happening and weak players exist, but the evidence shows Finolex on the donating side — leaders grew while its volumes fell.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Poor → Weak. Now: FY26 volumes declined ~4% (Q3 down 14% year-on-year) and management conceded peers saw restocking-led growth while Finolex stayed flat, indicating share loss rather than capture. | The long view had said: Volumes fell 4.4% in FY26 and were flat/negative while peers reported ~20% growth in Q1FY25, and the MD explicitly disclaims any share gain, aiming only to hold share.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Weak. Now: Customers demonstrably put price before trust — agri volumes fell in Q4 purely because farmers waited for cheaper pipes, and April demand was 'subdued' simply because prices were falling. | The long view had said: The brand carries quality assurance in agri pipes and BIS/QCO norms favour certified suppliers, but customers visibly postpone purchases on price, so price is not secondary to trust.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Finolex has genuine brand standing in agri pipes ('predominantly in the agri side relatively stronger'), but nothing in the calls evidences monopoly-like pricing or an aftermarket lock, and farmers openly deferred purchases on price. | The long view had said: Finolex holds genuine mindshare as the default agri PVC pipe brand, acknowledged by both analysts and management as its structural strength.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: PVC pipes carry no switching costs or captive aftermarket — buyers timed purchases purely on price expectations, and the only durable advantage cited is a manufacturing cost edge, not customer captivity. | The long view had said: There is regional/segment concentration in West-India agri pipes but no switching costs or captive aftermarket — buyers defer purchases purely on price expectations.

*Unchanged: Halo effect (caution); Pricing, Mix and Volume; Structural end-market growth; People matter (talent development); Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Distribution to shareholders; Cyclical market growth; Barriers to entry and rationality; Return on capital (headline); Asset turn; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Out of limelight; Technology advantage; Cost of working capital; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Security by obscurity.*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 2 unchanged; 4 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The product is a near-commodity where buyers time purchases on resin prices — Q4FY26 agri volumes stalled because farmers waited for cheaper pipe — and an analyst flagged rivals turning 'more aggressive', with management conceding it has had to be 'careful on our pricing' and accept flat volumes for realisation. (The long view had said: The product is a standardised commodity — management calls it 'primarily a pass-through business' and manages margin through 'schemes and discounts' and 'controlled discounts in the market' — and the company has repeatedly traded volume for price, losing share to cheaper competition (volumes -4.4% in FY26 while margins expanded). Brand strength in agri and backward-integrated cost provide real but partial protection.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Every call in the window frames results around PVC resin/oil price swings rather than end-demand: EBITDA moved from INR207cr (Q1FY25) to INR94cr (Q1FY26) to INR332cr (Q4FY26) on broadly flat-to-lower volumes, and management repeatedly says realisations and margins depend on where commodity prices go, most recently on Strait of Hormuz oil disruption. (The long view had said: Across all 14 calls the business is discussed almost entirely in commodity-price terms — PVC/EDC/VCM spreads, inventory gains and losses, $1,450/t PVC in FY23 collapsing to ~$600-650/t by Q3 FY26 — and results swing violently with it (Q1 FY23 EBITDA -40% year-on-year; Q4 FY26 EBITDA nearly doubled with FY26 margin ~16.5% vs guidance of 'sub-15'). Demand is additionally hostage to monsoon and agri sowing, with volumes falling 14% in Q3 FY26 and -4.4% for FY26.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Technological Innovation (ELEVATED); New Entrants (ELEVATED); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 10 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
