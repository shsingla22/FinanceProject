# Indian Overseas Bank  (IOB) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

Indian Overseas Bank (IOB) is a government-controlled Indian commercial bank, headquartered in Chennai and majority-owned by the Government of India (92.44% shareholding), that takes deposits from the public and lends the money out. It serves over 46 million customers through roughly 3,500 branches, about 3,700 ATMs and more than 13,000 business correspondent agents across India, plus branches in four overseas countries (Singapore, Hong Kong, Thailand and Sri Lanka), and sells personal, corporate and agricultural banking along with credit cards, loans and insurance products. It earns mainly from the spread between the interest it charges on loans and what it pays depositors (net interest income of about Rs 12,574 crore in FY26, at a net interest margin of roughly 3.2-3.4%), plus fee income, gains on its bond/treasury portfolio, commission from selling priority-sector lending certificates, and recoveries on loans previously written off. As of June 2026 it had a total business mix of about Rs 6.98 lakh crore (Rs 3.76 lakh crore of deposits and Rs 3.22 lakh crore of loans) and annual net profit of Rs 5,208 crore for FY26.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Deposits (CASA and term deposits)** — The bank's funding side — current and savings accounts (CASA, about 41% of domestic deposits, Rs 1,54,415 crore), retail term deposits (about 53%, Rs 1,83,601 crore) and a small bulk deposit slice (about 6%) — with total deposits of Rs 3,76,193 crore growing around 14-18% a year.
- **RAM segment (Retail, Agriculture, MSME)** — The core lending engine and management's stated focus, making up about 83% of domestic loans, and the main driver of the bank's loan yields.
- **Retail loans** — Home loans, vehicle loans, personal loans, jewel (gold) loans and deposit-backed loans to individuals; about 31% of domestic advances and the fastest-growing piece at roughly 45% year-on-year in FY26.
- **Agriculture loans** — Farm and rural credit, the single largest slice of domestic lending at about 35% of domestic advances, growing roughly 40% in FY26.
- **MSME loans** — Lending to micro, small and medium businesses, about 17% of domestic advances, growing about 13% overall (23.77% in core MSME), and the channel through which government-backed schemes like ECLGS (Rs 4,400 crore eligible, Rs 2,600 crore disbursed so far) are routed.
- **Corporate and overseas lending** — Loans to large companies, state government entities and the four foreign branches — the remaining roughly 17% of domestic advances, concentrated in A-and-above rated borrowers and carrying very little bad debt (0.24% of NPAs).
- **Treasury / investments** — The bank's bond and SLR securities book, Rs 1,19,810 crore globally as of March 2026, earning a yield of about 6.66% and contributing trading profits to non-interest income.
- **Non-interest income (fees, PSLC, recoveries)** — Fee-based income of Rs 2,732 crore in FY26 plus commission from selling priority-sector lending certificates (Rs 863 crore in the June 2026 quarter alone) and recoveries from technically written-off accounts (Rs 2,252 crore in FY26) — described by management as recurring every quarter.
- **Digital banking** — The delivery channel rather than a separate profit line: about 98% of all transactions now happen digitally, via the IOB Connect mobile app, WhatsApp banking, video KYC, UPI and roughly 4,100 tablet devices that open 40-50% of new accounts.
- **Financial inclusion / rural banking** — Basic no-frills accounts under the government's PMJDY scheme (94.57 lakh accounts) served largely through business correspondents and the roughly 2,040 of 3,494 branches located in rural and semi-urban areas.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** IOB is a nationalised bank 92.44% owned by the Government of India; pricing, provisioning and capital are policy-determined — RBI repo decisions drive NIM, RBI's new ECL guideline creates a ~Rs 3,000 crore provisioning requirement, income depends materially on PSLC commission (Rs 863 crore in one quarter) and government schemes (ECLGS Rs 4,400 crore universe, CGTMSE/CGFMU covering 15–16% of credit), the largest single loss event was PSU exposure MTNL where 'the Government of India is actively working on the resolution', and staff cost is set by the industry-wide bipartite settlement.

*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**What the calls show:** Banking credit quality is inherently cyclical and IOB's own 5-year record is a full cycle — GNPA of 9.82% in FY22 (post-PCA repair) down to 1.42% by FY26, with lumpy events like the MTNL slippage forcing a Rs 2,300 crore upfront provision in Q4 FY25; earnings are lending-flow rather than customer-capex driven, and slippage has held in a narrow 0.06–0.13% band for ten quarters, but NIM still swung with the rate cycle (3.58% Mar-25 to 3.04% Jun-25) and treasury income turned negative in Q4 FY26.
