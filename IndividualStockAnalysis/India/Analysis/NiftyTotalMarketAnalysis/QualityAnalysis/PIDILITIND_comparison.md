**Checks that improved:**

- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Management sees an urban demand upturn beginning (after ~16-20 quarters of rural outperformance) aided by GST 2.0 and tax cuts, with benign VAM at $830 vs $980 — consistent with being early in a recovery rather than late. | The long view had said: The portfolio is deliberately de-cyclicalised (70-75% renovation/repair), but the input-cost cycle has just turned sharply against them with a 40-50% raw material basket inflation, so they sit nearer a cost-cycle top than a bottom.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Good → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹24 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹24 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±2.5 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Management claims growth well ahead of category in tile adhesives with leadership already in retail and closing on overall leadership, plus a full portfolio build and a low-penetration category leaving long runway. | The long view had said: Consistent evidence of taking share from unorganized and smaller players plus rural penetration runway, but in the core adhesives franchise Pidilite is already dominant, limiting headroom.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: Share is being taken from cement-based/unorganised alternatives and from a competitor on its home turf, giving identifiable donators, though the newer entrants are strong paint majors rather than weak players. | The long view had said: An abundant supply of weak donors — unorganized and small players squeezed by input-cost inflation and lacking distribution — from whom Pidilite has demonstrably taken share across art materials, adhesives and tile solutions.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: Demand is driven by reliability of bonded outcomes — thinner, vitrified and vertical/exterior tiles requiring 'more sophisticated solutions' — where failure cost dwarfs product price, letting Roff sell at ~2x cement cost. | The long view had said: The proposition is reliability and efficacy rather than price — customers 'swear by' product performance, and in construction chemicals the cost of failure vastly exceeds the product cost.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Communication is professional and substance-heavy, but this is a widely-covered large cap whose MD does same-day media rounds, so it is neither celebrity-driven nor genuinely under the radar. | The long view had said: Tone is substance-led and operationally specific with no celebrity posturing, though the MD does regular CNBC and media appearances so the profile is not deliberately obscure.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Evidence of long-horizon category-building — a full pyramid portfolio in tile adhesives, a new architect/interior-designer GTM, Pidilite Professional Solutions — and a publicly held 20-24% EBITDA corridor rather than chasing short-term margin. | The long view had said: The core/growth/pioneer architecture has been executed with tenacity across the whole period, shifting the mix from 80-20 to 55-45 over 7-8 years, and rural investment was defended as a 24-month-plus payback rather than a quarterly one.

Now: Deep customer intimacy is evident in district-, town- and dealer-level management plus new influencer-facing GTM for architects and interior designers and expanding e-commerce/quick-commerce presence. | The long view had said: Distribution intimacy is extraordinary and compounding — Pidilite Ki Duniya outlets nearly doubled from 7,500 to 14,000 and direct village coverage reached 28,000 villages over the period, driving eight straight quarters of double-digit rural growth.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: Brand-level ownership of the customer's mind is evident (Roff leadership in retail tile adhesive, mass-media Roff tile cleaner, category defined by the company), but the calls show real competition rather than a true monopoly position. | The long view had said: Fevicol, Dr. Fixit, Fevikwik and now Roff occupy category-defining positions in the customer's mind such that end-consumers specify them even in a done-for-me market, which is the definition of a mind-share monopoly.

Now: Structure is not a stable oligopoly — legacy paint majors have entered tile adhesives and competitive intensity is called out in Gujarat, Hyderabad and Kerala — though Pidilite still retains a price premium. | The long view had said: Pidilite faces a fragmented tail of unorganized players rather than a stable few-player oligopoly, but its scale means the end customer has essentially no negotiating power in core adhesives.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Internal progression exists (Kavinder Singh moving JMD to MD/Chairman), but the five-year history shows repeated churn at MD, Chairman and CFO level, and the calls offer no evidence of a systematic talent-development engine. | The long view had said: Leadership succession has been managed through long overlapping designate periods — Vats sat as Deputy MD then MD-Designate alongside Puri before taking over, with Kavinder Singh similarly groomed as JMD — indicating a planned internal pipeline.
- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: After direct input costs, the company keeps ₹56 of every ₹100 of sales — pricing power customers are happy to pay for. And from the calls: Brand strength supports a premium and steady demand with gross margins improving 100-200bps and a 20-24% EBITDA corridor, but realised pricing was near zero this year, so pricing power was not actually exercised. | The long view had said: After direct input costs, the company keeps ₹56 of every ₹100 of sales — pricing power customers are happy to pay for. ### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 8 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 7. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 3 weakened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Management claims growth well ahead of category across the year — domestic UVG inching up over eight quarters to 11%+ and 15.3% in Q4 FY26 against a soft FMCG backdrop — plus retail leadership in tile adhesive and progress towards overall leadership, with growth from penetration rather than credit or price concessions. (The long view had said: Consistent claims of gaining against unorganised and smaller players, eight-plus quarters of double-digit rural growth and UVG rising ~100–120bps year on year to 11.1% in FY26 — but management never gives explicit market-share numbers or share versus named competitors, and international exposure was reduced (US/Brazil exits).)

**That weakened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is clear portfolio-building and premiumisation language (a complete tile-adhesive portfolio 'right up to the top', new launches, Haisha initiatives), but no R&D spend disclosure, named product revenue contribution, or share-of-industry-R&D evidence in these calls. (The long view had said: The core/growth/pioneer framework has been delivered on over years — mix moved from 80:20 to 55:45 in 7–8 years, with named launches each year (Roofseal Classic 2022, exterior 'bridge between paints and cement' 2024, Roff Cera Clean 2026) and capex redirected to pioneer categories; the gap is that explicit R&D spend is never disclosed.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The calls point to a decades-old incumbency a funded copycat cannot quickly rebuild — the tile-adhesive competitor's regional stronghold that never travelled in 20 years, plus the carpenter/contractor network and rural distribution depth — but the excerpts stop short of articulating a concrete, quantified barrier to replication. (The long view had said: The articulable barrier is decades of rural distribution depth plus carpenter/applicator loyalty that money alone cannot compress — 14,000 branded rural outlets and 28,000 villages covered directly, built over 24+ months of investment, layered on brands that have been household names for generations; management notes the payoff shows only 'over 24 months', not quarters.)
- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → NO FIT. Why now: In FY26 Pidilite moved away from forward integration — it swapped out its BuildNext construction-solutions platform to JSW One for an insignificant stake, and treats third-party marketplaces as something to 'keep evaluating' rather than own; there is no own-store, franchise-royalty or D2C disclosure. (The long view had said: There is channel-branding depth (14,000 'Pidilite Ki Duniya' outlets, quick-commerce/e-commerce presence for Roff) but no owned stores, franchising or royalty stream; the one genuine forward-integration asset, the BuildNext construction platform, was swapped away to JSW One in FY26.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (LIKELY FIT).*

*Unchanged: Friendly Middleman (STRONG FIT); Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 2 worsened, 6 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Government Dependency**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Government policy visibly moved reported numbers this year: US tariffs drove a 13.5% export decline in Q3 (calling it 'probably the harshest quarter'), the new Wage Code forced a one-time INR47 crore standalone charge, the EU trade deal is awaited for future benefit, and management attributes part of the Q4 demand buoyancy to GST 2.0 and budget tax cuts. But pricing is not regulator-set and the business is not anchored in tenders, subsidies or fixed infrastructure. (The long view had said: No regulated pricing, subsidy or tariff-set revenue; the government appears only as one of several construction end-segments and as an indirect demand factor (GST 2.0, budget tax relief). The one genuine policy exposure — US tariffs hitting pigment exports, driving a 13.5% export decline in Q3FY26 — is explicitly a small share of the business and was described as largely behind them.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: No single customer, licensor or distributor is cited as driving results — management describes managing performance state-by-state, district-by-district and even dealer-by-dealer — but the pigments export business is admittedly concentrated on the US, and that single geography's tariff hit was enough to pull group UVG from 11% down to 9% in Q3; VAM is also flagged as the single biggest raw material. (The long view had said: Results are driven by an extremely fragmented dealer/retail base rather than named customers — 7,500 Pidilite Ki Duniyas in FY23 rising to 14,000 by FY25 with ~28,000 villages covered directly — and no single customer, licensor or supplier is ever cited as a swing factor in any of the 16 calls.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
