### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 8 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Management cites 'disciplined pricing actions' contributing to 361bps of cumulative gross-margin expansion over FY24-25 and premiumisation driving organised-channel saliency to 30%, and consolidated gross margin is guided at 66-67%; but no explicit FY26 price increase taken without volume loss is discussed, Q1 FY26 gross margin fell 73bps, and domestic sales grew only 2.4% in FY26 with seasonal brands down 18.8%. (The long view had said: There is repeated evidence of calibrated price increases plus premiumisation lifting gross margin (Q1 FY25 GM +314bps y/y, FY26 consolidated GM guided to 66–67%), but it is not uniform: Complan faces category down-trading and price war, and Nutralite's 'value growth trailed volume due to market driven prices' — i.e. deflation in part of the portfolio.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: There are explicit rising share numbers across the year — sweetener +97.9bps (Sep-25), +80bps (Dec-25), +24bps (Mar-26), Everyuth scrubs +262bps, Complan moving from fifth to fourth rank, Sugar Free Green's 20th consecutive quarter of double-digit growth — but gains are narrow and some reverse (peel-off masks -56.4bps), and FY26 headline growth of 46.4% came overwhelmingly from acquisitions while the domestic business grew just 2.4%. (The long view had said: Explicit, repeated share gains across four years: Glucon-D 60.4% (+203bps) in Aug 2022, Everyuth scrub 41.8%→46.2% (+373bps), peel-off 76%→78.2%, Sugar Free 95.5%→95.9% then +80bps and +24bps in FY26, plus international expansion (Comfort Click into Poland/Finland/Portugal, RiteBite into 9 countries in year one). Gains are not bought with margin — gross margins rose alongside — though Complan is the standing exception at ~4% share.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The core Indian brands show pricing power — 96.1% sugar-substitute share, 33.3% in Nycil, 48.7% in scrubs, and 'most brands recorded gross margin expansion' — but the newer growth engines (protein bars, gummies, supplements sold on Amazon/D2C) are exactly the kind of easily-replicated products where cheaper own-label and challenger listings compete on the same shelf. The calls never address that price-defence question. (The long view had said: Complan shows the classic good-enough attack — cheaper pouches and sachets taking category share while the branded large packs decline, with management conceding it is only a ~5% player choosing to 'protect' rather than win; the protein-bar and European VMS categories are similarly low-complexity and crowded (8–10% share there).)

**That worsened:**

- **Government Dependency**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Fortunes are not regulator-set, but tax policy moves results in both directions: GST 2.0 caused 'transitory business disruptions' in trade channels, >85% of the domestic portfolio sits in the 5% slab so slab changes flow straight to affordability, and Q3 FY26 other operating income fell simply because a INR90mn GST budgetary support grant was in the base. Q4 also flagged gas supply headwinds at Nutralite. (The long view had said: Revenue is consumer-brand, market-priced and not set by any regulator, tariff or subsidy, and the business owns no fixed infrastructure; the only policy touchpoints in four years of calls are a small INR90 million GST budgetary support booked in Q3 FY25 (whose absence dented other operating income) and one-off new labour code costs.)
- **Fashion Risk**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: A growing share of growth now sits in taste- and trend-driven wellness formats — protein bars, Korean-flavour chips, adult gummies, Himalayan Shilajit resin — and management explicitly leans on 'cultural relevance' and a celebrity partnership with a 14-year-old cricketer to revive Complan. These categories can cool as fast as they heated. (The long view had said: Growth is credited to functional, repeat-purchase categories — glucose powder, prickly heat powder, scrubs, sweeteners, supplements — not to a trending product moment; celebrity endorsers (Pankaj Tripathi, Katrina Kaif, Vaibhav Suryavanshi) are used to build category relevance rather than being the demand driver, and repeat rates are measured and rising.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (ELEVATED).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
