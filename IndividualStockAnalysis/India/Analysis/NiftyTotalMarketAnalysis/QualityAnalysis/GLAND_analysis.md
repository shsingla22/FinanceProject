# Gland Pharma (GLAND) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Gland Pharma is an India-based maker of injectable medicines — drugs given by needle or drip, such as vials, lyophilized (freeze-dried) vials, ampoules, prefilled syringes and cartridges — manufactured at plants around Hyderabad (Dundigal, Pashamylaram, Genome Valley) plus sites in France and Belgium. It makes money two ways: selling its own generic injectables (Enoxaparin, Ertapenem, Bortezomib, Pemetrexed, Dalbavancin and the like) largely to pharma partners, distributors, hospital group-purchasing contracts and government tenders rather than directly to patients; and, increasingly, manufacturing under contract for other drug companies (CDMO), which was 46% of FY26 revenue. Doing this requires heavy regulatory paperwork — as of mid-2024 it and its partners had filed 356 ANDAs with the US FDA and held 1,708 product registrations worldwide. The United States is by far its largest market (about INR 33 billion of FY26's INR 64.3 billion total), followed by rest-of-world markets (MENA, LATAM, APAC), Europe and other regulated markets, and a small India business; FY26 revenue was INR 64,307 million with a 26% adjusted EBITDA margin.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Base business – United States (core market)** — Sells its own generic injectables in the US through partners and hospital GPO contracts; the single biggest piece of the company at INR 33,181 million in FY26 (+11%), with 31 new product launches during the year, though management repeatedly calls the market crowded and price-competitive.
- **Base business – Europe and other regulated markets** — Sells the same injectable portfolio into Europe, Canada and Australia; much smaller, at INR 3,249 million in FY26 (+11%), and grouped with the US as 'core markets', which were 76-82% of revenue in the earlier years covered.
- **Base business – Rest of the world** — Supplies partners across MENA, LATAM and APAC, largely through lumpy tender-led orders; INR 6,511 million in FY26 (+7%), roughly 12-20% of revenue over the period, and the segment most exposed to timing swings in tenders.
- **Base business – India** — The smallest market at INR 2,672 million in FY26 (only about 4-6% of revenue), including a dedicated insulin line; management said in 2024 it was deliberately shrinking this business and weighing strategic options for it.
- **CDMO (contract development and manufacturing)** — Makes drugs on contract for other companies — including monoclonal antibodies and plasma-based proteins at its Genome Valley biologics site, biosimilars, oncology products, peptides, GLP-1 cartridges and prefilled syringes — and is now the company's stated growth engine at 46% of FY26 revenue, growing 28%, with cartridge fill-finish capacity being expanded from 40 million to 140 million units.
- **Cenexi (European CDMO subsidiary)** — An acquired French/Belgian contract manufacturer with plants at Fontenay near Paris, Hérouville, Osny and in Belgium doing ampoule and aseptic fill-finish work; roughly €43-45 million of revenue a quarter, it lost money for several years and only became EBITDA-positive by FY26.
- **R&D and product filings** — Spends about 4-5% of base-business revenue developing new injectables and filing ANDAs (8 filed in one quarter of FY25), which is what feeds the launch pipeline that management credits for most growth.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Good Enough Goods — HIGH RISK
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** This is the core structural risk and it recurs in every call: generic injectables are undifferentiated, the buying decision sits with GPOs and hospital intermediaries rather than any brand-aware end patient, and pricing is the lever — 'visible price pressure in the US' in FY23, a low-margin heparin GPO contract diluting FY25 margins, and by FY26 a standing 1-2% cost-savings programme explicitly framed as needed to 'offset any pricing pressure'.

> "These measures typically deliver savings of 1 to 2 percentage points, helping offset any pricing pressure or product mix impact." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Genuine niche/scale protection is being built — reallocation of capacity to complex and niche formulations, 505(b)2 and RTU-bag platforms, Dalbavancin, and dedicated-line CDMO contracts where switching costs and tech-transfer barriers replace brand as the defence*

- ✅ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs held near 63% (was 56%) — no gross-level price erosion.
- ✅ Operating margin (25%) remains near its historical peak (38%) — no collapse in the premium.
- ⚠️ Operating margin drifted down from about 36% to 24% — steady profit erosion, the mark of rising competitive pressure.

### Stakeholder Concentration — ELEVATED
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** A B2B model where named partners, GPO contracts and single suppliers drive results: FY23 revenue fell 26% because of API/primary-packing shortages and dependence on BD for syringes, a single GPO heparin contract visibly diluted group gross margin in Q1FY25, and Fosun's stake sales drew repeated analyst questions; the trajectory improves but the structure persists.

> "we have enough supplies commitments from BD. But we are qualifying a Chinese supplier as well as Italian supplier" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate broadening — alternate vendor development and alternate API sourcing as a standing cost programme, 8 GLP-1 contracts signed with 6-7 more pending, multiple new CDMO partners across oncology/peptides/PFS, and geographic spread via Cenexi in Europe*

- ⚠️ Customers now take about 102 days to pay, up from 84 — lengthening collection terms are how weak bargaining power against concentrated customers shows up in the numbers.

### New Entrants — ELEVATED
*"A new player changes the rules"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Management repeatedly frames the US generic injectables market as structurally crowded and competes by lowering cost rather than holding price — winning contracts 'with competitive pricing' funded by procurement savings and scale economies — the classic margin-investment-as-competitive-response pattern, though no single new-format entrant is named.

> "the US market remains crowded, requiring manufacturers to maintain continued focus on cost competitiveness" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Building its own differentiated channel — CDMO now 46% of total revenue growing 28%, with dedicated-line long-term contracts that are harder for a new entrant to contest than open ANDA competition*

- ⚠️ Operating margin drifted down from about 36% to 24% — steady profit erosion, the mark of rising competitive pressure.
- ⬜ Insufficient sales history.

### Technological Innovation — WATCH
*"Out-invented by the industry"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** The installed base is overwhelmingly small-molecule sterile fill-finish while value is migrating to biologics, GLP-1 cartridges, peptides and biosimilars; management concedes the capability gap ('most of our capacities and capabilities are on the small molecule side') and is buying its way in via new lines, in-licensing and CDMO tie-ups rather than owning the platform.

> "Today, most of our capacities and capabilities are on the small molecule side." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Steady small-scale, value-adding innovation cadence — 24 ANDAs filed and 31 US launches in FY26, RTU bags, 505(b)2 pipeline, ~5% of sales on R&D — plus early positioning in cartridges/GLP-1 rather than defending a legacy format*

- ⬜ Insufficient sales history.
- ✅ Operating margin (25%) remains near its historical peak (38%) — no collapse in the premium.

### Government Dependency — WATCH
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Fortunes turn on regulators rather than a single government customer: US FDA surprise inspections with Form 483 observations, Indian price control made domestic supply uneconomic ('not making any sense to operate at the price level which are under price control in India'), export restrictions on Enoxaparin, and RoW revenue is tender-led procurement that slips with government buying cycles; the Biosecure Act is a policy tailwind that could as easily reverse.

> "it's not making any sense to operate at the price level which are under price control in India" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate diversification away from the policy-priced Indian market (down to ~4% of revenue by FY25) toward US/Europe and contract-backed CDMO revenue with durable multi-year visibility*

### Cyclicality — LOW
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the judge affirmatively found the risk absent or mitigated in the calls.

**What the calls show:** Across all four calls demand is described as steady hospital/therapeutic consumption of injectables — a flow product, not customer capex; when revenue fell 26% in Q1FY23 management attributed it explicitly to supply, not demand, and FY26 shows 14.5% revenue growth with 26% EBITDA margins and countercyclical INR2,000 crore capex.

> "Primarily it's a supply chain issue... The good aspect is that the demand stays strong." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/consumable demand (hospital injectables) plus countercyclical investment — INR2,000 crores brownfield capex and cartridge capacity raised from 40m to 140m units while margins were still recovering*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 6 years with swings of ±26 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 23% and 38% (±6.2 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 7% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 12%).
