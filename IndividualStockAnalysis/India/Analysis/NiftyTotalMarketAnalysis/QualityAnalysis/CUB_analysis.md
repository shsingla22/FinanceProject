# City Union Bank Ltd. (CUB) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

City Union Bank is an old private-sector commercial bank in India, incorporated on 31 October 1904 and headquartered in Kumbakonam, Thanjavur district, Tamil Nadu, with its shares listed on the NSE and BSE. It does plain, traditional banking: it collects deposits from the public — current and savings accounts (CASA) plus term deposits — and lends that money out, mainly as working-capital and business loans to small and medium enterprises (MSMEs), loans against gold (especially agricultural gold loans), and secured retail loans such as housing loans and loans against property. It earns most of its money from the spread between what it charges borrowers (yield on advances around 9.8%) and what it pays depositors (cost of deposits around 5.6%), giving a net interest margin of roughly 3.6%–3.8%, topped up by fees like loan processing charges and income from its investment/treasury book. Management repeatedly stresses that it deliberately stays away from large corporate consortium lending, infrastructure and unsecured retail, and by mid-2026 the bank had grown to about Rs. 67,645 crores of advances and Rs. 79,342 crores of deposits, with return on assets around 1.55%.

**The parts of the business:**

- **MSME lending (core business)** — Loans to small and medium-sized businesses — described as the bank's core strength and the segment that 'will continue to dominate' the loan book, and the main engine behind the 24%–26% loan growth reported in FY26 and Q1FY27.
- **Gold loans (including agricultural gold loans)** — Lending against pledged gold jewellery, with a particular focus on agricultural gold loans; it is named alongside MSME as one of the two traditional lines that carried growth, and management says gold loans still fetch 'a decent rate' that supports margins.
- **Secured retail (housing loans, loan against property, micro-LAP)** — A newer vertical, built out from around FY25 with added staff, a retail team and Newgen loan-origination software; it contributed almost nothing at first ('incremental credit growth from retail are yet to start' in FY25) but by FY26 was described as an additional growth enhancer on top of MSME.
- **Deposits (CASA and term deposits)** — The funding side of the bank — savings and current accounts plus fixed deposits from customers, Rs. 79,342 crores as of June 2026 and growing 21% year-on-year, with management tracking average CASA (Rs. 20,062 crores) and a credit-to-deposit ratio kept around 84%–85%.
- **Treasury / investments** — The bank's bond and investment portfolio, which yields roughly 6.4%–6.7%; it can add trading profits in good years but produced almost nothing in FY23 when rates rose (only Rs. 51 lakhs of trading profit in one quarter), and in Q1FY27 included about Rs. 700 crores parked in mutual funds earning Rs. 12 crores.
- **Other income / fee income** — Non-interest earnings, chiefly loan processing charges (which roughly doubled from Rs. 23 crores to Rs. 38 crores between Q1 and Q2 of FY25) plus items like mutual fund income and income-tax refund interest; small relative to interest income but a visible swing factor in the cost-to-income ratio.
- **Recovery and asset-quality management** — An in-house effort to collect on bad loans, including technically written-off accounts, which management treats almost as a profit line — recoveries (Rs. 206 crores in Q1FY27) have exceeded fresh slippages (Rs. 195 crores) for several years running, pulling gross NPAs down from 4.65% in mid-2022 to 1.73% by mid-2026.
- **Third-party / DSA sourcing** — Loans sourced through outside direct selling agents rather than the bank's own branches — explicitly kept small, at only 1%–2% of the overall bank book.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## The verdict: Decent — 59 out of 100 ★★★☆☆

In one breath: the quality framework finds an average business today (58/100); it strongly fits 1 of the 11 patterns long-term winners share; the risk review found nothing severe. Weighing those together gives 59 out of 100 — decent.

**The exact arithmetic:** Overall = 45% × 58 (business quality) + 30% × 43 (multibagger fit) + 25% × 80 (risk safety) = 59 out of 100 → Decent (3 stars).

- **Business quality (58/100):** The 34-check quality framework scored the business +0.32 on its −2 (poor) to +2 (excellent) scale, with 79% of checks backed by evidence; mapped onto 0–100 that is 58 points.
- **Multibagger fit (43/100):** the foundation test (steady cash + high returns on capital + growth) passed in full (+25); 1 pattern fit strongly (Corporate Culture) (+15); 1 numbers-only hint (+3) → 43 of 100.

### The story in depth

City Union Bank scores 59 out of 100 — "Decent", three stars — built from 58 for business quality, 43 for multibagger fit and 80 for risk safety. Almost all of the strength sits in one place: management. That area scores 1.5, far above every other, on three top marks — candor, disciplined stewardship, and independent long-term thinking. The same evidence produces the screen's only strong pattern fit, Corporate Culture: across sixteen calls management sets explicit numeric targets (1.5% return on assets, a narrow margin band, slippages of Rs.700-750 crore, cost-to-income of 48-50%) and then reports itself against them, including naming its own bad year, when unwinding the farm-credit gold book cut growth to 2%. One managing director has run the bank for fifteen years and sixty calls through the asset-quality review, the 2014 gold crash, demonetisation, GST and COVID, and refused the unsecured retail lending boom while admitting it cost a year of growth. That refusal is not just a character trait — it is why the risk review rates borrower concentration LOW (loans spread across MSME at 55-60%, gold at 31-32% and secured retail around 10%, with the only named single exposure, SpiceJet at Rs.97 crore, fully provided for) and why the cyclicality entry has a silver lining at all.

The growth engine is branches and technology, and both show up twice — once as a pattern, once as a risk. The bank owns its distribution and keeps building it: 50-75 new branches promised in 2022, roughly 1,000 branches now, 70 opened in the first month of FY27, with agent-sourced business deliberately capped at 1-2% of the book. That earns a partial fit on Forward Integrators and on Cost to Replicate — a 122-year-old deposit and Tamil Nadu MSME relationship base a funded newcomer cannot assemble quickly — and it is exactly the reason Friendly Middleman is a clear no fit, since the bank avoids the third-party channel that pattern requires. It is also the stated defence against the New Entrants risk, where analysts keep probing gold-loan competition from other banks and non-bank lenders and fintech digital lenders. Technology reads the same way: the innovation checks credit a run of India-firsts (a service robot, cardless cash withdrawal, wearable debit cards, video KYC now used for over 90% of new accounts, an own credit card, then the Newgen loan-origination system credited for the 26% advance growth), yet the risk review flags that management spends long passages rebutting the perception that older private banks lag on technology — the same activity, read as capability by one lens and as an incumbent defending itself by another. Market share gain follows the identical shape: explicitly targeted and delivered at 24-26% business growth in FY26, the best since 2013, but only a partial pattern fit because of the 2% growth lapse in December 2023.

What caps the score is the earnings model itself, and here the checks, the patterns and the risks all point the same way. Growth is volume, not price: advances rose 26% while the yield on loans barely moved (9.81%, then 9.75%, then 9.79%), and management says it cannot push pricing. That single fact produces three negative checks — pricing and mix, intangible benefits, and asset turn — a no fit on Pricing Power, only a partial on Brand Strength and Low Price Plus, and the Good Enough Goods risk, where an analyst put it bluntly that the only remaining lever for margin is the cost of funds. Being a regulated lender compounds it: the Government Dependency risk notes that RBI decisions move the profit line directly, with about half the loan book repriced off the policy rate. Returns on capital score the worst of any area at -1.0, since a Rs.78,000 crore balance sheet earning about 1.55% is structurally asset-heavy rather than capital-light. And the cycle check is negative not because conditions are bad but because they are as good as they get — bad loans below 2% for the first time in eleven years, early-stress loans at 0.72%, management calling it the best asset quality in 22 years — meaning the 0.40% credit-cost guidance has more room to rise than fall. The overall picture: a well-run, honestly reported, low-blow-up-risk regional lender whose upside depends on volume and cost control rather than on any pricing or brand advantage, which is why risk safety scores 80 while multibagger fit scores only 43. Resilience is recorded as unknown.

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Bank earnings visibly swing with credit, rate and gold-price cycles — advances growth went from 14% (Dec 2022) to 2% (Dec 2023, unwinding the KCC gold portfolio) to 26% in Q4FY26, treasury income vanished in FY23 on rising yields, and NIM/CIR guidance is repeatedly framed around the rate cycle; management itself catalogues cycle events (2008 GFC, AQR, 2014 gold crash, demonetisation, GST, COVID) it had to navigate. > "we had to greater extent navigated through multiple cycles, be it the 2008 global financial crisis... the AQRs, the gold price crash of 2014, demonetization, and the introduction of the GST" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Technological Innovation — WATCH
*"Out-invented by the industry"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

## What to watch

- Credit cost against the 0.40% guidance — the Cyclical market growth check is negative precisely because bad loans are at an 11-year low and early-stress loans at 0.72%, so any rise would come off the best asset quality in 22 years.
