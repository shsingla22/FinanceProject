### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: The business model is forward integration: 223 exclusive showrooms across 161 cities plus SIS and factory shops, ~70% of revenue through own retail, and online run as own-warehouse B2C marketplace (seven warehouses) rather than outright sale to platforms — with new warehouses in Ludhiana, Hyderabad and Howrah to get closer to the customer. (The long view had said: RedTape is a manufacturer-brand that has moved fully to the customer: ~70% of sales through its own exclusive stores across 300+ cities, and the 30% online is run B2C on its own marketplace warehouses (seven, going to nine) rather than outright sales to platforms.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Repeatedly evidenced across both FY26 calls: 30 years of brand equity, a deliberate multi-brand architecture (RedTape flagship plus Ozark, Bond Street, Mode launched in FY26), sustained marketing investment including the RedTape 10K Runs, and successful adjacency stretch into accessories (sunglasses, hard luggage, grooming — 3% of revenue but fastest-growing and highest margin). (The long view had said: Genuine assets — claimed 30 years of brand equity, a multi-brand architecture (RedTape, Ozark, Bond Street, Mode), A&P investment including the RedTape 10K Run, and successful stretch into sunglasses, luggage and grooming — but the brand is sold at a permanent 70-80% off, so it delivers volume without the premium pricing the pattern requires.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: ASPs are rising 8-10% via mix (Ozark at INR2,100-2,500, ETPU replacing EVA, premium leather) and gross margin held at 43.4% in Q4 FY26, but management admits nothing sells at MRP — a permanent 70%-off architecture with an inflated MRP is the opposite of demonstrated price realisation, and the FY26 volume catalyst was a GST cut passed to consumers. (The long view had said: Management explicitly declines price increases despite raw-material pressure, passed the entire GST cut through as deeper discounts (70% off to 74-80%), and admits ASPs fell after GST; gross margin has drifted down for years — this is the price-deflation opposite the pattern warns against, notwithstanding some premiumisation talk around Ozark and leather.)

**That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No licence, AMC, subscription or deferred-revenue stream exists — this is outright retail sale of footwear/apparel, and working capital runs heavily positive (inventory of 175 days on sales, ~300 on COGS, with working capital days up 62 in H1 FY26) rather than customer-funded. (The long view had said: the calls are silent and the numbers are mixed (2 of 3 checks supporting).)
- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Disclosure quality and stability both work against this: the CFO's own FY26 overview garbled the revenue history (INR1,831 crores in 2024 then INR218 crores in 2025), store counts are inconsistent between the two calls (513 exclusive stores in Nov 2025 versus 223 exclusive showrooms in May 2026), basic questions were deflected ('we will come back to you' on lease liability; 'we do not measure' on repeat customers), and the company carries no inventory ageing provision despite 175-300 days of stock — alongside repeated CFO and MD turnover across FY2023-FY2026. (The long view had said: They did deliver what they promised on the Nov 2025 call — 'Q3-Q4 the results will speak for itself' turned into 17.8% SSSG and INR244cr PAT, and the promised inventory-day reduction (250 to 170) is happening — but disclosure quality is weak: garbled revenue history in the CFO's own opening, repeated 'we'll have to come back on this' on payable days, no segment margins, no repeat-customer measurement, and heavy churn in the Chairman/MD/CFO seats across FY23-FY26.)

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Cost to Replicate (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 6 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Pricing power is optically, not structurally, defended: management concedes it never sells at MRP, that the MRP is inflated and a permanent 70% discount is the real price — a mass sub-INR2,500 price band sold alongside cheaper substitutes on marketplaces where the platform, not the brand, frames the comparison. Rising ASPs, 43.4% gross margin and EBITDA expanding to 19% show the premium is holding for now. (The long view had said: The entire model is permanent deep discounting — 'we don't sell anything on MRP', a jacked-up MRP with a constant 70% off, taken to 74–80% plus BOGO after the GST cut — which is the behaviour of a business with limited pricing power in a low-complexity, price-shopped category; an analyst directly challenged whether this erodes brand positioning, and gross margin has drifted down (47% to 39% in Q2, 43.4% in Q4) while management declined to disclose category-level margins.)

**That worsened:**

- **Fashion Risk**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: This is a taste-driven fashion and lifestyle portfolio where management itself says freshness is what brings customers back, yet inventory ran at ~175 days on sales (peaking above 250) versus peers, with no ageing provisions taken at all — meaning any style that stops selling sits on the balance sheet at cost. Growth is also being pinned on newly launched trend brands (Ozark, Bond Street, Mode) whose durability is untested. (The long view had said: This is a taste-driven fashion business carrying 175 days of inventory (peaked above 250) with no aging provisioning at all, on the assertion that nothing has ever been sold below cost — an analyst explicitly flagged freshness versus Bata and Metro; growth also leans on trend categories like sneakers and athleisure.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
