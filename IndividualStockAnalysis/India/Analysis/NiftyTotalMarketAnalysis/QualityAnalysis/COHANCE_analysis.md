# Cohance Life (COHANCE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Cohance Lifesciences (formerly Suven Pharmaceuticals, listed in India as COHANCE) is a contract manufacturer and developer of complex chemicals for other companies — it does not sell its own branded drugs. Its customers are global innovator pharmaceutical firms, biotech companies, other Western CDMOs, generic drug makers, and agrochemical and specialty-materials innovators, who pay it to develop and then manufacture drug ingredients, intermediates and specialised chemicals. It makes money by supplying these molecules under contract: development-stage programs that scale up into commercial supply once a customer's drug is approved, plus a steadier book of active pharmaceutical ingredients and finished formulations sold to many customers. Manufacturing and R&D are based in India (sites at Pashamylaram, Suryapet, Vizag, Nacharam and a Genome Valley R&D centre in the Hyderabad area), with a US operation via NJ Bio, serving export customers in the US, Europe and Japan; FY26 revenue was about INR 22.68 billion, down roughly 13% year on year.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Pharma CDMO** — Contract development and manufacturing of drug intermediates and active ingredients for innovator pharma and biotech customers under patent, including advanced niche platforms — ADC payloads and payload-linkers (with NJ Bio in the US doing bioconjugation) and oligonucleotides/amidites via the Sapala platform; it brought in INR 8.89 billion in FY26 (commercial products over 70% of it), runs 140+ active projects and a 10-program Phase 3 pipeline, and management treats it as the strategic growth engine even though it declined sharply in FY26 on customer destocking.
- **API Plus** — A broad portfolio of niche small-to-mid-volume active pharmaceutical ingredients plus finished-dosage formulations sold to generic and other pharma partners, backed by DMF/CEP and partner ANDA filings; at INR 10.88 billion in FY26 it is the single largest revenue segment, down 8% year on year after a warning letter and production disruption at the Nacharam formulation site.
- **Specialty Chemicals** — Contract manufacture of active ingredients and advanced intermediates for large global agrochemical innovators, plus performance chemicals such as photochromic coating materials and OLED materials with early work in electronic/semiconductor chemistries; the smallest segment at INR 2.913 billion in FY26 (down 2.1%), with management calling FY27 a qualification year and growth expected only from FY28.

### The story in depth

Cohance Life is a contract manufacturer for drug innovators that scores 37 out of 100 overall — a Mixed, two-star verdict built from 47 on business quality, 33 on multibagger fit and just 24 on risk safety, with the resilience read coming out as STRAINED. The genuine strength sits in what the company makes and who it makes it for. It keeps ₹71 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs, its end markets — outsourced innovator work, antibody-drug-conjugate payload-linkers, oligonucleotides and the shift of supply out of China — are in real long-term expansion, and there is a serious capability build behind that: a 25,000 sq ft research centre at Genome Valley, 640 scientists, three payload filings made in FY26, a cGMP oligonucleotide facility, and capital spending of ₹2.15bn in FY26 rising to roughly ₹3bn planned for FY27. That build is the reason Innovation Dominance is the single pattern rated a likely fit, and the reason three more — Toll Roads, Cost to Replicate and Market Share Gainers — come in as partial: regulatory filings, 16-plus innovator audits in one quarter, being the exclusive payload intermediate supplier to a major innovator, and leadership positions in eight of the top ten molecules are all things a newcomer cannot assemble quickly.

The trouble is that the same traits generating those patterns also generate the biggest risks. Being the exclusive supplier on a deep, hard-to-replicate product is exactly what makes Stakeholder Concentration an elevated risk: two large commercial products alone accounted for about ₹260 crore of the FY26 shortfall, three API products caused most of the rest, and the top 15 products are roughly half of API revenue. The heavy investment that funds the innovation story is what drags Return on Capital and Capital Allocation into negative territory — every ₹1 of assets produces only ₹0.40 of sales, about 263 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and the capital spent so far has bought falling output rather than growth, with revenue down 13% and admitted low utilisation. That combination of a fixed, asset-heavy cost base against swinging demand is precisely why Cyclicality is the one channel rated high risk. And the fat gross margin should not be read as pricing power: Pricing Power is scored a clear no-fit, with a 4-5% API price correction in FY24, pricing pressure on mature molecules in FY26, and management in May 2026 asking customers to absorb 100-150 basis points of cost inflation. The generic API and agrochemical half of the business faces cheaper Chinese competition and interchangeable substitutes, which is what puts both New Entrants and Good Enough Goods at elevated risk and dilutes the otherwise attractive innovator mix.

The third thread is who is steering. The Suven-Cohance combination was a large transformational merger rather than a modest add-on, and adjusted margins have fallen from 39.4% in FY24 to 21% in FY26, with the acquired businesses NJ Bio and Sapala explicitly named as a drag — this is the worst-scoring check in the whole framework alongside working capital. The FY24 pledge to double the combined platform organically within five years is already off-track two years in, and leadership has turned over repeatedly, with a new chief executive arriving barely nine or ten days before the May 2026 call. That record is why Corporate Culture is a no-fit pattern and why the Management and Capital Allocation areas both score negative. It also colours the one genuinely hopeful forward signal: management calls the first quarter of FY27 the low point with recovery from the second half, and the framework does credit the business with sitting at or near a cyclical trough — but that call comes from a team whose previous promises did not land. Separately, regulators have already shown they can gate the profit and loss account, with the Nacharam inspection outcome and subsequent US warning letter deferring roughly ₹55-61 crore of shipments and halting US supply. In short: a real and hard-to-copy capability inside a business that is asset-heavy, cash-hungry, concentrated, price-taking and mid-restructure — worth watching for evidence the recovery is real, not yet worth paying up for.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Technological Innovation, Stakeholder Concentration, New Entrants, Good Enough Goods, Fashion Risk. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.1× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 84% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 2 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across the 21 calls the revenue line swings violently on factors outside the company's control: COVID one-off molecules (~INR120cr) that vanished in FY23, Ag-chem destocking and 'commodity pricing' in FY24, then pharma customer destocking of two large commercial molecules worth ~INR260cr plus biotech funding-cycle slowdowns in FY26, taking FY26 revenue down ~13% and adjusted EBITDA margin from ~41% (FY24) to 21%. Demand is operations-linked (commercial API/intermediate supply, >70% of Pharma CDMO revenue is commercial), not customer capex, but customer inventory and launch-sequencing cycles have repeatedly whipsawed both sales and margins.

> "FY'24 was a perfect storm with a global slowdown, Ag-Chem destocking, COVID flush-out and commodity pricing." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/consumable character of demand — commercial products are >70% of Pharma CDMO revenue with reload conversion above 90%; countercyclical investment continues (INR2.15bn capex FY26, INR3bn planned FY27, ADC/oligo build-out) while revenue declines.*

- ⚠️ Year-to-year sales growth swings by ±50 percentage points — boom-and-bust revenue, not a steady compounder.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from 19% to 46% (swinging ±9.4 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 12% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 21%).

### Technological Innovation — ELEVATED
*"Out-invented by the industry"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** The business is chemistry-manufacturing rather than a proprietary format, and management is investing on the right side of the modality shift (ADC payload-linkers, oligonucleotides, exatecan-linked payloads, semiconductor-linked chemistries) — this is the small-scale, value-adding innovation cadence that is a mitigant. But the installed small-molecule/API base does face obsolescence at the product level (a large commercial molecule at patent expiry, OLED 'going through a product cycle transition'), and the platform pivot itself signals that legacy small-molecule capacity is not where growth sits.

> "capabilities such as ADCs and oligonucleotides, which are well-differentiated and hard to replicate" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Continuous incremental platform innovation — 3 payload DMFs filed in FY26, adjacent payload platform onboarding two new large innovators, cGMP oligo building-block facility, 640 scientists.*

- ✅ Growth is holding up: 37% a year earlier, 19% recently — no structural stall.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has collapsed from a peak of 46% to 19% — the Nobel-Biocare-style fingerprint of a premium being competed away.

### Stakeholder Concentration — ELEVATED
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but the only computed fingerprint does not confirm it — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Repeatedly, a handful of relationships and molecules decide the year: two large commercial products drove ~INR260cr of the FY26 shortfall, three API products 'accounted for most of the impact', top 15 products are ~half of API revenue, and the company is 'the exclusive payload intermediate supplier to a major innovator'. > "we are beginning to see diversified customer base and diversified customer orders coming in versus our historical reliance on one or two customers" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Active broadening — 140+ active projects, a nine-person global BD team, Western CDMO and Japanese customer onboarding, API portfolio repositioning 'to reduce dependence on a small set of products'.*

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 2 numeric fingerprints reinforce it.

**What the calls show:** The generic API and formulation half of the business is genuinely exposed to cheaper substitutes — management conceded a 4-5% price correction across products in FY24 and 'select mature molecules experienced pricing pressure' in FY26 — and here the customer, not the end patient, controls the choice. ## What to watch
