### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 1 weakened, 10 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: AMC fees are inherently recurring on an installed base — management repeatedly cites 50.8 lakh folios, 21 lakh active SIP accounts and INR755-768 crores of monthly SIP/STP inflows as the franchise's core strength (Nov 2025, Jan 2026, May 2026 calls). But there is no upfront product sale, no attach/renewal-rate disclosure, and no customer advances/deferred revenue funding the business. (The long view had said: Management repeatedly frames AMC economics as annuity revenue on a sticky asset base with heavy operating leverage — a block of business earns fees in years two, three and four at the same cost structure. FY26 revenue of INR424.9cr on INR1.17 lakh crore average AUM, 21 lakh SIP accounts and a INR41,000cr SIP AUM are the recurring engine; no deferred-revenue/customer-advance funding is discussed.)

*Unchanged: Friendly Middleman (STRONG FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Innovation Dominance (NO FIT); Market Share Gainers (NO FIT); Cost to Replicate (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 2 worsened, 3 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Revenue is a fee on AUM, so it moves with the market: the Apr-2026 call reported Nifty correcting ~15% in Q4 FY26 and closing AUM falling to ~INR1.07 lakh crores (up only 3.2% year-on-year) before recovering to ~INR1.2 lakh crores in Q1 FY27, and management concedes the cost/revenue asymmetry. But this is market-level, not capex-cycle, exposure and the SIP/STP book (INR755-768 crores monthly, 21 lakh accounts) is operations-like recurring flow that kept building through the correction. (The long view had said: Revenue is a fee on a 91%-equity AUM book, so market drawdowns flow straight through: NIFTY fell ~15% in Q4 FY26, closing AUM dropped from ~INR1.18 lakh cr (Sep-25) to ~INR1.07 lakh cr (Mar-26), and MTM treasury gains swung from INR26 cr to INR2 cr in adjacent quarters. It is market-linked rather than a capex/commodity cycle, and the recurring SIP book (INR41,000 cr AUM, ~1/3 of assets) is an operations-linked, annuity-style flow that cushions the trough.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The calls affirmatively show a broad and broadening base on both sides: over 56,890 empaneled distribution partners (up from 53,955 a year earlier), 29 branches (from 23), 50.8 lakh folios, 86% individual investors and ~24% of AUM from B-30 locations. No single customer, distributor or licensor is discussed as driving results across the four calls. (The long view had said: This is an admittedly distribution-led business where large distributors dictate pricing — management repeatedly framed the choice as accepting 2 bps of yield dilution to win a distributor's block of business, and Canara Bank parentage drives brand recall in the South. Against that, the panel base broadened from ~53,955 to ~56,890 empanelled partners and branches from 23 to 29, with 86% individual investors, so no single relationship is called out as decisive.)
- **Fashion Risk**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Demand is functional and repeat-purchase — SIP contributions of ~INR755-768 crores a month across 21 lakh accounts — not taste-driven, and management explicitly refused to chase the trending gold/silver ETF and commodity categories when an analyst pushed for them in Apr-2026, on the grounds that they are cyclical rather than all-weather. (The long view had said: Industry flows concentrated into whichever categories were in favour — arbitrage, small cap and mid cap — and CRAMC's QAAUM grew only ~1% quarter-on-quarter partly because its book is not positioned there, while performance quartiles were questioned as the direct driver of net inflows. Management deliberately avoids trend products (gold/silver ETFs) and the SIP base is a functional, repeat-purchase demand source, which bounds the exposure.)

**That worsened:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Management itself calls the business commoditised and concedes that any product innovation 'can always be copied by the industry' — the cheaper good-enough alternative (index funds/ETFs at a fraction of active TER) is exactly what analysts kept asking for, and the intermediary (distributor) largely controls the end-investor's choice, mirroring the Nobel Biocare mechanism. Management admits entering that space would compress yield. (The long view had said: Management itself describes the product as commoditised with regulator-enforced sameness across 52 AMCs, and CRAMC's equity yield (37 bps FY26) sits below peers with analysts asking about it on every call; the intermediary — the distributor — controls the end investor's choice and is paid out of the same TER, exactly the Nobel Biocare structure where the channel captures the margin. Telescopic pricing and the coming passive/low-cost alternatives compound the squeeze, and the defence offered is trust and performance rather than a protected niche.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Investor preference is migrating toward passives, ETFs and commodity/international funds — analysts in Apr-2026 pointed to gold and silver ETFs outperforming and to NASDAQ-100/S&P-500 offerings — while CRAMC remains active-only and equity-concentrated (91:9). Management acknowledges it will 'diversify from the mutual fund into other spaces'. The offsetting shift, household savings moving into market-linked products, is a tailwind the company directly captures. (The long view had said: The consumer-preference trend runs in the company's favour — household savings are migrating into market-linked products, industry SIP inflows hit an all-time high of over INR32,000 cr in March 2026 and SIP AUM crossed INR50.1 lakh cr, with B-30 penetration rising and CRAMC's own B-30 share at ~24% of AUM. Weakness in SIP accounts was attributed to market volatility and the ELSS tax change, not to eroding appetite for the product.)

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
