# CG Power and Industrial Solutions Ltd. (CGPOWER) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: INDUSTRIAL MANUFACTURING*

CG Power and Industrial Solutions makes heavy electrical equipment in India — electric motors, drives, railway propulsion and signalling systems, power transformers, and switchgear such as circuit breakers, instrument transformers and lightning arrestors. It sells mainly to industrial customers, power utilities and grid operators (for example Power Grid Corporation and Techno Electric), Indian Railways, and increasingly to overseas buyers, including a US data-centre project that placed a roughly INR 900 crore (about $99 million) transformer export order in January 2026. Revenue comes from winning equipment orders, largely through tenders, and executing them over months or years, so the company reports an order intake and an unexecuted order backlog alongside sales; contracts in the power business typically carry price variation clauses that pass commodity cost changes on to customers, while industrial products rely on periodic price increases. It is based in India (standalone FY26 sales of INR 11,331 crores) and also runs subsidiaries in Sweden, Germany and the Netherlands plus semiconductor ventures, taking consolidated FY26 sales to INR 12,418 crores with PAT before exceptional items of INR 1,232 crores and a closing backlog of INR 17,107 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Power Systems** — Makes and services transformers (up to 765 kV) and switchgear/EHV products for utilities, grid projects and data centres; FY26 sales were INR 5,138 crores, up 46%, at a 21.9% PBIT margin, and it is the company's fastest-growing and most profitable segment with a backlog of INR 12,644 crores, up 91% year-over-year.
- **Transformers business** — The transformer half of Power Systems, which won the largest-ever domestic order of INR 641 crores for 765 kV transformer packages from Power Grid and the largest-ever single order overall, roughly INR 900 crores of power transformers exported to a US hyperscale data-centre project.
- **Switchgears & EPD / EHV business** — The other half of Power Systems, supplying medium-voltage and extra-high-voltage circuit breakers, instrument transformers and gas-insulated switchgear; management said it is running neck-and-neck with transformers on orders and the board approved a INR 748 crore greenfield expansion in October 2025 for domestic and export demand.
- **Industrial Systems** — Covers motors, railways and drives; FY26 sales were INR 6,197 crores, up only 6%, with PBIT margin slipping to 9.9% from 12.1% because of commodity inflation the company could not fully pass on and competitive pricing and mix changes in railways.
- **Motors business (India subcontinent)** — Sells low-tension and high-tension electric motors to industrial customers; a soft market forced a roughly 5% price increase from July 2025, and by Q4 FY26 management said motors had stabilised and were growing at double digits.
- **Railway business** — Supplies propulsion systems for locomotives and Vande Bharat trains (including an order from an RVNL joint venture) and KAVACH train collision avoidance systems; it grew fast enough to shift the Industrial mix, but its tender pricing and a restrictive price variation clause dragged segment margins down.
- **Drives & Automation (and international motors)** — Overseas operating subsidiaries in Sweden, Germany and the Netherlands whose weak sales in Q1 FY26 hurt consolidated margins through poor fixed-cost absorption, though management pointed to recovering bookings and healthy order growth by year-end.
- **CG Semi Private Limited** — A subsidiary building India's semiconductor packaging (OSAT) capacity at Sanand, Gujarat — the G1 plant opened in August 2025 at a peak half a million units a day and G2 is due by end-2026 at about 14.5 million chips a day — a INR 7,584 crore project with roughly INR 3,501 crores of central and INR 1,400 crores of state government support that is currently a cost, not a profit contributor.
- **Axiro Semiconductor Group** — A radio-frequency semiconductor components business bought from Renesas and affiliates, first consolidated in Q1 FY26; together with CG Semi it cut consolidated FY26 profit by INR 111 crores (89 basis points of margin).
- **G.G. Tronics** — A subsidiary in railway signalling that won a roughly INR 148 crore order for station KAVACH supply, installation and commissioning in North Western Railways, executable over two years.
- **Consumer Products business** — A separate business headed by its own EVP who joined the FY26 earnings calls, though the excerpts give no sales, margin or product detail for it.
- **CG Adhesive Products** — A consolidated operating subsidiary listed in the group results, with no size or activity detail given in the calls.

### The story in depth

CG Power comes across as a company that is genuinely good at the operating basics and is being handed unusually strong end markets. The quality framework gives full marks to the structural growth in what it sells into — grid and transmission spending under the government's National Electricity Plan to 2029, hyperscale data centres, railway signalling and domestic semiconductor packaging — and to the way management is spending money: transformer capacity going from roughly 17-20,000 MVA to 65,000 MVA in a year with 110,000 MVA planned, a ₹748 crore greenfield switchgear plant and the ₹7,584 crore chip-packaging project, all of it building new capacity rather than just maintaining old. It is also winning on something other than price. The $99.2 million US data-centre transformer order, the largest in the company's history, was won on reliability, efficiency and uptime, and came out of two years of patient key-account work rather than a lucky quarter. That patience is why the strongest pattern match in the whole screen is Corporate Culture, rated a strong fit purely because promises kept turning into delivery: the transformer capacity path tracked exactly as guided call after call, and the chip plant opened on schedule at 98-99% yields. Market share gain is the second-best fit, with motors growing 8-10% while the industry was flat to negative and power order intake growing 69% in FY26.

The problem is that the same three traits that produce those strengths also produce the three loudest risks, which is why the risk review lands at only moderately safe even though overall resilience reads as sound. First, the end markets that make growth structural are largely government-set: Power Grid is the single biggest domestic order at ₹641 crore, the transmission outlook is justified by a state electricity plan, and railways is a reverse-auction tender business where margins already fell — so 'structural end-market growth' and 'high government dependency' are the same fact seen from two sides. Second, the capacity build that earns top marks for growth capex is being added while every domestic peer adds capacity too, into customers whose own spending is cyclical, which is exactly what makes cyclicality an elevated risk and keeps the door open to the Chinese transformer competition analysts asked about twice on the January 2026 call. Third, the export win that is the company's best answer to government dependency is itself a single large customer, and that lands in the stakeholder-concentration risk alongside Power Grid, Indian Railways and Renesas — the same Renesas that sold CG the radio-frequency chip business and then contracts a chunk of its output. That semiconductor bet is the one place where the framework is openly negative: it is scored as a large, complex, out-of-core acquisition that has already pulled consolidated margins down by ₹111 crore, or 89 basis points, in FY26, and it is also the specific exposure the technology risk flags — the danger is not that transformers get disrupted, it is that CG's own big capital bet outside its core does not pay off.

Where the story thins out is durability of the advantage rather than quality of the execution, and that is what holds the overall score to 51 out of 100, or three stars. Business quality scores a respectable 67, but the multibagger screen scores only 23, because almost everything below the top two patterns comes back partial or absent: no evidence of brand pricing power or advertising leverage, no move to sell direct or own the shelf, no professional recommenders steering demand, and services still too small to count as genuine recurring revenue even though export and service bookings more than doubled in the fourth quarter. The toll-road test fails outright — price-variation clauses cover almost the whole power order book, which passes commodity costs through rather than proving pricing power, and railways runs on reverse auctions. Pricing power is real in power systems, where margins rose 281 basis points to 21.9% on better price realisation, and CG led a 5% low-tension motor price rise in July 2025 that competitors followed, but it only collected about half of that increase and the industrial business repeatedly could not pass commodity costs through, which is what keeps the 'good enough goods' risk alive. The framework's own area scores tell the same story: growth, competitive advantage, customer benefits and management all score at or above par, while industry structure and capital allocation are weak and return on capital scores zero. Add a 7% average sales growth rate that arrives lumpily, a company that is the opposite of under-covered with brokers on every call and a share sale oversubscribed more than three times, and this reads as a well-run business riding a real cycle at a price the market already knows about — not a hidden compounder.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** Promises made in earlier calls were delivered in later ones — the transformer capacity path (20,000 → 40,000 by September → 65,000 → greenfield 45,000) tracked exactly as guided, and the CG Semi G1 OSAT opened on schedule with 98-99% yields 'much better than what we anticipated'; management measures itself on ROCE (22% FY26 standalone) and free cash flow every quarter, and answers bad news directly (KAVACH approval delay, the lapsed CLW order, Industrial margin erosion) rather than deflecting, with a stable Kaul/Todi leadership team through FY26.

> "there was a delay in initial approval because we are also doing it for the first time" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Market Share Gainers — LIKELY FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern, but the numbers push back (0 of 1 check supporting) — held one step below a full confirmation.

**What the calls show:** Share gain is persistent and explicitly framed: motors grew while the LT market was flat-to-negative for 4-6 quarters ('either eating through the market share or putting across the value proposition'), Power Systems order intake grew 69% in FY26 with backlog +91% while capacity went 20,000→65,000 MVA, and international expansion delivered >50% export order growth in 9M FY26 plus the largest-ever INR900 crore US data-centre transformer order — and management insists margins were not bought ('we are not compromising on margins to get more orders').

> "We are actually going out in the market to make sure that we are capturing and increasing the pile of pipeline of orders, and that's where the win percentage is also increasing." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Sales compounding at 7% a year — below the 12% pace the share-gainer pattern looks for.

## What to watch

- Semiconductor drag: whether the ₹111 crore, 89 basis point margin hit from the Axiro/Renesas and chip-packaging bet narrows or widens — the single negative in the quality checks and the core of the technology risk.
- Export repeatability: whether the US data-centre order is followed by other named customers, since it is both the 'geographical expansion' strength and, as one buyer, part of the stakeholder-concentration risk.
- Capacity absorption: whether transformer utilisation holds as capacity goes from 65,000 towards 110,000 MVA while peers expand too — the point where 'capex for growth' turns into the cyclicality and new-entrant risks.
- Tender pricing: any sign of Chinese players re-entering PSU tenders or further reverse-auction pressure in railways, which would test the power systems pricing power and confirm the 'no fit' on toll roads.
- Motor price realisation: whether more than about half of announced low-tension motor price increases actually sticks — the direct test of the 'good enough goods' risk against the market-share-gainer pattern.
