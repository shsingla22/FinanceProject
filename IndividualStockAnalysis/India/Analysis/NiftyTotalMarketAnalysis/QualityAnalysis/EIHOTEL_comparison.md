**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.4 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing was rational across a year of repeated demand shocks — the industry raised rates rather than discounting into flat occupancy — and new luxury supply is slow, though the calls give no direct evidence on new entrants. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±29.6 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Very high land and development cost forms a real barrier and pricing behaviour is rational — rates were raised and waste eliminated rather than discounting — though management concedes supply is rising to meet demand.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹35 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±1.4 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹35 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±29.6 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Several destination properties are effectively local monopolies with no comparable competitor — management notes hotels like Vanyavilas and Maidens have no STR comp set at all. | The long view had said: Some assets are locationally irreplaceable (Amarvilas, Vanyavilas, Mayfair) but guests face essentially zero switching costs, and the Mumbai lounge lease loss to Adani showed how little contractual lock-in exists.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: The company is mid-cycle rather than near a trough — it reported its highest-ever EBITDA in FY26 after several strong years — so no identifiable cyclical upturn is being bought into. | The long view had said: The company is well past the cyclical bottom, reporting record RevPAR and its highest-ever EBITDA off a multi-year post-COVID upcycle, so the easy cyclical recovery is behind rather than ahead.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: A consistent multi-year ARR-premium strategy is restated across all four calls alongside a pipeline planned out to 2030, indicating long-horizon execution rather than quarterly management. | The long view had said: Family-controlled management sustains a decade-plus premium-positioning strategy and accepts near-term occupancy loss from floor-by-floor renovations at Trident Nariman Point and The Oberoi Mumbai to protect long-term leadership.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Consistent RevPAR outperformance versus the comp set (FY26 Oberoi 10.4% vs luxury 6.4%; Trident 10.2% vs 5%) with RGI rising to 191, though EIH already leads its segment so the runway to gain further share is limited. | The long view had said: Consistent RGI above 100 for years, 13 of 15 hotels ranked first or second versus their STR comp set, and FY26 RevPAR growth of 10.4% for Oberoi vs 6.4% for the luxury segment and 10.2% for Trident vs 5%.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. Now: FY26 capex of INR 680 Cr funded from operating cash flow of INR 993 Cr with a 30-hotel/2,450-key pipeline and 825 owned keys by 2030, though incremental ROIC is unproven as new hotels (Rajgarh, Vindhyavilas) are dragging RevPAR growth in ramp-up. | The long view had said: Large, sustained growth capex (Rs 680 cr in FY26 alone) into a 30-hotel/2,450-key pipeline with an explicit ≥15% IRR hurdle and disclosed 19% consolidated ROCE (45% on Oberoi assets at historic cost).
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: International portfolio delivered 13% RevPAR growth in Q4 and 11% in Q3 across Mauritius, Egypt and Marrakesh, with a new Cairo management contract added, showing the brand travels — but overseas is still only 408 of 4,209 keys. | The long view had said: Credible, capital-disciplined international build-out — a 21-key Mayfair London luxury hotel structured 50/50 debt-equity with a target 49% partner, plus Cairo, Nile cruisers, Mauritius and Al Zorah, with international RevPAR up 11-13%.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: Direct-booking share, the proxy for customer intimacy, has stopped improving and the segment mix is unchanged year over year. | The long view had said: A strong direct-booking channel built over years plus deep trade relationships with overseas tour operators and DMCs, though management notes the direct mix has now plateaued.

Now: Management discloses weak cities by name, reports like-to-like ex-Oberoi Grand numbers, quantifies both one-time hits (Mashobra INR 109 Cr, wage code INR 30 Cr), and admits the year was hard despite record EBITDA — though the narrative leans heavily on external causes for every miss. | The long view had said: Management openly admits what it does not know (the January demand shortfall), names its own weak asset in Bangalore, quantifies one-time hits (Rs 30 cr wage code, Rs 109 cr Mashobra) and apologises for rescheduling a call.

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No single customer, licensor or supplier is named as driving results across the four calls; revenue is spread across leisure, corporate, MICE and direct segments, 15+ benchmarked hotels in multiple Indian cities plus international properties, and management describes segment mix as broadly unchanged year on year. (The long view had said: Discrete single-counterparty relationships have materially moved results: the Adani-controlled Mumbai airport lounge lease lapsed and was repeatedly named as the reason FY26 revenue/margin growth trailed peers, and the fast-growing Oberoi Flight Services business leans on a small set of airlines with IndiGo singled out ('we provide substantial catering to them already'), while being margin-dilutive.)
- **New Entrants**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Management never discusses a structurally different competitor or format defensively, and no discounting is framed as a competitive response; instead they cite sustained rate-led leadership over the STR competition set with 13 of 15 hotels ranked first or second. The one caution is that FY26 saw occupancy gain but ARI slip versus the comp set, which is worth watching. (The long view had said: Investors have pressed management since FY23 on rivals adding capacity faster ('we are among the top two players... now others have gone up in capacity'), and industry supply growth is acknowledged; in Bangalore peers grew double-digit versus EIH's high single-digit, with management conceding the older product must be renovated 'in order to maintain our competitiveness'.)

**That worsened:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The entire strategy is an explicit defence of price premium — management says the objective is to keep pushing ARR because of its flow-through — while FY26 shows ARI slipping versus the competition set and the cheaper Trident brand outgrowing Oberoi (Trident +12.5% vs Oberoi +5.4% in Q3; +10.2% vs +4.2% in Q4), a signal that value-for-money alternatives are taking rate share. (The long view had said: The calls affirmatively show the price premium holding rather than being competed away: renovated rooms command higher rates with 'guests have been completely accepting the rate increases', ARR for owned hotels rose from ₹24,548 to ₹28,198 in a single Q4, and management describes international leisure demand as price-inelastic — though the same commentary concedes domestic customers are more price-sensitive and some domestic volume was lost to rate increases.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Technological Innovation (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
