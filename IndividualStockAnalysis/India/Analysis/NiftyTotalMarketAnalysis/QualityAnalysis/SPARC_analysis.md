# Sun Pharma Adv. Res (SPARC) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

SPARC (Sun Pharma Advanced Research Company Ltd.) is an India-based drug research and development company, listed on the NSE and BSE and majority-backed by the Sun Pharma promoter group, that invents new medicines rather than running a large sales operation of its own. It does not market drugs to patients directly; instead it takes molecules from lab discovery through clinical trials and regulatory filings (mainly with the US FDA, plus India and Australia), and then licenses or partners them out to other pharma companies — for example Elepsia is commercialised by US CNS specialty firm Tripoint Therapeutics, Xelpros sits with Sun Pharma in the US, and PDP-716 was licensed to Visiox Pharma (since bought by Ocuvex Therapeutics). Money comes in from upfront and licensing payments, platform licensing deals, service revenue and royalties/economics on these out-licensed products — management says roughly $277 million of the ~$500 million spent over 15 years was funded from such internal accruals, with the rest from rights and preferential share issues. Its R&D and management base is in India while its trials, regulatory filings and partners are global, and after two failed Phase 2 readouts (vodobatinib in Parkinson's and vibozilimod) it has narrowed its focus to cancer and autoimmune disease.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Commercialised / near-market 505(b)(2) products** — Improved versions of existing drugs — Elepsia (high-dose levetiracetam for epilepsy), Xelpros (preservative-free latanoprost for glaucoma), a benzyl-alcohol-free phenobarbital for neonatal seizures, PDP-716 (once-a-day brimonidine eye drop) and SDN-037 (an ophthalmic steroid) — which management calls the validated, lower-risk part of the model and describes as the main source of the non-dilutive cash that funded everything else.
- **SBO-154 — MUC1 ADC (Oncology)** — An antibody-drug conjugate that carries the toxin MMAE to MUC1-expressing solid tumours; it is one of the two anchor assets management has prioritised, with INDs approved in the US, Australia and India and a Phase 1 dose-escalation study underway as of January 2026.
- **SCD-153 — topical itaconate analogue (Immunology)** — A skin-applied treatment for alopecia areata (autoimmune hair loss) developed with Johns Hopkins University; it is the other prioritised anchor asset, having finished healthy-volunteer Phase 1 and started a Phase 1B patient study in India, with early efficacy data expected in late 2026.
- **Neurodegeneration / vodobatinib (largely parked)** — Formerly the company's flagship — a c-Abl inhibitor tested in a 500-plus-patient Phase 2 Parkinson's trial called PROSEEK that failed in 2024, after which management parked nearly all its neurodegeneration programs pending a review of the risks.
- **Early-stage / preclinical and platform programs** — Discovery-stage work including a biologics platform, small-molecule drug conjugates and new synthetic-lethality cancer targets; management describes this as where it now takes early-stage risk, and says almost 60% of the portfolio has first-in-class potential.
- **Partnering, NewCos and short-term cash options** — A deliberate shift to out-license assets earlier and spin work into separate companies — SCO-155, a PSMA-targeted drug conjugate for prostate cancer, was signed over to Tiller Therapeutics (formed by UCSF collaborators) in exchange for significant equity — alongside one-off cash items such as a pediatric rare disease priority review voucher that a US court ruled in SPARC's favour on and that management says could fetch north of $100 million.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Government Dependency — WATCH
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.
