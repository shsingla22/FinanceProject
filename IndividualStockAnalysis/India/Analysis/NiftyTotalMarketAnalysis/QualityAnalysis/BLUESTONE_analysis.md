# Bluestone Jewellery (BLUESTONE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

BlueStone Jewellery and Lifestyle Limited is an Indian jewellery brand that designs, manufactures and sells its own gold and diamond (studded) jewellery — rings, earrings, pendants and similar pieces — directly to retail consumers rather than through a marketplace or other people's designs. It started in 2011 as an online-only, direct-to-consumer brand on bluestone.com and now runs an 'omnichannel' model where customers discover and research online (management says more than 80% of sales originate online) and usually complete the purchase in a physical store; it operated 340 stores across 134 cities at March 2026 and 352 stores by the June 2026 quarter. It makes money on the retail sale price of each piece, with margins coming from design-led, higher-value studded jewellery and from in-house ('vertically integrated') manufacturing, rather than from low-margin commodity items like coins and chains, which it deliberately does not push. It operates only in India, spanning metros and smaller Tier-2 cities, and reported standalone revenue of Rs. 2,441 crore for FY26 and Rs. 733 crore in Q1 FY27 (up 49% year-on-year), with its first reported quarterly net profit (Rs. 71.5 crore) in Q3 FY26.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Online / D2C channel (bluestone.com)** — The original business — the company's own website and app where customers browse its own designs (shown via 3D renders rather than photographs); management says over 80% of all sales now originate online even when the final purchase happens in a store.
- **Physical retail store network (own + franchisee stores)** — Company-operated and franchisee stores where customers see and try the pieces and complete the purchase — 323 stores in 130 cities at December 2025, 340 in 134 cities at March 2026 and 352 by the Q1 FY27 call, with 65 stores added during FY26; all sales including franchisee stores are booked at retail (consumer) value.
- **Omnichannel model** — Management's term for treating online and stores as one continuous customer journey rather than separate channels; they call it the key driver of performance and say online-built intent makes their stores convert better than a purely physical retailer's.
- **In-house design and manufacturing (vertical integration)** — The company brought jewellery manufacturing in-house by 2013-14 to enable just-in-time production (cut turnaround from 3-4 weeks to 3-5 days) and still uses it to control design, costs and margins — it credits this for quickly re-creating affordable entry-level price points after gold prices spiked.
- **Studded (diamond) versus plain gold jewellery** — Studded pieces are the higher-margin core of the product mix — 61% of revenue in Q3 FY26 — and the company deliberately avoids commodity, investment-led items such as gold coins and chains, which it describes as price-led and lower margin.
- **In-house technology stack** — The company says it is the only jewellery company to have built its entire technology stack internally and asks investors to view it as a consumer internet company that chose jewellery as its category; it uses internet-style metrics such as cohorts, repeat share and customer lifetime to run the business.
- **Repeat customer business** — Sales to existing customers, which management highlights as a growing and cost-efficient revenue source — 57.8% of revenue in Q3 FY26 rising to about 60% by Q1 FY27 — and which helps keep advertising spend low (5.7% of sales in Q3 FY26).
- **Old gold exchange programme** — A scheme letting customers trade in old gold against new purchases, which management describes as the most competitive such offer in the market and a support to demand; no separate size is given.
- **Men's and kids' jewellery store format** — A new, small initiative described as an industry first — an exclusive store selling only men's and kids' jewellery — intended to widen the range beyond the traditional women's focus; presented as an early experiment rather than a material revenue line.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

BlueStone's whole story runs through one decision: it makes its own jewellery and sells it straight to the buyer. That is why the multibagger screen gives Forward Integrators its only STRONG FIT — manufacturing was brought in-house by 2013-14, and the company has pushed all the way to the customer through its own website plus a store count that climbed 275→292→323→340→352 across six calls, with franchisees used deliberately as a way to fund expansion. The same choice is what earns the highest marks in the quality framework: over 80% of sales start online across 352 stores in 134 cities with the store till being the website itself, giving direct, name-by-name knowledge of who is buying (Convenience benefits, +2), and ₹43 kept out of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs (Intangible benefits, +2). But integration cuts both ways. Owning the metal, the making and the shop floor is precisely why about 659 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers (Cost of working capital, −2), why closing inventory is ₹2,652 crore with stock turning just 1.13 times company-wide, and why the business generated minus ₹6 of operating cash for every ₹100 of capital employed this year (Return on capital, −2). The strength and the weakness are not two separate findings; they are the same business model seen from the front and the back.

The second thread is design. Management's own research says 86% of buyers chose the product because of how it looks, not what it costs, and that is the engine behind repeat business rising from 50.7% to 57.8% to roughly 60% of sales and a customer base near one million. It is also why the company is taking share from incumbents who are stepping back from everyday, design-led pieces to concentrate on low-margin commodity gold — the framework calls that a textbook 'share donator' setup, and the numbers support it with 49% revenue growth and 39% same-store sales growth. Yet the screen stops short of calling this a moat. Brand Strength, Innovation Dominance, Market Share Gainers, Corporate Culture and Cost to Replicate all come back PARTIAL rather than full fits, and Pricing Power is a flat NO FIT. The gold-price spike is the evidence for all of it at once: entry price points were 'vacated', the company re-engineered designs and lowered karatage to defend the price rather than raise it, growth slowed in November and December, and new customer additions fell from around 50,000 to about 40,000 a quarter. That single episode is simultaneously the Cyclicality risk, the Good Enough Goods risk (a design premium sitting on top of a metal the customer values by weight) and the Fashion Risk of a franchise built on taste — three separate risk channels that all trace back to the same design-over-price positioning that scores so well on the quality side.

What holds the rating up is honesty and end-market direction; what holds it down is cash. Management strips out inventory gains voluntarily every quarter, explains retail versus primary sales, and owned the merchandising failure plainly — 'we struggled', 'that merchandise dislocation was real' — rather than blaming conditions, which is why Candor scores +2 and Management comes in at 0.83. That candour is also the reason the cyclicality damage is visible at all. The end market itself is genuinely expanding, not just recovering: branded, design-led, everyday jewellery for younger Indian buyers, plus the drift from unorganised to organised retail. Set against that, three of the eight risk channels are WATCH for reasons outside the company's control — the gold cycle, a customs duty that went from 6% to 15% and softened demand through May before normalising in June, and new entrants now contesting the digital-first segment BlueStone effectively created, with analysts benchmarking it against CaratLane and management noting 'a lot of companies getting VC funding' in lab-grown. Three risks are LOW, including lab-grown disruption (under 1% of revenue, no observed shift in three to four years) and any single-supplier or single-customer dependence, so overall resilience reads SOUND and the risk score is the strongest of the three inputs at 80. The company is also the opposite of overlooked — a high-profile venture-backed listing with Axis, Kotak, IIFL, Emkay and HDFC Life all on one call, scoring −2 on obscurity. The arithmetic lands where the tension suggests: 57 for business quality, only 15 for multibagger pattern fit, 80 for risk safety, blending to 50 out of 100 and a 'Decent' three-star grade. Fast growth and real customer affection, funded by a balance sheet that is not yet producing cash.

## What to watch

- Cash return on capital (Return on capital check, −2): does the −6% operating cash return on capital turn positive as stores mature, or does growth keep consuming more cash than it makes?
