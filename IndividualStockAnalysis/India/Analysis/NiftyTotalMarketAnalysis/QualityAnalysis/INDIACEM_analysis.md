# India Cements Ltd. (INDIACEM) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CEMENT & CEMENT PRODUCTS*

India Cements Limited is an Indian cement maker, run out of Chennai, that manufactures and sells bagged grey cement to builders, dealers and government/institutional buyers, mostly in southern India (its Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh belt, with a smaller share sold outside the south). It makes money by burning coal and petcoke to produce clinker at a collection of plants of different vintages — some built by the company, some acquired, such as the Raasi group, Sankarnagar/Sankari, Chilamakuru and Dalavoi — grinding that clinker with fly ash into cement, and selling it as close to the factory as possible so freight stays low; profitability swings mainly on cement prices, fuel cost per kilocalorie and how much of the plants' capacity it can actually run. Roughly 57% of volumes went through the 'trade' (dealer/retail) channel in Q4 FY24, the rest non-trade (bulk/institutional). By 2026 the company had become a subsidiary of UltraTech Cement: it now manufactures and sells cement under the UltraTech brand under a tolling-style arrangement, with marketing and distribution costs sitting at the parent and UltraTech charging it a markup per bag, and it reported EBITDA of ₹497 per tonne and a quarterly profit of ₹60 crore in Q4 FY26.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Cement** — The core and overwhelmingly dominant business — making clinker and grinding it into grey cement across several southern plants and selling it through trade and non-trade channels; everything management discusses on the calls (fuel cost per kilocalorie, prices, capacity utilisation, lead distance, efficiency capex) is about this business.
- **Ready Mix Concrete (RMC) division** — A small side business supplying ready-mixed concrete, doing about ₹27 crore of revenue and ₹2.2 crore of EBITDA in the March 2024 quarter — a rounding error next to cement.
- **Shipping** — A small shipping operation that management reports separately as a 'housekeeping' number, with about ₹7.4 crore turnover and ₹1.7 crore EBITDA in the March 2024 quarter.
- **Wind power (windmills)** — A tiny captive/wind-generation arm described by management as 'very marginal' — about ₹0.5 crore of revenue and negative ₹1 crore EBITDA in the March 2024 quarter, i.e. it loses money.
- **Non-core asset and land monetisation** — Not a trading business but a stated source of cash used to cut debt — selling surplus land parcels when the price and buyer are right, and disposing of units such as the Parli grinding unit, sold for ₹315 crore, with management saying it will sell only to the extent necessary.
- **Efficiency / modernisation capex programme** — An ongoing spending plan of roughly ₹700 crore over two years (about ₹500 crore on efficiency projects such as waste heat recovery at Chilamakuru, a vertical roller mill at Sankari, cooler upgrades and a crusher at Sankarnagar, plus ~₹100 crore a year of routine capex) aimed at lowering power and grinding costs and lifting output — continued after the UltraTech acquisition as an 'investment phase'.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

India Cements scores 31 out of 100 and rates Weak (one star), and the split behind that number tells the story: the business-quality work found real operating strengths, the multibagger screen found almost nothing durable, and the risk review rates overall resilience as STRAINED. The strengths are genuine on their own terms — customers effectively fund the business, with cash coming back 28 days before suppliers need paying; ₹78 of every ₹100 of sales survives direct input costs; distribution reach spans 76 operating facilities, an expanding bulk-terminal network and a 360 km lead distance. But the pattern screen returned nine outright NO FITs and two PARTIALs out of eleven, scoring zero, and the area scores show why: Competitive Advantage and Capital Allocation both sit at zero, and Return on Capital at -1.0, with the headline check recording roughly zero cash return on the capital employed this year.

The tension runs through almost every item, because the same facts drive a strength on one page and a risk on another. The rebranding is the clearest case: retiring India Cements' own B/C-category labels entirely by March 2026 in favour of UltraTech is treated as the winning side of the Shifting Consumer Preferences risk (rated WATCH) and is the source of the pricing the Intangible Benefits check rewards — yet it is exactly why Brand Strength is a NO FIT, why the advertisement check scores -1 with no marketing spend left at the entity, and why the acquirer, not this company, is credited with the premium. Distribution reads the same way: a standout, hard-to-replicate strength that now runs through the parent's terminal network under a related-party tolling arrangement, which is precisely what makes Stakeholder Concentration ELEVATED. The 19% volume growth in Q1 FY27 earns a positive market-share check, but the screen calls it UltraTech-branded volume on an acquired asset rather than self-generated share, so Market Share Gainers is still a NO FIT.

The history explains why the company ended up here rather than gaining from it. It was structurally high-cost — a patchwork of acquired plants of different vintages, ball mills instead of vertical roller mills, no waste heat recovery, running at about 61% utilisation — so Low Price Plus fails, and past acquisitions score -1 after Parli had to be sold for ₹315cr because it was not producing much EBITDA. That cost position met a cycle it could not pass on: coal moving from $90 to $230 a tonne cut standalone quarterly EBITDA from ₹165cr to ₹39cr, which is why Cyclicality is the single HIGH RISK, why Pricing Power and Toll Roads are NO FITs, and why Good Enough Goods stays ELEVATED. Working capital starvation then cost volume outright — Q4 FY24 volumes fell about 12% while the southern market grew 9-10% — which sits awkwardly beside the negative-working-capital strength, since the screen notes working capital appears in the record as a constraint rather than as customer funding. There was no dividend or buyback across the period, and a ₹60cr quarterly profit in Q4 FY26 was described as arriving after a very long time. Post-acquisition capex of ₹1,592cr on efficiency plus ₹400cr on capacity is the first genuinely forward-looking spend, but on the record so far the durable assets belong to the parent, and Cost to Replicate concludes the company's own advantage was so absent that it was acquired.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Stakeholder Concentration, New Entrants, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.1× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 2 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across the whole 2022-2026 arc results swing on input-commodity and construction cycles: coal going from $90 to $230/t crushed standalone EBITDA from Rs.165cr to Rs.39cr in Q1FY23 with no ability to pass it on, fuel cost per kcal moved 1.6 -> 2.7 -> 1.95, and by FY26-27 the West Asia crisis again spiked fuel, bag and freight costs; volumes swing with elections, monsoon and southern capacity utilisation stuck at 60-70%.

> "Coal that was $90 a ton went up to $230 a ton... the EBITDA came down to Rs. 39 Crores which is down from Rs. 165 Crores last year" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Cement is an operations/consumption-linked building material with structural housing (55-60% of demand) and infra pull, and the parent is investing counter-cyclically (WHRS, ~86% green power by FY28, Rs.2,000cr efficiency capex) to blunt fuel swings*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 5 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from -8% to 18% (swinging ±8.4 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 4% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 5%).

### Stakeholder Concentration — ELEVATED
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but the only computed fingerprint does not confirm it — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** The trajectory ends in near-total single-counterparty dependence: India Cements is now an UltraTech subsidiary operating under a related-party tolling arrangement where it manufactures and sells only the UltraTech brand, carries no marketing or distribution, and its reported margin is set by a markup per bag charged by the parent; 100% of its brand identity was extinguished by March 2026 and even its reporting basis is changed by the parent. Earlier calls also showed dependence on imported fuel (65% imported coal) from a handful of origins.

> "India Cements manufactures and sells the UltraTech brand, but does not carry any direct marketing and distribution costs... we charge a markup per bag on ICL volumes" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Customer base itself is broad and dealer-led (57% trade sales), and the controlling stakeholder is a financially strong parent funding Rs.2,000cr of capex rather than an extractive licensor*
