### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 4 weakened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Both FY26 calls describe the entire model as multi-year subscription contracts with per-member/per-transaction billing, ARR up 21% over March 2025, FY26 NRR of 110% (114-115% organic) and add-on products (Engage, Insights, Rewards, aiRA) lifting attach rates; 9M operating cash flow of INR101cr vs INR71cr adjusted EBITDA (142%) points to advance/collected-ahead billing. (The long view had said: Pure subscription model on 3/5/7-year contracts with ARR of INR765 crores, NRR of 110% (114-115% organic), and billing in advance that funds the business — FY26 operating cash flow of INR150 crores against INR107 crores adjusted EBITDA, FCF/PAT ~200%. Expansion comes from usage overages, contractual escalators and add-on modules on an installed base of 115 enterprises/400 brands.)

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Contracts carry annual inflationary escalators plus usage overages that feed 114-115% organic NRR, and aiRA is priced on outcomes/actions; but pricing power is contractual/usage-led rather than demonstrated headline price rises, and the acquired base is being discounted (inorganic NRR 94%). (The long view had said: Contracts carry automatic escalators (3-4% in the West, 7-8% in high-inflation geographies) plus per-member/per-transaction metric expansion, and management says roughly a third of growth comes from these 'things that don't need any effort'; gross margins rose ~1% in FY26 to 69-70% while volume kept growing 23%, and aiRA is being monetised on outcome-based pricing after free pilots.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Consistent consolidation and logo wins — 12 new customers in 9M FY26, new order book INR66cr vs INR53cr, three to four Fortune 500 signings in FY26 including a $20mn Fortune 50 deal, plus four acquisitions of competing platforms (Brierley, Kognitiv, SessionM from Mastercard) taking ARR toward $115mn — but no explicit market-share percentages versus named competitors. (The long view had said: Persistent share gain across two calls: 12 new customers in nine months, new ACV of INR121 crores, three to four Fortune 500 logos in FY26 including a $20m four-to-five-year Fortune 50 retailer, plus a deliberate roll-up of a fragmented industry (five acquisitions, SessionM from Mastercard at 0.5x revenue) — and the share is bought profitably, with margins rising from -3% to 14.6% EBITDA over four years rather than being sacrificed.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management articulates concrete replication barriers — an auditable system of record with five-nines uptime, ~9 integrations per client, 1.9bn consumer profiles, 10bn+ transactions and a trillion dollars of commerce a year feeding the analytics agent — yet the fact that competing platforms are bought for 0.5-1.5x revenue suggests the category itself is not hard to enter, only hard to displace once installed. (The long view had said: A concrete replication barrier is articulated: the ledger is the audited system of record with ~9 integrations per client, 1.9 billion consumer profiles and 10 billion transactions a year of proprietary data feeding the analytics agent, and staying current has required surviving five technology waves at $10-20m of annual platform investment — which is exactly why sub-$50m rivals decay and become acquisition targets at 0.5-1.5x revenue.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The economics are the opposite of low-price-plus: 69-70% platform gross margins, premium positioning as the top-rated global vendor, and discounts appear only as a one-off concession when migrating acquired customers, not as a strategy. (The long view had said: Real cost discipline exists — headcount up 1% against 23% revenue growth, non-COGS up 14% vs revenue 23%, sales spend at 17% of top line called 'best-in-class' — but none of it is passed to customers as low price; the company instead takes annual inflationary price rises at 69-70% gross margin, so the low-price half of the pattern is absent.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (STRONG FIT); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
