# JK Paper (JKPAPER) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

JK Paper is an Indian paper manufacturer that makes and sells writing and printing paper (maplitho), coated paper, office/copier cut-size paper, and virgin-fibre packaging board, plus corrugated boxes through recently acquired packaging companies. Its paper goes to printers, offices, schools and courts (helped by the new education policy and offices returning to full working), while its packaging board and corrugated boxes go to FMCG, pharma and food companies that are also shifting away from plastic. It earns money by converting wood — grown through its own large plantation programme — into pulp in-house and then into paper and board, selling mainly in India (exports were around 8-10% of volume, cut back when domestic demand is strong), so profits swing with paper selling prices (NSR) versus wood, pulp, chemical and energy costs. It runs plants in Gujarat, Odisha and Telangana, reported consolidated revenue of about ₹6,772 crore in FY23 with a ₹1,208 crore profit, and describes itself as wood- and carbon-positive.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Paper and packaging board (JK Paper standalone)** — The core business making writing/printing paper, coated paper, copier paper and packaging board at the Gujarat, Odisha and Telangana plants — management says roughly ₹6,000 crore-plus of the group's ~₹7,000 crore expected revenue comes from paper, including a new Gujarat board machine and pulp mill running at about 90-100% of capacity.
- **Sirpur (Sirpur Paper Mills subsidiary)** — A separately consolidated, purely paper company acquired earlier and running at about 90% of its capacity, which delivered over ₹300 crore of profit in FY23 and EBITDA margins of roughly 22-23% in FY24 excluding a state incentive.
- **Corrugation / corrugated packaging business** — The newest arm, built by acquiring Horizon Pack and Securipax (together described as India's largest corrugated packaging player), later Manipal Utility Packaging, and by commissioning its own Ludhiana plant in August 2023 — expected to contribute roughly ₹1,000-1,200 crore of revenue at 10-16% margins, with about ₹800 crore spent on the three acquisitions mainly to buy established customer relationships.
- **Plantation / farm forestry** — The wood-sourcing activity feeding the integrated pulp mills — 8.32 crore saplings planted over 55,700 acres in FY23 and about 110 crore saplings across 6 lakh acres to date — which matters because wood is the basic raw material and its cost jumped 30-35% in FY24.

### The story in depth

JK Paper's story starts with the same fact from three directions: this is a well-run company operating in a business it does not control the price of. The management checks are the strongest part of the record — Candor scores a 2, with the company pre-warning that FY23 margins would not repeat, disclosing the Ludhiana teething problems, admitting corrugation utilisation of only 55-60%, and stripping non-cash Sirpur incentives out of its own numbers. That same behaviour is what makes Corporate Culture the one STRONG FIT among the eleven multibagger patterns, alongside the Aug 2022 pledge of no major borrowing that was followed through into net debt of roughly Rs. 1,900 crore and a near-debt-free position. The Management area scores 1.17, the highest of the seven quality areas. But candour here mostly means honestly reporting a cycle, not steering it: FY23's 33.1% EBITDA margin and profit jump from Rs. 544 crore to Rs. 1,208 crore is described by management itself as an outlier year, and FY24 fell back to 26-27% with coated paper prices down around 20%. Cyclicality is rated HIGH RISK for exactly the reason Candor scores a 2 — the swings are real and management says so plainly.

The second thread is that JK's genuine advantage is a cost and integration advantage built over decades, and it shows up as a partial fit almost everywhere rather than a decisive one. The farm-forestry base — roughly 6 lakh acres, 110 crore saplings, 55,700 acres planted in a single year with a deliberate 35-40% oversupply — plus full captive chemical pulp and the BCTMP mechanical pulp mill targeting $100-150 per ton of savings, is what earns Independent, long-term and tenacious a 2 and what makes Cost to Replicate and Low Price Plus PARTIAL fits. It lets JK sell copier paper at Rs. 76-80 per kg against landed imports at Rs. 85-90. The capital behind it has worked: the roughly Rs. 2,000 crore Gujarat board line and pulp mill ramped to 90-100% within a year, Sirpur was turned around to about 90% utilisation, and the next capex is underwritten at around 15% return. Yet the same integration that keeps costs low does not create a moat the framework recognises. Competitive Advantage scores -0.67. Network effects score -2 because paper has no self-reinforcing scale effect. Pricing Power and Toll Roads are outright NO FITs, and Pricing, Mix and Volume scores -1: a 20% realisation gain in FY23 reversed into 10-20% price falls in FY24. Cost leadership defends the margin floor; it does not set the price.

The third thread is where the pressure comes from, and it points at the one place the company has no answer. Structural end-market growth scores 1 on genuine drivers — plastic substitution, packaged food, pharma, FMCG, the education policy — and JK has pushed capital toward them through the Rs. 2,000 crore board investment and the corrugation acquisitions (Horizon Pack, Securipax at Rs. 590 crore for 85% plus Rs. 104 crore for the balance, and Manipal), which is why Forward Integrators and Market Share Gainers are PARTIAL fits and why Shifting Consumer Preferences and Fashion Risk are both LOW risks. Against that, imports are the recurring threat across the whole record: New Entrants is ELEVATED because management moved from saying there was no dumping in Aug 2022 to opening the May 2024 call with ASEAN dumping as the defining challenge, conceding that Indonesian producers' forest-concession cost advantage will always remain. Good Enough Goods is ELEVATED for the same reason — the premium is defended mainly on parity with landed import prices. Government Dependency is a WATCH not because JK sells to the government but because the ASEAN FTA lets Indonesian paper in duty-free and the antidumping petitions against Indonesia, China and Chile are pending, with a FTA review management admits may never come quickly. Cyclical market growth scores -1 because the identifiable move as of May 2024 is downward, with wood costs up 30-35%. The combined rating of 50 out of 100 — Decent, three stars — reflects this balance: business quality 53, multibagger fit 40, risk safety 56, with resilience judged SOUND.

The honest read is that the strengths and the weaknesses are the same trait seen twice. The long-horizon plantation and pulp integration that make this company hard to replicate also tie it to a commodity whose price is set elsewhere. Candid management is worth a great deal when the cycle turns down, but candour does not change the cycle. The parts of the business that could break that link — packaging board's secular growth, and corrugation moving JK closer to the end customer — are real and funded but not yet proven at scale, with corrugation running at 55-60% utilisation and coated paper still 50-60% imported, leaving a substitution runway. Geographical expansion scores -1 because exports are being deliberately cut from 15% to 5-8% and are a residual outlet rather than a franchise, so the domestic market is the whole story. Growth and Return on Capital both score 0.0, Capital Allocation 0.2 — solid execution that has not yet compounded into anything exceptional. This is a good operator in a hard industry, priced by the framework accordingly.

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** Consistent, long-tenured management (Mehta/Veerappan on all three calls) that owns bad news early and delivers what it promised: the Aug 2022 pledge of no major borrowing and accelerated repayment produced net debt down to ~Rs. 1,900 cr and near-debt-free by FY25; management flagged in May 2023 that FY23 margins would not repeat and they duly fell to 26-27%; it publicly cites the 2013-15 losses as a lesson against over-levered capex, refuses to give guidance it cannot stand behind, and measures every project against a WACC-plus-4-5% return hurdle.

> "just after that, for two years, we were in redd. We incurred loss, heavy losses in the year '13-'14 and '14-'15... And that we don't want to repeat." — *management, on an earnings call*

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 1 check supporting).

**What the calls show:** There is a genuine integrated-cost story — 100% captive chemical pulp, 6 lakh acres of plantation, and a BCTMP mill targeted to save $100-150/ton — which lets JK price below landed import cost (Rs. 76-80/kg vs Rs. 85-90/kg copier), but there is no itemised cost-culture disclosure and management concedes Indonesian rivals with forest concessions are structurally cheaper.

> "we always calculate that it should give us a saving of $100 to $150 per ton of pulp" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹38 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 0.7× a year — not the high-turnover, low-margin machine this pattern needs.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, New Entrants, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.5× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.

- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 9% to 12% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across the three calls the business swings with global pulp, wood and paper prices: FY23 EBITDA margin of 33.1% (PAT up from INR544cr to INR1,208cr) is repeatedly called an 'outlier year', then FY24 fell to 26-27% with coated paper prices down ~20%, board down 10-12% and wood cost up 30-35%. Management itself frames demand and margin in commodity-price terms (pulp $900 to $500 to $750) and declines to guide, admitting any quarter is 'unique'.

> "'22-23 was the outlier year, and this was bound to come down" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Demand is operations-linked (office/education paper, FMCG, pharma, food packaging) rather than customer capex; the company also invested countercyclically (INR2,000cr board line, three corrugation acquisitions) and deleveraged into the downturn*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 2 of the last 11 years with swings of ±22 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 12% and 31% (±6.3 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 43% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 4% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

## What to watch

- Whether the antidumping petitions against Indonesia, China and Chile succeed and the FTA review actually happens — the Government Dependency WATCH and the ELEVATED New Entrants risk both hinge on this, and management concedes the Indonesian cost advantage is otherwise permanent.
- Corrugation utilisation moving up from the admitted 55-60% — this is the test of whether Forward Integrators becomes more than a PARTIAL fit and whether the Securipax and Horizon Pack money bought closer customer relationships or just capacity.
- Whether the BCTMP mechanical pulp mill delivers the stated $100-150/ton saving at the ~15% return hurdle — the Low Price Plus and Cost to Replicate patterns rest on cost integration widening, not just holding.
- Direction of wood costs after the 30-35% rise and of board and coated paper realisations after the 10-20% FY24 falls — the Cyclicality HIGH RISK and the -1 on Pricing, Mix and Volume are the same issue, and a second down year would test the 'outlier' framing of FY23.
- Whether the coated paper import share falls from 50-60% toward domestic supply — this is the most concrete route for the Gain in Market Share check and the Market Share Gainers pattern to convert capacity into durable share rather than cyclical volume.
