Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Capital and concession intensity keeps new entrants out, and pricing is rational — a price increase was taken while domestic port EBITDA margin reached a record 74.2%. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±5.4 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Capital intensity and concession-based access make entry very hard and pricing has been rational, with realisation per ton rising and port EBITDA margins holding around 70%+.
- **Network effects** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Good. Now: There is a genuine self-reinforcing loop — asset-light freight forwarding and trucking feed volume into the asset-heavy ports and ICDs, and backhaul fills return legs — but it is an integration flywheel rather than a true two-sided network. | The long view had said: There is cross-selling between logistics and ports but no self-reinforcing two-sided network; volumes still depend on trade flows and shipping-line decisions.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: Segment-wise ROCE disclosure does allow skill to be partly separated from position, but results still lean on concession-protected geography and captive group ecosystem volumes (Mundra power plants), amid heavy self-congratulation. | The long view had said: Calls are saturated with congratulation and management attributes outperformance to culture rather than to concessions, land bank and scale advantages, so results and skill are hard to separate.
- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: The product has essentially no direct input costs — nearly all of every ₹100 of sales is available for people, marketing and profit (typical of software / internet platforms). And from the calls: Very high and rising margins (74.2% domestic port EBITDA) with a realised price increase point to real pricing power, though it derives from location and capacity rather than brand image or taste. | The long view had said: The product has essentially no direct input costs — nearly all of every ₹100 of sales is available for people, marketing and profit (typical of software / internet platforms). And from the calls: Margins are high and demand steady, but pricing power comes from location, capacity and efficiency rather than any image or taste-based intangible.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Good → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹59 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹59 is operating profit. And from the calls: The core business remains capital-heavy (₹75,000 cr capex plan, 16% consolidated ROCE) even though management is deliberately shifting the logistics mix to asset-light/asset-zero services, which lifted logistics ROCE from 6% to 10%. | The long view had said: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹0.21 of sales — a fairly asset-heavy business. ### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 6. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 4 strengthened, 4 weakened, 2 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Share gain is quantified and rising in every call — all-India market share 27% (FY25) → 28.1% (Q2 FY26), containers 44.4%→45.9%, coking coal 36.5%→41.9%, coastal coal 27.8%→31.1% — and it is won alongside rising ROCE (domestic ports 21%→23-24%) rather than bought with margin. (The long view had said: Explicit, rising share numbers across all four years: container share +2.5% and coal share +1% in Q1 FY25, domestic share 27.1% in FY26, and a stated policy of growing ~2x India trade growth — accompanied by rising, not falling, margins and ROCE, so the share is not bought.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Q4 FY25 management attributes part of the EBITDA outgrowth to price increases and repeatedly shows EBITDA growing faster than volume with domestic port margins at a record 74.2% in H1 FY26; but in Q4 FY26 margins fell from ~59-60% to ~56% and the company gave away free storage at Mundra, so pricing power is evident but not unconditional. (The long view had said: Feb 2023 shows realisation per ton up 13% y-o-y and Aug 2024 a busy-season surcharge passed on, but management attributes most of the gain to customer/cargo mix rather than list-price increases, and FY26 saw free storage given away plus contractual step-down pricing at FCI.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: the only computable check points this way but the calls offer no evidence — numbers alone are only ever a screening hint, never a confirmed fit. (The long view had said: Reputation is institutional rather than consumer: World Bank CPPI rankings, India Maritime awards, and exporting the 'Adani DNA' to international ports lifted overseas EBITDA 180% in FY26 — but there is no heritage story, A&P spend or consumer franchise, and governance episodes (auditor qualification, Gujarat land case) cut against trust.)
- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Across all four calls guidance was set and beaten (FY25 surpassed, FY26 exceeded the upper end of revenue, EBITDA and capex; the 500 MMT promise delivered; Logistics ROCE hit 10% three years ahead of the Ambition 2030 plan), leverage held at 1.8-1.9x with ROCE/ROE as the stated scorecard, and reporting lines were re-cut on investor feedback — though the Q4 FY26 margin miss was explained largely by externalities rather than owned as an error. (The long view had said: The delivery record is genuinely strong — the FY23 promise of 500 MMT and 2.5x net debt/EBITDA was met and beaten (1.9x, 500 MMT delivered in FY26), with guidance exceeded every year and ROCE as the stated yardstick — but leadership churned (Karan Adani→Ashwani Gupta as CEO, CFO and IR head both replaced) and the auditor qualification was removed via a group-commissioned review rather than through candid disclosure of the underlying issue.)

**That weakened:**

- **Toll Roads**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Port handling charges are a small slice of a shipper's landed cost yet critical to operations, and the calls show customers concentrating cargo at APSEZ (container share 44.4%→45.9%, coking coal 36.5%→41.9%) with margins held at ~74% — but management never frames it as certification, standards or non-negotiated pricing, and the Q4 FY26 free-storage concession shows charges are not immune to negotiation. (The long view had said: Port charges are a small slice of cargo value but the asset is unavoidable for trade, which shows up as ~70-72% domestic port EBITDA margins sustained from FY23 to FY26 and 23% domestic ROCE; management even positions itself as the nation's trade conduit during the West Asia crisis.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: LIKELY FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: the only computable check points this way but the calls offer no evidence — numbers alone are only ever a screening hint, never a confirmed fit. (The long view had said: Repeated investment in Navis, an in-house port community system, and automated terminals at Vizhinjam Phase 2 drives measurable throughput (Mundra at 94% utilisation vs nameplate), but no R&D spend is ever disclosed and there is no product pipeline or premiumisation-from-innovation language.)
- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: APSEZ is deliberately moving down the chain toward the cargo owner — trucking, ICD-to-customer delivery, international freight forwarding and marine services — described as becoming an 'integrated transport utility', with Logistics revenue +79% in Q2 FY26 and +55% in FY26; but this is B2B service integration, not own-stores, franchising or D2C. (The long view had said: Consistent multi-year move down the chain from berth to end customer — ICDs, 60mn sq ft warehousing ambition, rakes, agri silos, then asset-light freight forwarding and trucking — with delivery evidenced by logistics revenue +55% and ROCE rising 6%→10% in FY26, ahead of the 3-4 year plan.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: the calls are silent and no computable check supports the pattern. (The long view had said: No discussion of attach rates, AMC/licence streams, renewal rates, or customer advances/deferred revenue as a funding source; the one contracted-revenue detail disclosed (FCI) actually steps pricing down after 10 years.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Cost to Replicate (STRONG FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: The business is fixed port infrastructure operating under concessions and SEZ status, and management repeatedly frames volume swings in terms of policy and state action — 'Make in India or use in India' substituting coastal coal for EXIM coal, duty/policy impact on global trade, Operation Sindoor and West Asia disruptions, and free storage extended as a public-service act 'for the country'. (The long view had said: The business is fixed infrastructure whose growth depends on government-awarded concessions and approvals — Deendayal Port and Tanzania concession agreements, an O&M contract at Syama Prasad Mookerjee Port, Kolkata, Myanmar divestment held up on 'certain CPs which are government approvals', a Gujarat state land-recovery case now before the Supreme Court, and the H1 bid on Karaikal — and near-term volume outlook is explicitly pinned to a policy directive on coal.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); New Entrants (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW); Good Enough Goods (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 9 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
