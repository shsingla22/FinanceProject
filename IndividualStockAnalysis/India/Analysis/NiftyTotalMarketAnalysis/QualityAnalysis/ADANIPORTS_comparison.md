**Checks that improved:**

- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Neutral → Excellent. - **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Good → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹59 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹59 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±5.4 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Leverage held at 1.8–1.9x net debt/EBITDA with maturity extended to 5.2 years and a Fitch outlook upgrade to Stable, but the "no big M&A" test is failed by NQXT, Gopalpur, Astro Offshore and fleet acquisitions in a single year. | The long view had said: Leverage discipline is real and improving (2.5x target to 2.1x to 1.9x net debt/EBITDA, no intercompany loans) but this is offset by continuous large inorganic expansion rather than an organic-only focus.

And from the calls: Entry requires concessions, coastline and multi-thousand-crore capex — a ₹75,000 crore five-year programme illustrates the ticket size — and no new entrants are mentioned in any call; pricing appears rational with an achieved price increase. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±5.4 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Entry requires coastline concessions and multi-thousand-crore capex won through competitive bidding, and new capacity comes to incumbents rather than new entrants; no discounting war is evident.
- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Neutral. Now: M&A is frequent and sizeable rather than small bolt-on (Gopalpur, Astro Offshore, NQXT Queensland, en-bloc vessel deals), and while post-deal profitability improved (international ports EBITDA +180%, marine ROCE 13%→15%), international ports ROCE is still only 7% and NQXT is a promoter-affiliated asset. | The long view had said: M&A is large, frequent and cross-border (Haifa, Karaikal, Gopalpur, Tanzania, Colombo, NQXT Australia) rather than small complementary bolt-ons, and a further multi-year acquisition buffer is planned, though post-deal international EBITDA did jump 180%.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 6 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 6. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 4 weakened, 3 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Explicit, rising share numbers across all four calls — 27% (FY25) → 28.1% (Q2 FY26), containers 44.4% → 45.9%, coking coal 36.5% → 41.9%, coastal coal 27.8% → 31.1% — with growth at 1.6–1.7x all-India cargo growth, and crucially the share was gained while ROCE rose (domestic ports 21% → 23–24%), not bought with profitability. (The long view had said: Share gains are quantified and repeated across the whole span: Aug 2024 container share +2.5%, coal share +1%, growth of 8.8% vs India's 4.3% ('we grow 2x more than the India growth'); by FY26 domestic market share is 27.1% and the 500 MMT target set in FY23 was delivered. International expansion (Haifa, Colombo CWIT, Tanzania, Gopalpur, NQXT Australia, Vizhinjam) adds share outside India with local operating control.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: The May 2025 call notes a price increase contributing alongside volume, and domestic port EBITDA margin hit a record 74.2% in H1 FY26 — but Q4 FY26 saw margins fall to ~56% from 59–60% with free storage given away to shipping lines, so pricing discipline is not demonstrated through the disruption. (The long view had said: There is real evidence of price realisation — Rs.58/ton (+13%) and Mundra +25% on flat volumes in FY23, busy-season surcharges passed on in FY25 — but it is offset by contractual price deflation (FCI 10-year step-down repricing) and by voluntarily giving away free container storage at Mundra in FY26, which compressed margins to 56%.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: the only computable check points this way but the calls offer no evidence — numbers alone are only ever a screening hint, never a confirmed fit. (The long view had said: Reputation shows up as operational credentials rather than consumer brand equity — four ports in the World Bank Container Port Performance Index 2023, 'best container terminal of the year' awards, and the export of 'Adani's DNA' as a management system to newly acquired international ports — but there is no heritage story, A&P spend or brand-led pricing premium.)

**That weakened:**

- **Toll Roads**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: APSEZ is genuinely toll-road-like in economics — a critical, hard-to-bypass node handling 28.1% of India's cargo and 45.9% of containers at 73–74% domestic port EBITDA margins — but management never articulates the classic toll-road proof points (port charges as a tiny share of customer cost, customers not switching once converted, certification/standards anchoring). (The long view had said: Port charges are a small, unavoidable slice of a shipper's landed cost on cargo that must move through a fixed set of berths, and APSEZ has repeatedly lifted per-ton realisation without losing volume (Feb 2023: +Rs.58/ton, +13% y/y; Mundra realisation +25% on flat container volume). By FY26 it handled 27.1% of India's cargo with domestic port ROCE of 23% and EBITDA margins of ~70-72%, the economics of a rational, capacity-constrained toll.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: LIKELY FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: the only computable check points this way but the calls offer no evidence — numbers alone are only ever a screening hint, never a confirmed fit. (The long view had said: Sustained technology and automation investment is described (Navis terminal software, an in-house port community system, an automated Vizhinjam Phase 2, and a dedicated technology/decarbonisation allocation in Ambition 2031), but there is no disclosed R&D spend, named product pipeline, or share-of-industry-R&D framing — this is operational digitalisation, not innovation-led dominance.)
- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: APSEZ is deliberately moving down the chain toward the cargo owner — trucking, ICDs, freight forwarding and international freight network services, explicitly to own the last leg the port previously handed off; Logistics revenue grew 79% in Q2 FY26 and 55% in FY26 with ROCE up 6%→10%. It is forward integration into services, not own-store/franchise/D2C. (The long view had said: APSEZ has consistently moved down-chain toward the cargo owner — rakes, ICDs, agri silos, warehousing, trucking, freight forwarding and last-mile — pitched explicitly as an integrated end-to-end platform; logistics revenue grew 64% y/y in Q3FY23 and 55% in FY26, with ROCE up from 6% to 10% via asset-light/asset-zero services that feed the asset-heavy base.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: the calls are silent and no computable check supports the pattern. (The long view had said: A genuine efficiency culture is visible — automation to lift throughput (Mundra at 94% utilisation vs nameplate), a 'leaner organization' pushed into international ports, and ROCE as the stated scorecard — but this is a high-margin (56-72% EBITDA), capital-heavy business, not a low-price/high-turns model, and no itemised cost savings passed to customers are discussed.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Cost to Replicate (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
