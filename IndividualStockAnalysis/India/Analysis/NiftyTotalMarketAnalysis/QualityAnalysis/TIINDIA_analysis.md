# Tube Investments of India Ltd. (TIINDIA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: AUTOMOBILE*

Tube Investments of India (TIINDIA) is an Indian engineering and manufacturing group, part of the Murugappa group and based in Chennai, that makes metal components and finished products — precision steel tubes and engineering products, metal-formed parts such as chains, bicycles, and, through subsidiaries, electric vehicles, electrical equipment and industrial gears. Most of its sales go to other manufacturers, especially automobile makers (management describes the business as 'mainly dependent on auto as well as non-auto'), with about 15% of company-level revenue coming from exports; the bicycle and EV businesses sell to end customers. It makes money by manufacturing and selling these parts and products at a margin, and it passes commodity-cost increases on to customers under contracts, usually with a one-to-two-quarter lag. In FY2026 the parent company alone had revenue of Rs.8,556 Crores with PBT (before exceptional items and CCPS fair-value gain) of Rs.1,099 Crores, while consolidated revenue including subsidiaries was Rs.22,847 Crores; management repeatedly cites a return on invested capital of around 44%.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Engineering (TPI)** — The core tube and precision-products business and by far the largest part of the parent company — Rs.5,612 Crores of revenue and Rs.689 Crores of profit before interest and tax in FY2026, up from Rs.5,029 Crores and Rs.617 Crores the prior year; management says Q4 revenue growth was essentially all volume growth, and a new railway product line (parts for Vande Bharat coaches) is still at the development/customer-approval stage.
- **Metal Formed Products (MFPD)** — Makes metal-formed parts including automotive chains; steady rather than growing fast, at Rs.1,603 Crores revenue and Rs.162 Crores PBIT in FY2026 versus Rs.1,565 Crores and Rs.161 Crores the year before, with management saying its auto-chain market share is intact or marginally improved.
- **Mobility (TI Cycles)** — The bicycle business, explicitly described on the call as 'the bicycle business', which grew to Rs.783 Crores revenue in FY2026 from Rs.671 Crores and turned a thin profit of Rs.19 Crores PBIT versus Rs.5 Crores — small in profit terms but improving.
- **Others** — A catch-all for smaller lines including industrial chains (a roughly Rs.900 Crore market where TI says it holds close to 50% share) and TI Medical, the medical-devices business; together Rs.923 Crores of revenue and Rs.70 Crores PBIT in FY2026, with TI Medical held back by delayed European CE certifications and a limited number of Indian acquisition targets.
- **TI Clean Mobility (TICMPL)** — A separate electric-vehicle arm — electric three-wheelers, small commercial vehicles, e-tractors, and heavy electric trucks under 'IPL Tech' — which grew revenue about 160% in FY2025 on total volume of 7,540 units; it is still loss-making and management says it is at the stage of building toward EBITDA and cash-flow breakeven, calling it a business to 'double down on' despite competition and the GST cut making petrol/diesel vehicles relatively cheaper.
- **CG Power and Industrial Solutions** — A 56%-owned listed subsidiary in electrical/industrial products that is the single biggest contributor to consolidated numbers — Rs.12,418 Crores of revenue and Rs.1,662 Crores of pre-exceptional profit in FY2026, up from Rs.9,909 Crores and Rs.1,348 Crores.
- **Shanthi Gears** — A 70%-owned subsidiary in the gears business, and the one segment shrinking — FY2026 revenue of Rs.519 Crores and profit of Rs.107 Crores, down from Rs.605 Crores and Rs.130 Crores the year before.
- **3xper Innoventure** — A newer venture in the CDMO (contract development and manufacturing) space whose revenue-generating plant in Andhra Pradesh was delayed more than 18 months by permissions; management said in Feb 2026 that production would start within about three months, followed by a long certification cycle.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Tube Investments earns its keep in the core business and in the people running it. Management scores full marks on the quality framework, and the reason is unusually plain speaking: over the period the leadership owned up to misjudging the EV opportunity, getting capital estimates wrong, and repeatedly missing timelines in railways, medical certification and CDMO, without dressing any of it up. Returns on capital also score full marks — return on invested capital held at 44-49% and free cash flow rose to 100% of after-tax profit, on a balance sheet light enough that every ₹1 of assets generates ₹1.07 of sales. Profit margins have moved only about ±1.8 points a year, which the framework reads as costs under control and as a sign that competitors are not fighting on price. These same facts are what earn the 'Corporate Culture' pattern a partial pass, and the steady margins tie directly to the 'Barriers to entry and rationality' check.

The single trait that does the most work here also creates the most exposure. Long OEM approval cycles — even existing customers must re-qualify the new Nasik and large-diameter tube lines over three to four months — are why the 'Toll Roads' and 'Cost to Replicate' patterns register at all, and they are also the stated silver lining that keeps the 'Good Enough Goods' risk from being worse in the tube franchise. But that protection covers only the legacy tube and chain business, where share is described as intact or marginally improved and the company holds around half of a ₹900 crore industrial chain market. It does not cover the newer businesses, and that is exactly where the risks cluster. In heavy electric trucks the company went from deploying 172 of the industry's 206 vehicles in FY2025 to facing seven or eight competitors by November 2025, and in EV three-wheelers the stated response is to bring down cost structures against cheaper rivals — competing on price, not on approval-gated performance. So the EV arm simultaneously supplies the only evidence for the 'Forward Integrators' pattern (direct selling and a dealer network growing from 85 toward 120) and drives the 'New Entrants' and 'Technological Innovation' risks. Growth capex is being kept two to three years ahead of demand at 44-49% returns in the core, while a separate ₹500-750 crore of parent capital flows to the newer venture — the same capital allocation decision read two different ways.

What holds the overall rating down is that almost nothing outside the core is converting yet, and several growth levers have simply failed. Exports have been stuck at roughly 15% of sales for years, the US market is shut by a 50% Section 232 duty, Europe is weak, and management concedes the export lever did not work — the geographic check scores negative. Growth is explicitly volume-led with no pricing benefit; commodity inflation is passed through with a one-to-two quarter lag and Q4 FY26 revenue growth equalled volume growth with no major price movement. That is why 'Pricing Power' is a clear no-fit, alongside 'Brand Strength', 'Low Price Plus' and 'Recurring Revenue' — the business is component and vehicle sales with negligible service income. Two risks rate high: the ₹1,000 crore railway contract has slipped from Q4 FY26 to two or three quarters out pending government approval on Vande Bharat coaches, and Metal Formed Products is openly tied to one named Korean account while sitting flat for two years at ₹1,565 crore to ₹1,603 crore. Year-to-year growth is lumpy, resilience reads as strained, and the arithmetic — 45% × 61 for business quality, 30% × 10 for multibagger fit, 25% × 26 for risk safety — lands at 37 out of 100, a Mixed two-star verdict: a genuinely high-return core business paying for a set of bets that have not yet earned their own barriers.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, Stakeholder Concentration, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.1× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ⚠️ Only about 49% of recent operating profit became cash (was 102%) — profits are getting stuck in paper, an early stress signal.
- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 20% to 22% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Policy is repeatedly the swing factor across all five calls: the Rs.1,000 Cr railway contract has slipped from Q4 FY26 to 'two or three quarters from now' pending Government of India approval on Vande Bharat coaches and management concedes tender work carries no profitability; GST rate changes directly reversed EV total-cost-of-ownership economics; Section 232 duties shut engineering exports out of the US; and PLI eligibility is discussed as a competitive variable in three-wheelers.

> "railways, where maybe we have not got the profitability in the tender business" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Policy-linked revenue is a minority of the mix — domestic non-government auto demand delivered double-digit organic growth when exports and railways both failed to deliver*

### Stakeholder Concentration — HIGH RISK
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 1 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Metal Formed Products' fortunes are openly tied to one named account — 'if one of the Korean customer does well, obviously we will do well' — and that division has been flat for two years (Rs.1,565 Cr to Rs.1,603 Cr) while the segment also waits on a single railway customer's approval cycle. Supplier concentration bit hard in Q4 FY2026, when a single body-in-white vendor cut three-wheeler output to half and forced TI to take over the supplier's facility outright.

> "our Q4 volume took a beating because we could only produce 50% of what we could have produced otherwise" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Management is explicitly building capability for other MFPD customers and new product lines 'so that maybe even if one customer is not doing well, how we can do well as a company'; vertical integration of the failed supplier removes that bottleneck*

- ⚠️ Customers now take about 64 days to pay, up from 47 — lengthening collection terms are how weak bargaining power against concentrated customers shows up in the numbers.

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Where TI sells a differentiated new product it is already being forced to defend on price rather than performance — in EV three-wheelers the stated fix is to 'bring down all cost structures' against cheaper incumbents, and in railways the tender route offers no profitability. The engineering business is better protected: OEM approval cycles of several months for new plants and extended-range tubes make substitution slow and the business 'sticky'.

> "the objective is to kind of bring down all cost structures that we did" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Long OEM qualification cycles and existing-customer approval requirements protect the tube franchise from cheap substitution; ~50% share in a niche Rs.900 Cr industrial chain market*

- ⚠️ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs fell from about 40% to 34% — pricing is being competed away at the gross level.
- ✅ Operating margin (10%) remains near its historical peak (13%) — no collapse in the premium.
- ✅ Operating margin held (about 8% then 11%) — no drift down under competition.

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Standalone results are levered to Indian auto/industrial demand — GST-driven festive uptick in Nov-2025, then a Q4 FY2026 question on 'challenging macro environment', commodity and fuel inflation — and the capex-linked units swing hardest (Shanthi Gears revenue fell from Rs.605 Cr to Rs.519 Cr in FY26 while CG Power boomed). Offsetting this, the tube/chain volumes feed customers' ongoing production rather than their capex, and steel cost is contractually passed through, so segment PBIT stayed in a narrow band (engineering PBIT Rs.617 Cr FY25 to Rs.689 Cr FY26) across all five calls.

> "commodity price increase, we get it from our customers because we have contracts in place, which comes with sometimes a one or two quarter lag" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/consumption-linked auto component demand plus contractual raw-material pass-through; countercyclical capacity build (Nasik CRSS, Phaltan) held through the soft patch*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 9 years with swings of ±33 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 7% and 13% (±1.8 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 5% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 6%).

### Technological Innovation — WATCH
*"Out-invented by the industry"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** The company has deliberately placed a large bet on an industry in technological flux — Vellayan's thesis is that 'all of the IC components and IC products... are going to get replaced over time' — and the calls show the standard incumbent dilemma inside TICMPL: battery-swap infrastructure they have already built may be obsoleted by higher-capacity cells and fast charging, and an analyst probed sodium-ion commercialisation in May 2026. *"A new player changes the rules"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

### Shifting Consumer Preferences — WATCH
*"The customer is changing"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** The bicycle business faced a structurally eroding standard-cycle demand base and was pivoted toward specialised, e-bike and fitness products — a pivot that worked, taking mobility PBIT from Rs.5 Cr in FY25 to Rs.19 Cr in FY26 on Rs.783 Cr revenue. ## What to watch
