# Rail Vikas (RVNL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Rail Vikas Nigam Limited (RVNL) is a government-owned company set up in January 2003 under India's Ministry of Railways (a "Navratna" public sector enterprise) that builds transport and other infrastructure — it is essentially a project builder and project manager, not an operator of anything it builds. Its traditional business is constructing railway infrastructure for Indian Railways, which it hands over to the railways once commissioned; management says it has built over 16,000 route kilometres and spent about INR 1.5 lakh crore over its life, and it once contributed roughly 30% of all Indian railway infrastructure creation. It earns money by executing projects and booking the construction revenue, historically getting work handed to it directly by the Ministry of Railways ("nomination" work) and increasingly by winning competitive tenders in the open market, where management says margins are thinner; it also earns dividends and profit share from joint ventures and special purpose vehicles. It works mainly across India (railways plus metros in seven cities) and has pushed overseas into neighbouring and other countries — Maldives, Kyrgyzstan, Mauritius, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, UAE, Saudi Arabia and Africa — with a total order book of INR 99,262 crore as of 31 March 2026 and around 1,300 staff.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Railways (rail infrastructure)** — Building new lines, doubling, tunnels and other railway works for Indian Railways — the company's core and largest business at about INR 57,000 crore of the order book, roughly INR 40,000 crore of it directly-awarded railway "nomination" work that management expects to yield INR 10,000–11,000 crore of income a year over three years; the flagship job is the 125-km Rishikesh–Karnaprayag hill/tunnel line costing INR 37,000 crore, 74% complete and due December 2029.
- **Signalling** — Railway signalling and related systems work, the second-largest slice of the order book at about INR 14,900 crore.
- **Port, roads and highways** — Harbour, port and road/highway construction, about INR 10,400 crore of the order book, including overseas harbour work such as the UTF Harbour project in the Maldives and a new memorandum with Visakhapatnam Port Authority for port infrastructure.
- **Metros** — Building urban metro rail systems — management calls this technically challenging work it is "very comfortable" with, running across seven Indian cities plus bids abroad (e.g. Mauritius), at about INR 9,900 crore of the order book.
- **Power and transmission** — Electricity transmission and power infrastructure work, a smaller diversification line at about INR 4,000 crore of the order book.
- **Hydro and irrigation** — Water-related civil works such as canals (an early example was two Ahmedabad canal orders worth about INR 500 crore), the smallest named segment at about INR 2,000 crore of the order book.
- **Vande Bharat train manufacturing (SPV Kinet Railway Solutions)** — A wholly new line for RVNL — manufacturing 120 Vande Bharat sleeper train sets (16 coaches each) with a Russian partner under a INR 14,400 crore contract that also includes 35 years of maintenance, with the first prototype targeted for December 2026.
- **BharatNet** — A INR 13,236 crore telecom contract from BSNL to lay 8,206 km of optical fibre for high-speed rural broadband — about 15% physically complete, and management expects it to be a good source of revenue and margin.
- **Joint ventures, subsidiaries and SPVs** — Project-specific companies (five railway SPVs were commissioned historically) that added INR 399.86 crore of consolidated revenue, INR 64.23 crore of PAT and INR 47.88 crore of dividend income in FY26 — small relative to the main business, but also the source of the reconciliation adjustments that hurt recent profits.
- **Export / overseas business** — Bidding for and executing foreign infrastructure funded by lines of credit and multilateral lenders (World Bank, ADB, AFDB) in Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Maldives, Mauritius, Africa, UAE, Saudi Arabia and Kyrgyzstan (a possible 1,000-km, INR 20,000 crore railway programme) — described as a growing footprint rather than a large share of current revenue.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Rail Vikas is the Ministry of Railways' own construction arm, and almost everything good and bad about it flows from that one fact. The quality framework finds only two things to credit, and both are about the market rather than the company: the end market is in a long build-out under the National Rail Plan, and FY26 looks like an admitted trough — flat revenue, profit down 32% against the prior year — sitting under a ₹99,262 crore order book and a rising railway budget, which at least gives an identifiable upturn to point at. Everything about how the company actually earns money scores badly. It sells to a customer that holds all the negotiating power: the Ministry unilaterally switched from handing work over by nomination to putting it out to open tender, sets the price through that tender, and withheld ₹3,400 crore of dues past the year-end. The effect shows up directly in the numbers — operating margin fell from 10.36% to 5.83% in a single quarter, growth is pure volume with a worsening mix as good-margin nomination work is replaced by competitively bid work, and for every ₹100 of capital in the business the company generated ₹13 of negative operating cash this year. Capital allocation (-0.75) and return on capital (-0.67) are the weakest areas; competitive advantage and customer benefits score a flat zero.

The multibagger screen tells the same story from another angle: not one of the eleven patterns is a clean fit, five are partial and six are outright misses, and every partial fails for the same reason — the strength is real but nobody pays for it. There is a genuine hard-to-copy capability, twenty-one years and 16,000 route kilometres of complex brownfield rail work plus more than 100 km of Himalayan tunnelling on Rishikesh-Karnaprayag, and there is real institutional standing: Navratna status, a CARE AAA rating, an 'excellent' government rating for twelve straight years. Neither produces a price premium, because in lowest-bidder tendering the client buys on price and the integration expertise is not paid for — which is exactly what the 'Good Enough Goods' risk channel flags, and why FY26 carried loss-making contracts. The company is also genuinely lean, about 1,300 staff against ₹20,000 crore of turnover with disclosed productivity rising from ₹21.23 crore to ₹22.46 crore per head, but that low price is imposed by the tender rather than chosen as a weapon, so 'Pricing Power' is a flat no fit. The same tender shift shows up as a high-risk 'New Entrants' channel — open bidding exposed the company to private contractors it never had to face — and as a market-share loss, since the roughly 30-35% share of Indian Railways infrastructure came from nomination work that has now been withdrawn. Management does volunteer bad news promptly, warning of a profit dip in advance and disclosing the loss-making contracts and negative cash flow, but the delivery record undercuts it: against a promise of ₹25,000 crore of fresh orders every year, standalone FY26 inflow was ₹5,875 crore.

The risk review rates three of its eight channels high risk — government dependency, customer concentration and new entrants — and these are not separate problems but three descriptions of the same relationship. The Ministry is simultaneously the source of the order book, the setter of prices, and the counterparty whose unpaid ₹3,400 crore turned cash flow negative; roughly 96 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. The stated defences all cut both ways. Diversification into metros, roads, ports, transmission, BharatNet and overseas work, with about half the book now won competitively, genuinely reduces dependence on nomination — but competitive work is precisely the low-margin work. The asset-light, debt-free model with customer-funded mobilisation advances is offered as protection against the cycle, yet the quality framework marks the same trait down because the company is deliberately not investing for growth. Growth through minority stakes in joint ventures — 25% in Kinet, 49% in Jackson Green, 49% in Chhatra Expressway, the Kyrgyz venture — has by FY26 started damaging the reported numbers through reconciliation issues and loss-making contracts. Governance scores worst of all: four chairmen listed in FY2026 alone and a managing director one month into the job issuing FY27 guidance. Technology is not a threat here, and the review calls overall resilience sound, but the combined rating lands at 27 out of 100 — 41 on business quality, 10 on multibagger fit, 24 on risk safety — a Weak, one-star verdict on a company whose market is growing while its economics are not.

*"Sells once, earns forever"*

> "a INR 14,400 crore project with 35 years maintenance arrangement being executed by our SPV Kinet Railway Solutions" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ About 96 days of cash is tied up in the trade cycle — customers are not funding the business.
- ✅ Capital spending is about 0% of sales — growth needs little capital.
- ❌ Operating cash was positive in 7 of 12 years — not fully consistent.

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**What the calls show:** There is a real lean-cost culture — ~1,300 staff against INR 20,000 crores turnover, employee productivity disclosed rising from INR 21.23 crore to INR 22.46 crore, and Feb 2026 cites cost cutting lifting gross margin 50-60bps — but the low price is imposed by tendering rather than converted into durable share or high returns, and margins fell rather than held.

> "it is a very lean and keen organization with around 1,300 persons" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Has essentially no direct input costs and turns its assets 0.9× a year — not the high-turnover, low-margin machine this pattern needs.

> "Last 12 years, it has been rated excellent. The CARE rating has been AAA" — *management, on an earnings call*

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** RVNL is a Navratna PSU under the Ministry of Railways whose order book, pricing model and cash flows are set by government: ~50% of the ₹99,262 cr book is still railway 'nomination' work, order visibility is pegged to the Union budget's railway capex, and the loss of nomination-based awarding (a policy decision) is the single biggest driver of the margin de-rating across FY23-FY26. Even the diversification is into other policy-driven pockets — BharatNet (BSNL), RDSS state discom work, port trusts, NHAI, and sovereign-funded overseas projects (LOC, World Bank, ADB).

> "there is a shift by Ministry of Railways that they are not going to give us a nomination they will be calling this" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate diversification into metros, roads, ports, transmission, BharatNet and overseas markets; ~50% of order book now won competitively rather than by nomination*

### Stakeholder Concentration — HIGH RISK
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 1 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** The Ministry of Railways remains both the dominant customer (railways ~45-57% of the order book across FY26 calls) and the counterparty on which working capital hinges — ₹3,400 cr of FY26 receivables from MoR went uncollected into April, causing negative cash flow, on top of the long-running ₹1,116 cr Krishnapatnam SPV receivable (₹890 cr of it interest) disputed since the 2022 call. > "Cash flow is a challenge, because we are working for Ministry of Railways, and we have to get money from Ministry of Railways" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Broadening client base — NMDC, Visakhapatnam Port Authority, state PSUs, BSNL, NHAI, seven metro cities and international projects (₹3,500 cr) — reduces but does not remove MoR dominance*
