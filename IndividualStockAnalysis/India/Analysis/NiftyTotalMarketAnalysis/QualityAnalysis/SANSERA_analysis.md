# Sansera Engineering (SANSERA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Sansera Engineering is an India-based supplier of precision forged and machined metal components — parts like connecting rods, crankshafts and crankshaft assemblies — that it makes to order for other manufacturers rather than selling anything to consumers. Its customers are vehicle makers (OEMs) for two-wheelers, passenger vehicles and commercial vehicles, both in India and abroad, plus a growing set of non-automotive customers in aerospace, defence, semiconductor equipment, off-road, agriculture and industrial/marine engines. It earns money by winning multi-year component programs, then forging and machining those parts in its own plants and shipping them; management tracks a running "order book" of peak annual revenue from new business (INR 19.2 billion as of March 2026) and, separately, a five-year unexecuted ADS backlog of INR 44.6 billion. Manufacturing is centred in India (Bangalore/Karnataka, Pantnagar), with a subsidiary in Sweden, exports to Europe and North America, and a newly signed 60% joint venture with Nichidai Corporation of Japan; FY26 revenue was INR 34,979 million with EBITDA margins improving to 19.3% in Q4.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Auto ICE (internal combustion engine components)** — The core business — engine and drivetrain parts such as connecting rods and crankshafts for motorcycles, scooters, passenger vehicles and commercial vehicles; it is by far the largest piece, at INR 6,426 million of Q4 FY26 revenue (roughly two-thirds of the quarter) and still growing over 20% year-on-year, with existing plants running at peak utilisation.
- **Auto Tech-Agnostic and xEV** — Vehicle parts that are not tied to the combustion engine — components that work regardless of powertrain, plus parts sold specifically for electric two-wheelers and EVs — described by management as a strategic diversification lever; it was INR 1,217 million in Q4 FY26, up about 20% year-on-year, having grown from a very small base (9% of auto revenue in Q1 FY23).
- **Non-Auto** — Everything outside vehicles — aerospace and defence, off-road equipment (mainly North American end-markets), agriculture, engineering/capital goods, semiconductor equipment and industrial and marine engines — at INR 1,736 million in Q4 FY26 and growing about 70% year-on-year, the fastest-growing part of the company.
- **ADS division (Aerospace, Defence and Semiconductor)** — The headline growth engine within Non-Auto, run by its own CEO, supplying precision parts to customers including Saab and Triumph Aerospace and equipment makers in the semiconductor space; FY26 product-sale revenue was INR 3,155 million (up 155% for the year, INR 3,498 million including scrap sales) against a five-year unexecuted order backlog of INR 44.6 billion.
- **Sweden subsidiary / international business** — An overseas manufacturing and sales arm serving European and other export customers, singled out by management as a driver of strong commercial-vehicle growth and record international sales; international customers accounted for 63% of the order book as of June 2024.
- **Nichidai joint venture (Japan)** — A newly signed JV in which Sansera is investing INR 500 million for a 60% stake over a couple of years, bringing in a Japanese partner's tools, dies and cold/warm-forged precision component expertise (operations in Japan and Thailand) to open up Japanese auto customers and add tech-agnostic products; still at start-up stage with no revenue reported.

## The verdict: Decent — 53 out of 100 ★★★☆☆

In one breath: the quality framework finds a good business today (67/100); no winning pattern is strongly confirmed yet; the main risks are elevated but not severe (Technological Innovation, Stakeholder Concentration). Weighing those together gives 53 out of 100 — decent.

### The story in depth

Sansera comes across as a well-run parts maker whose best qualities sit with the people running it rather than with the business it happens to be in. The quality framework scores Management highest (1.33) and Growth next (1.17), and the reasons behind those scores are concrete: management discloses bad news early and specifically, including the Rs 3,000cr contract withdrawal, the 20% degrowth in Sweden, missed export shipments and the loss of a key EV customer; growth has been organic and brownfield rather than bought; debt has been cut to the point where quarterly finance cost is down to Rs 79mn against Rs 3,972mn of cash; and heavy growth capex (Rs 4,500mn in FY25 rising to Rs 5,097mn in FY26, with a similar figure planned for FY27) has gone into new capacity while returns improved (ROCE 18.0% versus 16.2%). The 60/40 target set at the IPO has been pursued for five years, taking non-auto, tech-agnostic and electric-vehicle work from roughly 12% to 30-32%. Against that, Competitive Advantage scores 0.0 — the lowest of the seven areas — and one check is outright negative: about 98 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, which means the same growth that management is funding also keeps swallowing working capital.

The multibagger screen tells the same story from a different angle, and its low score (21 out of 100) is mostly the mirror image of the quality strengths rather than a contradiction of them. The one pattern rated a likely fit, Innovation Dominance, rests on exactly the spending the quality framework praised — the 4,000-ton press, aluminium forging, 5-axis machining of 4-5 metre parts, the MMRFIC radar stake — but the screen notes that this is investment in machines and processes, with no disclosed research spend and no revenue attributed to a named product. Two more links run straight between the sections. First, the very steady margins that earn a top mark under the barriers check (moving only about 0.8 points a year, with 18 rupees of operating profit per 100 of sales) come from product mix, operating leverage and utilisation, never from price — which is why Pricing Power is scored as no fit, with commodity increases having to be renegotiated customer by customer and Q4 FY26 already flagging inflation. Second, the long validation, first-article-inspection and certification cycles that lock in an approved supplier are the same fact behind three separate readings: a partial fit on Toll Roads and Cost to Replicate, and a low risk verdict on both New Entrants and Good Enough Goods. That combination is worth stating plainly — the qualification gates protect Sansera from being undercut from below, but they have not translated into the ability to raise prices at will.

The two elevated risks are, in each case, the shadow side of a strength. The market-share gains celebrated in the quality framework — Tata Motors, Force Motors, Saab, Triumph, rising content per vehicle, 2-wheeler crankshaft outsourcing — are gains won one large customer at a time, and that is precisely why Stakeholder Concentration is elevated: single relationships repeatedly move the profit line, as when the Rs 3,000+ crore North American contract was withdrawn in 2022 and when Volvo's own de-risking cut Sansera's share from 100% to 70% and produced the 20% Swedish degrowth. Notably, the top score on candour was earned by disclosing these very events, so the strength and the risk are two readings of one set of facts. The other elevated risk, Technological Innovation, explains why the diversification push exists at all: connecting rods and crankshaft assemblies carry no content in a battery vehicle, so the tenacity and independence rewarded under the quality checks is a race to move into aerospace, defence, semiconductor equipment and light-weighting before the core erodes — and the aerospace and defence business growing 155% in FY26 is the evidence that the race is being run, not merely described. Cyclicality, government dependency and shifting end-demand are all rated watch rather than serious, and fashion risk, new entrants and cheaper substitutes are all low, which is how the review still lands on sound overall resilience. The final rating pulls the three strands together arithmetically: 45% of a business-quality score of 67, plus 30% of a multibagger fit of 21, plus 25% of a risk-safety score of 68, giving 53 out of 100 and a Decent, three-star verdict. In plain terms, this is a capable, honest, self-funding manufacturer executing a genuine pivot, held back from a higher grade by the absence of a durable moat and by the fact that its growth is bought with capital and customer wins rather than with pricing power.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Technological Innovation, Stakeholder Concentration. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

### Technological Innovation — ELEVATED
*"Out-invented by the industry"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 2 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** The core franchise is ICE powertrain forgings (connecting rods, crankshaft assemblies) that carry zero content in a battery vehicle, and the whole strategic narrative from FY23 to FY26 is an explicit race to de-risk into tech-agnostic, xEV and non-auto before the ICE base erodes; ICE was still ~68-70% of FY26 revenue and the 2022 cancellation of a Rs.3,000+ crore North American order was itself a 'new technology engine' programme being dropped.

> "60% has to come -- will come from auto ICE and another 40% will come from non-auto as well as tech -agnostic and xEV components" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate, funded platform-agnostic pivot: xEV and tech-agnostic components supplied to 10+ EV customers, aluminium light-weighting (common to all powertrains), ADS non-auto now ~30-32% of revenue, and the Nichidai JV targeting driveline/steering parts 'common between ICE and EV'.*

- ✅ Growth is holding up: 10% a year earlier, 14% recently — no structural stall.

### Stakeholder Concentration — ELEVATED

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is per-vehicle content consumed in OEM production (a flow/operations-linked base) rather than customer capex, but across the 16 calls management repeatedly frames results around alloy-steel and commodity price pass-through, CV up-cycles, export slowdowns, OEM inventory levels and tariff-driven offtake; the newer semicon-equipment and aerospace ADS lines are themselves tied to customer capital cycles, and by FY26 they are the main growth engine.

> "the operating environment remains fluid with inflationary pressures across steel, aluminum, energy, tooling, consumables and freight" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Demand is component content per vehicle produced (operations-linked flow product, not customer capex), and management invested counter-cyclically through the FY25-26 export slowdown (Pantnagar plant, ADS capacity, Bidadi land) while margins expanded from 17% to 18-19%.*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 7 years with swings of ±12 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.

## What to watch

- Whether the non-auto, tech-agnostic and electric-vehicle share keeps climbing past the current 30-32% toward the 60/40 target — the direct test of the elevated Technological Innovation risk to the ICE forging core.
- Any further single-customer shock of the kind already seen (the Rs 3,000cr contract withdrawal, Volvo going from 100% to 70%, the lost EV customer) — the elevated Stakeholder Concentration risk is the one that has repeatedly moved results.
- The roughly 98-day gap between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, the only negative check — if it lengthens while capex stays near Rs 5,000mn a year, the self-funded, low-debt position that earns the disciplined-stewards score gets harder to hold.
- Whether margins ever expand because of price rather than mix, utilisation and operating leverage — that would be the first evidence against the no-fit verdict on Pricing Power, especially with Q4 FY26 already flagging inflation.
- Whether the aerospace and defence ramp (155% growth in FY26) holds up alongside the semiconductor equipment thesis, since the structural end-market growth check and the government dependency watch risk both hang on policy-linked demand the company does not control.
