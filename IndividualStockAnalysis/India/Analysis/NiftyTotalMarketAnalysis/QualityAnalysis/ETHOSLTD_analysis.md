# Ethos (ETHOSLTD) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Ethos Limited is an Indian luxury retailer that sells high-end wristwatches — mostly Swiss brands — through its own network of physical boutiques and its website, ethoswatches.com. It buys watches from luxury brands and resells them to affluent Indian customers at retail, so it makes money on the gross margin between purchase and sale price, earning better margins on the brands it has signed exclusive India retail rights for (about 31% of revenue) and on its certified pre-owned business, alongside servicing and after-sales support. It operates only in India (73 boutiques across 26 cities as of March 2025, targeting 100+, plus a 22,000 sq ft flagship 'City of Time' in Gurgaon), with a newly formed, exploratory UAE subsidiary. Revenue was about INR 1,252 crore in FY25, up roughly 25% year-on-year, with EBITDA of about INR 161 crore.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Luxury watch retail (multi-brand boutiques + omnichannel)** — The core business — selling luxury and high-luxury watches through Ethos boutiques (multi-brand formats such as Ethos Summit and the newer Ethos Haute Horology) backed by a website, social media, HNI customer database and home/office delivery; this is the bulk of the roughly INR 1,252 crore FY25 revenue, and management said the digital channel alone contributed nearly one-third of billings back in Q1 FY23.
- **Exclusive brand portfolio / exclusive brand boutiques** — Watch brands that have appointed Ethos as their exclusive India retail partner (35 brands as of 2022, with Jacob & Co., NORQAIN, Ulysse Nardin, Nivada Grenchen, Gerald Charles, Chronoswiss, ID Geneve, Singer Reimagined and Hautlence added since); management calls these long-nurtured relationships a competitive moat and says they carry higher margins, contributing about 26% of billings in Q1 FY23 and around 31% of revenue by FY24–FY25.
- **Certified Pre-Owned (CPO) — 'Second Movement'** — Buying, authenticating and reselling second-hand luxury watches — increasingly on a consignment basis — through a dedicated lounge and the secondmovement.com website; described as a 'new growth pillar', it did INR 32 crore in H1 FY24 and INR 43 crore of billings in H1 FY25, growing 25–34% a year and over 30% in FY25.
- **Luxury jewellery — Messika** — Retailing the French jewellery brand Messika in India, with the first dedicated boutique opened at Chanakya Mall, New Delhi in May 2025; it is an early-stage diversification that management says it is approaching cautiously with a long-term outlook.
- **Premium luggage / lifestyle — Rimowa** — Retailing the LVMH-owned luggage brand Rimowa, starting with a roughly 500 sq ft boutique opened October 2023 at Jio World Plaza, Mumbai (their first non-watch luxury store), with a second Delhi boutique planned; small in size but positioned as a future growth vertical.
- **After-sales service and watch servicing** — Repair, servicing and related customer support (including watch insurance and specialised luxury watch consultants) offered alongside sales; management repeatedly names it as a pillar of the strategy and a reason customers and brands trust Ethos, though no separate revenue figure is given in these calls.
- **Favre Leuba (group-invested Swiss brand)** — A Swiss watch brand the promoter group has invested in and relaunched globally in Geneva in August 2024; Ethos has a long-term exclusive agreement to sell it in India, adding a brand-ownership angle on top of pure retailing.
- **International expansion — Ficus Trading LLC (UAE)** — A wholly-owned UAE subsidiary set up as of May 2025 which management describes as purely exploratory at this stage, to understand what Ethos might do outside India.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Demand is discretionary and visibly swings with festive/wedding seasons, elections, weather and forex rather than any commodity or customer-capex cycle — H1 FY25 growth slowed to 22.6% (volume +5%) on heatwaves, elections and a shradh shift, then October rebounded +47%; meanwhile the company is investing heavily and pro-cyclically (IPO, Rs 175 cr QIP, 50→150 boutique plan, inventory Rs 440 cr → Rs 593 cr) into an unusually strong post-COVID luxury upcycle.

> "extreme heatwaves that led to more customers spending time outside of India... The general election resulted in slower cash movements... a decline in weddings" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow-type retail demand tied to consumer operations, not customer capex; luxury watches framed as 'a store of wealth'; countercyclical-style expansion funded largely by equity with gross debt near zero (Rs 6.9 cr)*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 7 years with swings of ±22 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 8% and 16% (±2.7 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 4% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 7%).

### Government Dependency — WATCH
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Economics are materially set by policy the company does not control: a proposed GST hike from 18% to 28% requiring an ~8.5% revenue-neutral price increase dominated the Nov 2024 call, EFTA/customs-duty cuts are the swing factor for FY26 gross margin, and GRAP IV pollution restrictions shut Delhi-NCR construction for 90 days, delaying the flagship and stranding inventory; management is actively lobbying ministers.

> "we are in discussion with the right representatives of the government to also give our views on how it can be beneficial for the country to have a lower tax rate" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: No government contracts, tenders or subsidies; revenue comes from private consumers, and duty/GST changes hit the whole authorised industry rather than Ethos alone*

### Stakeholder Concentration — WATCH
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** This is a licence-dependent retailer in the Safilo mould: brands set MRP, control allocation and can grant or withdraw exclusivity — Ulysse Nardin's move to Ethos after two decades with others shows how freely such relationships migrate — and supply is concentrated in a single geography (Swiss brands, CHF exposure, June logistics disruption in Switzerland). Exclusive brands are only ~30% of revenue, so the bulk rests on multi-brand supply the company does not lock up.

> "The allocation is really a policy that the brand follows. It's not something that we can comment on." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Basket of ~60 brands with no single named dependency disclosed; broadening into CPO, lifestyle (Rimowa, Messika) and its own brand Favre Leuba*

- ✅ Customers pay in about 5 days (was 9) — no sign of weakening bargaining power in collection terms.

### Fashion Risk — WATCH
*"Cool today, uncool tomorrow"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Demand rests on taste and status: growth has been driven by ASP moving from ~Rs 84,000 to ~Rs 2.16 lakh and by a bet on 'independent brands' currently in vogue with collectors, plus celebrity-led positioning (Messika at Cannes) — desirability that can move without warning, as the post-2022 secondary-market cooling showed globally.

### Good Enough Goods — WATCH
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** The threat is not private label but cheaper channels for the identical product — grey/unauthorised sellers and overseas purchase, with India pricing running only 5–7% cheaper than global destinations and a GST hike capable of flipping that arbitrage against Ethos; management's own framing shows price is a live competitive variable managed through discount levels and brand-borne rebates.

> "It can be a 5% price increase and a 3.5% decrease in discount or 3.5% borne by the brand" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Genuine niche/scale protection — high end-customer brand awareness (the customer chooses the watch, not an intermediary), authorised-dealer warranty and servicing, and steadily reducing discounting through FY23 as exclusivity mix rose*

- ✅ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs held near 30% (was 28%) — no gross-level price erosion.
- ✅ Operating margin (13%) remains near its historical peak (16%) — no collapse in the premium.
