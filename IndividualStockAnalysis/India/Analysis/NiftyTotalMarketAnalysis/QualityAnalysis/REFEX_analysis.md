# Refex Industries (REFEX) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Refex Industries is a Chennai-headquartered Indian company, incorporated in 2002, that mainly makes its money collecting, transporting and disposing of the ash and coal left over at thermal power plants — it charges power producers to haul the ash away and then sells or repurposes it for road, highway, embankment and mine-filling work, and it calls itself the largest organised player in ash handling in the country. It runs a large truck fleet (owned and leased) across power-generating states including Chhattisgarh, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Bihar, Odisha, Tamil Nadu and Karnataka, and this one business supplied roughly 95% of revenue in Q1 FY26. Alongside it, the company has built a wind turbine business (Venwind) that began delivering 5.3 MW turbines to large power developers in FY26, and an electric-vehicle fleet business that ferries corporate employees and airport passengers, which is being spun off into a separate listed entity. Older, smaller lines — refrigerant gas refilling and power trading — have been wound down or exited, and standalone revenue from continuing operations was about INR2,039 crores in FY26.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Ash & Coal Handling** — Collects, transports and disposes of ash from thermal power plants and handles coal for them, reusing the ash in roads, embankments and mine-filling; it is the core business — about 95% of Q1 FY26 revenue, INR945.58 crores in FY24, with an open order book of roughly INR1,500 crores as of FY26 and segment margins around 11.8%.
- **Wind (Venwind / wind energy)** — Builds and supplies 5.3 MW wind turbine generators to large independent power producers such as Jindal Steel, Torrent and KP Green; it raised its first invoice in Q1 FY26, began deliveries in February 2026, contributed about INR233 crores in Q4 FY26 and carries a cumulative order book of INR1,860 crores, making it the company's main new growth engine.
- **Green Mobility / Refex Mobility (formerly Refex eVeelz)** — Rents out electric vehicles with vetted drivers and a booking app for corporate employee transport and as airport taxis in cities such as Bengaluru, Chennai, Pune and Hyderabad; it crossed INR100 crores of revenue in FY26 after three years but has been loss-making (around -50% margin in Q1 FY26) and is being demerged into a separate company, expected around April 2026.
- **Refrigerant gases** — Refilled and sold HFC refrigerant gases (including consumer 'Refex cans') for air conditioning, refrigeration and aerosols from a Chennai facility with Delhi and Mumbai warehouses; it was small (INR72.31 crores in FY24, ~0.9% margin) and management decided to exit the business.
- **Power trading** — Bought and sold electricity under a Category 1 trading licence obtained in 2022; it brought in INR280.9 crores in FY24 but margins turned negative and the company wound the vertical down during FY26.
- **Solar power and other services** — Small residual income from solar power generation (INR10.36 crores in FY24) and other services (INR55.64 crores in FY24), a minor part of the overall business.
- **Refex Engineering Products** — A newly formed subsidiary sitting under the Venwind wind business, set up to target retail customers within the same wind segment.

### The story in depth

Refex's story starts with one thing it does demonstrably well: taking work away from weak competitors. It hauls and disposes of ash for thermal power plants, a job that is small in a plant's budget but legally unavoidable, and it does that across 41-42 plants in 15 states while 99% of the competing field is unorganised, regional and often single-plant. Daily ash handled went from 35,000 tonnes in May 2024 to 70,000 tonnes in 2025/26, with 90,000-95,000 tonnes targeted; ash grew 28% in FY26 and the company has compounded around 20% a year for six years, against a total market put at INR68,000-75,000 crore versus its own roughly INR2,000 crore of revenue. That is why the share-gain checks score well (Gain in market share 2, Share donators 2) and why 'Market Share Gainers' is the one pattern rated a strong fit: the rivals that keep failing on compliance are precisely the pool Refex converts, and management names only one meaningful organised competitor. Geographic spread and growth capex support the same engine — the owned ash fleet went from 39 to about 119 trucks — and the growth and industry-structure scores (0.67 each) are the best marks in the whole framework.

The trouble is that the same features that create the opportunity also create most of the risk, so the strengths and the risks are largely the same facts read from opposite ends. Demand exists because environmental rules force plants to move ash, which makes the whole franchise policy-created — hence Government Dependency as the single high risk, with volumes tracking government thermal capacity targets and wind depending on approved-model rules. The customer base that gives scale is dominated by state-owned buyers such as NTPC and APGENCO, which sets the working-capital cycle and shows up as stakeholder concentration. The fragmented, low-barrier field that is easy to take share from is also easy to enter — the CFO acknowledged in August 2025 that competition is coming in — and because ash haulage is not a complex product, Refex has to keep justifying its premium on compliance guarantees, GPS tracking and national coverage rather than on anything protected. That shows in the economics: after direct costs the company keeps only about INR19 of every INR100 of sales, there is no discussion of price increases anywhere, realisations are set by tender, and four patterns tied to pricing and brand — Pricing Power, Brand Strength, Low Price Plus and Friendly Middleman — are outright no-fits. The FY26 margin jump from 10.4% to 20.1% came from dropping low-margin trading and changing the mix, not from charging more, and earnings remain swingy: FY24 revenue fell on a roughly 45% collapse in coal prices, first-quarter FY26 revenue dropped about INR246 crore against the prior quarter, and quarterly margins have ranged from 7.5% to 20.1%.

Where the record is weakest is what management does with the money, and this is the main reason the overall grade lands at Mixed rather than higher. Capital allocation, management and return on capital all score zero as areas: the business generated only about INR6 of operating cash for every INR100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings, growth has averaged roughly 39% a year but arrives in lumps, and the heavy spending on trucks, an electric vehicle fleet growing from 479 toward 2,000 and a wind plant at Silvassa has so far produced poor incremental returns in mobility. The clearest example is the airport-taxi acquisition: the company bought a business that had won a tender it could not execute, ran the mobility arm at minus 50% margins, and is now demerging it out — which is also why the forward-integration pattern is only a partial fit, since both consumer-facing moves (the refrigerant cans and the airport taxi service) are being wound down or separated. The culture read is genuinely two-sided: bad news does get disclosed promptly, including the December 2025 tax search, the loss-making mobility arm and a 'point well taken' response on capital allocation, and the exits from power trading and refrigerants were painful but rational. The wind turbine business is management's answer to the long-term shift away from coal, but it swaps one set of risks for another — an INR1,860 crore order book resting on a handful of named buyers, and a 5.3MW turbine platform that an analyst challenged as unproven in India. Net of all this, the combined rating is 45 out of 100 (58 on business quality, 25 on multibagger fit, 44 on risk safety) with resilience judged sound: a real share-gain engine in a fragmented market, attached to thin pricing power, policy-created demand and an unproven record of turning capital into returns.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, Cyclicality, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Demand is created by environmental compliance mandates on thermal plants, the customer base is dominated by PSUs (NTPC, APGENCO), and every growth vertical is policy-anchored: ash volumes track government thermal capacity targets of 50GW, wind depends on ALMM approval and renewable policy, and mobility growth is pitched on the Prime Minister's EV shift. Management repeatedly frames the opportunity in terms of government pressure and government targets.

> "Government of India, again, after a few years, again, they are pushing the thermal power plant. 50 gigawatt capacity has been targeted in next five to seven years" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** FY24 revenue fell on a ~45% plunge in coal prices, Q1 FY26 revenue dropped ~INR246 crores quarter-on-quarter on monsoon/cement demand, and quarterly EBITDA margins have swung from 7.5% to 20.1%; the new wind turbine vertical is pure customer-capex equipment sold to IPPs. Offsetting this, the core ash business is operations-linked (ash is generated whenever plants burn coal), sits on multi-year contracts with diesel escalation pass-throughs, and management has deliberately exited the commodity-price-exposed coal/power trading revenue.

> "The main factor contributing to the decrease in our revenue stems from a significant drop in coal prices in the current year, which plunged by approximately 45%" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/operations-linked ash volumes with multi-year contracts and 40-60% diesel cost pass-through; deliberate exit from low-margin coal and power trading*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 4 of the last 10 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
