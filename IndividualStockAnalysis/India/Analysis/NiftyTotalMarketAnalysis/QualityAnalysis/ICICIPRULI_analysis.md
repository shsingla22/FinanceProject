# ICICI Prudential Life Insurance Company Ltd. (ICICIPRULI) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

ICICI Prudential Life Insurance is an Indian life insurance company, listed on the BSE and NSE and associated with the ICICI group, that sells life insurance and long-term savings products to individual retail customers in India (including NRIs through GIFT City) and to corporate/institutional groups. Management describes the business as resting on two pillars: protection, where the company collects premiums for providing death and disability risk cover, and savings, where it takes in customer money and earns from managing those funds over long periods — as of June 30, 2026 it managed assets of about ₹3.34 trillion and had ₹48.06 trillion of in-force sum assured covering its policyholders. Money is made from new business premium and renewals (new business premium of ₹248.10 billion in FY2026), from the spread between what it charges and what claims and expenses cost, and from scale and cost efficiency in the savings book (savings cost-to-premium of 13.6% in Q1-FY2027); profitability is tracked as Value of New Business (₹26.29 billion in FY2026, margin 24.7%) and profit after tax (₹16 billion in FY2026). It sells almost entirely in India through banks, its own agents, direct channels and non-bank partners, and pays claims with a settlement ratio of about 99.3%.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Retail protection** — Pure life-cover (term) policies sold to individuals, described as the company's core focus area — it was about 10.5% of APE in Q1-FY2027 and grew 60%+ year-on-year for three straight quarters after the September 2025 GST exemption, with management saying only ~13% of the addressable population is covered, making it a multi-decade growth opportunity.
- **Group protection (credit life and group term)** — Life cover sold to institutions — credit life bundled with loans from lending partners (including microfinance/MFI borrowers) and yearly-renewable group term cover for employee/member pools; it grew 7.1% in FY2026 and 37.8% year-on-year in Q1-FY2027, and management underwrites it only when the risk-reward fits.
- **Linked savings (ULIPs)** — Unit-linked policies where the customer's savings are invested in market-linked funds and returns move with equity markets; it is the single largest savings line — 51.4% of APE in Q1-FY2025 — and its sales swing with market volatility, so the company is pushing higher sum-assured ULIPs for stability.
- **Non-linked savings (participating and non-participating)** — Traditional guaranteed or bonus-bearing savings policies, including the flagship par product 'Gold' aimed at customers wanting early income/liquidity; contribution has moved around with rates and competing fixed-return products, sitting at roughly ₹4.94 billion of APE (with annuity) in Q1-FY2027.
- **Annuity** — Retirement-income products that pay a regular pension to the customer, growing at roughly a 20% four-year CAGR and stabilised at about 7% of the retail mix, with demand tilting toward regular-premium over single-premium annuities.
- **Group funds / group savings business** — Fund-management-style savings products for corporate schemes (e.g. employee benefit funds), a smaller line that grew 26% in FY2026 and 42.2% year-on-year in Q1-FY2027.
- **Bancassurance** — Selling through bank branches — ICICI Bank plus other banks — with 53 bank tie-ups and access to over 26,400 branches; it contributed 29.8% of APE in FY2026.
- **Agency channel** — The company's own advisers/agents, over 2.42 lakh of them, contributing ₹26.86 billion of APE in FY2026 — a proprietary channel management is deliberately investing in via a micro-market branch strategy and analytics-driven agent productivity.
- **Direct channel** — Sales made straight to customers online and through company staff, ₹14.30 billion of APE in FY2026, with focus areas being the NRI segment through GIFT City and scaling the online channel.
- **Partnership distribution** — Sales through non-bank partners such as brokers, fintechs and lenders — over 1,500 partnerships — contributing 13.2% of APE in FY2026 and growing 23.4% that year.
- **Investments / shareholder and policyholder funds** — The in-house investment operation run by a Chief Investment Officer that manages the ₹3.34 trillion of assets backing policies, with a stated record of no non-performing asset since inception and a solvency ratio of 225.4% against a 150% requirement.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

*"Sells once, earns forever"*

**What the calls show:** Every call from Aug 2022 to Jul 2026 is built around the renewal book: persistency cohorts (13th/49th month), renewal premium, in-force sum assured of ₹48.06 trillion and AUM compounding from ₹2.3tn (Q1-FY23) to ₹3.34tn (Q1-FY27), with policyholder float funding the business. > "our total in-force sum assured stood at ■ 48.06 trillion, reflecting the trust reposed in us by our customers" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Regulator and fiscal policy repeatedly determine product economics: IRDAI's Oct-2024 surrender-value norms forced wholesale product and commission redesign, the FY2024 commission regulations moved cost ratios, the Sep-2025 GST reform drove retail protection growth of 50-60% for three straight quarters, and the accompanying loss of input tax credit is a named drag on FY2026-27 margins and operating assumptions. Solvency, Ind-AS, risk-based capital and even KMP compensation are regulator-set.

> "retail protection, grew by 60.4% year-on-year in Q1-FY2027, driven by the GST exemption on protection products" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Diversified product/channel mix absorbs individual rule changes, and management has repeatedly re-engineered products and commissions to neutralise regulatory impact*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 2 numeric fingerprints reinforce it.

**What the calls show:** Across the 18 calls the savings book visibly swings with equity and rate cycles — ULIP-led APE surged 78% in Q1-FY2025 when markets were strong, then linked APE grew only 1.6% in FY2026 'impacted by volatile equity markets', and non-par is currently losing to fixed deposits on sticker price. But this is flow business, not customer-capex equipment: recurring renewal premium, 84-89% 13th-month persistency and a ₹3.34 trillion AUM base cushion the swings, and protection growth offset the savings slowdown in FY2026-27.

> "Linked business APE grew by 1.6% year-on-year in FY2026, impacted by volatile equity markets" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/recurring-premium demand base with high persistency; diversified product and channel mix lets the company pivot between segments as the macro shifts*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 6 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between -4% and 9% (±3.8 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
