# CCL Products (I) Ltd. (CCL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSUMER GOODS*

CCL Products (India) Limited buys green (raw) coffee beans and processes them into instant/soluble coffee — spray-dried and freeze-dried — which it sells mostly business-to-business to overseas customers in bulk or as private-label packs that those customers sell under their own brand names. It also sells its own retail brand, Continental Coffee, in India, plus the UK retail brand Percol it acquired in July 2023. Management describes it as a cost-plus model where profit is earned as a roughly fixed EBITDA per kilo (about INR 130-140/kg recently), so earnings track volume growth rather than the sales figure, which moves up and down with green coffee prices; volume growth has run roughly 14-20% a year with FY26 group revenue of about INR 4,466 crore and net profit of about INR 388 crore. It manufactures in India (registered at Duggirala, Guntur district, Andhra Pradesh, plus units referred to as NCL and F&B) and in Vietnam, and is described as naturally hedged because it imports much of its coffee and exports the finished product.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Exports / international B2B (bulk and private label)** — Sells instant coffee in bulk and in private-label packs to overseas customers across North America, Europe, East Asia/China and other geographies; this is the bulk of the business — management said the domestic market used only about 10% of capacity in FY23, and in FY26 India was roughly INR 650 crore of about INR 4,466 crore group revenue.
- **Domestic branded business (Continental Coffee)** — CCL's own consumer coffee brand sold in India, now the number 3 player nationally (number 2 in some regions and platforms), at about INR 440 crore of branded sales in FY26 (up from roughly INR 210 crore in FY24) and INR 125 crore in Q1 FY27, growing fast off a small base and requiring ongoing brand investment.
- **Domestic bulk and private label** — Unbranded and own-label instant coffee sold to other Indian buyers, making up the remainder of the roughly INR 650 crore India gross turnover in FY26 (about INR 180 crore gross in Q1 FY27 with INR 125 crore of that branded).
- **Percol (UK retail brand)** — A retail coffee brand acquired in July 2023, relaunched in 2024 with new packaging, design and formulation, with supply shifted from a third party to CCL's own plants so the company can invest behind and grow it.
- **Product mix: spray-dried vs freeze-dried** — Two manufacturing routes with different economics — freeze-dried historically earned a higher EBITDA per kilo than spray-dried — with freeze-dried capacity added in Vietnam (commercial operations targeted for late 2024) and freeze-dried utilisation improving fast enough by FY26 that management is weighing more capacity or a strategic tie-up.
- **Manufacturing capacity and expansion** — Capacity was about 38,500 tonnes in FY22 with plans taking it past 70,000 tonnes, built out through Indian units and the Vietnam plant; as of May 2026 management says existing capacity is sufficient for the next two years of planned growth.

### The story in depth

CCL Products earns its 53-out-of-100 'Decent' rating on the strength of what it is doing in the market rather than what it owns in the way of protection. The clearest bright spot is share capture: the quality framework gives 'Gain in market share' its top score of 2, citing South urban share moving from low single digits to over 6%, a #3 national position, and double-digit shelf share at Reliance and DMart — and the multibagger screen independently flags 'Market Share Gainers' as a LIKELY FIT on the same evidence, alongside 18-20% volume growth. That share is being bought deliberately: advertising and brand-building are treated as investment, taking branded sales from INR210 crore in FY24 to INR440 crore in FY26. Management scores highest of all seven quality areas (0.8), and 'Corporate Culture' is the screen's only STRONG FIT, based on long-tenured family leadership, owning the FY24 miss of 14% against 18-20% guided rather than deflecting it, and declining to raise guidance after a 20% quarter. Underneath sits a steady operating business: about INR16 of operating profit per INR100 of sales with margins moving only about ±3 points a year, a 23% cash return on capital, and an end market described as in steady secular expansion with price-inelastic demand.

The awkward part is that the single trait producing most of the growth is also the trait producing most of the risk. CCL runs a cost-plus, back-to-back model where the price of green coffee is passed straight through and profit per kilogram is set by the market, not by CCL. That model is why 'Low Price Plus' registers as a PARTIAL fit — scale and low-cost Vietnam capacity won European accounts as Brazilian supply became uncompetitive — and it is also why 'Pricing Power', 'Toll Roads' and 'Cost to Replicate' all come back as NO FIT, with management itself conceding in May 2024 that it could not lift per-kilogram profit for two years despite trying, and putting the time to build competing capacity at only 1.5-2 years. The quality framework says the same thing from the other side: growth is marked down (-1) precisely because it is volume-led with profit per kilogram deliberately held flat, and Competitive Advantage is the only negative quality area at -0.33. The risk review then names the consequence directly — 'Good Enough Goods' is ELEVATED because CCL is largely the unbranded supplier behind other people's brands, commoditised enough that it buys generic product from smaller third parties to fill orders. 'Stakeholder Concentration' is ELEVATED for a related reason: the freeze-dried expansion was only sanctioned after assurances and underwriting from key clients, which is comforting on paper but shows how much rests on a few relationships. The Continental brand build and the geographic push into North America, East Asia and the UK are the deliberate answer to both, but the screen still rates 'Brand Strength' and 'Forward Integrators' as only PARTIAL — the brand is young and sub-scale outside India, and the UK acquisition stumbled badly enough to need a full relaunch.

The third thread is the balance sheet, which is where the record reads STRAINED rather than comfortable. Growth capex has been real and sustained — capacity heading from 38,500 tonnes toward 70,000-plus, new plants in Vietnam and India — but the returns arrived late: debt tripled and pre-tax profit fell in FY24, and utilisation had only reached 65-70% by FY26. Working capital is the framework's worst check at -2, with roughly 166 days of cash tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. That number is not an accident of poor housekeeping; it is the cost-plus commodity model showing up on the balance sheet, which is why 'Cyclicality' sits on WATCH — green coffee price swings drove FY26 revenue up 43% on about 19% volume, and the FY24 price spike froze customer contracting and pushed debt to roughly INR1,950 crore. To management's credit, the FY26 efficiency drive cut the working capital cycle by 80 days and net debt by INR750 crore, and that same clean-up is cited as evidence for the 'Low Price Plus' cost story. Two further WATCH items are really one question about asset mix: most installed capacity is spray-dried while demand keeps migrating toward freeze-dried and roasted blends, and freeze-dried lines are expensive and need to run continuously to pay for themselves. 'New Entrants' is ELEVATED from two directions at once — quick commerce reshaping distribution, and specialty brands attacking the premium end — though CCL is meeting the first of these on its own terms, with 20-25% of branded sales now online. Net of all this: a well-run, share-gaining, cash-generating business with a genuine consumer brand emerging, but one that currently sells a commodity on someone else's pricing terms and carries the working capital and debt that go with it.
