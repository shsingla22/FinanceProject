### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 4 strengthened, 2 weakened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Share gain is explicit and repeated across all four calls — generation +52% (Q2), +65% (Q3), +48% (Q4) and capacity from 7.74 GW to 14.6 GW against national demand growth of 0.9% in FY26 — and it is being won on contracted, remunerative capacity rather than by underpricing, though a large part came inorganically (KSK, O2 Power, Tidong, Maruti Clean Coal) funded by a ₹10,150 crore raise at ~4.95x net leverage. (The long view had said: Persistent, quantified share gain across all seven calls: capacity 7.7 GW (Sep-24) to 14.6 GW (Q1FY27), generation +52%/+60%/+58% while national demand grew 0.9-3.3%, 47% of India's Q3FY25 wind additions, largest private hydro IPP, and thermal PLF 73-78% vs country 65.8-68.7%. The caveat the pattern warns about is live — the growth is debt- and equity-funded, with PAT down year-on-year in several quarters and leverage at ~5x.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Regular named firsts are delivered — India's largest green hydrogen plant (3,800 tpa) at Vijayanagar, first 20 MW floating solar, agrivoltaics, a 5 GWh battery assembly plant that has already won a ₹440 crore external order — but no R&D spend is disclosed, and these are deployments of available technology rather than proprietary innovation funding pricing power. (The long view had said: There are named new capability launches each year — agrivoltaics, first 20 MW floating solar, 3,800 TPA green hydrogen, a 5 GWh battery assembly plant that won a ₹440 crore external order — but no R&D spend is disclosed, and the core technology (boilers, turbines) is acquired from GE and Toshiba rather than developed.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Revenue is annuity-like — 82% of Q3 FY26 power sales under long-term PPAs, a 25-year Utkal PPA, and a 14 GW under-construction book 'fully tied up' — but the pattern's actual mechanics (an upfront product sale with an attached service/AMC stream, advance billing, customer-funded negative working capital) are absent; a generator's counterparties are DISCOMs that pay in arrears. (The long view had said: Revenue comes from long-term PPAs and capacity charges, not from a front-end product sale with an attached service/licence annuity; there is no discussion of attach rates, renewals or customer advances, and the model is the opposite of capital-light — ₹1,30,000 crore of planned capex to 2030 and net debt rising from ~₹26,500 crore (Dec-24) to ~₹62,000 crore (Sep-25) with 62-96 debtor days (positive working capital).)
- **Forward Integrators**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Movement toward the end customer is real but narrow: ~3.3 GW (25% of installed capacity) is contracted directly with group-captive and C&I customers at a blended renewable tariff above ₹3.65/unit, rising to ~5.7 GW; the dominant integration direction in these calls, however, is backward into boilers, turbines, blades and batteries. (The long view had said: The integration runs the other way — backward into wind blades (Halol), boilers (GE Power), turbines (Toshiba JV stake raised to 10.7%), water/rail SPVs and battery assembly — while output still goes to DISCOMs and exchanges, with no own-channel or direct-customer economics.)

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Within this 12-month window promises are converted into delivery — Kutehr built in under six years and stabilised, Tidong commissioned in May 2026 versus an October 2026 schedule, 36% of the FY27 3 GW target achieved in Q1, and bad news (curtailment from evacuation constraints, soft merchant prices, the paused solar module plan) disclosed rather than buried; against that, leverage near 5x is not a conservative balance sheet, the CFO changed mid-year, and with no management history on file the multi-year record cannot be judged. (The long view had said: Old-call promises are visibly delivered in later calls — the 10 GW-by-FY25 target, Kutehr commissioned in under six years, Tidong commissioned ahead of its October 2026 schedule, 36% of the FY27 3 GW guidance already achieved by Q1 — and bad news is disclosed promptly and specifically (₹16 crore quarterly curtailment loss, KSK PPA back-downs, weak Sutlej/Ravi hydrology, the CERC BESS tariff rejection, and the decision to pause solar module manufacturing). Leadership under Sharad Mahendra has been stable; the balance sheet, however, is levered rather than conservative.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The output is an undifferentiated commodity electron sold at auction- or PPA-determined tariffs; across four calls there is no A&P spend, heritage/premium-pricing claim or brand-led share discussion — only group balance-sheet credibility in the ₹10,150 crore raise. (The long view had said: the calls are silent and the numbers are mixed (1 of 2 checks supporting).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Stressed on the long view, Stressed on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Merchant prices, coal costs and weather-driven demand swung materially across the four calls (DAM ~Rs3.92 to Rs3.86 falling through FY26, then Rs5.10 in Q1FY27; coal $110 to $91 to $81/t; national demand +0.9% in FY26 versus +8.5% in Q1FY27), and the company is mid-way through a very large capex cycle (Rs20,000 crore in FY27, Rs1,30,000 crore to 2030) funded partly by a Rs10,150 crore raise — but electricity is an operations-linked flow product and 82% of sales sit under long-term PPAs with open capacity cut from 8% to ~5%. (The long view had said: Earnings swing with merchant prices (DAM ~Rs 5.00 in Q3FY24 to Rs 3.71, Rs 3.86 through FY26, then Rs 5.10 in Q1FY27), imported coal ($116 to $81 to $113/t), hydrology (hydro generation -26% year-on-year in Q1FY27) and demand growth ranging from -1.6% to +8.5%, while net debt rose from ~Rs 26,500 cr to ~Rs 62,000 cr in a record capacity-addition boom with PAT down 27%/17% in several quarters. But demand is a flow product tied to consumption, not customer capex, and open merchant capacity has been cut from double digits to under 4%.)

**That worsened:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Electricity is undifferentiated and procured by intermediary DISCOMs through lowest-tariff reverse auctions, so no brand premium is defensible — management's own margin defence is contractual and operational (back-to-back short-term trades, PPA tie-ups, lowest-capital-cost build) rather than product-based, and the same commoditisation is now visible in its new battery business bidding for external orders. (The long view had said: Power is a homogeneous product sold through reverse auctions, so there is no brand premium to be undercut; the company competes on cost and is affirmatively lowering it — domestic coal from mines nearby, vertical integration into boilers, turbines and wind blades to reduce LCOE and capital cost.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
