### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 3 weakened, 7 unchanged.

*Foundation test: FAIL on the long view, FAIL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Named launches in every call with early revenue traction — Amstel Grande (recipe designed by a local R&D team for India, 6.4% ABV, zero sugar), Kingfisher flavors, Heineken Silver — and premium volumes up 27%/33%/24%/17%; but no R&D spend is disclosed and gross margin is flat-to-down (~42-44%), so innovation is not yet funding pricing power. (The long view had said: Named launches recur every year with revenue impact — Heineken Silver, Heineken Draught, Ultra Max, Amstel Grande, London Pilsner, Kalyani Black Label — but there is no disclosed R&D spend, no share-of-industry-R&D claim, and gross margins (42-44%) are not high enough to fund outsized innovation; it is portfolio/packaging extension rather than R&D dominance.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Share gains are claimed in all four calls but almost entirely within premium (up 27%, 33%, 24%, 17%) with no explicit share percentages given; overall volumes fell 3% in Q2 FY26 and the company lost ground in West Bengal, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu, so the gain is segment-level rather than persistent whole-market share capture. (The long view had said: Premium share gain is persistent and quantified across the whole window — premium volumes +14% (Q3FY24), +32% FY25, +17% in a down Q2FY26 — with share still 'mid-single digit and growing' toward a stated 35% ambition, and share gains in Karnataka even as the category declined double digits.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The stated strategy is premiumization, not low price with scale; the cost work (INR50 crore productivity programme, Mangalore brewery closure, fixed-to-variable conversion) is reactive margin defence under volume deleverage rather than a cost culture funding price leadership, with EBIT down 18% at H1 FY26. (The long view had said: There is itemised cost work — bottle-return improvement, Mangalore brewery closure, network optimisation, fixed-to-variable conversion, trade-spend streamlining (Oct 2025) — but it is reactive margin defence rather than a scale-based low-price model; the stated strategy is premiumisation, and EBIT margins remain thin/volatile (8.9% in Q1FY23, EBIT down 18% in H1FY26).)
- **Forward Integrators**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Route to market is structurally blocked by state control — sales go through government corporations (Telangana) and distributors (Maharashtra), with no owned stores, franchising or D2C; the closest move is a distribution-coverage redesign and visi-cooler placement in third-party trade. (The long view had said: Regulation bars own stores, franchising or D2C, but UBL is pushing toward the customer through route-to-market changes (taking over from agency partners in Tamil Nadu/AP), a redesigned Maharashtra distribution network targeting next-day delivery from 62% to 95%, and 37,000 owned visi-coolers in trade.)

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: In state-monopoly markets the government corporation is effectively the sole customer — Telangana, described as the largest profit market, forced a supply suspension over delayed pricing and unpaid dues, and UBL was still chasing receivables a quarter later. On the supply side it leans on third-party contract brewers (Wave Distilleries in UP; heavy reliance again when its own breweries flooded). (The long view had said: In corporation states the government is effectively the sole buyer and payer — Telangana left UBL chasing overdues ("we are still not out of the wood there") and forced a commercial decision to suspend supply — while route-to-market changes in Tamil Nadu/Andhra (~10% of volume) and consolidation among key glass-bottle suppliers created a supply glitch. Offsetting this, revenue is spread across many states and a broadening distributor/retail base.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Duty-driven price rises are pushing consumers down-market — economy-segment growth is repeatedly flagged as diluting mix in multiple states, and by Q2 FY26 management admits affordability is hurting part of the business while it has 'limited ability to price'. The premium end is holding, but the mainstream Kingfisher volume base is exposed to cheaper alternatives whenever the consumer price gap widens. (The long view had said: Affordability is management's self-declared number one concern: when economy prices rose in Karnataka the category fell double digits, and UBL is responding by pushing cheaper local brands (London Pilsner in Orissa, Kalyani Black Label in West Bengal) rather than defending price. Its premium share is still only mid-single-digit against cheaper and unlisted rivals.)
- **Cyclicality**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Beer is a repeat-purchase consumption product, not tied to any customer capex programme or commodity price cycle; the volatility management describes is weather and excise-duty driven (unusual monsoon in Q2 FY26, 3% volume decline) rather than a capital cycle, and management explicitly chose to keep investing through the downturn rather than pull back. (The long view had said: Beer is a flow/consumption product, not customer capex, and management keeps investing counter-cyclically (greenfield UP brewery, brand spend up 20% in a down quarter). But results still swing on barley/glass/aluminium input inflation and on weather — an unusual monsoon turned Q2 FY26 into a 3% volume decline and an 18% half-year EBIT fall through fixed-cost deleverage.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (ELEVATED); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
