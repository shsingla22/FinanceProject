### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.4 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Entry requires multi-year regulatory gauntlets and heavy capital — EIA reports, public hearings at Keni and Murbe, environmental clearances, concession awards — evidenced across every call, with no mention of discounting or price wars. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±3.6 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Very high barriers — capital intensity, multi-year environmental/regulatory clearances (EIA, public hearings, state pollution board) and scarce coastal concessions — but competitive bidding for each terminal tempers pricing rationality.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: Results are hard to separate from group parentage — group cargo rose to 54% of the mix in Q2 FY26 while third-party volumes fell 1%, and the Jaigarh/Dharamtar growth thesis rests on JSW Steel's Dolvi expansion, so the record reflects captive support as much as standalone operating skill. | The long view had said: Reported growth is materially attributable to non-skill factors — captive JSW Steel/group cargo at 54% of volumes, government privatisation awards, and acquisition consolidation (Rs 162 cr of a Rs 265 cr revenue uptick was Navkar) — so results overstate standalone operating skill.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Private Indian port capacity sits with a handful of players and the company is the only one building three greenfield ports simultaneously, but state-owned majors still hold over 50% of capacity and tariffs remain partly regulated, limiting pricing power over end customers. | The long view had said: The sector has a handful of large private operators, but concessions are won through open competitive bidding that sets aggressive price benchmarks, so pricing power against the awarding authority is limited.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Orderly senior succession is visible within the window — a new JMD/CEO in Jan '25 and a CFO handover by Oct '25 with Lalit Singhvi moving to Strategic Advisor and Board Member while Nagarajan J took the CFO role, indicating continuity rather than disruption. | The long view had said: Orderly leadership transitions (Maheshwari→Rinkesh Roy as JMD/CEO, Singhvi→Nagarajan as CFO) and broad ESOP usage, but the CEO was hired externally from the government port service rather than promoted from an internal pipeline.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Revenue is outpacing tonnage on mix rather than price — Q2 FY26 operational revenue rose 10% on only 3% volume growth, and full-year FY25 revenue grew 20% on 9% volume — driven by higher-yield third-party and liquid/container cargo, with tariff deregulation still only a draft proposal. | The long view had said: Growth is overwhelmingly capacity/volume-led with only a modest pricing component — of a Rs 265 cr revenue uptick, Rs 27 cr came from rate increases at SWPL/Ennore and the rest from acquisition and volume, though realisation per tonne did rise ~7% on richer service mix.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Network effects** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Weak. Now: Nothing two-sided or self-reinforcing exists; the 'pan-India network' described is physical asset scale and multimodal connectivity, which does not strengthen with each additional user. | The long view had said: The 'pan-India logistics network' is a scale/coverage build-out whose EBITDA depends on completing the chain, not a self-reinforcing two-sided network.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: One clear cyclical drag is disclosed — Paradip iron ore short 3.4 mt in H1 FY26 on weak seaborne export conditions — but management identifies no timing or catalyst for the upturn, while coal demand is already running strong rather than near a trough. | The long view had said: The seaborne iron-ore export cycle bottomed in H1 FY26 (7 mt of volume lost across east-coast terminals, 3.4 mt at Paradip) with management pointing to prices firming and shippers returning — a visible near-term upturn.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 2 could not be compared.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Toll Roads**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Port handling is a small slice of the delivered cost of coal, iron ore or containers yet is indispensable to the customer's operations, and the calls repeatedly show that relationship being locked in contractually — take-or-pay with JSW Steel on the 302 km slurry pipeline, an anchor customer signing the Jatadhar concession agreement, and a 30-year Kolkata container concession. Group cargo at 54% of the mix plus captive service to Dolvi steel underlines the non-negotiated, must-pass-through character of the revenue. (The long view had said: Port handling charges are a small fraction of the value of the steel, coal and iron ore moving through, and management repeatedly describes cargo as captive or sticky to the hinterland, with assured anchor cargo at Jatadhar; but there is no certification/standards or oligopoly-pricing language, and terminals are won through competitive bidding.)
- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Across the four calls management restates the same three priorities verbatim and then reports against them — Goa covershed completed on time (8.5 → 11 MTPA), slurry pipeline km-by-km progress on track for March '27, Keni/Murbe public hearings held as scheduled, Navkar turned profitable after acquisition — while disclosing bad news promptly and quantifying it (the 3.4 mt Paradip iron ore shortfall, MTM losses). The balance sheet stays conservative at 0.54x net debt/EBITDA and the stated logistics goal is explicitly framed on return on capital; the one soft spot is reiterating 10% FY26 volume guidance while H1 tracked 4%. (The long view had said: Positives are real — conservative balance sheet through the period (net cash in FY25, net debt/EBITDA only 0.75x by Sep 2025), upgrade to investment grade by S&P and Fitch, orderly CEO/CFO succession, and candid quantification of misses (the 3.4 mt Paradip iron ore shortfall, Jaigarh/Dharamtar declines) — but delivery has slipped against the 'we deliver what we commit' claim: Jaigarh LPG moved from Jan '26 to June '26 to FY27-end, Tuticorin slipped a quarter, and the reaffirmed 10% volume guidance came in at 4-5% through H1 FY26.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Third-party cargo share climbed from 40% to a record 52% (Q1 FY26) and capacity rose 170 → 177 MTPA with a path to 400 MTPA, backed by wins at SPM Port Kolkata, Tuticorin, JNPA and three simultaneous greenfield ports — but management never cites market share versus named competitors, and H1 FY26 group cargo growth of 4% (third-party volumes actually down 1% Y-o-Y in Q2) shows the gain is not yet persistent. (The long view had said: Persistent, quantified share gain across all nine calls: capacity from 170 to 177 mtpa today with a stated path to 288 by FY28 and 400 by FY30, third-party share rising 36% → 46% → 52%, plus wins at Murbe, Keni, Jatadhar, Kolkata Berth 7/8 and acquisitions (Fujairah, PNP, Navkar, NCR Rail) — and management stresses the 16% project-IRR hurdle rather than buying volume.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Brand Strength (QUANT SIGNAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Cost to Replicate (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Not comparable: Low Price Plus (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view); Innovation Dominance (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 2 worsened, 1 unchanged; 5 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Volumes are commodity-cargo driven (coal, iron ore) and the Oct 2025 call attributes a 3.4 mt H1 shortfall directly to the seaborne iron ore export cycle, while the Feb 2025 call ties the next leg of growth to JSW Steel's Dolvi 10→15 mtpa capex commissioning — i.e. customer capex, not just operations. Simultaneously the company is in a heavy expansionary phase (177→400 mtpa, ~INR5,500 crore FY26 capex, three greenfield ports at once), the classic over-investment setup. (The long view had said: Volumes are explicitly tied to commodity price cycles and to JSW Steel's production and capex: H1 FY26 growth was cut from ~10% to 4% by a 3.4 mt shortfall at the Paradip Iron Ore Terminal as seaborne iron ore prices fell below economic levels, and guidance for the rest of the year is openly conditioned on how the iron ore market pans out. Group-cargo growth is described as inherently lumpy, stepping up only when JSW Steel adds capacity (INR2,400 crore Jaigarh/Dharamtar expansion sized to the Dolvi expansion), and Jaigarh+Dharamtar volumes have been range-bound at 43–46 mt for years.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Group (JSW Steel) cargo was still 54% of Q2 FY26 volumes, Jaigarh and Dharamtar exist essentially to serve the Dolvi steel plant, and the newly acquired slurry pipeline is a single long-term take-or-pay contract with JSW Steel — analysts repeatedly frame volume growth around JSW Steel's utilisation and expansion. (The long view had said: JSW Steel is the anchor customer across the portfolio — Dharamtar is 100% captive and Jaigarh ~85–90% captive, group cargo was still 54% of volumes in Q2 FY26, and analysts repeatedly interrogate the divergence between JSW Steel production and port volumes. Growth capex, the slurry pipeline and Jatadhar's 'anchor customer' are all built around the same group relationship.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (QUANT FLAG); Good Enough Goods (LOW); Technological Innovation (NO SIGNAL); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL); Fashion Risk (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 8 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
