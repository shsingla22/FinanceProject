# KPIT Technologi. (KPITTECH) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

KPIT Technologies is an India-headquartered (Pune) software and engineering services firm that works almost entirely for the automotive and wider mobility industry — it writes, integrates and validates the software that goes inside vehicles, covering things like powertrain and electrification, vehicle middleware and electronic architecture, connected/cloud services, digital key and other in-car features, and after-sales systems. Its customers are vehicle manufacturers (OEMs) — passenger car makers plus, more recently, truck and off-highway/commercial vehicle makers — with whom it runs long, deep engagements; management ranks its top accounts as 'Diamond' and 'gold' clients and says Tier-1 suppliers were never a focus. It earns money by billing these clients for engineering programs, historically on a per-person time basis but increasingly on fixed-price contracts (66% of revenue in Q3 FY26 versus 59% a year earlier), with new business reported as deal wins/TCV each quarter; EBITDA margin has run around 18.5%–21%. It operates across three main regions — the USA, Europe and Asia (mainly Japan, Korea and India) — with a recent push into China, and it has bought companies (Somit Solutions, Technica, Caresoft, N-Dream) to add depth and reach.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Feature development and integration** — Builds and integrates individual vehicle software features for OEM programs; described as a core revenue line growing about 20% year-on-year in FY23, and the kind of hands-on delivery work that underpins the bulk of the business.
- **Architecture and middleware** — Designs the underlying electronic/electrical architecture and middleware layer of the vehicle's software; it was the fastest-growing practice in FY23 (up 73% year-on-year) and was boosted by the Technica acquisition, and management calls it a main growth driver alongside powertrain.
- **Cloud-based connected services** — Software connecting vehicles to the cloud and related connected-car services; grew 49% year-on-year in Q3 FY23 and was cited as the second-biggest contributor to growth in Q4 FY26.
- **Powertrain (including electrification)** — Software for how the vehicle is driven and powered — covering conventional engines, hybrids, electric and pilot hydrogen systems; a long-standing growth engine, though management says its growth is no longer the highest of the four areas.
- **Vehicle engineering** — Added mainly through the Caresoft acquisition, this goes beyond software into benchmarking and cost reduction of the whole vehicle — including what management calls one-of-a-kind 'software benchmarking' — aimed at OEMs' biggest pain point, cost.
- **After sales** — Solutions helping OEMs earn revenue over the vehicle's life rather than only at the point of sale; described as one of KPIT's fastest-growing areas and one where it has built strong solutions.
- **Trucks and off-highway / commercial vehicles** — A deliberate expansion beyond passenger cars decided in FY25, helped by the Caresoft acquisition; it grew 18% year-on-year in FY26 and led Q4 FY26 growth at 11.6%, versus passenger cars which had been the main engine earlier (up 26.4% year-on-year in Q2 FY25).
- **Solutions and products (the 'next pivot')** — Selling largely pre-built offerings that need only some per-customer customisation rather than pure people-based project work — management says this now accounts for 21% of the total pipeline and is the reason fixed-price mix and revenue per person are rising.
- **AI initiatives** — A newer investment area with two AI wins already in customer production programs, a Microsoft partnership (KPIT named a 'frontier partner'), an agentic-solutions agreement with a leading CRM company, and a dedicated CTO for AI; spending on it is over and above the roughly USD 3.8 million per quarter of other business investment.
- **Geographies (USA, Europe, Asia/China)** — Revenue is reported across three regions because most clients are global: Europe led Q3 FY26 deal wins followed by the USA, Asia (Japan, Korea, India) drove Q2 FY25 growth, and China is a new but strategically important market where KPIT has landed two OEM engagements that are still small in revenue.

### The story in depth

KPIT scores 60 out of 100 — "Decent," three stars — and the number comes from an unusual split. On people and stewardship it is close to the top of the framework: management scores 1.33 out of a possible 2, with candor rated 2 for naming a client's cancelled programs, admitting its own AI work is eating its services revenue, and flagging that middleware and Qorix fell short rather than burying it; founders have run the business for decades, a 31-year insider was promoted to COO, and attrition is the lowest it has ever been. The money side backs this up: ₹27 of operating cash for every ₹100 of capital in the business, ₹19 of operating profit per ₹100 of sales, margins moving only about three points a year, and almost no direct input costs, so nearly all of every ₹100 of revenue is available for people and profit. Those same facts are what make Corporate Culture a strong fit on the multibagger screen. But competitive advantage scores 0.0 and industry structure 0.17, and the persistence-of-growth check scores minus 2 for lumpy year-to-year growth — the business is well run and cash-generative, but the record does not show it is protected.

The clearest thread running through all three analyses is that KPIT's biggest strength and its biggest exposure are the same thing. Technology spending was raised from about 3% of revenue to over 5%, against under 1% for most peers, and it has produced named offerings year after year — Technica middleware, QORIX, N-Dream, Caresoft, phone-as-key, AI triage, a digital cockpit platform now sold to three carmakers heading to five. That is why Innovation Dominance is a strong fit and why the capex and R&D checks both score positive. The risk review reads the identical facts as elevated technology risk: the industry is mid-shift to software-defined vehicles and AI, management concedes AI is cannibalising its own services base, and the middleware and autonomous-driving bets disappointed. Spending ahead of the shift is the only sensible response, but it is being funded out of a margin the company does not control. The second thread is price. Offshoring, a fixed-price mix up from 59% to 66% and productivity gains hold EBITDA near 20.8%, described as best in the industry — the Low Price Plus pattern, but only a partial fit, because the same records show guidance landing at the low end specifically "due to cost reduction, which we have to give to our clients." Pricing Power is scored NO FIT for exactly that reason, and Good Enough Goods sits on the watch list: this is people-based engineering where the customer extracts the saving.

The third thread is concentration, which cuts both ways again. Deep, long-standing relationships with a handful of large carmakers are what create the replication barrier the Cost to Replicate pattern rests on — decades of production-programme know-how, familiarity with customers' own source code and architecture, certified test platforms — and what let the company chase a bigger share of each customer's wallet through 17 consecutive quarters of growth, 24%-plus organic growth in FY23 and around 20% in FY25. Those same relationships are why stakeholder concentration is rated elevated and cyclicality high: revenue is tied to carmakers' engineering budgets, one client's decision to cancel all new platform programmes moved the whole outlook in May 2026, and growth went from 44.7% in Q3FY23 to 1.5% with negative organic growth by Q3FY26. That stall is precisely why Market Share Gainers and Cost to Replicate are marked partial rather than strong — the patterns are visible in the history but not in the current numbers. Three further risks sit on watch rather than active: policy steering demand through tariffs, EV rules and the US ban on Chinese software; new entrants in the form of Chinese carmakers and platform owners who capture the biggest spend pool in autonomous driving; and the reset in electric-vehicle adoption that has forced powertrain work to be rebalanced. Fashion risk is low and resilience is judged sound, since demand is functional, contracted and repeat. Read together: a candid, founder-led, cash-generative business investing correctly into a genuinely expanding end market, with no demonstrated ability to defend its price or diversify away from the customers whose budget swings it rides.
