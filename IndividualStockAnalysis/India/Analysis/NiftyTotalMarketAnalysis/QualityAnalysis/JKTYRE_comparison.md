**Checks that improved:**

- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Domestic volume growth of 15-21% and TBR replacement growth of 15-22% ran far ahead of the 7-8% industry growth management itself guided to, with 200-240 dealer additions per quarter, indicating genuine share capture from a non-dominant base. | The long view had said: Volume growth is strong (domestic +16% in Q3FY26, +21% in Q4) but management conceded a peer grew ~25% on a similar base, so the trajectory shows participation in industry growth rather than clear share capture.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Capital intensity is a real barrier — incumbents commit thousands of crores for single-digit capacity adds — and pricing behaviour is rational, with staggered 4-7% hikes taken to recover input costs rather than discount wars, and no new entrants mentioned. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.4 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Capital intensity and import regulation keep new entrants out, but every incumbent is adding capacity simultaneously, which is a rationality risk rather than evidence of disciplined pricing.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: The benefit mix is well matched to both customer types — brand shops, premium PCR and digital campaigns for retail replacement buyers, and TCO/reliability plus OEM homologation for fleets and manufacturers. | The long view had said: The mix is sensibly balanced (63% replacement retail, 26-30% OE) and OE is served on approvals/TCO, but the retail side is sold on price and mix rather than intangibles, so the benefit match is only partial.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: Growth capex is high and rising across the year — Rs.1,400cr projects, then a Rs.1,130cr TBR/ASLTR/PCR expansion (+7% capacity), then a further Rs.4,980cr brownfield programme (+24% TBR/PCR) — alongside EBITDA up 25% and PBT crossing Rs.1,000cr, indicating capacity added into demand rather than maintenance. | The long view had said: Growth capex has been consistently large and rising across the period — Rs.1,100cr in FY24, Rs.1,130cr announced in Q3FY26 and a further Rs.4,980cr brownfield programme to FY29 — and is genuinely capacity-additive at >90% utilisation, though returns are only mid-teens (ROCE 16.8%) and it is largely debt-funded.
- **Advertisement and promotion** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: Consistent brand investment shows up as digital campaigns, exclusive brand shops and long-running ICOTY/IMOTY sponsorship, with external validation as a top-15 global tyre brand, though A&P spend is never quantified. | The long view had said: A&P appears mainly as a cost line and channel/brand-shop additions rather than as a measurable brand-equity builder, with no evidence of A&P translating into pricing power.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Export volumes grew and the Mexican Tornel platform improved materially (Q3 revenue +21% year-on-year, EBITDA +45%, new Brazil/LATAM push), but margins there remain thin at 7.6-9.4% and USMCA revision is an acknowledged risk. | The long view had said: The Mexico platform is durable but has gone nowhere over three years (Rs.2,147cr FY25 to Rs.2,138cr FY26, constant-currency flat) and exports remain ~10-11% of revenue growing 5%, so expansion is steady but not a premium brand travelling well.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: The Indian tyre market behaves as a stable oligopoly with industry-wide pricing discipline, but the OEM half of the business shows clear customer negotiating power since price increases only come through with a lag. | The long view had said: The Indian tyre market is a concentrated few-player industry protected from Chinese imports, but the customer clearly retains negotiating power given OEM RM indexation and admitted 4-5% under-recovery in replacement.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Good. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹12 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹12 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±2.4 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Good. Now: There is a credible and broadening technology thread — embedded smart tyres, patent filings, EV-specific approvals and AI in manufacturing — but no evidence of a decisive multi-product edge over global tyre majors. | The long view had said: There is a real product-development cadence (EV, smart tyres, OTR, sustainable materials) but it has not produced a differentiated pricing outcome, with growth still volume-led and margins RM-determined.

Now: JK is clearly gaining volume share, but the calls never identify weak competitors donating it, and its main rivals are financially strong domestic and global players. | The long view had said: No weak competitor is identified to take share from; the growth comparison in FY26 runs the other way, with a peer outgrowing JK partly on acquisition.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 8 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: All four calls show volume growth far above the 7-8% industry growth management itself guided to — domestic volumes +11%, +15%, +16%, +21% across Q1-Q4FY26, TBR replacement +7%/+22%/+15%/+19%, passenger line +16-18% — and it was won alongside expanding margins (EBITDA +25% for FY26, PAT 3.7x in Q3) rather than bought with profitability; JK Tornel likewise posted record Mexican sales. No explicit market-share percentages are given, however. (The long view had said: Volume growth turned strong by FY26 (domestic +16% in Q3, +21% in Q4; TBR replacement +19%, OE +53%) and Tornel claims the highest share in Mexican mass merchandise, but explicit domestic share numbers are never given and an analyst pointed out CEAT grew ~25% against JK's 15%, which management explained away rather than rebutted with share data.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Real but replicable barriers appear: OEM homologation with Tata Motors, Ashok Leyland, Mahindra, VECV and Hyundai, plus multi-thousand-crore capacity blocks that take years to build. But the calls also show a business that passes GST cuts through in full and chases raw-material inflation with lagged price hikes, indicating a well-funded competitor could contest the position rather than face an un-replicable barrier. (The long view had said: Nothing in the calls articulates why a funded entrant could not catch up — capacity is buyable (JK itself is adding Rs.6,110 crore of brownfield lines), protection from Chinese imports comes from regulation and anti-dumping duty rather than any company asset, and the Mexican business is described as exposed to peso moves, tariffs and Asian import influx.)

**That weakened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Management's stated strategic direction is premiumisation and mix-led realisation, funded by heavy capital-intensive expansion (Rs.1,130 cr plus a further Rs.4,980 cr brownfield programme to 2029) — not a no-frills, high-asset-turn low-price model, despite generic references to cost optimisation. (The long view had said: Cost optimisation is a persistent theme across the calls — fixed-cost reduction and digitalisation at Tornel, 'sustained cost optimization initiatives' in FY26, interest-cost reduction despite rising rates — and capacity utilisation above 90% shows asset sweating, but there is no itemised savings culture, no no-frills positioning, and the company competes on premiumisation rather than price leadership.)

*Unchanged: Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Tyres sit in the structurally exposed shape — the dealer intermediary largely steers the replacement buyer, and management's answer is continuous spend on brand (53 million digital impressions, 'top 15 strongest tyre brands globally'), dealer/brand-shop expansion and premiumisation to hold realisation. The calls do not, however, show any actual pricing squeeze from cheaper substitutes; realisation improved on mix and management pushed through 4-5% replacement hikes. (The long view had said: Pricing power is visibly conditional: in Q3FY25 management admitted 4-5% of cost increases remained unrecovered because it must 'abide by the competitive market scenario', and in Q3FY26 the 15% revenue growth was almost entirely volume with 'very minor' price increases. Cheaper Asian imports and, in Mexico, 'more cost-effective products to mass merchandisers' show the pull toward good-enough price points in a category where dealers heavily influence the end choice.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Margins are visibly commodity- and cycle-driven: EBITDA expanded 470bps year-on-year in Q3FY26 on 'benign raw material prices', then management guided raw material costs up 18-20% in Q1FY27 on the West Asia crisis, with staggered 4-7% price hikes to catch up; simultaneously the Board approved Rs.4,980 crore of brownfield PCR/TBR expansion (on top of Rs.1,130 crore already under implementation) explicitly because of current demand momentum — expansionary capex committed at a record-volume, record-margin point in the auto cycle, and OE volumes (+42% in Q4) are the swing factor. (The long view had said: Across the 11 calls, margins swing violently on raw-material and OEM cycles — consolidated EBITDA margin 12.5% (Q1FY24), 9.1% (Q3FY25 on natural rubber spike and carry-forward inventory), 13.8% (Q3FY26), with RM costs now guided up 18-20% in Q1FY27 on the West Asia crisis; demand is repeatedly framed around government/private capex cycles and CV OEM cycles, and the company is committing Rs.6,110 crore of expansion at record utilisation and record profits — textbook peak-cycle over-investment, debt-funded.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
