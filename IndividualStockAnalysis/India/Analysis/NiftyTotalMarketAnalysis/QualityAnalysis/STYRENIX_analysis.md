# Styrenix Performance (STYRENIX) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Styrenix Performance Materials is an India-listed (BSE 506222 / NSE STYRENIX) maker of styrene-based plastics — mainly ABS and SAN, which management groups under "Specialties", plus polystyrene (PS) — which it sells in bulk to manufacturers in the automotive, consumer durables, electrical and electronics industries, split between direct OEM customers and non-OEM/trade buyers. It buys commodity monomers (styrene, butadiene, acrylonitrile) from multiple suppliers globally, converts them into resin grades at its plants (Dahej among them), and earns money on the spread between monomer costs and finished-polymer prices, largely passing raw-material moves through into selling prices; volumes are reported in KT and FY26 standalone sales were about 195 KT on roughly INR 2,647 crores of income. It was acquired by the Agrawal family (Shiva group) in late 2022 and has since debottlenecked capacity, launched its own branded grades, and added a subsidiary in the UAE. In January 2025 it bought Styrenix Performance Materials Thailand (the former INEOS ABS/SAN business), which sells across Asia and lifts consolidated FY26 income to about INR 3,454 crores on 248.3 KT.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Specialties (ABS and SAN)** — Makes acrylonitrile-butadiene-styrene and styrene-acrylonitrile resins — sold under the Absolac (ABS) and Absolan (SAN) brands — for automotive, consumer durables and electricals; management calls it the higher-value, more profitable of the two Indian lines, it carried the larger share of segment profit in the FY23 disclosures, and over half its sales go to OEMs on validated, customer-registered grades.
- **Polystyrene (PS)** — A more commodity-like styrene plastic, the second Indian segment; capacity at Dahej was debottlenecked from 66 KT to 100 KT in September 2024 with minor capex, and OEM sales here — historically under 25% — have been pushed above 30%.
- **Value-added / new brands (STYROLOY, ASALAC)** — Newer higher-grade products — STYROLOY covers PC-ABS and Nylon-ABS blends, ASALAC is weatherable ASA for automotive and other uses — launched to improve product mix and margins; still a small but growing part of the mix.
- **Thailand subsidiary (Styrenix Performance Materials Thailand)** — An ABS/SAN plant acquired from INEOS in January 2025, selling across Asia; grades were switched from the old Novodur/Lustran brands to Absolac/Absolan with ~90% of customers retained, and it is still loss-making and pre-breakeven — management targets roughly 65-70% utilisation to break even, and it dragged consolidated FY26 margins (10.4% EBITDA) below standalone (14%).
- **UAE subsidiary** — A separate overseas entity set up around 2024; the calls mention it only in passing without detailing its size or role.

### The story in depth

Styrenix comes out of the three reviews as a genuinely well-run maker of styrenics plastics that sells into a growing Indian market, but one whose profits are ultimately set by raw-material prices it does not control. The quality framework scores it best exactly where the business touches its customers and its market: customer benefits full marks, growth 0.8, management 0.67. Customers are buying assured supply — annual contracts, multi-source raw-material buying that kept deliveries going through the Hormuz disruption — into an end market that is expanding on its own, with EV, consumer durables and PLI-driven demand adding 30-50KT of ABS demand a year against roughly 200KT of India's 350-370KT consumption still imported. Management has matched words to actions, which is why Corporate Culture is the single STRONG FIT in the multibagger screen: the PS debottlenecking from 66KT to 100KT was promised and completed, the ABS expansion stayed on track through FY26, and bad news — the Thailand inventory losses, weak GPPS demand — was disclosed without spin. The lower scores sit on the financial side, with capital allocation and return on capital both at 0.33, and the combined rating lands at 55 out of 100, or Decent: 64 on business quality, 78 on risk safety, and only 23 on multibagger fit.

The screen explains that low multibagger score in a way that ties directly to the biggest risk. Pricing Power is a flat NO FIT — management itself says contracts carry built-in raw-material formulas that are a pass-through, and ABS and PS prices moved up with the Middle East crisis and back down again, with FY26 standalone revenue falling 3.9% on pricing even as volumes grew. That single trait is also what makes Cyclicality the one ELEVATED risk: standalone EBITDA margin has swung from about 10.9% in Q3 FY25 to 19.2% in Q4 FY26, and nine-month FY23 pre-tax profit dropped to INR 188.7 crore from INR 312.5 crore. Because price cannot be defended, the company has to earn its returns through cost and mix instead, and it visibly does. The Low Price Plus pattern is a LIKELY FIT built on an itemised, repeated cost programme — debottlenecking PS with only minor capital spending, switching Dahej from natural gas to renewable fuel, third-party power, formulation changes — and the same discipline shows up in the quality checks as growth capex rather than maintenance capex, ₹1.20 of sales from every ₹1 of assets, 25% return on equity and 32% return on capital. On the mix side, in-house R&D with no licensor or royalty support has kept producing value-added grades (STYROLOY, ASALAC, PC-ABS and Nylon-ABS blends, FR grades, pre-coloured HIPS), and the payoff is visible in Q4 FY26, where EBITDA rose 51.9% despite volumes falling 4.6%. Those grades also create the only real stickiness the company has: qualification cycles of six months to two years, and 3-9 months for GPPS and HIPS approvals, which is what earns Toll Roads and Cost to Replicate their PARTIAL verdicts — real switching friction, but on a large commodity resin rather than a small, unavoidable one.

The risk review is largely the same set of facts seen from the other side. The huge import gap that makes growth look easy is also what is pulling competitors in: Supreme Petrochem announced ABS capacity on different technology in February 2023 and a third player had arrived by May 2026, which is why Market Share Gainers is only PARTIAL and New Entrants sits on WATCH — share gain here is structural import substitution, not competitive wins. At the cheap end of the range the pressure is already showing: low-priced GPPS imports into the unorganised trade have forced the company to consciously walk away rather than match prices, leaving newly expanded capacity under-used, which is the Good Enough Goods WATCH; the defence is the deliberate shift toward OEMs, where mix is rising in ABS above 50%, PS from under 25% to over 30% and HIPS to 50-60%, and where HIPS already runs near full utilisation and is sold out. The Thailand acquisition is where the tensions concentrate. It scores -1 on the merger check as sub-scale, below breakeven and margin-diluting — consolidated FY26 EBITDA of 10.4% against 14% standalone, with inventory losses through the brand changeover — and it is the main blemish on otherwise disciplined, organic brownfield stewardship. Yet the same deal supplies the Asian reach behind the market-share case, and retaining about 90% of customers through the switch off the INEOS Novodur and Lustran names is the strongest evidence for the PARTIAL Brand Strength verdict, even though brand still buys no price premium and no advertising budget is disclosed. The re-registration of every customer during that changeover is also part of why Stakeholder Concentration sits on WATCH, alongside imported styrene and acrylonitrile and locally sourced butadiene from a handful of producers. Balanced against that, technology risk and government dependency are both LOW and overall resilience is judged SOUND — so the honest picture is a capable, candid operator with a long runway and no control over its own selling prices.
