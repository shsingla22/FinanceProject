# Hitachi Energy (POWERINDIA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Hitachi Energy India Limited (listed as POWERINDIA) sells the heavy electrical equipment and control systems that move electricity from where it is generated to where it is used — transformers, high-voltage substations (both AIS and GIS types), HVDC long-distance power links, capacitors and reactors, plus the automation, protection and software that runs a grid. Its customers are power transmission utilities (central and state, such as inter-state networks and state boards like Gujarat's), renewable developers building solar and wind projects, industrial companies, railways and metros, infrastructure builders and, increasingly, data centres. It makes money mainly by winning large orders and executing them over time — the typical scope is 'design, engineering, manufacturing, supply, erection, testing and commissioning' — so orders build an order backlog (a record Rs 29,555 crore as of March 2026) that converts into revenue over several quarters, alongside product sales and services; FY26 fourth-quarter revenue was Rs 2,754.1 crore with profit after tax of Rs 330.5 crore. It is headquartered in Bengaluru with factories in Karnataka (Mysore, Peenya, Doddaballapur) and Gujarat (Vadodara/Halol), selling mostly across India, with some exports and projects in neighbouring countries such as Sri Lanka, Bangladesh and Bhutan.

**The parts of the business:**

- **HVDC (high-voltage direct current) links** — Builds long-distance, high-capacity power links, such as the 1,000 MW Kudus–Mumbai 'city infeed' project that lifts Mumbai's power supply by roughly 50% — a single order of this type (Rs 3,054.6 crore of inflows in the quarter it was won) can dominate a quarter's order book, and management repeatedly cites it as the flagship proof of its technology and execution.
- **Transformers (power and traction)** — Makes power transformers — the calls mark 25 years of power transformer manufacturing in India in October 2025 — as well as traction transformers sold to Indian Railways and private rail equipment makers; described as a long-standing core product line.
- **High-voltage products and substations** — Supplies and installs substations and high-voltage gear — 400/33 kV and 220/33 kV substations, 130 kV and 220 kV GIS, 765 kV reactors, capacitors and reactors for renewable grid connection — and this is the bread-and-butter 'base order' work that management says grew 73% year-on-year in Q3 FY26 once the lumpy HVDC order is stripped out.
- **Grid automation, control and protection** — Provides substation automation, control and protection systems, RTUs, telecommunication systems, enterprise software and network control — product lines management highlighted as less dependent on scarce semiconductors during the 2022 chip crunch and now pitched as 'digital service solutions'.
- **Services, consulting and modular solutions** — Offers digital services, consulting and newer packaged formats such as a compact mobile 400 kV substation for rapid deployment (sold to a leading Indian conglomerate in Kutch, Gujarat); described as a 'modular concept gaining traction' rather than a large revenue line.
- **Utilities / transmission (T&D) customer segment** — The largest demand pool, covering central and state transmission utilities plus distribution-company upgrade schemes; management points to state-level spending such as Gujarat's planned Rs 1 lakh crore transmission programme.
- **Renewables customer segment** — Grid connection work for solar and wind — examples include a 432 MW DC solar project in Bikaner, a 100 MW SECI solar project and a 300 MW solar project in Koppal — supported by India adding close to 50 GW of renewables in 2025.
- **Industry, rail, metro and infrastructure customer segment** — Direct sales to factories, chemical and oil-and-gas plants, railways and metros; industry orders doubled year-on-year in the June 2022 quarter, and rail modernisation is flagged as a large opportunity given the Rs 2.5 lakh crore earmarked for it.
- **Data centres** — A newer and, in management's words, 'significant growth opportunity' — the company commissioned a 220 kV GIS substation for a data centre in Pune — driven by AI-related power demand in India.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## The verdict: Decent — 61 out of 100 ★★★☆☆

In one breath: the quality framework finds a good business today (75/100); it strongly fits 2 of the 11 patterns long-term winners share; the risk review found 1 high risk (Government Dependency). Weighing those together gives 61 out of 100 — decent.

### The story in depth

Hitachi Energy India earns its "Decent" 61-out-of-100 rating on the strength of what it sells and who it sells it to. The quality framework's top marks go to technology (HVDC converter valves, 765 kV reactors, gas-insulated switchgear, grid automation), to high entry barriers in that high-voltage niche, and to an end market in secular rather than cyclical expansion — energy transition, roughly 50 GW a year of renewables, state and inter-state transmission spending, rail modernisation and AI data centres. Two multibagger patterns fit outright and rest on the same foundations. Cost to Replicate is concrete: more than half the world's installed HVDC base runs on this technology, about 80% of the global portfolio is made in India, and the new large-power-transformer plant deepens that. Corporate Culture is the other strong fit — the same managing director and finance chief across all 16 calls, bad news itemised plainly in FY23 when pre-tax profit fell to Rs 2.1 crore on Rs 991 crore of revenue, and the promised move to sustainable double-digit margins delivered. Market Share Gainers is a likely fit, supported by FY26 revenue up 27.6%, an order backlog up 53.6% to Rs 29,555 crore, and exports holding a quarter of orders for four straight years across Australia, Canada, Portugal, Greece, Iraq and elsewhere. Financing is clean throughout: customers pay 42 days before suppliers need to be paid, borrowing costs are 0.1% of revenue, and the Rs 2,000 crore Karjan plant plus the earlier programme — roughly Rs 4,000 crore cumulative against a prior maintenance run-rate near Rs 100 crore a year — is being funded with equity and organic growth, no acquisitions.

The more interesting finding is that several of these same traits are simultaneously the source of the biggest risks, so the strengths and the warnings are not separate lists. The company wins work by bidding directly into utility and industrial tenders, with no intermediary recommending it and no small, repeat, low-stakes purchase pattern — which is precisely why the Friendly Middleman and Toll Roads patterns are marked NO FIT, and equally why Government Dependency scores HIGH RISK: the demand story in nearly every call runs through Union Budget allocations, discom schemes, PowerGrid and state utility tenders, and tariff-based competitive bidding. The same tendering also splits the portfolio in two. At the high end, technology and track record decide the winner; in transformers, switchgear and standard substations, price decides, which is why Good Enough Goods is ELEVATED and why Brand Strength and Innovation Dominance land only as PARTIAL — the heritage and the technical lead are real, but there is no evidence of charging more and gaining share at the same time. Pricing power is likewise PARTIAL because it is contractual rather than reputational: over 70% of the backlog carries price-escalation formulas, which is what carried margins through the FY23 commodity and semiconductor squeeze. Finally, the large-project shape of the business cuts both ways — the Rs 29,555 crore backlog gives multi-year visibility, but the same reliance on a handful of very large contracts, with the Mumbai and Adani HVDC work dominating order books and commentary for years, is what makes Cyclicality ELEVATED and keeps Stakeholder Concentration on WATCH despite a genuinely broad customer list.

Read together, the picture is a technically strong company operating in a structurally growing market, whose economics are set less by brand and more by the tender it happens to be bidding. That shows up in the scoring: competitive advantage is the highest-rated quality area, while return on capital is the lowest and industry structure is below average, and the middle-of-the-road multibagger fit (48) and risk safety (52) scores pull the overall down to 61. Management's response to the pressures is visible and consistent rather than defensive — building the Chennai and Karjan plants ahead of demand while openly acknowledging a temporary industry slowdown, expanding exports and a separate global Service business for lifecycle ownership, and shifting the mix toward private industrial buyers, who were 43% of Q3FY26 orders against 47% for utilities. Whether that response works is the whole question. The risks rated LOW — technology change, changing preferences, fashion — are low for a shared reason: this is mandated, functional grid equipment, and the one big shift in play, decarbonisation and electrification with AI-linked loads, adds to demand rather than replacing it. Resilience is judged SOUND. The realistic path to a better verdict runs through proving pricing holds as new domestic transformer capacity arrives, converting the record backlog into steadier profits, and turning the new service business into genuinely repeating revenue.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** Same MD (N. ### Cost to Replicate — STRONG FIT
*"Could a newcomer copy this?"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** The replication barrier is concrete and articulable: over half the world's installed HVDC base runs on this technology, ~80% of the globally manufactured portfolio is localised in India including HVDC converter valves and transformers, and the new large-power-transformer greenfield at Karjan will itself take until Q4 CY2028 to build — a well-funded entrant would need the reference list, the qualification history with utilities, and multi-year factory lead times before it could bid the same projects.

> "today, we are manufacturing more than 80% of our globally manufactured portfolio, we manufactured locally here" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Market Share Gainers — LIKELY FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern, but the numbers push back (0 of 1 check supporting) — held one step below a full confirmation.

**What the calls show:** Growing faster than the market is a claim made consistently from Jul 2022 through FY26 and backed by numbers — FY26 revenue +27.6%, backlog +53.6% to Rs 29,555 cr, exports at 25-27% of orders with wins in Australia, Canada, Portugal, Greece, Iraq and a 'global feeder factory' model — and crucially the share was not bought with profitability, since PBT margin doubled to 16.2% over the same period.

> "helping us to grow higher than the market continuously for the last several quarters" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Sales compounding at 12% a year — below the 12% pace the share-gainer pattern looks for.

*"Sells once, earns forever"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (1 of 3 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Service is consistently named a 'value creator' alongside exports (10% year-on-year service growth in Q1FY23, service orders from PSTCL/Vardhman/data centres), and by FY26 a separate global Service BU was created with data-centre customers wanting a 'life cycle partner' — but no attach rates, renewal rates, AMC revenue share or deferred-revenue-as-funding disclosure appears in any of the 16 calls; cash generation (FY26 OCF Rs 1,746 cr on Rs 8,148 cr revenue) is discussed as execution, not customer float.

> "looking not only supplying of our technology, be it our product system services but also life cycle partner" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Customers effectively fund the business — cash arrives 42 days before suppliers are paid.
- ❌ Capital spending is about 8% of sales — growth is capital-hungry.
- ❌ Operating cash was positive in 5 of 8 years — not fully consistent.

### Pricing Power — PARTIAL
*"Raises prices, keeps the customers"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 2 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** This is conditional/contractual pricing power rather than brand pricing power: over 70% of the backlog carries price-escalation formulas that protected margins through the FY23 commodity and semiconductor squeeze, and in Feb 2026 Venu argued that even with announced industry capacity additions 'there is a gap to close' so pricing holds — but prices are set in competitive tenders and FY23 showed margins collapsing to ~1.5% PBT when costs ran ahead.

> "more than I think 70% of our portfolio is having a price escalation" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹44 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — limited pricing headroom.
- ❌ Operating margin moved from about 8% (early years) to 7% (recent years) — no widening trend.

### Brand Strength — PARTIAL
*"The name itself sells"*

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, Cyclicality, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.0× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 246% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 19% to 22% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** The demand narrative in almost every call is policy-driven: Union Budget allocations to MNRE, RDSS discom schemes, green hydrogen outlays, PowerGrid and state transmission utility (GETCO, MP Transco, PSTCL) tenders, tariff-based competitive bidding, plus duty and FTA changes (EU-India, US-India) framed as order opportunities. Utilities were still ~47% of Q3FY26 order intake, and the business is anchored in fixed transmission infrastructure — exactly the profile the channel warns about.

> "the government in Union Budget 2024 allocated Rs. 19,100 crores to the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy against the revised estimate of Rs. 7,080 crores" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Steady diversification away from PSU tenders — industries at 43% of Q3FY26 orders versus utilities 47%, private customers (Adani, ReNew, data centres, chemicals), and exports consistently 23-27% of orders across markets from Australia to Canada and the Middle East*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is tied almost entirely to customers' capex — HVDC links, transmission substations, renewable grid connections, rail/metro and data-centre build-outs — and the P&L has swung violently across the 16 calls: Q1FY23 PBT was Rs 2.1 crore on Rs 991 crore revenue with margins crushed by commodity, freight and semiconductor costs, while FY26 delivered 15.4% EBITDA and PBT up 166%; management itself attributes quarterly revenue declines to 'the cyclical pattern' and to order timing, and Q4FY26 acknowledges a 'temporary industry slowdown'.

> "there was a decline of 21.9% quarter-on-quarter, because of the cyclical pattern which has impacted and further impacted by the revenue mix" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Rs 29,555 crore backlog giving multi-year visibility; ~70% of backlog carries price-escalation formulas; growing service/export/digital mix; and countercyclical investment — Rs 2,000 crore extra capex approved in Q4FY26 during the slowdown, taking cumulative capex to ~Rs 4,000 crore*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 6 years with swings of ±13 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 6% and 10% (±1.5 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 3% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 8%).

## What to watch
