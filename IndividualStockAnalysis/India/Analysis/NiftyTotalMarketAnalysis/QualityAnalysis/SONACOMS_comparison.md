### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Q4 FY26 shows contractual commodity pass-through but explicitly flags unpassed costs and lag pressuring margins going forward, with no evidence of price increases above cost inflation; this reads as conditional/contractual recovery rather than genuine pricing power. (The long view had said: Across the whole 2022–2026 arc pricing is a lagged cost pass-through, not price-setting: Aug 2022 margins hit a six-year low on steel inflation even 'after cost pass-throughs', and May/Jul 2026 repeat the same story with commodity, freight, wage and energy inflation compressing EBITDA margin from 27.4% to 24.7%. No price increase above cost inflation, premiumisation or scarcity language appears.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The Jan 2026 annual share review says share was 'broadly held' globally with gains only excluding China, though Q4 FY26 added four driveline orders including three from Europe — the first EV win there in four years — so gains are emerging but not yet persistent in the numbers. (The long view had said: Share gain is demonstrated repeatedly against a stated market baseline: +18% organic growth while global light vehicle sales fell 17% (Aug 2022), +22% while its three largest markets grew 3% (Jul 2024), +47% with BEV revenue +22% while US EV sales fell 28% (May 2026); EV programmes grew from 36 across 23 customers to 67 across 35, with explicit European share gains and leadership in 2W/3W traction motors.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across all four calls management discusses order books, programs and nominations, but never license fees, AMC/service revenue share, renewal rates, attach rates or customer advances as a funding source. (The long view had said: No licence/AMC/subscription stream is ever discussed across the 18 calls; the only recurring-economics element appears in Jul 2026, where the DENSO JV structure gives Sona reciprocal royalty income on its 2W/3W traction technology. Revenue is otherwise programme-based order-book conversion (₹237bn net order book), not billed-in-advance recurring service revenue.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 3 worsened, 3 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Policy actions repeatedly and materially moved results in this window — China's heavy rare-earth export ban halted traction-motor production in Q1, US tariff policy and its 5-year USMCA relief extension were discussed in three calls, US EV subsidy cuts were called 'a setback' for that market, and the Haryana minimum-wage rise and gas availability hit costs; the newly acquired Indian railway business also depends on government approvals to supply. Bounded because pricing is set commercially with private OEMs, not by a regulator or tariff, and railways is one segment. (The long view had said: Demand for the growth engine is materially policy-set, exactly the European-solar mechanism: management attributes the EV two-wheeler shortfall to 'FAME subsidy and the subsequent fallout on demand', the North American BEV setback to 'the US subsidy cuts for EV', and cites US tariffs and the rare-earth magnet ban as FY26 shocks; PLI income was worth 1.9% of quarterly EBITDA margin (its lapse created a negative base effect), Haryana minimum-wage and labour-code changes cost ₹30 crore, and the newly acquired railway business is anchored in state-procured fixed infrastructure with a structurally lower margin.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: The calls affirmatively show pricing power rather than premium erosion: EBITDA margin was sustained at ~25% (FY26 full year 24.7%) at a time management notes 'global auto component industry peers and bigger companies are talking about weak demand and margin compression', and share was held or gained in the core products while cheaper European rivals went insolvent. No discussion of customers substituting toward lower-priced alternatives. (The long view had said: Sona bids against large global Tier-1s and admits it does not win on price — 'we weren't the best commercially' — implying continuous price competition, and the FY26 margin slide (27.4% to 24.7%, with a further 0.8% from mix and commodity inflation) reflects growth in structurally lower-margin traction motor and railway lines. The offsetting protection is real, however: the buyer is an engineering-literate OEM running process audits, not an unaware end-customer served through an intermediary, so the Nobel Biocare mechanism does not map cleanly.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The business sits inside the powertrain technology transition: management discusses electrification, hybrids, rare-earth-free motor architectures and robotics/physical AI in every one of the four calls, and admitted a structural product gap — 'we did not participate at all in high voltage electric as well as hybrid powertrain systems' — that required a JV with Denso to fill. Legacy exposure is real (starter motors are an ICE-era format they still track share in), and three direct European competitors filed for insolvency in six months, evidence of how destructive this transition is for incumbents. (The long view had said: The company sits inside a large-scale powertrain re-platforming: ICE-dependent revenue collapsed from 27% to 9% over the period, its legacy starter-motor/ICE gear base is being made obsolete, and analysts repeatedly force management onto the BEV-vs-hybrid question. Management itself admits a total capability gap in high-voltage EV and hybrid systems that it had to close via the FY27 DENSO JV, and is now betting on yet another technology wave (robotics/physical AI, ADAS radar) — an industry with a very high innovation rate where incumbents' installed infrastructure keeps needing replacement.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Two consecutive calls attribute the entire EV revenue decline to one customer and largely one model — BEV revenue fell 17% quarter-on-quarter and 21% in H1, forcing a restatement of the order book — and the sole source of heavy rare-earth magnets (China) was concentrated enough to stop motor production outright. Management is also structurally barred from discussing customers, so investors cannot size the top-customer dependence. (The long view had said: Results have repeatedly hinged on a handful of relationships and programmes — a single model forced 'a significant correction to the order book' in Q2 FY26, one North American customer's re-award lifted order value by 170%/₹6.8 billion, and management refuses all customer-specific questions, which limits visibility. The trajectory, though, is clearly toward broadening: from 36 EV programs/23 customers in FY23 to 67 programs/35 customers in FY26, and top-4 products at 86% of revenue widening to top-8 at 86%.)
- **New Entrants**: NO SIGNAL → LOW. Why now: Rather than facing a structurally different entrant resetting economics, the calls show the opposite: three direct European competitors entered insolvency, the RFQ pipeline is roughly 3x year-ago levels, and the company broadly held or gained global share in differential gears and starter motors while sustaining ~25% margins. The one adjacent threat — Chinese supply chains — is only noted as a market where they have minimal presence, not as a competitive attack. (The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and all 2 computed fingerprints are clean.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
