# Piccadily Agro (PICCADIL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Piccadily Agro Industries is an Indian alcoholic-drinks maker that started in 1994 as a sugar mill at Indri in Karnal district, Haryana, and later added a distillery (2007) and a malt plant (2010) next to it. Today most of its money comes from its "Alco-Bev" (alcoholic beverage) business: it distils its own malt and grain spirit and bottles it into its own premium branded liquor — the Indri single malt whisky, Camikara rum, Cashmir vodka and Whistler blended whisky — while also selling ethanol and cheaper country liquor in bulk, plus sugar from the original mill. It sells through state liquor retail channels in 29 Indian states and union territories, the defence/CSD and paramilitary canteen network (about 30% of branded sales), and exports to 29 countries and 30 duty-free outlets (about 25%). FY'26 standalone revenue was about INR1,143 crores (Alco-Bev INR908 crores, up 42%) with PAT of INR140 crores, and management is demerging the sugar business into a separate listed entity so the company can be a pure global spirits player.

**The parts of the business:**

- **IMFL / branded Alco-Bev portfolio (Indri, Camikara, Cashmir, Whistler)** — Its own bottled premium and luxury spirits — Indri Indian single malt (launched 2022, grew from 15,000 cases in FY'23 to over 100,000 in FY'24, up ~16% in FY'26 on supply constraints), Camikara rum, Cashmir vodka (launched May 2025) and the revamped Whistler blended whisky — and this is the profit engine, with EBITDA margins of roughly 45%-50% and management saying FY'27's targeted 60%-70% growth will come "mainly through IMFL only."
- **Distillery – ENA, ethanol and country liquor** — The rest of the Alco-Bev segment: bulk extra neutral alcohol, ethanol and country liquor made at the distilleries, with total capacity now 450 KLPD (420 KLPD ENA/ethanol plus 30 KLPD malt) after the Indri expansion in October 2025 and the new 200 KLPD Chhattisgarh plant commissioned December 2025 — management says it does not sell malt in bulk at all, keeping all of it for its own brands.
- **Malt distillation and barrel maturation (Indri, Haryana)** — The upstream engine behind the whisky brands — malt capacity raised from 12 to 30 KLPD and about 85,000 filled barrels ageing today, going to 100,000 this year, with the local climate swinging from 0°C to 50°C cited as what gives the spirit its distinctive flavour; a Scotland distillery is planned (land and licences in place, construction starting, a couple of years out).
- **Sugar business (being demerged)** — The original 1994 sugar mill, described as a "traditional heritage business," which is seasonal and low-margin (EBITDA fell from 11% to 2% in the March quarter and dragged down group margins) and is being hived off into a separate listed entity — the scheme was filed with SEBI, KPMG is advising, and completion is targeted in FY'27.
- **Distribution and channels** — Sales run through domestic retail in 29 states and UTs (roughly 45%-50% of branded revenue) plus HORECA (hotels/restaurants/bars, where a dedicated team has been rebuilt), the CSD and paramilitary canteen network (~30%), and exports to 29 international markets and 30 duty-free outlets (~25%), with a stated ambition to take exports to about 50% within 3-5 years.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** The legacy sugar mill is a commodity, season-driven business whose EBITDA margin collapsed from 11% to 2% year-on-year in Q4 and dragged consolidated margins down ~300bps; power/fuel costs also spiked on husk commodity inflation. The alco-bev core is a consumption/flow product and management is demerging sugar, which bounds the exposure.

> "sugar margins drop, which was 11% EBITDA margin last year and last year same quarter and 2% this year quarter" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Demerger of the sugar business filed with SEBI; premium IMFL is a repeat-purchase consumption product, not customer-capex linked*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 11 years with swings of ±14 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 7% and 23% (±5.7 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 30% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 5% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.
