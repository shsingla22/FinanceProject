**Checks that improved:**

- **Distribution to shareholders** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: Consistent and rising cash distribution (interim INR 9.77 plus final INR 29.59 per share, dividends every year since listing) is a coherent policy, but there is no buyback programme and therefore no evidence of valuation-aware repurchase. | The long view had said: Dividend is paid (₹29.59 final for CY25) but cash has compounded from ₹3,100 crore in 2022 to ₹7,600 crore in 2026 with no buyback and no articulated distribution policy for the growing surplus.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Good. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹15 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±2.8 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹15 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±7.3 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Good → Excellent. Now: A multi-product technology edge is evidenced across divisions in a single year — rare-earth-free IE5 motors, next-generation machinery drives, ABB Ability SCADAvantage digitalisation and a locally built wind power converter — backed by the new R&D investment. | The long view had said: Genuine multi-product technical depth localized from a 140-year-old innovation base with a new R&D investment, but the underlying technology is the parent's and R&D intensity is never disclosed.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.8 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Seventy-five years of local manufacturing, five plants, 25 dedicated shop floors and deep localisation create material entry barriers, and no price war or irrational discounting is mentioned across the four calls. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±7.3 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Barriers are real (75-year manufacturing base, QCO certification, technology), but pricing rationality broke down in 2026 with competition intensity explicitly costing roughly a percentage point of margin.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Management openly reports the negative headline order growth, the profit decline and the market slowdown rather than hiding behind base orders, though it softens the setback as 'momentary'. | The long view had said: Repeatedly owns shortfalls with quantified bridges — the 2022 sub-10% PBT admission, the group-vs-India order reconciliation, and the Q1 2026 margin miss broken into competition/commodity/mix.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Commentary is largely operational and process-focused, but the calls also lean on promotional showcasing such as the 8,500% total-shareholder-return slide delivered from the NSE boardroom. | The long view had said: Understated, engineering-first tone with repeated insistence that sustainability and CSR are done for substance rather than presentation, and no personality-driven promotion.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Only generic team praise and external CSR skilling are mentioned; the same business heads recur across all four calls, but no systematic internal talent-pipeline programme is described. | The long view had said: Evidence of talent building through women's engineering scholarships with internship/mentorship, skilling CSR and a stable divisional presidents' bench across four years, but no explicit succession or pipeline framework.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Much of the celebrated record (39% PBT CAGR, 8,500% TSR) coincides with an unusually strong post-COVID Indian capex cycle and rests on parent-supplied technology, so results cannot be cleanly attributed to managerial skill on this evidence. | The long view had said: Management itself separates cycle from skill by attributing past margin expansion partly to post-COVID pent-up demand and QCO-driven supply advantage, letting the reader discount the halo from 2023-24's 39% PBT CAGR.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 8 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Every one of the four recent calls names new products with commercial impact — SCADAvantage for THINK Gas, IE5 motors, a next-generation machinery drive and new energy-efficient drive line, ARTU Formula switchgear, and the first locally made wind power converter — backed by a stated $75 million manufacturing-and-R&D investment in Q1 CY26. (The long view had said: Named launches appear regularly across the years — ACH180 compact HVACR drive (Q1 2024), next-generation machinery drive and energy-efficient drive line (2025), ARTU Formula LV platform and first locally made wind power converter (Q1 2026), plus a $75m manufacturing/R&D investment — but R&D spend is never quantified or shown as a rising share, and margin gains are attributed to mix and cost, not innovation.)

- **Brand Strength**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Heritage is stated repeatedly and cannot be copied — a 140-year-old group, 75 years manufacturing in India — and customers are said to see ABB as best-in-class, but the recent calls disclose no A&P spend, no premium-price-plus-share-gain proof, and no brand-stretch launches. (The long view had said: The heritage claim is repeated in every call (140-year group, 75 years manufacturing in India) and converts into demonstrated premium positioning and best-in-class supplier status with hyperscalers, railways and metals majors — including in Tier 2/3 cities where 'aspirational' buyers still demand the brand.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Across all four calls management frames demand in cycle terms — the market 'taking a breather' after five strong years, large orders explicitly 'a bit cyclic' (orders -3% in Q3 CY25, then +52% in Q4 and +25% in Q1 CY26), and revenue tied to customer 'capex formation' in data centres, railways, metals and renewables, i.e. equipment sold into customers' capacity expansion rather than their operations. Large orders are 30-35% of backlog, so quarters flip from 52% order growth to subdued on decision timing.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
