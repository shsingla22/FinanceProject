# MSTC (MSTCLTD) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

MSTC is an Indian government-owned company (a "Govt. of India Enterprise", listed on BSE/NSE) that runs online auction and e-commerce portals on behalf of other people who want to sell or buy things — mainly scrap and end-of-life vehicles, iron ore and other minerals, coal blocks and coal linkages, timber and forest produce, land and property, and even telecom spectrum. Its customers are almost entirely government bodies and public-sector companies (Department of Telecom, Coal India, NMDC, DGFT, CPCB, state governments like Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Tamil Nadu, development authorities such as HMDA) plus some private principals and temple trusts. It does not usually own the goods; it earns a service fee/commission on the value transacted through its portal — the calls describe roughly INR 797 billion of gross value transacted in FY26 producing about INR 359 crores of revenue from operations, and management calls it an "asset light" business. Alongside the auction platform it builds and operates custom software platforms for government departments (SIMS for the Ministry of Steel, a gold-bullion quota allocation platform for DGFT, an EPR certificate trading exchange for CPCB), and it is deliberately exiting its old physical trading business to become purely an e-commerce/platform company.

**The parts of the business:**

- **E-commerce (E-auction / E-sale)** — The core business — running online auctions and sales of scrap, minerals, vehicles, coal blocks, property and other assets for government and corporate clients in return for a fee; it is by far the biggest and fastest-growing part, at roughly INR 330 crores of standalone e-commerce revenue in FY26 and the segment management says now drives the whole company.
- **E-procurement** — The mirror service — online tendering/buying platforms for clients who need to purchase rather than sell; the calls consistently show it as a very small line (a few crores a quarter, e.g. Rs. 2.80 crores in Q1 FY25).
- **Marketing / trading** — An older business of buying and selling material on its own account, which shrank drastically (from INR 112 crores to INR 10 crores in a single Q1 comparison) and which management said in FY26 it is in the final stages of exiting, down to INR 1.49 crores.
- **Other income** — Interest and similar non-operating income on the company's cash and float, which is material to profits — around INR 20 crores in a quarter and INR 121.83 crores of "others" in FY26 total revenue.
- **Scrap Recovery and Allied Jobs (FSNL, 100% subsidiary)** — Ferro Scrap Nigam Ltd, a wholly-owned subsidiary doing physical scrap recovery work at steel plants, contributing around INR 100-122 crores of quarterly consolidated revenue until the government strategically disinvested it, which produced a one-off exceptional gain of about INR 273.5 crores in FY25-26.
- **MMRPL (50:50 joint venture with Mahindra)** — A vehicle-recycling/scrapping joint venture that has been loss-making and dragging down consolidated profit; management closed its high-cost Noida unit, opened one at Kalyan, and reports the losses are shrinking each quarter.
- **End-of-life vehicle (ELV) scrapping platform** — A dedicated portal matching buyers and sellers of old government and other vehicles for scrapping — volumes were small early on (about 1,459 vehicles in a quarter in FY23) but management says ELV sales have since picked up as the ecosystem improves.
- **Custom government software platforms** — Building and running bespoke portals for ministries — SIMS 2.0 for the Ministry of Steel, a Chhattisgarh timber auction portal, and a DGFT platform allocating tariff-rate quotas for gold bullion imports — described as a "blue-sky" growth area next to the auction business.
- **CPCB Electronic Trading Platform for EPR certificates** — India's first exchange for trading Extended Producer Responsibility certificates, built for the Central Pollution Control Board, developed and tested by FY26 and awaiting launch; management calls it a future "bread earner" that could open a whole new exchange-operating vertical.
- **MSTC Smart Travel portal** — A new unified B2B (later B2C) travel, accommodation and logistics booking platform in final testing, expected to launch around April/shortly after FY26 with revenues starting the following year.
- **Transaction advisory (tie-up with SBI CAPS)** — A newer end-to-end advisory service for realising value from assets of PSUs, government departments and private entities, combining MSTC's auction expertise with SBI CAPS' domain knowledge.
- **Data centre / data hosting (enabling provision)** — An extension of the company's existing in-house data centre — hosting clients' data and possible virtual data rooms — added to the memorandum of association as an enabling provision rather than an active revenue line, with no significant capex planned.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

MSTC runs government e-auction platforms and earns a small fee on the value of what passes through them — about ₹797 billion of transactions in FY26. The economics of that fee are attractive on the surface: the product has essentially no direct input costs, so nearly all of every ₹100 of sales is available for people, marketing and profit, and the balance sheet is virtually debt-free. The quality framework gives the company its best marks for customer benefits and management, and the risk review rates its overall resilience as sound. Three of the strongest points hang together. First, the company is embedded in official processes and mandated platforms — SIMS for the Ministry of Steel, the DGFT gold bullion quota system, the CPCB recycling-certificate exchange — which is why the 'Toll Roads' pattern registers a partial fit. Second, it builds these systems in-house and quickly: SIMS 2.0 was delivered in two months against a normal nine-to-twelve-month timeline, and a steady run of new portals (Chhattisgarh timber, gold quota allocation, the recycling exchange, a travel portal) earns credit under both the research-and-development check and the partial 'Innovation Dominance' fit. Third, management communicates candidly — bad news such as the Coal India exit and the loss-making Mahindra joint venture was disclosed promptly and quantified — which supports the partial 'Corporate Culture' and 'Brand Strength' readings.

The difficulty is that each of those strengths is also the source of a matching risk, so the same fact appears twice in the file with opposite signs. Being embedded in government processes is what makes the toll-road pattern work at all, but it is also why 'Government Dependency' is rated high risk: revenue is almost entirely policy-created, so the mandate that protects the fee is also the thing that can be withdrawn. Being asset-light and cheap to run is what leaves nearly the whole rupee of sales as gross profit, but it also means the platform is cheap for someone else to rebuild — which is exactly what happened when Coal India commissioned a portal from NIC and moved its entire auction business in-house in FY25. That single episode is why the 'Cost to Replicate' pattern is judged a clear non-fit, why the barriers-to-entry check scores at the bottom of the range, and why 'New Entrants' and 'Good Enough Goods' both sit on watch. It is also the clearest example under 'Stakeholder Concentration', rated elevated: an analyst raised Coal India as a risk in August 2022, management played it down as less than a tenth of revenue, and by August 2024 the business was gone. The fee-on-transaction-value model has the same double edge. It gives the company a share of very large flows without owning inventory, but it ties revenue to commodity prices and auction volumes rather than to anything the company controls — management has never cited a price increase across the 2022 to 2026 calls and explains revenue moves by steel and scrap price swings, so 'Pricing Power' is a non-fit and 'Cyclicality' is rated elevated.

The financial record so far reflects the risks more than the strengths. Sales have shrunk roughly 22% a year on average and the year-to-year path is lumpy; e-commerce revenue has been broadly flat at around ₹350 crore from FY23 to FY26 on management's own account, which is why both the market-share check and the 'Market Share Gainers' pattern point to share loss rather than gain — management itself frames the task as growing the overall pie rather than taking share. Returns are the weakest area: for every ₹100 of capital employed the business generated ₹3 of negative operating cash this year, and about 224 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. The asset-light stance that protects margins also means there is little visible spending on growth, only replacement of end-of-life hardware and software plus a one-off Delhi office, so it is hard to see what future revenue is being funded. The one inorganic effort, the 50:50 Mahindra recycling joint venture, has lost money for years with impairment charges and a unit closure, while the FSNL subsidiary was sold rather than built on. Diversification into temple trusts, urban development authorities, bank asset sales and travel is real but still small. That combination — decent customer economics and honest management, no pattern rated better than a partial fit, and returns and growth both negative — produces a combined score of 35 out of 100 and a Mixed, two-star rating: 45 for business quality, 10 for multibagger fit and 48 for risk safety.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, Cyclicality, Stakeholder Concentration. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.2× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.

- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 11% to 30% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** MSTC is a Govt of India enterprise whose revenue is almost entirely policy-created: DoT 5G spectrum auction, coal block and linkage auctions, mineral/sand/limestone block auctions for states, SIMS for Ministry of Steel, CPCB's EPR trading exchange, DGFT gold-bullion tariff-rate-quota allocation, and ELV scrapping volumes that depend on state implementation of the central scrappage policy; even dividend and buyback decisions are set by DIPAM/DPE guidelines. Across all four years management attributes both upside and shortfalls to government decisions rather than to its own commercial franchise.

> "some of the flagship projects of the Government of India, like, mines and natural resources auction, allocation auction, and of course spectrum recently" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Partial diversification attempts — temple trusts, HMDA property, banks' SARFAESI sales, SBI CAPS advisory tie-up and a B2B/B2C travel portal — but these are still small and several are themselves government/PSU-anchored.*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 3 numeric fingerprints reinforce it.

**What the calls show:** Fees are levied on gross transaction value of scrap, iron ore, pellets, chrome ore and coal, so commodity price swings pass straight into revenue — management itself explained a ~20% fall in steel/scrap prices in FY23 and noted 'scrap sale this year has been a little better than what it was in previous two, three years'. The exposure is bounded because the underlying demand is flow-type: scrap and minerals are generated by customers' ongoing operations, not their capex, and MSTC is asset-light with no capacity to over-build.

> "there will be around 20% drop in the prices of steel as well as scrap. Still, we have been able to keep very robust performance" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Operations-linked, consumable-style demand (continuous scrap/mineral disposal) plus an asset-light model — EBITDA margin was held flat through FY26 despite volume and price swings.*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 6 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from -14% to 59% (swinging ±24.3 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 34% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 5% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

> "CIL that is Coal India Limited, which was hitherto a major client for us, has now shifted its entire coal auction and linkage auction work to its own portal. This has impacted our revenue stream" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Broadening of the client base over FY25-FY26 (Vaishno Devi shrine board, HMDA, Tamil Nadu and Chhattisgarh mineral/sand blocks, AYUSH, CPCB) and recovery of the CIL linkage auction through open tender.*

## What to watch

- Whether any other large customer follows Coal India in building its own portal — the single event behind the elevated 'Stakeholder Concentration' risk and the failed 'Cost to Replicate' test.
- Whether cash return on capital turns positive from this year's minus 3%, and whether the roughly 224-day working capital cycle shortens — the two weakest checks in the file.
- Whether e-commerce revenue breaks out of the flat ₹350 crore range seen from FY23 to FY26, which would be the first evidence against the 'Market Share Gainers' non-fit.
- Whether new mandated platforms — the CPCB recycling exchange, the DGFT gold quota system, SIMS — grow into a meaningful share of revenue, which is what would turn the partial 'Toll Roads' fit into a real one and soften the high 'Government Dependency' risk.
- Whether the company ever wins business on price rather than mandate, and whether management cites a fee increase — the missing evidence behind the 'Pricing Power' non-fit and the 'Good Enough Goods' watch rating.
