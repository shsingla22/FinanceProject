# Texmaco Rail (TEXRAIL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Texmaco Rail & Engineering is an India-based manufacturer whose main business is building railway freight wagons (also called freight cars) — the open, closed and specialised goods carriages that haul coal, cement, fertiliser and other cargo. Its biggest customer is Indian Railways, which places multi-year bulk orders (management cites a single 2022 order for about 20,067 wagons worth roughly Rs. 6,450 crores over 39 months), alongside private buyers such as logistics firms, leasing companies and industrial users who need specialised wagons. It also runs steel foundries that cast wagon components — sold both to its own wagon plants and exported, notably to the United States, since its Belgharia (Kolkata) foundry is approved by the Association of American Railroads — and a rail infrastructure contracting arm doing electrification and track/signalling project work. Revenue comes from delivering wagons and castings against these order books; in FY26 the company reported revenue of Rs. 4,377 crores, EBITDA of Rs. 450 crores (10.2% margin) and profit after tax of Rs. 194 crores, with an order book of Rs. 5,661 crores as of 31 December 2025.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Rolling Stock / Freight Car Division (wagon manufacturing)** — Builds railway freight wagons for Indian Railways and private customers; this is the core of the company — management said it accounts for roughly 70–80% of turnover excluding rail EPC (about 60% including it) — and it delivered 8,372 freight cars in FY26 (2,196 in Q4 FY26), with the company describing itself as the largest freight-wagon maker with the highest market share.
- **Steel Foundry / Component Business** — Casts steel components (at Belgharia in Kolkata and Urla) used in wagons and sold to outside customers including exports to the US and Europe — the Belgharia foundry is the only one whose product is approved by the Association of American Railroads — and it shipped about 34,000 metric tons in FY26, though US tariffs cut export volumes by around 30% year-on-year in Q3 FY26.
- **Rail EPC / Rail Infra & Green Energy (the "Kalindee" division)** — Takes on turnkey railway infrastructure project contracts; management repeatedly flags it as the weak spot — it booked a roughly Rs. 13 crore loss in Q4 FY24 and dragged on FY26 performance — and it is small relative to wagons.
- **Bright Power Division (rail electrification / infra)** — Does railway electrification and related infrastructure work; it was the standout performer in FY26, growing 66% to revenue of Rs. 610 crores at a 10.8% EBIT margin, though management notes its revenue is lumpy quarter to quarter.
- **Wagon leasing business** — Leases wagons to private players rather than selling them outright; still small, but management says it is being restructured and given more emphasis as part of the growth plan.
- **"Texmaco 2.0" new/planned businesses** — Not yet earning money — announced intentions to enter railway safety systems (Kavach), propulsion systems, urban mobility including metro and EMU coach manufacturing, wheel sets, and unrelated areas such as iron pellet manufacturing and mining, as part of a stated goal to double the company's size in 3–5 years.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is tied to Indian Railways' and private players' capex programmes, and management itself repeatedly names cyclicality as the core problem — the 2022 boom ('the industry never had it so good') gave way to FY26 revenue down 14% to Rs.4,377 crores, order book shrinking from ~Rs.10,000 crores to Rs.5,661 crores, and a Rs.700 crore contingency provision; the whole Texmaco 2.0 plan exists to dilute wagon dependence.

> "we are looking forward to scaling the railways business profitably even as we complement its cyclical nature with nonlinear growth drivers" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate diversification into flow-type/operations-linked revenue — foundry components, rail electrification (Bright Power +66%), leasing, signalling/Kavach and services — plus a spread of private wagon end-sectors (cement, steel, auto, container)*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 3 of the last 10 years with swings of ±29 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 4% and 9% (±1.6 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 10% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 3% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Fortunes are set by the railway budget, PSU tenders and the National Rail Plan; every call anchors the outlook on government allocation (record Rs.2.93 lakh crore in the 2026-27 budget) and on tenders whose timing management cannot explain, while the FY26 downturn stemmed partly from railway-controlled wheel-set policy and no fresh IR wagon tender for ~2 years.

> "there is a huge requirement is going to come... it should be no less than a range of 1.5 lakh to 2 lakhs wagons" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Conscious shift toward a 70-30 private/export mix, AAR-approved foundry exports to the US, and exit from loss-making government EPC contracts*

### Stakeholder Concentration — ELEVATED
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but the only computed fingerprint does not confirm it — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Indian Railways is the single dominant customer (the Rs.6,450 crore/20,067-wagon order defined three years of output), and on the supply side wheel sets — controlled by railway workshops and a single viable import source, China, after Ukraine dried up — repeatedly determined results, causing the Q1 FY23 loss and the ~20-25% delivery shortfall in FY26.

> "lower wagon production levels... arising from continued wheel set availability constraints" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Broadening into private lessors/logistics players (Adani, GATX, NYK, TCI), exports, and a stated plan to internalise wheel-set capability*

- ✅ Customers pay in about 98 days (was 176) — no sign of weakening bargaining power in collection terms.

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Wagons for IR are standardised types bought on competitive L1 tenders where pricing 'follows a certain norms', leaving thin margins — Q4 FY24 EBITDA of 7% was flagged by an analyst as the lowest versus peers JW and TWL — so differentiation is hard to monetise on the bulk railway book.

> "This is the lowest which is -- with compared with our peers like JW or TWL" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Niche protection through specialised and multimodal wagons where Texmaco is the only validated prototype supplier, richer realisation per wagon, and AAR-approved foundry castings*

- ⚠️ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs fell from about 29% to 22% — pricing is being competed away at the gross level.
- ✅ Operating margin (9%) remains near its historical peak (9%) — no collapse in the premium.
- ✅ Operating margin held (about 6% then 9%) — no drift down under competition.

### Technological Innovation — LOW
