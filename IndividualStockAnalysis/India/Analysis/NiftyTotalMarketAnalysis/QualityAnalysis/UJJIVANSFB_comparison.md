### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 3 could not be compared.

*Foundation test: FAIL on the long view, FAIL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Not a product-upfront recurring model, but the bank does describe a lending-plus-annuity dynamic: customer deposits (CASA 28.6%, retail TD+CASA ~70%) fund the book cheaply, and fee streams from insurance cross-sell, third-party products and MF distribution are described as deepening with the installed customer base. There is no renewal-rate, AMC-share or attach-rate disclosure of the kind the pattern requires. (The long view had said: the calls are silent and no computable check supports the pattern.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The articulable barriers are the SFB licence, an 814-branch network reaching deep micro-banking geographies, and a nine-year-old affordable housing book with a state-wise collateral framework — none instantly replicable. But the core products are commoditised lending in a crowded SFB/NBFC-MFI field, the sector went through a fresh stress cycle, and the RBI declined the universal-bank upgrade, so a well-funded entrant is not durably locked out. (The long view had said: Nothing in the calls articulates why a funded newcomer could not replicate the model — the bank competes on rate in deposits, faces 'competitive pressure' on affordable-housing yields, and its own new lines (gold, vehicle, pre-owned cars, mid-corporate) are built from scratch in a few quarters, which is itself proof the barriers are low.)

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across all four calls margin improvement is attributed to falling cost of funds, CRR relaxation and liquidity utilisation — never to raising prices. Loan yields were flat-to-lower (17.6% to 17.5%) and the deliberate shift to secured lending (43.5% to 50.4% of book) structurally dilutes yield; deposit rates were repeatedly cut and then raised only to defend ALM, not for value. (The long view had said: NIM is structurally high (8.5% in FY26/Q1FY27) and management defends yields via ticket-size and geography mix, but the Jul 2026 call shows yields flat for 6-8 quarters and explicit competitive pressure rather than repeated above-inflation price increases; earlier calls describe 'risk-based pricing' and market-competitive rates, not scarcity or negligible elasticity.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Brand Strength (PARTIAL).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
