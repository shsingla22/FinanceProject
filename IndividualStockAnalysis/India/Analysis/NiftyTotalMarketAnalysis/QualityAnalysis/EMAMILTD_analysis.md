# Emami Ltd. (EMAMILTD) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSUMER GOODS*

Emami Limited is an Indian consumer-goods (FMCG) company, headquartered in Kolkata and listed in India, that makes and sells low-priced branded personal-care and ayurvedic healthcare products — cooling hair oils and talc, antiseptic creams, hair-fall oils, men's grooming creams, prickly-heat powders, pain balms and ayurvedic health remedies — mostly to ordinary household shoppers. It earns money by selling these branded goods at high gross margins (around 68%-70% in FY26) through distributors into general trade and wholesale, plus fast-growing modern trade, e-commerce and quick-commerce channels, which together were about 32% of domestic sales by FY26; it spends heavily on advertising (now roughly half of it digital) to support the brands. Most sales come from India, with an international business selling the same brands into the Middle East/GCC, SAARC, CIS and Africa, including some manufacturing in the UAE. Full-year FY26 consolidated revenue was about INR3,780 crore with EBITDA of INR964 crore and profit after tax of INR775 crore, and the company describes itself as debt-free.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Navratna (and Dermicool)** — Cooling hair oils, cool talc and prickly-heat powder — the core 'summer portfolio' and one of the company's biggest revenue drivers, so much so that a delayed summer in Q4 FY26 dragged the whole quarter's revenue down 4%.
- **Healthcare range (Zandu / Zanducare)** — Ayurvedic health and immunity products sold under the Zandu brand, including digital-first launches on its own Zanducare D2C site and e-pharmacies; a consistently growing line (up 7%-11% in recent quarters) backed by two WHO-GMP factories.
- **Pain Management range** — Balms and pain-relief products (the Zandu balm family) that sell mainly in winter — a swingy, seasonal category that fell 30% in one quarter in FY23 but grew 8%-11% in FY26.
- **BoroPlus** — Antiseptic cream and related winter skincare plus a talc line; a large winter-season brand that grew 16% in Q3 FY26, though its talc part suffered badly in the weak FY26 summer.
- **Kesh King** — Premium ayurvedic anti-hair-fall oil and hair care; a sizeable but volatile brand that fell 15% in one quarter of FY25 and returned to 10%-14% growth in FY26.
- **Male Grooming (Fair and Handsome / Smart and Handsome)** — Men's face creams now being extended into sunscreens, deodorants and body washes; a discretionary category that has swung between 32% growth and 4%-5% declines.
- **7 Oils in One** — A light hair-oil brand that is one of the faster-growing smaller brands, up 34% in Q4 FY26 and also selling well in international markets.
- **International business** — Sells the same brands plus Creme 21 across MENA/GCC, SAARC, CIS and South Asia, with about half of those goods produced in the UAE; it grew 9%-11% in most periods but fell 5% in Q4 FY26 on Middle East shipping disruption.
- **Strategic investments / subsidiaries (The Man Company, Brillare, Axiom, IncNut)** — A portfolio of newer, premium, digital-first companies — men's grooming (The Man Company), professional hair and skin care (Brillare), fruit juice (Axiom, already profitable) and personalised beauty brands Vedix and SkinKraft (IncNut) — still small but growing 31%-34% and targeted to keep growing about 30% a year.

### The story in depth

Emami is a brand-led consumer goods company that earns very well on the money invested in it. After direct input costs it keeps about ₹70 of every ₹100 of sales, turns ₹25 of every ₹100 into operating profit, and generated a 26% cash return on the capital employed this year, with only about eight days of cash tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers and no debt on the books. That profitability and the single strongest pattern in the screen — Brand Strength, the only clear fit — are the same fact seen twice: fifty years of category-owning names like Navratna, BoroPlus, Zandu, Kesh King and Dermicool are what let the company price the way it does, and the quality framework reads the near-70% gross margin as genuine intangible value customers willingly pay for. Management behaviour supports the picture: the Goenka family keeps a low profile, calls are handled by functional heads rather than promoter self-promotion, bad news is owned rather than reframed, and shareholders were paid ₹10 a share in the first nine months of FY26 including a 600% second interim dividend.

The same brand-and-margin model is also the source of most of the risk. Holding those brands up costs 22-23% of sales in advertising and promotion, spending that was raised 21% in one quarter and 12% in another quarter when operating profit fell 15% — and the payoff is still unproven, because FY26 revenue was down 1%. That is why Pricing Power and Innovation Dominance both come out only partial: price hikes have lifted gross margin from 62.6% to 69.9%, but volumes in Kesh King, hair oil and the summer range lean on consumer promotions, which is exactly what the Good Enough Goods risk flags in a mass-market range of talc, balms and hair oils. Seasonality cuts the same way: a failed summer alone took the summer portfolio down 22% with talc down 40%, drove a 4% fourth-quarter decline and the 1% full-year fall, and is why Cyclicality is the one elevated risk — yet that same washout is what the framework counts as a positive, a company sitting near a cyclical trough against a low base with GST rate cuts to 5% and early rural recovery ahead of it. Meanwhile the core demand base has visibly eroded: the premium hair-fall oil market has been declining for some time, Kesh King fell 15%, male grooming fell across several quarters, and the fairness brand had to be renamed — the mirror image of the only-partial Market Share Gainers verdict.

What is missing is a structural moat beyond the brands themselves, and the numbers say so plainly: competitive advantage is the one quality area scoring negative, Toll Roads and Recurring Revenue register no supporting evidence at all because Emami simply sells finished consumer goods, Cost to Replicate is only partial with BCG-led brand rescues cited as a sign of strain, and Low Price Plus is an outright non-fit given premiumisation and 33.4% operating margins. That is why multibagger fit scores just 31 out of 100 while risk safety scores 74 — the company is defensively solid rather than structurally special, and resilience is judged sound. The defensive side is real: no single customer, licensor or supplier drives results, wholesale dependence has been cut to 27% of domestic sales, rural coverage has gone from 32,000 to about 50,000 towns, and rather than being displaced by quick commerce the company is building into it, with organized channels near 32% and D2C brands like The Man Company, Brillare and Vedix bought in. Blending 64 for business quality, 31 for multibagger fit and 74 for risk safety gives 57 out of 100 — a decent, three-star business: high-return and honestly run, with growth of about 6% a year that has yet to show the step-change the brand spending is meant to buy.

> "This decline was primarily driven by weak summer rather than any structural or competitive weakness" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/consumable demand tied to consumer operations not customer capex; diversified brand portfolio (pain, healthcare, hair oil) offsets category corrections; continued A&P investment (+12%) into a down quarter*
