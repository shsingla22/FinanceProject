# Tamilnad Mercantile (TMB) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Tamilnad Mercantile Bank (TMB) is a 105-year-old Indian private sector bank, headquartered in Thoothukudi, Tamil Nadu, whose shares were listed on the NSE and BSE in September 2022. It does ordinary banking: it takes in money from customers as deposits (current, savings, term and foreign-currency deposits) and lends that money out as loans, mainly to small borrowers — retail customers, farmers and small businesses (what management calls the RAM segment: Retail, Agriculture, MSME) — with gold-backed loans making up close to half of the loan book. It earns mostly from the gap between the interest it charges borrowers and the interest it pays depositors (net interest margin of roughly 4.2%–4.5%, which management calls among the best in the industry), plus a smaller stream of fee and other non-interest income. It operates through a branch network heavily concentrated in Tamil Nadu, and is now steadily opening branches outside the state (44 branches opened in FY26, 15 of them outside Tamil Nadu); total business stood at about INR1,21,715 crores as of June 2026 (deposits INR64,409 crores).

**The parts of the business:**

- **Deposits (CASA and term deposits)** — The bank's funding side — current and savings accounts (CASA) plus fixed/term deposits and FCNR(B) foreign-currency deposits — totalling INR64,409 crores as of June 2026, with CASA at roughly 26%–28% of deposits and term deposits the fastest-growing piece (up about 21% year-on-year).
- **RAM advances (Retail, Agriculture, MSME)** — The core lending business — loans to individuals, farmers and small and medium businesses — which is essentially the whole loan book (RAM grew about 28% year-on-year in Q1 FY27); the portfolio mix is roughly agriculture 40%, MSME 28% and retail 25%.
- **Gold loans** — Loans secured against gold jewellery, booked inside the retail and agri buckets, which management calls the single biggest driver of loan growth — about 46% of total advances as of March 2026, at roughly 53% loan-to-value, a yield near 10.1%–10.6% and almost no bad loans.
- **Other / corporate advances** — A small residual book of larger and non-RAM loans, about 5.6% of advances, growing far more slowly (around 7% year-on-year).
- **Non-interest income** — Fees, commissions and other non-lending earnings — INR227 crores in the September 2024 quarter, up about 46% year-on-year — a secondary but growing profit source alongside interest income.
- **Export credit and non-fund based facilities** — Trade-related lending and guarantees/letters of credit for exporting customers; export credit was about INR685 crores (3.53% of advances) in Q1 FY27, a small piece that the bank has now fully provided for.
- **Branch network and distribution** — The physical delivery arm — branches, newly set-up CMCs (12 in FY26) and dedicated liability relationship managers — historically centred on Tamil Nadu, with hiring of officers from outside the state to push expansion into other regions.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Tamilnad Mercantile is a century-old, low-profile Thoothukudi bank that scores best on the people running it. The quality framework gives its highest marks to management: candor (management restates its own prior guidance and scores itself against it, and voluntarily disclosed the ED show-cause notices and the PBI accounting distortion), disciplined stewardship (purely organic growth, no acquisitions, secured-only lending with unsecured at just 10 basis points of the book, and over-provisioning ahead of the coming accounting change), and a tenacious multi-year transformation that was flagged in FY25 and delivered on schedule in FY26 despite legacy shareholder and ED litigation. The multibagger screen agrees, and Corporate Culture is the single pattern rated a strong fit: explicit quarterly guidance was set and beaten seven quarters running, with CASA guided at 15% and delivered at 22.35%, advances guided at 16-17% and delivered at 20.32%, and return on assets guided above 1.85% and delivered at 2.05% — while the missed 50-branch promise was openly disclosed rather than buried. The bank is also genuinely unfollowed: not a single mutual fund held a share until 2026 and foreign holding is under 7%, which the framework counts twice, as being out of the limelight and as security by obscurity. Management is the one quality area scoring above 1; the combined rating lands at 51 out of 100, a Decent three stars, built as 45% business quality (61), 30% multibagger fit (15) and 25% risk safety (76).

The engine driving the recent numbers is the same thing that creates most of the risk. Business grew 23%, roughly 7 percentage points above the industry, against a ten-year growth rate of only about 8.8% — real share capture, and improving rather than deteriorating credit alongside it (gross bad loans 0.73% in FY26 and 0.69% in the June 2027 quarter, credit cost 9 basis points, early-warning accounts at 1.29%). But gold loans are 46.44% of advances by March 2026 and drove retail growth of 62%, so the Market Share Gainers pattern rates only a partial fit for being recent and narrow, and the Cyclicality risk flags the same fact as a balance sheet now heavily tied to one commodity price that management themselves say may stall. The quality framework marks this down twice: cyclical market growth is scored negative because credit quality is at a 40-quarter best rather than a trough, and pricing, mix and volume is scored negative because growth is overwhelmingly volume-led (advances up 27%) with historically weak pricing power — management consciously declined to pass on rate hikes in 2022. Pricing Power and Brand Strength both come out partial for the same reason: margins are among the industry's highest (4.47% in FY23, 4.18-4.29% in FY26-27) and the 105-year trust with Tamil Nadu traders and small businesses is repeatedly called the differentiator, yet there is no premium pricing or brand investment behind it, margin has drifted toward a guided '4 plus', and the Good Enough Goods risk notes that deposits and gold loans are price-shopped products where the customer has little attachment. The offsets are real — 53% loan-to-value on the gold book with stated ability to absorb a 20-25% fall in gold prices, and granular retail tickets of INR 25-30 lakh with no single-name dependence.

The weak spots cluster where the quality framework scores zero: competitive advantage and return on capital both at 0.0, growth at 0.2, which is why multibagger fit contributes only 15 points to the rating. Innovation Dominance is a clear no fit — the record is catch-up, with management admitting service deficiencies in November 2024, still integrating tax and customs payments, benchmarking net banking against peers and calling itself a late mover — and the Technological Innovation risk describes the same gap from the other side, with analysts pressing the bank on it and roughly INR 100 crore of IT capex being spent to close it. That spend is scored positively as growth capex, with 44 branches opened in FY26 and business per new branch more than doubling year on year, but the FY26 branch target was missed. Customer behaviour is also moving underneath the branch build: over-the-counter transactions fell from 26.3 million to 24.1 million even as branches rose from 578 to 622, and deposits are shifting from low-cost current and savings accounts toward term deposits — which is exactly why Low Price Plus is only half met, with genuinely low operating costs (cost-to-income around 39-45%) and exceptional loan quality but a deposit franchise that is not cheap. Geography remains concentrated, with only 171 of 628 branches outside Tamil Nadu, and the Cost to Replicate pattern notes management's own admission that it must buy 'local connect' elsewhere. On policy, the bank is exposed by construction: branch licensing was restricted by the regulator until October 2022, about 40% of the book reprices with the policy rate, and the ECL transition from 1 April 2027 carries a quantified INR 324 crore extra provision — against which INR 276 crore is already pre-funded, with capital adequacy at 32-34%. No risk channel is rated worse than watch and Fashion Risk is low, which is why risk safety scores 76; overall resilience, however, is recorded as unknown.
