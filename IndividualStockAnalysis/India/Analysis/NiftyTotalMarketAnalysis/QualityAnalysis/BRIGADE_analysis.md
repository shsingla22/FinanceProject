# Brigade Enterprises Ltd. (BRIGADE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSTRUCTION*

Brigade Enterprises is an Indian property developer that builds and sells homes, and also owns and rents out commercial property. Its main business is developing residential projects — apartments, plotted developments and mixed-use townships — which it pre-sells to buyers (mostly end-users, plus about 10% NRI buyers) and collects cash from as construction progresses; in FY26 it sold INR 7,424 crores of homes at an average realization of INR 12,107 per square foot. Alongside that, it keeps ownership of office buildings and shopping malls that it leases to corporate tenants and retailers for recurring rent, and it owns and operates hotels. It operates almost entirely in south India — Bengaluru is the core market, with Chennai, Hyderabad, Mysore and Kochi, plus an office project at GIFT City, Gandhinagar; FY26 consolidated revenue was INR 5,909 crores with a 28% EBITDA margin and PAT of INR 725 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Real Estate (residential)** — Buys land, builds and sells apartments, plotted developments and mixed-use townships under the Brigade brand — the largest segment and the main driver of performance, with FY26 turnover of INR 4,002 crores (EBITDA INR 525 crores) and about 85% of pre-sales above the INR 1 crore ticket size.
- **Leasing (commercial office)** — Develops and holds Grade A office buildings (e.g. Brigade Tech Gardens in Bengaluru, Brigade International Financial Centre at GIFT City) and rents them to global capability centres, IT/ITES, BFSI and flex operators — portfolio occupancy around 93%, rental collections at 99%, with roughly 10 million square feet more to launch over FY27–FY28.
- **Leasing (retail malls)** — Owns and runs three Orion shopping malls, earning rent from retail, F&B, cinema and family-entertainment-centre tenants; reported inside the leasing segment, which together with offices generated FY26 turnover of INR 1,303 crores and EBITDA of INR 906 crores — the most profitable segment by margin.
- **Hospitality** — Owns hotels operated under brands such as Sheraton Grand, Grand Mercure and Four Points, earning room, restaurant and banquet/MICE revenue; FY26 turnover was INR 604 crores with EBITDA of INR 207 crores and occupancy around 78%, and the separately named Brigade Hotel Ventures Limited is building out 1,700 keys across 9 hotels.
- **Industrial Parks & Logistics** — A smaller, newer effort with its own COO, tied to land such as the 75 acres added near the Bengaluru aerospace park for possible industrial, logistics, office and residential use; the calls give it no separate revenue or profit figures.

### The story in depth

Brigade's core strength is a name people will pay more for. After direct input costs the company keeps ₹84 of every ₹100 of sales, and both the quality framework and the pattern screen trace that back to the same place: four decades in Bengaluru, a 15th annual Brigade Showcase selling event, and the Brigade name stretched across malls, offices, hotels and interiors. Pricing Power and Brand Strength are the only two patterns rated a strong fit, and the evidence is the same in each — a 5-7% annual like-for-like price discipline, a 15% increase on the Hyderabad Gateway relaunch, 10-15% uplifts on re-leased office space and a 9% rise in FY26 realisations, from ₹9,483 per square foot in Q1 FY25 to ₹13,142 by Q3 FY26. That same pricing acceptance is why two of the eight risk channels come back Low: buyers are brand-aware and buy direct with no middleman steering them, so cheaper local competition has not forced discounts. Operating margin sits at ₹25 per ₹100 of sales and has moved only about two points a year, which the framework reads as rational pricing rather than a price war.

The trouble is what that brand is attached to. Every ₹1 of assets produces only ₹0.22 of sales, roughly 4,345 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and the business generated minus ₹1 of operating cash for every ₹100 of capital employed this year — a negative cash return. These are not separate problems from the strengths; they are the same decisions seen from the other side. The 57 million square foot land bank, the three Orion malls, the 8.7 million square feet of leased offices and the 1,700 hotel keys are exactly what makes Cost to Replicate and Forward Integrators partial fits and what gives the recurring-revenue story its ₹1,303 crore of FY26 leasing revenue at ₹906 crore of EBITDA — and they are also what makes the balance sheet heavy and the cash cycle long. Management has been disciplined about carrying that weight: gearing came down from 0.66 in FY23 to 0.23-0.27 while funding heavy building, 88-92% of debt is backed by rental income, and there have been no large acquisitions. But the new money going in — ₹2,100 crore of land in nine months of FY26, about ₹6,000 crore for a 10 million square foot commercial pipeline, ₹800 crore on hotels — is underwritten to roughly an 18% return, which the framework calls stated but unspectacular. Growth is real and geographically proven (Chennai, Hyderabad, GIFT City, Kochi, Trivandrum, with the first Hyderabad tower sold out and 30% of new project value now from Hyderabad), yet no share number against named rivals is ever given, and FY26 presales fell 5% while its own core markets grew — which is why Market Share Gainers stays only a partial fit.

The risk review lands on one high risk and three watch items, and each hooks directly back to a strength. Government approvals are the single biggest one: FY26 launched 8.3 million square feet against a 12 million plan with 3.3 million pushed into FY27 mainly on approval delays, and Q1 FY25 sales were hit the same way. Notably, the quality framework scores the very same approval, land and capital hurdles as a positive — they are what keeps new competitors out and margins steady. The barrier protects Brigade and blocks it in equal measure. The annuity book that is supposed to cushion the residential cycle brings its own two watch items: Amazon vacated 630,000 square feet at WTC Bengaluru in May 2026 with only about 100,000 re-let at the time, and 58% of the leased portfolio is global capability centres with another 26% traditional IT services, in a quarter where management named AI as a macro concern. Premiumisation, the strategy that delivers the pricing power, is also why Low Price Plus and Toll Roads are outright non-fits — a ₹2-3 crore home is the largest item in the buyer's budget and buyers are described as ticket-size sensitive — and it leaves the company exposed if preferences move again, with 85% of presales now above ₹1 crore. Marketing spend stayed high in FY26 while sales fell, which hurt cash flow. Netting it out, the combined rating is 47 out of 100, Mixed, with resilience judged Strained: a genuinely strong brand and a careful balance sheet sitting on top of an asset-heavy, approval-dependent, cyclical business that is not currently converting capital into cash.
