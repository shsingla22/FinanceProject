# Olectra Greentec (OLECTRA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Olectra Greentech is an Indian company that builds and sells electric buses, and to a smaller extent electric tipper trucks, using battery technology licensed from its partner BYD (agreement extended to December 2030) with battery cells imported from China. Its main customers are Indian state transport undertakings and government bodies that buy buses through large public tenders such as those from CESL, MSRTC and BEST, and it typically manufactures the buses, supplies them and then maintains them, sometimes taking a minority stake in the special-purpose companies that hold the contract. Alongside the bus business it runs a separate, long-standing insulator division that sells largely into export markets at much higher margins. Manufacturing is based near Hyderabad, including a new plant on 150 acres, and essentially all bus sales are within India; Q3 FY26 revenue was about Rs 664 crore with roughly 14% EBITDA margins.

**The parts of the business:**

- **EV segment (electric buses)** — The core business — designing and manufacturing electric buses in 4-5 model variants sold mainly to state transport undertakings through tenders; management calls it the number-one player with about 29% market share in Q3 FY26, roughly 3,600 buses delivered since inception, a very large order book (about 10,969 buses as of FY24) and Q3 FY26 top-line growth of about 23% in this segment.
- **EV segment (electric tippers / trucks)** — Electric tipper trucks aimed at private infrastructure, construction, road, mining and port companies rather than government tenders; still very small and described repeatedly as a nascent segment where 'traction is yet to take off' — only about 116 tippers delivered since inception versus thousands of buses.
- **Insulator segment** — A separate division making electrical insulators, sold with a heavy export tilt; small relative to the EV business but the more profitable one, with EBIT margins management says rose from about 26% to 32-33% and segment growth of 33% in one quarter cited.
- **SPVs / associate companies (Evey Trans and others)** — Where a tender requires the manufacturer to hold equity in the operating vehicle, Olectra invests in special-purpose companies that run the buses — eight associates at 26% stake, one subsidiary at 51% and one wholly-owned JV — with the group targeting a minimum 15% IRR before entering any such tender.
- **Maintenance of supplied buses** — Beyond selling the vehicle, the stated philosophy is to 'manufacture, supply and take care of the maintenance of buses', so contracts carry an ongoing service obligation over the life of the fleet; the calls do not size this separately.

### The story in depth

Olectra Greentec sits in a market that is genuinely growing — the end-market check is one of the few clear positives, with the Indian EV industry projected to expand at 45% a year to 2030, commercial EV volumes up 120% year-on-year, and a government target to convert 8 lakh buses to electric by 2030. The company has converted that tailwind into volume: management cites 29% share in Q3, 24% year-to-date, the No.1 position for two straight years, 3,600 buses deployed and an order book of 10,969 buses, and sales have compounded at roughly 34% a year. It is also spending to serve that demand, with a 150-acre greenfield plant at Seetharampur meant to take capacity from 5,000 to 10,000 buses, a Rs.500 crore term loan behind it, and a further Rs.300-350 crore earmarked for new platforms. That is the strongest part of the story, and it is why the multibagger screen marks Market Share Gainers as a partial fit rather than a miss.

The trouble is that almost every strength here has the same root, and that root is also the source of the risks. The growth is volume growth won in government price auctions, and the screen and the risk review both land on that fact from different directions. Growth is 'purely volume-led with adverse price and mix': realisation per bus fell from Rs.1.75 crore to Rs.1.70 crore between the two BEST tenders, and the 9-metre and truck mix is compressing margins further. Pricing Power is a clear no-fit and Low Price Plus is a no-fit too, because the company prices on a cost-plus 10-12% EBITDA formula rather than from any position of strength — and by February 2026 management itself concedes that 'historical margin percentages are not sustainable', with profit flat despite revenue up 29-30%. The same tender mechanism that fills the order book is scored as the worst structural feature in the quality framework: buyers are highly concentrated, CESL and the state transport undertakings run lowest-price auctions and can demand price matching across cities, and a single tender can move 5,000 of 5,500 buses to a rival — which is what happened in 2022, and Olectra also lost the first PM e-Bus tranche outright. Government Dependency and Good Enough Goods are both rated high risk for exactly this reason, and the industry structure area scores worst of all seven quality areas. The capex tells the same story from the returns side: it is unambiguously growth capex, but the business generated only about Rs.6 of operating cash for every Rs.100 of capital employed, so more volume at these prices does not obviously become more profit.

What is missing is anything that would let the company step out of that auction. The screen finds no fit on Brand Strength, Cost to Replicate, Toll Roads, Friendly Middleman and Corporate Culture — there is no advertising or premium pricing, the core drivetrain and battery technology is licensed from BYD under an agreement running only to 2030, all cells are imported from China with no domestic capability, and orders are re-bid every cycle while Tata, JBM, PMI, Switch and IPLTech are already in the market. The recurring-revenue and forward-integration elements are real but unproven: Olectra does earn maintenance and per-kilometre income through Evey Trans and eight 26%-owned operating vehicles on 12-year contracts, which the risk review credits as a partial offset to the lumpy, tender-timed nature of bus sales, but management never quantifies attach rates or maintenance revenue and frames the structure as a tender requirement rather than a strategy. Delivery has also repeatedly fallen short of guidance — 1,000 buses guided for FY23 became 507 in FY24, 2,500 for FY25 was cut to 2,000, FY26's 2,000 became '1,500 to 2,000', and the Seetharampur plant slipped from mid-2023 to partial production in 2024 — while the promotion is loud, with 'world's largest' claims and the managing director pointing to his own television interview as the guidance channel. The combined rating of 27 out of 100, or Weak, follows directly: strong end-market, weak position within it. Balance-sheet resilience is judged sound, so this is a question of earning power, not of survival.

### Low Price Plus — NO FIT
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

## What to watch

- Whether realisation per bus stops falling and margins hold — the Pricing Power no-fit and the Good Enough Goods high risk both turn on the Rs.1.75cr to Rs.1.70cr slide and the 'historical margins not sustainable' comment.

- Whether management finally hits a delivery number — the Corporate Culture no-fit rests entirely on the FY23, FY25 and FY26 guidance cuts and the plant delay.
