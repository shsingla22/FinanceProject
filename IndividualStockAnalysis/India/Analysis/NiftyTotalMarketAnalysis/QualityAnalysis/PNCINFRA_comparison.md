### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 1 weakened, 7 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: There is a genuine post-construction annuity stream — 13-14 HAM projects with contracted annuities, 2 BOT-annuity assets, and management flags 10-year O&M revenue from Jal Jeevan Mission works — but it is the opposite of the capital-light, billed-in-advance model: the HAM book alone needs Rs. 1,744 crore of equity, of which Rs. 542 crore is still to be infused as of the May 2026 call, and there is no discussion of attach rates, renewals or customer advances funding the business. (The long view had said: There is no product-plus-recurring-stream model: revenue is lumpy EPC/HAM construction billed on percentage completion, and rather than customers funding the business, PNC funds them — 121-day working capital cycle in Q1FY25, Rs. 2,212 crore debtors, Rs. 822 crore of water dues stuck with states, and Rs. 1,744-3,092 crore of equity it must inject into HAM SPVs.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Order book grew from over Rs. 17,000 crore (Jun-25) to over Rs. 22,000 crore (Mar-26) while sector awarding shrank, with entry into coal mining (SECL Rs. 3,489 crore), solar+BESS (NHPC), airport (AAI Varanasi), railways and two bids into Uzbekistan — but no share figures are given, Q1 FY26 revenue de-grew 13% against a guided 20% growth, and the diversification is new-segment entry rather than demonstrated share capture from named competitors. (The long view had said: Share is being lost, not gained: standalone revenue fell from ~Rs. 6,300 crore (FY22) to Rs. 4,633 crore (FY26), 9M FY26 revenue declined 22.5% year-on-year, and management attributes three years of weak inflow to unhealthy competition; the two Uzbekistan bids (~Rs. 1,500 crore) are a first, unproven overseas step.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The only articulable barrier in these calls is regulatory pre-qualification — tightened RFP norms plus the balance sheet needed to fund Rs. 1,623 crore of HAM equity — which does screen out newcomers, but nothing here is un-replicable by a well-funded incumbent peer, and each project is re-competed at L1 with no installed base, data scale, time-based asset or finite location protecting the earnings. (The long view had said: The only articulated barriers are qualification criteria and an owned equipment fleet — both buyable by a funded entrant — and management's own explanation for three years of weak inflow is competitive intensity, which is exactly what a durable cost-to-replicate barrier would prevent.)

**That weakened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The company wins on being lowest bidder (repeated L1 declarations across all four calls), but the 'plus' is absent — standalone EBITDA margin drifted 12.4% (Q1) to 13.9% (Q2) to 12.4% (Q3) on falling revenue, and the Q4 FY26 call attributes relief on input-cost inflation to a government escalation mechanism rather than any internal cost advantage; no itemised savings or cost culture is described. (The long view had said: Execution efficiency is real and repeatedly monetised — Rs. 37 crore early-completion bonus on Purvanchal Package 6 (Q1FY23) and Rs. 56 crore on a Maharashtra EPC project (Q1FY25) — and management insists bids are calibrated rather than aggressive, but no itemised cost culture is ever described and EBITDA margins drifted down from 14.7% (Q1FY23) to 12.0-12.6% (FY26) with revenue falling from Rs. 6,300 crore to Rs. 4,633 crore, so scale advantage is not compounding.)

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT); Innovation Dominance (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 2 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: NHAI/MoRTH remains the single dominant counterparty at 62% of the unexecuted order book (was 67% in Q1FY26), and one SECL coal-mining contract alone is 13-15% of the book, so a handful of relationships drive results. Exposure is bounded by visible, deliberate broadening across FY26 into new clients — SECL, NHPC, AAI, BSRDC, LDA, UP State Bridge Corp — and by asset monetisation to KKR's Highway Infrastructure Trust and Vertis. (The long view had said: One counterparty — NHAI/MoRTH — repeatedly determines results, order inflow and even receivables; management states plainly it remains the focus client and targets 60-70% of new orders from highways, with over Rs 70,000 crore of the identified bid pipeline being NHAI. Order-book concentration is compounded by a heavy UP/Uttar Pradesh geographic tilt raised by analysts as early as FY23.)
- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: The calls affirmatively describe the industry structure moving against new entrants rather than toward them: NHAI tightened RFP qualification norms in FY26 specifically to restrict awards to established players, and management frames the emerging multi-sector pipeline as being for firms with proven track records and healthy balance sheets. No competitor-driven discounting or defensive margin investment is discussed in any of the four calls. (The long view had said: Management attributes lost order inflow directly to structurally more aggressive bidders entering an award-starved market — 'unhealthy competition' over two-and-a-half years — while itself refusing to chase price, which is a real but bounded competitive reset rather than a new business format displacing the industry.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Construction contracting here is awarded on lowest price — every win in the FY26 calls is announced as an 'L1 bidder' outcome across roads, solar-BESS, mining and bridges — so there is no premium to defend and pricing power sits with the tendering authority, which is the structural good-enough condition. Exposure is bounded because prequalification thresholds and the shift toward fund-based HAM/BOT mandates requiring balance-sheet capacity keep the lowest-cost fringe out. (The long view had said: Work is awarded on lowest-price L1 tenders to a technically qualified pool, so there is no brand premium to defend — the 'unhealthy competition' cited over FY24-FY26 is precisely cheaper bidders taking share, and EBITDA guidance has ratcheted down from 13.5% to 12%.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (QUANT FLAG); Fashion Risk (QUANT FLAG); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (HIGH RISK).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
