# Page Industries Ltd. (PAGEIND) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: TEXTILES*

Page Industries makes and sells branded clothing in India under licence from Jockey International — management calls Jockey "brand Jockey" and describes itself as its licensee, having won Jockey International's "Licensee of the Decade" award twice — and also runs a Speedo business. It manufactures the garments itself (cotton is its main raw material, and it runs plants including a newer facility in Orissa), then sells them through a very wide retail network: roughly 116,600 multi-brand outlets served via distributors, about 1,615 exclusive brand stores, large-format stores, and e-commerce/D2C channels. It makes money by selling volume at a controlled margin — management says it deliberately regulates EBITDA to around 21% (19%–21% range) and passes the rest to consumers to stay a "value for money" brand — with occasional calibrated price increases when cotton and other input costs rise. In FY26 it sold 228.4 million pieces for revenue of ₹52,468 million, EBITDA margin 22%, PAT ₹7,638 million, and it describes itself as debt free; operations discussed are entirely India (metros through Tier-3 and Tier-4 towns).

**The parts of the business:**

- **Jockey (licensed brand)** — The core business — Page manufactures and sells Jockey-branded products in India as its licensee, and effectively all the revenue, retail network and product launches discussed on the calls (e.g. "JKY Groove" and "bonded technology") sit under this brand, which celebrated 150 years globally in 2026.
- **Speedo** — A second, much smaller brand Page handles; management only mentions it in passing (in Aug 2022 saying the Speedo business was "back on track" and in line with budget), and gives no separate revenue figure.
- **Athleisure** — One of the named growth categories and, by management's own commentary, a large and closely watched one — it carried the biggest distributor-inventory overhang (over 10 days cut, a correction that "plagued" them for about two years) and its revival was a main driver of the strong Q4 FY26 volume growth.
- **Men's business** — Flagged repeatedly as a focus growth category alongside athleisure and juniors; no separate revenue split is given on the calls.
- **Juniors (kids)** — The third named priority category management says it gives "undivided attention" to; size not disclosed separately.
- **Outerwear / value-added premium products** — Newer, higher-priced lines that management credits in FY26 with premiumization and a higher average selling price (net realization up ~4% in Q3 FY26 purely on mix, with no price hike).
- **General trade / MBOs (multi-brand outlets via distributors)** — The biggest distribution channel — around 116,600 multi-brand outlets at end-FY26, billed through distributors on an auto-replenishment system that management spent two years using to bring channel inventory down to healthy levels.
- **EBOs (exclusive brand stores)** — Own-brand stores, grown from 1,144 in mid-2022 to 1,615 by end-FY26, used to expand reach across metros and Tier-2/Tier-3 cities.
- **LFS (large format stores)** — Sales through big modern-retail chains; the reported count moves around a lot across the calls (3,026+ in 2022, 1,137 in 2024, 1,778 in Q3 FY26, 893 in Q4 FY26), so it is a real but secondary channel.
- **E-commerce / D2C** — Online selling through marketplaces and the company's own direct-to-consumer channel — the fastest-growing channel (up 32% in Q1 FY25), with management repeatedly saying it "leads across e-commerce platforms."
- **Manufacturing and supply chain** — Page runs its own factories and capacity expansion (including a new Orissa facility) with a workforce of 28,000+ associates in 2022; recent calls stress lean initiatives, automation and value-stream mapping that let headcount fall by ~2,500–3,000 while protecting margins.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

In one breath: the quality framework finds a good business today (70/100); it strongly fits 2 of the 11 patterns long-term winners share; the main risks are elevated but not severe (Stakeholder Concentration, Good Enough Goods). Weighing those together gives 68 out of 100 — strong.

### The story in depth

Page Industries earns a Strong rating of 68 out of 100, and the case rests almost entirely on how much cash the business produces from how little it uses. For every ₹100 of capital in the business — shareholders' funds plus borrowings — it generated ₹45 of operating cash this year, and every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.84 of sales. That combination shows up in the scoring as a perfect Return on Capital area score of 2.0, and it is not an accident of one good year: operating profit is ₹22 on every ₹100 of sales and has moved only about ±1.5 points a year, while what the company keeps after direct input costs is ₹59 of every ₹100. Underneath those numbers sit two things the framework and the multibagger screen both pick up. The first is distribution: roughly 116,600 multi-brand outlets, 1,615 exclusive stores, large-format stores and category-leading e-commerce, run on auto-replenishment that keeps stocking in step with actual selling — scored 2 on both Distribution advantage and Convenience benefits, and the concrete part of the Cost to Replicate pattern. The second is how the company is run: consistently debt-free, entirely organic growth, unprofitable large-format accounts exited, and brand, product and the Odisha plant funded straight through a multi-year demand slowdown. That earns a 2 on Disciplined stewards and makes Corporate Culture one of only two strong pattern fits, alongside Brand Strength.

The awkward part is that several of the traits producing those strengths are the same traits producing the two elevated risks. The Jockey name is the reason Brand Strength is a strong fit — 150 years of heritage, 'Licensee of the Decade' for a second consecutive term, advertising held at 4-5% of revenue every year — and it is also a large part of what a competitor would have to rebuild. But the brand is licensed, not owned: Jockey and Speedo belong to Jockey International, and management has ruled out building any owned brand beside them, which is precisely why Stakeholder Concentration reads Elevated. The same decision cuts twice again. Refusing a cheaper sub-brand or flanker is part of what makes management look disciplined, but it is exactly what analysts have been pressing on since FY25, and by FY26 entry-level products in men's innerwear — the largest category — are the weakest part of the book while the price gap to smaller brands widens. That is the Good Enough Goods risk, also Elevated. Pricing behaviour tells a similar double story: steady margins and no new competitors arriving suggest rational pricing, and premium ranges are genuinely outselling mid-premium with average selling price up 4% on mix, but the company took no price increase for roughly three and a half years after July 2022, so Pricing Power scores only Partial — and that same restraint, along with regulated profitability with savings passed to consumers, is what the Low Price Plus pattern half-recognises in a premium-priced brand. Finally, the distribution machine that is the core moat is largely an offline machine, and multi-brand outlet count has fallen from 113,715 in June 2022 to 104,696, which is why Market Share Gainers is only Partial despite management's leadership claims. The build-out of exclusive stores and three e-commerce verticals — the Forward Integrators pattern, also Partial — is best read as the answer to the online channel shift that sits behind both the New Entrants and Shifting Consumer Preferences watch flags.

What is missing is as informative as what is there. Three patterns fail outright and for structural reasons rather than execution ones: revenue is outright product sale with no subscriptions, service contracts or deferred billing, so there is no Recurring Revenue; retailers are fulfilment partners on auto-replenishment rather than experts who steer the buyer, so there is no Friendly Middleman; and this is discretionary household clothing with visible price sensitivity at the cheap end, the opposite of a small-but-essential input, so no Toll Roads. That absence is why the multibagger score is 63 against a business-quality score of 70. The weakest framework areas point the same way — Industry Structure at 0.17, Capital Allocation at 0.25 and Competitive Advantage at 0.33 — and growth, at about 12% a year on average but lumpy, with FY26 revenue up 6.3% and volumes up 3.9%, scores 0.67. The one clear operational drag is working capital, the only negative check in the notable set: about 138 days of cash sits between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, though management is visibly working on it, with inventory cut from 93 to roughly 67-73 days and working capital tightened from 73 to 52-56 days, helped by shift working that added 46% capacity without new assets. Against that, four of the eight risk channels come back Low — the business is not tied to customer capital spending, has no platform or format threat, takes no tender, tariff or subsidy revenue, and depends on functional replacement buying rather than any trend — which is why resilience reads Sound and risk safety scores 72, the highest of the three inputs. The honest summary: an exceptionally cash-generative, well-run distributor of someone else's brand, whose remaining risks are competitive and structural rather than shocks, and whose upside case depends on defending the low end and the online shift without giving up the pricing and brand discipline that produced the returns in the first place.

## What to watch

- Any change in the Jockey International licence terms, duration or relationship — the Stakeholder Concentration risk is the single largest, since a loss would take the whole franchise with it.
- Entry-level men's innerwear volumes and the widening price gap to cheaper brands — if this keeps deteriorating, the Good Enough Goods risk hardens and the refusal to launch a flanker stops looking like discipline.
- Multi-brand outlet count, which has already fallen from 113,715 to 104,696 — further decline would undercut both the Distribution advantage check and what is left of the Market Share Gainers pattern.
- Whether the January 2026 price increase sticks and is followed by others — three and a half years of no increases is what holds Pricing Power at Partial rather than a fit.
- E-commerce share and exclusive-store additions versus the shrinking offline base — the Forward Integrators build-out is the only visible defence against the New Entrants and Shifting Consumer Preferences channel shift.
- The 138-day cash cycle flagged by the Cost of working capital check — continued progress on inventory and working capital days is what keeps the 45% cash return on capital intact.
