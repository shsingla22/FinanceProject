### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 7 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Explicit above-market growth is reported quarter after quarter — 15% vs industry 14% (Q1 FY26), 18% vs 13.4% and GDPI 9.3% vs market 1.8% (Q2), ranked first among private players while maintaining share (Q3) — and it is share taken without buying it, with the combined ratio still described as among the lowest in the multiline market; the Q4 shortfall (8.3% vs market 11%) was a deliberate withdrawal from underpriced crop and motor, which reinforces rather than undercuts the pattern. (The long view had said: Explicit, repeated share gains over the full period: BALIC's private-market IRNB share rose 6.4%→7.4% in 9M FY23 and grew 22% vs industry 12%; BAGIC grew 12% vs industry 9% in FY25 and ranked first among private players on GDPI in Q3 FY26; BFL AUM compounded from Rs.2.31 lakh cr (Dec 2022) to Rs.5.10 lakh cr (Mar 2026). Critically, the share was not bought with underwriting or credit quality — GNPA stayed ~1% and they repeatedly ceded price-competitive motor/crop/group-health business.)

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The Allianz buyout was framed explicitly as a brand event ('100% Bajaj'), and the Bajaj name clearly carries distribution weight across general insurance, life, finance and the AMC; but no A&P spend, no premium pricing and no share-gain-plus-price evidence appears in these four calls. (The long view had said: The Bajaj name is stretched across adjacencies — Finserv Health, Bajaj Markets, Bajaj Finserv AMC (fastest to Rs.30,000cr in 2.5 years), and the rebranding to Bajaj General/Bajaj Life after the Allianz buyout — supported by a consistency promise ('Caringly Yours', lowest grievance ratio, highest NPS) and a 10-crore customer franchise. No A&P spend, heritage narrative or premium-pricing evidence is offered, so it stops short of full brand strength.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 10 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
