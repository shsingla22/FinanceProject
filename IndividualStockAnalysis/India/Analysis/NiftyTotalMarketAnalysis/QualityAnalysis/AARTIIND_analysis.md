# Aarti Industries (AARTIIND) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Aarti Industries is an Indian chemicals manufacturer that makes a wide range of industrial chemicals and intermediates, built mainly out of two basic feedstocks — benzene and toluene — plus related chemistries such as nitration, chlorination, hydrogenation and ethylation. It does not sell to consumers; it sells these chemicals in bulk to other manufacturers who use them in agrochemicals (crop protection), pharmaceuticals, dyes and pigments, paints, polymers and fuel/energy applications, and it earns money on the spread between raw material costs and product prices, with cost pass-through mechanisms and long-term supply contracts with global customers. It runs plants in India (Vapi, Jhagadia, Dahej SEZ and a new greenfield site called Zone IV), spends heavily on capex to add capacity, and exports a majority of what it makes — over 50% of revenue in FY23 rising to about 65% by Q3 FY26, shipped to the US, Europe, Middle East, Africa and elsewhere. For FY26 the company reported revenue of INR 9,018 crore, EBITDA of INR 1,172 crore and profit after tax of INR 419 crore; its former pharma manufacturing arm was demerged in 2022 into a separate listed company, Aarti Pharmalabs.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Energy applications (led by MMA)** — Supplies chemicals into fuel/energy uses where profitability moves with crude, gasoline and naphtha cracks; management calls it a key growth driver with a market-leading global position, contributing roughly 40% of revenue, with capacity being expanded from about 290+ KT to 360 KTPA.
- **Agrochemicals** — Makes intermediates used in crop-protection products — volumes have been steady but pricing stayed weak due to Chinese dumping, and in FY26 the company signed a $150 million multiyear supply deal running to March 2030 for a critical agrochemical intermediate, needing no new capex.
- **Pharmaceuticals** — Supplies chemical intermediates to pharma customers — described as a stable, steady-volume application; note the company's own pharma manufacturing business was demerged into Aarti Pharmalabs Limited and listed separately in December 2022.
- **Polymers (PDCB / DCB, PDA / MPDA)** — Chemicals feeding polymer makers, currently in a strong growth phase on electric-vehicle-related demand (PPS), with DCB capacity being debottlenecked from 120 to 140 KTPA, though the PDA/MPDA chain has been weak on US exposure and Chinese competition.
- **Dyes, pigments and paints** — One of the older end-markets; demand was weak through FY23 and described as relatively stable by FY26, with domestic volumes expected to offset export softness.
- **Benzene and Toluene value chains (NCB, nitrotoluene, hydrogenation, ethylation)** — The integrated backbone the company runs everything else from — management calls itself the 'go-to integrated player' here and reports quarterly production volumes for these building blocks (e.g. ~19,500 MT of nitrochlorobenzene and ~7,600 MT of nitrotoluene in Q1 FY25).
- **Long-term contracts / custom manufacturing for global customers** — Multi-year, dedicated supply arrangements where Aarti builds capacity for a specific global customer in exchange for committed volumes and margins; these were small in FY23 but by FY26 management points to them as a main source of earnings visibility, including a new 15-year backward-integration deal with a global chemical company (INR 200-250 crore capex).
- **Joint ventures and partnerships** — A 50-50 JV with UPL called Augene (also referred to as the Superform JV) making downstream amine derivatives for agrochemicals, paints and coatings — roughly INR 150 crore invested by each side, commissioning around H1 FY27 with peak revenue potential of INR 400-500 crore — plus a JV with RESL and other 'circularity' initiatives being built out.
- **Zone IV greenfield site and R&D pipeline / Advanced Materials** — The main growth platform: a new multipurpose plant, chlorotoluene chain, PEDA and downstream blocks being commissioned in phases using in-house technology, backed by a 250+ scientist R&D team and 50+ products in the pipeline, and pointed toward what management now calls Advanced Materials — higher-value, application-led products rather than bulk chemicals.

### The story in depth

Aarti Industries comes out of all three reviews as a well-run company operating in a bad neighbourhood, and the combined rating of 44 out of 100 ("Mixed", 2 stars) is essentially that tension put into a number: business quality scored 60, risk safety 40, but fit with the multibagger patterns only 25. The strengths are genuine and consistent across the three records. Management scores 0.83 and Competitive Advantage 1.0 in the quality framework, and the Corporate Culture pattern picks up the same evidence from the other side — founder-family (Gogri) ownership, the 2026 Gallup Exceptional Workplace Award, and the decision to withdraw FY25 EBITDA guidance openly rather than bluff. Research spending is real, not decorative: 250+ scientists and a 50+ product pipeline in 2022, a Zone-IV pilot plant, and Zone-IV blocks eventually commissioned on the company's own in-house technology. That same evidence is why the Innovation Dominance pattern registers as a partial fit and why Technological Innovation is one of only two risks rated LOW — steady small improvements, no danger of the product being made obsolete. Fashion Risk is the other LOW: these are industrial intermediates bought for function, with volumes described as stable even through price downturns.

The single trait that runs through the whole record — and explains why seven patterns land on "partial" rather than "yes" — is that Aarti has been buying volume with price. The Market Share Gainers pattern is documented (30% year-on-year volume growth in Q1 FY25, exports at a record 65% of revenue, utilisation pushed toward 80-85% and higher, self-declared global leadership in MMA), but management admits some of that share was won by conceding price. The very same fact shows up as the framework's worst score, Pricing, Mix and Volume at -2, as the outright NO FIT verdict on Pricing Power, and as the HIGH RISK rating on Good Enough Goods — a large part of the portfolio is bulk isomer-level chemistry where the buyer decides on price alone, with MPDA specifically called out as underperforming against heavy Chinese competition. The cause is common to all of them: Chinese producers with structurally different costs reset pricing across the portfolio for two to two and a half years, which is why New Entrants is ELEVATED, why Barriers to Entry scores -1 with margins swinging plus or minus 4.4 points, and why the Cost to Replicate pattern fails its own counter-test — the barriers are articulable (multiple benzene and toluene chemistries, integration, customer qualification cycles, MMA scale) but the last four years show well-funded capacity walking straight through them. Toll Roads fails on the same point: the contracts and qualification cycles look right, but prices are pass-through, so the toll cannot be raised. Brand Strength and Friendly Middleman are NO FIT for structural reasons, and Recurring Revenue is NO FIT twice over — there is no subscription-type income, and working capital is a drain rather than customer-funded.

The second thread is that Aarti kept spending heavily through the price war and has not yet been paid for it. Growth capex ran roughly Rs 1,100-1,200cr in FY23, about Rs 3,000cr planned across FY24-25, Rs 1,500-1,800cr in FY25 and Rs 1,125cr in FY26, yet incremental returns disappointed — hence the -1 on Capex for Growth, Capital Allocation at just 0.25, Return on Capital at -0.33, and the plain arithmetic that every Rs 100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings produced about Rs 7 of operating cash, a 7% cash return. The downstream moves meant to fix the mix — the 50:50 JVs with UPL (about Rs 150cr each side) and the RESL circularity JV, plus PEDA downstream of ethylation — are exactly why Forward Integrators reads partial and why M&A scores only +1: nothing is commissioned yet, so the promised margin benefit is unproven. The offsetting item is timing. Cyclical Market Growth is the framework's single best score at +2: after two to two and a half years of falling prices the company sits near a cyclical trough with named catalysts — China's anti-involution supply discipline and VAT-rebate withdrawal already lifting pricing in the NCB chain, plus the India-EU FTA. Notice that these are the same forces that create the ELEVATED New Entrants and Cyclicality risks, only pointing the other way, and that they are policy decisions made abroad, which is why Government Dependency is on WATCH despite no government being a customer. Two more concentration points sit alongside: MMA guided to settle at 30-40% of the portfolio with the US as the key market (Stakeholder Concentration, WATCH — an earlier long-term contract was outright terminated in Nov 2022), and roughly 40% of revenue tied to a gasoline fuel-additive application whose demand follows refinery blending economics, partly hedged by EV-linked PDCB/PPS growth (Shifting Consumer Preferences, WATCH). The overall resilience reading of STRAINED is the fair summary: good people, real chemistry skill, honest disclosure, all currently absorbing an industry-wide price shock they did not cause and cannot price their way out of.

Sales have grown about 10% a year on average, lumpy from year to year, which sits inside the 10-15% band the framework favours but says nothing about profitability.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Good Enough Goods, Cyclicality, New Entrants. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.8× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.

### Good Enough Goods — HIGH RISK
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 3 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** A large part of the portfolio is bulk isomer-level chemistry where the buyer's decision is price, and cheaper Chinese equivalents have compressed margins for years despite stable volumes — MPDA 'continues to underperform due to heavy competition from China', PDA needs a 'structural long-term solution', and Aarti has had to fall back on Tier-2/Tier-3 customers with weaker pricing power. Management's own pivot 'from bulk products toward high-value, application-led solutions' is an admission the commodity layer is not defensible.

> "Within the polymer application, MPDA specifically continues to underperform due to heavy competition from China" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate move up the value chain into Advanced Materials, backward/downstream integration, and customer conversations shifting from 'product-specific to chemistry-led, R&D-focussed' — plus long-term contracts that price on formula rather than spot*

- ⚠️ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs fell from about 46% to 41% — pricing is being competed away at the gross level.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has collapsed from a peak of 28% to 14% — the Nobel-Biocare-style fingerprint of a premium being competed away.
- ⚠️ Operating margin drifted down from about 19% to 14% — steady profit erosion, the mark of rising competitive pressure.

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**What the calls show:** Across all four excerpts the business is described in cycle/commodity terms — benzene, aniline, toluene, methanol prices moving 'over 60%', margins swinging with gasoline-naphtha cracks, and an energy application that is now ~40% of revenue and explicitly 'seasonal' and volatile; this sat alongside a Rs 3,000 crore FY24-25 capex programme and Rs 1,500-1,800 crore annual spend, i.e. expansion at the very point margins were compressing, with management later admitting it 'pushed volumes sometimes even at the expense of a slight compromise on the margins'.

## What to watch

- Whether the cyclical upturn named in the Cyclical Market Growth check (+2) actually shows up — China's anti-involution discipline and VAT-rebate withdrawal lifting NCB-chain prices — or whether the New Entrants risk stays ELEVATED and pricing pressure remains 'across the board'.
- Whether realised prices start contributing to growth alongside volumes; the -2 on Pricing, Mix and Volume and the NO FIT on Pricing Power both turn on management pushing volume at the expense of margin, and both would need to change together.
- Whether the heavy capex programme (Rs 1,125cr in FY26, after roughly Rs 3,000cr planned across FY24-25) finally lifts the 7% cash return on capital behind the Return on Capital -1 check and the 0.25 Capital Allocation score.
- Whether the UPL/Superform and RESL joint ventures and the PEDA downstream move get commissioned and deliver margin — the unproven step that keeps both Forward Integrators and the M&A check at partial credit, and the main answer to the HIGH RISK Good Enough Goods verdict.
- Concentration in MMA, guided to 30-40% of the portfolio with the US as key market, given the Stakeholder Concentration risk and the precedent of a long-term contract being terminated in Nov 2022 — and the related policy exposure (US tariffs, India-US and India-EU deals) behind the Government Dependency watch.
