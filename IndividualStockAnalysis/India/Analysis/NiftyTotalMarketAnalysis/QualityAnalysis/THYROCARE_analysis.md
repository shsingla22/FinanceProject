# Thyrocare Tech (THYROCARE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Thyrocare Technologies is an Indian diagnostics company that runs blood-testing (pathology) laboratories and sells lab tests — over 1,375 of them, from routine thyroid and lipid panels to preventive health check-up packages and newer allergy and genomic tests. It mostly works business-to-business: it does not run its own patient clinics but collects samples through a franchisee network of independent collection centres (roughly 11,700 active franchisees by mid-2026, up from 2,700 in FY21), plus home collection by about 2,000 phlebotomists, and through corporate tie-ups with insurers, health-tech apps, hospitals and government programmes. Money comes from charging franchisees and partners a per-test price, with volume-based ("pay-for-performance") discount slabs; samples are run in a hub-and-spoke network of 44 labs (17 in FY21) so that most of India is within 150 km of a lab, and about 210 million tests were processed in FY26 for 19.2 million patients. Operations are almost entirely across India, with a small overseas foray into Africa (a lab set up in Tanzania) and some partner activity in Malaysia; the company has been owned by API Holdings since July 2021.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Pathology (blood testing) — the core business** — Processing blood and other samples for a large menu of tests; management repeatedly calls it the core business driving overall growth, with pathology revenue growing about 20% in FY26 and 26% in Q1 FY27.
- **Franchisee business (channel)** — Sales through independent franchisee collection centres who book tests and send samples to Thyrocare labs; it is the largest revenue channel, with 10,800–11,700 active franchisees and growth of 18% in FY26 and 27% in Q1 FY27 under the slab-based pay-for-performance pricing.
- **Partnerships / B2B business** — Bulk testing for insurers (pre-policy and annual health checks, including at-home ECG via the Think Health acquisition), health-tech platforms and hospitals, often plugged in through API integrations; described as a strong growth engine, up 32% in FY26 and 29% year-on-year in Q1 FY25.
- **B2G (government) projects** — Testing contracts for state governments, such as TB programmes in Gujarat and Maharashtra; mentioned as a distinct but small part of the partnership business.
- **API group / parent-linked business** — Volumes routed from parent API Holdings' businesses such as PharmEasy pharmacy counters and its app; in Aug 2022 this was about 13% of pathology revenue and served roughly 5,000 patients a day, and it is later treated as a slower-growing piece separated out from the rest of partnerships.
- **Aarogyam** — The flagship preventive health check-up package brand, the bulk of consumer testing volume, growing about 20-21% year-on-year in recent quarters.
- **Jaanch** — A newer brand of disease- and symptom-specific packages (fever, hair fall, cancer screening, diabetes, heart health) for curative and chronic needs; small at about 2% of pathology revenue but fast-growing — 66% year-on-year in Q4 FY26.
- **Specialty diagnostics (allergy and genomics)** — A newly launched, higher-value testing line — allergy testing on the Phadia platform with 250+ SKUs and genomic tests including prenatal and carrier screening — commercially live from mid-2026 and positioned as the next long-term growth engine, currently very small.
- **Radiology / nuclear imaging (including Pulse Hitech)** — A separate imaging and nuclear medicine arm that is a minor contributor, growing 15% year-on-year in Q1 FY25 but at much lower profitability (about 8% EBITDA margin) than the pathology business.
- **COVID testing (historical)** — A large temporary revenue stream during the pandemic that collapsed 96% year-on-year by Q1 FY23, after which the labs were repurposed for other PCR uses; not a part of the business thereafter.
- **International (Africa)** — An early-stage overseas expansion with a lab set up in Tanzania to take the same low-cost model abroad; referred to often as ambition but with no material revenue disclosed in the calls.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Thyrocare's appeal starts with a business that turns very little capital into a lot of activity. Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.11 of sales, the company keeps ₹74 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs, and it generated ₹33 of operating cash for every ₹100 of capital employed this year, with ROCE rising from 15% in FY23 to 34% in FY26. Crucially, that improvement came alongside five years of genuine growth spending — ₹250cr+ invested, labs from 17 to 44 — so the money went into new capacity, not into simply keeping the lights on. The physical result is the distribution backbone the quality work flags as the real advantage: 11,700 collection points, 2,100 phlebotomists, a lab within 150km of anywhere in India, and a 3.37-hour national turnaround in a business where speed decides who wins the sample. Management's record supports the numbers — targets set in early calls were met later (NABL accreditation, falling turnaround times), and bad news is disclosed plainly, including the weak three-year non-COVID growth, weak North India, declining radiology, and a ₹4cr one-off that flattered a prior year.

The pattern screen rates only Corporate Culture a strong fit; most of the rest are likely or partial, and the reason is that the same trait that powers a pattern also powers a risk. Low price plus accreditation is the stated core of the model in all 17 calls, and it works — 210 million tests in FY26, 'higher than some of our biggest competitors combined', franchisees from 2,700 to 11,700, tests up 23% in FY26 and pathology up 26% in Q1 FY27, all won without sacrificing profitability. But because the model is built on lowering price rather than raising it, both Pricing Power and Toll Roads come out as no fit, and the Good Enough Goods risk is rated elevated: a routine blood test is close to a commodity, and the franchisee or prescribing doctor — not the patient — picks the lab. That intermediary structure is simultaneously the Friendly Middleman pattern (doctor advisory boards, 9 doctor meets, 256 doctor lab visits, 13 franchisee meets) and the channel through which the company can be undercut. The 2022 call is the proof: discount-led online entrants helped shrink the franchisee base from 4,500 to 2,700 and forced a re-cut to slab-based, pay-for-performance pricing.

The mitigants are real but they are cost and scale mitigants, not pricing ones. The defence against both elevated risks — New Entrants and Good Enough Goods — is the same: being the lowest-cost operator at a volume rivals cannot match, plus hard quality proof, with 100% of owned labs NABL accredited against only 2% of Indian labs. That accreditation gap is also the source of the share on offer, since the donor pool is a large unorganized standalone-lab base losing ground to accredited, faster chains. Two risks sit on watch: continuous re-equipping (Phadia, HPLC, next-generation sequencing, BioFire, NIPT/genomics, flagged AI capability) against a menu that has grown from about 700 tests to 1,375+, and the legacy API Holdings/PharmEasy relationship, once 13% of pathology revenue in FY23 and now down to roughly ₹13 crores. Four further risks — cyclicality, government dependency, consumer preference shifts and fashion — are all low, and the balance sheet carries zero debt and ₹230cr+ net cash. The weakest scores are industry structure at zero and competitive advantage at 0.67, and the Recurring Revenue read is a numbers-only screening hint with no call evidence behind it. Combined, that is 70 out of 100 — Strong, with resilience judged sound.

## What to watch

- Franchisee count and mix: the 2,700 to 11,700 build is the Market Share Gainers pattern and the Cost to Replicate barrier — a repeat of the 2022 collapse from 4,500 to 2,700 under the New Entrants risk would undo both.
- Discount structure and realisation: the slab-based pay-for-performance discounts and the FY26 insurance-segment pricing normalisation are what make Pricing Power a no fit — further price cuts to hold volume would confirm the Good Enough Goods risk rather than the Low Price Plus pattern.
- Turnaround time and accreditation: 3.37-hour lab TAT and 100% NABL on owned labs are the stated basis of the Distribution advantage and Convenience benefits checks; slippage on either removes the quality gap against the 98% of non-accredited labs.
- Returns on continued capex: ROCE moving 15% to 34% is what makes the 'capex for growth, not maintenance' check work — if the next round of lab and genomics spending does not carry returns with it, the Technological Innovation watch risk becomes the story.
- Concentration cleanup: the API/PharmEasy relationship has fallen from 13% of pathology revenue to roughly ₹13 crores under the Stakeholder Concentration risk — any renewed reliance on one partner channel would reverse the diversification credit.
