# Phoenix Mills Ltd. (PHOENIXLTD) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSTRUCTION*

Phoenix Mills is an Indian property company that builds and owns large, city-centre shopping malls and rents space in them to retailers — global and domestic brands like Apple, Ikea, Uniqlo, Rolex, Lego and Tanishq — under leases structured as a minimum guaranteed rent plus a share of the tenant's sales, so income rises as shopper spending rises. Around these malls it develops mixed-use campuses that also contain Grade-A office towers leased to corporates, hotels, and apartments it sells to homebuyers. Money comes mainly from rent and revenue share (retail rental income was Rs. 2,157 crore in FY26 on shopper spending of Rs. 16,587 crore), plus office rent, hotel room revenue and residential sales; consolidated revenue in FY26 was Rs. 4,423 crore with EBITDA of Rs. 2,637 crore. It operates only in India, with assets in Mumbai, Pune, Bangalore, Chennai, Lucknow, Indore, Ahmedabad and Agra, and new projects under construction in Kolkata and Surat.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Retail (malls)** — Owns and runs large destination malls — Phoenix Palladium Mumbai, the Phoenix MarketCity centres, Palassio Lucknow, Citadel Indore, Palladium Ahmedabad, Mall of the Millennium Pune and Mall of Asia Bangalore — leasing space to retailers, F&B and entertainment operators; management calls it "the core engine of the Phoenix portfolio," contributing Rs. 2,246 crore of EBITDA in FY26, the large majority of the group total.
- **Commercial offices** — Grade-A office buildings sitting inside the same retail-led campuses and leased to corporate occupiers; the portfolio grew from about 2 million sq ft in FY24 to roughly 4.8 million sq ft across Mumbai, Pune, Bangalore and Chennai, but is still small in earnings terms (Rs. 213 crore income and Rs. 141 crore EBITDA in FY26) with management expecting a step-up from FY27 as newly delivered buildings fill up.
- **Hospitality (hotels)** — Runs hotels — the marquee St. Regis in Mumbai and a Courtyard by Marriott in Agra — earning room and related revenue; FY26 hotel income was Rs. 596 crore with EBITDA of Rs. 276 crore, making it the second-largest profit contributor after retail.
- **Residential** — Builds and sells apartments, such as the One Bangalore West towers, booking sales as flats are sold and revenue when completion certificates come through (FY24 gross sales of about Rs. 566 crore and collections of Rs. 646 crore); management treats it as a source of "incremental cash flows" rather than a core earnings engine.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Phoenix Mills comes out of all three reviews as the same kind of company seen from three angles: an owner of scarce, city-centre shopping destinations that can raise what it charges without adding anything. The quality framework scores Competitive Advantage at the maximum 2.0 and notes that after direct input costs the company keeps about ₹93 of every ₹100 of sales, with FY26 rental income up 10-14% on zero new floor area — the growth came from repricing renewals and upgrading the tenant mix, not from building more. The multibagger screen reads the same evidence and calls Pricing Power, Brand Strength and Cost to Replicate all STRONG FIT: Palassio repriced 120 renewals at over 20%, brands choose the Phoenix and Palladium centres for India-first and South-India-first stores, and the underlying asset — prime land with existing infrastructure and decade-long approval-to-delivery cycles — simply cannot be copied quickly. Corporate Culture is the fourth strong fit and is really the management check restated: the FY22 promise of 1-2 million sq ft of retail a year turned into four delivered malls (Indore, Ahmedabad, Pune, Bangalore) that filled to 88-95% occupancy, and the 2027 roadmap is on schedule. Together these produce a combined score of 80 out of 100 — 45% weight on business quality at 77, 30% on multibagger fit at 92, and 25% on risk safety at 70.

The interesting part is that the single trait behind the moat is also the source of the two worst marks in the whole record. The advantage is scarce physical property, and property is heavy: every ₹1 of assets produces only ₹0.19 of sales, and roughly 734 days of cash sits tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. Both score -2, Return on Capital as an area is negative at -0.33, and Capital Allocation sits at only 0.5. That is not a contradiction to fix — it is the price of the Cost to Replicate pattern. The same decade-long approval cycles that keep competitors out (Thane, Coimbatore and Chandigarh are still in excavation years after the land was bought) are what park capital on the balance sheet for years before a rupee of rent appears, and they are also why Government Dependency shows up as a WATCH risk: occupation certificates gate multiplex and residential revenue, and the environmental clearance at Kolkata is still awaited. A second trait cuts both ways in the same manner. Premiumisation drives the Pricing Power fit, is the reason Low Price Plus is NO FIT, and shows up in residential realisation moving from ₹15,000 to ₹29,000 per sq ft — but it also anchors demand to discretionary spending, which is exactly why Cyclicality is the one risk rated ELEVATED rather than WATCH, with Covid cited as proof that this revenue can stop outright.

What keeps the risk side manageable is that the cushions are the same operating habits the strength checks reward. Five risks sit at WATCH and two at LOW, and resilience is judged SOUND. Rent is tied to tenant operations but sits on a minimum-guarantee floor, so a soft patch does not take income to zero; the company kept building through Covid rather than pausing, which is the tenacity the framework scores at 2. Tenant concentration is a live rather than theoretical worry — the Lifestyle block at Palladium was around ₹25-27 crore of annual rent and its exit dragged standalone income and like-for-like consumption for a full year — but the deliberate de-anchoring at Bangalore and Pune, plus 920 leasing deals and 400 store openings in FY26, spreads that exposure and simultaneously feeds the Market Share Gainers pattern. The online-shopping shift and changing consumer habits are answered by format work rather than technology spending: Gourmet Village and the Phoenix Racquet Club were launched, measured, then templated across the portfolio, which is why Innovation Dominance is only a LIKELY FIT. Growth is being funded out of operating cash flow with net debt to EBITDA improving to 1.19x even through peak construction and the ISMDPL buyout, and against a stated requirement that new projects earn over 20% before tax. Two screen patterns — Friendly Middleman and Forward Integrators — are flagged simply as not applicable to a business that already owns and operates its own space, and Recurring Revenue and Toll Roads register as PARTIAL because the rent is genuine annuity income but there is no upfront product sale behind it.
