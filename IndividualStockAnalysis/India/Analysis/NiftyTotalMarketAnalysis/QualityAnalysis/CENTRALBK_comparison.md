- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Weak → Good. - **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: Headline profit growth owes much to non-repeatable items rather than demonstrated skill — provisions fell 54% year-on-year, write-off recoveries swung violently quarter to quarter, and FY26 saw a one-time INR632 crore DTA hit — so results cannot yet be separated cleanly from cycle and low-base effects. | The long view had said: Headline improvement is materially flattered by non-core items — write-off recoveries swinging from INR1,062 crores to INR352 crores in a quarter, treasury gains from INR300+ crores to INR9 crores, tax-regime benefits — so results cannot be cleanly attributed to management skill, and core metrics (NIM down, cost-income ~58%) lag.
- **Candor** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Management proactively names its misses rather than burying them — flagging cost-to-income as a 'major area of concern', listing the three guidance parameters it failed, and explaining the quarter-on-quarter operating profit drop by naming treasury income falling from INR300 crore to INR9 crore and write-off recoveries from INR1,062 crore to INR352 crore. | The long view had said: Management leads with bad news — flagging the INR632 crores one-time DTA hit up front, attributing the operating-profit fall to treasury and lower write-off recovery, and admitting the 46% corporate growth is a low-base optical — but deflects on technology spend and lacks corporate-yield data on request.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: The bank appears past the trough of the rate/margin cycle — NIM bottomed at 2.89% in Q2 FY26 and recovered to 3.06% as cost of deposits fell 33bps to 4.60% and stabilised, with CD ratio rising from 64% to 74%. | The long view had said: The bank was near the credit-cycle bottom in 2022 (GNPA 15.52%, under PCA) and has ridden the full upturn to GNPA 2.60% and credit cost 0.40%, so the cyclical tailwind is now largely spent rather than ahead.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: A genuine regional stickiness exists in rural/semi-urban India where 65% of the 4,605 branches sit and 65% of branches carry over 50% CASA, producing customer inertia and cheap sticky deposits. | The long view had said: Some regional captivity exists via 65% rural/semi-urban branches, 52 lead-district responsibilities and sticky salary/government accounts, but switching costs in retail banking are low and no pricing benefit is visible.

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
