# AU Small Finance Bank Ltd. (AUBANK) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

AU Small Finance Bank is an Indian bank, headquartered in Rajasthan and listed in Mumbai (NSE: AUBANK), that started banking operations in April 2017 and lends mostly to small, self-employed borrowers — management says around 80% of its book is informal, self-employed customers who live off business earnings rather than a fixed salary. It takes deposits from retail savers, businesses, government bodies and financial institutions through a branch and touch-point network plus its AU 0101 digital app, and lends that money out as vehicle loans, small-business and home loans against property, gold loans, business/commercial banking loans, microfinance and unsecured credit cards and personal loans. It makes money mainly on the spread between what it pays depositors (cost of funds around 7%) and what it earns on loans (yield on advances around 14%), giving a net interest margin near 5.9-6%, plus fee income from insurance distribution, card interchange, deposit and lending charges, wealth management, foreign exchange and remittances, and treasury. By the June 2026 quarter it held about ₹1.58 lakh crore of deposits with a loan book growing 23% year-on-year, earning roughly ₹796 crore of quarterly profit at a 1.7% return on assets; it has applied to the RBI to convert into a full universal bank.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Deposits / liability franchise** — Takes savings, current and term deposits — ₹1.58 lakh crore as of June 2026, with a 29% CASA ratio — organised into four verticals: branch banking (60% of deposits), government and interbank (21%), financial institutions group (8%) and commercial banking/wholesale (7%).
- **Wheels (vehicle finance)** — The bank's oldest secured lending line, financing vehicles for small operators and self-employed borrowers; management calls it a core, well-performing secured vertical and is rolling it out into roughly 250 additional touch points inherited from the Fincare merger.
- **MBL (mortgage-backed / small business loans)** — Secured loans to small businesses against property; a key growth engine alongside Wheels, with disbursements up 18% quarter-on-quarter in Q2 FY25 and now sharing back-end teams with home loans to cut cost.
- **Home loans** — Housing loans to retail customers, one of the four secured retail verticals management lists as performing well; being merged operationally with the MBL business.
- **Gold loans** — Small loans against gold jewellery, part of the secured retail book and the first business to go live on the bank's new AI-based loan origination platform in 2026.
- **Secured retail assets (combined)** — Wheels, MBL, home loans and gold together make up about two-thirds of the loan book; management says they are well collateralised with historically low ultimate losses even when reported bad loans rise temporarily.
- **Commercial banking / business banking** — Lending and transaction services to businesses, including cash management, trade and FX; around 20% of the loan book, with the agri business folded into it in 2026.
- **Microfinance (MFI)** — Very small group loans to rural borrowers — about 7% of the book with an average outstanding of roughly ₹26,000 per loan across 61,000 villages — a cyclical business that saw sharply higher bad debts in FY25 before recovering by FY26.
- **Credit cards and personal loans (unsecured, digital)** — Digitally underwritten unsecured lending; growth was deliberately slowed in FY25 on rising credit costs and returned to growth by FY26, with card interchange and fees adding to non-interest income.
- **Fee and other income** — Non-lending earnings from distributing insurance for about 14 partners, deposit and loan fees, card interchange, wealth management, authorised-dealer (foreign exchange) and remittance services, PSLC sales and treasury — running at roughly a third of net interest income and growing 33% year-on-year in the June 2026 quarter.
- **Digital banking and AI platform (AU 0101)** — The bank's app and technology stack, now handling over 90% of transactions and service requests, with an agentic-AI platform being used to rebuild loan origination, customer service voice bots in 11 languages, and analytics-driven credit and collections.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## The verdict: Decent — 51 out of 100 ★★★☆☆

In one breath: the quality framework finds an average business today (57/100); it strongly fits 2 of the 11 patterns long-term winners share; the risk review found 1 high risk (Government Dependency). Weighing those together gives 51 out of 100 — decent.

**The exact arithmetic:** Overall = 45% × 57 (business quality) + 30% × 30 (multibagger fit) + 25% × 64 (risk safety) = 51 out of 100 → Decent (3 stars).

- **Business quality (57/100):** The 34-check quality framework scored the business +0.30 on its −2 (poor) to +2 (excellent) scale, with 88% of checks backed by evidence; mapped onto 0–100 that is 57 points.
- **Multibagger fit (30/100):** the foundation test did not pass (+0); 2 patterns fit strongly (Market Share Gainers, Corporate Culture) (+30) → 30 of 100.
- **Risk safety (64/100):** started from a clean 100; 1 high risk (Government Dependency) (−20); 4 worth watching (−16) → 64 of 100.

### The story in depth

AU Small Finance Bank's case rests on reach and the people running it. The 34-check review gives its top marks to distribution (2,400-plus touch points scaled to roughly 900-1,300 branches per product line, extended further through Fincare), to the convenience that dense branches plus a digital channel and a large field force create in semi-urban markets bigger banks underserve, and to management — a founder who has run the bank for a decade with an explicit multi-year 'forever bank' horizon and RBI approval to stay to April 2029, an internal bench that has filled the CFO, Deputy CEO and Executive Director seats from within, and unusual candor in admitting missed guidance and that credit cards are loss-making. Those same traits are what turn two of the eleven multibagger patterns into strong fits rather than partial ones: Market Share Gainers, because deposits are growing 23-24% against roughly 13-14% for private-sector banks and loans 21-23% against 13-17%, off a small base into a retail and small-business credit market that is still expanding; and Corporate Culture, which is essentially the management checks restated. Customer Benefits, Growth and Management are the only quality areas scoring at or above 1.0.

The trouble is that most of the other traits pull in both directions at once. The banking licence, nine years of build-out, the ₹1.58 lakh crore granular deposit book and the underwriting data on informal self-employed borrowers (AU is the sole lender to 41% of that book) are what make the franchise expensive to copy — and they are also why the biggest single risk, Government Dependency, is rated HIGH: the licence itself, the pending universal-bank application, the NOFHC amendment, the approval of the founder's tenure and draft liquidity norms all sit with the regulator. Pricing tells the same double story. Cost discipline is real and itemised — branch profitability up from 25% to 43%, cost-to-income cut from 61% to 57%, cost-to-assets down 19 basis points to 4.1%, margins at 5.9-5.96% — but the Pricing Power and Low Price Plus patterns only half-fit, and the Good Enough Goods and New Entrants risks both sit on WATCH for the same underlying reason: core banking products are largely undifferentiated, so the bank pays up for deposits ahead of the industry and concedes it must price with discipline in a mortgage market where it is growing only 11-12%. Brand spending ('Badlaav Humse Hai', premium accounts up 80%) and the innovation programme (AU 0101 carrying over 90% of transactions, AI scorecards, Agentic AI, voice bots in 11 languages) score partial for the same honest reason management gives — the brand is being built rather than proven, and the technology keeps AU ahead for now while the risk review flags that AI is reshaping the very distribution and underwriting edge that is its moat.

What holds the rating to 51 out of 100, or Decent, is the economics underneath the growth rather than the growth itself. Return on Capital scores the worst of the seven quality areas at -1.0, and the asset-turn check is negative by design: a bank earns through leverage on a ₹1.58 lakh crore deposit base at a 1.6-1.8% return on assets and roughly 15% return on equity, which is the opposite of asset-light, and Industry Structure is also negative. That is why business quality comes in at 57 and multibagger fit at only 30 — nine of the eleven patterns are partial fits, none is a miss, but only two are strong. Risk safety at 64 is the highest of the three inputs, helped by two risks rated LOW: the customer base is genuinely granular on both sides, with microfinance spread over 61,000 villages, no district above 3% and no state above 12% of that book, and the one visible shift in customer preference — banking moving to digital — is one AU is winning, with new CASA acquisition up 62% and over a lakh accounts added monthly. Cyclicality stays on watch because credit costs did swing to about 1.28% in FY25 through the microfinance and credit-card cycles, with five straight quarters of card de-growth, cushioned by two-thirds secured retail assets. Resilience was not established either way in this review, so the verdict is a fast-growing, well-run, well-distributed lender whose returns and differentiation are ordinary and whose fate is closely tied to its regulator.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Fortunes are pervasively set by the regulator and government schemes across all 16 calls: the SFB licence itself and the pending universal-bank application (including RBI amending the NOFHC requirement and approving the MD's tenure), draft LCR norms, RBI's foreclosure-charge circular on pricing, the coming ECL framework that analysts say adds 12-20bps to steady-state credit cost, PSLC/priority-sector, NHB refinance, AD-I licence, and 92-96% of the MFI book now leaning on the government CGFMU guarantee (whose premium is a visible cost line).

> "92% of the MFI book is now covered under the CGFMU guarantee scheme" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Diversification of revenue across wheels, mortgages, gold, commercial banking and fee income reduces reliance on any single scheme, but the regulatory frame itself is inescapable*

*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

> "MFI, which is 7% of our book is cyclical nature and asset quality is elevated, driven by industry-wide issue of overleveraging of customers" — *management, on an earnings call*
