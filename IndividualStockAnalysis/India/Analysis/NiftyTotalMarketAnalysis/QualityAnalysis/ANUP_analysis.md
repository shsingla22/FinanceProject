# Anup Engineering (ANUP) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

The Anup Engineering Limited is an Indian fabricator of large custom-built process equipment — mainly heat exchangers, plus pressure vessels, reactors, columns, silos and centrifuges — that it builds to order for plants in oil & gas, refining, petrochemicals, fertilizers, hydrogen and, more recently, nuclear and thermal power. It sells to refiners, EPC contractors and technology licensors (for example a Graham Corporation tie-up in the US where Anup is the exclusive manufacturer, and licensed designs from Lummus and Brembana & Rolle on which it pays royalties), winning work order-by-order and recognising revenue as each project is executed, so results depend on the pending order book (about INR 770 crores entering FY27) and an inquiry pipeline of roughly INR 1,100–1,200 crores. Money is made on the margin between the contract price and steel, labour and subcontracting costs, with FY26 consolidated revenue of INR 822.3 crores and EBITDA of INR 174.2 crores (about 21%). It manufactures from three locations — Ahmedabad and Kheda in Gujarat and Mabel Engineers in Tamil Nadu, plus a design office at Vadodara — and ships slightly over half its revenue as exports, to the Middle East, Europe and the US.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Heat exchangers** — Its core product and the biggest revenue line — around 56–57% of revenue in FY26 (72% in H1 FY25) — including specialised and licensed proprietary designs such as helical and EMBaffle exchangers, focused mainly at the Ahmedabad plant.
- **Vessels, reactors and columns** — Large pressure vessels, reactors and distillation columns at about 35% of FY26 revenue, made largely at the Kheda plant, which management says is the proof that the Kheda investment is paying off.
- **Silos, centrifuges and other products** — A smaller catch-all bucket that rose to about 7% of revenue, into which management has been adding first-time products such as skids, air heaters, low-pressure feed water heaters for NTPC thermal projects and precision machined turbine-frame components for GE.
- **Ahmedabad (Odhav) plant** — The original and largest works, delivering INR 540 crores of FY26 revenue (66% of the 9-month total), and the site of the heat-exchanger focus, a 150-tonne bay and clean room for exotic materials.
- **Kheda plant** — The new Gujarat facility built in phases from FY23 onward; after Phase 2 it has 20,000 square metres of fabrication area and about 8,000 tonnes a year of capacity, capable of INR 400–450 crores of revenue, and it contributed INR 239 crores in FY26.
- **Mabel Engineers (Tamil Nadu subsidiary)** — A 100%-owned acquired unit doing silos and site-fabrication work; still small at INR 43 crores of FY26 revenue and behind management's earlier INR 100 crore ambition because site projects ran long.
- **Anup Technical Services** — A young services vertical that had over 10 orders executed in FY26 — small today, but management calls it potentially highly profitable and wants to grow it into a sizeable business.
- **Vadodara design office** — An in-house engineering and design centre that removes a bottleneck in design work as volumes grow and has begun taking on small paid work of its own.

### The story in depth

Anup Engineering builds heat exchangers, columns, reactors and similar heavy engineered equipment for refinery, petrochemical, fertiliser, LNG and power projects, and the single most convincing thing in these records is that management does what it says it will do. The 34-check framework gives top marks for candor (shortfalls on working capital, the Mabel miss and the Q4 margin dip are owned unprompted, and FY27 guidance is withheld rather than invented) and for growth capex: the multi-year Kheda greenfield promised back in 2022 was delivered on schedule, lifting group capacity from about Rs400cr of revenue to Rs1,200cr while holding a roughly 21% return on capital employed. Geographic expansion was another promise kept, with exports going from about 10% of the order book in FY23 to 53.4% of revenue by 9M FY26 on the back of a Dubai sales head and wins in the US, Middle East, Australia and Nigeria. This is exactly why Corporate Culture is the only outright STRONG FIT in the 11-pattern screen, and why Management is the one quality area scoring a perfect 1.0 — the pattern and the checks are reading the same evidence.

The harder part of the story is that the trait producing Anup's best patterns is also the source of its worst risks. Anup competes on customer approvals, licensor qualifications and the ability to make difficult things — exotic materials, clean-room work, 200 MT single-piece equipment — which is what earns PARTIAL scores on Toll Roads, Cost to Replicate and Market Share Gainers, and which management protects by refusing volume at lower prices, capping its strike rate and walking away from orders in FY26 rather than booking them cheap (Low Price Plus is a clear NO FIT). But that same posture is precisely the New Entrants and Good Enough Goods risks, both ELEVATED: this is competitively tendered work where cheaper bidders undercut, the historical strike rate is only 15-20%, and raising it would mean giving up margin. Pricing Power is also a NO FIT because every contract is fixed price, so steel inflation is absorbed rather than passed on — EBITDA margin slid from 24.5% in FY22 to around 21% in FY26, and the May-2026 call describes deferring raw-material purchases and delaying order booking because prices cannot be recovered. The same overlap appears on the technology side: capability keeps extending through the Vadodara design office, Graham exclusivity and entry into skids, air heaters and energy storage, but the technology is licensed in rather than generated (a 1.3% revenue royalty is disclosed), so Innovation Dominance is only a LIKELY FIT while the licensors and EPC channel that route the orders — Graham, Lummus, Brembana & Rolle, and a single Rs103cr refinery order — are the ELEVATED Stakeholder Concentration risk. Friendly Middleman and Stakeholder Concentration are, in effect, the same fact scored twice.

What holds the overall verdict down is cash and capital rather than operations. About 199 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and the headline return-on-capital check records zero operating cash generated per Rs100 of capital employed this year — both scored at the bottom of the range, which is why Return on Capital comes out negative, Capital Allocation and Competitive Advantage both score zero, and overall resilience is marked STRAINED. Growth has averaged about 27% a year but is lumpy year to year, and margins, while steady at roughly Rs21 of operating profit per Rs100 of sales, have been volume- and capacity-led rather than price-led. FY26 itself was a trough — stalled US projects and weak order booking — with the recovery case resting on identifiable but not-yet-banked drivers: the India-US trade deal, 14GW of revived thermal power, and a record Rs1,200cr enquiry bank. The company's own answer to the cyclicality that all of this reflects is the new Anup Technical Services arm doing health assessments, site repairs and MRO on the installed base, targeted at about Rs200cr in three years at roughly 40% margins; that is both the PARTIAL Recurring Revenue pattern and the stated silver lining on the ELEVATED Cyclicality risk, and it is still a plan rather than a track record. Netting it out — 57 on business quality, 41 on multibagger fit, 48 on risk safety — the combined score is 50 out of 100, a Decent three-star rating: a well-run, honestly managed builder whose execution is proven but whose economics, pricing and cash conversion are not yet.
