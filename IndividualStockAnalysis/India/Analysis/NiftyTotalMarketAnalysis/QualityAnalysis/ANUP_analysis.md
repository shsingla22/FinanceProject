# Anup Engineering (ANUP) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

The Anup Engineering Limited is an Indian custom-fabrication ('project industry') company that builds large made-to-order process equipment — mainly heat exchangers, plus pressure vessels, reactors, columns, silos and centrifuges — out of steel and exotic alloys. Its customers are process plants and the EPC contractors that build them, chiefly in oil & gas and petrochemicals (about 71% of FY26 revenue), with fertilizers, hydrogen, nuclear and thermal power making up the rest. It makes money order-by-order: it books orders (a pending order book of roughly INR 550-930 crores at various points, with an inquiry pipeline around INR 1,100-1,200 crores), fabricates the equipment over long cycles at its plants, and bills on delivery — FY26 consolidated revenue was INR 822.3 crores at a 21% EBITDA margin. It manufactures at Ahmedabad and Kheda in Gujarat plus Mabel Engineers in Tamil Nadu, with a design office at Vadodara and a sales presence in Dubai; roughly half of sales are exports (53% of revenue in 9M FY26), including the Middle East, Europe and the US.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Heat exchangers** — The core product and biggest revenue line — equipment that transfers heat inside refineries and chemical plants — at 72% of revenue in Q2 FY25 and 56-57% in FY26, including licensed proprietary designs such as helical exchangers for Lummus Heat Transfer and EMBaffle exchangers for Brembana & Rolle; management targets roughly a 60:40 split with vessels.
- **Vessels, reactors and columns** — Large fabricated pressure vessels, reaction chambers and tall distillation columns, at 35% of FY26 revenue (24% in Q2 FY25) — the growth here is what management credits to the newer Kheda plant.
- **Silos, centrifuges and other products** — A smaller catch-all product bucket, around 4-7% of revenue, that rose to 7% in 9M FY26.
- **Ahmedabad (Odhav) plant** — The original and largest works, focused strategically on heat exchangers; it did INR 540 crores of FY26 revenue (66% of the total) and runs about 60% on rooftop solar and windmill power.
- **Kheda plant** — The new Gujarat greenfield plant built in phases and focused on vessels, reactors and columns; it contributed INR 239 crores in FY26 (30%) and, with Phase 2 complete, now has 20,000 sq m of fabrication area and about 8,000 tonnes/year capacity worth INR 400-450 crores of annual revenue.
- **Mabel Engineers (100% subsidiary, Tamil Nadu)** — An acquired unit doing silos and site-fabrication work; the smallest location at INR 43 crores of FY26 revenue (4%), and it fell short of the INR 100 crore target management had hoped for because site projects ran long.
- **Vadodara design office** — An in-house engineering/design centre that removes design bottlenecks as volumes grow and has begun taking small paid third-party work of its own.
- **Anup Technical Services** — A newer services vertical (site work and technical services rather than shop fabrication) — over 10 orders executed in FY26; still small, but management expects it to be highly profitable and wants to scale it.
- **Technology tie-ups / licensed equipment** — Collaborations that let Anup build other firms' patented designs — most notably being the exclusive global manufacturer for Graham Corporation (USA) vacuum and heat-transfer systems, estimated at INR 30-40 crores of FY26 revenue from India alone — with royalty costs of about 1.3% of revenue attached.
- **New product and sector entries** — Recent first-time orders opening up adjacent markets: nuclear (an NPCIL Kaiga order of INR 20-30 crores), thermal power (low-pressure feed water heaters for NTPC projects, INR 20-30 crores), precision machined turbine-frame components for GE, plus first orders for skids, air heaters and clean-energy storage equipment — each small today but positioned as multi-year openings.

### The story in depth

Anup Engineering makes large engineered equipment — heat exchangers, columns, pressure vessels — for refinery, petrochemical, fertiliser and power projects, and the combined verdict on it is Mixed: 46 out of 100, or two stars, built from 51 on business quality, 41 on multibagger fit and 44 on risk safety, with overall resilience judged Strained. The clearest strength across all three reviews is the same one: management. The quality framework scores Management at +0.67 and Customer Benefits at +0.75, and the pattern screen gives Corporate Culture its only STRONG FIT — the Kheda greenfield plant promised on the August 2022 call was operational by November 2024, Phase 2 was finished by February 2026, the capacity now supports about Rs 1,200 crores of revenue, and management volunteers its own bad news, including working capital misses, the Mabel shortfall and a weak fourth quarter. That credibility is why the capex check scores +1: the money was spent on genuine growth, not upkeep, and it is visibly producing revenue.

The trouble is that the very traits that earn credit in one review create the exposure in another. The growth is real — revenue went from about Rs 52 crores in a single quarter of FY23 to Rs 822 crores for FY26, and exports rose from roughly 10% of the order book to more than half of revenue — which is what makes Market Share Gainers and Geographical expansion partial positives. But that growth comes from winning large, lumpy, competitively bid, fixed-price contracts, and that is exactly what makes Cyclicality and Stakeholder Concentration both ELEVATED risks: revenue moves with other companies' capital spending, and the US portion of the export push has already collapsed to near zero on tariff uncertainty, so the expansion is genuine but fragile. Similarly, the discipline of refusing cheap orders — walking away from work in May 2026 and declining to push the win rate above 20–25% because it would hurt margins — is a mark of quality, yet it is also why Low Price Plus, Toll Roads and Pricing Power all score NO FIT and why New Entrants and Good Enough Goods are ELEVATED. Because contracts are fixed-price and the company absorbs raw-material moves itself, operating margin slid from 24.5% in FY22 to about 21%, and in May 2026 it deferred Rs 200 crores of steel buying and withheld FY27 guidance. The same pattern shows in technology: new capability is added every year, but it is licensed in from Graham, Lummus and Brembana & Rolle at a 1.3% royalty, which keeps Innovation Dominance at only a likely fit while adding to the partner-concentration risk.

The weakest link is what happens to the cash. Capital Allocation scores -0.5 and Return on Capital -0.33, and two individual checks score the worst possible -2: about 199 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and for every Rs 100 of capital in the business the company generated Rs 0 of operating cash this year — a 0% cash return, even though the new capacity itself is earning roughly 21%. In other words, a business that delivers its projects on time is not yet converting that delivery into cash, and it is doing so at a visible cyclical low: the order book is down to Rs 550 crores, steel costs are high, US projects are stalled, FY26 order intake fell, and the Mabel acquisition guided to Rs 100 crores finished at Rs 43 crores. Against that, the identified upturn drivers are the India-US trade deal, a 14 GW thermal revival and the Rs 1,200 crores of installed capacity now sitting idle-capable, plus one genuinely new leg: Anup Technical Services, the inspection and site-repair business at roughly 40% margins with 10 purchase orders in six months and a Rs 200 crore three-year target. That services push is the single item that answers several problems at once — it is the reason Recurring Revenue and Forward Integrators score partial rather than no fit, and it is named as the silver lining against the cyclicality risk. Whether it scales is the hinge on which the Mixed rating turns.
