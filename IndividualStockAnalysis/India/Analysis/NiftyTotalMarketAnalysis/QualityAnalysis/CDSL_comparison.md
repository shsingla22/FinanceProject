### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 6 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Toll Roads**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: CDSL is one of two licensed depositories, a SEBI-designated Market Infrastructure Institution whose fees are a trivial share of an issuer's or investor's cost base yet indispensable to settlement; management repeatedly frames itself in regulatory/standards terms (vertical 1/2/3 SEBI regulations, MII status) and processes reforms like nomination Phase 2 and Form 15G/15H-to-Form 121 as mandated plumbing. (The long view had said: Depository fees are a trivial part of an investor's or issuer's cost yet operationally indispensable, in a SEBI-licensed duopoly where tariffs are regulator-approved rather than negotiated; the Feb 2026 KRA discussion shows the same regulatory anchoring ('the KRAs are here to stay') protecting the CVL toll.)

**That weakened:**

- **Friendly Middleman**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Depository participants (brokers) choose which depository an investor's account sits with while the investor/issuer pays the charges, and management explicitly credits DP trust for its incremental share of 85-90%. However, the FY26 calls contain no discussion of trade training programmes or channel investment, so the classic lock-in mechanics are only partly evidenced. (The long view had said: The depository participant, not the end investor, decides where an account is opened, and the May 2026 call frames the entire tech spend as sustaining the 'value proposition' and loyalty of DPs of differing scale via APIs — driving 85-90% incremental account share without end-customer price competition.)
- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Leadership is stable and long-tenured (Vora as MD/CEO and Amesara as CFO since FY2022 across the whole record), spend is framed as long-term infrastructure rather than quarterly optics, and the CFO proactively flagged that prior-year profits were flattered by INR47.5cr/INR62cr intra-group dividends — a candid disclosure. Against that, management declines routine disclosure requests (IPO vs corporate-action split, listed vs unlisted issuer revenue) and gives no guidance, so transparency is partial. (The long view had said: Vora, Amesara and Alvares have led continuously from FY22 to FY26 with a consistent no-guidance discipline, openly reported bad news (CVL PBT halving, tech-cost overruns, consolidated profit decline) and repeatedly chose long-term capacity investment over near-term margin while paying out dividends from a debt-free balance sheet.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Cost to Replicate (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (LIKELY FIT); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 0 worsened, 7 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Revenue is levered to capital-market activity: management flagged ADTV down ~18% year-on-year in Q2 FY26 and ~8.3% year-on-year in Q3, and consolidated 9M FY26 PAT fell to INR375cr from INR426cr while CVL's 9M PAT halved to INR42cr from INR91cr; but the annuity base (annual issuer charges, 18.01 crore demat accounts, ~2.7 crore accounts opened in FY26) is operations-linked and kept growing through the slowdown. (The long view had said: Revenue is levered to market activity across the whole 2022-2026 arc: muted volumes hurt Q1FY23, FY24 income jumped 46% on a 126% turnover surge and record 1cr+ demat openings, then FY26 flattened (consol income INR1,239cr vs INR1,199cr) with consolidated PAT falling to INR455cr from INR526cr and CVL PAT halving to INR55cr, while technology cost kept compounding ~30% through the downturn — the classic peak-investment-into-trough margin squeeze.)

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
