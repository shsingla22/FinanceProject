# Mankind Pharma (MANKIND) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Mankind Pharma is an Indian drug maker that sells branded generic medicines — prescription products promoted to doctors, plus over-the-counter consumer health brands sold in shops and online. It earns money mainly by getting doctors to prescribe its brands and then selling those medicines through a large distribution network (50-plus C&F depots) at deliberately low, affordable prices, backed by a very wide field force covering about 4 lakh doctors and manufacturing roughly 75% of its products in its own plants. The business is overwhelmingly India-focused (about 97% of revenue at the time of listing in 2023), with a smaller and faster-growing export arm; FY26 revenue was about INR14,278 crores, of which INR12,217 crores was domestic and INR2,061 crores was exports. Over time it has shifted from mostly short-course 'acute' medicines toward long-term 'chronic' lifestyle-disease drugs (heart, diabetes, respiratory, neuro/psychiatry), which rose from 34% of domestic sales in FY23 to roughly 39% in FY26, and has bought its way into higher-barrier specialty areas via the Panacea and Bharat Serums & Vaccines acquisitions.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Domestic (India) pharma business** — The core engine — branded prescription medicines sold to Indian doctors and patients across acute therapies (gastro, anti-infectives, vitamins, gynaecology, derma) and chronic therapies (cardiac, anti-diabetic, respiratory, neuro), worth INR12,217 crores in FY26 (about 86% of company revenue) and consistently ranked No. 1 in prescriptions for nine straight years with a 15.1% prescription share.
- **Chronic / specialty chronic segment (within domestic)** — The strategic growth priority — long-term medicines for heart disease, diabetes, respiratory and neuro conditions, including in-licensed and niche launches such as Crenzlo (inclisiran from Novartis), Vonalong, inhalers, and a pertuzumab biosimilar; management says it grew from 34% of domestic sales in FY23 to about 39% in FY26 and consistently grows faster than the overall Indian pharma market.
- **Consumer Healthcare / OTC business** — Non-prescription consumer brands sold directly to shoppers — Manforce condoms, Prega News and Ova News pregnancy/ovulation tests, Unwanted 72, AcneStar, Gas-O-Fast and Nimulid — carved out into a wholly owned subsidiary in FY25; it brought in INR879 crores in FY26 (roughly 6% of revenue) and is increasingly sold through modern trade, e-commerce and quick-commerce, which now make up about 13% of OTC sales.
- **International / export business** — Sales of its products outside India, mostly into emerging and semi-regulated markets, which grew 35% in FY26 to INR2,061 crores (about 14% of revenue), supported by EU GMP certification of its Udaipur and Ambernath plants.
- **BSV (Bharat Serums and Vaccines) specialty portfolio** — Acquired and consolidated from Q3 FY25, this adds hard-to-make women's health, fertility/IVF, critical care, recombinant, biologics and immunoglobulin products (brands like Anti-D, Foligraf and HMG) sold both in India and for export; management says it makes Mankind the No. 1 player in gynaecology and is delivering strong double-digit growth.
- **In-house manufacturing** — Mankind makes about 75% of its own products in its own plants, which management ties directly to its ability to keep prices affordable and protect gross margins (around 71-73% in recent quarters).
- **R&D and in-licensing** — A relatively small but rising spend (about 1.9% of sales in Q2 FY25, guided at 2-2.5%) used to develop products in-house — management cites Dydrogesterone as its signature achievement — and to license innovator products, with BSV adding complex biologics and delivery-technology capability.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Mankind Pharma comes out of all three reviews as a Strong business, rated 76 out of 100 (45% × 65 on business quality, 30% × 89 on multibagger fit, 25% × 80 on risk safety), with resilience judged sound. The single thread running through every record is reach: coverage of 4 lakh doctors, more than 50 C&F depots that were expanded when peers were cutting, 84.1% prescriber penetration and nine straight years at the number-one spot in prescriptions. That one asset scores the maximum on both the distribution and convenience checks, and it is what makes the Friendly Middleman pattern a strong fit — the doctor writing the prescription, not the patient, is the customer being won. It also explains the money: after direct input costs the company keeps ₹72 of every ₹100 of sales, ₹25 of every ₹100 is operating profit, margins have moved only about ±2.9 points a year, and ex-cash returns on capital run at 25–35%. Alongside that sits a brand record that has compounded — brand families above ₹50cr went from 29 in FY20 to 37 in FY23 to 54 in FY26, and ₹200cr+ brands from 11 to 13 — and a management team the culture pattern rates a strong fit for keeping its word: the FY23 promise of 24–26% margins was delivered (25.4% adjusted in FY26), and the pledge to hold net debt to EBITDA below 2x by FY26 was beaten at 1.1x.

The uncomfortable part of the story is that the same traits that earn the patterns also create the risks. The prescription-led model that makes Friendly Middleman a strong fit is exactly what the Good Enough Goods risk flags: because the doctor chooses and end-patients have low brand awareness, and because the molecules are not complex, a cheaper equivalent can undercut — and Mankind's own answer is to compete on price rather than to defend a premium. Pricing follows the same pattern: the Pricing Power screen is only a partial fit because every price increase is anchored to what NLEM and industry ceilings permit, and the Government Dependency risk shows what that costs in practice, with Codistar hit for four consecutive quarters and Unwanted-72 restricted. The brand-building strength has a mirror too — the New Entrants risk is not a rival company but the e-commerce and quick-commerce channel reshaping how over-the-counter products are sold: modern trade and e-commerce grew over 40% and now make up 13% of OTC sales while general trade turned soft, and OTC growth slowed to 9% in FY26 with an outright decline in one quarter. Even the growth engine cuts both ways: the structural shift toward chronic and lifestyle disease, which lifted the chronic mix from 32% in FY20 to about 39% in FY26 and earns a maximum score for end-market growth, is the same shift the Shifting Consumer Preferences risk describes on its losing side, with anti-infectives staying muted right through FY26.

FY26 is where the strains show up together. The Market Share Gainers pattern is only a likely fit rather than a confirmed one because the trajectory broke that year — secondary sales grew 8.7% against industry growth of 10.7% excluding GLP-1 drugs, with acute and anti-infective products losing ground during the sales-force reset. That reset traces straight back to a strength: the independence check scores the maximum partly for deliberate five-yearly commercial-excellence resets that knowingly sacrifice near-term growth, and management has openly owned the FY26 field-force disruption. Meanwhile GLP-1 is both the biggest named opportunity in the end-market check and the sharpest item in the Technological Innovation risk — the segment now distorts industry growth so much that comparisons must be reported excluding it, and Mankind launched only around April 2026, behind the rush. Innovation Dominance is likewise only a likely fit, since R&D is rising (2.3% of sales in FY23 to 2.8% in FY26, with a new Vadodara biotech facility) and launches such as Crenzlo and Vonalong carry real revenue, but much of the pipeline is in-licensed. The scorecard is honest about the ceiling: industry structure scores zero and competitive advantage only 0.33, the Cost to Replicate pattern is partial because a well-funded incumbent could contest a branded-generics network, and ₹117 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. Against that, the risk review found genuinely low exposure on cyclicality, stakeholder concentration and fashion risk — no single product or category dependence, a very wide doctor and stockist base, and functional repeat-purchase demand.

## What to watch
