# Coforge (COFORGE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Coforge is an India-headquartered IT services firm that builds, tests, runs and modernises software and technology systems for large companies, and lately sells AI-based work — what it calls AI-led engineering, data and integration, and cloud services, which made up 86% of revenue in the June 2026 quarter. Its customers are big enterprises concentrated in banking and financial services, insurance, travel and transportation, healthcare and hi-tech, and non-Indian government bodies; the top 10 clients were about 26-31% of revenue, with one client above $100 million a year. It earns money mainly through multi-year outsourcing and project contracts won as 'large deals' (21 signed in FY26, order intake $691 million in Q1 FY27) that convert into a rolling 12-month 'executable order book' of $2.23 billion, delivered largely by offshore staff in India (about 51% offshore back in 2023) plus onsite teams, so revenue tracks headcount, utilisation (~82.5%) and billing rates. Revenue was $1.445 billion in FY25, grew 29.2% in FY26, and Q1 FY27 was $592.2 million, with staff of 46,228; selling is concentrated in the Americas and EMEA, with a smaller Rest of the World business.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Banking and Financial Services (BFS)** — Technology work for banks, payments and financial firms — historically the biggest vertical at around 31% of revenue in 2023 and still 24.7% in Q1 FY27, and the source of the company's earliest large deals such as a 5-year $300 million contract.
- **Insurance** — Software, claims and policy-processing work for insurers, including AI tools that pull data out of policy and claims documents and plug into insurance platforms like Duck Creek and Guidewire; roughly 22.6% of revenue in 2023, trimmed to 13.6% by Q1 FY27.
- **Travel, Transportation and Hospitality** — Systems and AI solutions for airlines, transport operators and air-transport communications firms (for example cutting weather alerts to pilots by 75%); about 18.5% of revenue in 2023 and 21.3% in Q1 FY27, and one of the fastest growers at 62% in FY26.
- **Healthcare and Hi-Tech** — A newer, fast-expanding vertical serving healthcare and technology companies that grew 98% in FY26 and contributed 17.3% of revenue in Q1 FY27.
- **Government outside India** — Public-sector technology contracts in markets other than India, growing 17.5% in FY26 and contributing about 6% of revenue in Q1 FY27; management separately announced it was exiting the India government business.
- **Others / emerging verticals (retail, manufacturing)** — A catch-all group of remaining industries including retail and manufacturing that grew 27% in FY26 and was 17.1% of revenue in Q1 FY27, though it declined 8% after deliberate exits from unwanted business.
- **AI-led Engineering** — The largest service line at 50% of revenue in Q1 FY27 — building and modernising software with AI embedded in the delivery process, supported by 11,000+ data and AI practitioners, 8 AI platforms and roughly $58 million of AI investment in FY26.
- **Data and Integration** — Work on clients' data systems and connecting their applications together, at 21% of revenue in Q1 FY27.
- **Cloud** — Moving and running client systems on cloud infrastructure, at 15% of revenue in Q1 FY27.
- **Intelligent Automation and Business Process Management** — Automating and running back-office business processes for clients, each contributing about 7% of revenue in Q1 FY27.
- **Nuuron (AI operationalisation platform)** — The company's own software layer, launched by FY27, meant to take clients from AI pilots to live AI operations by tying together enterprise knowledge, workflows, governance and AI agents — positioned as the centrepiece of its future growth story rather than a separately sized revenue line.
- **Cigniti (acquired software testing business)** — A listed testing-services firm bought around 2024 that management says was fully absorbed, lifted from 12% to 19% EBITDA margin, and whose top two clients grew from $25-30 million to $75 million a year through cross-selling.
- **Encora (acquired in FY27)** — An acquisition completed in mid-2026 that added 9,256 employees and about 25% more shares outstanding, contributed two months of Q1 FY27 revenue, and broadened the client base; earlier tuck-ins named on the calls include SLK Global and Incessant.

### The story in depth

Coforge is an asset-light services business that turns very little invested money into a lot of revenue: every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.10 of sales, physical spending on equipment is tiny ($8m in one quarter of FY24), and there are almost no direct input costs, so nearly all of every ₹100 of sales is available for people, marketing and profit. That structure shows up in the returns: ₹18 of operating profit per ₹100 of sales with margins moving only about 1.4 points a year, ₹17 of operating cash per ₹100 of total capital employed, and FY26 free cash flow of $135m with management guiding to converting all of its profit into cash. Growth has been unusually strong for the industry — 18.4% in FY24 after stripping out currency moves, then 31.5% and 29.2% in dollars in years when peers were flat or shrinking, with the ten largest clients growing 40.4% — and it came alongside a 3.7 percentage point rise in operating profit margin, so this is growth that pays for itself rather than growth bought with discounts. The people numbers back it up: staff turnover fell from 13.3% to 10.9% to 10.4% and is among the lowest in the industry, with campus offers honoured and bonuses paid in full through a weak period.

Those same traits are what the pattern screen picks up, and the three strongest patterns are really one story told three ways. Because the business needs so little capital, management can pour money into capability instead — roughly 100 AI solutions in 2023 becoming 200-plus and a marketplace by 2025, then eight platforms, 22 assets and 150-plus scaled deployments, with $58m of AI investment in FY26 — which is why Innovation Dominance scores a strong fit. That capability is then used to take work away from weaker incumbent suppliers rather than to wait for client budgets to grow, which is why Market Share Gainers is also a strong fit and why the framework flags 'share donators' as an explicit part of the plan: management talks openly about the AI shift creating 'legacy laggards' to feed off. Holding it together is a leadership team (Singh, Speight, Goel) that has run one consistent strategy for a decade, grown a $400m firm roughly sevenfold, and delivered on what it promised on earlier calls, including a contrarian purchase of Cigniti at a 12% margin that now runs at 19% — hence a strong fit on Corporate Culture. Reputation helps but is institutional rather than consumer-facing (25-plus AI recognitions and seven leader ratings from research houses), so Brand Strength is only a likely fit, and several other patterns land at partial: the $2.23bn executable order book, up 44.2% in a year from $897m in 2023, gives repeat-revenue-like visibility without being true recurring revenue; the cost engine (51% offshore mix, general costs capped in absolute terms, a 40% cut in acquired overheads) is real but the savings are kept as margin rather than passed to customers; and the shift to outcome-based pricing is stated intent, with no recorded case of raising prices without losing volume.

The risks are largely the flip side of the same characteristics. The absence of capital intensity that makes returns so good also means there is little that is genuinely hard to copy — the quality framework scores competitive advantage and industry structure lowest of all areas, the Toll Roads pattern is a clear no fit because IT services is a large negotiated line in a client's budget rather than a small essential ingredient, and the very fact that Coforge grows by prising work away from other vendors proves customers do switch suppliers. Technology change is the one elevated risk, and it is industry-wide rather than company-specific: management itself says labour as a default has been disrupted and that firms still billing by the hour are being left behind, which is precisely why the heavy AI investment behind the Innovation Dominance pattern is not optional. Management also concedes that horizontal AI skills and generative AI commoditize quickly, feeding the 'good enough' risk, while the customer shift from paying for effort to paying for outcomes sits behind both the partial Pricing Power score and the shifting-preferences risk; the mitigation is that 86% of Q1 FY27 revenue already comes from AI-led engineering, data and cloud work, up from 80%. The large-deal engine that powers share gains also concentrates risk in individual relationships — a $300m five-year banking deal, the $1.56bn Sabre contract, a $230m European AI programme and one client now above $100m — even though the top five clients fell from 25.1% to 18.0% of revenue and the top ten from 37.7% to 26.1%. Government work outside India at about 6% of revenue and the macro-sensitivity of discretionary technology budgets round out the watch-level risks, cushioned by the contracted order book, which is also why fashion risk is low and no new-entrant threat appears anywhere in the calls. Combining a business-quality score of 67, a pattern-fit score of 78 and a risk-safety score of 70 gives 71 out of 100 — a Strong rating with sound resilience: a high-return, fast-growing, well-run business whose main vulnerability is that its advantages are earned and maintained rather than structurally protected.

## What to watch

- Order book momentum — the $2.23bn executable book (+44.2%) is what makes the partial Recurring Revenue pattern and the watch-level Cyclicality risk tolerable; a stalling or shrinking book would weaken both at once.
- Evidence of real pricing power — the pivot to outcome-based pricing is currently intent only; a demonstrated price increase held without losing volume would upgrade the partial Pricing Power pattern, while margin giveback would confirm the Good Enough Goods risk.
- Renewal of the very large contracts (the $300m five-year banking deal, Sabre, the $230m European programme, the $100m-plus client) — the Market Share Gainers pattern and the Stakeholder Concentration risk run through the same handful of relationships.
- Whether AI investment keeps translating into revenue — the $58m FY26 spend and platform build underpin the Innovation Dominance pattern and are the direct answer to the elevated Technological Innovation risk; capability that stops converting into wins turns a strength into a cost.
- Staff turnover and margin steadiness — attrition at 10.4% and operating margin moving only about 1.4 points a year are the visible proof behind the People Matter and Profit Margin checks and the strong Corporate Culture fit; a break in either would be the earliest sign the model is straining.
