# IIFL Securities (IIFLCAPS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

IIFL Capital Services (formerly IIFL Securities) is an Indian capital-markets firm that lets people and institutions buy and sell shares and derivatives on the Indian exchanges, and sells them investment products alongside that. It earns brokerage commissions from retail customers and from institutional investors, fees from investment banking deals such as IPOs and NCD issues, commissions for distributing financial products like mutual funds, AIFs, PMS, insurance and fixed income, and interest income from lending money to clients to buy shares (the margin trading or MTF book). Management describes it as a focused Indian capital market and wealth management player, run out of India across a branch and partner network, and says the legacy retail broking business is being transformed into a wealth practice built on accumulating client assets rather than one-off transaction income. FY26 operational revenue was about INR2,439 crore; Fairfax India has proposed to invest INR2,000 crore and take at least 51%, subject to regulatory approval.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Retail broking (retail equity)** — Share and derivatives trading accounts for individual investors, largely opened and traded digitally on discount/flat brokerage plans, contributing roughly INR1,121 crore of revenue in FY26 and making it the single largest revenue line; SEBI's tightening of index derivative expiry rules cut into it.
- **Institutional equities (institutional broking)** — Broking and research services sold to large investing institutions, worth roughly INR450 crore of the combined INR712 crore institutional and investment banking revenue in FY26.
- **Investment banking** — Raising capital for companies through share and bond issues, a lumpy but high-margin business worth roughly INR250 crore in FY26 that swings sharply with primary market conditions.
- **Financial product distribution (FPD)** — Selling third-party investment and insurance products — mutual funds, AIFs, PMS, fixed income and insurance — for commission; income grew 16% to about INR590 crore in FY26 and assets under distribution rose from roughly INR31,000 crore to INR52,000 crore, making it the fastest-growing part of the company.
- **Wealth management / affluent business** — Relationship-manager-led advice for well-off clients, the declared strategic priority under joint CEOs hired from ASK, staffed by about 50 wealth RMs plus around 300 affluent/PCG broking RMs.
- **Margin trading facility (MTF) / lending book** — Lending clients money against securities so they can trade larger positions, generating interest income of about INR419 crore in FY25 and driving the company's borrowing and finance costs.
- **Manufacturing (own funds - AIF, PMS, credit and late-stage funds)** — A newer effort to run its own investment products rather than only distribute others', including a roughly INR500 crore credit opportunities fund and about INR4,400 crore of AIF and PMS AUM, with the income currently reported inside financial product distribution.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*
