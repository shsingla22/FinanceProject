# Aether Inds (AETHER) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Aether Industries is an Indian specialty-chemicals maker based in Sachin GIDC, Surat, Gujarat, that manufactures complex, hard-to-make organic chemicals and intermediates — many of which it says are being made in India for the first time, replacing imports — and sells them to industrial customers rather than to consumers. Its customers are chemical, pharmaceutical, agrochemical, oilfield-services, materials and electronics companies in India and abroad (named ones include Baker Hughes, Saudi Aramco Technologies, H.B. Fuller, Novoloop and Otsuka Chemical), with exports running roughly 36%-42% of sales and the rest domestic. It makes money three ways: selling its own catalogue products in bulk, doing paid research and small-scale manufacturing work for customers, and manufacturing products exclusively for a single customer under long-term contracts. Growth comes from heavy spending on R&D (labs, fume hoods, one of the world's largest pilot plants) and on new greenfield plants — Sites 1 through 5 plus solar power — at and around its Surat base.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Large Scale Manufacturing** — Bulk production and sale of Aether's own portfolio of chemical products, historically the biggest driver at about 66% of revenue in Q1 FY25 but down to roughly 41%-43% by FY26 as the other two models grew; products are priced around $30-$40 per kilo and pricing swings with global supply (China dumping hurt it in FY25, the FY26 conflict-driven disruption pushed prices up over 20% year-on-year).
- **Contract Research and Manufacturing Services (CRAMS)** — Paid R&D and process-development work for multinational customers that can later convert into supply — a smaller revenue line (about 8%-14% of sales in the quarters cited) but the strategic feeder for everything else, with inquiries rising and R&D capacity being roughly doubled to serve it, increasingly focused on non-pharma, non-agro areas like oil and gas and material science.
- **Contract / Exclusive Manufacturing (CEM)** — Making specific products exclusively for one customer under multi-year agreements; it grew from about 18% of sales in Q1 FY25 to around 43% in Q3 FY26, anchored by a six-product deal with Baker Hughes made at Site 4, whose sales jumped from INR50 crores to INR220 crores (21% of total sales) in FY26 — management expects CRAMS plus CEM to exceed 70% of sales in coming years.
- **Sustainability and Renewables segment** — A newer platform launched in FY25 covering Converge polyols (a polymer ingredient containing up to 40% carbon dioxide by weight, developed with Saudi Aramco Technologies and first sold to H.B. Fuller), technology with Novoloop to turn plastic waste back into virgin polymers, and organic electrolyte additives for lithium-ion batteries — described as high-margin and on-target for its FY26 sales goal, with good order visibility for FY27.
- **Electronic / semiconductor chemicals** — A new line started at Site 5 supplying chemicals to semiconductor-industry customers based in Japan, South Korea and Taiwan; validation batches have been dispatched, customer feedback is positive, and management expects the opportunity to roughly triple by 2030 — still early-stage with no meaningful revenue disclosed.
- **Manufacturing sites and capex (Sites 1-5)** — The physical backbone: Site 1 houses R&D and the pilot plant, Sites 2-4 are producing (Site 2 was rebuilt after a fire, with a INR1,000 million insurance claim), and Site 3++ plus the first blocks of Site 5 began commercial production around FY26 — supported by solar plants (16 MW, then a further 15 MW) that cut electricity costs.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*
