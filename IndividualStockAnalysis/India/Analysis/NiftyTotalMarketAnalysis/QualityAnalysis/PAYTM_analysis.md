# One 97 (PAYTM) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

One 97 Communications, which trades and operates under the brand name Paytm, runs a payments and financial-services platform in India, listed on the BSE and NSE. Its core business, in management's own words, is to acquire payment customers and distribute loans: consumers use the Paytm app to pay merchants, and merchants use Paytm's app, devices and software to collect payments, with more than 75 million monthly transacting users and 28 million merchants cited as of June 2022. It earns money mainly from fees on payments — merchant discount rate (MDR) on cards, wallet and other instruments, platform fees charged to selected consumers, device/software subscription fees (such as the Soundbox), and government incentives on UPI transactions — plus commissions for distributing loans, insurance-like financial products, and investment products for partner lenders and institutions, and from selling marketing, advertising and commerce services to merchants. Management stresses it is not a lender itself but 'a pure technology platform which helps different people disburse'; by FY26 payments was about 55% of revenue and financial services about 30%, with EBITDA margin around 6% and an aspiration of 15–20% in two to three years.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Payment services to consumers** — Fees earned when consumers use the Paytm app to pay merchants and bills — utilities, credit card bills, rent, BBPS — including a platform fee charged to selected users; this line grew 73% year-on-year in the June 2022 quarter and consumer payments remains the piece management says is growing at twice the UPI market's growth.
- **Payment services to merchants** — Merchants accept payments in their shops, apps and websites using Paytm's devices and software, paying subscription fees (notably for the Soundbox, described as the key way UPI acceptance is monetised) plus MDR on fee-bearing instruments; together with consumer payments this is roughly 55% of revenue by FY26 and covers both offline and online merchants, the online business now holding a full licence.
- **Financial services (lending distribution)** — Paytm sources and services loans for partner lenders rather than lending itself — Postpaid (buy-now-pay-later), personal loans and co-branded credit cards for consumers, and merchant loans (merchant cash advance) for shopkeepers — earning distribution and collection fees; it is about 30% of revenue in FY26, with merchant loans described as solid, personal loans recovering, and disbursement numbers no longer disclosed.
- **Wealth (equity broking and mutual fund distribution)** — A newer part of financial services covering stock broking and mutual fund distribution, which management calls a 'bright spot' gaining market share and the area getting the most aggressive push going into FY27.
- **Marketing services (formerly commerce and cloud services)** — Selling deals, tickets, advertising and merchant software/cloud services that help merchants bring customers back 'from checkout to check-in'; it was Rs. 331 crores with 64% year-on-year growth in the June 2022 quarter, but saw a double-digit revenue decline in FY26 and is described as a drag that management expects to recover.
- **AI products** — An emerging, non-payments, non-financial-services line that management says is currently earning only 'a few lakhs' of revenue and will eventually be reported inside the commerce/cloud (marketing services) line; it is one of the two areas the CEO says he is personally focused on.

### The story in depth

Paytm's real strength is physical and human rather than financial. The three checks that score best are all versions of the same thing: a very large feet-on-street sales force placing millions of card machines and Soundboxes with small merchants, on-ground servicing, redeployment of inactive units, and loans sold through that same channel. The quality framework calls this the company's strongest and hardest-to-replicate asset, and rates competitive advantage and customer benefits at the top of its range. That single asset is also what produces the two patterns that fit best: merchants pay a recurring monthly fee on top of the device placement, which is why the 'Recurring Revenue' pattern is a likely fit, and the same on-ground reach is what management points to when it claims consumer payments growing at twice the market's rate and merchant share gains, giving the 'Market Share Gainers' pattern its likely fit. The end market helps too — digital payments, credit distribution and retail broking are all expanding. So the strengths, the two best patterns and the growth story are not three separate findings; they are one asset described three ways.

The trouble is that the rails this asset sits on are set by someone else, and that is where nearly every risk comes from. The core payment fee is regulated: UPI carries a zero merchant fee with the government paying an annual incentive that management explicitly needs in order to post a profitable quarter, which is why government dependency is the one high risk in the review. The same fact kills the two patterns that would matter most for long-term compounding — 'Toll Roads' and 'Pricing Power' both come back as no fit, because the take rate has been deflating rather than quietly rising: net payment processing margin fell from 7-9 basis points to guidance of 5-6. Because UPI is interoperable and free, a rival's app works on Paytm's own QR code, which is what drives both the 'Good Enough Goods' and 'New Entrants' warnings, and analysts have already questioned rising cashback and promotional spending that did not bring matching user growth. The numbers echo all of it: only ₹6 of operating profit per ₹100 of sales, margins swinging by roughly ±64 points a year, growth averaging about 44% but lumpy, and — most seriously — a cash return on capital of minus 5%, meaning the business consumed rather than generated operating cash on its capital base this year. The industry-structure check is negative for exactly the reason the risk review flags: cashback-led customer acquisition and deep-pocketed new entrants.

The January 2024 regulatory action against Paytm Payments Bank is the hinge that connects almost everything left over. It is why 'Cost to Replicate' is only partial rather than a fit — the merchant base and in-house payments technology genuinely are hard to rebuild, but a regulator removed much of the advantage overnight. It is why 'Brand Strength' is only partial, since the brand needed reassurance campaigns afterwards. It is why 'Market Share Gainers' is flagged as not persistent. And it is the origin of the stakeholder-concentration warning, since the business had been running on a single in-house bank and, even by mid-2024, growth was gated on one partner bank's systems. Against that, the record shows real repair work: payments now run on four banks, lending partners are in double digits, employee costs were cut around 10% in a quarter with more targeted, indirect expenses grew slower than revenue, and AI used internally lifted the operating margin to 8% — which is what earns 'Low Price Plus', 'Innovation Dominance' and 'Corporate Culture' their partial verdicts, with leadership stable throughout and bad news quantified openly. The counterweight is visibility: this is one of India's most-covered fintechs with one of its most publicly visible founders and a promotional tone on the calls, so both 'out of the limelight' checks score badly. The overall rating of 44 out of 100 — Mixed — with resilience judged sound is a fair reading: a genuinely distinctive distribution machine, monetised on terms it does not control.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Fortunes are set by policy across the whole trajectory: UPI carries zero MDR with the government paying an annual incentive that management explicitly needs for a profitable quarter, NPCI permission gates new consumer onboarding, RBI action on Paytm Payments Bank cut off wallet/rent/credit-card-bill revenue lines in 2024, and the wallet licence is still 'pending' with RBI two years later.

> "the Government of India, in their Digital India Mission have subvented the UPI merchant fees. So, technically UPI has the MDR, which the Government is actually paying" — *management, on an earnings call*

> "Personal loans had a headwind because of credit cycle. A lot of that is turning into tailwinds from a slightly lower base." — *management, on an earnings call*
