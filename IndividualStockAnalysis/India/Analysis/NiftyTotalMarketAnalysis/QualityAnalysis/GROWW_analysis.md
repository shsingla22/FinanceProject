# Billionbrains (GROWW) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Groww (legal name Billionbrains Garage Ventures Limited, based in Bangalore and listed on the BSE and NSE since FY26) runs an app-based investing platform for retail savers and traders in India, which it describes as building 'India's largest investing and wealth platform.' Customers open an account and buy mutual funds, stocks, equity futures and options, ETFs, commodities and bonds, and the company also lends against their holdings; a US stocks offering via the GIFT City route is licensed and in testing. It earns money mainly from brokerage — a flat INR 20 per derivatives order plus charges on cash trades, with roughly 70% of that revenue from derivatives and 30% from cash — and from interest on loans such as margin funding priced at 14.95%. Management says about 80% of new customers arrive organically, with the rest largely from digital performance marketing, and the whole business is India-focused, aimed at a market of 70-80 million existing stock and mutual fund investors out of 500-600 million Indians already transacting online.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Broking (cash / equity)** — Buying and selling of shares and mutual funds/ETFs for retail customers; it is about 30% of broking revenue, and management says its cash yield has improved roughly 5% year-on-year as margin funding spreads through the base.
- **Broking (derivatives / F&O)** — Futures and options trading charged at a flat INR 20 per order; the largest revenue line at roughly 70% of broking revenue, with equity derivatives market share rising from 9.1% to 10.6% in Q4 FY26 and about 17 lakh customers trading in that quarter versus 14 lakh earlier.
- **MTF (Margin Trading Facility)** — Lending customers money at 14.95% to buy shares on margin — an AUM-style book that management calls their best-scaling product, where they aim for a double-digit market share within about three years.
- **LAS (Loan Against Securities)** — Loans against a customer's investments, so far only against mutual funds with loan against securities still to launch; early stage but growing fast, and by Q1 FY27 it made up 34% of disbursements.
- **Commodities** — A newly launched commodities trading product that reached more than 28% retail market share by notional average daily turnover.
- **Bonds** — Bond investing, launched first in the primary market and then extended to secondary; management says it is 'scaling really, really well.'
- **Wealth management — Fisdom, W by Groww, Groww Prime** — Three advice-led offerings added via the Fisdom acquisition in late 2025 — Fisdom sold through bank partnerships, W for affluent and HNI customers, and Prime (launched January 2026) for mass-affluent customers — covering research-backed investment selection, rebalancing and tax planning; still small and early, with Fisdom expected to turn profitable by FY28.
- **AMC (asset management)** — A planned fund-management business through a partnership with State Street Global (SSG), which had received SEBI and CCI approval as of the July 2026 call.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Groww's strengths all come from the same place: it owns the customer directly. A single app gives more than ten products with fully digital disbursement and no intermediary sitting in between, which the quality work scores as a genuine accessibility advantage, and roughly 80% of customers arrive on their own through brand and word of mouth. That combination shows up as the report's best-supported pattern — Market Share Gainers is a strong fit, with equity derivatives share rising from 9.1% to 10.6% in a single quarter, commodities reaching more than 28% of retail activity within a year of launch, and gains in equity and mutual funds through FY26 — and it is why Brand Strength is a likely fit. It also sits under Forward Integrators: no franchise or sub-broker layer means full control of experience and pricing, now extended toward manufacturing through the asset management venture. Management scores well too — ten years of an unchanged strategy, willingness to build slowly and forgo near-term earnings from mutual fund customers, and a refusal to give quarter-by-quarter guidance — and the end market is still early, with only 70-80 million unique investors against an online-transacting population of 500-600 million.

The catch is that several of these strengths are the very things that create the risks. Pricing is the clearest example. Charging 14.95% on margin funding while competitors offer 7.99%, and still adding ₹600-700 crore of book a quarter, is scored as strong revealed pricing power and is the main evidence behind the Pricing Power, Toll Roads and Low Price Plus patterns. The identical fact drives the New Entrants risk — rivals piled into the same product at roughly half the price within one quarter — and the Good Enough Goods risk, where management is repeatedly asked to defend its pricing and answers with experience rather than economics. The quality framework registers the same tension from the other side, marking barriers to entry at its worst score because margins swing by about 47.5 points and an active discount war is visible in the fastest-growing product. Direct ownership of the customer cuts both ways as well: it is why the Friendly Middleman pattern does not fit at all, and simultaneously the reason no channel partner can steer customers elsewhere. And the regulator, not the company, sets the economics of the core product — the November 2024 derivatives rules halved penetration from roughly 18% to 10%, it has not recovered, and an expiry-day margin decision was still open as of July 2026.

Put together, this is a business that is winning customers faster than it is turning them into durable earnings, which is why the combined rating lands at 46 out of 100 — 62 for business quality, 33 for multibagger fit, 34 for risk safety — a Mixed, two-star verdict, though resilience is judged sound. Sales have compounded at roughly 82% a year but lumpily enough to score badly on persistence, and the return on capital check is the weakest of all: for every ₹100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings, the business generated essentially no operating cash this year. Most of the eleven patterns come back partial rather than clean, and Cost to Replicate captures why — a decade of brand-built organic acquisition, high cross-product retention and regulatory licences are real barriers, but not unassailable ones. Meanwhile a meaningful slice of activity rides whatever is trending, with gold and silver ETF orders contributing heavily and then fading, and derivatives spiking on war headlines. The offsets are genuine and worth crediting: the product engine keeps producing revenue-bearing launches off existing teams, recurring streams from margin funding, loans against securities, SIP flows and float income are building underneath the ₹20-per-order transaction model, and that portfolio pivot already restored per-customer revenue to pre-November-2024 levels. The question the record leaves open is whether pricing holds and cash returns follow the share gains.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 2 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across all four calls management frames the business in explicit cycle terms — the post-Sept'24 down cycle, waiting for FIIs to return before calling the next growth cycle, F&O and DAU swings attributed to volatility/war, MTF book marked to market on the last day of each quarter, and gold/silver ETF order volumes booming then fading. Revenue and ARPU visibly fluctuate with market conditions even as customer count compounds.

> "we have seen the down cycle I think things are looking better than earlier... We might need to wait for few more quarters to say that we are in the next cycle of growth" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/operations-linked demand rather than customer capex: SIP-driven MF/ETF AUM accumulation, MTF and LAS AUM-based interest income, and float income diversify away from pure transaction volumes; cost-to-grow held broadly flat (~INR450-500cr) through the downturn rather than pro-cyclical scaling.*

- ⚠️ Year-to-year sales growth swings by ±75 percentage points — boom-and-bust revenue, not a steady compounder.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from -55% to 62% (swinging ±47.5 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 0% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 2%).

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** SEBI policy, not the company, sets the economics of the core product: the Nov-24 F&O regulations halved derivatives penetration from ~18% to ~10% and reset platform ARPU, True-to-Label reshaped pricing, and expiry-day margin news is a live question in Jul'26. The AMC venture with State Street was contingent on SEBI/CCI approval, and an investor explicitly raised the risk of F&O being 'banned all of a sudden'. Roughly 70% of revenue sits in the derivatives line the regulator is actively trying to curb.

> "any concern on the SEBI and government folks liking to sort of curb the speculation in futures and options especially for the retail traders who are major chunk of our class" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate revenue diversification away from the policy-targeted segment — MTF, commodities, bonds, LAS, wealth/Fisdom and AMC now backfill ARPU, and management engages the regulator continuously ('BAU for us').*

## What to watch

- Margin funding price gap (Intangible benefits check, New Entrants risk, Pricing Power pattern): if Groww cuts from 14.95% toward the 7.99% competitors, or the ₹600-700 crore quarterly book growth stalls, the strongest evidence of pricing power disappears.
- SEBI's open expiry-day margin question (Government Dependency, high risk): another rule change of the November 2024 kind would reset the economics of the core product again, as that one halved derivatives penetration from roughly 18% to 10%.
- Cash return on capital (Return on capital check, scored -2): the business generated essentially no operating cash per ₹100 of capital this year — this needs to turn clearly positive for the market-share gains to mean anything for owners.
- Customer mix drifting further toward lower-earning mutual funds and ETFs (Shifting Consumer Preferences risk): management says this is a changed mix rather than a cycle, so watch whether the newer products keep backfilling per-customer revenue.
- Cost to acquire customers (Low Price Plus and Brand Strength patterns): the ₹450-500 crore of branding spend is currently flat in absolute terms while revenue compounds and 80% of sign-ups come organically — if either of those breaks, the low-cost model and the brand case break together.
