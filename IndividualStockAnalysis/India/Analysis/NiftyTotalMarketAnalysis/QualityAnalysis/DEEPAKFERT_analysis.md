# Deepak Fertilisers & Petrochemicals Corp. Ltd. (DEEPAKFERT) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CHEMICALS*

Deepak Fertilisers and Petrochemicals Corporation (DFPCL) is an Indian manufacturer that turns imported/contracted natural gas (LNG) and ammonia into a chain of industrial chemicals, mining explosives chemicals and fertilisers, selling almost entirely to business customers in India — mining and steel companies, chemical and pharmaceutical manufacturers, and farmers through fertiliser dealers. It makes money on the spread between raw material costs (mainly LNG, ammonia and propylene) and the prices of finished products such as technical ammonium nitrate, nitric acid, isopropyl alcohol (IPA) and NPK fertilisers, so profits swing with input prices, monsoons, mining activity and government fertiliser subsidies. Management describes the group as running from gas to ammonia to nitric acid to downstream products, with 9 nitric acid plants and 4 ammonium nitrate plants and 45 years of customer relationships, and it is pushing from plain commodity products into branded, crop- or customer-specific 'specialty' grades and bundled services to earn higher margins. Operations are in India — plants at Taloja, Dahej and a new site at Gopalpur in the east — with consolidated FY26 revenue of about INR 11,506 crores and EBITDA of about INR 1,684 crores; the three businesses have been approved by NCLT to be demerged into separate legal entities.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Mining Chemicals / Technical Ammonium Nitrate (TAN)** — Makes technical ammonium nitrate, the chemical used in explosives for blasting in coal mines, limestone/cement and steel-related mining, and is moving from selling the product alone to 'total cost of operations' mining solutions including an acquired explosives unit and last-mile delivery to mines; management calls it a core business running above 100% capacity utilisation in good quarters, targeting a top-3 global position once the new Gopalpur plant (91% complete as of Jan 2026) starts up.
- **Industrial Chemicals** — Sells nitric acid (including specialty stainless-steel and solar grades) and isopropyl alcohol (IPA, including a pharma grade) to downstream chemical, steel and pharmaceutical customers; nitric acid also feeds the company's own ammonium nitrate production, and the calls describe it as Asia's largest acid position with a new Dahej acid project 79% complete as of Jan 2026, though pricing has been squeezed by imports and weak propylene/acetone economics.
- **Crop Nutrition Business (CNB) / Fertilisers** — Manufactures and sells bulk NPK fertilisers plus branded specialty products (Bensulf, Croptek and Smartek crop-specific grades, and specialty fertilisers via a tie-up with Israel's Haifa Group) to Indian farmers, with revenue depending on the monsoon, Kharif/Rabi sowing and government subsidy support; it was the fastest-growing segment in FY26 (Q3 revenue up 26% year-on-year) and specialty plus Croptek grades made up about 30% of its revenue.
- **Ammonia plant (raw material / value-chain backbone)** — A world-scale ammonia plant commissioned to feed all three businesses instead of buying imported ammonia, described as running at ~98% capacity and acting as a risk mitigator against raw material price swings, backed by a 15-year long-term LNG supply contract with a Norwegian supplier whose first cargo arrived in 2026.

### The story in depth

Deepak Fertilisers has spent the last four years doing one thing with unusual consistency: building its way from selling commodities to selling solutions. A decade-scale plan articulated in 2022 — LNG into ammonia, ammonia into nitric acid, then downstream — was pushed through COVID, war, gas price spikes and labour shortages, and it largely landed. The Rs 4,350cr captive ammonia plant was commissioned in FY24 and runs at 98%, the Gopalpur technical ammonium nitrate project sits at roughly Rs 3,050cr of construction-in-progress, Dahej-II nitric acid is coming, and FY26 capex was still Rs 1,569cr. A 15-year LNG contract with Equinor lowers the cost of making ammonia by a double-digit percentage against the imported benchmark. This is why the growth-capex check and the long-term-tenacity check both score at the top of the scale, and why Forward Integrators is the single pattern rated a strong fit: the company now deals directly with mines on a total-cost-of-ownership basis, delivers last-mile through bulk mixing units and regional warehouses, and bought a small explosives unit (DMSL) in FY26 to move one step closer to the end user. The branded crop-specific fertiliser grades, Croptek and Smartek, have climbed to 33% of crop nutrition revenue from 30%, and a business-to-consumer mining channel is now 16% — both of which are the same escape attempt showing up on the customer side.

The trouble is that the very build-out that earns the praise is also what creates the biggest problems in the record, and the two cannot be separated. Paying for a multi-year construction programme out of a business whose earnings were falling has pushed net debt to Rs 4,824cr, or 2.86 times EBITDA, which is why the stewardship check scores negative despite the spending being organic and counter-cyclical. Meanwhile the money already invested is not yet earning: for every Rs 100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings in the business, only Rs 2 of operating cash was generated this year — the weakest score in the whole framework, and the reason the return-on-capital area comes out at minus two-thirds while management scores just 0.17. FY26 was a cyclical low point in its own right, with EBITDA down 8% year-to-date and profit after tax down 18%, and one quarter's EBITDA down 27% on raw material costs. So the assets are new, expensive, financed with debt, and arriving into a soft market — the timing risk and the balance-sheet risk are the same risk, and it is the flip side of the capex strength rather than a separate flaw.

What the company sells explains why almost every other pattern comes out only partial, and why the risk review lands where it does. Ammonium nitrate, nitric acid and isopropyl alcohol are large, price-benchmarked products whose selling prices track international ammonia, FGAN and acetone quotes, so pricing power and toll-road economics are rated no fit outright: through FY26 the company could not pass on input inflation, IPA realisations fell about 22-23%, nitric acid prices were capped by imported material, and fertiliser margins were held down by government-set farm-gate prices and late subsidy payments. That last point is why government dependency is the one high risk — the fertiliser business depends on the subsidy regime, and TAN demand depends on Coal India and state mining, with an import duty change, an export ban and its lifting all moving results inside the period. Competition is arriving at the same time: analysts pressed management on Chinese ammonium nitrate exports rising from 7% to 30% share and on Chambal and GNFC adding roughly 500 KTPA into a market management itself calls flat, which is why new entrants is elevated and why the cost-to-replicate and market-share patterns stay partial — the company's own disclosed TAN share moved the wrong way across calls, from almost 50-60% in Aug-2024 to around 40% in Feb-2026. Set against that, TAN volumes still grew 11% in FY26 with Gopalpur adding about 400kt, 70% of the new Dahej nitric acid capacity is already tied up under long-term contracts, demand is functional and repeat-purchase rather than fashion-driven, and no technology shift threatens the products — which is why resilience is judged sound even as the overall rating comes out Mixed at 47 out of 100, built from 58 on business quality, only 25 on multibagger pattern fit, and 52 on risk safety.

## What to watch

- Whether the new plants convert into cash: the return-on-capital check is at Rs 2 of operating cash per Rs 100 of capital, so the Gopalpur TAN and Dahej-II nitric acid commissionings due in Q2 FY27 need to lift that number or the capex strength stays unpaid.

- The domestic TAN share figure, which slipped from almost 50-60% to around 40% across calls, tested against Chambal and GNFC adding roughly 500 KTPA and Chinese exports at 30% share — this is the New Entrants risk deciding whether Market Share Gainers and Cost to Replicate firm up or fail.
- Evidence of actual price-setting ability rather than price-taking: after IPA fell 22-23% and nitric acid was capped by imports, the Pricing Power and Good Enough Goods verdicts only change if realisations hold when input costs move.
- The cyclical upturn levers management named — firming FGAN prices and the Equinor LNG cost relief — actually showing up in margins, since the entire case for FY26 being a trough rather than a new normal rests on them.
