# Reliance Industries Ltd. (RELIANCE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: ENERGY*

Reliance Industries is a large Indian conglomerate that turns crude oil into fuels and plastics, sells mobile and broadband service, and runs a nationwide chain of shops. It makes money four main ways: refining imported crude into diesel, petrol and petrochemicals that it sells to Indian customers and exports to Asia, Australia and Europe (the 'O2C' business); monthly subscription fees from around 533 million mobile and 29 million home-broadband customers under the Jio brand; sales through more than 16,000 retail stores plus online channels like JioMart; and producing natural gas from its own Indian offshore and coal-bed fields. Almost all customers are in India, though refined products are exported and crude is bought from the Middle East, Latin America, the US, Canada, Africa and Russia. By FY26 the consumer businesses (Jio plus retail) accounted for more than half of group profits, with energy providing the rest; full-year group revenue and EBITDA were growing roughly 10-13%, and management stresses that capital spending is funded out of cash profits.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Digital Services / Jio Platforms (Reliance Jio Infocomm)** — Mobile phone, 5G and home-broadband service sold on subscription — 533 million connectivity customers (285 million on 5G) and 29 million fixed-broadband homes as of mid-2026, generating about Rs.1,46,000 crore of revenue and Rs.76,000 crore of EBITDA in FY26 at a 52% margin, making it the single largest profit engine and the fastest-growing one.
- **Reliance Retail** — Shops and online selling across grocery, fashion and lifestyle, and consumer electronics — over 16,000 stores as of 2022 with continued additions, roughly Rs.90,000 crore of quarterly revenue and Rs.6,309 crore of quarterly EBITDA by mid-2026; margins are currently being deliberately sacrificed to build out hyperlocal quick-delivery e-commerce.
- **O2C (Oil to Chemicals) — Refining & Marketing plus Petrochemicals** — Buys crude oil, refines it into transport fuels and sells them at its own pumps and abroad, and converts feedstock (increasingly cheap ethane) into polymers, polyester and other chemicals — historically the biggest revenue line, up 30% on revenue and 17% on EBITDA to about Rs.17,000 crore in the June 2026 quarter, but the most volatile part, hit by margin swings, a special excise levy (SAED) and fuel-retail under-recoveries.
- **Oil & Gas — Exploration & Production (E&P)** — Pumps natural gas from Indian fields, chiefly the KG-D6 offshore block plus coal-bed methane, selling at dollar-denominated gas prices (around $9.7/MMBTU); it was around 20% of India's gas output in 2022 but is now a smaller, declining contributor as reservoir reserves fall.
- **New Energy** — A newer investment area that gets its own management slot on recent calls and absorbs part of the group's capital spending alongside O2C and retail; the excerpts describe it as a growth/capex priority rather than a material earnings contributor yet.
- **JioStar** — Media, streaming and sports broadcasting (Jio Hotstar), with about Rs.13,000 crore of quarterly topline and operating income up 30% year-on-year in mid-2026 — described as very strong given a weak advertising market.
- **Reliance Consumer Products (RCPL) — FMCG** — Own-brand packaged consumer goods and beverages, including the Campa and Independence brands, at about Rs.8,600 crore of quarterly revenue in mid-2026 — roughly double the prior year, with the current focus on building out manufacturing and supply-chain capacity.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Reliance comes out of the quality framework as a company whose strength is reach and engineering rather than the returns it earns on the money it puts to work. The standout marks are for distribution (20,000 stores reached at record pace, a technician force installing 50,000-60,000 homes a day within 24 hours), technology and research (a self-built 5G core, proprietary beam-forming cell design and a top-20 global patent ranking, converted into a 78% share of India's fixed-wireless additions), and end markets that are still expanding on their own (data traffic growing 27-31% a year to 69.4 exabytes, 100 million homes still unconnected). Customers are sold reliability and convenience rather than a bargain: third-party best-network ratings, 99.9% time on network, and full-assortment two-hour delivery from 3,100 hyperlocal stores across 1,200 cities. Set against that, the framework flags a fairly asset-heavy business (every rupee of assets produces 48 paise of sales, with over a lakh crore capitalised in a single year and Rs 1,23,000 crore of net debt), and the weakest area scores are return on capital, growth, industry structure and management. Operating profit of Rs 17 on every Rs 100 of sales has been steady within about two points a year, which reads as good cost control rather than expanding profitability. The combined rating is 66 out of 100, or Strong, built from 69 on business quality, 72 on pattern fit and 54 on risk safety, with overall resilience judged sound.

The multibagger patterns are mostly the same strengths seen from a different angle. Because the company owns its network, its stores and its last mile, it registers as a strong Forward Integrator (own fuel retailing with gasoline volumes up 44%, 1,000 big-box grocery formats, JioMart hyperlocal) and as strongly Costly to Replicate (spectrum holdings described as nearly equal to the other two major players combined, a lakh crore of network assets capitalised in one year, an in-house 4G/5G core). The subscription base that recurs off prepaid connectivity plans, rising from 428 million subscribers in October 2022 to 533 million by July 2026, supports the recurring-revenue pattern, while the innovation cadence and the persistent share gains (45% revenue share and 36% subscriber share in 2022, 170 million then 285 million 5G users, 70% of new 5G handsets converting to Jio, 65% of incremental home broadband additions) show up as separate likely fits. Two patterns explicitly do not fit, and both trace back to the same structural facts. There is no friendly middleman because the route to market is entirely direct, which is precisely the trait that earns the forward-integration and convenience scores. There is no toll road because the largest profit pool, oil-to-chemicals, is an admitted price-taker whose earnings move with crude differentials, cracks and government levies. That same limitation is why pricing power is only partial: the FY25 tariff increase lifted average revenue per user 12% to Rs 203 while still adding 3.3 million subscribers, and ARPU reached Rs 215.6 by Q1 FY27 with no tariff action at all, but this pricing ability exists only in telecom. Brand strength and culture are likewise partial rather than full, because the brands rest on value pricing and because the record of delivering on promises, while real, is only part of what those checks ask for.

The risk review largely re-describes the strengths as exposures. The biggest is government dependency, rated high risk, and it is the direct counterpart to the commodity leg that blocks the toll-road pattern: a special additional excise duty cost Rs 4,039 crore of profit in a single quarter and recurs in later years, and domestic gas realisation moves with a government ceiling price. Cyclicality is elevated for the same reason, with oil-to-chemicals profit falling 40% quarter-on-quarter in one period and rising 17% in another, though consumer businesses now supplying roughly 50-55% of profit cushions the swing. The heavy asset base that makes the business so hard to copy is also what creates the technology-innovation watch item, since the installed base has to be rebuilt periodically and the lakh crore of 5G assets capitalised in FY26 depresses reported profit through depreciation and interest; the quality framework marks the same trait negatively as a low asset turn. The low-price-plus operating philosophy that wins share also bounds what can be charged, which is exactly the good-enough-goods watch item: no tariff action for twelve months lifted ARPU by only Rs 7, with cheaper rivals setting the ceiling. And the convenience advantage that scores two out of two is currently being paid for out of margin, showing up as the new-entrants watch item, where quick commerce has reset retail economics, FY26 retail profit growth was muted because of the hyperlocal scale-up and Q1 FY27 retail profit fell year-on-year with margin down 80 basis points. Shifting consumer preferences is a further watch, with transactions growing 39-46% against 12% revenue growth as average ticket sizes fall. Only two risks score low: stakeholder concentration, thanks to 533 million subscribers and about 1.93 billion transactions a year with no single named customer or supplier, and fashion risk, because the bulk of earnings rests on functional repeat purchases. Worth naming as a separate consideration is the security-by-obscurity check at minus two: this is India's largest listed company with heavy analyst and media coverage and capital flowing into every segment it enters, so none of the above is a private discovery.

### Cost to Replicate — STRONG FIT
*"Could a newcomer copy this?"*

**What the calls show:** Several advantages a funded newcomer could not rebuild quickly: 5G spectrum holdings 'nearly equal to the combined holdings of the other two major players', over Rs.1 lakh crore of network assets capitalised in a single year, a self-built 4G/5G core with ~4,500 patents, KG-D6 acreage, and a physical retail/dark-store density explicitly described as unmatched in India.

> "533 million subscribers in connectivity... Monthly churn has been reducing every quarter." — *management, on an earnings call*

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Policy sets a large share of economics in both legs of the business: SAED on transport fuels cost Rs.4,039 crore of EBITDA in a single quarter and reappears in FY26/FY27, domestic gas realisation moves with the government ceiling price, fuel retailing carries 'under-recoveries', LPG/gas had to be diverted to priority sectors, and telecom runs on auctioned spectrum, SUC and regulatory clearance for new products. > "nearly three times or 280% year on year increase in all the large government tenders which have come out" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Partial diversification of the mix toward consumer/retail/digital businesses that are not directly price-regulated*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

*Silver lining: Diversified earnings — consumer businesses (Jio + Retail) now ~50-55% of EBITDA with flow-type, operations-linked demand (data, groceries, fuel retail); capex consistently funded from cash profits and net debt/EBITDA held ~0.64*
