### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No heritage, A&P, distribution leverage or premium pricing exists in a tendered EPC model; the only reputational asset discussed — standing with NHAI — was damaged this year by a show-cause notice over carriageway quality and a self-imposed bidding blackout to November 30, 2025. (The long view had said: KNR does move toward asset ownership — captive HAM SPVs generated 37-48% of the order book at times, it collects toll on the Bihar asset, and it recycles equity by selling four SPVs to Indus Infra Trust for INR1,543 crores against INR566.8 crores invested. But this is asset-ownership/monetisation, not own-stores, franchising or D2C, and the captive share is falling (16% by Dec-25).)

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: The calls affirmatively describe regulatory barriers being raised against marginal bidders rather than any new-format competitor pressuring KNR; management repeatedly cites tightened RFP norms and additional performance security as favourable to established contractors. No discounting or margin investment is framed as a response to new competition. (The long view had said: Management repeatedly describes a bidding field that has been reset by aggressive entrants chasing a shrunken tender pool, forcing KNR to accept structurally lower margins — new HAM wins are being underwritten at 11-12% EBITDA versus the 18-20% historically earned, and management concedes the bottom line cannot return to earlier levels. This is price aggression rather than a genuinely new-format competitor, which bounds the risk.)
- **Technological Innovation**: QUANT FLAG → LOW. Why now: Technology appears only as incremental, sector-wide adoption that KNR participates in rather than as a platform shift threatening its methods or installed base; no analyst on the four calls raised disruption as a concern. (The long view had said: Across 17 calls there is no defensive discussion of a competing technology platform, no suggestion the company's construction methods or installed base are becoming legacy, and no analyst question on disruption; the only technology item raised is GPS-based tolling, which concerns NHAI's fee collection rather than KNR's construction business.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Good Enough Goods (ELEVATED); Shifting Consumer Preferences (QUANT FLAG); Fashion Risk (QUANT FLAG).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (HIGH RISK).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
