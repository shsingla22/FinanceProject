### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 5. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 2 weakened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: The Narayana name carries clinical heritage and is stretched into clinics, insurance and pharmacy under one platform, and management notes patients and employers coming to it — but these calls contain no A&P spend, market-share-by-brand or premium-versus-competitor brand discussion, and pricing is credited to case-mix, not brand. (The long view had said: The Narayana name carries real regional equity — 'we are a very well-known brand in the region' in the Caribbean, and it has been stretched into clinics, insurance and now UK Practice Plus — but there is no sustained A&P spend, heritage-premium or distributor-leverage discussion, and in Gurgaon/North Bangalore the brand has not prevented share pressure from larger competitors.)
- **Forward Integrators**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: NH is integrating forward from hospitals into owned primary-care clinics, its own insurance underwriting (India and Cayman), captive pharmacies in every hospital and clinic, and the Arya subscription with home delivery — all inside the listed entity, with management explicitly rejecting a franchised standalone pharmacy in favour of owned fulfilment. (The long view had said: NH is integrating toward the patient — NHIC clinics (8 clinics, 51,000 transactions by Q1 FY25, being merged into NHL), its own insurance arm, in-house pharmacies in every hospital and clinic, and D2C health plans with home delivery — with clinics explicitly used as feeders into the hospitals. But it has deliberately refused the franchised/retail route and the direct-to-consumer verticals were still loss-making (~INR 66 crore in FY26).)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Across Q2–Q4 FY26 management repeatedly attributes 150–200bps of India margin expansion to rising realisation from payor-mix optimisation, higher-configuration beds and high-end robotic/quaternary work with occupancy and volumes intact — pricing-for-value rather than list-price hikes, with Bangalore ARPP above INR 250,000. (The long view had said: Management repeatedly says it does not push price: Q1 FY25 'we are also not very aggressive on the pricing', and Q4 FY26 confirms headline price rises of only low-to-mid single digits — roughly cost inflation. The large ARPP/realisation gains come from payer-mix optimisation, bed reconfiguration and higher-complexity case mix, not from pricing power.)

**That weakened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: NH's heritage is high-volume, low-cost cardiac care with high asset turns, and the current calls show continued asset-sweating — 20% India EBITDA growth with no bed additions and a bed count lower than at listing. But the stated strategy in this window is deliberately away from low price: payor-mix optimisation, higher-configuration beds and quaternary work to lift realisation. (The long view had said: Across all 18 calls the model is explicitly high-quality care at low price at scale — Dr. Shetty's affordability mission, 'unbeatable price' tertiary care in Cayman, a ₹1 crore surgical cover for ~₹10,000, itemised cost savings (INR 9.1mn energy, INR 3.6mn equipment in Q1 FY23), and a technology platform whose stated purpose is to 'meaningfully lower the cost base', now being exported to the UK acquisition.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Cayman shows clear share capture — ~70% revenue growth, insurance revenue doubling quarter-on-quarter, ~50% more discharges post Camana Bay — and the UK PPG acquisition adds a new developed market; but in India the north cluster is losing volume to new hospital competition and NH is deliberately shedding scheme volumes, and no India share figures are given. (The long view had said: Total patient footfalls grew 26mn (FY23) to 28mn (FY24) to 30mn+ (FY25) and the group expanded internationally into Cayman, the Caribbean and the UK, but India inpatient volumes were flat for three quarters in FY25 by choice, the North cluster lost scheme volumes in FY26, and management concedes ongoing share pressure in Gurgaon and new Bangalore competition — no explicit rising share numbers are ever given.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 0 worsened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The realization uplift rests on quaternary work that cheaper local hospitals cannot replicate at scale — ~100 robotic cardiac surgeries a month, 160 TAVR procedures in Bangalore alone, 45 paediatric BMT beds — and patients are actively trading up to higher-configuration beds rather than resisting the premium; no discussion across the four calls of defending price against cheaper substitutes. (The long view had said: Pricing power is visibly constrained — rack-rate increases run only low-to-mid single digits, management says it is 'not very aggressive on pricing', and Kolkata is conceded to sit permanently at a discount to Bangalore — so margin gains come from case-mix and payor-mix engineering rather than premium defence, and in commoditised markets like Gurgaon cheaper/larger alternatives have kept the unit near breakeven for years.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
