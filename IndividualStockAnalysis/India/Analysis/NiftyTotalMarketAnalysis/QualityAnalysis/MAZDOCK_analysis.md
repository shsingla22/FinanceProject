# Mazagon Dock (MAZDOCK) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders Limited (MDL) is a government-of-India-owned shipyard based at Dockyard Road, Mumbai that designs and builds large warships and conventional submarines, almost entirely for Indian defence customers — mainly the Indian Navy, plus the Coast Guard — under very long, fixed contracts awarded by the Ministry of Defence. It makes money by recognising revenue and profit as these multi-year projects progress and get delivered, so earnings swing with where each project sits in its life cycle rather than quarter to quarter; management says projects at a late stage throw off the most revenue and profit, and refunds of liquidated damages previously deducted by the Navy have added to profits in several years. Standalone revenue was Rs 11,432 crore in FY25 with a PBT of Rs 3,109 crore, and management guided to roughly Rs 12,500 crore for FY26; the order book was about Rs 27,415 crore as of the Oct 2025 call, expected to jump past Rs 1.25 lakh crore if the pending submarine contracts are signed. The company describes itself as cash-rich, zero-debt with near-zero or negative working capital, and it also holds a 47% stake in Goa Shipyard that is consolidated into its results.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Submarines (Project P-75 Scorpene, P-75 add-on, P-75(I))** — Building conventional submarines for the Navy — six Scorpenes under P-75 with five delivered between 2017 and 2022 and the last one pending as of mid-2023 — and management calls this the company's unique selling point since no other Indian yard has repeatedly built submarines; the three add-on Scorpenes and the P-75(I) programme (bid with Germany's TKMS) were still awaiting contract signature as of Oct 2025 and are the main reason management expects the order book and margins to rise sharply.
- **Shipbuilding – Project 15 Bravo destroyers** — Four destroyers ordered in 2011 with a contract value of Rs 32,081 crore, delivered between 2021 and FY25; management says MDL is the only Indian builder of destroyer-class ships, and the late-stage delivery of this project drove the unusually high margins reported in FY25.
- **Shipbuilding – Project 17 Alpha stealth frigates** — Four stealth frigates, described on the calls as roughly a Rs 27,000 crore project, with deliveries starting from FY25 at about one a year; as of Oct 2025 the third was due that calendar year and the fourth plus its guarantee period slips into the following financial year, making it the main revenue driver until the submarine orders land.
- **Smaller vessels – Coast Guard Fast Patrol Vessels and the MPV project** — Smaller craft for the Coast Guard and an MPV order that management flagged as loss-making — roughly Rs 1,000 crore of provisions for onerous contracts were booked across Q4 FY25 and Q1 FY26 for the Coast Guard FPV and the other contract, and these low-margin jobs are what will fill the yard during the gap between the big projects.
- **Exports** — A small overseas effort — a Denmark contract (also provisioned as likely loss-making) and an offshore project, plus an unsuccessful techno-commercial proposal to a Russian shipyard that stalled on geopolitics and scope mismatch; it is a minor part of the business compared with Indian naval orders.
- **New bids and partnerships** — MDL is chasing future work such as the Landing Platform Dock under a roughly Rs 79,000 crore DAC approval, for which it signed an exclusive MoU with Swan Shipyard (SDHI) to combine MDL's design, weapon-integration and project-management capability with the private partner's larger infrastructure; a further destroyer programme of around Rs 34,000-36,000 crore was described as still at a preliminary stage with the Navy.
- **Investment in Goa Shipyard** — A 47% shareholding in Goa Shipyard held as an investment and merged into MDL's consolidated results — it lifts consolidated PAT and net worth modestly above the standalone figures rather than being an operating business MDL runs.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

**Capital Allocation — Good (+0.67)** · 3 of 6 checks had evidence

- **Cost of working capital — Excellent** *(from the financial statements)*: Cash comes back 21 days BEFORE suppliers need to be paid — customers effectively fund the business (negative working capital, a rare strength).

  > "we are also doing having major CAPEX plans in that land as well as in the Nhava yard which we have a CAPEX of approximately Rs. 4,000 crores" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Merger and acquisition — Weak** *(from the conference calls)*: The one acquisition, Colombo Dockyard, is a cross-border commercial-shipbuilding yard that is loss-making rather than already strong, still not closed at Nov 2025, and requires MDL management to be 'pitched in' for control — the opposite of a simple bolt-on with immediate OPM accretion.
  > "By the information we have as per their balance sheet, etc, is around 15% margin, but there are some losses" — *management, on an earnings call*

**Return on Capital — Neutral (+0.33)** · 3 of 3 checks had evidence

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Cost to Replicate — STRONG FIT
*"Could a newcomer copy this?"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** A concrete, articulable barrier recurs across all four calls: the only Indian yard that has actually built and delivered conventional submarines (on two foreign platforms) and the only builder of destroyers, with finite Mumbai waterfront, capacity for 11 submarines/10 warships and a Rs 4,000 cr capex programme — decades of accumulated build experience plus MoD nomination that money alone cannot shortcut, though L&T-Navantia bidding P75I shows the barrier is not absolute.

> "we are the only builder of Destroyer class of ships in India. Nobody else has ever constructed a Destroyer class ship." — *management, on an earnings call*

### Market Share Gainers — LIKELY FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and the only computable check supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** Structural share of Indian naval newbuild is high and management claims pole position on the coming Rs 35,000–80,000 cr pipeline, but no rising share numbers are ever given, the order book shrank from ~Rs 38,500 cr (Jun-2023) to Rs 27,415 cr (Oct-2025) as promised orders slipped, and L&T/Navantia is a live competitor on P75I; international expansion (Denmark) was won at a loss.

> "the order book stands at around INR 27,415 crores" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Sales compounding at 12% while margins held or improved (5% → 17%) — growth that was WON, not bought with price cuts.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** MDL is a Government of India undertaking whose entire order flow, pricing and even provision reversals are set by MoD/Navy processes — AON/RFP approvals, DAC sanction, CCS-level contract signing, LD refunds granted at the Navy's discretion, MOU targets negotiated with the government, and nomination-basis margins administratively pegged at 7.5-8%.
