**Checks that improved:**

- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: Branch network grew 301→339 with 39 added in FY26 and 60-70 planned for FY27 plus doubled IT spend, yet opex/AUM held at 2.7-2.8% while ROE stayed above 20% — growth investment funded without diluting incremental returns. | The long view had said: Branch network grew from 211 (Jun 2022) to 339 (Mar 2026) with 60 more planned in FY27 plus doubled IT spend, and the incremental returns are high (ROE up from ~16% to 21.2%), though management deliberately kept branch capex 'bare minimum' and productivity-led rather than aggressive.
- **Distribution to shareholders** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: A modest, consistent dividend of INR4.50 was declared for FY26 while capital is retained to fund 20%+ AUM growth — coherent for a compounding lender, but there is no buyback discipline to judge. | The long view had said: Distribution is limited to a modest dividend (INR4.50 declared in FY26) with no buyback and no articulated valuation-linked distribution policy; capital is retained to fund 22-24% AUM growth.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: The company is riding a favourable funding-rate cycle, with cost of funds falling from 8.62% to 8.1% and spreads widening to 9%, though much of the benefit is already banked and partly passed through to borrowers. | The long view had said: Management explicitly argues the segment is non-cyclical (secured, self-occupied, low LTV) rather than positioning the company at a cycle trough with an identifiable upturn ahead.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Weak. Now: Switching costs appear weak — the company had to cut incremental home-loan rates by 50-75bps to defend and win customers — with the only real friction being physical branch proximity in Tier 3/4 towns. | The long view had said: Small-ticket, transaction-intensive loans in tier 3/4 towns create a de facto captive book because larger banks and HFCs will not underwrite them, evidenced by balance-transfer-out of only ~2%.
- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Weak. Now: Credit is a commodity here with no image- or taste-driven pricing power; high yields reflect risk pricing on informal-income borrowers and are being cut where competition bites. | The long view had said: Real pricing power exists — rates held for over a decade through a 140bps policy tightening with NIMs of 12.8-14.3% and spreads of 9% — but it derives from segment scarcity of credit rather than brand image or taste.
- **Network effects** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Weak. Now: The business has no self-reinforcing two-sided network; the closest analogue, the connector and construction-ecosystem referral channel, is described by management as still nascent. | The long view had said: Only a weak referral flywheel exists via the customer referral and construction ecosystem apps; there is no self-reinforcing two-sided network that strengthens with scale.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Neutral → Weak. Now: Growth is volume- and ticket-size-led while pricing is being actively cut — 50-75bps off incremental home loans — so price is a headwind rather than a driver, with only ATS/mix upgrade working in the company's favour. | The long view had said: Growth is volume and ticket-size led, not pricing led — rates were held flat for over a decade and then cut 50-75bps on incremental home loans in FY26, with mix improvement coming from a deliberate INR1 lakh/year ATS increase partly attributed to inflation.
- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Good → Neutral. Now: Lending is inherently balance-sheet-heavy rather than asset-light — growth consumes equity — though the returns earned on those assets are exceptional at 8.2% ROA and 21.2% ROE with best-in-class 2.8% opex/AUM. | The long view had said: As a lender the balance sheet is inherently asset-heavy, but yield on assets is exceptional and sustained (ROA 8.8% in FY23, still 8.2% in FY26 with spreads of 9%) and opex of 2.7-2.8% of AUM is best-in-class, protected by a small-ticket niche large banks avoid.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: Entry barriers are low — a competitor can open a branch simply by hiring away Aptus staff — and pricing is drifting downward with incremental rate cuts rather than holding firm. | The long view had said: Pricing has been broadly rational — rates unchanged for over a decade until a modest FY26 cut passing on lower funding costs, not a discount war — but entry barriers are low, with competitors opening branches by poaching staff.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: The Tier 3/4 self-employed niche is genuinely unglamorous, but the company is listed, institutionally held and covered by Citi, Yes Securities and Dolat, so it is hardly ignored by capital. | The long view had said: The niche — INR9 lakh average ticket, EMI ~INR12,000, self-construction loans in Tier 3/4 towns — is one larger capital deliberately avoids as too transaction-intensive, though Aptus itself is a widely covered listed company.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 3 weakened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared.

*Foundation test: FAIL on the long view, FAIL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Good marks for candour and long-term choices — the Q1 disbursement dip was explained rather than buried, the sub-INR7 lakh pullback sacrificed near-term growth for customer quality, write-off policy was tightened to 500 days, and founder-chairman Anandan has led throughout; but guidance was overshot (Aug 2025 promised ~22% disbursement growth and 28-29% AUM growth for FY26 versus 11% and 21% delivered), and the 5-year record shows three CFO changes, two chairman exits and MD churn. (The long view had said: Founder-led with Anandan and Balaji stable across all 16 calls, guidance repeatedly delivered (credit cost held at the guided 0.5%, ROE guided and reached 20%+, 40 branches planned/39 opened, 22–24% AUM growth), conservative choices owned openly — retaining the Rs.45cr overlay, 100% write-off beyond 24 months, sacrificing disbursements to drop small-ticket loans — and bad news disclosed promptly (Karnataka collections, MSME NPA uptick, the April 2024 tech-migration disbursement hit); the main blemish is the FY23 promise to bring 30+ DPD to 4–5%, which still sat at 6.2–6.5% in FY26, plus CFO churn.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The last year shows price deflation, not pricing power: 50-75bps cuts on incremental home loans in Q3 FY26 and further 'optimized' rates in Q4, justified by lower borrowing costs and the need to aid growth, plus admitted competitive pressure in Tamil Nadu. Rising average ticket size is inflation-linked, not price realisation. (The long view had said: For most of the period Aptus held rates flat through a 140bps RBI hiking cycle without volume loss and claimed affordable-HFC pricing power ('In the last 11 years, we have hardly revised our interest rate'), but by FY26 it cut incremental home-loan rates 50–75bps to defend growth, which is the opposite of raising price above cost inflation.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Management itself describes the advantage as people-based and portable — competitors entering Tamil Nadu simply hire Aptus staff, attrition is 'slightly high', and rates had to be cut to defend growth; nothing in these calls points to a barrier a well-funded affordable-housing entrant could not rebuild in a few years. (The long view had said: Real but not un-replicable barriers: a 15-year underwriting and collections footprint in tier-3/4 towns that large banks and HFCs won't service (hence low ~2% BT-out on Rs.8–9 lakh tickets) — yet management itself admits entrants simply poach its staff to open branches, which is precisely the copycat path a well-funded newcomer can take.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
