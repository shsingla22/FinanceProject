# Bajaj Auto Ltd. (BAJAJ-AUTO) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: AUTOMOBILE*

Bajaj Auto is an Indian vehicle maker, based in Pune, that designs and manufactures motorcycles, electric scooters and three-wheelers (auto-rickshaws used as passenger taxis and small cargo carriers). It sells them to retail buyers through a dealer network in India and exports them to emerging markets across Latin America, Africa and Asia — exports alone were about 40% of revenue and USD 2.2 billion in FY26. Money comes from selling vehicles (plus spare parts), with a deliberate tilt toward higher-priced 125cc-and-above bikes, premium brands like KTM and Triumph, and newer 'green' products (electric and CNG), which management says lifts both revenue and margins. In FY26 the company reported record revenue of over INR 58,000 crore, EBITDA above INR 12,000 crore at a 20.5% margin, and profit of about INR 9,800 crore.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Exports business unit** — Ships motorcycles and three-wheelers to overseas emerging markets — Nigeria and Africa, Latin America (Mexico, Brazil, where it also manufactures locally), and Asia (Sri Lanka, Philippines, Nepal) — and is the single largest piece at roughly 40% of company revenue, about 7,32,000 units and USD 735 million in the June 2026 quarter.
- **Domestic motorcycles business unit** — Sells Bajaj-branded bikes in India — mainly the Pulsar sports range (125cc to 400cc), the Boxer/commuter end, and the CNG-petrol Freedom 125 — and accounts for about 25% of company revenue, with management focused on the faster-growing 125cc-plus half of the market where its 125cc share is around 25%.
- **Commercial vehicles / three-wheelers** — Makes petrol/CNG and electric three-wheeler autos and cargo carriers for drivers and fleet operators; it is the company's most dominant position, with roughly 78% share of the conventional Indian three-wheeler market, about 35% of the electric auto market, and record export volumes of 100,000 units in a quarter.
- **Chetak (electric scooters)** — The company's electric scooter brand, sold through 500-plus exclusive Chetak stores and shared motorcycle dealerships in 850+ cities; it crossed 5 lakh units and about INR 4,000 crore of revenue in FY26 with roughly 23% market share, and has just begun exporting.
- **Pro-biking (KTM and Triumph)** — The premium motorcycle arm, building and selling KTM bikes (in partnership with KTM Austria) and Triumph models like the Speed 400 through dedicated and joint showrooms; combined domestic volumes reached about 40,000–43,000 units a quarter, growing 40–50% year on year, and these bikes are also exported from India.
- **Electric mobility (EV two-wheelers and three-wheelers together)** — Management groups its electric scooters and electric autos as one growth story, saying they now make up about 30% of domestic revenue and earn double-digit EBITDA margins; the broader 'green' portfolio including CNG was cited at 44% of domestic revenue.
- **Spare parts** — Sale of replacement parts to the installed base of vehicles, mentioned as one of the three drivers (alongside domestic and export vehicle sales) of record quarterly revenue.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Bajaj Auto comes through the three reviews as a high-quality, cash-generative business that earns a Strong rating of 75 out of 100 — built from 79 on business quality, 79 on multibagger fit and 64 on risk safety. The everyday economics are unusually comfortable: about ₹21 of every ₹100 of sales is operating profit, and that margin has moved only about ±1.6 points a year, which the framework reads as both tight cost control and a rational industry where few new entrants are undercutting prices. Customers effectively fund the business — cash comes back one day before suppliers have to be paid — so the company has been able to pay for growth out of its own pocket: new EV plants, Brazilian manufacturing scaled from 35,000 to 60,000 units a year, and a board-approved capacity build from 7 million to 9 million units, all while margins stayed above 20%. The two visible soft spots are that return on capital is by far the weakest of the seven quality areas, and that overall resilience is judged strained.

The patterns that fit best are the ones these same strengths create. Brand Strength and Corporate Culture are strong fits because heritage names — Pulsar, Boxer, Dominar, and the revived Chetak — do the selling, and because the same management team across all sixteen earnings calls kept multi-year promises: Chetak went from 6,200 units a quarter in 100-city ambitions to 5 lakh units across 850 cities, and the Delhi e-3W trials became market leadership. Forward Integration is the third strong fit and explains much of the share capture credited in the quality checks: over 500 exclusive Chetak stores and 4,500 touchpoints, 120 Triumph stores, and around 75 owned stores in Brazil, where a 2024 plant start became a top-five position in three years. Recurring revenue, innovation and share gains rate as likely fits — spares run at ₹1,700–1,800 crore a quarter off the installed base, and named launches with real revenue arrive every year. Pricing Power, Cost to Replicate and Low Price Plus only partially fit, and Toll Roads does not fit at all: this is a consumer purchase, not an embedded input into someone else's costs.

The risk review turns several of those same traits around. Seven of eight risks sit at Watch and only one at Low. The heritage brands that power the strongest pattern also carry Fashion Risk — management itself launched a turnaround in November of FY26 to address end-of-cycle fatigue in parts of the portfolio and promised a full Pulsar makeover within six weeks. The large installed ICE base that makes the spares annuity work is the same base exposed to the platform shift under Technological Innovation, with EV scooters and 150cc-plus bikes driving essentially all industry growth while 100cc and 125cc went flat. The emerging-market retail footprints that make the business hard to replicate are also the source of Stakeholder Concentration: Nigeria, the largest export market, ran at half its peak through FY26, and the KTM/Pierer stake took roughly ₹600 crore off consolidated profit in one quarter. EV and 3-wheeler leadership brings Government Dependency, since GST cuts drove the FY26 recovery and the expiry of the PM E-DRIVE subsidy was absorbed into margin. New Entrants and Good Enough Goods point the same way — EV-native players pushed Bajaj to third place in scooters, and Chinese-origin commercial bikes are attacking Africa. Only Shifting Consumer Preferences scores Low, because premiumisation and electrification are moving in the company's favour.

*"A culture that compounds"*
