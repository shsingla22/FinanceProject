# TVS Motor Company Ltd. (TVSMOTOR) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: AUTOMOBILE*

TVS Motor Company is an Indian vehicle maker that designs, builds and sells two-wheelers — motorcycles and scooters, both petrol (ICE) and electric — plus three-wheelers used as passenger autos and cargo carriers. It sells to retail consumers and small commercial users through dealer networks in India and exports to Africa, Asia (Sri Lanka, Nepal, Bangladesh, ASEAN) and Latin America, with manufacturing in India (Hosur), Indonesia and the UK (Solihull). Money comes mainly from vehicle sales volume — FY26 sales were 5.9 million units on revenue of INR 47,270 crores with an EBITDA margin of ~12.9% — supported by a mix shift toward premium and electric models, price increases and steady cost-reduction programmes. A second, sizeable profit stream is its lending arm, TVS Credit, which finances vehicle and consumer purchases, and the group also owns the British premium motorcycle brand Norton.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Domestic two-wheeler ICE (petrol motorcycles and scooters)** — The core business — brands like Jupiter and Jupiter 125 scooters, Apache and Raider and Ronin and Star/Radeon motorcycles and Ntorq 125 — sold across India, growing 19% in FY26 versus industry growth of about 10% and making up the bulk of the company's roughly 5.9 million annual units.
- **EV two-wheelers (iQube / Orbiter)** — Electric scooters sold in India and increasingly exported, scaled from about 16,000 units in a quarter in FY23 to 3.7 lakh units in FY26 (up 33%) and 130,000 units in a single quarter by Q1 FY27, with multiple battery sizes and a battery-as-a-service subscription option.
- **Three-wheelers (TVS King, King Kargo, King EV MAX)** — Passenger autos and cargo three-wheelers in petrol, CNG and electric versions, a smaller but fast-growing line that reached 220,000 units in FY26, up 63% on the prior year.
- **International business / exports** — Two- and three-wheelers shipped to Africa, Asia and Latin America — the HLX motorcycle series alone has crossed 5 million units — reaching a record 15.8 lakh units in FY26, up 33%, roughly a quarter of total volume.
- **TVS Credit Services** — The group's retail lending arm, financing two-wheelers, consumer durables, mobile phones, used cars, tractors, used commercial vehicles and unsecured loans, with a loan book of INR 32,053 crores, about 2.6 crore customers and PBT of INR 1,248 crores in FY26 — a meaningful share of group profit.
- **Norton Motorcycles** — A UK premium/super-premium motorcycle brand bought in 2021 and being relaunched with the Manx, Manx R, Atlas and Atlas GT models, built at Solihull (UK) and Hosur (India) for sale in the UK, Europe, India and later the US — currently an investment phase rather than a profit contributor.

### The story in depth

TVS Motor comes through these three reviews as a company that has been winning share the hard way and has been rewarded for it. The quality framework gives full marks on gaining market share (domestic ICE volumes up 19% in FY26 against an industry at 10%, international up 31% against 23%, EV up 86% in Q1FY27) from a starting point that was not dominant — a clear No. 3 position. The multibagger screen independently flags Market Share Gainers, Pricing Power and Corporate Culture as strong fits, and they are really three views of the same thing: the company raised prices roughly 1% in Q2FY23 and covered about a third of a 3-5% commodity shock with price in FY26, yet volumes still grew 24-28% and the Jupiter 110 ran a waiting list. The same leadership team (Venu Srinivasan, Sudarshan Venu, Radhakrishnan, Desikan) appeared on every call from 2022 to 2026 and delivered what it promised, which is why the framework scores management and disciplined stewardship highly: growth has been organic, dealer stock stays under 30 days on cash-and-carry terms, and customers pay about 60 days before suppliers need to be paid, so the customer effectively funds the working capital. That negative working capital is what allowed a 1.5 million-unit capacity addition, EV two-wheeler capacity going from 40,000 to over 50,000 a month and three-wheeler from 20,000 to 30,000, while margins still climbed from 10.2% in Q2FY23 to 13.1% in Q4FY26.

The interesting part is that the traits producing the best scores are the same traits producing every open risk. The electric-vehicle push is the clearest example. It underpins the top score on structural end-market growth (two-wheeler EV penetration rising from 6.2% to over 10.6% a month, three-wheeler EV past 40%), it is the proof point for the tenacity check — the 2022 promise of a full 5-25kW electric range was carried through loss-making early years to 130,000 units a quarter from 16,000 — and it is the reason both Technological Innovation and New Entrants sit on WATCH. A shift from petrol engines to electric is exactly the kind of change that strands an incumbent's existing plant and dealer setup, and it is what let electric-native rivals reset the economics of the category; by July 2026 an analyst was asking directly about a major competitor on a discounting spree in scooters. Government Dependency is the same story from another angle: the fastest-growing part of the business leans on PM E-DRIVE's eight quarters of visibility, a PLI benefit worth about 0.9% of revenue, and GST relief that management credits for FY27 demand. Premiumisation cuts both ways too. It is what makes the Brand Strength and Innovation Dominance patterns fit — Jupiter past 5 million units, HLX past 5 million with the last million in a single year, iQube past 1 million, Norton launched across the UK, France, Italy, Germany, Spain and India — and it is why the company could hold its price and still take share against a discounter, which is what keeps Good Enough Goods at LOW. But it also leaves management conceding that the challenge will continue in the economy category where inflation-hit buyers sit, which is the Shifting Consumer Preferences risk. The advertising spend that the framework treats as investment rather than cost is the thing keeping all of this working.

What the three reviews agree on is where the story is thinner. The quality framework scores competitive advantage at only 0.33 and return on capital at zero, and industry structure comes out slightly negative — in other words, the winning is being done by execution rather than by a protected position. The pattern screen says the same in its own language: Cost to Replicate is only a partial fit, because while the decades-old dealer networks across India, Africa, Asia and Latin America, TVS Credit's 2.6 crore customers and 62,000 touchpoints, and 13 years of HLX equity in Africa are genuinely hard to copy, the manufacturing itself is not. Forward integration is real but limited to captive finance funding about 44% of purchases and the newly announced Paddock premium retail channel, with no owned stores. Friendly Middleman and Toll Roads are outright no-fits — a two-wheeler is a big-ticket consumer decision, not a small critical input someone else pays for — and the company is heavily covered by Goldman Sachs, UBS, Nomura and Axis Mutual Fund, so there is no under-the-radar discount here (the framework's only -2). The risk review calls overall resilience STRESSED, with five of eight channels on WATCH and none clean of cyclicality, since management frames every year around the monsoon, rural sentiment and tax relief. The combined rating lands at 66 out of 100 — 65 on business quality, 71 on pattern fit, 60 on risk safety — which reads as a well-run share gainer executing very well inside a cyclical, competitive, policy-exposed industry, rather than a business insulated from it.

## What to watch

- Pricing Power: if price increases start costing volume — particularly against the scooter rival flagged as discounting in Q1FY27 — the strongest pattern in the file weakens and the Good Enough Goods risk stops being LOW.
- Government Dependency (WATCH): expiry or reduction of PM E-DRIVE support, the ~0.9%-of-revenue PLI line, or the GST relief credited for FY27 demand would slow the EV growth that carries the structural end-market growth score.
- New Entrants and Technological Innovation (both WATCH): loss of EV share — the run from 2.9% to 9.9% stalling or reversing — would undercut the market-share-gainer case and expose the ICE plant and dealer base to the shift.
- Cost of working capital check: any slippage in the under-30-day dealer stock or the cash-and-carry terms would remove the customer funding that is currently paying for the 1.5 million-unit capacity addition and the EV capacity ramp.
- Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH): if the economy-segment weakness management already concedes spreads into the premium and scooter lines that drive the Brand Strength and Innovation Dominance patterns, the premiumisation strategy loses its offset.
- Return on Capital (0.0) and Competitive Advantage (0.33): margins have risen from 10.2% to 13.1% — if that expansion stops while capex stays growth-directed, the weakest two areas of the quality framework become the binding constraint on the rating.
