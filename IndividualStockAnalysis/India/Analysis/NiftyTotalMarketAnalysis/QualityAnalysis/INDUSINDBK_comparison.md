Now: The new CEO articulates a long-term universal-banking vision and is willing to shrink the book for returns, but with only three quarters in the seat there is no track record of tenacious execution yet. | The long view had said: Successive multi-year plans (PC-5, PC-6) were abandoned mid-course after the leadership exit, and the new three-year PACE agenda is too recent to evidence tenacity, though it is coherently articulated.

Now: Licensing provides a real entry barrier, but the microfinance segment showed clearly irrational prior underwriting across the industry requiring sector-wide MFIN guardrails. | The long view had said: Evidence of irrational competition is explicit — pricing and collateral relaxation in secured retail, and industry-wide microfinance over-leverage that produced the credit blowup.

Now: Trust — the core assurance benefit for a bank — was broken and is being rebuilt, though Q4's all-retail net deposit accretion of Rs.6,800 crores shows partial repair. | The long view had said: Trust — the core product of a bank — was impaired: deposits de-grew for three consecutive quarters, senior management exited, and rebuilding perception is now an explicit strategic priority rather than an assumed strength.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 2 could not be compared.

*Foundation test: FAIL on the long view, FAIL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Share gain is aspirational and confined to pockets: vehicle finance claims OEM leadership and home loans grew 45–94% year-on-year off a tiny base, while the aggregate balance sheet shrank — average loans down 2% quarter-on-quarter in Q3 and Q4 FY26, deposits down, micro loans down 5% quarter-on-quarter, credit cards down 5% quarter-on-quarter. Card spend share of 4.9% is stated once with no trend. (The long view had said: Share gains claimed in FY23–FY25 (card spends share to 5%, NRI share 3.3%→3.7%, 18–23% loan growth guidance) reversed into outright contraction — micro loans down 17% quarter-on-quarter, wholesale down 5–6% quarter-on-quarter, average advances down 2% for two straight quarters — with the FY27 ambition merely to match system growth, the classic case of share bought with underwriting that then had to be given back.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The one articulable barrier is the decades-built vehicle finance franchise — OEM relationships, geographic spread and through-cycle underwriting that a funded newcomer could not assemble quickly. Everything else is replicable, and the deposit franchise is the opposite of a moat, requiring four quarters of effort to move retail share from 46.5% to 47.9%. (The long view had said: Management explicitly frames the business as regulated and commoditised with execution as the only differentiator, and the 'endowments' it names (vehicle finance, rural reach) sit alongside a costlier deposit base than peers — nothing here a well-funded competitor could not rebuild.)

**That weakened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The INDIE apps and new Chief Data Officer/Head-Digital hires are mentioned, but the stated near-term posture is cost containment to single-digit opex growth with no disclosed innovation spend, named revenue-generating launches, or margin structure that could fund outsized investment. (The long view had said: There is a steady cadence of named digital launches with disclosed metrics — Indus Easy Credit, IndusMerchantSolutions, INDIE (1.3mn users in FY25 to 2.7mn MAU by Q3 FY26), and now a GenAI Centre of Excellence with ten use cases — but no R&D disclosure, no pricing power from it, and management itself calls the industry commoditised.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT); Corporate Culture (NO FIT).*

*Not comparable: Toll Roads (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view); Forward Integrators (NOT ASSESSED → PARTIAL — could not be assessed on the long view).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

**That eased:**

- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Nothing in the four calls is defensive about a competing technology platform or a legacy format; the innovation discussed is incremental and value-adding — INDIE and INDIE for Business apps, digital enablement of 500+ branches for gold loans, and a Calypso treasury system upgrade — supported by dedicated senior hires. **That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The concentration is on the funding and business-line side rather than a named customer: bulk/wholesale deposits and certificates of deposit had to be shed over four straight quarters, and the bank remains disproportionately dependent on two legacy engines — vehicle finance and BFIL microfinance — with BFIL's governance failures in FY25 flowing straight into group results. (The long view had said: The book is granular by construction — millions of retail/micro borrowers, no single named customer driving results across any of the 15 calls — and management is actively de-concentrating both sides: 'granularization' of wholesale, rationalising large corporates, and shedding bulk deposits so retail LCR deposits rose to 47.9%.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
