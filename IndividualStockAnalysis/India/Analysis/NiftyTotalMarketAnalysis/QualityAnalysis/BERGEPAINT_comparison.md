**Checks that improved:**

- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Halo risk is low because reported results are weak (PAT down 8.8% YTD, value growth ~1%) and management describes them plainly, so skill claims like share gain and gross-margin defence can be assessed on their own evidence rather than on a glowing P&L. | The long view had said: Skill is partly separable from luck — the FY25 volume divergence was credibly attributed to distribution and urban execution — but flat value growth, declining PAT and FY26 share loss mean results do not yet independently validate the narrative.
- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Good. Now: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.18 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: The business remains net-cash and self-funding (net cash rising from Rs 689 cr in Mar-25 to Rs 918 cr in Dec-25) while expanding through dealer touchpoints rather than heavy fixed assets, but no explicit asset-turn or ROCE data is given. | The long view had said: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.18 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: Balance sheet is conservatively run (net cash improving from Rs.242cr to Rs.918cr) but asset turnover is never discussed and the concurrent build-out of stores, plants and colour machines against flat value sales implies turns are diluting rather than improving.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Excellent → Good. Now: Distribution reach plus tinting-machine installed base is the company's stated core moat and is being widened aggressively, but management concedes it is still behind in many regions and is the #2 player. | The long view had said: Distribution reach is the explicit, management-identified source of outperformance and the primary strategic lever, expanded at a deliberate pace faster than peers across all three calls.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: There is regional strength (notably the East, which the new entrant is targeting with an Eastern plant) but no switching costs, and management concedes weak distribution in many parts of the country. | The long view had said: Regional strength in the East and upcountry plus thousands of company-installed colour-bank tinting machines at dealer counters create real, if incomplete, dealer switching costs.

Now: The opposite of obscure — the sector has drawn large new capital (Birla Opus building capacity including in the East) and heavy sell-side scrutiny on every quarter. | The long view had said: The opposite of obscure — the sector is intensely analyst-covered, attracted a large diversified conglomerate's capital, and is the subject of a contested MNC asset sale.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 8 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: A 1923-vintage brand in its 100th year with sustained TV and digital A&P (anti-dust easy clean, roof cool and seal), >20% share and successful stretch into construction chemicals, waterproofing and wood coatings under the same name — but brand strength is not converting into premium pricing, since value growth is flat and prices have been cut. (The long view had said: Real heritage (centenary year, new paint-can-shaped head office), sustained TV and digital A&P maintained even when margins were squeezed, and successful stretch into construction chemicals, waterproofing and wood coatings under the same name. But the brand is not converting into premium pricing or defended share — value growth is ~0% and Feb 2026 concedes marginal share loss, so the durability test is being failed on the pricing leg.)

**That weakened:**

- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The last twelve months are a live test of replicability and the moat did not hold: a well-funded newcomer reached ~₹1,200cr of sales in nine months and ~3.5% share, forcing Berger into price cuts and flat value growth, while management concedes competitive intensity will stay elevated. Nothing in the calls articulates a barrier — time, data, capital or location — a rich entrant could not rebuild. (The long view had said: There is an articulable barrier — decades of dealer density, ~2,000 retail touchpoints and 2,000-2,500 tinting machines added per quarter, and a 100-year brand — and management notes serial MNC entrants (Jotun, Hempel, Sherwin) have never been disruptive. But the test is partly failed in real time: a well-funded copycat took ~3.5% share within a year and forced price cuts and higher painter spends, showing capital can buy a meaningful position in a few years.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
