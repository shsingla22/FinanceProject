# Asian Paints Ltd. (ASIANPAINT) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSUMER GOODS*

Asian Paints has been in business since 1942 and describes its purpose as beautifying, preserving and transforming spaces and objects. Its core business is making and selling paints and coatings in India — decorative paint for homes across economy, premium and luxury price ranges, plus fast-growing adjacent products like waterproofing, construction chemicals and adhesives — sold mainly through a retail dealer network that management says had crossed about 1.63 lakh retail points, three to four times any competitor. It earns money by selling volume through that network at rising realisations (price increases and a richer product mix), and by adding higher-margin new products, which management says are roughly 11-12% of the top line. Alongside the core paints business it runs a B2B/Projects arm selling to builders, factories, government infrastructure and hospitality, a home décor business (kitchens, bath, lighting, UPVC windows, rugs, décor stores), industrial coatings joint ventures with PPG of the U.S., and an international business spanning Asia, the Middle East, Africa and the South Pacific.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Decorative paints (India)** — The main business — interior and exterior wall paints, enamels and specialty finishes sold across Economy, Premium and Luxury ranges through dealers; management says the Economy end is still about 70% of the market and that Asian Paints claims a strong share in all three tiers.
- **Waterproofing and construction chemicals (SmartCare / Damp Proof)** — Products that stop damp and protect buildings — from foundation admixtures to membranes, repair chemicals and topcoats, over 280 products developed in-house — described as growing at exponential rates, number one in the retail waterproofing market, with Damp Proof called the company's single largest product.
- **Projects / B2B business** — Selling directly to builders, large factories, hospitality chains and government infrastructure projects (airports, ports, tunnels, bridges); repeatedly called a strong growth engine, though it slowed in Q4 FY24 when the election code delayed government spending.
- **Industrial coatings and JVs (APPPG and PPGAP with PPG of the U.S.)** — Coatings for industrial and automotive-type applications run through two joint ventures with PPG; management says it performed strongly for three years running and adds roughly one percentage point to reported growth when included.
- **Home décor — Beautiful Homes stores and services** — A push from 'share of surface' to 'share of space,' offering kitchen, bath, sanitary, lighting, tiles, flooring and rugs under one roof through about 31 Beautiful Homes stores plus a personalised interior-design service in nine cities, anchored on the beautifulhomes.com digital platform.
- **Kitchen and Bath businesses** — Modular kitchens and bath fittings — in Q1 FY23 kitchen grew 66% to about Rs. 111 crores and bath grew 116% to about Rs. 119 crores, both crossing the Rs. 100 crore mark in a quarter, with bath turning a small profit and a new Pune plant planned.
- **Acquired décor brands — Weatherseal and White Teak** — Weatherseal sells UPVC windows and doors and White Teak sells decorative lighting/fans; White Teak was described as a Rs. 20 crore quarterly business against Rs. 57 crores for the whole prior year, so both are small but fast-growing add-ons.
- **Services (Beautiful Homes Painting Service, Total Assure, Smart Assure, Metacare, AP Assure)** — Paid, supervised painting and asset-care services — a consumer painting service management calls the world's largest, supervision-led services for large projects and waterproofing, and Metacare for maintaining and protecting factory assets against corrosion — positioned as a differentiator no competitor matches.
- **International / AP Global business** — Paint operations outside India across Asia, the Middle East, Africa and the South Pacific, which delivered double-digit growth in the quarter discussed.
- **Manufacturing and backward integration** — Its own plants — expanded from about 17 lakh to 22.7 lakh KL a year through brownfield projects at Kasna, Khandala, Ankleshwar and Mysuru, with a 4,00,000 KL greenfield plant in Madhya Pradesh targeted for 2028 — plus a white cement plant in Fujairah to make key raw materials in-house and cut costs.
- **Innovation and patents** — An in-house R&D engine that management says has produced more than 160 patents, with commercialised launches such as Graphene-based exteriors, the anti-termite 'PU Gold' finish and 'Damp Secure', used to keep products hard for competitors to copy.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Asian Paints scores 80 out of 100 — 45% weight on business quality (68), 30% on multibagger fit (100) and 25% on risk safety (78) — which lands it at Outstanding, five stars, with overall resilience judged SOUND. The engine behind that score is very concentrated: the strongest quality areas are Return on Capital (1.33), Customer Benefits (1.25) and Competitive Advantage (1.00), while Growth and Industry Structure both sit near the bottom at 0.17. In plain terms, the business turns capital into cash extremely well — ₹28 of operating cash for every ₹100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings, and ₹19 of operating profit on every ₹100 of sales, with margins moving only about ±1.7 points a year — and it reaches the customer better than anyone else, but the setting it operates in and the pace of value growth are where the framework marks it down.

The patterns the screen picks up are not separate discoveries; they are the same handful of company traits seen from different angles. The distribution base — over 1.6 lakh retail points, up from about 1.2 lakh, three to four times the nearest competitor, growing 4,000 to 10,000 points a year — is simultaneously the top-scoring quality check (Distribution advantage), the concrete barrier behind Cost to Replicate, and the delivery system for Convenience benefits. Layered on it, the sale is routed through painters, contractors, architects and designers (23 colour academies, 6.8 lakh tradespeople trained in FY24 against a target of 8-9 lakh, the 23-year ColourNext property), which is the Friendly Middleman fit. The 74 Beautiful Homes stores across 20 states, the company-owned White Teak stores, beautifulhomes.com and the world's largest own painting service are the Forward Integrators fit — and that same service layer is what turns the network into something rivals cannot copy. Research feeds all of it: 126 patents in FY24 rising past 160 by FY26, new products moving from 11-12% of sales to 17%, world-first launches and latex-at-distemper-cost technology, which is why Innovation Dominance is a strong fit and why the Technological Innovation risk is rated LOW — the innovation is self-generated and incremental, with no rival platform being discussed defensively. Management candour is the last thread: Kitchen and Bath losses, White Teak's weak bottom line and decor described as struggling are all volunteered unprompted, which is both the Candor check and the Corporate Culture pattern.

The risks are largely the flip side of the same traits, not a separate list. The company's deliberate high visibility — national cricket sponsorship, celebrity ambassadors, prime-time TV — is what scores -2 on Security by obscurity, and the records tie it directly to the ELEVATED New Entrants risk: being the most visible player has attracted well-capitalised competition. The response to that competition then shows up as the second -2 check, Pricing, Mix and Volume: a 3.7% price cut in FY24, four straight quarters of deflation in FY26, and FY26 volumes of about 9% against value growth of only 4.3%, a persistent 4-5% gap. That single fact simultaneously downgrades Pricing Power to PARTIAL and explains why Growth scores low even while volumes hold up — the company is buying volume with realisation. The painter-intermediary strength cuts the same way: because the tradesperson, not the homeowner, often picks the product, the Friendly Middleman advantage is also the mechanism behind the Good Enough Goods risk in a market where the economy tier is roughly 70% and downtrading recurs whenever prices rise. Cyclicality is a WATCH because gross margin swung from 37.9% in Q1 FY23 to an all-time-high 45.6% in Q4 FY25 on raw-material cycles, and Government Dependency is a WATCH because the fastest-growing segment is tied to infrastructure spending — management attributed one quarter's slowdown to the election code of conduct. Against these, three risks are LOW precisely because of the breadth already described: no single customer or distributor drives results across 1.6 lakh touchpoints, volume declines never appear, and the taste-exposed part of the portfolio is small — space decor is about 4% of decorative revenue. Market Share Gainers stays PARTIAL only because share gain is claimed directionally rather than with explicit percentages, and Toll Roads is a clear NO FIT: paint is a discretionary purchase with demonstrated price elasticity, which is the same elasticity that forced the price cut.

## What to watch

- The volume-value gap (Pricing, Mix and Volume, -2): FY26 volumes ~9% against value 4.3% and four quarters of deflation — if realisation stays negative, the Pricing Power PARTIAL becomes a NO FIT and Growth stays depressed.
- New Entrants (ELEVATED): whether the 3.7% price cut and price-matched bottom-of-pyramid response stop at defence or become a lasting reset of industry pricing.
- Distribution advantage and Cost to Replicate: retail points must keep compounding at 4,000-10,000 a year from the 1.6 lakh base, and painter training must reach the 8-9 lakh target — this is the single trait carrying the most weight in the rating.
- Innovation Dominance versus Good Enough Goods: new products at 17% of revenue and 160+ patents are what defend premium mix against a market that is ~70% economy tier; a stall in new-product share weakens both at once.
- Forward Integrators and Candor: Kitchen and Bath, White Teak's bottom line and 'struggling' decor are management-flagged losses inside the direct-to-customer push — if they do not turn, the forward-integration story costs money without adding to the moat.
- Cyclicality (WATCH): gross margin has already travelled from 37.9% to 45.6%; a reversal in raw-material costs would test the ±1.7 point margin steadiness that underpins the Profit margin check.
