## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 30 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 3 improved, 6 regressed, 21 unchanged. 3 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them).

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Allocation | Good (+1.00) | Good (+1.40) | +0.40 |
| Return on Capital | Good (+0.67) | Good (+0.67) | +0.00 |
| Growth | Good (+1.17) | Good (+1.33) | +0.17 |
| Management | Good (+1.17) | Good (+0.83) | -0.33 |
| Industry Structure | Neutral (+0.50) | Neutral (-0.17) | -0.67 |
| Customer Benefits | Good (+1.00) | Good (+0.75) | -0.25 |
| Competitive Advantage | Neutral (+0.33) | Good (+0.67) | +0.33 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Muuchstac is a textbook bolt-on — small (INR80cr sales), already highly profitable (~INR30cr EBITDA), complementary to personal care, bought at 4x sales/10x EBITDA below benchmarks, and completed on plan by Q3. | The long view had said: Deals are small and complementary (Muuchstac at ~INR70cr in men's face wash, RCCL/Park Avenue) and management claims value creation, but execution has lagged the underwriting — Deos GT share lost after a distribution-merger mistake and profitability behind the business case.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Good → Excellent. Now: Distribution is the stated engine of value creation — acquiring a single-SKU, two-channel brand precisely to push it through Godrej's full channel stack, scaling KS99 pan-India, and rolling aer Pocket across all African markets within a year. | The long view had said: Distribution is a demonstrable asset — rural vans driving above-market rural growth, and Muuchstac acquired explicitly because GCPL's GT/MT/e-commerce reach can scale a single-channel brand — but reach is matched or exceeded by HUL/ITC, and GT execution errors in Deos show it is not unassailable.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: Growth is predominantly volume-led (9-11% sales on 7-9% UVG, only low-single-digit price/mix) with soaps undergoing volume-price rebalancing and Indonesia revenue actually negative on price cuts, though Fab's 5% price rise and premiumisation into EDPs/face wash show some pricing power. | The long view had said: Growth is deliberately volume- and penetration-led, with pricing largely reactive cost pass-through (palm oil, crude) and mix explicitly negative as low-margin incense sticks and laundry scale up.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Weak. Now: These are low-switching-cost consumer categories where a rival can undercut on shelf immediately, as HUL's Sunlight launch at INR70/litre against Fab illustrates; there is no captive aftermarket or regional lock-in. | The long view had said: The electrics format carries a genuine razor-razorblade lock-in — machines sell first and pull proprietary refills — but most of the portfolio (soaps, laundry, hair colour) has essentially zero switching costs.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Calls are plain and operational with no personality-building, but the repeated 'goodness manifesto' framing is mild self-promotion and there is no direct evidence of a low-profile posture either way. | The long view had said: Calls are consistently substance-heavy — three-year CAGRs, offtake versus primary, cost-to-serve bps — and management repeatedly declines to talk about rivals, though brand-building does lean on celebrity ambassadors.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: There is some efficacy-as-assurance positioning (RNF formulations in insecticides, an immunity claim in pet food), but the calls repeatedly show price, not trust, driving category outcomes. | The long view had said: In insecticides the purchase is efficacy and safety rather than price — hence the RNF superior-formulation platform and the repositioning of Goodknight around protecting infants' sleep — though soaps and laundry remain price-led.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: Category structures are not stable oligopolies — Indonesia saw both retailers and companies cut price aggressively, and Reliance is entering pet food with 20-40% lower pricing, so the end consumer has ample alternatives. | The long view had said: Structure is mixed: HI is concentrated among a few branded players, but soaps, body wash and pet food are crowded with large legacy rivals, D2C entrants and a substantial unorganised/local segment.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: The Q3/Q4 recovery is attributable in meaningful part to exogenous help — moderating palm prices, GST rate cuts and a supportive base — alongside execution, so results should not be read purely as management skill. | The long view had said: The assessment is reasonably halo-proof because management itself separates luck from skill — attributing swings to weather, palm oil, base effects and macros while flagging where it under-delivered (Indonesia, RCCL, pet food).
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: There is a clear pool of weak/unorganised share to take — incense sticks in insecticides, soaps ceding to face wash, deodorants ceding to EDPs — plus across-the-board share capture from rivals in Indonesia. | The long view had said: Abundant weak donors: the entire non-GCPL incense-stick market is illegal/unorganised, locals in laundry and HI are being squeezed by kerosene and LAB cost inflation, and management notes it is lapping a weak competitor.

*Unchanged: Return on capital (headline); Cost of working capital; Research and development; Disciplined stewards; Profit margin; Structural end-market growth; Security by obscurity; Technology advantage; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Convenience benefits; Asset turn; Geographical expansion; Advertisement and promotion; Intangible benefits; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Gain in market share; Mini-monopolies; Barriers to entry and rationality; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Candor; Cyclical market growth.*

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these — identical by construction): Network effects (Weak); People matter (talent development) (Neutral); Persistence of growth (Excellent).*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 8 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 7. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 4 weakened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Share gains are claimed in every call of the window — Electrics share gain on relaunched products (Q1), soaps and other key categories through the GST disruption (Q2), Indonesia share gains across all categories on 5% UVG plus HI gains in India (Q3), and personal wash plus key home care categories (Q4) — and gains came with margins recovering to 24.7-24.8% standalone, not bought with profitability. (The long view had said: Explicit, rising share numbers across years — incense-stick share in high single digits with ~50% share in outlets where present (Jan 2025), electrics and coils gaining, Godrej Fab winning share in a seasonally weak quarter, Indonesia gaining share 'across all categories' and near-flat soap revenue described as market-beating; gains are made while holding ~20-25% EBITDA margins, not bought.)

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is one clean demonstration — a 5% price increase on Fab with no volume impact despite HUL undercutting with Sunlight at INR70/litre — and repeated 'calibrated pricing actions' supporting Q3/Q4 FY26 margins; but this is offset by soaps volume-price rebalancing and outright price deflation in Indonesia across all four calls, where revenue fell on pricing pressure. (The long view had said: Repeatedly raises price without losing volume: the Jan 2025 soap price/grammage actions taken to restore 'nominative margins' of 24-26%, and most strikingly a 30% weighted-average price increase in incense sticks with no volume slowdown by Jan 2026; May 2026 guides to pricing growth ahead of expectation.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Named launches with stated revenue traction recur every quarter — Block anti-perspirant, AirPlug, Amazon Woods 4X, Godrej Fab, Goodnight Agarbatti, KS INR99, Godrej Spic, aer Pocket — and Q3 FY26 attributes HI share gains to 'superior RNF based formulations'; but no R&D spend, share of industry R&D or pipeline economics is disclosed, and new-category attempts (pet food) are explicitly mixed. (The long view had said: A named, revenue-bearing innovation each year across the record: Magic ready-to-mix body wash (2022), Godrej Fab liquid detergent called 'one of our most successful innovations', the RNF molecule rolled through incense sticks/coils/LV/aerosols (2024-26) driving HI share gains, and aer pocket in Africa; management explicitly ties the share gains to the new formulation.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The only professional-channel evidence in the window is the nascent pet food build-out in Q3 FY26, where management describes building a vet contact programme alongside a kennel and sales team; the core FMCG portfolio is sold through mass retail with consumer advertising, not expert recommendation. (The long view had said: The only professional-channel evidence is the nascent pet-food build-out in Jan 2026, where management describes building a vet contact programme alongside a kennel and sales team; the core soaps/HI/hair portfolio is sold on consumer advertising, not expert recommendation.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The calls give no articulable reason a funded newcomer could not catch up, and instead show well-capitalised entrants attacking directly — HUL launching Sunlight at INR70/litre under Fab, Reliance Consumer planning pet food at 20-40% lower pricing, and an Indonesian price war from retailers and competitors that has depressed revenue for four straight quarters. (The long view had said: Real but partly contestable barriers: decades-old brands, a rural van distribution network that drives above-market rural growth, and proprietary RNF chemistry with regulatory registration lags — yet the same calls show illegal/local players holding most of incense sticks, Reliance entering pet food at 20-40% lower price, and GCPL itself 'behind the curve' on body wash, so a funded entrant is not structurally excluded.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (QUANT SIGNAL); Toll Roads (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (QUANT SIGNAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 6 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Cheaper challengers are a live theme — Unilever's Sunlight 'significantly undercutting even Fab', Reliance's 20-40% discount plan in pet food, and Indonesian competitors cutting price — and management is explicitly asked to defend its pricing. It held, but these are simple household goods where price-led substitution is structurally easy. (The long view had said: Cheaper unbranded substitutes are a persistent structural feature: illegal/proxy incense sticks were flagged as an unresolved problem as far back as Aug 2022 and by 2025 management concedes essentially the entire incense-stick market outside GCPL is illegal, while by May 2026 laundry and HI face 'a lot of pressure on locals' and urban consumers were down-trading in FY25. These are low-complexity, price-transparent products where a good-enough alternative is easy to supply.)

**That worsened:**

- **Government Dependency**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Fortunes are not set by any regulator, tariff or tender, but policy still moved a full quarter: the GST rate change disrupted the trade channel and dented soaps and hair colour in Q2 FY26, and an ICAI expert-committee opinion forced a restatement of how revenue is presented from Q4 FY26. (The long view had said: No revenue is set by regulator, tariff, subsidy or government tender, and the business is not anchored in fixed infrastructure; policy appears only as a second-order input (GST rate cut aiding soap affordability, product-registration/licence timelines for new HI molecules, kerosene availability constraining local players).)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
