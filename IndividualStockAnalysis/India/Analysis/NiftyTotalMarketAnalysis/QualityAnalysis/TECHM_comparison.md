### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Across all four calls management tracks itself publicly against the April-2024 three-year plan and the promised sequencing (margins first, growth in year three) has actually landed — margin expansion every quarter to 14.4% and every vertical growing in Q1 FY27 — while bad news is stated plainly rather than buried (hi-tech -4.6%, healthcare decline, US auto 'wait-and-watch', BFSI furlough impact). (The long view had said: Mixed record: CP Gurnani's 2022 promise that 11% was the margin bottom and 14% would be the Q4 exit was badly missed (margins were still 8.5% two years later), but the Joshi-era April-2024 three-year plan has been delivered on schedule and openly tracked — margins 9.7%→12.6%, growth pivot in year three, FY27 targets reiterated — alongside a 104% payout and conservative net-cash balance sheet; leadership turnover has nonetheless been high.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management claims 'value-based price optimization' and 'pricing discipline' as a margin driver in Oct 2025 and Jan 2026, but the same window records contractual productivity give-backs to a large client — the price-deflation dynamic the pattern says must be avoided — and no explicit price increases without volume loss are quantified. (The long view had said: Beyond a one-off ~50bps pricing benefit in Q1 FY23, the trajectory is price deflation: growth is being won on vendor consolidation and outcome-linked AI deals that explicitly commit to fewer tickets and lower cost for the client, and FY26 constant-currency growth was just 0.6%.)

**That weakened:**

- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across all four calls the buyer is the paying enterprise itself — CIO/CTO organisations, vendor-consolidation decisions, direct account teams — with no prescriber or installer channel; hyperscaler/Google/JP Morgan alliances are co-sell partnerships, not an expert intermediary who specifies the product for a price-insensitive payer. (The long view had said: Vendor selection in IT services is heavily influenced by sourcing advisors and analyst houses, and TechM explicitly invests in that channel — an Advisor Day in Pune (Jul 2024) and repeated citation of ISG/Everest/Avasant/Forrester rankings as a growth lever — but the buyer is also the payer, so price sensitivity is not removed and no professional-training-based lock-in exists.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Cost to Replicate (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 2 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Core IT services are substitutable and management talks defensively about holding price — 'value-based price optimization' and 'pricing discipline' — while clients push cost optimization, vendor consolidation and contractual annual productivity give-backs; the offset is a deliberate move up-stack into domain-tailored, AI-led and niche capability (payments modernization via the Avant Techno acquisition) where the premium is better protected. (The long view had said: IT services is a low-differentiation purchase where clients run vendor-consolidation and cost-rationalisation exercises, and management's own pricing was under sustained pressure — EBIT sank to 8.5% in FY25 with recovery driven by internal cost programmes (Fortius, offshoring, pyramid/freshers) rather than premium pricing; AI further lowers the cost of a 'good enough' substitute. The defence is domain expertise and IP, not a protected niche.)
- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Revenue is enterprise-contracted across BFSI, manufacturing, comms, retail/logistics and healthcare with no regulator- or tariff-set pricing; government appears only as a capability credential (India AI mission sovereign LLM) and indirectly via healthcare policy dampening client discretionary spend, not as a source of revenue or pricing. (The long view had said: No revenue is set by a regulator, but policy is repeatedly cited as a demand driver: healthcare declined 0.6% in FY26 on 'regulatory policy challenges' in Provider and Life Sciences, tariffs opened FY26 as a stated uncertainty, and the company anchors part of its AI positioning in the India National AI Mission and sovereign-model work; telecom clients are themselves regulated fixed-infrastructure operators.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: AI dominates every call — Orion, Orion Marketplace, TechM Helix, agentic development and modernization services — and management frames the effort-based delivery model as needing to be re-architected around agentic AI, the classic incumbent-infrastructure problem; the mitigant is that the shift so far reads as continuous, value-adding capability build rather than displacement, with management arguing AI favours its experienced workforce. (The long view had said: The entire arc of the calls is dominated by a platform-scale shift — from 5G/metaverse labs in 2022 to VerifAI, Indus LLM, Project Helix, Orion, agentic AI and 350+ deployable agents by 2026 — and management now sells outcome-based contracts that explicitly destroy the billable-effort base (40% fewer tickets, 40% reduction in incident handling effort, upgrade timelines compressed from months to weeks). Labour-arbitrage IT services is precisely the infrastructure built around a method that agentic AI threatens to reprice; the company is responding aggressively but the industry itself is the risk.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); New Entrants (QUANT FLAG); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
