# Elgi Equipments Ltd. (ELGIEQUIP) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: INDUSTRIAL MANUFACTURING*

Elgi Equipments is an Indian engineering company, founded in 1960 and based in Coimbatore, that designs and manufactures air compressors — the machines that supply compressed air, which management describes as a utility "just like electricity" that virtually every factory needs. It sells industrial and portable compressors, plus downstream accessories like dryers and filtration systems, to customers across essentially every industry (no single industry is more than 2-3% of revenue), partly direct and largely through a network of roughly 600 distributors, only about 60 of which are in India. Revenue comes from selling machines and related equipment and aftermarket accessories/service; it does business in 120-plus countries with direct presence in about 28 and manufacturing in three locations, and management notes it is a significant net earner of foreign exchange. In FY26 consolidated revenue was close to ₹4,000 crore, with the compressor business split roughly 50-50 between India and the rest of the world, and compressors making up about 90% of sales versus about 10% from automotive equipment.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Compressors (industrial)** — The core business — a full range of air compressors (screw, reciprocating, high-volume machines) sold to factories on the strength of energy efficiency, reliability and service; roughly 90% of company sales sit in compressors, and management says about 80% of customers buy on efficiency rather than price.
- **Portable compressors / construction and mining** — Portable and construction-and-mining compressors sold for infrastructure and rental use; in the US these are sourced from the group's Italian operation Rotair, and management calls this a structurally less profitable business that was hurt by tariffs on Italian imports.
- **Accessories and downstream products** — Dryers (refrigeration, with desiccant planned), filtration and other downstream equipment that used to be bought in and are now manufactured under Elgi's own brand — described as a strategic area of focus for the future.
- **Railways** — Compressors and equipment for Indian Railways — an India-centric, government-driven business that management calls "reasonable" rather than headline-grabbing, but which won a locomotive tender from Siemens that is running well and could open the door to locomotive OEM work worldwide.
- **Automotive equipment** — Garage/automotive workshop equipment, a separate product line from compressors; the sales mix is described as stable at roughly 10% of total sales against 90% for compressors.
- **North America — ELGi Industrial** — Supplies air compressors to independent distributors across the US; it drove record North American revenue in FY26 after the company doubled its sales headcount calling on distributors and converted many from competitors.
- **North America — ELGi Distribution Operations** — Houses the two US distribution acquisitions, Patton's and Michigan Air, i.e. company-owned distribution rather than third-party channel.
- **North America — Patton's Medical** — The medical air and gas business in the US, called a success story in FY26 and expanding into lower-presence regions like California, New York and the Northeast.
- **Vacuum (licensed)** — A newer line added through a license agreement with DVP for vacuum products — mentioned as a recent addition to the portfolio rather than a material revenue contributor so far.
- **Backward integration (foundry, motors, pressure vessels)** — In-house manufacture of castings, electric motors, pressure vessels and some of its own machining — not standalone revenue but described as a major competitive pillar, done for quality, safety and to remove sourcing risk from Chinese motors while cutting cost.
- **Regions** — Management reports by geography: ISAAME (India, South Asia, Africa, Middle East) as the home base, North America as the second-largest market, plus Europe (a consolidation/cost-realignment story now sized to fit and profitable), Australia, Southeast Asia (a ~$700m annual market being rebuilt under new leadership) and Brazil.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Elgi Equipments comes out of the three reviews as a genuinely well-run compressed-air business that rates Strong, scoring 73 out of 100 (business quality 72, multibagger fit 64, risk safety 84), with overall resilience judged sound. The quality framework's highest-scoring area is Management, and the reasons are specific: every earnings call opens with an unprompted walk-through of why profit fell short, and management openly owns its own failures, including the year lost to an ERP change, being caught out by commodity prices and an acquisition that ran inefficiently. Stewardship matches the talk — recent growth has been organic with an explicit refusal to chase deals, the balance sheet holds net cash, and effectively all operating profit converts into cash. The operating record is steady rather than spectacular: sales up about 11% a year with unusually little year-to-year variation, roughly Rs 15 of operating profit per Rs 100 of sales with margins moving only about 2.8 points a year, and about Rs 16 of operating cash per Rs 100 of capital employed.

The patterns screen shows that these same habits are what create the investment case. The strongest fit, alongside the culture pattern, is forward integration: Elgi has spent years buying and running its own distribution — Patton's and Michigan Air in the United States, Pulford in Australia — and shifting from selling only through channels to selling direct, which is also why it scores well on winning market share across India, the US and Australia. Pulling in the other direction, backward integration into its own foundry, pressure vessels and motors is what lets it claim a real cost culture (in-house motors beating the landed cost of Chinese ones through design rather than cheap labour, and three-day instead of three-to-six-month lead times) and it is the same integration that earns the top mark on customer assurance, because the overwhelming majority of buyers choose on efficiency, reliability and uptime rather than price. Research spending has repeatedly turned into products that sell — the PG850 portable, super-premium two-stage machines, own-brand dryers, the Demand=Match launch — which supports both the innovation and the pricing-power patterns, since price rises have been passed through regularly, including the successful navigation of tariffs. The long list of patterns rated only 'partial' is the honest counterweight: the business is still heavily channel-led with about 600 distributors, aftermarket service is the profit-quality differentiator but not yet a large enough annuity, and management itself concedes that outside India the brand is a weakness that needs funding and that the motor design edge is copyable.

The risk review found nothing severe — four items on watch, four low — but the watch items line up almost exactly with the patterns that fell short of a full fit. Cheap Chinese machines are taking the low-kilowatt segment across India, Europe and the US, and the price-driven slice of Indian customers is admitted to be growing; that single fact is why both the new-entrant and good-enough-goods risks are flagged and why the brand and cost-advantage patterns stop at partial. The portables business is openly cyclical and structurally less profitable precisely because it lacks a large aftermarket, so the same product line drags on the cyclicality risk and on the recurring-revenue pattern. Policy exposure is real too — management labels the government-centric railways business a risk, US tariffs of 10 to 50 percent hurt North America and Rotair through FY26, and tax refunds have sat stuck with Indian and European authorities. The one weak spot inside the quality framework itself is money tied up in operations: about 114 days of cash sits between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and capital allocation is by far the lowest-scoring area at 0.4. That is the flip side of the forward-integration strategy that the patterns screen likes most, since owning distribution means carrying inventory and receivables that a channel-only model would not. The offsets are equally concrete: no single industry is more than 2 to 3 percent of revenue, roughly 600 distributors of which only about 60 are in India, sales across more than 120 countries, and compressed air behaving like a factory utility that customers are reluctant to disturb once installed.

## What to watch

- The 114-day cash cycle and the 0.4 capital-allocation score — whether owning more distribution keeps absorbing cash, or whether the net-cash, 100%-cash-conversion discipline holds as the Forward Integrators strategy widens.
- Chinese low-cost entrants in the low-kilowatt segment (New Entrants and Good Enough Goods risks) — whether Elgi's dedicated lower-priced range holds the line, or whether the price-driven customer cohort keeps growing and pulls the Low Price Plus and Cost to Replicate patterns down.
