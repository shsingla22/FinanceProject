## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 31 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 6 improved, 7 regressed, 18 unchanged. 2 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them).

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Allocation | Good (+0.83) | Neutral (+0.33) | -0.50 |
| Return on Capital | Good (+0.67) | Good (+0.67) | +0.00 |
| Growth | Good (+1.00) | Good (+1.00) | +0.00 |
| Management | Excellent (+1.50) | Good (+0.83) | -0.67 |
| Industry Structure | Neutral (+0.33) | Good (+1.33) | +1.00 |
| Customer Benefits | Good (+1.25) | Good (+1.25) | +0.00 |
| Competitive Advantage | Excellent (+1.50) | Excellent (+1.50) | +0.00 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Weak → Good. Now: Engineered, nuclear and valve segments are a narrow qualified field with effectively one other certified player, though the standard pump business (48% of order intake) faces a broader competitive set. | The long view had said: The project pump business is described by management itself as highly competitive with OEM/EPC customers holding negotiating power, not a stable oligopoly with a price-taking end customer.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Pumps and valves is an unglamorous niche, but KSB is actively covered and courted by institutional investors (ICICI, LIC MF, Sundaram, B&K) and the nuclear angle attracts attention rather than deflecting it. | The long view had said: KSB is a widely tracked listed MNC subsidiary holding regular institutional investor meets with ICICI, B&K, 361 and Monarch, so it is well covered rather than under the radar.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: Management never discusses realisation or price increases; the only mix signal is the rise of higher-margin valves (18%) and SupremeServ (15%) in order intake, with growth otherwise described purely as order volumes. | The long view had said: Growth is volume and new-segment led rather than price/mix — EBITDA has been anchored at 13-14% for years, management explicitly refused to guide margins higher, and raw-material increases could not be fully passed on.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Entry is gated by long certification cycles (ISO 19443, FM/UL, MNRE, China SAMR/TSG, MBK 3-star) and no discounting war is described, but cost-saving and contract-manufacturing of low-end products signal price pressure at the commodity end. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Barriers are high and pricing rational in nuclear, energy and certified firefighting products, but the standard-pump and project segments face open competition and margin-capping price pressure, leaving the picture mixed.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: A captive aftermarket on its own installed base — SupremeServ spares and service is ~15% of order intake served through 350+ service centres — reinforced by requalification switching costs in nuclear and process applications. | The long view had said: Approval-based lock-in creates real switching costs — type-testing for marine, UL/FM listing, TSG certification for China, customer approval of KSB mechanical seals, plus an installed base served by 350+ service centres — but only in the qualified segments.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: A near-monopoly in Indian nuclear main coolant pumps — sole pump company with ISO 19443 and supplier to essentially every PHWR from Narora onward including all four 700MW units. | The long view had said: A genuine mini-monopoly exists in nuclear primary coolant pumps (only Indian manufacturer, sole ISO 19443 holder, ex-NPCIL director on the board) and in the captive SupremeServ spares/service franchise, though it does not extend to the broader pump business.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Neutral. Now: Communication is transparent on numbers and one-off items but uniformly celebratory in tone, with no discussion of misses, competitive losses or execution problems across the four meets. | The long view had said: Management repeatedly owns shortfalls plainly — attributing the modest 7% top line to nuclear slippage, admitting FGD aftermarket is not high margin, and flatly refusing to endorse the analyst's margin-expansion thesis.
- **Distribution to shareholders** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: Distribution is a steady, modestly rising dividend (200% raised to 220%) with a large idle net cash pile of ~Rs 283 Cr and no buyback programme discussed. | The long view had said: A steady, rising dividend policy (200% for 2024, 220% for 2025) alongside a persistent net-cash position, but no buyback programme or explicit valuation-linked distribution discipline is discussed.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. Now: Capacity additions are growth-oriented — Shirwal area expansion with newly commissioned machines, valves capacity enhancement, in-house solar controller manufacturing and a new central warehouse — alongside 23-24% ROCE, though absolute capex numbers were never quantified. | The long view had said: Sustained multi-year growth capex — additional land at Shirwal, new sheds at Sinnar, multi-axis machine additions, new admin block, solar captive project — funded from internal cash while ROCE stayed ~23-24% and revenue compounded 17-20%.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Evidence of share capture in adjacent niches rather than a dominant position — No.1 in domestic GGC valves, first supercritical order, first data-centre firefighting order, new municipal breakthroughs and added valve dealers. | The long view had said: Small base in several segments with explicit share capture — firefighting 68% CAGR, water/wastewater 30% CAGR, solar order intake 112% CAGR, marine and building services newly entered — and management states it is gaining share where it was previously absent.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Systematic people investment shown by a Great Place to Work Top-50 large manufacturing ranking, a dedicated HR VP on the senior team, and plant/segment heads groomed to present to investors. | The long view had said: Systematic internal development — SupremeServ Excellence Centre Academy, an idea-management scheme with 927 ideas from 416 employees, ITI skill labs — plus visible internal promotion (Bhave from Company Secretary to CFO) and Great Place to Work Top 50 recognition.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Much of the result rests on inherited advantages — parent technology transfer, the 1970s DAE nuclear agreement and government tender schemes — so strong ROCE and CAGRs cannot be attributed to current management skill alone. | The long view had said: Management deflates its own headline numbers rather than claiming credit, attributing high segment CAGRs to a low base and warning against quarter-to-quarter extrapolation, which makes the read on skill more reliable.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. Now: Development spend consistently converts into new products and qualifications — DC motor and solar controller, Ecoline steam traps and low-emission valves, B Pump/Sewatec vertical turbine pumps, FM/UL firefighting certification and localisation of the boiler recirculation pump. | The long view had said: KSB Tech captive engineering centre plus a steady stream of commercialised new products (UL/FM firefighting pumps, Sewatec/VT pumps, MFMO, IE5 motors, in-house solar controller, mechanical seals, marine and green-hydrogen pumps), with management attributing the majority of growth to them.

*Unchanged: Geographical expansion; Merger and acquisition; Distribution advantage; Share donators; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Cost of working capital; Technology advantage; Assurance benefits; Intangible benefits; Convenience benefits; Profit margin; Asset turn; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Disciplined stewards; Out of limelight; Cyclical market growth; Return on capital (headline); Structural end-market growth.*

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these — identical by construction): Persistence of growth (Good); Advertisement and promotion (Good).*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 5. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 2 weakened, 7 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: None of the four calls in the window discuss price increases, elasticity, premiumisation or realisation; margins are shown only in aggregate (EBITDA ₹350 Cr → ₹387 Cr) with no pricing attribution. (The long view had said: Pricing ability is conditional and partial: standard products (~50% of sales) and PVC-protected nuclear contracts allow pass-through, but in 2022 management conceded it 'could not pass everything' and offset inflation internally, and EBITDA margin has been range-bound at 13–14% for years with no premiumisation or waiting-list language.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Genuine heritage is presented (KSB since 1871, KSB India since 1960, global 37 production sites, 'Made by KSB' quality audit rating) and the company opens its meets by explaining 'what is KSB as a brand', but there is no A&P spend, premium-pricing or market-share-plus-premium evidence in these calls. (The long view had said: KSB carries real heritage (Indian operations since 1960, German parent, group CTO and CFO on the board) and invests in brand promotion, exhibitions, dealer meets and a 'Great Place to Work' Top-50 manufacturer listing, which helps it win OEM and municipal approvals. But there is no disclosed A&P spend, no premium-pricing evidence, and it is an industrial B2B brand rather than a consumer franchise stretched into adjacencies.)

**That weakened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Named new products with revenue impact recur every call — Sewatec vertical turbine and B Pump, Ecoline steam traps, low-emission valves, compact ball valve, solar DC motor and in-house controller, LUV boiler recirculation pump localisation, BP&CL reciprocating-pump technology — but R&D spend is never disclosed or discussed as a share of sales or of industry R&D. (The long view had said: Every call names new products with revenue impact — Aqua Series (2022), mechanical seals, solar pumps and in-house solar controller, Sewatec/Vertical Turbine, MFMO, UL/FM Etanorm, IE5 motors, Etaseco train pumps (~400 sold), green hydrogen and marine pumps — supported by KSB Tech, the group's Pune R&D/engineering centre, and management explicitly attributes the majority of recent growth to these launches while flagging that development costs sit in current margins.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Some explicit share/position evidence — first position in GGC domestic valves, export share of order intake rising to a record 17% with 22% CAGR, non-nuclear order intake compounding 16% — but no year-by-year market-share numbers or share-versus-named-competitor tracking in these calls. (The long view had said: Across the series KSB compounds revenue at 17–20% versus a general-industry market management itself pegs at ~8% CAGR, with segment CAGRs of 68% (firefighting), 30% (water/wastewater), 112% (solar order intake) and 22% (exports, now 17% of order intake including US wins), plus explicit share-gain language in building services and marine. Growth is order-book-backed (₹2,585 crore on hand) rather than bought with margin, which has held at 13–14% throughout.)

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Cost to Replicate (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Management presents a top-10 customer slide each meeting and names the same handful of OEM/EPC counterparties repeatedly (L&T, Thermax, ISGEC, Megha, Veolia, Wabag, Voith), and the ~₹12,800 mn nuclear order book rests on a single state counterparty. Exposure is bounded by an unusually wide distribution base. (The long view had said: A single counterparty, NPCIL, accounts for roughly half the order book and its readiness — not KSB's — determines revenue recognition; the Mar 2026 top-line shortfall was attributed entirely to nuclear slippage, and analysts have questioned the NPCIL relationship and its ordering cadence in every call since Aug 2022 ('after four-five years we are getting the second order').)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Nearly half of order intake is standard pumps sold through a dealer channel, a segment where cheaper local substitutes compete on price; management's own response is cost reduction, standardisation and outsourcing the cheap end rather than premium defence. The higher-spec lines are better insulated by certification barriers. (The long view had said: About half of order intake is standard/domestic and solar pumps sold through an 800-1,100 dealer network and into price-driven government tenders, where dealers largely control the end-customer's choice and management repeatedly frames actions as cost/competitiveness moves (localising solar controllers, local value addition, holding rather than cutting prices); the engineered, nuclear and UL/FM-certified niches are genuinely protected by qualification barriers.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (NO SIGNAL); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL); Fashion Risk (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Cyclicality (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
