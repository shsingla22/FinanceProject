**Checks that improved:**

- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Weak → Good. Now: Six consecutive quarters of double-digit-to-mid-single-digit LTL against an explicitly sluggish apparel market, plus a return to significant double-digit growth in small towns, indicates real share capture with a long store runway remaining. | The long view had said: Full-year revenue grew only 7% and Lifestyle 8%, which analysts repeatedly flagged as 500-600bps below mid-premium peers such as Arvind Fashions, indicating share donation rather than capture despite a large store base.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Weak → Good. Now: The power-brand portfolio occupies a strong position in the Indian consumer's mind for premium western/formal wear, with all brands growing in similar high-single-digit fashion, though this is mindshare rather than a true monopoly. | The long view had said: No monopoly-like position — the brands compete directly across every channel, with management noting peers growing faster and innerwear facing Jockey, Dollar and Lux, and no aftermarket or services annuity exists.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Good → Excellent. Now: A 3,348-store network across ~800 cities and 4.9m sq ft, plus deep department-store and MBO trade relationships, is a reach very few Indian apparel rivals can replicate, and management explicitly frames it as the strategic foundation. | The long view had said: Distribution reach is genuinely deep and management calls it the foundational pillar (3,348 stores, ~800 towns, 4.9m sq ft, plus trade, department store and e-commerce), though rivals like Arvind and value formats have comparable reach and are growing faster.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Weak players are visibly exiting or ceding ground — Forever 21 shut down entirely, and the unorganised small-town trade is where ABLBL is adding 550+ stores and posting significant double-digit growth. | The long view had said: Some weak players are exiting (Forever 21 shut, distressed franchise networks taken over, Centro transition) giving space to absorb, but the company is currently growing slower than its stronger listed peers rather than feeding on them.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: Accessibility is a genuine strength — 3,348 stores across nearly 800 cities plus department stores, MBOs and e-commerce — and management treats reach as a foundational strategic pillar. | The long view had said: Accessibility is the company's clearest strength — 3,348 stores and 4.9m sq ft across ~800 towns, all channels integrated, plus privileged access to new mall real estate through developer partnerships like Phoenix.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Management describes a persistently soft discretionary cycle through the year — sluggish consumption, GST transition disruption, softer wedding calendar, geopolitical volatility — but identifies no specific upturn catalyst ahead. | The long view had said: The company is coming off a sluggish apparel-consumption trough with visible turn signals — e-commerce and wholesale corrections completed, innerwear recovering after several subdued quarters, and double-digit growth returning in H2FY26.
- **Distribution to shareholders** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: A token maiden dividend of 50 paisa per share was declared in the first year post-demerger with no buyback and no articulated distribution policy, cash instead being directed to stores and debt reduction. | The long view had said: A coherent, disciplined policy was articulated and initiated in year one post-demerger (50 paise dividend, 15-25% of net profit payout), though there are no buybacks and the company still carries INR726cr net debt.

*Unchanged: Intangible benefits; Cost of working capital; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Merger and acquisition; Barriers to entry and rationality; Asset turn; Geographical expansion; Profit margin; Technology advantage; Advertisement and promotion; Network effects; Candor; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Capex for growth (not maintenance); Pricing, Mix and Volume; Halo effect (caution); Return on capital (headline); Structural end-market growth; Disciplined stewards; Independent, long-term and tenacious; People matter (talent development); Assurance benefits; Security by obscurity; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Out of limelight.*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
