**Checks that regressed:**

- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Good → Weak. Now: The company is anything but under-the-radar — quarterly calls hosted by JM, DAM Capital and Ambit with ICICI and IIFL analysts, and J.P. Morgan writing a $75 million PTC cheque. | The long view had said: Management explicitly frames the segment as under-served and under-competed because informal borrowers lack documentary credit history that mainstream lenders require.

Now: Switching costs on the borrower are weak — the entire credit problem arose because customers freely stacked microfinance, personal loans and credit cards on top of a Five-Star loan after disbursement. | The long view had said: Real switching costs from exclusive (non-paripassu) property charge on 7-year loans to borrowers with no formal credit documentation, making refinancing elsewhere difficult.

Now: Five-Star occupies a distinctive niche in small-ticket secured self-employed lending but shows no monopoly-like hold — management itself flags new competitors entering and bidding for its people. | The long view had said: Occupies a priority position in the borrower's repayment hierarchy — during stress, customers repaid Five-Star ahead of other lenders — a mind-share position backed by property collateral.

Now: The segment is fragmented and contested rather than a stable oligopoly, with microfinance, personal loan and credit card lenders all competing for the same borrower and new entrants arriving. | The long view had said: The small-ticket secured lending market is fragmented rather than a stable oligopoly, and competitive intensity is rising to the point of bidding up employee compensation.

Now: The promoter-chairman has run the model 22 years, explicitly framed the fixes as long-term rather than short-term, and refused cosmetic write-offs that would have flattered near-term optics. | The long view had said: 22+ years in one niche, refusal to take cosmetic technical write-offs or ARC sales to flatter NPAs, and explicit refusal to give growth guidance until collections are genuinely fixed.

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Genuine transparency and long-termism — management openly admitted slipping standards, refused cosmetic technical write-offs to flatter Stage-3, kept investing in branches and people through the stress, and the promoter has run the business 22 years — but delivery against its own promises failed: the 25% AUM growth and 1.20-1.25% credit-cost guidance reaffirmed in Aug 2025 ended with AUM growing ~4% sequentially and credit cost at 1.85-1.88%, alongside the CEO's exit. (The long view had said: Across 14 calls management disclosed bad news early and voluntarily (the Sept-2024 bureau scrub revealing 14% overleveraged borrowers before the sector stress hit; slowing growth against its own guidance; admitting 'behavioural crisis' in Q3FY26), refused the cosmetic fixes peers used (no ARC sales, no large technical write-offs, credit cost shown gross of recoveries), and then delivered the promised recovery in Q1FY27 with record disbursements and improving collections — with a 20+ year promoter-led team through five crises.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The economics are the opposite of low-price-plus: lending yields of ~22.5%, cost of funds of 8.8-9.3% (borrowed, not cheap deposits), and an opex-to-assets ratio that has risen from ~5% to ~6% with management explicitly declining to promise operating leverage this year because it must pay up on employee compensation. (The long view had said: The bank-variant traits are only half present: funding is cheap and getting cheaper for the segment (incremental debt at 8.19% in Q3FY26, cost of funds 9.63%→9.12%→8.80%, AA- rating, ADB $100mn line) and they passed 200bps of that to borrowers in Nov 2024, but this is a high-yield 22-24% lender whose cost-to-assets has risen from ~5% to ~6% on collections hiring, so there is no low-cost, low-margin, high-turns culture.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Over the window the company deliberately shrank — disbursements fell from Rs.1,290cr (Q1 FY26) to Rs.1,196cr to Rs.976cr as it tightened underwriting — and management notes new competitors entering; the Q1 FY27 record disbursement is a recovery to prior levels, and no market-share figures are cited anywhere. (The long view had said: AUM compounded from INR 6,242 crores (Dec 2022) to INR 13,722 crores (Jun 2026) with 31-32% year-on-year growth in the early years in a segment they describe as under-served, and Q1FY27 was a record disbursement quarter — but management never cites market-share figures, and they voluntarily cut growth guidance to 25% in FY25 and slowed further through FY26 to protect asset quality, so share gain is not a stated or persistent objective.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
