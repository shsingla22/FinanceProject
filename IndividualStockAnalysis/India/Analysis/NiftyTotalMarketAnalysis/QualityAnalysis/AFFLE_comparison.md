### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Management never names a new-format competitor defensively, but the repeated emphasis on 'ring-fencing our technology moat' with patents, and on competing 'ahead of several large global tech companies' for share, points to structural pressure from walled gardens and AI-native self-service buying platforms in a low-barrier category. There is no evidence of discounting as a competitive response — CPCU rates rose and margins expanded, so the exposure is real but not yet visible in economics. (The long view had said: Walled gardens with AI automation tools and GenAI-native entrants are structurally different competitors that management concedes are gaining ('the automation tools that the walled gardens are providing... that's definitely helping them'), and analysts have twice pressed whether the multi-quarter gross-margin decline is a competitive response — inventory and data cost drifted from ~61.6% in Q1 FY25 to 63.3% in Q4 FY26. Management denies price competition, but the margin trend is the disputed evidence.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: The calls affirmatively show pricing holding rather than eroding: CPCU rate rose steadily from INR 57.1 (Q2 FY25) to 58.0, 59.6 and 60.0, alongside seven consecutive quarters of EBITDA margin expansion to 22.5% for FY26 — the opposite of the Nobel Biocare pattern. Protection comes from measurable, attributable ROI outcomes and enforceable IP rather than brand awareness, though inventory and data cost at ~61% of revenue means the value-add sits in a relatively thin measurable layer. (The long view had said: Programmatic ad buying has many cheaper substitutes and management is repeatedly put on the defensive about its price premium — analysts asked in both Aug 2024 and May 2026 whether falling gross margins reflect cheaper competition, and roughly a quarter to a third of revenue flows through agencies, an intermediary that controls the platform choice. The counterweight is that ROI here is directly measured by the advertiser, so 'good enough' is harder to disguise than in the Nobel Biocare case.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Across all four calls growth is described as broad-based with no named customer, licensor or supplier driving results, and management repeatedly cites 'new logo additions' and 'steady new account additions' in developed markets alongside a stated diversified mix. No analyst pressed on a single relationship's renewal. (The long view had said: Customer concentration is genuinely low (direct advertisers 70-75%, broad-based across E/F/G/H verticals, no named account driving results), but supply-side concentration is real and unmanaged: inventory and data cost is 62-63% of revenue and is sourced through gatekeepers — Google, Apple, app stores and OEMs (TCL, Oppo, Honor) — whose unilateral policy decisions on ad formats, cookies and device privacy were discussed in multiple calls. Agency channel adds a further intermediary layer that visibly stretched collection days in FY26.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Ad tech is a high-innovation industry where platform shifts (iOS privacy/SKAN, agentic AI, self-service buying) can reset economics, and management itself frames FY2026 as AI 'fundamentally reshap[ing] how brands engage and convert consumers' — the same force that could disintermediate a mediation/DSP layer. Affle's response is continuous small-scale innovation (Niko, OpticksAI, 18 granted patents, ~300 enforceable claims) rather than defensive, but its dependence on the iOS/Android ecosystems' rules remains outside its control. (The long view had said: Adtech is exactly the high-innovation industry the framework warns about, and the calls show repeated platform-level resets imposed from outside: Google's cookie-deprecation reversal and privacy sandbox delay, Apple's iOS ATT changes, Google restricting in-app pop-up ad formats (Aug 2022), and by May 2026 analysts asking directly about GenAI natives and walled gardens rewriting campaign management and targeting. Management's own framing of agentic AI ('this will be an evolving trend over the next 5 years') concedes the ground rules are still moving.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
