**Checks that improved:**

- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹53 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹53 is operating profit. - **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Entry is visibly heavy — traditional brokers, PE-backed platforms and all-time-high private equity interest — with competition for RMs and clients intensifying, even if a full-stack platform is expensive and slow to replicate. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±11.3 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Barriers are low and behaviour is irrational at the margin — new entrants are flooding in and bidding for RMs with equity promises management itself calls stratospheric, while PE interest in the sector is at an all-time high.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Good → Weak. Now: No monopoly-like hold on the client's mind — management itself describes rising competitive intensity from brokers and PE-backed platforms spreading down into its HNI and affluent core. | The long view had said: Custody and clearing is a licence-restricted niche where Nuvama is one of only three non-bank holders and claims a dominant position in its chosen FPI/HFT segment, though this is only one segment of the group.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Post-listing focus has been explicitly organic with no large acquisitions, disciplined lending (ROE fixed before book expansion, ECL taken upfront) and a deliberate pause on RM hiring to fix productivity — though openness to M&A and leverage-funded lending temper the score. | The long view had said: Consistently organic growth with deliberate capital discipline — shrinking the loan book to fix ROE before regrowing it, and explicitly refusing to overpay for RM capacity when competitors were bidding up talent.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Offshore platform build-out is being deliberately dialed up and Asset Services is bringing overseas HFT clients into India, but execution is still early and unproven in results. | The long view had said: Offshore expansion was promised and delivered on schedule — DIFC live in FY25 with breakeven guided at 6-9 months and achieved, Singapore following, plus GIFT City and new domestic locations in Jaipur, Surat and Kanpur.

Now: Results are strong (45% profit CAGR over 3 years) but materially market-beta dependent — flows, capital markets and lending all ride the cycle — so skill cannot be cleanly separated from a favourable Indian market; segment-level disclosure at least allows the reader to try. | The long view had said: Much of the FY24-FY25 profit surge coincided with a bull market, but FY26 provides a partial separation of skill from beta: profits still grew despite flat indices, yield compression and the loss of a large client.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 5. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 1 weakened, 7 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls management frames the business around an annuity/ARR stream from managed products (AIF/PMS/MF) that compounds with the asset base — MPIS moved from ~54-55% of wealth revenue in Q1 to ~60% by Q3 and grew 38% for FY26 with assets +32% and net flows ~30% of opening assets, and Nuvama Private is explicitly run for 'ARR'. Trail-based fees on a growing installed asset base, not one-off broking, is now the core engine (broking is 'less than 10-11% of overall revenues'). (The long view had said: Across all calls management steers the mix toward annuity/ARR revenue — trail-based AIF commissions replacing upfront, MPIS at 60% of Nuvama Wealth revenue by FY26, ARR at 60% of Private revenue on ~INR54,000 crores of assets at a disclosed ~90bps blended retention, plus custody/asset-services float that they explicitly call recurring and sticky.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Nuvama is a young post-demerger identity — management describes still 'establishing ourselves as an independent company with our own governance framework, brand, rating' — so there is no heritage story or A&P-led consumer brand; what exists is institutional credibility that attracts RMs and product manufacturers, plus new sub-brands like the Virasat estate-planning service. (The long view had said: The Nuvama name only dates to the 2021 PAG carve-out from Edelweiss, so there is no heritage story, but the calls show brand-building into adjacencies under one name — 'One Nuvama', Infinity advisory, Virasat estate planning, NUWAI — and management argues a full-stack platform brand is what attracts both clients and RMs.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: the only computable check points this way but the calls offer no evidence — numbers alone are only ever a screening hint, never a confirmed fit. (The long view had said: No price increases are ever discussed; instead yields drift down and are defended rather than raised — Private ARR retention guided to 90-95bps with quarters at 0.85%, asset services hit by 50bps yield compression, SEBI capping upfront AIF commissions, and management flagging rising competitive intensity from PE-backed platforms moving into HNI and affluent segments.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Growth is well above the segment's underlying market — MPIS assets +30% year-on-year, revenue +48% for nine months and +38% for FY26, client assets +19% in Q1 to ~INR4.6 lakh crore, wealth profits +23% — and management explicitly targets higher share in asset services. But no explicit market-share percentages versus named competitors are given, and management concedes competitive intensity is rising from traditional brokers and PE-backed platforms in HNI and affluent segments. (The long view had said: Share numbers rise repeatedly and are quantified across the five years — institutional equities 5.4% (Nov 2023) to 6.2%+ (Feb 2025), investment banking 18% to 19% of value in FY26, 22% of incremental AIF/PMS registrations in asset services, and wealth's share of group revenue from 50% to 57% — with growth funded organically alongside a 32% ROE rather than bought.)

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Economics are shaped by SEBI and RBI rather than by government contracts: management flags short-term strain from new SEBI announcements, RBI ECL provisioning norms directly suppressed reported NII in two quarters, RBI's capital-markets exposure rules for banks/NBFCs are watched closely, and the asset-management expansion into SIF is gated on a pending final mutual fund licence approval. (The long view had said: Regulator action repeatedly and materially reset economics: the May-23 SEBI AIF commission circular cut reported wealth revenue growth to 16% from a like-for-like ~30%, SEBI F&O rules hit broking revenue and institutional equities volumes, RBI ECL norms suppressed NII, and the entire asset-management growth plan hinges on a pending SEBI mutual fund licence for SIF; management even caveats asset-services guidance as 'subject to regulatory situation remaining the way it is'.)
- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Revenues remain tied to capital-market activity: Q1 FY26 saw IPO/QIP issuance dry up, and core capital markets (institutional equities + investment banking) was the one segment declining through H1 and 9M FY26, with Q4 hit by West Asia volatility changing ESOP-loan behaviour and NII recognition. The deliberate shift to annuity managed products materially dampens, but does not remove, the market-cycle beta. (The long view had said: Revenue is levered to market levels and activity — group revenue growth swung from ~45% (9M FY25) to ~8% (9M FY26), with F&O rule-driven volume falls, ADT down 6-14%, QIP/ECM billing slipping a quarter and client assets marked to market; but the damage was confined to the capital-markets segment while wealth (57% of revenue) grew 18-23% and ARR/annuity assets kept compounding.)

**That worsened:**

- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Management repeatedly and defensively flags structurally different entrants: traditional brokers moving into wealth, and private-equity-backed platforms aggressively building RM teams, with competition spreading from ultra-HNI down into the HNI and affluent segments where Nuvama Wealth operates. By Q4 FY26 they concede consolidation and a 'long tail' are coming, and PE interest is at an all-time high. (The long view had said: Management devoted successive 2026 calls to structurally different entrants — PE-backed wealth platforms aggressively hiring, competition spreading from ultra-HNI down into HNI and affluent, and new players offering RMs 'stratospheric' valuations — plus SIF as a new-format vehicle taking flows from their AIF public-market strategies; PE interest in the industry is described as all-time high.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
