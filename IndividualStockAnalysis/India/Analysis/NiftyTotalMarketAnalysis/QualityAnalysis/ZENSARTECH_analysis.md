# Zensar Technolgies Ltd. (ZENSARTECH) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: IT*

Zensar Technologies is an Indian IT services company, part of the RPG group and headquartered in Pune, that builds, modernises and runs software and technology systems for large corporate clients. It earns money by billing clients for the work of its roughly 10,000-plus engineers and consultants — through project contracts, ongoing application and infrastructure support, and renewals — with revenue reported in US dollars and running at about $643.7 million for FY26 (roughly $158-160 million a quarter). Its customers are mainly banks and financial firms, healthcare and life sciences companies, manufacturers and consumer businesses, and telecom/media/technology firms, and its main markets are the US (the largest), Europe and South Africa, delivered largely from offshore India plus centres such as a new one in Belgrade, Serbia. The calls describe the company reinventing itself as an 'AI-native' services firm — 85% of staff AI-certified by FY26, its own agentic AI platform called ZenseAI, and about 20% of new order booking described as AI-influenced.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Banking and Financial Services (BFSI)** — Technology work for banks, financial firms and insurers; the strongest-growing vertical in the later calls, up 11.4% for full-year FY26, and the source of large AI/mainframe-modernisation engagements.
- **Healthcare and Life Sciences** — The newest industry vertical (management called it 'our newest baby' in FY24), serving healthcare and life-sciences clients and growing 8% in FY26.
- **Manufacturing and Consumer Services** — IT services for manufacturers and consumer-facing businesses; a large but soft vertical, down 0.6% in FY26 and hit earlier by clients cutting discretionary spend.
- **Hi-Tech / Telecom, Media and Technology (TMT)** — Work for technology, telecom and media clients; repeatedly named the weakest area, down 9.7% in FY26 and singled out by the CEO as the persistent drag on growth.
- **Cloud Infrastructure and Security services (earlier called Foundation Services)** — Running and securing clients' cloud and IT infrastructure; one of the faster-growing service lines, up 13% year-on-year in Q4 FY26.
- **Data Engineering and Analytics** — Building clients' data platforms and analytics — management says client conversations have shifted towards 'AI-ready data foundations' — and it grew 8-13% in the recent quarters.
- **Enterprise Application Services** — Implementing and supporting business software packages and SaaS applications; a large but slow-moving line, roughly flat to slightly down in FY26.
- **Products and Platforms, including CMO services** — Software products, platforms and marketing-technology-type services for clients, growing a modest 3-6% in the recent quarters.
- **Advanced Engineering Services and Experience Services** — Product/software engineering and digital customer-experience design, which together with the other lines make up the 'Experience to Engineering to Engagement' pitch; the service lines as a group grew from about 35% of revenue in FY23 to 71.6% by Q4 FY26.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Zensar's strongest asset is the way it is run, and the record is consistent on this from three directions at once. The quality framework gives its top marks to management: candour (margin bridges explained basis point by basis point, one-offs volunteered, and management openly budgeting for continued decline in its largest technology-and-media client), disciplined stewardship (four years of purely organic focus, no acquisitions, $319.5M of net cash and no borrowings, and repeated refusal to buy revenue at the expense of profitability), a low-key substance-first tone including an explicit refusal to inflate AI metrics the way peers inflated 'digital', and real investment in people (98.6% learning coverage, an Ignite AI Academy reaching 85% of staff, five straight quarters of attrition below 10%, and a smooth internal CFO handover from Zute to Bhandari). The multibagger screen picks up the same thing as its single STRONG FIT: Corporate Culture, evidenced by a mid-teens margin promised in January 2023 and actually delivered and held (17.8% in FY24, 17.4% in the December 2025 quarter, up from 11.3% in the December 2022 quarter), attrition falling from 22.8% to 9.5%, customer satisfaction moving from 53 to top-three in the industry, and dividends rising from 450% to 750% of face value. Today the business earns ₹16 of operating profit on every ₹100 of sales, with margins moving only about ±2.4 points a year, and turns ₹100 of capital into ₹16 of operating cash. The risk review calls the overall resilience SOUND.

The problem is that all of this good behaviour sits on top of a business with no structural protection, and the same cost discipline that produced the margin is also why it produced so little growth. On competitive advantage and industry structure the framework scores the company at the bottom of its range: no monopoly-like position anywhere (Zensar is displaced from accounts during vendor consolidation and is one of many bidders on every deal), no network effects (management itself describes the model it inherited as a slightly more advanced form of staff augmentation), and a fragmented, worsening market in which Tier-1 vendors now bid for mid-size deals they previously ignored and clients hold the negotiating power. Six of the eleven multibagger patterns are outright NO FIT for exactly these reasons — Pricing Power (AI budgets are being repurposed out of existing work, and no call in five years cites a price increase taken without losing volume), Brand Strength and Market Share Gainers (revenue grew 1.0% in FY24 and 3.1% in FY26, against a stated FY24 aspiration to match industry growth in FY25 and lead it from FY26), Cost to Replicate (nothing explains why a funded newcomer could not copy the offering, and management conceded in January 2023 it had no settled 'right to win'), Toll Roads (this is discretionary IT budget clients defer and optimise away, not a small unnegotiated line item) and Recurring Revenue (project work with quarterly order-book renewals, no licence or maintenance stream, and 71-80 days of receivables rather than customer money paid upfront). The Low Price Plus pattern is the clearest illustration of the trade-off and only rates PARTIAL: the six levers, freshers and pyramid management, 83-84% utilisation, the shift of work offshore and terminated leases are a genuine, itemised cost culture, but the savings were kept as margin rather than spent on price to win volume. The framework's harshest single verdict says the same thing from the revenue side — growth is volume-led with adverse mix, because moving work offshore lowers the revenue earned per unit of effort while AI deflates pricing at renewal.

The risk review then shows how the same two traits reappear as the two ELEVATED threats. Because delivery is linear and headcount-based rather than differentiated, artificial intelligence attacks the core: the CFO says AI budget is being repurposed out of existing application development and testing engagements, squeezing renewals and pricing — which is the identical force behind the Pricing Power and Good Enough Goods conclusions. Because the client base was never broad enough, a single Hi-Tech/TMT customer has driven that vertical backwards for eleven to twelve consecutive quarters, and management has now budgeted for further decline as the client brings work in-house; the fact that we know this in such detail is itself the candour the framework rewarded with a top score. Four further risks — cyclicality (demand swings with discretionary IT budgets, furloughs and deferred spending), new entrants (a structural reset in who competes rather than a new format), shifting buyer preferences (away from on-site effort-based work toward cost optimisation, consolidation, offshore delivery and outcome-based contracts) and good-enough substitution — are all WATCH, and all trace back to the same undifferentiated position. Only government dependency and fashion risk are LOW, because the client base is entirely private commercial and demand is functional repeat enterprise work. The offsetting evidence is real but unproven: 85% of the workforce AI-certified, the ZenseAI agentic platform, Agentic Foundry and FinOps for AI, 20% of the order book described as AI-influenced, a $210mn mega deal won by displacing a Tier-1 incumbent, $206mn of other bookings, a $401.8mn order book, service lines at 71.6% of revenue, TMT cut from a high-teens to a high-single-digit share, 34 new logos in FY24 and 15-plus clients in the $10mn bracket. That is why Innovation Dominance rates only PARTIAL — named offerings with revenue attached, but no disclosed research spending behind them. The combined score reflects the split: 49 out of 100 on business quality, 25 on multibagger fit and 64 on risk safety, weighted to 46 overall — a Mixed, two-star verdict, meaning a well-run, cash-rich, honestly-managed company operating in a position that does not yet protect it.
