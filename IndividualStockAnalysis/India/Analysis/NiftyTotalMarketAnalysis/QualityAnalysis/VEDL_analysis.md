# Vedanta Ltd. (VEDL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: METALS*

Vedanta Limited is an Indian natural-resources company that digs up and processes raw materials — aluminium, zinc, silver, lead, crude oil and gas, iron ore, steel, copper, ferrochrome and nickel — and sells them mostly as metals, alloys and fuel to industrial buyers in India and abroad, with prices set by global commodity markets (LME for metals, crude benchmarks for oil). It makes money by mining or extracting at very low cost and selling at market prices, so profit is the gap between cost per ton/barrel and the prevailing commodity price; management repeatedly stresses being in the top quartile (aluminium) and top decile (zinc) of the global cost curve, and increasingly sells 'value-added' products like wire rod and billets rather than plain metal. Operations are mostly in India (Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Rajasthan, Karnataka, Goa, Punjab, Jharkhand, Andhra Pradesh) plus zinc mines in Africa (Gamsberg), an iron ore mine in Liberia and a copper rod plant in Fujairah. In FY26 it reported record revenue of about Rs. 1.74 lakh crore, EBITDA of about Rs. 56,000 crore and profit after tax of over Rs. 25,000 crore, and it has historically paid large dividends; the group also runs its own power plants and, effective 1 April 2026, is splitting itself into separately listed companies through an NCLT-approved demerger.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Aluminium** — Mines bauxite, refines it into alumina at Lanjigarh and smelts it into aluminium at Jharsuguda and BALCO — the biggest single business, producing 2.46 million tons of aluminium in FY26 at a hot metal cost of $1,752/ton, targeting a fully integrated 3.1 million ton capacity and management's stated potential of roughly $4 billion of EBITDA.
- **Zinc India (Hindustan Zinc / HZL)** — A listed subsidiary that mines and refines zinc, lead and silver in Rajasthan — record 1.1 million tons of mined metal and 627 tons of silver in FY26 at a five-year-low cost of $959/ton, with management pointing to a 1.2 million ton metal target and about $2.7 billion of annual EBITDA.
- **Zinc International** — Zinc mining outside India, centred on the Gamsberg mine, which management calls under-appreciated by investors; production rose 27% to 225,000 tons in FY26, with 662 million tons of ore reserves and resources and an ambition to eventually reach 1 million tons of zinc, roughly mirroring Zinc India.
- **Oil & Gas (Cairn Oil & Gas)** — India's largest private-sector oil producer, pumping crude and gas mainly from the Rajasthan Mangala-Bhagyam-Aishwarya fields (about 85,000 barrels of oil equivalent a day gross in Q3 FY26), using techniques like polymer and ASP injection and infill wells to slow natural field decline, with reserves and resources of about 1.4 billion barrels and EBITDA potential of over $1 billion.
- **Iron Ore** — Mines and sells iron ore from Karnataka and Goa (6.2 million tons in FY26) plus a Liberia mine with over 3,000 million tons of reserves and resources that management calls the next big growth opportunity, targeting around 30 million tons a year there.
- **Steel (ESL, Bokaro)** — A fully integrated steel plant making billets, TMT bars and wire rod (1.3 million tons produced in FY26), described as a 'trophy asset' on 2,300 contiguous acres that is being expanded to 3.5 million tons — worth about $500 million of bottom line at that size — and could in theory scale to 15 million tons at one site.
- **Pig Iron (Goa)** — A blast-furnace operation in Goa selling pig iron, at a record 895,000 tons in FY26 (up 10%), with a ductile iron (DI) plant under construction alongside it.
- **Power** — One of India's largest power producers with about 11 GW of capacity, roughly 5 GW of it merchant power sold to the grid (TSPL in Punjab, Athena in Chhattisgarh, Meenakshi in Andhra); sales grew 30% to 16.4 billion units in FY26, and it also supplies captive electricity that drives the aluminium smelters' costs.
- **Ferrochrome (FACOR)** — Mines chrome ore and produces ferrochrome, an alloy input for stainless steel — record 101,000 tons in FY26 with cost down 19% after restarting its own Kalrangiatta mine, with an ambition to become India's largest ferro alloys producer at 500,000 tons capacity.
- **Copper** — Refines and fabricates copper at Silvassa in India and Fujairah abroad, together making 282,000 tons of copper rod and a record 170,000 tons of cathode in FY26; it is one of the segments staying with the post-demerger Vedanta Limited.
- **Nickel** — A nickel-cobalt plant in Goa that started up in July 2022 — the smallest business, mentioned only in passing but listed as one of the segments remaining in the demerged Vedanta Limited.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Every check, area by area

**Capital Allocation — Good (+0.75)** · 4 of 6 checks had evidence

- **Cost of working capital — Excellent** *(from the financial statements)*: Cash comes back 16 days BEFORE suppliers need to be paid — customers effectively fund the business (negative working capital, a rare strength).
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance) — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: Sustained and rising growth capex ($3bn over 2 years in FY23, an $8bn programme announced in FY25, ₹14,918 cr delivered in FY26) funded from internal accruals with group ROCE of ~32%, though returns are commodity-price dependent rather than proven incremental ROIC.
  > "During the year, we deployed INR15,000 crores of growth capital in line with guidance... Our return on capital employed stood for the year at 32%" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Merger and acquisition — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: Bolt-ons have been complementary and value-accretive — FACOR production doubled since acquisition, Athena gave captive power security, and Incab was bought for downstream synergies — though Athena/Meenakshi power are sizeable rather than small.
  > "At FACOR ferro chrome, we have already doubled our production since acquisition" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Distribution to shareholders — Weak** *(from the conference calls)*: Distributions are large but structurally driven by the parent's debt-servicing need (brand fee plus dividend upstreaming to VRL), with no buybacks, and the FY26 policy change from rule-based to discretionary weakens the framework.
  > "So with 4% to 5% dividend and the routine brand fee, VRL can be managed" — *management, on an earnings call*
- *Not assessed (the evidence was silent — never guessed): Advertisement and promotion; Research and development.*

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

**Overall:** the company partly passes the foundation every multibagger shares (steady cash + high returns on capital + growth), 2 of 3 testable checks passing, and fits 1 of the 11 patterns: Cost to Replicate.

### Cost to Replicate — STRONG FIT
*"Could a newcomer copy this?"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** The one pattern with a concrete, articulable barrier: finite, permitted, world-scale orebodies and fully backward-integrated infrastructure that a funded newcomer cannot rebuild — 662 Mt zinc R&R at Zinc International, 3,000 Mt at Liberia, 1.4 bn barrels R&R, a 5 Mtpa single-location alumina refinery, captive bauxite and coal — which management frames as a >$50bn replacement cost.

> "most of our assets now cannot be replicated... the replacement cost is in excess of $50 billion" — *management, on an earnings call*

*"Sells once, earns forever"*

- ❌ Capital spending is about 27% of sales — growth is capital-hungry.
- ✅ Operating cash was positive in 12 of 12 years — the reliable cash engine recurring-revenue businesses show.

*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

> "Our aluminium and zinc operations consistently outperformed industry benchmarks, ranking in the top quartile and top decile respectively of the global cost curve" — *management, on an earnings call*

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Government Dependency, Fashion Risk. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 3 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across all four years the P&L is driven by LME/crude prices and input-commodity costs: FY23 opened with 'softening in commodity prices', COP above LME in aluminium and an analyst asking when production would be cut, while FY26 records were explicitly credited to 'improvement in metal prices' and favourable pricing/FX; EBITDA margin swung from 24% (Q1FY24) to 34% (Q1FY25) to 44% (Q4FY26), and the company runs dynamic commodity hedging precisely because of price volatility. Alongside this, capex has been counter-scaled up ($3bn over 2 years in FY23, ~$8bn programme, INR15,000cr in FY26) at what looks like a cycle peak.

> "We have started dynamic commodity hedging for proactive risk management amidst unprecedented price volatility" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Large capex was sustained through the FY23-24 downturn (countercyclical investment), cost is in the global top quartile/decile, and the portfolio spans zinc, aluminium, oil, power, steel rather than one commodity*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 4 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from -29% to 34% (swinging ±16.8 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 27% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 9% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Fortunes hinge repeatedly on government action: the July 2022 special additional excise duty on crude and the PSC/RSC framework, iron-ore export duty hitting steel EBITDA, coal linkage materialisation (22%→36%) and auction/linkage allocations, captive mine approvals (Jamkhani, Sijimali FC1, Kuraloi and Sijimali 'awaiting EC', ESL expansion gated on EC), NCLT approval of the demerger, merchant power tariffs, and critical-mineral block bidding. Management's own answer to a policy question was to frame the business as partnering with the state on energy security.

> "From July 1st '22 the government of India has started levying the special additional excise duty on crude oil... we are engaging with the government on this within the framework of PSC and RSC" — *management, on an earnings call*
