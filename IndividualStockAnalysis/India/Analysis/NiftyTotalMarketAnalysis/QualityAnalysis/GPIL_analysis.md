# Godawari Power (GPIL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Godawari Power & Ispat Limited (GPIL) is an Indian steel and iron-ore company based in Raipur, Chhattisgarh, that digs its own iron ore, converts it into iron-ore pellets, and then turns those pellets into sponge iron, steel billets and finished steel products such as structural rolled sections, wire rod, H.B. wire, MS rounds, ERW pipes and galvanized fabricated items. It sells iron-ore pellets to other steel makers in the domestic market and sells its finished and value-added steel products to industrial buyers and government-linked agencies such as Indian Railways and power distribution companies. It makes money mainly on the spread between cheap, self-mined ore and the price of pellets and steel — because it owns its Ari Dongri iron ore mine, its own captive power (waste-heat recovery, biomass and a large captive solar fleet), it keeps costs low, running EBITDA margins of roughly 20-30% and FY26 revenue producing INR 1,253 crores of EBITDA and INR 802 crores of PAT. Everything operates from plants in and around Siltara/Raipur in Chhattisgarh, India, and by FY26 the company was debt-light with a cash position of INR 837 crores, while expanding into cold-rolled steel and, newly, battery energy storage system (BESS) manufacturing.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Iron ore mining (Ari Dongri mine)** — The company's captive iron ore mine, which feeds its own pellet plants and is being expanded from 2.35 million to 6 million tons a year (environment clearance received Feb 2026, full-scale operation targeted FY28) — management calls it the core of its raw-material security and backward integration.
- **Iron ore pellets** — Its largest and most-discussed product line: high-grade pellets both sold to outside steel makers and used in-house, with capacity raised from 2.7 to 4.7 million tons after a 2-million-ton natural-gas grate-kiln plant was commissioned in December 2025; pellet realization was around INR 10,000-10,500 a ton.
- **Sponge iron (DRI)** — Pellets are converted into sponge iron, the intermediate feed for steel making; capacity was expanded from 0.59 to 0.65 million tons and production exceeded 100% of target in FY26.
- **Steel billets / steel melting shop** — Sponge iron is melted into steel billets, the semi-finished steel that feeds the rolling mills; billets hit 96% of targeted production in FY26.
- **Finished and value-added steel products** — Rolling mills turn billets into structural rolled steel, MS rounds, wire rod and H.B. wire (H.B. wire capacity raised to 0.115 million tons); a strip mill feeds ERW pipes, and this group has been the fastest-growing sales line, up 15% Y-o-Y in Q3 FY26.
- **Galvanized fabricated products** — Structural steel is fabricated and galvanized in-house for supply to Indian Railways, power utilities and discoms — previously the steel was bought from the market, so doing it captively is described as cost and value accretive; one of the few products whose realization held up in FY26.
- **Ferro alloys (Hira Ferro Alloys / Alok Ferro Alloys)** — Subsidiary plants making ferro alloys used in steel making, progressively bought up as part of group simplification (Alok Ferro Alloys stake raised to 88.34% direct, 97.62% including holdings via Hira Ferro Alloys); one of the few lines with firm realizations in FY26.
- **Captive power** — Self-generated electricity from waste-heat recovery (49 MW), biomass, a windmill and a large solar fleet being tripled from 165 MW to 540 MW, plus a 45 MWh battery storage unit — all consumed in-house to cut grid power cost and carbon, not sold.
- **CRM complex (upcoming)** — A 0.7-million-ton cold rolling mill complex under construction with equipment ordered and bank funding secured, targeted for commissioning by March 2027.
- **Integrated steel plant (upcoming)** — A board-approved 1-million-ton integrated plant for structural steel and wire rods, with land and environmental approval in place and construction expected to begin October 2026 — a scaled-down successor to the 2-million-ton plant discussed in earlier calls.
- **Battery energy storage systems (BESS) manufacturing (new venture)** — A brand-new, non-steel business: a 20-gigawatt BESS manufacturing facility costing INR 1,025 crores, with LFP cell and balance-of-system supply agreements signed, targeted to commission from March 2027.
- **Ardent Steel (divested)** — A merchant iron-ore pellet plant in which GPIL held 37.85%; the stake was sold for about INR 91 crores and completed in FY26 as part of simplifying the group structure.

### The story in depth

Godawari Power's best qualities are its people and its balance sheet. The same three executives have fronted every call from 2022 to 2026, they are low-key Raipur owner-managers who hand credit to staff rather than build a personal story, and they have shown real staying power — the mining expansion first flagged in 2022 only got its environment clearance and operating consent in February 2026, and they refused to overcommit spending until those approvals landed. Long-term debt has been fully repaid and the company has stayed in net cash right through the cycle (Rs 560cr in 2022, Rs 1,261cr in 2024, Rs 837cr in FY26). It scores well on capital allocation and on returns — every Rs 100 of capital in the business threw off Rs 19 of operating cash this year — and it shares the proceeds through a stated policy: a buyback of 21.5 lakh shares at Rs 1,400, a special 25% dividend, a bonus and a share split. The current spending is clearly for growth rather than upkeep: pellet capacity from 2.7 to 4.7 million tonnes commissioned in December 2025, mining from 2.35 to 6 million tonnes, a cold rolling mill, captive solar from 165MW to 540MW, and roughly Rs 2,000 crore planned in FY27, funded largely out of cash.

The engine underneath all of that is a single asset: the captive Ari Dongri iron ore lease, which delivers ore at about Rs 3,000 a tonne against NMDC's Rs 4,500-5,500. That one fact drives most of what the pattern screen picked up. It is the basis of the 'Cost to Replicate' partial fit, because the lease is a finite asset and its expansion to 6 million tonnes took from roughly 2021 to February 2026 to clear the regulators. It is also the basis of the 'Low Price Plus' partial fit, alongside a genuine and repeated cost obsession — captive solar, 49MW of waste-heat recovery, a Rs 75 crore efficiency project with a two-year payback and Rs 38 crore of annual savings. But that same cost-led identity is exactly why the screen found no full multibagger fit anywhere and scored the fit at only 10 out of 100. Cheap ore lowers the cost of a product the company cannot price. 'Pricing Power' is a clear no-fit: realizations softened across almost every product in FY26 and fell about 10% across the supply chain in Q1 FY27, with profit support coming from a safeguard duty and cost cutting rather than from raising prices. 'Toll Roads' is a no-fit for the mirror-image reason — pellet is the customer's dominant raw material cost, not a small unnoticed input, so its price reprices violently. And the review flags growth itself as a weakness, because it is almost entirely volume and capacity led while prices soften; the only price nuance is a modest premium of about Rs 1,000 a tonne for high-grade, low-alumina pellet. There is real process innovation — India's first natural-gas grate-kiln pellet plant, an IIT Bombay carbon-capture pilot — and steady forward integration into structural steel, wire, ERW pipes and galvanized products sold direct to Indian Railways and the electricity distribution companies, but R&D spend is never disclosed, no market share number appears in any of the 17 calls, and there is no brand or distribution story at all.

The risk review lands on the same handful of facts from the other side. Cyclicality is rated high risk because results are driven by prices management openly says it does not control — pellet realisation went from Rs 12,000-14,000 in April 2022 to Rs 7,775, back to Rs 11,000 in 2024 and Rs 8,500-10,000 in FY26 — and its one genuine defence is the balance sheet described above: captive ore, captive power and net cash let it keep building through the down-leg instead of pulling back. Government dependency is the other high risk, and it is the same event that cost the company its only mark-down on geographic reach: the May 2022 export duty took pellet exports from zero to 45% duty overnight, exports stopped entirely, management has ruled out returning, and the footprint is now a single state. Three further items sit on watch and all connect back to the strengths. New domestic pellet supply from Lloyds, NMDC's 2 million tonne plant and a nearby entrant tests whether the high-grade premium — the only pricing advantage the company has — actually holds. The slow shift away from coal-based sponge iron, which management concedes contributes 1.8 of its 2.4 tonnes of CO2 per tonne, cuts both ways: decarbonisation is the one demand shift moving in the company's favour, and it has pivoted toward it, but it also threatens the core process. And the unrelated 20GW battery venture is the one place where the otherwise disciplined record wobbles — it leans on single named suppliers, EVE Power of China and Shanghai Shenyi, and is planned on a thin 7-8% margin. The combined rating is Mixed, 38 out of 100 and two stars, built from a middling 53 on business quality, a weak 10 on multibagger fit and 44 on risk safety, with underlying resilience judged sound. In plain terms: a well-run, well-financed, low-cost commodity producer whose durability is real but whose upside is tied to prices it does not set.

**What the calls show:** Across all 17 calls results are driven by iron ore/pellet/sponge prices — pellet realisation swung from Rs 12,000-14,000 (Apr 2022) to Rs 7,775, back to Rs 11,000 in 2024 and ~Rs 8,500-10,000 in FY26 — and management concedes it has no control over revenue; the company is simultaneously running a very large multi-year capex programme (pellet 2.7→4.7 mt, mining 2.35→6 mt, 1 mt steel plant, CRM, 540 MW solar, 20 GWh BESS, ~INR 2,000 crore in FY27 alone) into that cycle.

> "we are sitting in a commodity market and at the moment everything said is demand and supply so it is difficult to ascertain how prices will unfold going forward so at least on the revenue side we do not have control" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Captive iron ore, captive power/solar and a net-cash balance sheet let it invest through the down-leg rather than retrench*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 3 of the last 11 years with swings of ±20 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 11% and 35% (±6.4 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 20% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 8% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Policy repeatedly resets the P&L: the May 2022 export duty (0→45% on pellets) instantly ended exports ('till the time the duty is not revoked we do not think so we will be able to export a single tonne'), NMDC administered prices and IBM-published royalty rates set costs, the FY26 safeguard duty lifted prices 5-10%, and the entire growth plan hinged on MoEF environmental clearance and CECB consent-to-operate, which delayed the 6 mt mine from 2024 guidance to February 2026.

> "government levied export duty on iron ore fine from 0% to 30%... In pellets there was from 0 to 45%... This has led to sharp drop in the domestic iron ore prices" — *management, on an earnings call*

## What to watch

- Pellet and sponge realizations from here — the Cyclicality high risk and the negative mark on 'Pricing, Mix and Volume'. New volume from the 4.7mt pellet plant and 6mt mine only becomes earnings if prices stop falling; a repeat of the FY23 slide to Rs 7,775 would swamp the volume gains.
- Whether the roughly Rs 1,000/tonne high-grade pellet premium survives new supply from Lloyds and NMDC's 2mt plant — this is the test of both the 'Cost to Replicate' pattern and the Good Enough Goods and New Entrants watch risks.
- Any policy change touching the export duty, the safeguard duty, NMDC administered prices or IBM royalty rates — the Government Dependency high risk, and the same lever that produced the negative score on geographical expansion.
- How much capital the 20GW battery venture actually absorbs and whether the EVE Power and Shanghai Shenyi single-supplier arrangements hold — the caveat inside the 'Disciplined stewards' check and the Stakeholder Concentration watch risk, made tighter by the planned 7-8% margin.
- Whether the Rs 837cr net cash position survives the roughly Rs 2,000cr FY27 capex without new long-term debt — net cash is the stated silver lining on Cyclicality and the reason the 'Capex for growth' check scored positively.
