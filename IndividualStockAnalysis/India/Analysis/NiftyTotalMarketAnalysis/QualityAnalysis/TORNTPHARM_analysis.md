# Torrent Pharmaceuticals Ltd. (TORNTPHARM) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: PHARMA*

Torrent Pharma is an Indian drug maker that sells finished medicines — mostly branded generics, meaning copies of off-patent drugs sold under Torrent's own brand names — to doctors, pharmacies, hospitals and government tender buyers. It earns money by manufacturing pills, capsules, injectables and consumer health products and selling them through large field sales forces (about 7,100 medical reps in India by FY26) that promote its brands to doctors, plus tender-based and contract supply in export markets. Its biggest therapy areas are chronic ones — cardiac, diabetes, gastro and central-nervous-system/psychiatry drugs — and branded markets make up roughly 70-76% of total revenue. India is by far the largest market (about half of sales), followed by Brazil, Germany and the US; quarterly revenue grew from about Rs 2,491 crore in Q4 FY23 to Rs 4,197 crore in Q4 FY26 after acquiring JB Pharma, with operating EBITDA margins around 32-33%.

**The parts of the business:**

- **India (branded generics)** — Sells prescription branded medicines to Indian doctors and chemists through ~7,100 reps, with cardiac as the largest contributor plus diabetes, gastro and CNS; it is the single biggest business at roughly Rs 1,257 crore of quarterly revenue in FY23 rising to Rs 2,215 crore in Q4 FY26, and management says Torrent is the 6th largest player in the Indian pharma market with 20 brands in the country's top 500.
- **Consumer Health / Curatio** — An over-the-counter and skin-care arm built around the acquired Curatio business and brands like Shelcal, sold through chemist outlets (75,000 covered by FY25) and supported by consumer advertising; it grew 27% in FY26 and is called out as a key growth lever, though it is smaller than the core prescription business.
- **Brazil** — Torrent's second branded-generics market, selling CNS and cardio-diabetes brands through its own ~295-strong sales force; quarterly sales rose from BRL 201 million in Q4 FY23 to BRL 259 million in Q4 FY26, with about 58-60 more molecules waiting on regulator (ANVISA) approval.
- **Germany** — A pure generics business that mainly wins supply contracts through insurer tenders plus a smaller OTC range, running around EUR 29-31 million a quarter; growth has been held back since FY26 by a third-party supplier's regulatory problems, and Torrent has just launched its first biosimilar there.
- **United States** — A deliberately small, niche generics business of roughly USD 32-38 million a quarter, historically hurt by price erosion and pending FDA plant inspections, now growing again on new launches including oncology products.
- **Contract manufacturing (insulin CMO)** — Makes insulin for another company on a service basis at roughly Rs 75-80 crore of revenue per quarter — small in sales terms but management says almost all of it drops through to profit.
- **JB Pharma (acquired)** — A separately listed Indian pharma company in which Torrent bought a controlling ~48.8% stake in January 2026 and is merging in; it is consolidated from that date, added about a quarter of group revenue in Q4 FY26, brings an international CDMO (contract development and manufacturing) business, and is expected to yield Rs 400-450 crore of cost synergies over three years.
- **R&D and new launches** — Develops incremental-innovation and first-to-market products, increasingly aimed at India — including generic semaglutide (launched as Semalix and Sembolic with 38% share among generic players), generic Brexpiprazole, and a Phase 3 trial for the NASH drug Resmetirom — funded largely by reallocating existing R&D spend rather than raising it.

### The story in depth

Torrent Pharmaceuticals earns an overall score of 77 out of 100, or 'Strong', built from 62 on business quality, a full 100 on multibagger pattern fit, and 76 on risk safety. The engine behind almost every strength is the same one: a salesforce that talks to doctors. The medical representative base has gone from 5,500 to 7,100 and is guided to 7,500, retail coverage reaches 75,000 outlets, and management directly credits this expansion for growing faster than the market. That single asset is what earns the top scores on distribution advantage, convenience benefits and market-share gain, and it is also what makes three of the four strongest patterns fit: the 'Friendly Middleman' model where the doctor chooses and the patient pays, 'Pricing Power' where price rather than volume is the biggest growth contributor and is consistently above the market, and 'Brand Strength', with 20 brands inside the industry's top 500 and 13 of them above Rs.100 crore. The company keeps Rs.76 of every Rs.100 of sales after direct input costs and Rs.33 as operating profit, which is the financial proof that the branded model works. Management's own conduct reinforces it — the 'Corporate Culture' pattern fits strongly because old promises show up delivered later: the Curatio integration and its margin benefit, a field force guided to 'just over 7,000' that landed at 7,100, and net debt cut from 1.55 times profits to 0.51 times.

The uncomfortable part is that the same trait that drives the strengths also creates the largest risk. Because the prescribing doctor and the dispensing chemist choose the product while the patient pays for it, the premium Torrent charges rests on relationships and brand equity rather than on anything the end customer can verify — and the identical molecules are sold more cheaply by others. That is exactly the 'Good Enough Goods' risk, flagged as a watch item, and the review notes it bites hardest where brand equity is weakest. The protection against it is the very same 7,100-rep network, the 75,000-outlet consumer reach and the Curatio advertising that is starting to build recognition with the end customer directly. The advertising spend is treated like an investment rather than an expense — a Shelcal pilot in four states was scaled nationwide, money is added to brands showing traction and cut from those that are not, and Curatio's 27% growth is explicitly attributed to it. Two further watch-list risks attack from the opposite side of the business, the roughly quarter of revenue that is not branded India and Brazil. Government Dependency is real because prices are set by state actors in three of four markets — Brazilian prices are revised annually by decree and Germany runs entirely on a rolling two-year insurance-fund tender. New Entrants matter because in the generic-facing businesses the economics reset every time a competitor arrives; management itself says outcomes 'depend upon the number of competitors that show up' and has candidly guided to 45-50% price erosion on Brazilian Semaglutide. Those same external price controls are the reason the 'Toll Roads' pattern is scored as no fit at all: the pricing here is constrained rather than uncontested.

Underneath the strong headline grade, the balance sheet and the industry setting are the weak spots, and they are why resilience is rated 'STRAINED' rather than comfortable. Every Rs.1 of assets produces only Rs.0.32 of sales, and about 163 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. Capital allocation, return on capital and industry structure are the three lowest-scoring areas of the quality framework, which sits oddly beside the near-perfect scores on customers, distribution and management honesty. Growth has averaged about 11% a year — inside the steady band the framework rewards — but it is lumpy, and operating margins have swung around five points a year, which suggests important costs sit outside management's control. The fourth watch-list risk, Stakeholder Concentration, has already shown up in cash terms: one third-party supplier's regulatory shutdown pushed German revenue down 6% in constant currency for several quarters, with management admitting it had no visibility and expecting three to four quarters to recover. Set against that, three risk channels are rated low — Cyclicality, Technological Innovation and Fashion Risk — because demand is repeat-prescription chronic therapy rather than anything tied to a customer's spending cycle or a trend, and the one big technology wave in the period, GLP-1 drugs, was captured rather than suffered, with 38% of the generic Semaglutide market in India including all of the oral format. Innovation is a 'likely' rather than certain fit for the same reason it is not yet a risk: the output is real and named — first-to-market Brexpiprazole, a Resmetirom trial, 58-60 molecules pending in Brazil — but research spending is being reallocated rather than expanded. In short, the case rests on a distribution and brand machine that is genuinely hard to copy, funding growth organically while margins rise, offset by a capital-hungry structure and pricing that outsiders partly control.

## What to watch

- Field force productivity — the entire 'Friendly Middleman' and distribution-advantage case rests on the 5,500-to-7,100 expansion continuing to buy above-market growth; if the move to 7,500 reps stops beating the market, the premium loses its justification.
- Price versus volume in India — 'Pricing Power' is scored a strong fit because price is the largest growth component with no volume loss; any quarter where above-market pricing starts costing volume would point straight at the 'Good Enough Goods' risk.
- Brazil and US generic price erosion — management's own 45-50% Semaglutide erosion estimate and the 'depends on how many competitors show up' framing make the New Entrants risk the fastest-moving item on the list.
- The 163-day working capital cycle and 0.32 asset turn — these, plus the bottom-scoring capital allocation and return-on-capital areas, are what make resilience 'STRAINED'; improvement here would lift the weakest half of the quality score, deterioration would offset the deleveraging from 1.55x to 0.51x.
- Third-party supplier and tender dependencies — the German supplier shutdown already cost 6% of constant-currency revenue with a 3-4 quarter recovery; a repeat would confirm Stakeholder Concentration and Government Dependency as more than watch-level concerns.
