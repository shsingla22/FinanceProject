# UltraTech Cement Ltd. (ULTRACEMCO) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CEMENT & CEMENT PRODUCTS*

UltraTech Cement makes and sells cement in India — mainly grey cement sold in bags under the UltraTech brand, which buyers use to build houses, roads, metros, ports, offices and other construction. It earns money by producing cement at its own plants and kilns and selling it by the ton, so profit depends on the volume it ships, the price per bag it can charge, and its costs for fuel (petcoke and coal), power and freight to the customer; management tracks profitability as 'EBITDA per ton', around INR1,200–1,300 in recent quarters. Its business is overwhelmingly domestic Indian, with capacity growing from about 116 million tons in 2022 to over 200 million tons in India by 2026 — described on the calls as the largest cement company in the world by sales volume outside China — plus a smaller overseas operation in the UAE. A large share of sales goes to individual home builders buying retail bags, which is why the calls stress brand strength, 'premiumization' and paying up for a trusted A-category brand rather than the cheapest bag.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Grey cement (domestic)** — The core business by far — bagged and bulk cement made at plants across India and trucked to customers (average 'lead distance' now about 360 km), with roughly 55%–60% of Indian cement consumption coming from housing and urban real estate and the rest from infrastructure like roads, metros, ports and data centres; consolidated volumes crossed 44 million tons in a single quarter (Q4 FY26).
- **Blended and premium cement / premiumization** — Within grey cement, management pushes a higher share of blended cement (about 71% back in 2022, with the clinker conversion ratio rising from 1.41 toward a target of 1.54) and premium-priced products, because both lift realizations and cut carbon emissions.
- **White cement, wall putty and Birla White** — A separate, much smaller branded business selling white cement and wall putty for finishing work; the calls mention a 4 lakh ton putty capacity expansion in 2022 and the UAE-based RAK White, which became a subsidiary in July 2024 and is consolidated into results.
- **Construction chemicals** — A small but growing add-on business of building chemicals sold alongside cement — revenue of about INR132 crore in one quarter of FY23, up from INR82 crore a year earlier.
- **Acquired cement businesses (India Cements and Kesoram)** — Two cement companies bought in FY25 whose plants have been folded into UltraTech and 100% rebranded to UltraTech by March 2026; together about 13% of consolidated capacity, and management is spending roughly INR2,000 crore on India Cements and INR400–500 crore on Kesoram to lift their per-ton earnings toward the group's level.
- **Overseas (UAE) operations** — A modest international arm outside India that management said 'contributed substantially' to EBITDA per ton in Q4 FY26, though the business is otherwise described as domestic.
- **Green power and fuel efficiency platform** — Not a product but a cost engine: about 1,897 MW of renewable and waste-heat-recovery power meeting roughly 43%–47% of the company's own electricity needs by 2026 (targeting 85% by FY30), plus a shifting mix of petcoke, coal and alternative fuels — all aimed at cushioning the volatile fuel costs that dominate every call.

### The story in depth

UltraTech's strongest ground is with the customer. The quality framework gives full marks to what it calls Assurance benefits: cement is a once-in-a-lifetime purchase for the individual home builder, and management says reliability, quality consistency and governance are what earn the premium, with price explicitly secondary. That shows up in the money — after direct input costs the company keeps ₹81 of every ₹100 of sales — and in the multibagger screen, where Brand Strength is a strong fit on the back of realizations above peers quarter after quarter, 19–21% growth in branded volumes, and the conversion of the acquired India Cements and Kesoram brands from B/C-category names into premium UltraTech volumes. Sitting behind it is a genuinely growing end market: urbanisation moving from 35% to 39% by 2030, a ₹75 trillion infrastructure outlay, PMAY housing, data centres and ports, with management guiding to 7–8% sustainable volume growth. Sales have compounded at about 13% a year with unusually steady year-to-year growth, and the company has taken share from weaker, underinvested rivals in quarters when industry volumes actually shrank.

The second pillar is execution, and the screen and the framework agree on it. Corporate Culture is a strong fit because the promises are checkable and were kept: 131mt by FY23, 157/159mt by FY25, 200mt delivered a full year early, the ICL/Kesoram brand migration finished a quarter early, and cables and wires on time and on budget. That capacity was growth capex, not maintenance, funded from the company's own cash, taking it from about 116mt in 2022 past 200mt, with a further ₹17,000cr committed toward 242.5mt — and it was not deferred when quarters turned weak. Cost to Replicate is a strong fit for the same reason the expansion is credible: limestone is secured behind every committed tonne, the network runs to 76 plants, roughly 2,000 warehouses and 150,000 channel partners, and a new entrant would face $110–120 a tonne and seven years to build. The looser fits are honest about what the business is not. Pricing Power, Low Price Plus and Forward Integrators are only partial — realizations do beat the industry, but there have been genuine price-deflation years, the efficiency savings are reinvested rather than shown as widening margins, and the RMC plants, 5,000+ UBS outlets and bulk terminals are still dealer and distribution assets rather than the company selling direct. Recurring Revenue and Toll Roads are no fits at all: every tonne is a one-off sale at a price negotiated and argued over every single quarter.

The risks are mostly the flip side of those same traits rather than separate problems. Cyclicality is the one elevated risk: results swing with monsoons, elections, fuel prices and cement prices — Q2FY25 saw EBITDA per tonne at a four-to-five-year low on about 68% utilisation, and the West Asia crude shock hit FY26-27 — and the very willingness to keep expanding through downturns that makes the culture score well is what puts new capacity into soft markets, though it is self-funded and the pan-India spread cushions it. Good Enough Goods is on watch precisely because the brand premium exists: cement is physically undifferentiated and sold through 150,000 dealers who steer the choice, so analysts ask call after call why UltraTech's realization should stay above the industry — the reassuring answer so far is that the gap has widened, with ICL's EBITDA per tonne climbing from ₹386 to ₹603 after rebranding. Government Dependency is also on watch: the same infrastructure programmes that make the growth story credible are the ones management leans on to justify demand, alongside limestone leases and imported fuel economics, offset by housing being roughly 55–60% of Indian cement consumption. New entrants, technology, supplier concentration and fashion risk are all low. Weaker spots in the framework are returns on capital, industry structure and the sheer visibility of the company — the largest cement maker outside China is the opposite of an under-followed idea — but management's candour is rated highly: it leads with the bad news, sizes one-offs precisely (₹80cr of excess maintenance, ₹90cr of bag cost, mark-to-market currency moves), flags a soft coming quarter unprompted and declines to guess where it does not know. Overall the record scores 76 out of 100 — 77 on business quality, 71 on multibagger fit, 82 on risk safety — a Strong four-star rating with resilience judged sound.
