### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 3 weakened, 8 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Order book reached an all-time high of INR6,814 crores with entry into Maharashtra, Odisha, Bihar, Chhattisgarh and four NTPC BESS states, and management claims first-mover position in the 90 GW storage pipeline; but no market-share figure or named-competitor comparison is ever given, FY26 order inflow of ~INR1,500 crores missed the INR2,500 crore target, and revenue grew only 7.5%. (The long view had said: Order book grew from INR1,960cr (Dec 2024) to INR6,814cr (Jun 2026) with new states (Maharashtra, Odisha, Bihar, Chhattisgarh) and new segments, but no market-share number is ever quoted, roughly 35-40% of the new book is renewables bought partly via acquisition (Suyog Urja, a BESS project), and FY26 revenue grew only 7.5% against 35-40% guidance — expansion of book, not demonstrated share capture.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The model is the opposite of low-price/high-turns: EIEL runs 24-28% EBITDA margins on a slow-turning EPC balance sheet with ~90-day working capital, and management attributes margin to project mix (wastewater share) rather than to any itemised cost culture passed through as price. (The long view had said: There is a real cost culture — in-house process design and self-execution with no back-to-back subcontracting, and FY26 cost of manufacturing down ~1pp — but management explicitly refuses to translate that into lower bids, choosing to lose orders rather than cut margin, so the 'low price' half of the pattern is absent.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Nothing on these calls describes a barrier a funded newcomer could not rebuild: the technologies are standard licensed process systems, contracts are re-tendered project by project against open bid pipelines (INR8,000 crores bid, INR26,000 crores AMRUT pipeline), and the company itself bought its way into wind EPC by acquiring Suyog Urja rather than building an un-replicable capability. (The long view had said: There is one articulable, time-based barrier — accumulated pre-qualification credentials from completed complex plants (100 MLD IFAS STP, 55 MLD Varanasi, 25 MLD Sarigam CETP) that gate eligibility for larger tenders — but it is a licence to bid, not protection: competitors underbid them in FY26, they need JVs to enter desalination, and a well-funded EPC contractor can accumulate the same credentials.)

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Work is won through competitive public tendering where price is the decisive variable, and management itself frames output in commoditised per-MLD cost benchmarks rather than any defended premium; the differentiation claimed (UF/RO/MVR/MBR, ZLD) is bought-in technology available to other bidders. (The long view had said: Award is by lowest-price public tender where the government intermediary controls the choice and brand carries no premium, and in FY26 cheaper bidders took work the company would not price down for — the classic good-enough dynamic. The protection is genuine but partial: complex CETP/ZLD and desalination-adjacent work is technically niche with per-MLD costs of INR3-15 crores and in-house process design, whereas plain water treatment plants are, by management's own account, mostly civil work.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Revenue is pure project/EPC work tied to government capex programmes, not customer operations, and the swings are visible in the last four quarters: guidance of 35-40% top-line growth ended at 7.5% for FY26, Q3 revenue grew 1% on orders that 'didn't come to the company', monsoon halted execution in H1, and Q4 EBITDA margin collapsed to 18.7% from 27.1% in Q3. (The long view had said: Revenue is order-book-driven EPC tied entirely to government capex programmes (AMRUT, JJM, Namami Gange), so inflow timing swings the P&L: growth fell from ~50% (H1 FY25) to 7.5% in FY26 and Q4 FY26 EBITDA margin dropped to 18.7% from 27.1% in Q3, with management admitting the 35% growth guidance was missed because bid evaluation and design stages elongated. The long-dated O&M/annuity and HAM book (~INR951 crores over 5-15 years) is an operations-linked, flow-type buffer against pure project cyclicality.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
