### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 8 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Forward Integrators**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: There is a consultant-run programme to grow the e-commerce channel that management says is tracking to plan, and in-shop branding investment at dealer counters, but no own-store count, franchise royalty or D2C revenue share was disclosed on any FY26 call — the route to market remains dealer- and modern-trade-led. (The long view had said: Beyond e-commerce being 13-17% of sales (Q1 FY23) and a consultant-led e-commerce growth project in FY26, the calls say nothing about own stores, franchising, royalties or D2C economics.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management repeatedly claims share gain — improved share in top loaders, double-digit growth in front load and microwaves in Q4, and an aspiration to grow faster than industry — but refuses to give share numbers or gross margins, and the gains coexist with muted ACs and heavy dealer scheme spend, so the quality of the share is unverifiable from these calls. (The long view had said: Share gains are real but narrow and slow: top-load grew ~19% in FY26 with share moving 9%→10%, while front-load share was only 'maintained' at ~23% and AC share sits at 3-3.5% with big OEM volumes lost — and gains come alongside rising discounts, not with profitability.)
- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Candour exists in patches (owning the fixed-cost failure, disclosing the Labour Code charge), but the FY26 record is promises missed — a Rs.200cr annualised saving that closed at INR79cr, a stated double-digit margin intent against 6.1% PBDIT and falling PAT margin — alongside refusal to disclose gross margin, a Q1 call where the Chairman had to correct his own CFO and sales head on the advertising number live, and revolving-door leadership with four MDs exiting in five years and a new Home Appliances MD/CEO arriving in April 2026. (The long view had said: Management does own bad news candidly — the FY24 compressor shortage ('we just did not foresee it'), unexplained washer weakness — but the promise record is poor: double-digit appliance margins targeted for Q2/Q3 FY25 in the Jun 2024 call were still ~5.5% PBDIT in FY26, and the MD seat has turned over repeatedly (Sen, Ray, Sachdev, now Abraham from Apr 2026).)

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (QUANT SIGNAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Innovation Dominance (NO FIT); Cost to Replicate (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 1 worsened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The Nobel Biocare mechanism is visible: the product is not technically hard to replicate, cheaper Chinese/Indian and scale-global alternatives sit on the same shelf, and the purchase decision is effectively made by an intermediary IFB has to pay for — ~4,600 counter sales representatives plus INR1,500 crores of annual dealer scheme payouts. The result is ~40% gross margin converting to only 6.1% PBDIT and 2.4% PAT in FY26, and margins fell year-on-year despite INR79 crores of cost innovation. (The long view had said: For four straight years management has been unable to price to cost — in FY23 rivals absorbed 13-16% material inflation while raising prices only 4-6%, and in FY26 they still say full pass-through is impossible given their relative market position — so the promised double-digit appliance margin has never arrived and the P&L leans on serial cost-reduction programmes (A&M, INR79 crores, then INR150 crores) instead of pricing power. Rising dealer schemes and cashbacks, plus OEMs switching to cheaper suppliers, show the intermediary and the price point, not the brand, controlling the choice.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Technological Innovation (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Government Dependency (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
