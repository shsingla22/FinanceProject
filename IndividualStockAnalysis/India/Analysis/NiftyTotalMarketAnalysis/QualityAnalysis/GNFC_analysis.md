# GNFC (GNFC) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

GNFC (Gujarat Narmada Valley Fertilizers & Chemicals Limited) is a Gujarat-based manufacturer that runs chemical and fertilizer plants at Narmadanagar/Bharuch and Dahej, and has been operating for 50 years as of May 2026. It makes money two ways: selling industrial chemicals — TDI, nitric acid, ammonium nitrate, acetic acid, methanol, aniline, ammonia — mostly to Indian industrial buyers at prices set on an import-parity basis (with some exports), and selling fertilizers such as urea (including neem-coated and technical-grade) and complex fertilizers to the farm market, where realisations depend on government subsidy (NBS rates for complex fertilizer, fixed-cost and energy norms for urea). Its costs are driven by bought-in feedstock — natural gas, oil from IOCL, benzene, toluene and methanol — so profits swing with energy and raw-material prices; in FY26 it earned PBT of about Rs 1,065 crores and PAT of Rs 797 crores and paid a Rs 21 per share dividend. It is listed on BSE and NSE, is building out roughly Rs 2,800 crores of capacity projects, and also runs a small IT services business plus one consolidated subsidiary, Gujarat Green Revolution Company.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Chemical segment (Industrial Products)** — Makes and sells industrial chemicals from the Bharuch and Dahej plants, and is described by management as the main driver of profit improvement — chemicals are where realisations and volumes lifted FY26 earnings while fertilizer lost money.
- **TDI (toluene di-isocyanate)** — Its flagship chemical, made at two plants (TDI-1 and TDI-2 Dahej) with combined capacity of roughly 60,000-70,000 tonnes; GNFC holds about 60-65% of an Indian market of roughly 1 lakh tonnes used in flexible foam, is protected by an anti-dumping duty extended for another five years, and exports the balance — though TDI-2 at Dahej ran at heavy losses (Rs 259 crores in FY24, Rs 89 crores in Q1 FY25) before conditions improved.
- **Nitric acid and ammonium nitrate** — Weak and concentrated nitric acid plus ammonium nitrate; a concentrated nitric acid expansion was expected to add about Rs 110 crores of revenue, and a new 200,000 TPA weak nitric acid plant (about 57% more capacity) was approved in 2024 to feed an ammonium nitrate project — demand management called out as nationally important during the Q4 FY26 war-related disruption.
- **Acetic acid and methanol** — Methanol is both a product and the key input into acetic acid; in FY26 high gas and oil prices made in-house methanol production uneconomic and pushed up acetic acid costs, so management was weighing whether to keep making acetic acid or source it, and is in licensing talks with INEOS for added capacity.
- **Ammonia and captive utilities** — Ammonia is produced in-house as feedstock (a 50,000 TPA additional ammonia loop was due around 2026) and supported by capitalised infrastructure — a double-wall ammonia tank, a 4 MW solar plant wheeled to Dahej, a desalination plant, railway track renewal, and a roughly Rs 613 crore power plant at Dahej expected to add about Rs 82 crores net contribution.
- **Fertilizer segment** — Sells urea (neem-coated and technical-grade) and complex fertilizers through its own fertilizer marketing department; volumes have been stable to good, but the segment has been loss-making and widening, with management saying the overdue government revision of urea fixed cost and energy norms is what would fix it — subsidy receivables stood at about Rs 302 crores at end-Q3 FY26.
- **Others (IT division)** — A small non-chemical business, mainly IT services, which management said grew revenue about 20% in FY26 with profit doubling from Rs 17 crores to Rs 35 crores.
- **Gujarat Green Revolution Company** — The only subsidiary GNFC consolidates, described simply as a profitable operation.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across all four calls management refuses to forecast prices because earnings ride commodity spreads — TDI, aniline, acetic acid, methanol, ammonia, gas and fuel oil — and the swings are visible: record Q1 FY23 (PBT +108% year-on-year) versus a full-year TDI-2 loss of ~Rs.259 crores in FY24, EBITDA sliding 32%->28% on volume/price alone, and FY26 profit driven by 'benign raw material prices' rather than volume.

> "prognosis on price of chemical is really anybody guess. It is not possible to comprehend the price of future." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/consumable demand base rather than customer capex — nitric acid has 'very wide end use segments being basic chemical', TDI feeds flexible foam growing ~10% CAGR; and the company keeps investing through the cycle (Rs.2,800 crores capex, Rs.3,500 crores cash) rather than scaling back.*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 2 of the last 11 years with swings of ±24 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from -0% to 28% (swinging ±8.1 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 3% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 7%).

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** A Gujarat state-owned fertilizer producer whose fertilizer economics are literally set by the state: NBS rates, notified subsidy rates with chronic lags (Rs.702 crores locked up in Aug-2022), a government-set 6.2 Gcal/MT energy threshold where 'government takes some share' of any saving, and urea fixed-cost and energy-norm revisions still 'at discussion stage' four years later while segment losses widen. Chemical pricing also leans on anti-dumping duties the government grants or withdraws, and TGU volumes were redirected at the Department of Fertilizers' behest.

> "the revision both in urea of fixed cost as well as energy norms are yet to happen. And this is overdue as the losses are widening in case of Fertilizer segment." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: The chemicals segment (TDI, aniline, acetic acid, AN melt, formic acid) and the IT division carry the profits, partially diversifying away from policy-driven fertilizer revenue.*

### New Entrants — ELEVATED
*"A new player changes the rules"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 2 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Structurally different competitors are resetting economics in two of GNFC's product lines: Chinese aniline producers with 7-8x single-location scale and cheaper coal-based hydrogen have made GNFC's own aniline unviable standalone (now run as fixed-margin job work, expansion shelved), and a wave of new domestic nitric acid capacity from Deepak Nitrite, Deepak Fertilisers and Chambal is arriving exactly as GNFC commissions 200 KTPA of WNA with ~70,000 tonnes earmarked for merchant sale.

> "many of the players have put up their nitric acid plants, like Deepak Nitrite, even Deepak Fertiliser is also expanding. Chambal is also coming up with their own WNA, CNA plant." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Downstream integration of the new nitric acid into captive AN melt absorbs most of the added capacity, and anti-dumping duties (TDI extended another five years) plus ~60% domestic TDI share provide partial shelter.*

- ✅ Operating margin held (about 7% then 8%) — no drift down under competition.
- ✅ Growth is holding up: 2% a year earlier, 9% recently — no structural stall.

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** GNFC has no brand premium to defend — it prices every chemical off import parity, so cheaper Chinese material sets the ceiling; in aniline that dynamic has already destroyed the economics ('on a standalone, we are not viable'), and TDI and acetic acid depend on anti-dumping duties and Chinese export-rebate policy rather than product differentiation. Bounded because the products are specification commodities where the customer, not an intermediary, chooses.

> "there is huge dumping from China... it is very difficult to compete with the China market Aniline. So that's why we have not enhanced our capacity because Chinese Aniline is very cheap." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Freight, duty and logistics protection in bulk-hazardous chemicals plus captive integration (ammonia-to-nitric-acid-to-AN melt, own power/steam) give a genuine scale-and-location cost position in the domestic market.*

- ✅ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs held near 45% (was 47%) — no gross-level price erosion.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has collapsed from a peak of 28% to 11% — the Nobel-Biocare-style fingerprint of a premium being competed away.
- ✅ Operating margin held (about 7% then 8%) — no drift down under competition.

### Stakeholder Concentration — WATCH
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Real but bounded supplier concentration: fuel oil for the ammonia route comes from IOCL on a single formula-linked contract, benzene/toluene sit on one contract that expired at FY26-end and runs on a short-term extension, and methanol sourcing is exposed to Iran/Strait of Hormuz. Customer side is broad — merchant nitric acid, TDI to ~100kt of foam converters, fertilizer through the state channel — so no single buyer drives results.

> "our contract, which was there for procurement of toluene, benzene expired sometime end of financial year. And we have got a short-term extension on those contract considering the war situation." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Broad, diversified customer base across fertilizer and multiple industrial chemicals; no named customer or licensor ever discussed as driving results, and no supply interruption has actually materialised ('we are not facing any issue in terms of its availability so far').*

- ✅ Customers pay in about 26 days (was 105) — no sign of weakening bargaining power in collection terms.
