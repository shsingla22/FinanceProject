**Checks that improved:**

- **Candor** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Communication is unusually specific and self-critical — they named the Shivalik/Titagarh failure and its rationale, walked through the accounting of the impairment reversal in detail, and pre-warned of 50-100bps margin pressure rather than burying it. | The long view had said: Proactively flags bad news and owns learning curves — pre-announced 50-100bps margin pressure, explained railway project delays in detail, and admitted the two-quarter PCB pass-through lag as a mistake learned.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Poor → Weak. Now: Growth is volume- and acquisition-led with demonstrably weak pricing power: cost pass-through lags 1-1.5 quarters, 50-100bps of margin pressure is guided, and the new mobile business runs at 1.5-2% EBITDA. | The long view had said: Growth is explicitly volume-led with pricing being only commodity pass-through, and management is deliberately adding very low-margin high-volume work (mobile assembly at 1.5-2% EBITDA).

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: Convenience is geographic/capacity proximity to customers rather than distribution intimacy — Sri City capacity in South India and land near the new Noida airport — with no sales-force advantage described. | The long view had said: Accessibility is a genuine differentiator — manufacturing footprint across 23 geographies placed near customers plus an integrated one-stop component-and-product offering.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Neutral → Weak. Now: Management is visibly promotional — repeated public thanks to ministries, and a specially convened Saturday call at short notice to publicise the Oppo tie-up as an 'inflection point'. | The long view had said: Management is operator-like rather than celebrity, but runs a high-visibility guidance cadence including a specially convened business update call for the Oppo announcement, covered by CLSA, Macquarie, Ambit and others.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Outperformance versus a declining industry implies share was taken from someone, but no weak competitor is identified anywhere in the calls. | The long view had said: Large pools of share to take from imports and weak domestic players, aided by policy — 85% of PCB demand imported and anti-dumping duty directly delivering new customers.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Good → Neutral. Now: Technology depth is being bought rather than built — HDI capability via the Korea Circuit JV, industrial automation via Unitronics, BESS/inverters via Power-One — with no in-house R&D intensity evidenced, though the resulting stack is genuinely broadening. | The long view had said: A real multi-product technical layer (in-house inverter boards, ODM AC designs, HDI PCB roadmap, backward integration to ~70% of BOM) though not deep enough to command premium pricing.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Consumer Durables outperformed the RAC industry in every quarter of the window — down 18% when the industry fell 30-35% (Nov 2025), +27% in Q3, and +14% for FY26 against a flat industry, exactly as guided — with Electronics up 49% and Railways up 19%; management repeatedly attributes it to wallet-share gains inside existing customers plus new logos like Oppo. (The long view had said: Persistent, quantified share gain over the whole period: OEM value share 21.2% (FY18) to 26.6% (FY22), ~22-23% of India's AC inverter PCB boards by FY25, consumer durables +14% against a flat FY26 RAC industry, EMS +49% in FY26, and now India's largest bare-PCB manufacturer via Ascent/Shogini — gained through wallet-share deepening and new verticals, not by buying volume at negative returns.)

**That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls Amber describes pure build-to-order OEM/ODM manufacturing — no license fees, AMC, attach rates, renewals or deferred revenue anywhere; working capital runs the wrong way, with the company funding customers via inventory builds that dragged Q2 FY26 into a loss. (The long view had said: Only one thin thread of genuine recurring revenue across 17 calls — Sidwal's AMC business on metro/rail HVAC (Aug 2022) — with no attach rates, renewal rates or deferred-revenue disclosure ever given; the customer-funding trait shows up instead as very low net working capital (76→37 days in FY23, 14 days in Q1 FY25, 4-10 days claimed for the Oppo business), which is payables-driven rather than advance-billing driven.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No R&D spend, pipeline or named new-product revenue is disclosed on any FY26 call; capability comes from bought-in technology and partners — Korea Circuit JV for HDI, Yujin for pantographs, Unitronics for automation, Power-One and Shogini acquisitions — funded by a INR1,000 crore QIP and government ECMS grants rather than high gross margins. (The long view had said: There is a real, repeated product cadence — first Indian company to develop its own inverter PCB boards, first B2B ODM tower/cassette/window-top-throw inverter ACs (Q1 FY25), BLDC motors, doors and gangways at Sidwal — but R&D spend is never disclosed or quantified, and the innovation funds volume rather than gross margin, which is the reverse of the pattern.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Amber sells into intermediaries who control the choice and hold the pricing power: as a 'Tier 2 B2B supplier' it can only pass on 60% CCL and gold inflation with a 1-1.5 quarter lag, Indian Railways contracts are fixed price, and the new mobile business is entered at openly commodity economics of 1.5-2% EBITDA with no end-customer awareness of Amber at all. There is no brand premium to defend, so input-cost and price competition land directly on margin. (The long view had said: Amber sells into intermediaries who control the end choice and have no reason to pay a premium, and margins show it: consumer durable operating EBITDA is stuck around 7-8% despite scale, EMS started at 3%, Chinese competitors attacked the inverter-PCB business on price, and management has now voluntarily entered mobile assembly at industry margins of 1.5-2% EBITDA. Consumer-durable EBITDA grew only 6% in FY26 on 14% revenue growth — the value keeps leaking to customers.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The last four calls are dominated by swing factors outside management's control: the RAC industry fell 30-35% in Q2 FY26 on weather and GST-deferral, was 'flattish' for the full year, and Q2 consolidated EBITDA fell 19% with a INR32 crore loss after tax on elevated inventory and financing cost; the railway/metro and PCB legs are tied to customer and government capex programmes, and FY27 guidance already flags 50-100bps margin pressure from commodity and currency swings. (The long view had said: Across 17 calls results swing with the AC season and commodity prices: FY23 rode a post-pandemic boom (Q1 FY23 RAC +138%), FY25 an 'unprecedented' 35% industry summer, then FY26 saw Q1/Q2 volumes 'down by 30%' on bad weather with the industry flat; margins are repeatedly hostage to commodity pass-through on a quarterly (PCB: two-quarter) lag, with FY27 guided down 50-100bps on copper clad laminate and gold up ~60%. The railway/metro division is pure customer-capex revenue, going flat in FY25 purely on project deferrals.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: As a B2B contract manufacturer, results turn on a handful of named relationships: growth is described as 'adding wallet share within existing customers', compressor supply rests on a 3-year strategic cooperation with a single supplier GMCC, the railway division's INR700 crores of visibility came through Titagarh (whose Firema arm required a INR94 crore impairment), and the entire mobile entry depends on one counterparty, Oppo, in whose subleased facility Amber will manufacture. (The long view had said: Amber is a B2B supplier to a handful of AC brands and OEMs, and the record shows the vulnerability: ILJIN was acquired supplying 'primarily to LG and IFB, there were only two customers'; management now withholds window-AC volume splits because 'few of our customers taking that data and they attempted a negotiation bid'; compressors rest on a long-term agreement with a single supplier, GMCC; and the entire new mobile vertical is one relationship with Oppo, in Oppo's own subleased facility.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
