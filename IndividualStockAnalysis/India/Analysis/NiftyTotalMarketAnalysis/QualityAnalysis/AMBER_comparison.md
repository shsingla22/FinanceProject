## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 29 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 2 improved, 8 regressed, 19 unchanged. 2 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them). 2 were newly assessable in the recent view.

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Allocation | Neutral (+0.50) | Neutral (-0.20) | -0.70 |
| Return on Capital | Weak (-0.67) | Weak (-0.67) | +0.00 |
| Growth | Neutral (+0.50) | Neutral (+0.17) | -0.33 |
| Management | Neutral (+0.00) | Neutral (+0.17) | +0.17 |
| Industry Structure | Neutral (-0.17) | Neutral (-0.17) | +0.00 |
| Customer Benefits | Neutral (+0.00) | Neutral (-0.25) | -0.25 |
| Competitive Advantage | Neutral (+0.50) | Weak (-1.00) | -1.50 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: The Railway/Sidwal business enjoys genuine regulatory switching costs via RDSO approval and a INR2,600 crore order book, though the larger consumer durable business has none. | The long view had said: Some stickiness from integrated multi-location supply and backward integration, but switching costs are visibly weak — customers benchmark and negotiate, and brands are setting up their own ODM capability.
- **Candor** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Management openly owns the failed Shivalik/Titagarh investment, quantifies adverse input-cost moves and pre-warns on margins rather than burying them. | The long view had said: Management pre-announces bad news with specifics and owns operating mistakes — flagging 50-100bps margin pressure, explaining railway project delays, and admitting the Tier-2 PCB pass-through lag was a learning.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Good → Weak. Now: Higher-end technology is being imported through JVs and acquisitions (Korea Circuit for HDI, Unitronics for automation) rather than internally developed, and the new mobile business starts at assembly-level value addition. | The long view had said: A genuine multi-product technical layer — first Indian in-house inverter PCB, ODM firsts in tower/cassette ACs, and a move up to HDI PCB and semiconductor substrates — though intensity and pricing capture remain modest.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Weak. Now: As a B2B supplier to a handful of brands there is no distribution or service-reach advantage; the only reach claimed is manufacturing footprint and land banking. | The long view had said: There is a real manufacturing-footprint reach near customer plants across regions, but no distribution or service network moat in the classic sense for a B2B component supplier.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: Product development is referenced (Yujin JV products, doors/gangways, deeper value addition roadmap) but there is no disclosure of R&D spend or evidence of R&D efficiency. | The long view had said: R&D has repeatedly converted into first-to-market products and new customers — India's first in-house inverter PCB, first B2B ODM tower/cassette/window-top-throw ACs — though R&D intensity is modest and margins stay thin.
- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Weak. Now: M&A is frequent, large and complex rather than small bolt-on — Power-One 60%, Shogini 80%, a cross-border creeping stake to 50.4% in Israel-listed Unitronics — and the prior Shivalik/Titagarh Firema investment was written off, while divisional margins have not yet improved post-deal. | The long view had said: Serial, escalating M&A (ILJIN/Ever, Ascent, Power-One, Unitronics Israel, Shogini, multiple JVs) did lift electronics EBITDA from ~3% in 2018 to 8.8% in FY26, but these are neither small nor simple, and the Shivalik JV was written off for INR112cr.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Neutral → Weak. Now: Overseas expansion so far is via minority/creeping stakes rather than a travelling brand, and the Italy route intended to open Sidwal exports failed and was impaired. | The long view had said: Geographic expansion is mostly domestic multi-plant footprint; exports remain aspirational — the US/European rolling-stock order took four years to reach development stage and needs 2.5 more years.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: Amber is a contract manufacturer behind other companies' brands with no ownership of the customer's mind or aftermarket; it even describes itself as a Tier 2 supplier. | The long view had said: Claims of being India's only integrated one-stop B2B provider and now the largest PCB manufacturer, plus a small captive AMC/refurb stream at Sidwal, fall short of a monopoly-like hold on the customer's mind.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: There is evidence of customer intimacy through wallet-share deepening within existing accounts, but no distribution or accessibility advantage is claimed. | The long view had said: Amber's differentiator is accessibility — an integrated one-stop solution delivered across 23 geographies with plants located near customers, and proactive inventory buildup to guarantee supply.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Poor. Now: Growth is overwhelmingly volume-led with no pricing power — input costs are passed on only with a 1-1.5 quarter lag, and the newest business is explicitly high-volume, low-margin. | The long view had said: Growth is volume- and pass-through-led, not pricing/mix-led, and mix is actively moving down-market with the Oppo assembly entry at 1.5-2% EBITDA.

*Unchanged: Return on capital (headline); Share donators; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Independent, long-term and tenacious; Security by obscurity; Barriers to entry and rationality; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Halo effect (caution); Structural end-market growth; Intangible benefits; Cyclical market growth; Disciplined stewards; Assurance benefits; Gain in market share; Cost of working capital; Asset turn; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Profit margin; Out of limelight.*

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these — identical by construction): People matter (talent development) (Neutral); Persistence of growth (Weak).*

**Newly assessable in the recent view:**

- **Distribution to shareholders** — Weak: The company is raising equity rather than distributing — INR1,000 crores QIP plus INR1,750 crores into Electronics — with no dividend or buyback policy discussed.
- **Network effects** — Weak: The business is a linear contract-manufacturing model with no two-sided network described anywhere in the calls; scale brings cost, not self-reinforcing demand.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 3 weakened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Outperformance versus the industry is repeated and quantified in every results call — CD division down 18% against a 30–35% RAC industry decline in Q2, then +27% in Q3 and +14% for FY26 against a flat industry, Electronics +49%, Railway +19%, plus the new Oppo mandate — though FY26 CD EBITDA grew only 6% on 14% revenue, so some share is being won at the cost of margin. (The long view had said: Explicit, sustained and quantified share gain: OEM value share 21.2% (FY18) to 26.6% (FY22); ~22-23% of India's inverter PCB boards by FY25; RAC business +50% vs a ~35% industry in Q1FY25 and Consumer Durable +14% against a flat FY26 industry; and with Ascent/Shogini/Ascent-K, 'India's largest and most comprehensive PCB manufacturer'. Gains were funded by capex and margin-accretive diversification, not by underwriting or price sacrifice.)

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Within this window management delivered the 13–15% CD growth it guided to (14% actual) and disclosed the failed Shivalik/Titagarh Firema investment promptly, explaining the rationale and taking the impairment rather than deferring it; against that, the balance sheet is being stretched via a ~INR1,000 crore QIP, INR1,750 crore Electronics funding and rapid serial M&A, and PAT swung to a Q2 loss on financing cost and inventory. (The long view had said: Stable founder-led leadership (Jasbir Singh, Daljit Singh, Sudhir Goyal present across all 17 calls, FY21 to date), bad news disclosed promptly and unprompted (railway/Vande Bharat delays in Aug 2024, the INR112cr Shivalik JV impairment in FY26, and a pre-announced 50-100 bps margin hit), promises delivered over years (Sri City, Resojet washing machines, Sidwal greenfield commissioning, EMS EBITDA 3% in 2018 to 7.7% by Q1FY25), and a deleveraging balance sheet (net debt INR965cr to INR511cr). The one blemish is the ROCE promise of 17-20% made in Aug 2022 that was still being re-guided in FY25.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The four calls discuss only manufactured-product revenue (RAC boxes, PCBs/PCBAs, railway subsystems, phone assembly); no attach rates, AMC/service share, renewals, deferred revenue or customer advances are mentioned anywhere. (The long view had said: Only the Sidwal railway/metro business shows a genuine recurring stream (AMC contracts and Delhi Metro AC refurbishment), mentioned in Aug 2022 but never quantified as attach rates, renewal rates or deferred revenue in later calls; the core RAC/EMS business is pure order-book manufacturing. Negative-to-minimal working capital exists (NWC 14 days in Q1FY25, 9-29 days FY25-26) but is driven by supplier acceptances, not customer prepayment.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: New capability is bought rather than invented — Power-One, Unitronics (to 50.4%), Shogini, the Korea Circuit JV — with no R&D spend disclosed, no named self-developed products with revenue impact, and assembly economics of 1.5–2% EBITDA that cannot fund outsized R&D. (The long view had said: There is a genuine, repeated R&D-led product cadence — first Indian company to develop its own inverter PCB (now ~22-23% of industry inverter boards), and in FY25 'India's first B2B player' offering ODM tower, window top-throw inverter, tropical split and cassette ACs — but R&D spend is never disclosed or quantified, and gross margins (7-8% EBITDA) are far too thin to fund outsized innovation.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 3 worsened, 5 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls the core Consumer Durable business swung with weather and channel behaviour — RAC industry down 30-35% in Q2 FY26 producing a INR32 crore loss quarter, then a 38% growth quarter on pre-BEE channel filling, with the full year 'flattish' for the industry; input costs (CCL +60%, gold +60%) and currency drove a guided 50-100 bps consolidated margin hit for FY27, and railway/metro revenue is tied to government capex programmes rather than customer operations. (The long view had said: RAC revenue swings violently with weather and season — Q1 FY23 RAC grew 138% and industry +35% in FY25, then FY26 saw Q1/Q2 volumes 'down by 30%' and a flattish year; margins ride commodity pass-through with a 1-2 quarter lag (copper clad laminate and gold both +60% in a year, guided 50-100bps consol margin hit), and the Railway division is pure customer-capex exposure to metro/Vande Bharat programme timing.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Amber sits as an unbranded intermediary with visibly thin and compressing economics — Consumer Durable EBITDA grew only 6% on 14% revenue growth in FY26, the bare PCB business cannot recover CCL and gold inflation except with a 1-1.5 quarter lag because it is a 'Tier 2 B2B supplier', railway contracts with Indian Railways are fixed price, and the new mobile business is explicitly accepted at 1.5-2% EBITDA. Value accrues to the brand owner, not the assembler. (The long view had said: Amber's products are largely invisible to the end consumer and the intermediary brand controls the choice, so pricing is repeatedly attacked: Chinese players dropped inverter-PCB prices forcing a strategy change, a customer used disclosed volume data to open a negotiation bid, and the new mobile business is entered knowingly at 1.5-2% EBITDA; Consumer Durable EBITDA grew only 6% on 14% revenue in FY26, and PCB (Tier-2) cannot pass costs for two quarters.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Results lean on a small set of named relationships: a strategic compressor supply agreement with GMCC to secure three years of supply, growth framed as 'adding wallet share within existing customers' rather than new customers, INR700 crores of railway visibility hanging off the Titagarh association (whose Shivalik/Titagarh Firema arm required a INR112 crore impairment and exit), and the entire mobile entry resting on one partner, Oppo, in Oppo's own subleased facility. (The long view had said: Structurally a B2B supplier whose divisions started life on one or two anchors — ILJIN/Ever was 'primarily to LG and IFB, there were only two customers' — and the new mobile division rests entirely on a single Oppo/BBK relationship (subleasing Oppo's own facility, no Vivo or other brand), while compressors, which Amber does not make, come under a long-term GMCC agreement from a supply base of only five domestic manufacturers.)

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
