**Checks that improved:**

- **Distribution to shareholders** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Good. Now: A 10% interim dividend was approved in Q3FY26 on record profits, but the only capital-markets action beyond that was a government OFS to meet MPS norms — no buyback or articulated distribution policy. | The long view had said: Distribution policy is dilutive rather than returns-oriented — a INR5,000 crore equity raise is board-approved while the government sold down via OFS, and no buyback is contemplated despite 23% ROE.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: Board-approved 5-year plan for 1,000 new branches plus 'Project 321' (321 branches in 18 months) sited by external scientific study, funded alongside ROA of 1.86-1.9% and ROE of 23-24.65%, i.e. heavy growth spend at high incremental returns. | The long view had said: Growth investment is real and earning high incremental returns — 200 new branches a year at scientifically chosen pin codes plus a GIFT City IBU that broke even in year one against a 3-year plan, all at bank ROE of 23%+.
- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Weak → Neutral. Now: Balance-sheet-heavy by nature (INR 5.95-6.5 lakh crore business), but earns an unusually high ROA of 1.86-1.9% off a 52.5% CASA funding base — good returns on assets without an asset-light model or a durable moat. | The long view had said: A bank is structurally balance-sheet-heavy and this one is levering harder — CD ratio at 87-88% funded increasingly by refinance — so returns come from spread and leverage, not asset-light turnover with a protecting moat.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: RBI licensing is a genuine entry barrier and ownership is stable at 73.6% government, but pricing rationality is weak — bulk deposit rates and loan concessions are actively bid. | The long view had said: Licensing creates entry barriers but pricing is not rational — the bank must run special-bucket deposit schemes at 20-25 bps premiums to prevent flight, and deposits are also leaking to SIPs and mutual funds outside the industry entirely.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Good → Weak. Now: The bank has deliberately exited obscurity — courting international ratings and institutional investors, with FII holding up from 0.39% to 5.8% and DII from 0.24% to 6.86% in three years. | The long view had said: It was a genuinely ignored small PSU niche, though that obscurity is now eroding as institutional capital arrives — FII holding rose from 0.39% to 5.8% and DII from 0.24% to 6.86% in about three years.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Commentary is metric-dense rather than personality-driven, but the MD actively markets the stock — touting rating upgrades and FII/DII shareholding gains — and accepts effusive analyst praise without deflecting. | The long view had said: Management is low-profile and process-focused, discussing dashboards, verticals and pin-code branch siting rather than personality, though the MD does actively court the guidance-beat narrative on calls.
- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Publishes 15-19 explicit guidance parameters at each year's start and reports performance against every one, but deflected a direct question on rising cost of deposits into a CASA narrative rather than answering it. | The long view had said: Unusually specific and self-binding communication — 15-19 guidance parameters published annually and reported against, plus frank disclosure of the COVID-provision reclassification, the auditors' prompting, yield declines from penal-interest rules, and quantified debt-waiver haircuts.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Some captive stickiness via 52.5% CASA and institutional/government relationships that carry switching friction, but no regional monopoly — current account balances fell sharply in Q1 and bulk deposits are openly rate-shopped. | The long view had said: There is a genuine regional franchise with switching-cost stickiness — a dominant Maharashtra footprint, entrenched government department and ministry relationships being deepened through ERP/API integration, and CASA sustained above 50%.
- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: Management asserts brand-based pricing power and clients paying up for service, but the evidence contradicts it — yield on advances flat at ~9.05% and NIM down 10 bps Y-o-Y in a commodity product. | The long view had said: There is modest brand-based pricing power evidenced by an industry-leading CASA ratio above 50% (cheap sticky funding) and explicit instruction to charge what the brand deserves, but management simultaneously admits depositors chase 20-25 bps.

*Unchanged: Distribution advantage; Technology advantage; Mini-monopolies; Geographical expansion; Share donators; Disciplined stewards; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Structural end-market growth; Network effects; Halo effect (caution); Assurance benefits; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Pricing, Mix and Volume; Cyclical market growth; Gain in market share; Convenience benefits.*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

**That eased:**

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
