# Quess Corp (QUESS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Quess Corp is an Indian outsourcing company that rents out people: it hires workers, keeps them on its own payroll, and deploys them at client companies that would rather not employ them directly. Customers are large employers across banking and financial services, retail, telecom, manufacturing, infrastructure/construction, healthcare and IT, who pay Quess either a margin per worker deployed or a flat fee, so revenue scales with the number of 'associates' on its books (about 478,000 at March 2026) and profit comes from a thin spread over their wages — FY26 revenue of INR 15,305 crores produced EBITDA of only INR 312 crores and PAT of INR 230 crores, roughly a 2% margin business. Its base is India, where it calls itself the country's largest staffing platform and among the top 5 global staffing firms by headcount, with a smaller overseas arm in the Middle East, Singapore, Malaysia and the Philippines. In earlier years (FY23-FY24) the group was much broader — INR 19,100 crores of revenue in FY24 — also running call centres, payroll processing, facility management, security guarding and job portals, but the later calls report only staffing, overseas and a small digital-platform business.

**The parts of the business:**

- **General Staffing (Workforce Management / Quess Staffing Solutions)** — Supplies large volumes of mostly entry-level and blue-collar workers on Quess's payroll to clients in BFSI, retail, telecom, manufacturing and construction; it is the bulk of the company at INR 13,176 crores of FY26 revenue, about 470,000 of the total headcount, but earns only INR 189 crores of EBITDA.
- **Professional Staffing (including Quess IT Staffing)** — Places skilled IT, digital, technology and consulting people — 71-72% of them into global capability centres of multinationals — and is small in size (INR 930 crores FY26 revenue, ~6,900 people) but the profit engine, with EBITDA of INR 111 crores at over 12% margin, an all-time high.
- **Overseas / International business** — Runs the same staffing model outside India across the Middle East, Singapore, Malaysia and the Philippines, at INR 1,197 crores of FY26 revenue and INR 77 crores of EBITDA (margins above 6%, Middle East at 11%), with 125 new clients added during the year.
- **Digital / product-led platforms (foundit, formerly Monster; Qjobs; WorQ; Zuno; Hamara Jobs)** — Online job and hiring marketplaces — a white-collar job portal, a blue-collar app and an early-career platform — described throughout as an investment-phase business that burns cash rather than earns it (negative EBITDA of INR 24 crores in a single quarter of FY23), now being relaunched as an AI-led blue-collar marketplace.
- **Global Technology Solutions (GTS) — Conneqt and Allsec** — In the FY23-FY24 calls, the customer-service call-centre (BPM/CXM) and debt-collection operations plus Allsec's payroll-processing platform that handled 155 million payslips in one quarter; it was the group's margin-improvement story then, with Conneqt alone crossing INR 400 crores of quarterly revenue, but it is not reported in the FY26 calls.
- **Operating Asset Management (OAM)** — In the FY23-FY24 calls, the facilities-management arm — building upkeep and housekeeping (IFMS), catering and food services, manned and electronic security guarding, and telecom tower infrastructure services — growing revenue 7% with improving margins in FY24, but likewise absent from the FY26 segment reporting.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*
