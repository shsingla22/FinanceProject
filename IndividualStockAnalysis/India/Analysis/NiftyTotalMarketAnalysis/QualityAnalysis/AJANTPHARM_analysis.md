# Ajanta Pharmaceuticals Ltd. (AJANTPHARM) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: PHARMA*

Ajanta Pharma is an India-based drug maker that manufactures and sells generic medicines — copies of off-patent drugs — in India, other emerging markets across Asia and Africa, and the United States. In India and the emerging markets it sells its own branded generics, promoting them to doctors through a field force of about 3,750 medical representatives (in India it focuses on cardiology, eye care, skin and pain medicines), while in the US it sells plain generics to distributors after getting approvals (ANDAs) from the US FDA. It also supplies anti-malarial medicines in bulk to aid agencies that procure for African governments. Revenue comes from product sales across these four channels; in FY2026 total revenue was about Rs. 5,453 crores with net profit above Rs. 1,000 crores, gross margins around 78%, EBITDA margin around 27-28%, and roughly 5% of revenue spent on R&D.

**The parts of the business:**

- **India branded generics (including trade generics)** — Doctor-promoted branded medicines sold in India, concentrated in cardiology (~36% of India branded sales), ophthalmology (~31%), dermatology (~23%) and pain (~10%), with gynaecology and nephrology being newly added; it was about 30-32% of total revenue (Rs. 1,654 crores in FY2026, up 14%), and includes a smaller trade generics piece sold through the trade channel (Rs. 188 crores in FY2026).
- **Asia branded generics (emerging markets)** — Branded generic medicines sold in the Middle East, South-East Asia and Central Asia, largely chronic-disease therapies with 15 new product launches in FY2026; sales were Rs. 1,175 crores in FY2026, roughly flat as Middle East supply-chain disruptions hurt dispatches.
- **Africa branded generics** — Branded generic medicines sold mainly in West and East Africa through its own sales force and new launches; sales were Rs. 861 crores in FY2026, up 15% — together Asia plus Africa branded generics made up about 38-40% of total revenue.
- **US generics** — Plain (unbranded) generic drugs sold to US distributors and customers after FDA approval, where growth depends on new launches and is squeezed by price erosion; it grew 49% to Rs. 1,557 crores in FY2026 on the back of 8 launches, lifting it to about 29% of total revenue and making it the company's fastest-growing part.
- **Africa institution business (anti-malarials)** — Bulk supply of anti-malarial medicines to aid agencies and procurement bodies serving Africa — described by management as lumpy and unpredictable because it depends on donor funding and order flow; it is the smallest part at roughly 3% of revenue (Rs. 160 crores in FY2026).

### The story in depth

Ajanta Pharmaceuticals earns a combined score of 71 out of 100 — a "Strong" four-star rating built from business quality of 70, multibagger-pattern fit of 79 and risk safety of 62 — and its resilience reads as sound. The engine behind all three is a single, consistent model: the company sells branded generic medicines that doctors prescribe and patients pay for, and it grows that business by putting more sales representatives in front of more doctors — 3,750 in India and 2,080 across Asia and Africa. That is why the "Friendly Middleman" pattern is a strong fit, and it is also why the customer-benefit checks score highest of any area (1.75). After direct input costs the company keeps about ₹78 of every ₹100 of sales, and it was able to push through a roughly 7% price increase in India — the kind of pricing customers accept without complaint. Growth has compounded at about 13% a year with little year-to-year wobble, right in the steady 10–15% band the framework rewards most, and market share has been taken from a non-dominant starting point, with its rank in the Indian pharma market improving from 29th to 24th between 2022 and 2026 while growing 13% against the market's 10%.

The management story reinforces the same picture and is where the record is most flattering. The promoter family (Yogesh, Rajesh and Arvind Agrawal) has been unchanged across all five earnings calls, guidance has repeatedly been given and then met — US growth guided as "healthy" and delivered at 46–49%, Africa guidance raised and delivered — and the candor check is full marks because bad news gets volunteered rather than buried: the one-time inventory write-off in 2022, Asia running "modestly below our internal plan," a forex accounting distortion, and freight and raw-material inflation being absorbed rather than passed on. Growth has been entirely organic, with no borrowing beyond working-capital loans taken for interest arbitrage, and the company has explicitly refused to diversify away from what it knows. Spending backs the words: capital spending stepped up from roughly ₹200 crore in FY23 to ₹330 crore in FY26 with ₹400 crore guided for FY27 — of which only ₹150 crore is maintenance — and a new liquid line at Pithampur was commissioned, all while returns on capital employed held at 33–34%. Research spending has stayed at a steady 5–6% of revenue and converted into 8 US launches in 15 months, 26 launches in India and 15 in Asia, which is what makes the innovation and market-share patterns credible rather than merely asserted.

The tension in this business is that the trait producing the best recent numbers is the same trait producing the worst risk readings. The US vertical is now 29% of revenue and was the sole engine of FY26 growth at +49% — the achievement that makes the innovation and share-gain patterns fit — but it is also the source of all three elevated risks. It sells through a concentrated distributor and buying-group channel that management itself describes as a relationship business (stakeholder concentration); its economics get reset every time a new generic approval enters a molecule, with "severe" price erosion of 15–18% in FY22 and an ongoing baseline of 5–8% (new entrants); and the products are largely undifferentiated medicines where the distributor rather than the patient chooses, so 8 launches in 15 months function as a treadmill needed just to stand still (good-enough goods). That split is exactly why the pricing-power pattern only partially fits: real pricing power in India, permanent price decline in the US. The barrier to copying Ajanta — a 5,680-person prescriber-relationship field force built over decades plus its regulatory portfolio — is described as real but rebuildable with money and time, which management concedes, so "cost to replicate" also lands only as a partial fit. Two further items sit on watch rather than alarm: the company has no originator technology of its own for the GLP-1 shift and reached the molecule by in-licensing finished product from Biocon for 26 countries while filing its own generic semaglutide from Q1 FY27; and two channels remain policy-driven, the Africa anti-malarial institution business dependent on aid-agency buying and US growth having been gated for years by FDA facility inspections. On the balance sheet, the clearest single weakness is that about 246 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and the industry-structure area scores zero — a reminder that this is a strong operator in a structurally competitive business, not a protected one. What cushions it all is the four-way revenue split (India 30%, Asia/Africa branded 38%, US 29%, institution 3%), the fragmented base of prescribing doctors, and demand that is chronic-therapy prescription medicine rather than anything cyclical or fashion-driven — both of those risks read low.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

### Stakeholder Concentration — ELEVATED
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** The US vertical — now 29% of revenue and the sole engine of FY26 growth (+49%) — sells through a concentrated US distributor/buying-group channel, which management itself frames as a relationship business ('preferred partner of choice for the distributors and customers'); the GLP-1 emerging-market opportunity rests on a single supplier, Biocon. Against this, India (3,750 MRs, top-25 IPM rank) and Asia/Africa branded businesses are structurally fragmented across doctors and chemists.

> "We remain as a preferred partner of choice for the distributors and customers due to our superior and committed execution." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Four-vertical revenue split (India 30%, Asia/Africa branded 38%, US 29%, institution 3%) and a highly fragmented prescriber base in branded generic markets*

### New Entrants — ELEVATED
*"A new player changes the rules"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** US generics economics are periodically reset by new ANDA approvals entering each molecule — management described 'severe price erosion' of 15-18% in FY22 that structurally impaired US EBITDA, and normalised erosion of 5-8% as the ongoing baseline. Response has been product selection rather than price defence, and the FY26 surge came from launching into new molecules rather than defending old ones.

> "because of the severe price erosion, which last year was 15% to 18% it has adversely impacted the EBITDA margin of the US business" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate selection of complex / barriered ANDAs ('we have been selective in identifying the products which are complex and some other barriers') and continual launch refresh*

- ⚠️ Operating margin drifted down from about 34% to 28% — steady profit erosion, the mark of rising competitive pressure.

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED

## What to watch
