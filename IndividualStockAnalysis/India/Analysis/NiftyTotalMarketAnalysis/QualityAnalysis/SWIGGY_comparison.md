## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 29 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 9 improved, 2 regressed, 18 unchanged. 2 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them). 2 were newly assessable in the recent view.

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Allocation | Neutral (+0.20) | Neutral (+0.33) | +0.13 |
| Return on Capital | Weak (-1.33) | Weak (-0.67) | +0.67 |
| Growth | Neutral (+0.20) | Good (+0.67) | +0.47 |
| Management | Good (+0.80) | Neutral (+0.40) | -0.40 |
| Industry Structure | Weak (-1.33) | Neutral (-0.50) | +0.83 |
| Customer Benefits | Neutral (+0.50) | Neutral (+0.50) | +0.00 |
| Competitive Advantage | Neutral (+0.33) | Good (+0.67) | +0.33 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Poor → Neutral. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.8 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Barriers are demonstrably low and pricing is irrational: new and legacy entrants keep arriving with fresh capital, a large traditional player added 600+ dark stores, and competition is sustained through cart-level discounting and subsidies. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±34.1 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: The industry displays the opposite of rationality — persistent new entry, heavy cart-level discounting and minimum-order-value wars that management itself labels irrational and expects to persist.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Poor → Neutral. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹-14 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±2.8 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹-14 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±34.1 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: Switching costs are minimal — retention has to be manufactured through wallet incentives, Maxxsaver habit-forming and private label 'stickiness', which is precisely the admission that lock-in does not exist naturally. | The long view had said: Switching costs are essentially zero by management's own account, with customers hopping between platforms on price and no captive aftermarket or regional lock-in.
- **Network effects** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Good. Now: A real but modest two-sided effect exists — scale on the platform pulls brand participation and rising ad monetisation, which funds better consumer economics — yet it has not prevented competitors from scaling alongside. | The long view had said: There are genuine two-sided dynamics — restaurant partners actively seeking to join Bolt, brand monetisation improving with scale — but they are not self-reinforcing enough to prevent customer churn to rivals.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Expansion is domestic and well-sequenced — new dark-store geographies and Tier 2/Tier 3 warehousing to shorten the middle mile — but there is no international or premium-brand travel story. | The long view had said: Expansion is domestic-only and now deliberately paused — 127-130 cities with >90% demand coverage and no white-space additions planned — so execution has been competent but the further geographic runway is deferred rather than being worked.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Clear share capture with a long runway: three straight quarters of 100%+ quick-commerce GOV growth well ahead of the original 50-60% plan, and food delivery GOV +20.5% with MTUs +22%, though it is bought amid extremely high competitive intensity. | The long view had said: Food delivery is gaining ground (20.5% GOV and 22% MTU growth, above guidance), but quick commerce growth is explicitly decelerating and running well below Blinkit, so share capture is split between the two businesses.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Food delivery is a rational duopoly already generating a ~INR 1,000 crore annual profit run-rate, but quick commerce is a crowded multi-player fight including a large traditional entrant, so the structure is split. | The long view had said: Quick commerce is not a stable oligopoly — new entrants keep arriving and pricing is unstable, while the end customer has full negotiating power via discounts and app-switching.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: The real donators are offline retail and non-grocery traditional channels, which the Quick India Movement was designed to raid by pulling new categories and household wallet share onto the platform. | The long view had said: Weak, cash-burning unlisted players discounting heavily are potential eventual donators, but at present they are taking share rather than giving it, and Swiggy itself had to close SNACC.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: Capex is clearly growth-directed — dark-store network plus a warehousing footprint that more than doubled in four quarters to structurally cut middle-mile cost — but incremental ROIC is unproven since quick commerce is still contribution-margin negative and only guided to neutrality by the June 2026 quarter. | The long view had said: Capex is unambiguously growth-oriented — dark-store network scaled to 4.3mn sq ft across 127-130 cities and warehousing capacity more than doubled in four quarters — but incremental returns are still negative, with quick commerce only guided to contribution-margin zero by AMJ'26.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Growth is organic with no large M&A and the balance sheet carries ~$2bn cash rather than leverage, but discipline is offset by heavy loss-funded competitive spend and an equity raise that dilutes holders. | The long view had said: Organic-only growth, no acquisitions, shutdown of the failing SNACC line, and a repeated refusal to fund unprofitable growth show real capital discipline, tempered by the sheer scale of ongoing cash burn.
- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Management gives dated, falsifiable guidance and repeats it, volunteers headwinds (full-quarter cost drag, Maxxsaver take-rate dilution, MTU headwinds from the low-AOV base) and openly declines to answer where data is competitively sensitive rather than obfuscating. | The long view had said: Consistently candid across the trajectory — owning the INR14/order contribution deterioration and back-ended store adds in FY25, flagging that a strong AOV print was seasonality-driven rather than structural, and disclosing the SNACC shutdown costs.

*Unchanged: Cost of working capital; Advertisement and promotion; Distribution advantage; Return on capital (headline); Independent, long-term and tenacious; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Structural end-market growth; Halo effect (caution); Assurance benefits; Asset turn; Intangible benefits; Security by obscurity; Technology advantage; Distribution to shareholders; Mini-monopolies; Convenience benefits; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Out of limelight.*

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these — identical by construction): Persistence of growth (Poor); Research and development (Good).*

**Newly assessable in the recent view:**

- **Cyclical market growth** — Good: Not an end-market cycle but a clear profit-cycle trough: management states contribution losses peaked, delivered sequential margin improvement, and reiterates contribution-margin breakeven by the June 2026 quarter — an identifiable upturn ahead.
- **Merger and acquisition** — Neutral: No acquisitions in the window at all — the only portfolio action is monetising a minority stake (Rapido), so there is neither bolt-on discipline to reward nor empire-building to penalise.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Swiggy is a well-known consumer brand now stretching into own labels (Noice, Triply) and marketing events like the Quick India Movement, but the calls show brand strength that still requires heavy ongoing marketing spend to acquire and retain users, with no premium pricing demonstrated. (The long view had said: Swiggy has a decade-old consumer franchise it stretches into adjacencies under one name (Instamart, Bolt, 99 store, EatRight, Noice, Toing), but there is no premium pricing or heritage-led loyalty — marketing spend is acquisition discounting and management itself says customers hop platforms for price.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: The four FY26 calls discuss GOV, AOV, contribution margin, dark stores and ad monetisation, but never license fees, AMC/subscription attach rates, renewal rates or deferred revenue as a funding source. Evidence is silent. (The long view had said: Across all eight calls management discusses orders, MTUs and take rates but never a licence/subscription/AMC stream, attach rates or renewals; and the working-capital dynamic runs the wrong way — the cycle flipped from negative to positive and cash is now tied up in brand receivables rather than customer advances funding the business.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Growth is emphatic — three straight quarters of 100%+ quick-commerce GOV growth (Q2 FY26), food delivery GOV +20.5% and MTUs +22% in Q3 FY26 — but quick commerce share is still being bought below the contribution line, which is the disqualifier for this pattern until CM neutrality (guided for the June 2026 quarter) is actually delivered. (The long view had said: Food delivery has gained share consistently (19.2% year-on-year in Q3FY25 rising to 20.5% GOV and 22% MTU growth by Q3FY26, above its own 18-20% guide), but in quick commerce growth decelerated below Blinkit and management declines to claim share, choosing margin over volume.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Cost to Replicate (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: The core proposition is largely undifferentiated delivery of the same national brands, so customers can be bought with price — hence recurring cart-level discounting, wallet subsidies and 'discretionary' competitive investment across all four calls — and by Q4 FY26 management is explicitly 'doubling down on differentiation' and insisting its private label is not a commoditized value play, which is the classic defence of a premium under attack. (The long view had said: Quick commerce sells largely identical third-party brands, so the proposition is substitutable and price-led: management admits competitor irrationality is causing customers to switch platforms with no loyalty, that it had to match no-fee and lower minimum-order constructs, and by May 2026 is building private labels and differentiated assortment precisely because the core offer is commoditised. Contribution margin has been the repeated casualty.)

**That worsened:**

- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Every call in the window discusses structurally different entrants — a well-funded competitor raising capital, a large traditional retailer adding 600+ dark stores, and 'both new and legacy players' attracting investment — and the response is exactly the margin-investment pattern the risk describes: heightened marketing spend, cart-level discounting and wallet subsidies, with a QIP raised partly for 'strategic reserves' against this competition. (The long view had said: Every call from Feb 2025 through May 2026 is dominated by structurally different, deep-pocketed entrants in quick commerce; management repeatedly concedes heightened intensity, new players still entering, and that contribution losses widened because of competitive user-acquisition and discounting — including responding with no-fee and lower-minimum-order constructs against unlisted rivals.)

*Unchanged: Technological Innovation (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Not comparable: Government Dependency (NOT ASSESSED → WATCH — could not be assessed on the long view).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 2 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 2 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
