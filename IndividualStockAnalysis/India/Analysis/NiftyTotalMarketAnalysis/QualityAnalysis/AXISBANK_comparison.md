## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 29 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 7 improved, 3 regressed, 19 unchanged. 1 was newly assessable in the recent view.

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Allocation | Neutral (+0.25) | Neutral (+0.20) | -0.05 |
| Return on Capital | Weak (-1.00) | Neutral (+0.00) | +1.00 |
| Growth | Good (+1.00) | Good (+0.80) | -0.20 |

| Customer Benefits | Good (+1.00) | Good (+1.25) | +0.25 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Weak → Neutral. Now: A bank is structurally balance-sheet-heavy; the offsetting positives are best-in-peer fee-to-assets and steadily falling cost-to-assets, but consolidated ROA of 1.51-1.64% is mid-pack rather than evidence of an asset-light moat. | The long view had said: Structurally asset-heavy balance-sheet model with consolidated ROA of only 1.46-1.64% and growth requiring continuous capital and branch build-out, with no offsetting pricing moat as NIM fell 29bps YoY.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Large Indian private banks operate as a licensed, concentrated group; Axis was able to cut savings and term deposit rates early and still gain deposit share, indicating customers have limited pricing leverage. | The long view had said: Indian banking is a licensed few-player industry, but deposit competition is intense and customers hold pricing power, as management repeatedly concedes that system deposit growth constrains loan growth.
- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: Premiumisation is producing measurable image-driven benefits — NTB average balances up 53% YoY on premium-led sourcing and Burgundy AUM up 14% YoY — but core lending economics show no pricing power given NIM compression. | The long view had said: Burgundy/Burgundy Private is a genuine premium brand with award recognition and 32% 5-year CAGR, but it has not translated into pricing power at the franchise level where margins and fee growth are both compressing.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Corporate and transaction banking show real switching costs, with ~95% of eligible clients migrated onto the Neo platform and stickiness deliberately built through ecosystem flows and liquidity/collections integration. | The long view had said: Real but partial switching costs exist in wholesale transaction banking and corporate salary via Neo and API embedding (4.3 lakh corporate, 3.1 lakh business customers), while the retail deposit franchise faces open competition and is admittedly still not granular enough.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: Axis is a top-tier, heavily covered Indian private bank that actively courts visibility through awards, league tables and flagship product launches — the opposite of an ignored niche. | The long view had said: Axis is one of India's largest listed private banks with heavily attended quarterly and separate media calls, extensive sell-side and press coverage — the opposite of an ignored niche.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: No monopoly-like ownership of the customer's mind in core banking — Axis is a challenger to HDFC/ICICI — with the only near-dominant position being UPI payer PSP share, which is a low-monetisation, regulator-mandated interoperable rail. | The long view had said: No monopoly-like hold anywhere; even in its strongest franchises Axis is a ranked competitor (4th in cards, 4th in wealth, 2nd in merchant acquiring) rather than an owner of the customer's mind.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Regulatory licensing keeps new entrants out and pricing behaviour looks rational — cost of funds fell 39-46bps YoY without share loss, and growth was steered to high-RAROC clients rather than bought with price. | The long view had said: Licensing creates high formal entry barriers, but pricing has been anything but rational — a system-wide deposit rate war compressed NIM 29bps and management concedes ceding deposit quality (outflow rate) to gain share.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Out of limelight** (Management): Neutral → Weak. Now: Prepared remarks devote substantial airtime to award-collecting — FICCI CSR, SKOCH, Euromoney, TIME, Top Employers, ATD, BFSI summit — which is promotional rather than substance-over-celebrity communication. | The long view had said: Management is not personality-driven, but calls devote substantial airtime to award citations (Euromoney, Trailblazer, TIME, ATD, Kantar) rather than pure substance.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Excellent → Good. Now: Technology depth is real and multi-product — AXIOM AI operating model, GenAI assistant Adi live across 72 products, Kaleidoscope CX engine, and the only ISO 42001-certified BFSI firm globally — but the customer-benefit edge over peers is not yet shown in economics. | The long view had said: A deep, multi-product technology edge built over five years — cloud-native microservices, 410+ APIs, first Indian bank ISO-certified for AWS/Azure cloud security, only ISO 42001-certified BFSI firm globally, and 39% UPI payer PSP share.
- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Indian credit penetration and digital payments are in secular expansion, evidenced by UPI volumes, agentic payments rollout and management's medium-term thesis of growing faster than industry, though the end market is cyclical as well as structural. | The long view had said: End market is in secular expansion — MSME formalisation via GST, rural digital penetration and credit under-penetration in a growing Indian economy — which management has cited consistently from 2022 through 2026.

*Unchanged: Distribution to shareholders; Gain in market share; Halo effect (caution); Assurance benefits; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Network effects; Convenience benefits; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Geographical expansion; Merger and acquisition; Share donators; Cyclical market growth; Distribution advantage; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Disciplined stewards; Research and development; Candor; People matter (talent development).*

**Newly assessable in the recent view:**

- **Advertisement and promotion** — Neutral: No A&P spend disclosure in any of the four calls; only a passing reference to an improving brand consideration score, so no basis to judge brand-building efficiency either way.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 0 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared.

*Foundation test: FAIL on the long view, FAIL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Across the four calls management flagged self-inflicted bad news early (tightening technical slippage recognition criteria, disclosed in Q4FY25 and owned through Q1FY26's hit to NIM/credit cost), voluntarily enhanced standard-asset provisioning in Q4FY26, previously chose to fully amortise the Citi intangibles to protect dividend capacity, and delivered the promised positive operating jaws and cost-to-assets decline in every quarter of the year. (The long view had said: Strong marks on conservatism and delivery — GPS commitments surpassed, 7 straight quarters of >18% ROE by FY24, gratuity provisioned since Q3FY21 ahead of the labour code, a voluntary Rs 2,001 crore standard-asset buffer stress-tested at $150 oil, and candid admission that deposit quality still lags. Against that: management stonewalled questions on the abrupt exit of a group executive, and FY26 ROE fell to 13.59% from 19.29% in FY24.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Liability-side pricing power is evident — early, proactive cuts in savings and term deposit rates without losing growth (deposits still +14-15% YoY) — and SME yields were 'improved through data driven credit decisions'; but NIM fell from 3.80% to 3.73% over the year, so asset-side pricing power is not demonstrated. (The long view had said: The trajectory is price deflation, not pricing power: FY26 NIM fell to 3.69% (down 29 bps YOY) versus 4.07% in FY24, NII grew only 3% YOY, and Q4FY26 fee growth decelerated to 4%; management concedes it is 'optimizing for NII in the short term' by shifting mix to lower-yield wholesale.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

*Not comparable: Toll Roads (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 0 worsened, 7 unchanged. Financial resilience: Unknown on the long view, Unknown on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: As a bank, pricing and balance-sheet structure are substantially set by the regulator — RBI rate cuts drive NIM and cost of funds, LCR/SLR/PSL are mandated (Bharat Banking explicitly run to 'aid the PSL metrics'), and UPI economics are policy-determined; management repeatedly attributes the operating outlook to policy actions rather than its own pricing. (The long view had said: Core economics are set by the regulator: ~73% of loans are floating and repo-linked so RBI rate cuts drove FY26 NIM down 29 bps, PSL mandates force low-yielding RIDF bonds and PSLC costs (a named driver of opex), and RBI directives dictate AIF and standard-asset provisioning. Government business (Army, Navy, Police MoUs, nodal agency mandates) is actively courted as a growth channel.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Technological Innovation (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
