# Kaveri Seed Co (KSCL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Kaveri Seed Company Limited (KSCL) is an Indian company that breeds, produces and sells packaged hybrid and research seeds to farmers — mainly cotton, rice (both hybrid and 'selection'/research paddy), maize, bajra/millet, sunflower, mustard and vegetables. It makes money by selling branded seed packets through a dealer/distributor channel across Indian states such as Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Punjab, Haryana, Bihar and Uttar Pradesh, plus a small but fast-growing export business into international markets where it is registering its seeds. Its own in-house R&D breeds the hybrids it sells (management says it has 200+ hybrids in its portfolio and spends roughly 5%-10% of revenue on R&D plus capex), so profits depend on getting new products accepted while controlling the cost of growing the seed crop, which it cannot always pass on to farmers because cotton seed prices are government-controlled. The business is highly seasonal and monsoon-linked — the June quarter is by far the biggest — with FY26 revenue of about Rs. 1,304 crores, net profit of about Rs. 283 crores, and a debt-free, cash-on-books balance sheet.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Cotton (hybrid BT cotton seed)** — Sells hybrid cotton seed packets at a government-capped MRP; historically the single largest crop at more than 35% of the portfolio, but it shrank sharply (volumes -35% in Q1 FY25) due to lower acreage, illegal/unapproved BT seed competition and high production cost, and management expects a recovery led by new hybrids whose share of cotton volumes jumped from 10.3% to 30.05% in FY26.
- **Hybrid rice** — Hybrid paddy seed sold in markets like Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand; a core growth crop with FY26 revenues up 18.3% even though Punjab — described as one of its largest hybrid rice markets — imposed restrictions.
- **Selection rice / research paddy** — Non-hybrid 'selection' or research paddy varieties bred in-house; a steadily growing segment with FY26 volumes up about 2.4% and revenues up 9.8%, and research paddy volumes up 51% in Q3 FY26.
- **Maize** — Hybrid maize seed, called the key growth driver of FY26 with volumes up 18.8% and revenues up 40.2%; new products rose from 40% to 52% of maize volumes, and demand swings with commercial maize grain prices and rabi/spring sowing in states like Bihar, UP, Punjab and Madhya Pradesh.
- **Bajra / millet** — Pearl millet seed where new products already make up the bulk of volumes (75.8% in Q1 FY25), with growth expected in summer and rainy millet in Gujarat, Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh.
- **Vegetable seeds** — A smaller but consistently growing line covering crops such as tomato, beetroot, ridge gourd, sponge gourd and marigold; FY26 volumes were up modestly (about 1.5% in Q3) with revenues up 11.4%, and management groups it with rice and maize as a best-performing segment.
- **Other field crops (sunflower, mustard)** — Newer additions to the portfolio — sunflower volumes grew 94% and mustard 64% in Q3 FY26, and management says mustard is expected to become a sizeable business over the next couple of years.
- **Exports** — Selling seeds into international markets after registering products there over the last five years; still a small base but the fastest-growing part, with revenue up roughly 90% in FY26 (86% in Q3, 76% in Q4) and expected to keep growing.
- **R&D and breeding** — The in-house research engine that creates all the hybrids the company sells — it does not use government-released seeds, but accesses germplasm from universities, government bodies and international institutes — and management calls it the backbone of the business, absorbing roughly 5%-10% of revenue including capex, alongside about Rs. 40-50 crores a year of plant/processing capex.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

The heart of this business is its breeding programme, and it is the one thing the records rate without reservation. Research spending has compounded at 15-20% a year to 5-10% of revenue, funded alongside a new biotech and genomics centre, and it visibly turns into products: newly launched hybrids went from 40% to 52% of maize volumes, 67.67% to 75.79% in bajra and 10.3% to 30.05% in cotton by FY26. That is why the Innovation Dominance pattern is the only STRONG FIT in the eleven-pattern screen, and why the framework scores research and development, and stewardship, at the top of its range. The stewardship score rests on the same foundation: no debt and net cash throughout, growth entirely home-grown with no acquisitions in four years, and a promoter management team present on every call from 2022 to 2026 that openly admitted misjudging cotton acreage and inventory. Alongside this, a patient five-year effort to register seeds in Africa and Southeast Asia is now compounding, with exports up about 90% in FY26, and real share capture in maize to 10-11% and in hybrid rice.

The same breeding engine is also the company's main defence against its biggest named threat, and that is the tightest link in the whole record. The Good Enough Goods risk is rated ELEVATED because once a hybrid ages, cheaper rivals sell equivalent genetics and Kaveri has to discount — realisations were cut in FY23, cost inflation could not be passed on in FY25 and FY26, and gross margins have ground down. The only thing holding that back is the constant stream of new hybrids and the long breeding gestation a new entrant cannot shortcut, which is exactly what the Cost to Replicate pattern describes and exactly why that pattern is only rated PARTIAL: the time barrier is real but it demonstrably does not stop well-funded smaller organised players or illegal BT cotton seed from taking price and volume. Cotton is where several findings converge into one problem. Its selling price is set by government, which is why the framework marks pricing power negatively, why Toll Roads and Low Price Plus are outright NO FIT, why Government Dependency is the single HIGH RISK channel, and why Market Share Gainers is held to PARTIAL — maize and exports gained while cotton share went backwards, with a 35% volume fall in FY25. The licensed genetically-modified trait platform the cotton business sits on is not owned by the company, and approval for the next version has been described as a year or two away since 2022 and was still moving very slowly by May 2026.

The prudence that earns the top management score also produces the two worst numbers in the framework, and the record is explicit that these are the same decision seen from two sides. Inventory and production were deliberately built ahead of an expected cotton upturn; that counter-cyclical build is cited as evidence of discipline, and it is also why roughly 396 days of cash now sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from farmers, why the headline cash return on capital came in at zero for the year, and why the FY26 buyback was skipped with management conceding there is no formal payout policy. Growth capital spending is modest and steady at about Rs 40-50 crore a year plus Rs 99 crore of work in progress in FY26, funded from cash rather than borrowing. Netted out, the quality areas split cleanly: competitive advantage and management at the top of the scale, capital allocation, customer benefits and growth in the middle, and return on capital and industry structure below zero. Risk resilience is judged SOUND overall, with five of eight channels at WATCH or LOW, because demand is functional and repurchased every season across 200-plus hybrids, and because crop shifts move within the company's own portfolio rather than away from it. The combined rating works out to 50 out of 100 — 45% on business quality at 58, 30% on multibagger fit at 33, and 25% on risk safety at 58 — which lands it at Decent, or three stars: a genuinely well-run innovator whose economics are capped by a price-controlled core crop and a very long cash cycle.

### Pricing Power — PARTIAL
*"Raises prices, keeps the customers"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (1 of 2 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Genuine pricing came through in FY26 (price-led revenue growth, maize revenue +40% on +19% volume) and in Aug 2024 for maize/rice, but the four-year trajectory shows the opposite as often: 2022 discounting, government-capped cotton prices, gross margins sliding from ~49.5% to 47%, and FY27 guided with no price increase at all.

> "This year, we may not be able to increase the prices, like how we have increased last year" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Market Share Gainers — PARTIAL
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 1 check supporting).

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Cotton seed MRP is set by government, and across FY23-FY26 management repeatedly says it absorbed rising production cost because it could not raise price ('the selling price got increased by only Rs. 10'), consciously losing sales; add trait approval sitting with the regulator, Punjab restricting hybrid rice, the pending Seeds Bill 2025, subsidy sales at lower realisations, an unrecovered receivable from a government corporation, and three pending tax appeals on the agricultural-income exemption.

> "we were not able to pass it on to the farmer because it is controlled by government and that is one of the reason where we have lost the sales" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Diversification away from price-controlled cotton toward uncontrolled maize, rice, vegetables, mustard and exports (exports +90% in FY26) has reduced, not removed, the policy-driven share*

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** This is the clearest structural threat: once hybrids age, cheaper players sell equivalent genetics and Kaveri must discount — realisations were cut in FY23, cost inflation could not be passed on in FY25/FY26, and profitability has ground down (gross margin from ~49.5% to 47%, EBITDA margin from ~28-29% in FY20-21 toward ~24%, Q3FY26 gross margin at multi-year lows).
