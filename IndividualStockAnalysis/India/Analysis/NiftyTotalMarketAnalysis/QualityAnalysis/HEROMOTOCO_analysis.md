# Hero MotoCorp Ltd. (HEROMOTOCO) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: AUTOMOBILE*

Hero MotoCorp makes and sells two-wheelers — motorcycles and scooters — mainly to everyday retail buyers in India through a large dealer network, and the calls say it has been the world's largest motorcycle and scooter manufacturer for 25 straight years, dispatching 6.1 million vehicles in calendar 2025. Most of its money comes from selling petrol-engine (ICE) bikes and scooters, ranging from mass-market commuter models like Splendor, Passion and Glamour up to premium bikes in the 400-500cc range built under a partnership with Harley-Davidson; on top of that it earns a high-margin stream from selling spare parts, accessories and merchandise to the huge base of Hero vehicles already on Indian roads. Smaller but growing pieces are electric scooters under the VIDA brand and exports to overseas markets. In FY26 it reported revenue of about ₹46,830 crore, EBITDA of ₹6,871 crore and profit of ₹5,268 crore, with the ICE business running at roughly a 17% EBITDA margin and the EV business still absorbing investment.

**The parts of the business:**

- **India Business Unit (ICE motorcycles and scooters)** — The core business — petrol-engine commuter motorcycles (Splendor, Passion, Glamour, Super Splendor, Xtreme 125) and scooters (Destini, Xoom) sold across India; it drives almost all revenue and profit, with ICE EBITDA margin at about 17% in FY26 and scooters growing 48% year-on-year in the March 2026 quarter.
- **Premium segment** — Higher-priced, larger motorcycles including Karizma, Mavrick 440 and the Harley-Davidson 400-500cc range made and sold under partnership; management calls it a segment where Hero is under-represented and is investing heavily in launches, brand building and separate 'Premia' dealerships, with Harley-Davidson range volumes up 26% year-on-year in Q4 FY26.
- **Parts, Accessories & Merchandise (PAM)** — Sells replacement parts, accessories and branded merchandise to owners of the large installed base of Hero vehicles; it was ₹1,061 crore (12.6% of revenue) in one quarter of FY23, has roughly doubled in size over three to four years, keeps growing in double digits, and Hero is spending over ₹700 crore on a second global parts centre in south India to expand it.
- **Emerging Mobility Business Unit / EV (VIDA)** — Electric scooters sold under the VIDA brand plus the dealer and charging infrastructure behind them (180+ dealers across 120+ cities as of FY24); still loss-making and a drag on group margin — ₹220 crore of investment in Q4 FY26 alone — but volumes grew 2.5x year-on-year and capacity is being doubled.
- **Global business** — Exports of two-wheelers to overseas markets; management describes it as a growth pillar where Hero is under-represented, citing a 7.2% market share (up 230 bps) in Q4 FY24 and 41% year-on-year dispatch growth in Q4 FY26.
- **Associate company investments** — Minority stakes in other mobility companies — Ather (electric two-wheelers) and Euler Motors — described by the CEO as bold strategic moves rather than a day-to-day operating business.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Hero MotoCorp earns its quality marks on the plumbing of the business rather than on growth. Every ₹100 of capital in the business threw off ₹37 of operating cash this year, ₹15 of every ₹100 of sales is operating profit, and ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.42 of sales — an asset-light business that also gets paid by customers about 40 days before it has to pay suppliers, so the working capital is funded by the customer rather than by the shareholder. Those numbers are held up by two things the checks score highest: an unmatched distribution reach of roughly 1,000 primary dealers and about 6,000 touchpoints, described as the hardest-to-replicate asset in the industry and named by the incoming CEO as the source of leadership; and a near-monopoly position in the customer's mind in the Deluxe 100cc segment, where Splendor holds 91% share, the highest since FY18. Margins have barely moved over the years, within about ±1.6 points, which the framework reads as rational pricing and few new entrants in the core. Sales, though, have compounded at only about 5% a year, and the company is the opposite of undiscovered — the world's largest two-wheeler maker for 25 straight years, covered by every major brokerage on these calls, which is the one check scored firmly negative.

The multibagger screen finds the same three assets and little else, which is why it scores only 41 out of 100 despite business quality of 69. Just one pattern is a strong fit: Low Price Plus, where mass-commuter scale sits alongside an explicit and repeatedly-cited cost programme (LEAP savings, bill-of-material and value engineering, discretionary spend cuts). Two more are likely fits and both trace directly back to the quality checks — the parts, accessories and merchandise annuity, which doubled in three to four years to ₹5,387cr in FY24 and a record ₹1,673cr in Q3FY26, is simply the cash value of the largest installed vehicle park in India that the mini-monopoly check credits; and brand strength is the decades of Splendor, HF Deluxe, Glamour and Karizma heritage that produce that same 91% share. Seven of the eleven patterns land as only partial, and the reasons are consistent: the retail reformat and Premia stores are real forward movement toward the customer but are still a channel upgrade rather than a full move downstream; recent share gains are genuine but follow years of erosion analysts pressed on in FY24; and pricing power is capped because management concedes, in Aug-22 and again in May-26, that price increases lag cost inflation. Corporate culture is likewise mixed — no pandemic layoffs, a consistent 70%-plus payout, a conservative balance sheet and ₹9,395cr of operating cash in FY26, set against heavy leadership churn, which is the same weakness that drags the Management quality area down to 0.17 while Return on Capital sits at 1.67.

The risk review comes out SOUND overall, but the single elevated risk is the direct flip side of the greatest strength. Everything that makes Hero efficient — a manufacturing base and a dealer network built around commuter ICE motorcycles — is what makes the shift to electric powertrains a large-scale platform change, and the company has been in a multi-year loss-making build-out with an EBITDA drag of roughly ₹208-252 crore. The same dependence on the 100cc commuter core shows up again in shifting consumer preferences, where management openly concedes growth has migrated to categories where the company is relatively underrepresented, and in new entrants, where EV-native players arrived with a different product and cost model and management answers defensively on price gaps. The admission that pricing lags cost inflation, which held Pricing Power to a partial fit, is the same admission that keeps cyclicality on watch: EBITDA fell to 11.2% in Q1FY23 on steel and aluminium inflation before recovering to 14-14.7%, with FY26 guidance hedged again as aluminium and precious metals spiked. Two further watch-level risks sit outside the company's control — demand and EV economics are policy-driven, with the September 2025 GST cut credited for the volume upcycle and EV profitability explicitly dependent on PLI, and the premium push leans materially on the licensed Harley-Davidson X440 platform. Against that, the risks scored LOW are the ones the quality checks predict: fashion risk is minimal because demand is functional repeat-purchase transport, and premium erosion is absent because Xtec variants priced 7-10% higher saw demand far above expectations and per-vehicle price rises of ₹850, ₹1,200, ₹300 and about 2% were absorbed. Combined, this is a 61 out of 100, Decent — a high-return, cash-generative franchise whose defences are strongest exactly where the market is growing slowest.

*"Out-invented by the industry"*

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

*Silver lining: Flow/consumable PAM aftermarket business at ~13% of revenue plus largest installed 'park size'; countercyclical INR1,500 crore FY27 capex and continued brand investment while absorbing cost inflation*

## What to watch

- Whether the EV build-out drag of ₹208-252 crore of EBITDA narrows — the Technological Innovation risk is the only ELEVATED one and it directly contradicts the asset-light and return-on-capital checks.
- Whether price increases start keeping pace with input costs rather than lagging, as management conceded in Aug-22 and May-26 — this single fact caps the Pricing Power pattern at partial and keeps Cyclicality on watch.
- Whether the parts, accessories and merchandise annuity keeps compounding past the record ₹1,673cr quarter — it is the evidence base for the Recurring Revenue pattern and for the mini-monopoly check.
- Whether recent share gains hold — four consecutive quarters of entry-segment gains, scooters to ~7% and VIDA to 10.8% — since the Market Share Gainers pattern is only partial because those gains follow years of erosion.
- Whether leadership churn settles under the new CEO, and whether the Harley-Davidson X440 licence is renewed or replaced by own-brand premium — these are the specific reasons Corporate Culture is partial, the Management quality area sits at 0.17, and Stakeholder Concentration is on watch.
- Whether demand holds if the September 2025 GST unlock fades or PLI support for EVs changes, the two policy levers named under Government Dependency.
