# Wipro Ltd. (WIPRO) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: IT*

Wipro is a global technology services company headquartered in India that gets paid by large companies to build, run and modernise their IT — applications, cloud, data, engineering, cybersecurity, business operations and, increasingly, AI systems. It sells mainly through multi-year outsourcing and transformation contracts (management tracks 'order booking' and 'large deals', e.g. $3.4 billion of bookings and 13 large deals in the June 2026 quarter), plus consulting work that leads into bigger delivery engagements. Revenue is essentially all from its 'IT Services' segment — about $10.5 billion for FY26, with operating margins in the 16%–17.5% range — earned by charging clients for the work of a very large delivery workforce (nearly 250,000 people, presence in over 66 countries as of 2022). It is listed in India (NSE/BSE) and on the NYSE, sells across the Americas, Europe and Asia-Pacific/Middle East/Africa, and roughly two-thirds of its economic interest is pledged to the Azim Premji Foundation for philanthropy.

**The parts of the business:**

- **IT Services (the reported business segment)** — The single revenue-reporting segment covering essentially all of Wipro's business — application development and maintenance, infrastructure and operations, engineering, cloud, cybersecurity and business process work — at $2.61 billion in the June 2026 quarter and $10.5 billion for full-year FY26.
- **Americas 1 (Strategic Market Unit)** — One of four geographic 'markets' Wipro is organised around, covering sectors such as healthcare, consumer, technology and communications; it grew about 2.9% year-on-year in the March 2026 quarter but was flattish by June 2026.
- **Americas 2 (Strategic Market Unit)** — The second Americas market unit, weighted towards banking, financial services and insurance; it has been the weakest region lately, declining 6.7% year-on-year in Q4 FY26 and 7.3% year-on-year in Q1 FY27 on delayed deal ramp-ups and client-specific issues.
- **Europe (Strategic Market Unit)** — The European market unit, with traction called out in the UK, Germany and the Nordics in banking/financial services and technology, offset by soft energy, manufacturing and resources demand; grew 6% year-on-year in Q1 FY27.
- **APMEA (Asia-Pacific, Middle East, Africa Strategic Market Unit)** — The smallest-discussed but fastest-growing market unit (up 13.5% year-on-year in Q1 FY27), boosted by Southeast Asia and by the Olam Group deal expected to exceed $1 billion in contract value with $800 million of committed spend — one of Wipro's largest engagements there.
- **Capco** — Wipro's banking, financial services and insurance consulting arm — its largest-ever acquisition — which management repeatedly flags as a growth driver (up 3.2% quarter-on-quarter and 6.9% year-on-year in Q2 FY25) and as the front end that pulls in bigger BFSI transformation work.
- **Other consulting arms (Rizing, Designit, Edgile, LeanSwift, Ampion, CAS, Mindsprint)** — A set of acquired consulting and domain specialists — for example Rizing for SAP and Designit for design — that Wipro combines on large deals such as an SAP S/4HANA transformation for a transportation and logistics client; Mindsprint was acquired and integrated in the June 2026 quarter.
- **FullStride Cloud Services** — Wipro's cloud services business, backed by a stated $1 billion multi-year investment, which management describes as sitting at the core of most client transformation projects.
- **Wipro Intelligence / WINGS platform** — The AI delivery capability and platform used inside client engagements — agentic AI, automation and 'AI-powered digital command centres' applied to finance and procurement, pharmacovigilance, healthcare enrolment and application management — pitched as improving service quality while cutting client operating costs.
- **AI-native business and platforms unit** — A newly launched unit with its own leadership and operating model, meant to push Wipro beyond selling services alone towards 'services-as-a-software' — building AI industry platforms and incubating AI-led businesses alongside Wipro Ventures and partners, forming what management calls a 'dual engine' with core services.
- **Industry sectors (BFSI, Health, Consumer, Technology & Communications, EMR)** — Wipro also reports revenue by client industry; technology and communications has been the standout (up 10.4% year-on-year in Q4 FY26) while BFSI, health, consumer and EMR (energy, manufacturing, resources) have been flat to declining.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

The quality work and the rating tell the same story from two directions: Wipro is a well-run, financially safe company that is losing ground in its market. The strongest marks are all about how the money is handled rather than how the business is growing — ₹19 of operating profit on every ₹100 of sales, with margins moving only about 1.5 points either way in a year, and a shareholder payout policy that has been consistently over-delivered (88% of profit returned over three years against a 70% minimum, $1.3bn of dividends in FY26, and the largest buyback the company has ever done at ₹15,000 crore). That discipline shows up again as the positives inside the Corporate Culture pattern — no debt, net cash, and cash from operations running at 112.6% of reported profit — and it is the main reason the risk review comes out at 70 out of 100 and the company is judged financially sound. But the same scorecard puts Industry Structure at the bottom (-1.0) and marks share loss (-2), no pricing power (-2), and being the company others take share from (-2). The 44 out of 100 overall is simply those two halves averaging out: strong returns on capital, weak position in the market.

The patterns screen explains why the good financials do not add up to a compounding story: not one of the eleven patterns is a clear fit. Brand Strength is the closest, and it fails on exactly the evidence the strengths are missing — a 77-year history and real institutional standing are there, but the proof the pattern requires is charging more and winning share, and neither is happening. Four patterns are outright misses, and they cluster around the same fact. Toll Roads misses because IT services is a big, visible line in a client's budget that clients actively negotiate down. Pricing Power misses because management never discusses raising prices; it sells cost savings instead, and concedes that AI-driven productivity gains push prices down. Market Share Gainers misses because revenue from IT services fell 1.6% in FY26, fourth-quarter order bookings fell 13.9% against the year before, and one Americas unit fell 6.7%, while analysts kept pointing out that competitors were doing well in the very same sectors and regions. Cost to Replicate misses because the assets on display — people, partnerships, acquisitions, AI platforms — are things competitors are building at the same time. The one pattern that partly works, Low Price Plus, works for an uncomfortable reason: better staff utilisation, more offshore work, a younger workforce mix, fixed-price contracts, overhead cuts and acquisition savings held margins near 17% through a year when revenue was shrinking, and headcount fell by 2,500 in the first quarter of FY27. In other words, the profitability strength and the pricing weakness are the same trait seen from two sides — Wipro protects margin by taking cost out, not by being worth more.

The risk review then takes that same trait and turns it into the main threat. Technology change is the only risk rated elevated: across the later calls AI is discussed as a fundamental reset of how services are sold, analysts ask directly whether investors are right to fear that the existing book of work will be eroded, and management acknowledges the productivity gains while pivoting to an AI-native unit. Three of the five watch-level risks are downstream of the same thing — buyers shifting from paying for hours and headcount toward paying for outcomes, demand that is openly commoditised because cost reduction and vendor consolidation are named as the dominant deal themes from FY25 through FY27, and new AI-native competitors attacking the traditional labour-cost model. Notably, the cost-cutting demand that makes Wipro's position weak on price is also its cushion against the business cycle: when clients cut budgets, cost-takeout and vendor-consolidation work goes up, and long-running managed-services contracts keep revenue coming in. The same double-edge runs through the investment story. The declared growth spending — $1bn on FullStride Cloud, the AI-native business unit, $500m through Wipro Ventures, the WINGS platform, 44,000 employees AI-certified, an Anthropic Claude centre of excellence — is what keeps the Innovation Dominance pattern alive and is listed as the silver lining against technology risk, yet the quality framework marks that same spending down because it has coincided with revenue shrinking 1.6% and margins slipping to 16%, and no research spending is ever disclosed. Acquisitions follow the same shape: six deals including Capco, Rizing, HARMAN DTS and Mindsprint bought capability, Capco is performing, and group revenue still stalled. Adding to the fragility, results keep hinging on a handful of relationships — a notable decline in the largest client plus declines in the next four in the fourth quarter of FY26, with weakness blamed on one account's specific problem and a delayed start. The honest summary is a financially safe, generously distributing company with steady margins, competing in a market where price is falling, share is moving to others, and the technology underneath the service model is being rewritten.

## What to watch

- Revenue and order bookings: the Market Share Gainers pattern and the market-share check both fail on FY26 revenue down 1.6% and fourth-quarter bookings down 13.9% — a return to growth alongside peers, rather than continued sequential guidance of minus 2% to zero, is the single clearest thing that would change the verdict.
- Evidence of pricing rather than cost-cutting: the Pricing Power pattern is a clear miss and the pricing/mix check scores -2 because deals are won on cost takeout and vendor consolidation; any sign of realised price increases, or outcome-based work that lifts rather than erodes revenue per person, would break that link.
- Whether the AI spending starts producing revenue: the growth-capex check is marked down because $1bn on FullStride Cloud, the AI-native unit and $500m of Wipro Ventures coincided with shrinking revenue and margins slipping to 16% — visible revenue from the AI-native unit, or any disclosure of research spending, would firm up the Innovation Dominance pattern.
- The elevated technology risk playing out in the existing book: watch for management moving from conceding software-development productivity gains to showing that AI work is adding more than it displaces, which is precisely the concern analysts raised on the calls.
- Concentration and margin holding together: the stakeholder-concentration risk showed up as a notable decline in the top client, declines in the next four, and one account's delayed ramp-up — if that recurs while the Low Price Plus cost levers (utilisation, offshoring, staff mix, overheads) run out, the ₹19-per-₹100 margin strength and the 88% payout that underpin the sound financial rating come under pressure.
