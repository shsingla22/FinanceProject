# Equitas Small Fin (EQUITASBNK) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Equitas Small Finance Bank is an Indian bank that takes deposits from retail savers and lends the money out to small borrowers in what management calls the "informal and formalizing economy" — small shop and business owners, small fleet operators, first-time home buyers and low-income women borrowers. It gathers money through savings and current accounts, retail term deposits and NRI deposits (offering relatively attractive interest rates plus app-based service), and earns mainly the spread between the ~15.6% yield it charges on loans and the ~7.1% it pays on deposits, plus fee income from selling insurance, mutual funds and other third-party products. It operates through branches concentrated in South India (Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka are named) with vehicle finance rolled out across India, and services NRI customers in 140-plus countries. As of March 2026 it had roughly INR46,165 crores of loans and INR46,533 crores of deposits, with quarterly profit of INR213 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Small business loans (SBL), including micro LAP, secured business loans and merchant overdraft** — Loans to small/informal business owners, typically against property, and small overdrafts to merchants — described as the bank's "flagship" and largest product at INR18,559 crores (about 45% of the book), growing 13-27% a year across the calls.
- **Vehicle finance (new and used commercial vehicles, used cars)** — Loans to buy trucks and cars, with management increasingly steering toward used vehicles — used CVs at INR5,899 crores and used cars growing 31% year-on-year, while new CV lending is shrinking 20%; it is the second-biggest business and has been a repeated source of bad-loan slippage.
- **Affordable housing loans (AHL)** — Home loans for lower-income buyers, a newer product that has grown steadily to INR5,782 crores and was said in FY26 to have finally turned profitable.
- **Microfinance (MFI)** — Small unsecured group loans, historically 20% of the book in March 2024 but deliberately shrunk to about 8.5-10% (INR4,667 crores) after heavy defaults, and now capped at roughly 10% of lending on an ongoing basis.
- **MSE finance** — Lending to micro and small enterprises, one of the newest lines, crossing INR2,000 crores and growing about 24-35% a year.
- **Branch banking, liabilities, product and wealth** — The deposit-gathering and fee side — savings/current accounts, retail fixed deposits, NRI deposits (over INR2,000 crores), digitally-opened accounts, plus selling mutual funds/SIPs and insurance to customers; deposits were INR46,533 crores with CASA at 26% and retail deposits 68% of the total.
- **Treasury** — Manages the bank's funding, liquidity and capital-conservation steps such as IBPC sell-downs (raised to INR2,000 crores) and buying loan pools through direct assignment; a support function rather than a customer-facing business.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Equitas is a small finance bank whose best asset is the people running it, and whose weakest point is what it actually earns. The management-related checks are the highest-scoring part of the whole review: candour scores a full 2 because the CEO routinely opens with what went wrong, refuses to guide when he cannot see clearly, and puts numbers on bad news — slippages above comfort level, the one-time provision-coverage correction, the labour-code hit. The same person has led all 16 calls since July 2022 with one consistent multi-year plan, growth has been entirely organic with no acquisitions, and the calls are operational rather than promotional. That is why the Corporate Culture pattern comes out PARTIAL rather than absent. But the return-on-capital area scores the worst possible -1.0: this is a balance-sheet lender that is structurally asset-heavy, with return on assets reaching only about 1.46% at its best quarter, and margin falling from a pre-crisis 8.5-9% to 7.29%, which management now calls the roughly 7-7.1% 'new normal'. Good stewardship and thin economics sit side by side here, and the combined rating of 45 out of 100 — 'Mixed' — is essentially that tension expressed as a number.

The single most important thing to understand is that one decision drives several of the strengths and several of the weaknesses at the same time. Management deliberately cut microfinance from roughly a fifth of the loan book to about a tenth, built provision coverage above 70%, added a floating provision of INR180 crore, and used guarantee and loan-participation tools to conserve capital. The quality framework rewards this as disciplined stewardship. The pattern screen, however, records the direct cost: the Pricing Power pattern is a clear NO FIT because this is price deflation, not pricing power — yield on gross advances fell to 15.63% and to 14.97% outside microfinance on what management calls portfolio recalibration. The pricing-mix-volume check scores -1 for the same reason: growth came from volume and record disbursement of INR7,347 crore, while both price and mix worked against the bank. The Market Share Gainers pattern is likewise only PARTIAL — advances grew 22% year-on-year and the secured, used-vehicle and small-enterprise lines compounded at 21-35%, but the deliberate shrinking of microfinance means the bank grew and retreated at once, and deposits grew only 7-8%. Similarly, the recovery that earns a positive mark under the cyclical-growth check — credit cost down from 2.74% to 1.11%, net slippage at a 10-quarter low, margin turning up — is the very same fact pattern that makes Cyclicality a HIGH RISK: four years of calls describe a complete boom-bust-recovery loop, and nothing in the record shows the loop has been broken.

Where the story turns genuinely cautionary is on competitive protection, and here the pattern screen and the risk review agree almost completely. Four patterns come back NO FIT for one connected reason: there is no moat. Brand Strength fails because deposits are gathered by paying above-market rates and by rate tweaking, with management openly describing the shift away from rate-led gathering as an ambition still ahead of it. Low Price Plus fails on every leg — cost of funds of 7.13%, savings rates of 6.08-6.18% and term deposits at 8.48%, cost-to-income stuck around 70-72%, and a weak credit record. Cost to Replicate fails because nothing across four years explains why a well-funded newcomer could not copy branch-gathered deposits, commoditised secured lending and bought-in technology. That absence shows up again as three separate WATCH risks: new entrants competing for the same deposits and borrowers, good-enough lending where the customer simply shops on rate, and shifting saver preferences pulling money into market products, with low-cost deposits drifting from about 30% to 26% and retail deposits from 73% to 68%. The technology cadence is real — a new origination system, a CRM, the Selfie app with 1.5 lakh downloads, mobile banking, and digital and neobank channels crossing Rs 1,000 crore of deposits — but it earns only a PARTIAL on innovation and a WATCH on the risk side, because that spend is precisely what keeps costs stuck near 70% of income. Regulation cuts both ways too: the RBI's universal-bank rules forced the coverage reset from 56% to 70% that crushed first-quarter FY25 profit to INR26 crore, while industry guardrails drove the microfinance recovery — so government dependency is rated HIGH RISK even though the bank's use of those same policy tools is credited as prudence elsewhere. The one unambiguous comfort is granularity: no single customer or funding source matters much, with deposits 68-73% retail and a wide multi-product loan platform, which is why stakeholder concentration is the only LOW risk in the set. Overall resilience is recorded as UNKNOWN, which is an honest reflection of a bank that has proven its management but not yet its earning power.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; the numeric fingerprints could not be computed, so the call evidence decides alone.

**What the calls show:** The four-year arc is a full credit cycle: FY23 restructuring/COVID provisions, then the FY25-26 microfinance and CV downturn that pushed credit cost to 2.74% and PAT to INR26cr in Q1FY25, followed by the FY26 recovery to 1.11% credit cost and record INR213cr PAT — earnings amplitude driven by borrower cash-flow cycles, freight rates, fuel and monsoon rather than by the franchise. Management itself frames Q1/Q2 as seasonally weakest and openly says diesel-price pass-through would hit CV borrowers.

> "in commercial vehicles, freight rates may take some time to adjust upwards. And during such interim period, our borrowers are likely to be affected" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Borrowers are operations/flow-linked rather than capex-linked — 'our borrowers largely deal in daily use products and services' and can pass on cost increases; MFI mix deliberately cut from ~45-50% to ~10%, secured non-MFI book now INR40,409cr, structurally damping the amplitude*

- ⬜ Not enough sales history.
- ⬜ No margin history.
- ⬜ Insufficient capex data.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Nearly every major swing in the numbers traces to a regulator's decision: RBI's universal-bank guidelines forced a one-time PCR reset from 56% to 70% and crushed Q1FY25 PAT; MFIN guardrails drove the microfinance recovery; CGTMSE guarantee and IBPC are used to conserve capital; new labour codes cost INR29.5cr in one quarter; RBI rate cycle sets both yields and cost of funds. The SFB licence itself is a regulatory construct.

> "the new guidelines that RBI has come out with in the month of April for SFBs to convert into universal banks and the requirement there is that the net NPA should be less than 1%" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Diversification away from the priority-sector-heavy microfinance book toward secured SBL, housing, used vehicles and MSE reduces reliance on any single policy-driven segment*
