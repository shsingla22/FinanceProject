### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 8 unchanged.

*Foundation test: UNKNOWN on the long view, UNKNOWN on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: What management describes is contractual cost pass-through of copper/aluminium LME and USD-INR on a quarterly reset, not price rises above cost inflation; that protects margin (and gave inventory gains in Q3 FY26) but standalone EBITDA margin still compressed in FY26, and there is no premiumisation, scarcity or elasticity language. (The long view had said: Pricing is a mechanical quarterly pass-through of copper/aluminium LME and USD-INR in both directions, not price increases above cost inflation; the company also positions itself 15-20% below Western suppliers and abandoned the higher-margin aftermarket, so there is no premium or scarcity pricing.)

**That weakened:**

- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The Bus AC entry (and the Best Air Conditioning business-transfer acquisition) moves KRN up the value chain from component to system supplier, with ~Rs.10 crores in FY26 and a 15% share ambition, but this is forward integration within B2B — there are no own stores, franchising, licensing or D2C, and the aftermarket was explicitly declined. (The long view had said: No stores, franchising or D2C, but the company is deliberately moving up the value chain from component to finished system — buying the Spear bus AC business and selling complete bus air conditioners direct to OEM end users, backed by in-house FRP, tubing and coils.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The advantages management cites — a large new plant, backward integration into tubing and FRP, an AHRI-linked test lab and OEM qualification — are all things a well-funded entrant can buy in a few years; KRN's own record proves it, having built the 600,000 sq ft facility from IPO capital and added 40+ customers within a year, with pricing reset to LME quarterly and no time-, data- or location-based barrier described. (The long view had said: There are articulable barriers — multi-year OEM qualification and facility-audit cycles, geometry bandwidth built for Europe/US, the AHRI-linked lab and deepening backward integration — but heat exchanger fabrication is machine-bought capacity that a funded entrant could assemble in a few years, and the biggest OEMs already make coils in-house for mass volumes.)

*Unchanged: Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
