### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 3 weakened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There are isolated instances of price capture — better allergy prices in Q3FY23 and a stated premium on complex biologics on line 3 in the May 2026 call — but FY2026 EBITDA margin fell 99bps to 15.9% and Q4 fell 272bps, so pricing is not visibly outrunning cost inflation across the group. (The long view had said: Genuine pricing power exists in pockets — Ruby-Fill is described in May 2026 as growing in market size, share and price, complex biologics command a premium, and Feb 2025 noted a reimbursement model that keeps prices stable — but the group also contains a deflating generics book, so it is not a company-wide trait.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: No heritage narrative, A&P spend, distributor leverage or brand-adjacency discussion in any of the recent calls; the business is described in B2B capability terms (facilities, compliance, capacity). (The long view had said: Radiopharma products are described as 'branded or quasi-branded' with market leadership in Iodine-131 and Ruby-Fill, and the Bhartia group heritage is showcased (Feb 2025), but there is no sustained A&P spend, distributor leverage or consistency-promise language anywhere in the calls.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is a real proprietary pipeline (JBI-802 and JBI-778 with US FDA orphan drug designations) plus new SPECT/PET/MIBG launches and a discovery business growing 15% to over Rs 650 Crore in FY2026, but R&D spend is never disclosed or sized against the industry, and the novel-drug work is pre-revenue. (The long view had said: There is a real, disclosed pipeline with named products, TAMs and peak-sales estimates (Feb 2025: FY27/28/29 launches totalling ~US$550mn TAM, MIBG at US$70-100mn) plus proprietary novel drugs and heavy R&D/capex, but margins are mid-teens rather than innovation-grade, and delivery has slipped — by May 2026 a FY2027 launch was pulled out and MIBG filing had moved to H2 FY2027.)

**That weakened:**

- **Cost to Replicate**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Some articulable replication barriers exist — a six-hour-half-life radiopharmacy logistics network, sole-source venom extracts, and FDA-cleared isolator fill-finish lines whose customers face high switching costs — but the same calls show the position is supply- and compliance-dependent (Montreal, SPECT, USFDA observations) rather than structurally uncatchable. (The long view had said: There is a concrete, articulable barrier: six-hour isotope half-lives force an owned last-mile network that took decades and 46 sites to build, paired with FDA-approved manufacturing — management notes a loss-making pharmacy fetched US$250mn purely for logistics, and Feb 2025 argues the manufacturing-plus-pharmacy combination cannot simply be entered.)
- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The company manufactures radiopharmaceuticals and owns the downstream dispensing network — 46 radiopharmacies, with six new PET pharmacies flagged as a Vision-2030 driver — which is genuine forward integration, though there is no franchising, royalty or D2C economics disclosed. (The long view had said: Jubilant is a manufacturer that owns the route to the customer: 46 radiopharmacies (second-largest US network), a 350-vehicle fleet, 1,800 hospitals served, and a build-out from three to nine PET pharmacies; May 2026 confirms it will launch MIBG itself on the back of that downstream network rather than licensing out.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The four calls discuss segment revenue, EBITDA and capex but never attach rates, AMC/service revenue share, renewal rates or deferred revenue/customer advances as a funding source. (The long view had said: Ruby-Fill is a razor/razorblade model — the capital device is placed and then a generator is resupplied roughly every 7 weeks, with installed-base language across calls (Aug 2022: 'Ruby-Fill installations shows encouraging trend'), and CDMO tech-transfer creates multi-year commercial supply. But management never discusses attach rates, renewal rates, AMC share or deferred revenue/advances as a funding source.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 1 worsened, 2 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The highest-margin SPECT cold-kit franchise is the older imaging modality while management concedes 'all the growth is happening on the PET side', and CDMO economics are migrating from small molecules to complex biologics — both are format shifts the company is having to re-tool for rather than ones it leads. (The long view had said: Radiopharma is mid-platform-shift: the highest-margin legacy SPECT cold-kit portfolio sits against a PET/therapeutic wave management itself describes as new isotopes (Lu-177, Ac-225, Lead, Astatine, Thorium) and ~100 new products in 3-4 years, forcing them to build six new PET pharmacies and cyclotron capacity. The shift is incremental and they are funding the migration rather than defending a format, but the FY26 margin hit came precisely from the legacy SPECT line.)
- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Only one concrete signal in the window — management flagging rising competition for large-pharma discovery work — with no structurally different entrant format discussed in radiopharma, allergy or CDMO. (The long view had said: Big pharma is entering radiopharma with capital that resets economics — four large transactions in 12 months, a loss-making pharmacy bought for $250mn, Novartis leading Lu-177 — and in FY26 management conceded competitive intensity is rising in the large-pharma discovery segment. Management frames these as validation, but they are structurally different, deeper-pocketed entrants.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The generics/API arm is itself exposed to commodity price erosion — it ran negative EBITDA through FY23 and needed Rs.150cr of cost cuts and a geography re-mix to survive — while API still sits at a 15% EBITDA margin pending custom-manufacturing wins; the specialty franchises, however, are defended by scarcity and switching costs. (The long view had said: The Generics segment is structurally exposed to good-enough price competition — revenue fell from Rs 432cr to Rs 178cr on US pricing pressure and a sartan price collapse — and Ruby-Fill must justify its premium against a rival rubidium generator on generator life and image quality. The specialty side is better protected, and management has deliberately pushed toward complex generics and complex biologics where switching costs, not brand, defend price.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Supplier concentration is repeatedly the direct cause of missed quarters — generator/radioisotope outages in FY23 and, in FY26, a single contract site (CMO Montreal) starving the highest-margin SPECT products and dragging full-year margins; on the customer side CDMO growth rests on a handful of named large innovators including 'one of the world's largest oncology products'. (The long view had said: CDMO Sterile Injectables' growth case rests on a handful of named-but-undisclosed relationships — 10+ products on Line 3, including 'one of the world's largest oncology products' — and internal single-site dependence proved costly when the Montreal CMO shortfall knocked out SPECT supply, causing a $14mn H1 revenue hit and a Rs 200cr site loss, forcing the pipeline out to third-party CMOs.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL); Fashion Risk (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
