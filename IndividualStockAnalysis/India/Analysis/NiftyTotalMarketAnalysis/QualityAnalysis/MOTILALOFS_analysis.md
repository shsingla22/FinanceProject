# Motilal Oswal Financial Services Ltd. (MOTILALOFS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

Motilal Oswal Financial Services is an Indian financial services group that helps people and institutions invest and manage money — it runs stock broking and distribution for retail investors, manages mutual funds and alternative funds, advises wealthy families, does institutional equity research and investment banking, and lends home loans. It earns money from brokerage and trading fees, trail/distribution commissions, recurring management fees on assets it manages or advises (AUM of about Rs. 4.5 lakh crores as of June 2026), interest on margin funding and housing loans, and deal fees from investment banking. It operates across India, reaching over 98% of the country's PIN codes through 2,500+ business locations, ~2,000 in-house relationship managers and 8,800+ external partners, and is expanding via the GIFT City route for cross-border flows. Alongside the operating businesses it runs a large proprietary investment book (~Rs. 9,000 crores), which management calls its 'twin engine' model — the book funds the businesses' capital needs and has compounded at roughly 18% XIRR since inception.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Wealth Management (formerly Broking & Distribution)** — The retail-facing stock broking and product distribution arm — trading accounts, mutual fund and other product sales, margin funding and advice — which management renamed from 'Broking & Distribution' in FY25 to reflect a shift from one-off transactions to full financial planning; it had Rs. 265,000 crores of assets under advice and Rs. 177 crores quarterly profit in Q1FY25, with ~8% cash market share and ~9.5% F&O premium share.
- **Capital Markets (Institutional Equities + Investment Banking)** — Sells research and trade execution to institutional investors and raises capital for companies (IPOs, QIPs, deals) — in Q1FY25 it did 7 deals worth ~Rs. 5,400 crores and ranked #1 in the QIP league table, but it is the smallest of the main segments at Rs. 134 crores net revenue and Rs. 57 crores profit that quarter.
- **Asset Management (AMC — mutual funds, PMS and AIF)** — Runs mutual funds, portfolio management schemes and alternative investment funds for retail and HNI investors, and is the group's main growth engine — AUM went from Rs. 43,400 crores in mid-2022 to nearly Rs. 2 lakh crores by mid-2026, serving over 1 crore unique investors (~16% of all Indian mutual fund clients), and it contributed 40% of group operating profit in Q1FY27.
- **Alternates / Private Markets (private equity and real estate funds)** — Raises and manages closed-end funds for wealthy families and institutions — growth-capital funds (the fifth, IBEF V, raised close to a billion dollars), residential real estate funds, a maiden Rs. 3,000 crore private credit fund, and a planned commercial real estate fund — earning management fees on roughly Rs. 21,000 crores of fee-accruing AUM plus performance-linked carry.
- **Private Wealth Management** — A dedicated advisory business for high-net-worth and family-office clients, staffed by 441 relationship managers, with AUM up 37% year-on-year to Rs. 2.4 lakh crores by June 2026; management is pushing it toward recurring 'ARR' fee revenue (about Rs. 52,000 crores of ARR AUM) and away from lumpier transaction-based revenue.
- **Home Finance / Housing Finance** — A small housing-loan lender making mortgage loans and earning interest spread — AUM around Rs. 4,122 crores with roughly Rs. 28-32 crores of quarterly profit, yields near 14%, spreads of about 5.6% and low NPAs; it is by far the smallest contributor to group profit.
- **Treasury / Investment book (the 'twin engine')** — The company's own money invested in equities and its own alternate funds — about Rs. 9,000 crores by FY26, compounding at roughly 18-19% XIRR — which supplies capital to the operating businesses and lets the group grow without ever issuing new shares since its 2007 listing (it has instead done three buybacks).

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Motilal Oswal comes out of the three reviews as a genuinely good business operating in a genuinely hard industry, which is why the combined score lands at 51 out of 100 — a "Decent" three-star rating built from 61 for business quality, 56 for multibagger fit and only 26 for risk safety. The quality work is strongest where it is about people and customers: full marks for candor (separating operating profit from treasury mark-to-market, flagging the high base that caused a 50% fall in distribution revenue, admitting investment banking execution has been "extremely weak"), for disciplined stewardship (nineteen years without issuing new shares, no acquisitions, growth paid for out of its own earnings), and for shareholder distributions (three buybacks, a steady policy of paying out about 20% of operating profit). Those same traits are what the multibagger screen calls a STRONG FIT on Corporate Culture. The other strong fit, Market Share Gainers, is the commercial version of the same story: cash market share up 186 basis points in a single year to 8.0%, futures-and-options premium share from 7.0% to 7.6%, and a share of new mutual fund money (4.2%) running well ahead of its share of existing assets (2.9%) — all from a small base inside an end market the framework rates as a real multi-decade expansion.

The awkward part is that the very things producing those wins also produce the biggest risks, and the records are explicit about it. The company competes as a premium, full-service, high-ARPU house — it keeps ₹85 of every ₹100 of sales after direct costs, and reaches customers through 2,000-plus in-house relationship managers, 8,800 external wealth managers and 2,500 locations covering 98% of India's PIN codes. That premium position is exactly what the risk review attacks under Good Enough Goods (ELEVATED): cheap discount brokers against full-service broking, and low-fee passive funds against active ones. It is also why three patterns are marked NO FIT — Low Price Plus, because the company deliberately is not the cheap option; and Pricing Power, because prices are flat to falling and largely set by the regulator and by product mix. So the fair reading is that the company is winning volume and share without being able to set its own price. Government Dependency is rated HIGH RISK for the same reason — contract sizes, transaction charges, margin rules, expense-ratio caps and the July 2026 proprietary-trading rules are all decided elsewhere — and Cyclicality is HIGH RISK because daily turnover swung from ₹24,000 crore before COVID to ₹1.5 lakh crore in one quarter of FY23 and then fell back into broking degrowth in FY26.

The two weakest quality checks tie the same knot from the balance-sheet side. Return on capital scores minus two: for every ₹100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings, the business generated minus ₹18 of operating cash this year, and roughly 158 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. Those numbers are not separate from the strengths — the self-funded ₹9,000–10,500 crore treasury book that underwrites the independence, the buybacks and the counter-cyclical spending is the same book that took a roughly ₹1,000 crore notional mark-to-market hit when markets turned, and it is also one of the time-based barriers the Cost to Replicate pattern credits (PARTIAL). Management's stated answer to all of this is the shift toward repeat, fee-based income, which the screen rates a LIKELY FIT on Recurring Revenue: annuity revenue at 60% of the total in FY26 rising to 66% by the first quarter of FY27, a ₹16,000 crore annualised systematic-investment run rate, and ARR at 57% of wealth management revenue against 33% five years earlier. The overall resilience verdict is STRAINED, and that is the honest summary: strong people, strong distribution, real share gains and a real long-term market, sitting on top of earnings that the company does not fully control and capital that is not yet earning a cash return.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across 18 calls the group's earnings visibly swing with the market cycle: ADTO went from Rs24,000cr pre-COVID to Rs1.5 lakh cr in Q1FY23, then FY26 saw broking degrowth on weak volumes, a ~Rs1,000cr notional MTM loss on the Rs9,000-10,500cr treasury book, and IB revenue that management concedes arrives only in 2-3 month windows per year. Management itself frames FY25-FY26 as 'weak markets' and guides that quarterly outcomes 'really depend on market'.

> "if there's a quarter where there's no such window, then this business would be quite volatile for the whole industry including us" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate shift to annuity/ARR revenue (60% of revenues in FY26 rising to 66% in Q1FY27), Rs16,000cr annualised SIP run-rate, distribution and lending books growing 30%+ — flow-type, operations-linked income; plus counter-cyclical investment in people, brand and tech through the FY26 downturn and no equity dilution since 2007.*

- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 2% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 4%).

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Revenue economics are set by the regulator, not the company: SEBI's F&O contract-size and transaction-charge changes, higher margin requirements, TER revisions and the July 2026 prop-trading rules are discussed in call after call, and management explicitly attributes the FY26 broking decline to them — 'FY25 & FY26 marked regulatory tightening combined with weak markets'.

> "Broking revenue during FY26 ... was lower mainly because of the lower volumes and the impact of regulatory changes, which had come on the F&O segment" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Diversification away from the most regulated revenue line — broking fell from 60% to 35% of Wealth Management segment revenue over 5 years, with Asset & Private Wealth now 55% of operating PAT; TER changes were reported as leaving their AUM cohort's fees unimpacted.*
