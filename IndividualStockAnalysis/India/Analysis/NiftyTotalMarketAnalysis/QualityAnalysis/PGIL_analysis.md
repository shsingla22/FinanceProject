# Pearl Global Inds. (PGIL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Pearl Global Industries (PGIL), founded in 1987, is a contract apparel manufacturer — it cuts and sews garments to order for large Western clothing retailers and brands rather than selling under its own label. Its range covers knitted tees, polos and dresses, woven shirts and blouses, denim jeans, outerwear jackets, and active/athleisure wear for men, women and toddlers, with named customers including Kohl's, Macy's, Tommy Hilfiger, Gap, Old Navy, Next, Nordstrom and Muji. It makes money by manufacturing and shipping garments on a per-piece basis (exports on FOB terms), earning a margin between the price per garment and its fabric, labour and factory costs — FY26 consolidated revenue was about INR 5,025 crore with EBITDA of INR 468 crore (9.3%) and PAT of INR 270 crore. Production runs out of factories in India, Bangladesh, Vietnam, Indonesia and Guatemala (installed capacity about 101 million pieces a year in FY26), with design and client-servicing offices in the USA, UK, Spain and Hong Kong, and it sells mainly into the USA, European Union, UK, Japan and Australia.

**The parts of the business:**

- **India operations (largely the standalone entity)** — Garment factories in states including Haryana, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and a newer unit in Bihar; management says India runs at roughly INR 1,100 crore annualised revenue (standalone FY26 revenue INR 1,081 crore), about 22%-24% of group revenue, with capability built to exceed INR 1,500-1,600 crore, and was the geography hit hardest by the 2025-26 US tariffs before the India-US deal and the India-EU/UK FTAs.
- **Bangladesh operations (Dhaka)** — A mature, large manufacturing base described as growing 30%-plus in a recent year with new major customers added and an ongoing INR 110 crore capex to add about 6 million pieces of capacity by FY27-FY28; benefits from duty-free access to the EU, UK, Canada and Australia.
- **Vietnam operations (including Hanoi)** — Serves higher-end and fast-growing North American specialty brands; capacity utilisation improved to over 80% in FY26 from 63%, it was a key driver of high value-added product growth, and management plans to add further capacity there.
- **Indonesia operations** — A recently commissioned replacement factory (moved away from the seashore) that is still ramping up — utilisation rose to 47% in FY26 from 39% — focused on premium clients and expected to start contributing to both revenue and profit.
- **Guatemala operations** — A small, newer nearshore plant for the US market (about a week's transit) that is still loss-making; management is focused on improving efficiency and cutting losses, and notes Central American capacity is only a fraction of its Asian capacity, helped by zero US tariff access.
- **Partnership (asset-light) manufacturing model** — Instead of owning every factory, PGIL supplies working capital, design and product development, fabric procurement and its own execution/monitoring teams into a partner's factory and pays the partner per piece while the partner funds the capex — it scaled fast, contributing 21.3% of revenue in Q1 FY23 versus 2.8% a year earlier, and is used to add capacity quickly and lift return ratios.
- **Design, product development and client-servicing offices** — Over 75 designers across its locations, plus front-end offices in the USA, UK, Spain and Hong Kong, using 3D design tools (CLO, Browzwear, Optitex) so the company can offer 'concept to store' end-to-end service and win a bigger share of each customer's sourcing spend.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*
