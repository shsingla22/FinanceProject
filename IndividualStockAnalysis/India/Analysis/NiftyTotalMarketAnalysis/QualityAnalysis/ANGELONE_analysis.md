# Angel One (ANGELONE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Angel One is an Indian, technology-led retail stockbroker: through its mobile "Super App" and web platform it lets everyday individuals open demat and trading accounts and buy and sell shares (cash equity) and equity derivatives (F&O) on the Indian exchanges, and it is registered with SEBI as a stock broker, depository participant (CDSL), portfolio manager, research analyst and investment adviser, plus AMFI and PFRDA registrations. Its customers are mainly retail investors and traders across India, with management repeatedly stressing reach beyond the metros and tier-one cities into younger, first-time, small-ticket investors; the base had grown to over 38 million registered users by Q1 FY'27, with 431 million orders in a quarter and roughly 7.4 million average daily orders. It earns money principally from brokerage and related fees on the trades and orders clients place, from interest on lending money to clients against securities (the margin trading / client funding book, averaging ₹61.4 billion), and increasingly from distributing and manufacturing other financial products — mutual funds, loans, wealth management and its own asset management schemes. It operates only in India, reaching clients both directly (digital, self-serve) and through a network of channel partners, and reported Q1 FY'27 revenue of ₹14.3 billion and consolidated profit after tax of ₹2.3 billion.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Broking (Direct Business)** — The core business — self-serve online trading and investing in cash equity and equity derivatives via the Super App and web, and management calls it the "gateway product" that brings in most clients (about 20.2% share of overall retail equity turnover, 22.2% of equity derivatives turnover, 17.4% of cash equity turnover and 16.7% demat market share).
- **Assisted Business (channel partners)** — A partner-led channel where independent channel partners bring in and service clients using Angel One's dedicated NXT platform, run by its own Chief Business Officer and treated as a second distribution engine alongside the direct digital one.
- **Margin Trading Facility / client funding book** — Lending to clients against their holdings so they can trade with borrowed money, described as a key use of the ₹15 billion capital raise and reaching a lifetime-best average book of ₹61.4 billion — an interest-earning business the company calls out separately each quarter.
- **Mutual fund distribution / SIPs** — Selling and servicing third-party mutual funds and systematic investment plans on the app (compare funds, customise SIP amounts and dates, track portfolios); management calls it a high-engagement product and says Angel One is the second-largest provider of new SIP registrations in India, with ~1.4 million unique SIPs registered in a single quarter.
- **Credit (loans)** — A newer lending business offering credit and fixed income products to existing clients, with lifetime cumulative disbursements of ₹27.1 billion and ₹6.1 billion in a quarter — still small and being scaled "in a calibrated and disciplined manner", though management says its client base already borrows over ₹1 trillion of personal loans a year elsewhere.
- **Wealth Management (Ionic Wealth)** — A separately branded, tech-plus-relationship-manager wealth business for ultra-high-net-worth and high-net-worth families, launched around FY'24 under co-founder/CEO Srikanth Subramanian, with assets under management crossing ₹100 billion and a client base of 1,900-plus, over 45% of them from non-metro cities.
- **Asset Management (Angel One Asset Management Company)** — Its own in-house fund house, set up after regulatory approval and now launching schemes — the newest and smallest of the growth engines, described as "strengthening and expanding our strategy" rather than at scale.
- **Technology / AI platform (Super App, Ask Angel)** — Not a revenue line but the backbone management talks about most — the Super App plus AI features such as the "Ask Angel" conversational assistant (serving 1.1 million users), AI-driven KYC face-match onboarding and AI-assisted internal engineering, positioned as what lowers cost-to-serve and enables cross-selling across all the businesses above.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

In one breath: the quality framework finds an average business today (52/100); it strongly fits 2 of the 11 patterns long-term winners share; the risk review found 2 high risks (Cyclicality, Government Dependency). Weighing those together gives 45 out of 100 — mixed.

### The story in depth

Angel One is a flat-fee digital stock broker that has done the hard part of its job unusually well: getting large numbers of ordinary Indians to open accounts and keep using them. The customer-benefit checks are the strongest part of the whole record — convenience scores full marks for digital sign-up, the Super App, activation in under 15 days and reach into small towns that older brokers do not serve, and the customer benefits area scores a clean 1.0. That reach has turned into measurable share: demat share rose from 10.8% in June 2022 to 16.7% in March 2026, retail equity turnover share still grew through the F&O rule shock, and the client base went from 10 million to 38 million. The end market is genuinely growing too, with more household savings moving into financial products from a still-low base. This is exactly why two of the eleven multibagger patterns come out as a strong fit rather than a partial one — Low Price Plus, where 85% of net broking revenue sits on the flat plan alongside tight costs and a falling cost to serve, and Market Share Gainers, which is simply the share record above. Capital is being put behind growth rather than upkeep: a Rs.15bn share sale for the margin-funding book, technology and data centres, and Rs.1.5bn each into the wealth and lending arms.

The trouble is that the single trait producing those two strengths — a cheap, flat price on a service that is much the same everywhere — is also the source of most of the weaknesses. The industry structure check is negative because entry barriers are low and pricing is already commoditised by the flat-fee model, with profit margins swinging by about 12 points. The pricing check is negative because growth has come almost entirely from more orders rather than better prices, with revenue per client falling and management openly guiding that newer customers will pay less. The screen agrees from the other side: Pricing Power is a clear no fit, since prices were cut rather than raised and average revenue per user fell to Rs.453; Toll Roads is a no fit as well, with nothing in four years of calls resembling a position customers cannot walk away from. The risk review lands in the same place from a third angle — Good Enough Goods is flagged because the flat-fee product is itself the cheap-and-adequate option with low switching costs and no premium to defend, and New Entrants is flagged because Angel is now spending heavily to hold its ground, including a five-year IPL sponsorship at over Rs.1.2bn a season, with analysts asking whether that spend is converting into matching growth. Cost to Replicate is only a partial fit for the same reason: the 38-million user base, the data and the brand are real, but a well-funded rival has rebuilt them before. Seven of the eleven patterns sit at partial, which is a fair picture of a company that does many things adequately and none of them in a way a competitor could not copy.

Two things then decide the verdict, and both sit outside management's control. First, the money the business actually generates. The headline return-on-capital check is the worst score in the framework: for every Rs.100 of shareholder money and borrowings in the business, it produced minus Rs.29 of operating cash this year, a negative 29% cash return, and the return-on-capital area scores minus 0.67 even though the growth spending itself is described as earning around 43% on equity. Second, who sets the rules and the weather. Cyclicality is high risk because revenue rides on how actively retail investors trade — daily orders fell to about 5 million in February 2025 before recovering to 7.4 million in March 2026, and the most recent quarter softened again. Government dependency is high risk because SEBI and RBI set the economics directly: the F&O rules depressed volumes for several quarters and RBI limits on bank exposure tighten the credit the business runs on. The offsets are genuine but partial — around 40% of revenue now comes from interest, distribution and wealth, the customer base is spread thinly enough that no single client matters, the shift of savings into financial products runs in the company's favour, and demand is repeat and functional rather than fashion-driven. Put together, resilience reads as strained and the combined score is 45 out of 100, or Mixed: a real share gainer in a growing market, with no way yet demonstrated to charge more for what it does or to convert that growth into cash returns.

The picture that emerges is one company doing the same thing everywhere in the record — buying scale with a low price — and the reader should judge it on whether scale eventually turns into something the low price cannot be taken away by.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is tied to retail capital-market activity, which swings hard: ADO fell to ~5mn in Feb'25 before recovering to 7.4mn in Mar'26, Q1 FY27 orders and revenue moderated on 'softer trading activities', and reported EBDAT margin has oscillated between ~32.7% and ~44.8% across the period, with dividends deferred in FY24 to conserve capital during the boom. Demand is flow/operations-linked (trading, SIPs, MTF) rather than customer capex, and the 40% non-broking revenue mix is a genuine dampener, but the earnings base still tracks the market cycle.

> "These trends remain largely cyclical in nature and we expect gradual normalization as capital market issuance activity and credit demand strengthen." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/operations-linked demand (repeat trading, SIPs, client funding book) plus diversification to ~40% of revenue from non-broking lines; countercyclical investment (IPL brand spend, wealth/AMC/NBFC capital infusion) through the downturn*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 2 of the last 11 years with swings of ±34 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from 1% to 43% (swinging ±11.8 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 3% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 5%).

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** SEBI and RBI directly set the economics: full implementation of F&O regulations depressed order volumes for several quarters, RBI directions on banks' capital-market exposure tighten intraday credit and force higher bank-guarantee deployment, and every-day-expiry exchange rule changes materially raised working capital. Management repeatedly asks investors to read results 'in the context of' an evolving regulatory framework.

> "the regulatory landscape continues to evolve. While we closely monitor these developments and remain agile in adapting to regulatory changes..." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate diversification into wealth, AMC, credit and insurance distribution to reduce reliance on regulated broking economics; diversified funding via NBFCs, NCDs and CPs to blunt the RBI bank-exposure rules*

## What to watch

- Cash returns: whether the negative 29% cash return on capital in the return-on-capital check reverses as the margin-funding book funded by the Rs.15bn raise matures, or stays negative as growth continues.
- Price: any sign of a fee increase or a premium product actually sticking would move Pricing Power off no fit and lift the negative pricing, mix and volume check; the current guidance for lower monetisation of new customers points the other way.
