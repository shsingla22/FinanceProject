# Angel One (ANGELONE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Angel One is an Indian digital stockbroker: it runs a mobile and web app (its "Super App") through which retail customers in India buy and sell shares and equity derivatives (futures and options) on the NSE and BSE, and it also acts as their depository participant for holding shares in demat form. Its customers are everyday retail investors — increasingly young, first-time and from beyond the big metros — with over 38 million registered users by Q1 FY'27 and a client base that grew from about 22 million in early 2024. It earns money mainly from charges on the orders its clients place (431 million orders in the Q4 FY'26 quarter alone, roughly 7.4 million a day), plus interest on money it lends clients to trade (an average client funding/MTF book of ₹61.4 billion), commissions from distributing mutual funds and other products, interest on personal loans it arranges, and fees from its newer wealth and asset-management arms. It operates only in India, is regulated by SEBI (and RBI on the credit side), and reported Q1 FY'27 revenue of ₹14.3 billion with profit after tax of ₹2.3 billion.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Broking (Direct Business)** — The core business — executing customers' share and derivatives trades through the app; management calls it the "gateway product" that brings most clients in, and it holds roughly 20% of India's retail equity turnover, 22.2% of equity derivatives turnover, 17.4% of cash equity turnover and 16.7% demat market share.
- **Assisted Business (channel partners)** — A network of independent partners/sub-brokers who bring in and service clients, supported by the company's dedicated NXT partner app; it sits alongside the direct app-based channel as a second route to customers.
- **Client funding / Margin Trading Facility (MTF)** — Lending clients money against securities so they can trade larger positions, earning interest on the balance; the company raised ₹15 billion of capital in FY'24 partly to fund this book, which reached a lifetime-best average of ₹61.4 billion.
- **Mutual fund distribution (SIPs)** — Selling and servicing mutual fund investments, especially monthly systematic investment plans, inside the same app — described as a high-engagement product where Angel One is the country's second-largest source of new SIP registrations (about 1.4 million unique SIPs in one quarter of FY'24).
- **Credit / loans (New Business)** — Personal and other loans offered to existing clients through the platform; still small but scaling in a "calibrated" way, with ₹27.1 billion of lifetime cumulative disbursements and ₹6.1 billion disbursed in the Q4 FY'26 quarter, against a client base management says already borrows over ₹1 trillion a year elsewhere.
- **Wealth management (Ionic Wealth)** — A separate, tech-led advisory business launched in FY'24 for ultra-high-net-worth and high-net-worth families that is being extended to retail; its assets under management crossed ₹100 billion (up 23% quarter-on-quarter), across a client base of 1,900-plus of whom over 45% are from non-metro cities.
- **Asset management (Angel One Asset Management Company)** — Its own in-house fund house, set up with a hired leadership team and regulatory approval and now launching its own schemes — the newest and smallest of the businesses.
- **Technology / AI platform (Super App, Ask Angel)** — Not a revenue line but the backbone management emphasises most: a single app covering onboarding/KYC, trading, option strategies, mutual funds, credit and wealth, now with an AI assistant "Ask Angel" serving over 1.1 million users, which they say lowers cost-to-serve and drives cross-selling across all the other parts.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

**Capital Allocation — Neutral (+0.40)** · 5 of 6 checks had evidence

- **Capex for growth (not maintenance) — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: Capex is genuinely growth-oriented but asset-light (tech assets, data centre/DR, cloud) with most incremental capital going into working capital and the client funding book at high returns (ROE ~43%), plus fresh ₹1.5bn infusions each into wealth and NBFC.
  > "Depreciation and amortization costs increased by 27%... on account of augmentation of technology assets at a data center and disaster recovery site" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Research and development — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: Continuous in-house product/tech development has repeatedly shipped new revenue lines — Super App, mutual funds/SIP, credit, Ask Angel AI — with 50%+ of engineering AI-augmented, though returns show up as volume rather than pricing power.
  > "more than 50% of development is augmented by AI... A testament of this is the transformation of Ask Angel itself" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Cost of working capital — Neutral** *(from the financial statements)*: About 31 days of cash stays stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers — a long cycle that ties up capital.
- **Advertisement and promotion — Neutral** *(from the conference calls)*: Large, sustained A&P (5-year IPL associate sponsorship, ~₹1.2bn a season) has built visibility in tier-2/3 India, but management itself was challenged on weak correlation between heavy marketing and growth, so brand-equity payback is unproven.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Revenue and margins visibly swing with capital-market activity across the four years: FY24 saw record orders (471mn/quarter, PAT +31% QoQ), then activity collapsed after F&O regulation (ADO down to 5mn in Feb'25) before recovering to a 'six-quarter high' in Q4 FY26, with Q1 FY27 PAT up 102% YoY off that trough and orders moderating again on 'lower derivative volumes'; commodity broking income moves with bullion/energy rallies and management itself labels distribution swings cyclical.

> "These trends remain largely cyclical in nature and we expect gradual normalization as capital market issuance activity and credit demand strengthen." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/operations-linked demand (client trading, not customer capex), counter-cyclical investment (kept investing and raised Rs.15bn during the downturn), and ~40% of gross revenue now from non-broking lines (client funding, distribution, wealth, AMC)*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 2 of the last 11 years with swings of ±34 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from 1% to 43% (swinging ±11.8 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 3% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 5%).

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Economics are set by regulators, not the company: SEBI's F&O rules materially cut the order base (recovery framed as coming despite 'full implementation of F&O regulations'), RBI directions on banks' capital-market exposure tighten intraday credit and force higher bank-guarantee deployment, and every-day-expiry rule changes drove working-capital needs sharply higher; management repeatedly frames the outlook around 'the regulatory framework evolved'.

> "the recent RBI directions relating to banks' capital market exposures... While intraday credit availability from banks may tighten, leading to higher deployment of bank guarantees" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Diversification into credit distribution, insurance, wealth and AMC reduces reliance on regulated broking; funding diversified across NBFCs, NCDs and CPs*
