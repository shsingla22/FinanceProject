# Astra Micro Wave (ASTRAMICRO) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Astra Microwave Products is a Hyderabad-based electronics maker that has been designing and building RF (radio frequency) and microwave components, subsystems and complete systems for about 30 years, selling mainly into defence, space, meteorology and hydrology. Its customers are largely Indian government-linked buyers — defence PSUs like Bharat Electronics, DRDO labs, the armed forces, ISRO and the India Meteorological Department — plus a smaller export book; it makes money by winning development and production contracts and delivering hardware against them, with orders typically executed over 12-24 months. Revenue was INR 1,157 crores in FY26 with a standalone order book of INR 2,141 crores as of March 2026, of which the December 2025 split was INR 1,477 crores from defence PSUs/DRDO, INR 369 crores metrology and hydrology, INR 249 crores space and INR 130 crores exports/deemed exports. Operations are centred on multiple manufacturing units around Hyderabad, Telangana, plus an R&D centre at Bangalore Aerospace Park, with a joint venture, Astra Rafael Comsys, alongside wholly-owned subsidiaries; management has in-principle approved demerging the space, meteorology and hydrology business into a separate entity.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Radar** — Management calls this the core area — transmit-receive modules for active phased array radars plus subsystems (power amplifiers, receivers, exciters, filters, synthesizers, converters) and full radar systems such as shipborne, coastal surveillance, anti-drone, ground penetration, telemetry, instrumentation and precision approach radars; it is the biggest piece, described as contributing nearly 60% of FY26 revenue and the largest chunk of the order pipeline.
- **Electronic Warfare (EW)** — Supplies EW subsystems and components — direction-finding receivers, passive homing heads for RF seekers, jammers, filters, amplifiers and wideband receivers — to Indian Air Force, Navy and Army programmes, including a DcPP win for EW suites on the Su-30 platform; sized in early calls at roughly INR 1,000 crores of potential orders over five to six years and a regular second contributor after radar.
- **Missile and Telemetry (guided weapons)** — Makes telemetry subsystems such as S-band FM transmitters, airborne RF transceivers, ground up/down converters, switch antenna systems and tracking systems, plus guided-weapon work like a terahertz proximity sensor developed with DRDO and subsystems for QRSAM; a smaller line, put at around INR 1,000 crores of five-to-six-year potential together with telemetry.
- **Space** — Supplies microwave components and subsystems for ground stations and satellite payloads, launch-vehicle microelectronics for private players, and checkout hardware for ISRO's Gaganyaan mission; grew from a small base to over 11% of revenue in Q1 FY25 and INR 249 crores of the December 2025 order book, and is described as a major long-term growth driver.
- **Meteorology and Hydrology** — Builds wind profile radars, Doppler weather radars and automatic weather stations, largely for IMD, an area where the company says few Indian firms can design and build the equipment; INR 369 crores of the December 2025 order book, and together with space it made up about 16% of FY26 revenue.
- **SATCOM / communications** — Provides SATCOM and MSS terminals for disaster communication and, more recently, software defined radios (SDR), which management flags as a notable recent order win alongside Doppler weather radars.
- **MMIC and microelectronics** — In-house chip-level capability for radar and communication systems, held under joint IP rights with DRDO labs, which management describes as a competitive edge and part of a proprietary-product pipeline rather than a separately sized revenue line.
- **Exports and deemed exports** — Sales outside India, a mix of build-to-print and build-to-spec work; once as high as 39% of revenue in Q1 FY23, it settled around 21% in Q1 FY25 and 12% of the consolidated order book, with INR 130 crores in the December 2025 book.
- **Astra Rafael Comsys (joint venture)** — A separately reported JV serving PSUs and the Ministry of Defence that closed FY26 with an order book of about INR 625 crores and is expected to deliver over INR 600 crores of revenue in FY27, though FY26 profit was hit by forex-related provisions.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Innovation Dominance — STRONG FIT
*"Out-inventing the competition"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence and the numbers agree (1 of 2 checks computable supporting).

**What the calls show:** Across all four sampled calls management names new developed products each year (Uttam AAAU, shipborne radar AAAU, D4 counter-drone radar, PATM telemetry radar, Doppler weather radars, TeraHertz proximity sensor), discloses rising R&D spend, runs an in-house MMIC design division since 2005, and guides that ~40% of FY27 sales will be R&D programmes — funded by 35-42% gross margins.

> "we are in the business of monetizing intellectual property... IP we believe gives us sustained power and the moat" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹51 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ❌ High retained margin (51% of sales) alongside 6% yearly growth — the flywheel is incomplete.

### Cost to Replicate — STRONG FIT
*"Could a newcomer copy this?"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** The replication barrier is concrete and repeatedly evidenced: 30+ years of DRDO qualification and joint IP rights, an in-house MMIC chip-design division built in 2005, and horizontal breadth across radar/EW/telemetry/space that management itself calls hard to replicate — a well-funded newcomer still needs a decade of programme qualification to compete.

> "this horizontal capability stack creates resilience, adaptability, and staying power. And more importantly, it forms a moat which is difficult to replicate" — *management, on an earnings call*
