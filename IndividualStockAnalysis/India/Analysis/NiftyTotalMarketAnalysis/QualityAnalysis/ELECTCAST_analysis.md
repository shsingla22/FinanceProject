# Electrosteel Castings (ELECTCAST) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Electrosteel Castings makes ductile iron (DI) pipes, cast iron pipes and pipe fittings — the large iron pipes used to carry drinking water and sewage — and is described by management as the leader in DI pipes and fittings in India. Its demand comes overwhelmingly from government-funded water infrastructure: the Jal Jeevan Mission (tap water to every household), AMRUT, urban water and sewage networks, with JJM alone said to be 50%-60% of industry demand, so revenue rises and falls with central and state fund releases. It earns money on the spread between selling price per tonne and raw-material cost (notably imported coking coal); management quotes volumes in lakh tonnes and a blended EBITDA margin that ran around 17% in FY24 but fell to roughly 9-10% in FY26 when JJM funds were frozen. Manufacturing is integrated (it makes its own coke, sponge iron, pig iron and ferroalloys and sells the surplus), with plants at Khardah in West Bengal and Srikalahasti, a registered office in Rajgangpur, Odisha, land being acquired in Odisha for a new plant, capacity growing from about 8.5 lakh to 10 lakh tonnes a year, and a growing export business plus an Italian subsidiary.

**The parts of the business:**

- **DI Pipes, Fittings and CI Pipes (domestic)** — The core business — ductile iron pipes, cast iron pipes and fittings sold into Indian water supply and sewage projects; volumes were about 7.47 lakh tonnes in FY24 and 5.84 lakh tonnes in FY26 (down 25%), and this is what drives nearly all of standalone revenue of roughly Rs. 5,200-7,000 crores.
- **Exports** — The same pipes sold overseas, largely to the Middle East; volumes grew 11% year-on-year in Q3 FY26 and 7% for full-year FY26, which management repeatedly credits with partly offsetting the domestic slump — though it flagged an expected hit to Middle East sales from March 2026 due to the US-Iran conflict.
- **Intermediary products (coke, sponge iron, pig iron, ferroalloys)** — Surplus output from its own integrated iron-making chain sold on the open market; a side line rather than the main event, but management says it "partly compensated" the top line when pipe volumes and prices fell in Q3 FY26, and pig iron sales were also called out as meaningful back in FY23.
- **T.I.S. Italy (acquired manufacturing subsidiary)** — An Italian manufacturing arm whose revenue grew 15% in calendar 2025 to EUR 41 million with improved EBITDA margins; small relative to the group, but management wants to double its revenue in four years and is using it to start valve-castings production in India.
- **Subsidiaries / consolidated operations** — Group companies beyond the standalone parent that add roughly Rs. 300-500 crores of extra income (consolidated FY26 income of Rs. 6,133 crores versus Rs. 5,228 crores standalone), with intra-group profit on unsold stock adjusted out at the consolidated level.

### The story in depth

Electrosteel Castings scores 33 out of 100 and is rated Weak (one star), built from 52 on business quality, 10 on multibagger fit and 28 on risk safety. The single clearest strength is financial housekeeping: standalone gross debt has been cut to Rs.1,202 crore in FY26 from over Rs.2,000 crore in FY24, net debt to equity is 0.31, land in Odisha was bought out of the company's own cash rather than new borrowing, and every Rs.100 of capital in the business still threw off Rs.15 of operating cash. Growth spending has been genuine expansion rather than mere upkeep — Rs.400 crore of debottlenecking in FY23, Rs.650 crore planned with Rs.390 crore already spent by March 2024, and a strikingly cheap 1 lakh tonne addition at Srikalahasthi for just Rs.64 crore. Management scores best of all the quality areas at 0.6. But the areas that decide whether a business can defend its profits — competitive advantage, customer benefits and capital allocation — all score zero, and industry structure scores minus 0.5. Two checks are outright negatives: about 278 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and pricing is not the company's to set, with FY26 bringing what management itself called a collapse in realisations.

The pattern screen tells the same story from a different angle: no pattern is a clean fit, four are partial and seven are outright misses, which is why multibagger fit contributes only 10 points. The partial fits all trace back to the same two real assets. First, backward integration into coke, sponge iron, pig iron, ferroalloys and a coal block, plus very cheap capacity additions, gives a low-cost position — enough for a partial tick on Low Price Plus. Second, ten overseas marketing subsidiaries selling on a stock-and-sale basis and the bolt-on purchase of T.I.S. Italy (EUR 41 million, 15% revenue growth in CY25) give real ownership of the export channel — a partial tick on Forward Integrators, and the same fact behind the geographical expansion check. Crucially, the trait that earns the low-cost tick is exactly the trait that kills the others: a cost advantage on an undifferentiated product sold into public tenders means no pricing power (management concedes it cannot control input costs and can only pass them on a quarter late), no brand (export prices are broadly the same as domestic), and no toll-road indispensability. Domestic share of 20–25% is held rather than gained, so Market Share Gainers is only partial — and the reason is visible in the Cost to Replicate miss, where Tata Steel, Jai Balaji and Shyam Metalics simply built the same thing, pushing industry capacity from 46–48 lakh tonnes toward 57 lakh tonnes in two years. The Corporate Culture partial rests on promises kept — debt cut on schedule, capacity delivered on schedule, and bad news including the dividend cut disclosed rather than buried.

The risk review makes explicit what the pattern screen implies. The two high risks, cyclicality and government dependency, are really one risk: Jal Jeevan Mission accounted for 50–60% of industry demand, and when central funds were frozen over implementation problems, roughly Rs.90,000 crore of spending vanished in a year, volumes fell from 7.47 lakh tonnes in FY24 to 5.84 lakh tonnes in FY26, standalone EBITDA margin fell from 17.7% to 9.6%, and the fourth quarter of FY26 produced a standalone loss. Note the double edge here: the very same government programme is what earns the cyclical-market check its best score of 2, because the company is demonstrably at the bottom of the cycle with JJM 2.0's Rs.8.69 lakh crore outlay and enquiries already restarting — the biggest risk and the clearest recovery trigger are the same fact. Exports are the main offset, at 23% of fourth-quarter FY26 revenue and growing 11% quarter-on-quarter while domestic volumes collapsed, but roughly half of them go to one Middle East region, so the cure carries its own concentration. Two elevated risks — cheaper alternative materials such as OPVC in the 100–200mm range, which management answered defensively rather than with evidence, and the good-enough-goods problem of an identical product bought on price alone — attack the same undefended middle that the pattern screen flagged. New entrants and end-demand concentration sit at watch level. The saving grace is that resilience is judged SOUND: the low debt, the honest disclosure and the cost position mean the company should survive the trough intact. The verdict is a durable, competently run, cheap producer of a commodity with no way to protect its prices, whose fortunes are set almost entirely by one government's budget.

*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**What the calls show:** There is genuine backward integration (coke, sponge iron, pig iron, ferroalloys, a coal block) and repeated cost-reduction language, plus low-capex debottlenecking (1 lakh tonne for Rs. 64 crores), but no itemised savings culture and margins that swing from 17% to 4.7% show scale does not confer a durable cost edge.

### Forward Integrators — PARTIAL
*"Owns the shop, not just the product"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 2 checks supporting).

> "We have ten overseas subsidiaries and we are present into exports since last two decades" — *management, on an earnings call*

*"Winning customers from rivals"*

> "We have around 20%-25% of the domestic market" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Sales compounding at 9% a year — below the 12% pace the share-gainer pattern looks for.

### Toll Roads — NO FIT
*"Small cost, can't skip it"*

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Government Dependency, Technological Innovation, Good Enough Goods, Fashion Risk. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is tied to government water-infrastructure capex, not customer operations, and the swing is extreme: FY24 volumes 7.47 lakh t with 17.7% standalone EBITDA margin, versus FY26 volumes 5.84 lakh t (-25%) with 9.6% margin and a Q4FY26 standalone PAT loss of Rs.10.7 crores; margins also swing with imported coking coal (from $670 to $200/t in FY23) which management concedes it cannot control. The company expanded capacity from ~7 to 10 lakh tonnes plus a greenfield Odisha plant into what management itself later called 'the upper end', and the industry now runs at ~50% capacity.

> "most definitely this is not the new normal, this is the rock bottom... at the time of the last year and the year before that, we were quite certain that, that is also not a normal, that is the upper end" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Pipes are a one-time infrastructure spend but replacement of ageing pipelines, urbanisation and exports (23% of Q4FY26 revenue) provide a partial non-JJM base; net debt reduced to Rs.812 crores, so the balance sheet absorbed the downturn.*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 4 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 6% and 16% (±2.6 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 3% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 6%).

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** This is the dominant risk and the exact mechanism in the solar cautionary tale: Jal Jeevan Mission was 50-60% of industry demand, and when the centre froze funds over implementation irregularities, spending fell by ~Rs.90,000 crores in one year, volumes fell 26-31% Y-o-Y and NSR 'collapsed'. Across the 2022-2026 arc management framed demand almost entirely as 'push from the government', budget allocations, cabinet approvals and tender releases.

> "the funds against JJM have been put on a total hold so far. So it has reduced the demand so substantially, I think which has created a practically NSR collapse" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Exports (~23% of revenue, ten overseas subsidiaries, largest exporter to the Middle East), the T.I.S. Italy acquisition, and diversification into valves and paints are deliberate moves away from policy-driven revenue — though exports are themselves now hit by the US-Iran conflict.*

## What to watch

- Whether JJM 2.0 money actually reaches contractors and turns restarting enquiries into orders — this is the trigger behind the cyclical-market check's score of 2 and the release valve on the Government Dependency high risk.
