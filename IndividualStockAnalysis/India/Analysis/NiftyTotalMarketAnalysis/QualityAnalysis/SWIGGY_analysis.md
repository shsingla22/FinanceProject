# Swiggy (SWIGGY) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Swiggy Limited is an Indian company, headquartered in Bengaluru and listed on the BSE and NSE (symbol SWIGGY), that runs consumer apps through which people order things for delivery to their homes. It has two main businesses the management talks about: a food delivery marketplace (restaurant meals ordered on the app) and Instamart, a quick-commerce service that delivers groceries and household items in a short time from a network of small local warehouses called dark stores. It makes money by keeping a cut, or "take rate," of the order value plus delivery and platform charges, and increasingly from advertising and monetization deals with the brands whose products sell on the platform; it spends heavily the other way in customer incentives, discounts and marketing. It operates only in India, across metro cities and, more recently, satellite areas and Tier 2 and Tier 3 towns.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Food Marketplace (Food Delivery)** — The restaurant food-ordering and delivery business, led by its own CEO (Rohit Kapoor); management describes it as the mature, profitable side — gross order value grew about 20.5% year-on-year and monthly transacting users about 22% in the December 2025 quarter, with both contribution and adjusted EBITDA margins expanding, and guidance of 18%-20% growth.
- **Instamart (Quick Commerce)** — The fast grocery and household-goods delivery business run from company-operated dark stores, led by its own CEO (Amitesh Jha); it is the fast-growing but loss-making side — order value grew 108% year-on-year in one quarter, the store count went from about 800 in January 2025 to 316 more stores added in the March 2025 quarter alone, and management is targeting break-even at the contribution-margin level somewhere between the December 2025 and June 2026 quarters.
- **Advertising and brand monetization** — Fees and marketing money earned from the brands and sellers whose products are listed, which management says rises automatically as the platform's scale with each brand grows and is one of the "structural" levers pulling Instamart toward profitability.
- **Private labels and differentiated assortment (e.g. Noice, Triply)** — Swiggy's own or co-created products sold on Instamart — Noice is described as a "clean label" brand in categories like bread and high-protein eggs, and Triply is a cookware push — intended mainly to make customers stickier rather than to maximise margin, though management says it is margin-positive; it is still early and they decline to size it.
- **Supply chain and warehousing infrastructure** — The dark stores plus a larger warehouse network behind them, which management says more than doubled in capacity over four quarters and is a main use of capital spending, because it shortens the "middle mile" and lets stores be restocked faster, especially in smaller towns.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Swiggy's genuine strengths sit on the demand side and in the operating machine behind it. The quality framework gives full marks to convenience, describing a dense last-mile network across 130 cities that serves over 90% of demand, with 30,000 SKUs in metros and 10-minute delivery, and full marks to structural end-market growth, with quick commerce penetration described as very low outside the top 20 cities against a INR1 trillion medium-term ambition and food delivery still early. Sales have compounded at roughly 37% a year. Those same traits are what earn the two best pattern verdicts. Market Share Gainers is a likely fit because food delivery has kept taking share, moving from 19.2% year-on-year growth in Q3FY25 to 20.5% order value and 22% monthly user growth by Q3FY26, running ahead of the company's own 18-20% guidance. Innovation Dominance is a likely fit on the strength of a rapid launch cadence with disclosed results, including Bolt reaching 425 cities in three to four months and 9% of orders in a quarter, Maxxsaver at 28% user penetration, plus the 99 store, Noice and Toing.

The trouble is that the traits driving those patterns are the same ones generating the risks, so the story does not have two separate halves. Management's own account is that switching costs are essentially zero and customers hop between platforms on price. That single fact scores minus two on the competition check, and it is also why three patterns fail outright: Pricing Power (the trajectory is price deflation, with cart discounting, wallet subsidies, no-fee orders below INR299 and Maxxsaver basket discounts), Cost to Replicate (dark stores and warehouses are leasable and new entrants keep arriving), and Toll Roads. It is equally the root of the highest-rated risk, Good Enough Goods, where quick commerce sells largely identical third-party brands and management concedes it had to match rivals on no-fee and lower minimum orders. The Low Price Plus pattern shows the same double edge: there is a real cost engine in densification and doubled warehousing, but it only rates partial because the returns test fails, with quick commerce still loss-making at the contribution line. New Entrants is rated elevated for the same reason, dominating every call from February 2025 to May 2026, and it lands directly in the numbers: barriers to entry score minus two on margin swings of plus or minus 34.1 points, the operating margin is minus 14%, and the cash return on capital is minus 14%. Industry Structure and Return on Capital are the two areas scoring below zero, at minus 1.33 each.

What keeps this from being a straightforwardly poor case is the quality of the people running it and the safety of the underlying demand. Management is the strongest area at 0.8, with full marks for candour: the records credit the company with owning the INR14 per order contribution deterioration, admitting store additions were back-ended in FY25, and saying plainly that a strong basket-size print was seasonal rather than structural. It also set hard numeric promises and met them, guiding to 4 million square feet of footprint and delivering 4.3 million, and calling contribution losses as peaked before improving about 5.5 percentage points in a year. Four of the eight risk channels come out low, because demand is habitual household consumption spread across restaurants, brand partners and consumers rather than anything cyclical, concentrated or fashion-led, and the shift toward small frequent online baskets runs in the company's favour. Overall resilience is judged sound. But the framework also notes this is one of India's most scrutinised listed consumer-internet names, so there is no hidden-gem angle, and the fast-mutating format landscape that powers the innovation pattern is itself flagged as a technology risk because the company must keep re-platforming, with SNACC shut down in Q4 FY26. The combined rating of 45 out of 100, from 46 on business quality, 26 on multibagger fit and 66 on risk safety, lands at Mixed, or two stars, which is a fair reflection of strong execution inside a structurally unforgiving industry.
