# Polycab India (POLYCAB) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Polycab India makes and sells electrical products, above all electric wires and power cables, which are its largest business by far and are used in homes, buildings, factories and infrastructure projects. It sells partly through a large distribution network of dealers, retailers, electricians and contractors (its 'channel' or B2C side) and partly directly to large institutional and project customers (B2B), plus a smaller export/international business selling to overseas customers. Alongside cables it runs a consumer-facing FMEG (fast-moving electrical goods) business of fans, lights, switches, switchgears, pumps, conduit pipes and solar products, and a small EPC/projects arm. It is based in India, with its registered office in Gujarat and corporate office in Mumbai; sales are spread across the West, North, South and East regions, and revenue crossed INR 285 billion in FY26 with a net-cash balance sheet.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Wires & Cables** — Manufactures and sells electrical wires and power cables to both trade channel (dealers/retailers) and institutional customers, and is described by management as the company's largest business, growing 39% year-on-year in Q1 FY27 with EBIT margins of 13.3% against a guided 11–13% medium-term range.
- **International / export business (within Wires & Cables)** — Sells cables to customers overseas, contributing about 6–9% of consolidated revenue in recent years, with management targeting a double-digit contribution over the medium term; it declined in Q1 FY27 on geopolitical disruption but still carries a healthy order book.
- **FMEG (Fast Moving Electrical Goods)** — The consumer-products arm selling fans, lighting, switches, switchgears, pumps, conduit pipes and solar, much smaller than cables (about Rs 305 crore in a quarter back in FY23, with thin or negative margins for several years) but growing fast and outperforming industry growth for ten straight quarters by Q1 FY27.
- **Solar (largest category inside FMEG)** — Sells solar products to consumers, now the biggest category within FMEG and its main growth engine, more than doubling year-on-year in Q1 FY27 on the back of the PM Surya Ghar Yojana and state-level rooftop-solar incentives.
- **Others / EPC** — A small engineering-procurement-construction (project execution) business reported under 'other segments' — around Rs 75 crore of revenue in a quarter in FY23 — which carries lower margins than the rest of the company.

### The story in depth

Polycab scores 74 out of 100 — a Strong, four-star rating built from 73 on business quality, 71 on multibagger fit and 78 on risk safety, with overall resilience judged sound. The single thread running through all three analyses is distribution. Roughly 90% of sales go through the channel, the company keeps adding districts, distributors and retailers (about 100 distributors and 4,000 retailers in the period covered), and it runs dedicated key account managers and influencer programmes for the electricians and contractors who actually specify the product. That reach is what the quality framework calls the core capability and the source of competitive advantage, and it is what has driven the strongest pattern in the record: share of the organised wires and cables market rising from 18-19% in FY19 to 26-27% in FY25 to 30-31% in FY26, a gain of 300-400 basis points in FY26 alone, alongside exports going from 48 to 94 countries and an FMEG business that has grown faster than its industry for ten straight quarters. A large unorganised segment is steadily donating that share, and management treats the shift to organised players as permanent rather than cyclical.

The financial machinery behind this is unusually clean, and it is the same machinery that supports the culture pattern. Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.41 of sales, and for every ₹100 of capital in the business the company generated ₹31 of operating cash this year. Working capital has been run tight — 25 days in Q4FY26 and 15 days in Q1FY27 against a 45-50 day normal — leaving ₹41.9bn of net cash and debt-to-equity of 0.02x. That balance sheet is what lets the company step up capex to a record ₹14.8bn in FY26 and commit to a ₹60-80bn five-year growth programme in extra-high-voltage cable and new capacity, all while posting record profit, growing organically with no acquisitions over five years, and doing so counter to a volatile macro backdrop. The track record matches the talk: the ₹20,000 crore Project Leap revenue target set in FY22 was hit a full year early, and a successor plan to FY2030 is already running ahead of guidance. End markets help — electrification, renewables and solar, metro rail, data centres, with the data-centre cable opportunity sized at ₹20,000-25,000 crore — and brand and product work adds to it, with ranges launched in the last two to three years now contributing almost half of retail wire sales.

The catch is that the very trait powering the share gains is also the source of the main risks, and the analysis is honest about it. The product is a commodity-linked, cost-plus cable: prices are reset monthly against copper and aluminium benchmarks and the rupee-dollar rate. That pass-through is why the pricing power pattern is scored a clear no fit, why industry structure is the weakest area in the quality framework by a wide margin, and why cyclicality is the one elevated risk — dealers destocked when metal prices fell in mid-2022, and margins compressed when copper rallied 14% in Q2FY25. Because pricing is not a defence, the moat has to come from scale, backward integration and channel density instead, which is exactly why the cost-to-replicate and low-price patterns land at likely or partial rather than strong. The same channel-led model that keeps the business asset-light also puts electricians, contractors and distributors between Polycab and the end buyer, which is the mechanism behind the good-enough-goods risk and behind the constant influencer and seminar spending needed to hold that relationship; lighting prices have eroded, with downlights down 5-10% in Q2FY25. Two further items are on watch: aggressive competitors chasing volume at the cost of margin in retail wires, and rising policy-linked revenue through the government RDSS scheme, an ₹8,000 crore Bharat Net contract at guided high-single-digit margins, and solar demand tied to PM Surya Ghar — though the EPC exposure is bounded at mid-to-high single digits of consolidated revenue. Technology change, customer concentration, shifting tastes and fashion risk are all judged low.

## What to watch

- Market share gainers pattern: the 300-400 basis point annual gain in organised wires and cables slowing or stalling — this is the single strongest pattern and the main support for the growth and competitive advantage scores.
- Cyclicality (elevated risk): a sharp copper, aluminium or rupee move that squeezes margins the way the 14% copper rally did in Q2FY25, or dealer destocking of the kind seen in mid-2022, since prices reset monthly and there is no pricing power to absorb it.
- Capex for growth check and Project Spring: whether the ₹60-80bn five-year programme and the record ₹14.8bn FY26 capex convert into revenue and returns, or whether the ₹41.9bn net cash and 0.02x debt position erodes without matching growth.
- Good-enough-goods and new-entrants risks: further price erosion of the downlight type (down 5-10% in Q2FY25) spreading from lighting into retail wires, or competitors chasing volume at the cost of margin forcing Polycab to follow.
- Government dependency (watch risk): EPC and solar exposure growing past the guided mid-to-high single-digit share of consolidated revenue, which would tie more of the business to thin-margin policy contracts like the ₹8,000 crore Bharat Net order.
