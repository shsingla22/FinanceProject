## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 27 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 6 improved, 3 regressed, 18 unchanged. 5 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them).

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Allocation | Neutral (+0.00) | Neutral (+0.00) | +0.00 |
| Return on Capital | Good (+1.00) | Good (+1.33) | +0.33 |
| Growth | Neutral (+0.33) | Neutral (+0.33) | +0.00 |
| Management | Good (+0.67) | Good (+1.00) | +0.33 |

| Customer Benefits | Good (+1.00) | Good (+1.00) | +0.00 |
| Competitive Advantage | Good (+0.67) | Neutral (+0.33) | -0.33 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: CPCB IV+ emission norms plus after-treatment and localization requirements raise the entry bar, and post-transition pricing has stabilized with management holding prices rather than discounting. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.9 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: CPCB-IV+ raised technical barriers, but new credible entrants are landing large data-centre orders on imported engines and export markets see dumping, so entry barriers and pricing rationality are eroding at the high-value end.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Good → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹21 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹21 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±2.9 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: The company is coming off a strong up-year (FY26 sales +18%) and now guides only to 'moderate growth' with caution on commodity inflation and supply constraints, while industrial (construction/mining) is in a trough — a mixed rather than bottom-of-cycle setup. | The long view had said: The company is nearer a cyclical peak than a trough — after 18% growth and record margins it guides only 'moderate' FY27 growth, compressors are entering a downcycle and road construction has slowed.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: The strong headline numbers are decomposable because management itself separates the drivers — lumpy data-centre project execution and post-CPCB IV+ volume normalization versus ~20%/near-double-digit core power gen growth — so results are not mistaken for uniform skill. | The long view had said: Recent record results owe a lot to non-repeatable externals — CPCB-IV+ regulatory pricing, commodity softness, one-time supplier benefits and lumpy data-centre execution — which management itself calls a 'perfect storm', so skill is only partly separable from tailwind.
- **Candor** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Management repeatedly volunteers unflattering detail — quantifying the INR50 crore one-time expense true-up, disaggregating lumpy data-centre revenue from core power gen growth, and admitting it lacks market-share data rather than claiming gains. | The long view had said: Management proactively warns against extrapolating peak margins, flags one-off true-ups with quantification, and admits it cannot see market share — though it repeatedly declines volume-vs-price and data-centre splits.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: FY26 exports rose 12% (HHP +20%) on end-market focus, but Q4 exports fell 6% year-on-year with LHP down 18%, and management repeatedly refuses to commit to export growth given tariffs and geopolitics. | The long view had said: Exports have been the persistent weak spot — down 22% year-on-year in Q1FY25, no identifiable regional trend for years, dumping pressure in South Asia/LatAm/Africa, and FY27 export outlook explicitly withheld.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Excellent → Neutral. Now: Growth is volume- and project-mix-driven rather than price-led — volumes are back to pre-CPCB IV+ levels and pricing has merely 'settled' under continuing competition, with management unable to separate price from volume. | The long view had said: Growth has been led by pricing and CPCB-IV+ mix — record ~37.8% gross margins from price actions plus richer mix, with demand proving inelastic to a 15-20% regulatory price step-up.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Excellent → Good. Now: The Distribution business compounded 21-26% year-on-year every quarter of the window to INR3,278 crores in FY26, growing even when equipment sales fell — a service reach that carries the franchise through cyclical dips. | The long view had said: The distribution and service business is the standout structural asset — INR3,278cr and growing 22-26%, servicing even parent-executed projects, with globally transferred learning and a dedicated data-centre service focus rivals cannot easily replicate.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: The Indian genset market behaves like a competitive oligopoly rather than a comfortable one — management concedes persistent competitive pressure and deal-by-deal price evaluation, implying customers retain negotiating leverage. | The long view had said: Tightening emission norms concentrate the market among a few technologically capable players, and end customers treat the genset as a small share of project cost with little negotiating leverage.

*Unchanged: Mini-monopolies; Return on capital (headline); Structural end-market growth; Security by obscurity; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Assurance benefits; Research and development; Technology advantage; Disciplined stewards; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Asset turn; Gain in market share; Intangible benefits; Convenience benefits; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Out of limelight; Cost of working capital.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 3 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Toll Roads**: STRONG FIT → NO FIT. Why now: A genset is a large capital line item for the buyer, not a sub-1% critical input, and management repeatedly describes active competitive pricing pressure and deal-by-deal negotiation — the opposite of customers paying without negotiating. (The long view had said: Across calls management argues the genset is a small share of a project's capital cost yet mission-critical (backup power, 99.99% uptime for data centres), which is why 15-20% CPCB-IV+ price increases did not dent demand; regulatory/emission certification (CPCB-IV+) anchors the product and the Aug-2024 and Feb-2026 calls both lean on criticality rather than negotiation.)
- **Pricing Power**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management says post-CPCB IV+ pricing has 'settled' and that they endeavour to hold price while volumes returned to pre-transition levels, and PBT grew 24% on 18% sales in FY26 — but this is price defence under acknowledged competition, not repeated above-inflation increases; they explicitly declined to split volume vs price. (The long view had said: From Aug-2022 (multiple price tranches to recover commodity inflation, explicit refusal to give the increases back) through Aug-2024 (5-year-high 37.8% gross margin from pricing + CPCB-IV+ mix) to FY26 (18% revenue growth, PBT +24%), price rises were taken repeatedly without volume loss; management frames emission upgrades as a price-realisation event.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The four calls contain no discussion of brand heritage, A&P spend, distributor leverage or brand-led premium pricing; the conversation is entirely segment volumes, execution and margins. (The long view had said: Customers are said to value the Cummins end-to-end proposition and global learning over cheaper imported alternatives, and the name carries into adjacent segments (rail, marine, defence, hydrogen); but no heritage narrative, A&P spend or brand-led premium is ever articulated on the calls.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (LIKELY FIT); Forward Integrators (LIKELY FIT).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 2 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Data centres alone contributed 30-35% of domestic Powergen revenue in FY26 (~35% in Q4), a lumpy, project-based, few-large-buyer segment (hyperscalers and colos) whose absence single-handedly caused the Q3 Powergen decline. On the supply side, the high-horsepower engines driving that growth come from the parent group — QSK60 from CTIPL and the QSK95 imported — with analysts pressing repeatedly on related-party dependence and localisation. (The long view had said: Two concentrations compound: dependence on parent Cummins Inc. for the largest engines (QSK95 imported, QSK60 via CTIPL, "pretty much on allocation basis" for the biggest data-centre sets, lead times lengthening as global data-centre demand absorbs parent capacity), and end-market concentration in data centres, which rose to 30-35% of domestic PowerGen revenue in FY26 with a handful of hyperscalers and colo players, making revenue visibly lumpy (PowerGen -16% in Q3FY26 purely because a data-centre execution fell in the prior quarter).)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Management describes customers moving toward a blended energy mix — solar plus storage alongside diesel backup — which over time erodes the diesel genset demand base independent of the cycle; the company is responding by adding BESS to the portfolio rather than defending diesel alone. No volume decline has yet been attributed to this shift in the window. (The long view had said: The obvious erosion thesis — a more reliable grid and cleaner-energy attitudes reducing diesel backup demand — was put to management directly and answered with evidence to the contrary: India stopped being power-deficient yet backup demand kept growing, and CPCB-IV+ price increases of 15-20% did not dent volumes.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 8 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
