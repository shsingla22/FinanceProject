**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Regulatory licensing and funding-cost access provide some entry friction and NIM expanded rather than compressed, indicating no discount war in its chosen segments, but lending remains an easily entered, capital-driven business. | The long view had said: Pricing discipline exists on HDB's side but the industry does not reward it — they had to abandon high-value products because returns were competed away, implying rivals underwriting at lower risk-adjusted returns.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Customer franchise grew ~19% year-on-year to 23.9M with record quarterly disbursements and consumer durables book up over 50% year-on-year, off a small base in a vast, under-penetrated Indian retail-credit market — clear runway plus evidence of capture. | The long view had said: Pockets of clear share capture (consumer durables +50% year-on-year, gold book doubled) are offset by book growth of only 11.3% year-on-year versus their own nominal-GDP+6-7% aspiration and deliberate vacating of HCV/tractor-trailer segments, so net share movement is flat.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: A recently listed, heavily covered large-cap NBFC with an HDFC Bank parent and a high-profile IPO is the opposite of an ignored niche. | The long view had said: The opposite of obscure — a headline HDFC Bank subsidiary IPO covered on every call by Motilal Oswal, ICICI, Nomura, IIFL and Macquarie, with heavy institutional and regulatory attention.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Dense presence beyond the top-20 cities gives local density advantages, but retail loans carry negligible switching costs and borrowers can refinance freely, so no captive position exists. | The long view had said: Switching costs are low and customers refinance away — analysts flag balance-transfer-out driving enterprise-lending repayment rates up to ~11% from ~9%, the opposite of a captive book.

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
