# Lloyds Metals (LLOYDSME) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Lloyds Metals and Energy is an Indian mining-and-metals company whose core business is digging iron ore out of a single very large mine at Hedri/Surjagarh in the Gadchiroli district of Maharashtra and selling it to steel makers, mostly in India with some exports. It also converts part of that ore into higher-value products at its own plants — iron ore pellets, sponge iron (DRI) and electricity — and is now moving further downstream into actual steelmaking, with a blast furnace, arc furnace and a 1.2 million tonne wire rod mill due to start in FY27. It makes money on the spread between low-cost captive ore and the price of ore, pellets and sponge iron, helped by an 85 km slurry pipeline that pipes ore from the mine to the plants instead of trucking it; margins run around 34% EBITDA, ROCE 56% and the company describes itself as gross debt-free. Scale has grown very fast: iron ore output went from 10 million tonnes to 22 million tonnes in FY26, consolidated revenue crossed INR 17,000 crores and consolidated profit INR 3,829 crores, and the group has recently added a mining-contracting arm (Thriveni) and a copper/cobalt business in the Democratic Republic of Congo.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Iron ore (mining)** — Mines and sells iron ore from the Hedri/Surjagarh mine in Gadchiroli — the biggest and most profitable part of the company, scaled from 10 million tonnes to 22 million tonnes in FY26 with mine capacity approved up to 55 million tonnes and FY27 guidance of 26 million tonnes production and 27 million tonnes of dispatches, earning roughly INR 1,800–2,000 EBITDA per tonne.
- **Pellets** — Turns its own iron ore into pellets at two plants at Konsari totalling 8 million tonnes capacity, commissioned in FY26 and ramped to full utilisation within about four months; management calls Lloyds the largest merchant pellet player in India, with realisations around INR 10,289 and EBITDA around INR 4,535 per tonne, and a third pellet plant is being engineered at Chandrapur.
- **DRI / sponge iron** — Makes sponge iron (direct reduced iron) at Konsari and Chandrapur, with capacity expanded during FY26 — volumes grew 56% year-on-year to roughly 0.4–0.5 million tonnes and are guided at 825,000 tonnes for FY27 — though management repeatedly describes sponge iron realisations and margins as muted.
- **Power** — Runs captive power plants alongside the sponge iron units, tripled to about 100 megawatts, selling around 188 million units in FY24; a small, steady contributor rather than a profit driver.
- **Steel (forward integration)** — The planned next step: a blast furnace, arc furnace, coke oven and a 1.2 million tonne wire rod mill at Chandrapur, commissioning in the last quarter of FY27 with about 150,000 tonnes of wire rod planned in FY27, as part of a longer-term 4.2 million tonne steel ambition split between flat and long products.
- **Logistics — slurry pipelines** — An 85 km pipeline already carries ore from the mine at Hedri to Konsari, and a planned 195 km line from Hedri to Ghugus via a Chandrapur stockyard is expected to cut freight by more than INR 500 per tonne; management frames logistics as a core pillar of cost leadership, with total cost savings targeted above INR 2,000 crores a year by March 2028.
- **Beneficiation (BHQ)** — A plant under construction to upgrade low-grade BHQ ore into usable feed, with pilot yields described as excellent, European equipment ordered and first phase targeted for December 2027 — management calls it a first-of-its-kind project in India.
- **Thriveni (mine developer and operator, MDO)** — An acquired mining-contracting business that operates mines for others, including Surjagarh plus expanding operations in Odisha and Jharkhand with three new mines starting; it was ranked number 1 among 383 open cast mines in India by the Ministry of Coal, and its finance director now presents on Lloyds' earnings calls.
- **Copper and cobalt (DRC, Congo)** — A newer international arm in the Katanga copper belt of the Democratic Republic of Congo — the Surya copper plant was commissioned in six months and is targeted to reach 30,000 tonnes a year, and Lloyds bought 49% of CHEMAF, a copper-cobalt platform with 50-plus permits; combined ambition is 100,000 tonnes of copper and about 20,000 tonnes of cobalt over the next three to five years, described as a medium-term growth engine rather than a current earner.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Lloyds Metals earns its rating on one asset above all others: a single captive iron ore mine in Gadchiroli whose approved capacity was lifted from 10 million tons to 55 million tons and which pays no royalty premium until 2057, connected to buyers by a proprietary 85km slurry pipeline (a second 195km line is planned) plus self-funded roads and rail sidings. That one fact drives most of what the screens liked. It is why the Cost to Replicate pattern is a strong fit and why the quality framework gave full marks to distribution advantage — no rival at that location can copy the evacuation route. It is why Market Share Gainers is also a strong fit: iron ore volumes more than doubled in a single year and the company went from nothing to India's largest merchant pellet seller, winning its first RINL open-market tender. And it is why the risk review scores New Entrants as low, since roughly 100 million tons of competing supply is shifting from cheap allotted leases onto auction leases carrying 125-200% premiums — the 'share donators' check — leaving Lloyds structurally cheaper than the people it competes with.

The same trait cuts the other way, and that is the heart of the story. First, a mine's capacity, its clearances and its incentives are all granted by government, so the review rates Government Dependency as high risk using the company's own history: the FY25 volume shortfall came purely from an environmental clearance slipping, the jump to 55 million tons happened only through what management called a massive regulatory win, and about ₹2,500 crores of Maharashtra incentives sit inside the returns. The asset that creates the moat is the asset the state controls. Second, a cost advantage is not a price advantage. Because the product is undifferentiated bulk ore, pellets and DRI sold on benchmarks, the screens rejected Pricing Power, Brand Strength, Toll Roads and Friendly Middleman outright and flagged Good Enough Goods as a watch item — management openly declined to raise prices when it could and says it maintains pricing parity with competitors, with realisations essentially flat between FY25 and FY26. So every rupee of growth has to come from volume, which is exactly why the Cyclicality risk is rated high: the whole earnings base is commodity, and roughly ₹13,500 crores of capex over four years has been committed against it. Third, that spending shows up in the weakest quality area: return on capital scores negative and asset turnover is penalised at ₹0.41 of sales per ₹1 of assets, even though the capex itself is almost entirely growth capex earning an exceptional 56% return before work-in-progress.

What is left is a company that executes well but is stretched, which is why resilience reads as strained and the combined rating lands at 55 out of 100 — 61 for business quality, 56 for multibagger fit, 44 for risk safety. On the credit side, the FY24 roadmap of pipeline, pellet plant, DRI, expanded clearance and steel entry was published and then delivered close to schedule in a difficult region; the pellet plant hit full utilisation within four months; forward integration is real, with value-added products rising from 20% to 32% of standalone revenue in one year; and there is genuine process work on BHQ beneficiation with outside technology partners aimed at a first-of-its-kind Indian plant by December 2027. On the debit side, the Corporate Culture pattern is only a partial fit because of an unmet FY24 promise, the growth record is marked down for being lumpy despite averaging about 34% a year, and the acquisition check is negative: absorbing the Thriveni mine-operating business and then buying 49% of a copper-cobalt platform in the DRC is a large jurisdictional and operational leap away from the single mine that the entire cost advantage rests on. Concentration is bounded but present — Tata Steel is the sole consumer of the BRPL pellet plant under a take-or-pay contract — and the customer base is deliberately being broadened. In short: a rare low-cost asset, an execution team that has delivered on it, and a business whose upside depends on volumes it does not fully control and prices it has chosen not to push.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

**Overall:** the company partly passes the foundation every multibagger shares (steady cash + high returns on capital + growth), 2 of 3 testable checks passing, and fits 4 of the 11 patterns: Market Share Gainers, Cost to Replicate, Low Price Plus, Innovation Dominance.

### Market Share Gainers — STRONG FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence and the numbers agree (1 of 1 check computable supporting).

**What the calls show:** Volumes scaled from 10mt to 22mt of iron ore in a single year with EC raised 10mt→55mt, and the company became the largest merchant pellet player in India within months of commissioning, winning its first RINL open-market pellet tender; share is being taken on cost and volume, not by sacrificing margin (EBITDA/t stable at ~INR1,900).

> "reaching capacity of 8 million tons, making us the largest merchant pellet player by far" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Sales compounding at 34% while margins held or improved (5% → 30%) — growth that was WON, not bought with price cuts.

### Cost to Replicate — STRONG FIT
*"Could a newcomer copy this?"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** The advantage rests on a finite, un-replicable asset: a single 55mt-EC captive mine in Gadchiroli with no royalty premium until 2057 — while ~20mt/year of rival capacity migrates to auction routes at 125-200% premiums — plus an 85km slurry pipeline and clearances that took a decade and a difficult security environment to win.

> "This will become one of the largest mines in the country, irrespective of any mineral, except coal." — *management, on an earnings call*

### Low Price Plus — LIKELY FIT
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern, but the numbers push back (0 of 1 check supporting) — held one step below a full confirmation.

**What the calls show:** The entire thesis management repeats from May 2024 to May 2026 is structural cost leadership at scale — zero-premium royalty mine to 2057, the 85km slurry pipeline saving INR500-1,250/ton, a second 195km pipeline planned, and guided cost savings surpassing INR2,000 crores p.a. — delivering 33.8% EBITDA margin and 56% ROCE ex-CWIP while holding prices at parity with competitors.

> "our mines will fetch no premiums royalty till 2057, which is a big differentiator from every other steel maker in the country" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹89 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 0.4× a year — not the high-turnover, low-margin machine this pattern needs.

### Innovation Dominance — LIKELY FIT
*"Out-inventing the competition"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and every one of the 2 computable checks supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** No R&D spend is ever disclosed, but there is genuine process innovation: a BHQ beneficiation pilot plant run since FY25, technology partners in China then FLSmidth, and India's first plant of its kind targeted for Dec 2027, plus LNG-hybrid and 88 electric mining units.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 3 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** The entire earnings base is commodity iron ore, pellets, DRI, power and now steel and copper, with management itself conceding the unpredictability of pricing, while FY24–FY26 saw an aggressive expansionary capex of ~INR13,500 crores against a INR33,000 crore roadmap plus a DRC copper entry — the textbook peak-cycle over-investment pattern; realizations already wobbled (Q4 FY25 iron ore realization down 6% year-on-year, EBITDA/ton falling, sponge and power realizations 'muted').

> "Regarding EBITDA, steel is a cyclical business. Between March and April, the price has gone up by Rs. 2,500 to Rs. 3,000. Why? Difficult to answer." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Structurally low-cost position — no-premium royalty till 2057, captive ore, 85km slurry pipeline saving ~INR500-850/ton, and a rising value-added mix (32% of FY26 revenue vs 20% FY25) that management argues reduces volatility; capex funded largely from internal accruals with modest net debt of INR3,901 crores.*

- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from 3% to 36% (swinging ±12.2 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 58% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 8% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

## What to watch

- Any slippage in environmental clearances or a change to the Maharashtra incentives — the Government Dependency risk already caused the FY25 volume miss and underwrites the 55mt capacity the Market Share Gainers case depends on.
- Whether realisations stay flat while volumes grow — under the failed Pricing Power pattern, growth has only one lever, so a commodity downturn hits the high Cyclicality risk with no price defence.
- Progress and cost on the DRC copper-cobalt stake and the Thriveni absorption — the merger and acquisition check is already negative, and trouble there would pull management away from the single mine that drives everything.
- Asset turnover and return on capital as the ₹13,500 crores of capex converts — the weakest quality area today; if the pellet, DRI and steel assets do not fill up, the 56% return before work-in-progress will not hold.
- Delivery of the second 195km slurry pipeline and the December 2027 beneficiation plant — these carry the Cost to Replicate and Innovation Dominance patterns, and the Corporate Culture record is already blemished by one unmet promise.
