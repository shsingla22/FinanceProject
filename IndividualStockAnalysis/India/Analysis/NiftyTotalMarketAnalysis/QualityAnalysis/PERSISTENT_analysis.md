# Persistent Systems Ltd. (PERSISTENT) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: IT*

Persistent Systems is an India-headquartered technology services company (founded ~1990, based in Pune, listed on NSE/BSE) that management describes as "AI-led, platform-driven digital engineering" — it is hired by other companies to build, modernize and run their software, cloud and data systems, and it explicitly positions itself as a technology/engineering services firm rather than a general-purpose IT services firm. It makes money mostly by billing customers for people-based services under contracts it books as TCV (total contract value) and ACV (annual contract value), which then convert into revenue over following quarters; renewals and new deals across existing and new clients both count. Revenue was $241.5 million in the June 2022 quarter and grew every quarter for 24 straight quarters to $436 million in the March 2026 quarter, or $1.65 billion for FY26, with EBIT margin about 15.6% and PAT margin about 12.6% for FY26. Work is delivered largely from India by roughly 27,500 employees across 21 countries, but the customers are overwhelmingly American — about 81% of revenue comes from North America, Europe only about 8-8.5%, with India and rest-of-world small; in June 2026 the company announced a combination with Germany-based Nagarro to expand in Europe.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Banking and Financial Services (BFSI)** — Serving banks and financial-services firms; it crossed $100 million of revenue for the first time in FY25, was the fastest-growing vertical in FY26 (up 28.4% for the year), and now has its own dedicated executive who presents on earnings calls.
- **Healthcare and Life Sciences** — Serving hospital, insurer and pharma/biotech clients; it led growth in mid-2024 (up 66.8% year-on-year in one quarter) and grew about 10-14% in FY26, making it one of the three reported industry verticals.
- **Software, Hi-Tech and Emerging** — Serving software product companies and technology firms — historically Persistent's home turf — growing more modestly at roughly 2-14% year-on-year across the period, and the segment management expects to lead AI adoption.
- **AI platform and offerings (three pillars: engineering hyper-productivity, business hyper-productivity, enterprise data readiness)** — A cross-cutting push, run under a dedicated CTO for Engineering Hyper-Productivity, that packages AI tooling (its own SASVA platform plus OpenAI, Anthropic and Copilot stacks) into client delivery; the calls treat it as the company's central strategic priority rather than a separately reported revenue line.
- **Geographies (North America, Europe, India, rest of world)** — The other way management reports the business: North America is about 80-81% of revenue and the main growth engine, Europe is roughly 8-8.5% and the stated target for expansion, with India and rest-of-world small contributors.
- **Acquisitions / M&A** — A standing part of the strategy — smaller deals like Data Glove and Media Agility (2022) added revenue and headcount, and in June 2026 the company announced a much larger combination with Nagarro (about $1.14 billion of trailing revenue, based in Munich) to create a roughly $2.9 billion group with 46,000+ people in 40+ countries.

### The story in depth

Persistent Systems earns an overall score of 67 out of 100, or Strong, built from a business-quality score of 69, a multibagger-pattern fit of 63 and a risk-safety score of 70. The foundation is a business that needs very little capital to grow: every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.30 of sales, the return on capital excluding cash was 45.2% in the fourth quarter of FY26, and the headline measure shows ₹21 of operating cash for every ₹100 of capital employed. Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹19 is operating profit, and that figure has only moved about 1.8 points in either direction each year, which points to genuine cost control. Growth has been just as steady: 24 consecutive quarters of sequential growth, 17.4% revenue growth in FY26 and 23.9% annual compounding over five years to reach $1.65 billion. Management scores full marks, with the founder as chairman for 36 years, an unusually stable senior team including an orderly handover of the finance chief in FY25, and disclosure the framework calls consistently granular, down to quarter-by-quarter margin bridges and open admission of failed acquisitions and delayed invoicing.

These same traits are what drive the two patterns that fit strongly. Market Share Gainers is a direct read-out of the growth record, with client counts rising every year and the company winning work against far larger providers from a small base in a vast market. Corporate Culture rests on the founder-led continuity and a record of promises kept, from crossing $1 billion to setting out the path to $2 billion. Brand Strength is a likely fit on the back of B2B recognition and the merger rationale, and Innovation Dominance and Cost to Replicate are partial fits resting on the platform work — SASVA, iAURA, GenAI Hub, Agent Studio, more than 500 agents and over 120 patents filed — plus 27,500 trained people and deep engineering relationships. But the same records show where the story stops. Pricing Power is a clear no fit: offshore billing rates rose just 1.6% and onsite 0.2% against volume growth of 9-17%, and the margin improvement through FY25-FY26 came from utilisation rising from 78% to 88%, subcontractor management, currency and cost work — never from charging more. That matches the negative mark on intangible benefits, where flat realisations and mid-teens margins say the offering is priced on delivered outcomes, not on brand pull, and it explains why industry structure is the one quality area scoring zero. Innovation Dominance stays partial only because research spending is never actually disclosed, and Recurring Revenue stays partial because the bookings figures bundle renewals with new business without breaking either out.

The risk review, which rates overall resilience as sound, keeps returning to the same facts from the other side. The heaviest concern, technological innovation, is rated elevated precisely because the platform push that earns top marks for research is happening while the whole industry shifts underneath it — management is deliberately rebuilding delivery away from billable headcount. The good-enough-goods risk is the sharpest version of this: SASVA is described as a layer sitting on top of Claude, GitHub Copilot and OpenAI Codex, so the company's own positioning concedes that commercially available tools are the substitute for custom engineering labour. Shifting customer preferences carries the same double edge, because headcount grew only from 23,519 to 27,502 while revenue compounded around 17% — evidence the pivot is working, and evidence that the old volume-led growth engine behind the Market Share Gainers pattern is being retired. Concentration works the same way: the $650 million, six-and-a-half-year contract that supports both the recurring-revenue and cost-to-replicate patterns also puts roughly 7% of run-rate revenue in one relationship, with the top ten clients at 41.5% of revenue and rising from 39.6% a year earlier. New entrants and cyclicality are on watch, with the Nagarro combination and its $2.9 billion scale offered as the answer to the first and multi-year annuity work as the cushion for the second. Government dependency and fashion risk are both low, since revenue is private-sector enterprise work sold on function and contract rather than taste.
