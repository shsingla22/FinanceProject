# Escorts Ltd. (ESCORTS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: AUTOMOBILE*

Escorts Kubota Limited (formerly Escorts Limited) is an India-based machinery manufacturer, headquartered in Faridabad, Haryana, that makes and sells farm tractors and related agricultural equipment, construction machinery, and — until it was divested in FY26 — railway equipment. It earns money mainly by selling tractors to Indian farmers through a large exclusive dealer network (about 1,240 dealers in mid-2023, around 1,500 by late 2024) under the Farmtrac, Powertrac and Kubota brands, with quarterly tractor volumes running from roughly 26,000 units in 2023 to about 37,000 units by Q3 FY26 and a domestic market share of roughly 9.7% to 11.3%. Beyond India it exports tractors and, increasingly, components — a growing share routed through the global network of its Japanese partner Kubota (about 32% of exports in Q1 FY24, rising to roughly 68% by Q3 FY26) — and it has built an export hub in Faridabad to supply parts to Kubota group companies worldwide. Quarterly revenue has grown from about INR 2,328 crores (June 2023) to about INR 3,261 crores (December 2025), with EBITDA margins in the 10-14% range; the company describes itself as debt-free with liquidity to fund growth.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Agri Machinery Business Division (Tractor Business Division)** — The core and by far largest business — building and selling tractors under the Farmtrac, Powertrac and Kubota brands to Indian farmers plus exports, contributing segment revenue of roughly INR 1,667 crores in Q1 FY24 rising to about INR 2,770 crores in Q3 FY26 (well over two-thirds of company revenue), with EBIT margins moving from roughly 9% to 13.5%.
- **Non-tractor business within Agri Machinery (Agri Solutions)** — A range of farm mechanisation equipment — rotavators, harvesters, rice transplanters (including the Japan-engineered next-gen KA6 and KA8 models), balers and sprayers — sold under the Farmpower and Kubota brands; part of a non-tractor pool that grew from roughly 10-12% of agri machinery revenue before the 2024 merger to about 17-21% after it.
- **Engine business (within Agri Machinery)** — Makes high-performance diesel engines — Escorts engines from 7.5 kVA to 58.5 kVA sold mainly to generator-set makers, and Kubota engines in the 10-55 kilowatt range supplied to construction machinery manufacturers — one of the three legs of the non-tractor revenue that is roughly a fifth of agri machinery sales.
- **Service and spare parts business (within Agri Machinery)** — Supplies after-sales support and replacement parts across the agri machinery range so customers can keep their equipment running; counted alongside agri solutions and engines in the non-tractor revenue pool.
- **Construction Equipment Business Division** — Makes and also trades cranes, backhoe loaders, mini excavators and compactors for infrastructure and construction contractors — roughly 1,100 to 2,000 machines a quarter and segment revenue of about INR 338-516 crores per quarter (a low- to mid-teens share of sales), with margins swinging widely (7.6% to 11% in good quarters, down to 3.8% in Q2 FY26) as infrastructure spending and emission-norm changes move demand.
- **Railway Equipment Business Division** — Supplied products to the railways on an order-book basis (about INR 950 crores of orders as of June 2023) and was the highest-margin unit at times (20.9% EBIT on INR 298 crores of revenue in Q1 FY24), but the business was divested by Q3 FY26, prompting a one-time special dividend of INR 18 per share.
- **Escorts Kubota Finance Limited (EKFL) — captive finance** — A newly RBI-registered lending arm, set to begin operations in FY25, whose job is to finance customers buying the company's own products; management also cites strengthened financing partnerships as a support to tractor sales.
- **Global sourcing / component exports for the Kubota Group** — Using India's low-cost manufacturing and the company's vendor base to supply components and services to Kubota companies worldwide under a 'China plus One' strategy, anchored by a 58,000 square foot export hub inaugurated in Faridabad; described as an enormous long-term opportunity though still early.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Escorts Kubota comes out as a genuinely well-run company sitting inside a hard business, and the single score of 41 out of 100 (Mixed, two stars) is the arithmetic of that split: business quality 50, risk safety 62, but multibagger fit only 10. The clearest strength is the people running it. Management scores 0.8 across the quality areas, and the Candor check is the highest-scoring item in the whole review at +2, because management keeps volunteering bad news with specifics — the margin dilution from the merger, the loss of market share, gaps in the product range and models it cannot supply, the Powertrac launch slipping a quarter, the Rajasthan land walked away from over water assurances, and the labour issue. The Corporate Culture pattern is built on exactly the same evidence: flagging the 1.5–2% merger margin hit in advance and then owning it. So the best thing about this company and the longest list of problems come from the same source — the problems are visible because the people disclosing them are honest, not because someone else dug them out.

The company's growth engine and its biggest single risk are also the same fact: Kubota. The parent's global network is what turned exports around after two bad years — down 38% in one quarter of FY24 and a European recession in FY25 — into FY26 export growth of 26% and 63%, which is why Geographical expansion scores +1. Kubota is also behind the product cadence the Innovation Dominance pattern rests on (the U22-6 mini-excavator, the KA6 and KA8 transplanters, alongside PROMAXX and Worldmaxx), and behind the Low Price Plus pattern, where Kubota explicitly uses the Indian business as a low-cost base. But exports routed through Kubota's network went from about 20% to about 52% to about 68% of export volumes, which is why Stakeholder Concentration is flagged as a WATCH — the growth plan rests on someone else's network. It is also why the Cost to Replicate pattern is marked NO FIT outright: the main structural asset is a partnership, not something the company owns and a rival cannot copy. The merger itself scores -1 on the quality framework — roughly INR 2,000 crores of breakeven revenue that diluted group margin by 1.5–2% and needs two to three years of localisation work to fix — so the Kubota relationship is simultaneously the export story, the product story, the margin drag and the dependency.

The weakest part of the picture is that this strength does not translate into power over price or over customers, and that shows up in both the patterns and the risks. Competitive Advantage scores -0.5 and Industry Structure -0.5; three of the eleven patterns are outright NO FIT — Pricing Power, Cost to Replicate and Toll Roads — and no pattern in the entire screen is a clean YES. Pricing is reactive: the construction-equipment price rise of January 2025 met customer resistance and took two quarters to stick, and by February 2026 management said tractor pricing would wait to see what competitors did. That same evidence produces the Good Enough Goods risk (WATCH) and the -1 on the Pricing, Mix and Volume check, where the GST 2.0 cut actually lowered tractor prices and no portfolio-level price action was taken even into commodity inflation. Brand Strength stays PARTIAL for the same reason — eighty years of heritage, three brands and around 1,500 exclusive dealers, yet tractor share slipped from 11.8% to 11.28% and, in the strongest industry quarter on record, the company grew 12% against industry growth of 23.2%. Sitting on top of all this is timing: the Cyclical market growth check is -1 because the industry is heading to a record of roughly 11.5 lakh units on GST cuts, subsidies and a good monsoon, and management will not rule out a reversal in FY27 — which is also the substance of the Government Dependency risk (HIGH) and the Cyclicality risk (ELEVATED). Against that, resilience is still judged SOUND: the balance sheet is net debt free, working capital ties up only about five days of cash, the wholly government-dependent railway business has been sold to Sona Comstar, spares and service now run at 17–21% of agri-machinery revenue against 10–12% before the merger, and four risk channels — technology, consumer preferences, fashion and new entrants — come back LOW or with no signal at all.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**What the calls show:** Demand in both segments is repeatedly attributed to policy levers rather than end-market fundamentals: state tractor subsidies (softening in Maharashtra in FY24, bunching in FY26), MSP, the GST 2.0 rate cut credited for the FY26 upswing, emission-norm dates creating pre-buy and then a hangover in CE, government infra capex of Rs 12.2 lakh crore, a pending PLI scheme, and an RDSO hold on ~INR 382 crores of railway wagon orders.
