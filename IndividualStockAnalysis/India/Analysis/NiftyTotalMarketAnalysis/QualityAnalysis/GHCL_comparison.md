Now: The customer base is industrial (detergent, glass, solar) where reliability is offered but lock-in is absent, since these buyers switch to cheaper imports on price alone. | The long view had said: Customers are industrial glass and detergent makers who buy on landed cost with no switching-cost lock-in, so the benefit offered (price parity vs imports) is the weakest form for a corporate buyer.

Now: Domestic logistics reach and supply-chain planning give a real advantage over imports — sustaining deliveries during a shutdown and benefiting as freight costs made imports uncompetitive. | The long view had said: Marketing infrastructure is good enough to leverage across trading and to clear inventory through a shutdown, but it is not a reach rivals or importers cannot match given imports rose 10%.

- **Distribution to shareholders** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. - **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Poor. Now: Management separates cost delivery from pricing explicitly, and the ~50% raw-material-price component of their savings is candidly disclosed — but EBITDA margin still fell from 27.3% to 22.7% across the year, showing results are cycle-driven, not skill-driven. | The long view had said: The record separates cleanly: FY23's 31% EBITDA margins came from a price spike, and the fall to 24% is attributable to realization alone while volumes and costs improved, so skill (cost leadership, volume gains) is visible independent of the cycle.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Neutral → Weak. Now: Rather than capturing share, GHCL is ceding volume to imports running ~85,000 tons/month and up 10% year-on-year in 9M FY26, with domestic rivals also adding capacity. | The long view had said: GHCL is already a dominant domestic incumbent and lost ground to imports (up 10% in 9M FY26 after anti-dumping duty was denied), though it recaptured volume in Q4 FY26 (+11%) as freight costs made imports uncompetitive.

And from the calls: 10 million tonnes of global capacity added in two years plus new domestic lines, with predatory import pricing that failed to win antidumping protection — the opposite of rational, protected pricing. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±3.3 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Capacity has been added in Mongolia/China and domestically, imports are undercutting on price, and the state declined protection — the opposite of a rational, entry-proof structure.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Good → Neutral. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹22 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±4.2 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹22 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±3.3 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control.

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. (The long view had said: GHCL has grown volumes ahead of the ~5% Indian market (8% in Q1 FY25, ~11% in Q4 FY26) and gains when imports recede, but the share shift is driven by freight costs and duty changes rather than structural advantage, and the failed anti-dumping petition plus a 10% rise in nine-month import volumes in FY26 show the gains are not persistent.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
