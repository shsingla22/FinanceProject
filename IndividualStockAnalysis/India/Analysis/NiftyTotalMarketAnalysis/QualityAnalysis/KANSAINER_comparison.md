### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 8 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Friendly Middleman**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Sales are pushed through trade professionals rather than end-consumers: the Pragati painter loyalty programme (1 lakh+ painters), an architect/interior-designer programme across 45+ cities with ~8,000 architects onboard, plus body-shop and OEM line engineers in industrial — classic expert-recommends, consumer-pays economics with training-based switching costs. (The long view had said: Every call is built around the trade influencer: painters, architects/interior designers, contractors, dealers and A-class auto body shops choose or apply the product while the homeowner or OEM pays. The influencer programme is repeatedly quantified (architect reach in 40+ cities in FY25, painter 'extraction' rising, services at >5% of decorative by FY26) and management explicitly says brand, distribution and painter relationships 'take time' to build — the switching cost.)

**That weakened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Innovation is regular and named — 20+ launches in FY25, 11 new products in FY26, Paint+ now >10% of decorative (up 190bps in a year), plus a steady stream of industrial products (Excel Everlast 20, BTX-free monocoat, low-bake CED) drawn from Kansai Japan technology. What is missing is the profit engine the pattern needs: R&D spend is never quantified, and gross-margin/PBT are falling, so innovation is not yet funding pricing power. (The long view had said: Named launches appear in every single call with disclosed revenue impact — Beauty Little Master Sheen and Crystal Seal (FY24), 20+ products including Wow Whites (FY25), Excel Everlast 20 and Total Floor Coat plus BTX-free monocoats and low-bake CED (FY26) — with Paint+ salience crossing 10% of decorative and new-product contribution in double digits, funded by Japanese parent technology and an internal innovation programme.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The company is a share loser in its largest segment on its own account — it grew 1.5-1.9% for 9M FY26 while conceding the industry is growing and part of that growth is going to new entrants and smaller players; in decorative it ranks No.3 and is defending, not gaining. (The long view had said: Share gain is real and repeated in industrial/auto ('increasing market share in automotive even on the high base', 50%+ four-wheeler, auto-refinish and high-performance coating growing above industry), but decorative share is flat at best across FY25–FY26 and the company remains No.3 nationally, over-indexing on selected zones and towns rather than gaining broadly.)

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 1 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
