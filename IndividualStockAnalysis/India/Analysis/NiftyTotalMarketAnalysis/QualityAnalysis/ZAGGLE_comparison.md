### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 6 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management repeatedly frames India cost structure and 'cost efficiencies' as the lever, and the model is genuinely low-margin/high-volume (Propel points booked gross at 7-9% margin, EBITDA guidance held at 10-11%), but there is no itemised cost culture, no evidence of passing savings to price, and no returns/asset-turn framing. (The long view had said: The economics are the inverse of low-price-plus: Propel Points, ~57-60% of revenue, earns 3-6% gross margin (down from 10% to 4% in Q4 FY26) and the growth has come with negative operating cash flow, so low unit margins are not being offset by high asset turns and strong returns.)

**That weakened:**

- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Nothing in the recent calls describes a barrier a funded newcomer could not rebuild: the moat management itself nominates is faster AI-assisted development cycles, which is precisely the kind of advantage a well-capitalised copycat can replicate, and the bank/network partnerships are non-exclusive (they are actively adding 'two to three more banks'). (The long view had said: Some barriers a copycat must clear — multi-year exclusive-style tie-ups (7-year Visa and Mastercard agreements, HDFC), a 3.9mn-user installed base and sub-2% churn — but the core monetisation is interchange and reward-points pass-through in a crowded Indian spend-management market, and nothing on the calls articulates a reason a well-funded entrant could not rebuild it in a few years.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL).*

**That worsened:**

(The long view had said: A material slice of the Save business exists because of income-tax treatment of employee benefits — management devoted whole sections to Budget 2025 and to the draft 2026 income tax rules ('a huge kicker'), and to the old-vs-new tax regime; it also operates on RBI-regulated prepaid/card rails with NPCI and is chasing PSU oil marketing companies for fleet. Policy is a swing factor, but bounded and openly de-risked.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
