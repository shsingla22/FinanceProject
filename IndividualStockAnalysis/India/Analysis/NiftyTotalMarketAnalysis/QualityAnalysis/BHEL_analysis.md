# Bharat Heavy Electricals Ltd. (BHEL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: INDUSTRIAL MANUFACTURING*

BHEL is an Indian engineering and manufacturing company that designs, builds, supplies and installs the heavy equipment used to generate and move electricity — boilers, turbines and generators for coal-fired thermal plants, hydro and nuclear equipment, and pollution-control kit like flue gas desulphurisation (FGD) systems — mostly for large government-owned power utilities such as NTPC and NPCIL, state power companies, and private groups like Adani. It earns money by winning large, multi-year tenders (either full EPC turnkey projects or narrower BTG/equipment packages), booking revenue as project milestones are met, and it supplements this with a higher-margin, short-cycle spares and services business (around Rs 3,500-3,800 crores of orders a year). Alongside power, it runs an 'Industry' business selling railway propulsion systems and locomotives, Vande Bharat train sets, naval guns and defence equipment, transmission gear (transformers, substations, HVDC), industrial motors, compressors and captive power plants. It operates mainly across India with some exports — projects in Bangladesh, Mauritius and gas turbine parts to UAE — and carried an order book of about Rs 1,31,600 crores as of 1 April 2024 against annual revenue of roughly Rs 24,000 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Power Sector** — Supplies and erects thermal (coal), hydro and nuclear power plant equipment, including full EPC projects, BTG packages, FGD emission-control systems and renovation & modernisation (R&M) work; it is the larger half of the company — Rs 92,559 crores of the Rs 1,31,600 crore order book at April 2024, and Rs 52,000 crores (9.6 GW) of new thermal orders in FY24 alone.
- **Thermal (coal) power equipment** — The company's self-described core business and where it claims market leadership with essentially one domestic competitor, covering 660 MW and 800 MW supercritical units at sites like Talcher, Talabira, Yamuna Nagar, Singrauli, Lara, Mahan and Raigarh; management sees roughly 10-12 GW of ordering a year for the next several years.
- **Hydro** — Supplies turbines, generators and the electro-mechanical package for hydro projects — about 1 GW of orders in FY23 including the 850 MW Ratle project, plus the 2.8 GW Dibang package (over Rs 2,600 crores), described as the country's largest.
- **Nuclear** — Supplies equipment to NPCIL for atomic power projects (Kakrapar Unit 3 capacity addition); management calls it cyclical with no major package ordered in FY23, but flags it alongside hydro as a major long-term area.
- **Industry Sector (Industrial Systems & Products)** — The non-power half — transportation, defence, transmission and industrial products — which booked its highest-ever order inflow of about Rs 22,000 crores in FY24 (Rs 9,537 crores in FY23, the highest in 13 years) and currently runs at roughly 30-35% of order intake, with management targeting a 50:50 split with power over the long term.
- **Transportation / Railways** — Makes electric locomotives, traction motors, transformers and IGBT-based three-phase propulsion and control systems for Indian Railways and metros; its largest-ever single order is 80 Vande Bharat train sets worth about Rs 23,500 crores (won with Titagarh Wagons, BHEL doing electrics, control systems and bogies), executed over 6+ years with a 35-year maintenance tail where much of the profit is expected.
- **Defence** — Supplies Super Rapid Gun Mounts (the main gun on Indian warships — 38 guns ordered in 18 months versus 44 in the prior 30 years), plus bids on air defence guns, marine gas turbines, confidential strategic naval equipment and compact heat exchangers for the LCA Tejas; management calls it a major growth area, often partnered with European OEMs.
- **Transmission** — Sells extra-high-voltage substations (400 kV and 765 kV), power transformers, GIS/AIS equipment and, increasingly, HVDC systems with an international partner; management calls HVDC the high-value, steady-margin piece and names it one of three priority industry areas.
- **Spares and Services** — Sells replacement parts and servicing on the installed base of plants — highest-ever bookings of over Rs 3,800 crores in FY23 and about Rs 3,500 crores in FY24, with 25%+ revenue growth; management describes these as short-duration, high-margin orders expected to lift profitability.
- **Industrial products and captive power** — Supplies industrial motors, compressors (including centrifugal compressors for refineries), CFBC boilers, steam turbine-generator packages and captive power plants to steel, cement and refinery customers — seven STG packages totalling 383 MW were booked in FY23.
- **New growth / diversification areas** — A cluster of newer bets: coal gasification using BHEL's in-house PFBG technology suited to high-ash Indian coal (a JV and 2,000 TPD coal-to-ammonium-nitrate plant with Coal India), flexible operations retrofits for existing coal plants, solar EPC (100 MW Raghanesda, 8 MW Mauritius), battery energy storage, hydrogen, railway signalling and Industry 4.0 remote monitoring — all small today but positioned as the path to a bigger non-thermal business.
- **Exports / International** — A small slice of orders — the remainder after power and industry in FY23 — covering overseas projects such as the 660 MW Maitree plant in Bangladesh, a Mauritius solar project, and gas turbine rotors and parts sold to UAE.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

  > "the only proven technology for gasification of high-ash Indian coals" — *management, on an earnings call*

*"Sells once, earns forever"*

> "Our relentless focus on improving our order booking in high margin spares and services business resulted in BHEL booking its highest ever orders of over INR 3,800 crores in this area." — *management, on an earnings call*

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, Cyclicality, Stakeholder Concentration. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.3× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 92% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 2% to 6% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** BHEL is itself a PSU whose order book is almost entirely set by government policy and state/central tenders — NTPC, state gencos, Indian Railways, MoD, Coal India, plus regulator-driven FGD demand and the National Coal Gasification Mission; even its internal targets are a DPE MoU. Management repeatedly frames the opportunity as government plans (88 GW thermal by 2032, CEA draft NEP, 100 GW FGD tendering) and concedes tender dates slip unpredictably.

> "these are where, the dates are given for the current year, but then we have seen in the past that those dates are not so sacrosanct unfortunately" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Stated intent to move to a 50:50 power/industry mix — but the industry side (defence, railways, transmission) is also government-funded*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is tied entirely to customers' capex programmes, not their operations: thermal ordering vanished for about three years before FY23, nuclear is described by the CMD as 'cyclical in nature', and the FY24 order book tripled to ~Rs 78,000 crores while revenue grew only 2% — a classic peak-ordering, lagged-execution profile with material costs at 72% of sales and gross margin falling for seven straight years.

> "After a gap of about three years, demand for coal-based thermal power plants has started picking-up... Nuclear business being cyclical in nature, no major package has been ordered by NPCIL during FY '23." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Growing spares and services book (highest ever ~Rs 3,500–3,800 crores, 25%+ growth) is genuine operations-linked flow revenue, and the 35-year Vande Bharat maintenance tail — but both are small versus project revenue*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 3 of the last 11 years with swings of ±17 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between -18% and 8% (±7.3 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 1% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 2%).

### Stakeholder Concentration — ELEVATED
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but the only computed fingerprint does not confirm it — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Debtors are 39% central and 41% state government entities, receivables run at ~567 days, single projects (Patratu, Yadadri, Uppur) dominate the contract-asset balance, and Rs 7,000–8,000 crores of the backlog is non-moving on customer decisions such as an NGT/Supreme Court order. Supplier concentration is now equally acute — BoP is fully outsourced and vendors have exited the space, forcing BHEL to relax PQRs and hand-hold vendors back.

> "as such for balance of plants as on date we are not exactly manufacturing anything in totality... a lot of these players who used to be there are now no more interested" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate broadening — 40% of FY23 and ~30% of FY24 inflows from industry (defence, railways, transmission, exports), and a central procurement cell to widen the vendor base*

- ✅ Customers pay in about 74 days (was 304) — no sign of weakening bargaining power in collection terms.

### Technological Innovation — WATCH
*"Out-invented by the industry"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** BHEL's installed base and manufacturing infrastructure are built around coal BTG, which net-zero targets and renewables push toward legacy status; management's answer is retrofit flexibilisation (~Rs 3 crores per unit), pumped storage, BESS, hydrogen electrolysers and coal gasification. Notably, for most new technology areas — HVDC, air defence guns, marine gas turbines, railway signalling — it must tie up with European OEMs rather than own the technology.

> "As a cost-effective and quick solution for integrating renewables to the grid, BHEL is ready with flexible operation solutions for existing coal-based power plants" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Steady small-scale, value-adding innovation cadence (flexible operations demonstrated at Raigarh/Sagardighi/Trombay, PFBG gasification proven for high-ash Indian coal) plus an ordering runway of 10 GW/year of thermal to 2032*

- ✅ Operating margin (8%) remains near its historical peak (8%) — no collapse in the premium.

### Shifting Consumer Preferences — WATCH
*"The customer is changing"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** The underlying demand base for coal power is subject to a decarbonisation shift in policy and public attitudes even as BHEL executes; management explicitly frames hydro, nuclear, hydrogen and storage as the future 'given the net-zero and sustainability targets' and treats the thermal upcycle as a three-to-five-year phenomenon rather than a permanent base.

### Good Enough Goods — WATCH
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**What the calls show:** Procurement is lowest-price tendering, so the premium for BHEL's engineering is not defensible in price: the Talcher bid came in at ~65 million/MW versus ~80 million/MW for Lara, the Vande Bharat order value was cut 12% and admitted to be 'challenging', and the defence given is cost optimisation and indigenisation rather than product differentiation. *Silver lining: BTG remains technically complex with only one meaningful domestic competitor, and BHEL's high-ash coal gasification technology is claimed to beat foreign OEM offerings*

- ✅ Operating margin (8%) remains near its historical peak (8%) — no collapse in the premium.
