# Adani Energy Sol (ADANIENSOL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Adani Energy Solutions (formerly Adani Transmission) is an Indian electric utility that builds and operates the wires that carry electricity, plus the meters and supply arrangements around them. It runs India's largest private-sector power transmission network — about 28,000 circuit kilometres of high-voltage lines won mostly through competitive government bidding — and gets paid a fixed tariff for keeping those lines available (99.7% availability, which also earns bonus incentive income), plus it sells electricity directly to households and businesses as the licensed distribution utility for Mumbai and Mundra under regulator-set tariffs. It also installs and operates smart electricity meters for state distribution companies under long-term contracts, and from FY27 runs a fourth 'energy solutions' business supplying mainly green power to large commercial and industrial customers. It operates entirely in India, spends very heavily on building assets (about Rs 22,000 crore of capex planned for FY27), and earns returns on that invested capital over long contract or regulatory periods.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Transmission** — Builds and operates high-voltage lines and substations won through competitive bidding, earning availability-linked tariffs; it is the biggest business — about 28,000 circuit km, roughly 29% share of projects put out to bidding, a capex pipeline that grew from Rs 17,000 crore to Rs 55,000 crore, and about Rs 15,500 crore of the Rs 22,000 crore FY27 capex, including the Rs 25,000 crore Bhadla-Fatehpur HVDC project and the completed Mumbai HVDC.
- **Distribution (AEML - Mumbai and Mundra)** — Sells and delivers electricity to end customers as the licensed utility in Mumbai (since 2018) and Mundra (since 2020) under regulator-set tariffs on a regulated asset base that has grown from about Rs 5,400 crore to over Rs 10,500 crore, with distribution losses cut from 8.5% to 4.2% and supply reliability above 99.9%; a steady, cash-generating business with about Rs 2,350 crore of FY27 capex.
- **Smart metering** — Installs and runs smart electricity meters for other distribution companies under long-term contracts — 13.4 million installed against an order book of 24.6 million, rising to about 47 million with the IntelliSmart acquisition — and management sees it as a perpetual business rather than one that ends with the concession; about Rs 3,900 crore of FY27 capex.
- **Energy Solutions / C&I** — The newest vertical, scaled up from FY27, which ties up long-term power supply (about 5,000 MW, mostly green) and sells it to commercial and industrial customers such as data centres, plus a lower-margin services arm that manages energy assets other companies have built; still small (around 350 MW of C&I customers contracted) but flagged as a major growth driver.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Essentially every revenue line is policy-determined: MERC/CERC set distribution and transmission tariffs and RoE (the CERC return-on-equity approach paper was an explicit analyst worry in Aug 2023), smart-meter volumes depend on RDSS approvals and central grants of Rs 900/meter, and the growth pipeline is central and state government bidding (Rs 45,000cr → Rs 60,000cr → Rs 1,50,000cr identified). Tariff outcomes flow straight to P&L — a Rs 400cr regulatory surplus translating into a 70 paise/unit tariff cut.
