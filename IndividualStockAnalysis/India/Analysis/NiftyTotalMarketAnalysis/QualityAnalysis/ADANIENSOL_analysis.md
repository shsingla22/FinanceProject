# Adani Energy Sol (ADANIENSOL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Adani Energy Solutions (formerly Adani Transmission) is an Indian electricity utility that builds and operates the wires and meters that move and sell power — it does not describe itself as a power generator. It earns money four ways: fixed, availability-linked fees for running high-voltage transmission lines it wins in government bidding (paid for keeping lines available — management cites 99.7% availability and roughly Rs 100 crore of incentive income over nine months in FY25); regulated tariffs from selling electricity to homes and businesses in its licensed city areas of Mumbai and Mundra; per-meter fees from installing and running smart electricity meters for state distribution companies under long-term contracts; and, most recently, contracting green power and selling or managing it for large commercial and industrial customers. It operates across India, with a transmission network of about 28,000 circuit kilometres as of Q1 FY27 and headquarters in Ahmedabad, Gujarat. Growth is capex-driven — management guided to roughly Rs 22,000 crore of capital spending in FY27 — and the company stresses keeping its credit ratings (now mostly AAA/AAA+) intact while it spends.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Transmission** — Builds and operates high-voltage lines and substations won through competitive bidding or on a regulated-asset basis, and is the biggest part of the company — about 28,000 circuit kilometres, roughly 29% share of projects that went to bidding in FY26, a transmission capex pipeline that grew from Rs 17,000 crore to about Rs 55,000 crore in FY25, and the largest slice of spending (about Rs 15,500 crore of the ~Rs 22,000 crore FY27 capex), including the Rs 25,000 crore Bhadla–Fatehpur HVDC project and the completed Mumbai HVDC link.
- **Distribution (AEML and Mundra)** — Holds the licence to sell and deliver electricity to end customers in Mumbai (acquired 2018) and Mundra (2020), earning regulator-set tariffs; volumes grow slowly (about 3% sales growth in Q3 FY25) but management highlights cutting distribution losses from 8.5% to 4.2% and over 99.9% supply reliability, with modest capex of roughly Rs 2,000–2,350 crore a year.
- **Smart metering** — Installs and runs smart electricity meters for other utilities under long-term contracts, having started with 4.6 million meters worth about Rs 58 billion in 2023 and reached 13.4 million installed against a 24.6 million order book by Q1 FY27, rising to about 47 million once the IntelliSmart acquisition closes; management calls it effectively perpetual rather than limited to the concession period, with 100–120 million meters still to be tendered nationally.
- **Energy Solutions platform / C&I** — The newest vertical, fully operational from Q1 FY27, which ties up power supply (about 5,000 MW, mainly green) on long-term contracts and resells it to commercial and industrial customers (about 350 MW of C&I customers signed, with data centres flagged as a target), and separately manages energy assets for customers such as cement and coal businesses for a service fee — management says the position-taking side carries higher margins and expects to eventually hedge most contracted capacity on both buy and sell sides.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*
