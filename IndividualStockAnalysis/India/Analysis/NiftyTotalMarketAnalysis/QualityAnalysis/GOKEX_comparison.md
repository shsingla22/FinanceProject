Of the 34 quality checks, 28 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 5 improved, 8 regressed, 15 unchanged. 4 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them).

**Checks that improved:**

- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Weak → Good. Now: The assessment survives stripping out macro luck — management explicitly separates the tariff effect from underlying performance, and the 10% India growth against a -1.4% industry is skill-attributable rather than cycle-driven. | The long view had said: Much of the outcome is driven by exogenous forces (China+1, Bangladesh unrest, tariff and AGOA swings, FTAs) rather than demonstrable durable skill, so strong recent narrative should be discounted.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Weak → Good. Now: Organic-growth focus and an explicit decision to pause discretionary capex amid uncertainty, offset by leverage rising INR395 crores in a single disrupted year. | The long view had said: Counter-cyclical capex and avoidance of Bangladesh risk are prudent, but stewardship is compromised by large inorganic bets (Atraco, Matrix, ~INR750 crore BRFL) and net debt rising INR395 crores in FY26.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Low entry barriers and price-driven competition — production can migrate country-to-country on tariff arithmetic and the response to pressure was industry-wide discounting. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±5.2 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Barriers are minimal and pricing is not rational — the company competes on cost arbitrage against Vietnam, Bangladesh and China and resorted to outright discounting to hold business.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Good. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹9 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹9 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±5.2 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: FY26 was an identifiable trough (50% penal tariff plus AGOA expiry) with the upturn already visible — penal tariff withdrawn, AGOA restored, pricing reset upward and retailer inventories pared down. | The long view had said: The company appears to be exiting a trough — tariffs normalised to 10%, AGOA restored, Africa 'bottomed out' and management guides to above 10-12% growth in FY27.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Weak. Now: Customers are corporate brands, the right fit for a TCO/reliability proposition, but the lock-in that model requires is absent — the value proposition is landed cost, which brands re-tender freely. | The long view had said: The customer base is corporate (global brands) and the company correctly sells reliability and lead time, but it has failed to convert this into switching-cost lock-in or pricing power.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Weak → Poor. Now: Pure contract apparel manufacturing with no monopoly position in the customer's mind — orders are won seasonally in open competition with Bangladesh and other low-cost regions. | The long view had said: No monopoly-like position — it is one of many contract manufacturers competing for seasonal fashion orders against Bangladesh and other low-cost regions.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: Relationship depth did retain US business through a 50% tariff shock, but trust clearly was not stronger than price for every account, several of which relocated orders. | The long view had said: Customers clearly buy execution certainty — the company air-freighted at its own cost to protect delivery and retained/grew US volumes even under a 50% tariff on relationship strength.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: Well-covered listed mid-cap with EY-hosted quarterly calls fielding questions from Nippon MF, Unifi, ICICI and others — not an ignored niche. | The long view had said: Apparel exports is an unglamorous niche, but the company is well covered by institutional analysts (ICICI, IIFL, Equirus, Emkay, PMS funds) on every call, so it is not ignored by capital.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Weak → Poor. Now: Switching costs are effectively zero — a tariff differential alone was enough for customers to shift sourcing to other countries within a quarter. | The long view had said: Switching costs are low: when Indian tariffs stayed high, customers of the Indian subsidiaries simply moved orders to other countries rather than negotiate.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Only a single passing reference to strengthening the leadership team, with no detail on internal pipeline or systematic talent development; senior roles have also churned across the history. | The long view had said: Repeated, specific references to deepening the management bench ahead of growth and again through the tariff disruption, though no formal pipeline programme is described.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Poor. Now: Growth was purely volume/capacity-led with sharply negative pricing — the company gave away over INR90 crores of net tariff discounts and retail price realisation was flat. | The long view had said: Growth is capacity- and volume-led while pricing moved against the company — over INR90 crores of net tariff discounts were handed to customers in FY26, with realisations partly offset by negotiating suppliers down.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Communication is operationally focused but the CMD's flourishes — quoting Kahlil Gibran and promising to be 'dangerous' — are more theatrical than plain-spoken. | The long view had said: Calls are consistently substance-led with operational detail and no promotional flourish, and management explicitly declines to give guidance or extrapolate unsigned FTAs.

*Unchanged: Geographical expansion; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Return on capital (headline); Merger and acquisition; Gain in market share; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Structural end-market growth; Cost of working capital; Convenience benefits; Share donators; Candor; Intangible benefits; Asset turn; Distribution advantage; Capex for growth (not maintenance).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
