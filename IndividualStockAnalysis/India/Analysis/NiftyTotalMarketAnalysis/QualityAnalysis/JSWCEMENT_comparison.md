### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 0 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Share gain is explicit and repeated in both bookend calls — Q1 FY26 volumes +7.8% against ~3% regional industry growth, Q4 FY26 cement volumes +12% against 8% industry growth, plus an 84% GGBS share and a decade of 16.7% volume CAGR versus 6.15% for the industry, with the North entry now adding a new region. (The long view had said: Share gain is explicit and repeated: 84% of India's GGBS market, Q1 FY26 volumes +7.8% vs ~3% industry, Q4 FY26 cement +12% vs 8% industry, and 2015-25 CAGRs of 12.96% capacity / 16.73% volume versus industry 4.77% / 6.15% — and it is being won on cost and green differentiation rather than reckless pricing, though the deliberate GGBS price restraint shows some share is being bought.)
- **Toll Roads**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: GGBS carries standards/certification anchoring (BIS norms permitting up to 50% OPC replacement, approvals for metro, airports and railway bridges) and JSW holds ~84% of the Indian GGBS market, but cementitious material is a large, negotiated share of a concrete customer's cost — not a sub-2% invisible input. (The long view had said: Cementitious material is 42-45% of RMC raw-material cost, not a tiny sliver, and the May 2026 call states customers explicitly re-compare OPC-GGBS vs OPC-fly-ash economics and switch mixes — the opposite of 'don't negotiate, don't switch'; BIS/regulatory certification is the only tollroad-like trait.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Cement realisation rose across all regions in Q4 FY26 (+4.8% quarter-on-quarter) and premium products are 57% of trade sales, but GGBS pricing is explicitly deflationary — prices were cut in FY25 and deliberately held flat through FY26 to drive penetration, with FY26 GGBS realisation (INR3,684) below FY25 (INR3,773). (The long view had said: GGBS realisation has been flat-to-down for years (INR3,761 FY24 → INR3,773 FY25 → INR3,684 9M FY26) with management consciously refusing price increases to buy penetration, and cement realisation fell 3.9% quarter-on-quarter in Q3 FY26 — this is a price-taker in a commodity, with earnings improvement coming from volume and cost, not price.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: GGBS competes directly against a cheaper good-enough substitute — fly-ash-based RMC mixes — and management admitted it had to cut GGBS prices to defend volumes and has deliberately held them flat even as cement prices rose, leaving GGBS realisation lower in 9M FY26 (INR3,684) than in FY25 (INR3,773) despite four years of scale; the choice sits with RMC producers and contractors, an intermediary channel with limited end-customer brand pull. (The long view had said: GGBS competes head-on with fly ash as a cheaper OPC replacement, and the choice sits with the intermediary — the RMC producer optimising a design mix on cost — not the end customer, so pricing power is capped: management admits it deliberately held GGBS prices flat through a cement price upcycle to defend penetration, and GGBS realisation is roughly where it was in FY24 (Rs 3,761 vs Rs 3,682 in Q4 FY26) while cement realisation rose. Management explicitly disclaimed any pricing windfall even with steel prices up 20%.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Cement is a commodity whose demand management explains entirely through macro/capex drivers — GDP, monsoon, front-loaded government infra spend, elections, fuel and FX — and realisations swung materially within the 12 months (cement ASP INR4,894 in Q1 FY26 vs INR4,673 in Q4 FY26, GGBS prices cut in FY25 to defend volumes). At the same time the company is in a heavy expansionary phase (20.6 to 46 MTPA, ~INR2,000 crores capex a year) with net debt/EBITDA at 4.32x in Q1 FY26, exactly the over-investment-at-the-peak pattern the channel warns about. (The long view had said: Cement is a price-taking commodity whose realisations swing quarter to quarter (cement realisation -3.9% quarter-on-quarter in Q3 FY26, then +4.8% quarter-on-quarter in Q4; GGBS prices had to be cut in FY25 when demand dropped), input costs track petcoke/freight cycles, and the company is spending ~INR2,000-2,300 crore of capex a year into a 21.6 to 46 MTPA expansion while carrying net debt that was 4.3x TTM EBITDA at listing. Demand is also explicitly hostage to macro shocks — April FY27 was 'relatively soft due to the inflationary pressures arising from the Middle East war situation'.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The GGBS franchise — 41-42% of volumes and the company's differentiated product — rests on slag supplied under long-term contracts by group affiliate JSW Steel, and this dependency bit twice inside the 12-month window: the Dolvi blast-furnace shutdown forced costly third-party slag purchases in Q1 FY26, and Dolvi slag availability issues in Q4 FY26 forced dispatch realignment from Vijayanagar, hurting GGBS growth and raising lead distance and logistics cost by ~6% Q-o-Q. West-region clinker likewise comes from the Fujairah JV. (The long view had said: The GGBS franchise — 42% of volumes and the differentiated part of the business — depends on blast-furnace slag from JSW Steel, with grinding units co-located at JSW Steel plants and pricing set by a five-year contract in which JSW Steel unilaterally initiates price discovery every 2.5 years. That dependency has already hit results twice in three quarters: a Dolvi blast furnace shutdown forced third-party slag purchases in Q1 FY26, and Q4 FY26 slag unavailability at Dolvi cost ~120,000 tons of volume and raised lead distance and logistics cost.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (QUANT FLAG).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
