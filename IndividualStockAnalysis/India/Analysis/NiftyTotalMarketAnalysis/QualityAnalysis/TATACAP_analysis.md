# Tata Capital (TATACAP) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Tata Capital is an Indian non-banking finance company (NBFC) that lends money — it offers a suite of 25+ lending products to individual borrowers, small and medium businesses, and large corporates across India. It makes money mainly on the spread between what it charges borrowers and its own cost of borrowing (cost of funds was ~7.2-7.4%, with net interest margin around 6.5-6.6%), plus a growing stream of fee income. It funds itself with bank loans and market borrowings, helped by a AAA domestic credit rating and the Tata brand. As of March 2026 it managed assets of about INR 2.77 lakh crore, served over 8.4 million customers through 1,477 branches across 27 states and union territories, and reported quarterly profit after tax of roughly INR 1,500 crore with return on assets of 2.3%.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Retail — secured** — Loans to individuals backed by collateral, described as growing about 28% a year and one of the main growth engines; retail and SME together are 86% of total assets under management.
- **Retail — unsecured** — Personal and other loans without collateral, deliberately kept small at about 10-12% of the book after a stress period, with management aiming to grow it to 15% of AUM because it carries higher yields.
- **SME / Enterprise lending** — Loans to small and medium businesses, including working-capital and secured business loans; growth picked up in Q3 and Q4 FY26 after the GST cuts and it is called out as a key FY27 growth lever.
- **Corporate lending** — Loans to large companies, a smaller and opportunistic part of the book — management said a modest rise in corporate loans in Q3 FY26 reflected a few strategic opportunities rather than a push.
- **Housing finance (subsidiary, incl. affordable housing)** — Home loans and loans against property run through a separate housing finance company with AUM of INR 86,653 crore growing 29-30% a year and profit up 34%; within it, affordable housing (average ticket ~INR 12 lakh) is the fastest-growing such book among large housing financiers and a top-3 originator by monthly volume.
- **Motor Finance (merged Tata Motor Finance)** — Vehicle financing acquired and merged in May 2025, with AUM of about INR 26,584 crore that was deliberately shrunk while fixing risk and profitability; it broke even in Q3 FY26, made INR 43 crore profit in Q4, and is being broadened beyond a single vehicle manufacturer.
- **Cleantech financing** — Lending to clean-energy projects — over 500 projects financed — which management says earns the best return metrics and lowest NPAs in that industry, though no size figure is given.
- **Supply chain financing** — Short-term financing tied to business supply chains, mentioned only as a segment that slowed in Q1 FY26 without denting overall growth.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*
