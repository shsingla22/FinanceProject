### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 0 weakened, 7 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is a named, repeatedly cited innovation push — the Cogito AI platform, agentic AI wins in energy, P&C insurance, payments and MedTech across all four calls — but no R&D spend is disclosed, gross margins are services-level, and the innovation is currently deflationary rather than pricing-accretive. (The long view had said: There is a named AI/platform push (Cogito, agentic AI wins in BFSI and MedTech in Q3/Q4 FY26, earlier cloud GTM with Google/Microsoft/AWS), but no R&D disclosure, no share-of-industry-R&D claim, and the AI deals are currently revenue-dilutive rather than margin-funding.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Birlasoft's revenue is contractual IT services with renewal-heavy H2 quarters and a stated pivot toward 'outcome-based and managed services engagements', but there is no product-plus-attached-annuity model, no attach/renewal rates, and no deferred revenue or customer advances funding the business (DSO 54 days, cash from collections). (The long view had said: Across the calls revenue is described as project/ramp-up driven — Q1FY23 and Q1FY25 revenue misses were both blamed on delayed project starts, and management explicitly prefers project work over annuity; there is no discussion of attach rates, renewal rates, AMC share or deferred revenue funding the business (DSO 52–62 days, receivables-funded).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 2 worsened, 1 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Management repeatedly attributes revenue erosion to unnamed 'client-specific issues' — Q1 ramp-downs and insourcing at a few Manufacturing customers, and in Q4 'an operational issue at one of our customers affected our volume growth' — showing single accounts are large enough to move the quarter. No single named customer or licensor is disclosed as dominant, which bounds the exposure. (The long view had said: Disclosed concentration is material and repeatedly consequential — top 5/10/20 accounts at 36.2%/52.6%/65.1% of revenue — and single relationships have visibly moved consolidated results in both directions: two delayed $15–20m engagements explained the Q1FY23 miss, and in Q4FY26 'an operational issue at one of our customers' was a named driver of the sequential revenue decline. Deliberate rationalisation of tail accounts concentrates the base further.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The soft-demand commentary is consistently about customers 'optimizing spends', 'pushing for productivity benefits' and insourcing work — clients substituting cheaper or in-house alternatives for a service that is not scarce — and management walked away from 'several non-profitable revenue streams' rather than defend price. AI-driven productivity pass-throughs are effectively price deflation on delivered work. (The long view had said: Much of the base — staff augmentation and standard ERP/infra work — is commoditised and priced against cheaper alternatives: clients are 'pushing for productivity benefits', AI deals require passing savings upfront, offshore shift is customer-mandated on price, and management chose to walk away from whole revenue streams it could not make profitable. Revenue fell 6% in dollars in FY26 partly as a direct consequence.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Birlasoft's revenue is tied to enterprise discretionary IT spend and transformation programmes — effectively customer capex — and across all four FY26 calls management attributes flat-to-declining revenue to customers cutting discretionary spending and holding back on large transformational programmes; FY26 revenue fell 1.2% in rupee terms with dollar revenue stuck at ~$150.7m for four straight quarters. Exposure is amplified by concentration in Manufacturing-led verticals, which management itself flags as less steady. (The long view had said: Across all four years the calls attribute results to the customer spending cycle rather than execution: delayed project starts and 'revenue normalization' in FY23, discretionary pullback and deferred ERP ramp-ups in FY25, and FY26 dollar revenue down 6% on 'sustained macroeconomic headwinds'. Because revenue is tied to customers' transformation/capex-like programmes, both sales and margins swing hard — EBITDA margin ran 14.7%, then 13% for FY25, then 18.5% in Q4FY26 — and management concedes outsized exposure to manufacturing-led customers.)
- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: AI is reshaping the IT services delivery model and management concedes it is passing productivity gains to clients — an explicit revenue deflation from technology — while simultaneously rebuilding the delivery model and rebranding as 'AI First'; every deal conversation is now led with AI capabilities, indicating the industry's basis of competition has shifted under the incumbent labour-arbitrage model. The legacy ERP/Infra service lines are already declining. (The long view had said: The business sits directly in the path of a large-scale platform shift: analysts ask explicitly whether AI and Palantir's ERP-migration effort compression are 'deflating your ERP revenues', and management admits FY26 revenue was dented by productivity benefits handed to clients on AI deals. Its installed ERP/staff-augmentation base is the legacy-format exposure, and ERP has under-performed for many consecutive quarters.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Not comparable: Government Dependency (NOT ASSESSED → LOW — could not be assessed on the long view).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
