# Mrs Bectors Food (BECTORFOOD) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Mrs. Bectors Food Specialities is an Indian packaged-food maker that bakes and sells two things: biscuits and cookies (mostly under the 'Cremica' brand) and bakery items like breads, buns, cookies and frozen/ready-to-eat desserts (mostly under the 'English Oven' brand, plus a clean-label 'NaturBaked' range). It earns money by manufacturing these in its own plants — biscuit plants at Rajpura and Indore, bakery plants at Kolkata, Khopoli/Mumbai, Bhiwadi and Noida — and selling them to Indian consumers through a distributor and salesman network across general trade, modern trade and quick commerce (Blinkit), to institutional buyers such as QSR chains, and to customers in more than 70 countries as exports. Revenue is driven by volume plus price/'premiumization', with input costs (wheat, palm oil, crude-linked packaging) and pricing power being the main swing factors on margin. The company grew from INR988 crores of revenue in FY22 to INR2,044 crores in FY26 (about 20% a year), earning an EBITDA margin of roughly 12-13% and a net profit of INR141 crores in FY26.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Biscuits** — Makes and sells biscuits, crackers and cookies (Cremica brand, including Danish Butter Cookie tins and Diwali gifting packs) for the Indian market and for export; it is the larger of the two verticals — INR325 crores of the INR533 crores quarterly revenue in Q3 FY26 — and grew at about 20% a year over FY22-FY26.
- **Bakery** — Makes and sells breads, buns, cakes, bakery cookies and frozen/ready-to-eat desserts, led by the English Oven brand; it is the smaller but faster-growing vertical — INR198 crores in Q3 FY26 and 14% growth in FY26 — and management reports it in two pieces, retail bakery and institutional.
- **Retail Bakery (within Bakery)** — The branded, shop-shelf side of bakery sold to households under English Oven and NaturBaked, including health-led launches like Zero Maida and protein bread and premium items like Cheesecake Jars; management calls English Oven the key driver of bakery performance.
- **Institutional Bakery (within Bakery)** — Supplies buns and bakery products in bulk to business customers, notably quick-service restaurant partnerships, which management cites as a source of sustained momentum for the bakery vertical.
- **Exports / International business** — Sells biscuits and other products to more than 70 countries (Cremica alone in over 65), described as a strong performer that was hurt by 50% punitive US tariffs in FY26 and is expected to recover as tariffs drop to 18% under the India-US trade agreement.
- **Sales, distribution and technology build-out** — Not a revenue segment but a repeatedly discussed engine of growth: adding hundreds of area distributor salesmen, doubling the North India distribution network while expanding into West, South and now East India, and rolling out salesforce (SFA) and distributor (DMS) management systems plus handheld billing devices.
- **Capacity expansion / capex** — Ongoing plant building — new biscuit lines at Rajpura, a greenfield biscuit plant in Indore and greenfield bakery plants at Khopoli (Mumbai) and Kolkata — running at roughly INR350 crores in FY25, which management said could eventually support INR2,800-2,900 crores of sales at full utilisation, at an asset turn of about 2-2.5x for Khopoli after roughly three years.
- **Project IMPACT** — A company-wide cost-structure and efficiency programme started in FY24 that management repeatedly points to as the main lever, alongside calibrated price increases, for protecting margins against raw material, packaging, fuel and minimum-wage inflation.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Mrs Bectors Food comes out of the quality framework as a genuinely well-run maker of biscuits and bakery products rather than a fortress business. The strengths are operational and repeatable: sales have compounded at about 20% over FY22–FY26, from INR988cr to INR2,044cr, averaging around 15% a year with lumpy years in between; INR13 of every INR100 of sales is operating profit and that margin has only moved about a point and a half either way each year; INR45 of every INR100 of sales survives direct input costs; and INR100 of capital in the business threw off INR18 of operating cash this year. Behind those numbers sits the clearest single strength — distribution intimacy, with 600 salesmen added, sales-force and distributor management systems rolled out, handheld real-time billing for the bakery frontline, and 20%-plus growth with leadership positions in modern trade and quick commerce. Management has funded all of this organically, with no acquisitions and debt at 0.36 times equity, and kept building capacity through a demand slowdown.

The multibagger screen picks up exactly these traits, but only partly. The two closest fits, Innovation Dominance and Market Share Gainers, are built on the same facts the quality checks reward — a steady stream of named products that actually sell (Non-Stop crackers, Zero Maida, Golden Bites, NaturBaked protein bread, a frozen and ready-to-eat range now about 20% of B2B bakery), a 48% gross margin, and real geographic expansion into Indore, Kolkata, Khopoli, Hyderabad and 70-plus export countries. Neither is a full fit: R&D spend is never disclosed or quantified, and in domestic biscuits management describes holding share rather than taking it. Six more patterns come out only partial, and three are flat misses. Those misses are the heart of the story. Toll Roads fails because biscuits and bread are the customer's own product bought in a price and promotion fight, including a coinage battle over INR4.50 versus INR5 and full pass-through of GST savings. Cost to Replicate fails because greenfield lines, feet on the street and sales software are all things a funded newcomer can buy within a few years. That is why the framework scores Competitive Advantage and Industry Structure at zero even while Customer Benefits and Management score well: the company executes better than it is protected.

The risk review reads as the mirror image of the same facts, which is what makes the picture coherent rather than contradictory. Four of eight channels are only LOW risk, and for good reason — demand is daily, functional and spread across many products; quick commerce has been additive, a 'channel of significance' the company built and now leads in pizza bases and burger buns; and the health shift has been met with an executed pivot (Zero Maida atta bread, clean-label NaturBaked, baked-not-fried crackers) rather than an eroding base. The four WATCH items each attach to a strength. The thin brand loyalty that keeps Pricing Power at partial is the same thing that makes Good Enough Goods a live risk, with leaders' promotions having to be matched to defend share. The institutional QSR and large overseas retailer accounts that helped drive the Market Share Gainers growth are also the Stakeholder Concentration and Cyclicality exposure, with that B2B book on 'low burn' for two to two and a half years. The 70-plus country export reach that diversifies the franchise is the same reach that let 50% US tariffs cloud almost a full financial year and cut export growth to low single digits, alongside a GST 2.0 inventory hit in Q3FY26. And the counter-cyclical capex the framework praises — roughly INR350cr in FY25 alone, sized for INR2,800–2,900cr of revenue — was part-funded by a QIP that drew investor criticism about dilution, with only a token INR0.6 interim dividend, the one check scored negative. Resilience is SOUND and the combined rating is 63 out of 100, or Decent: a capable, conservatively financed operator whose returns depend on continued execution rather than on any moat.
