### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 7 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 7. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 1 weakened, 7 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Management explicitly rejects high-volume, low-value commodity work and targets hard, gap-filling technologies for margin reasons — the opposite of a scale-driven low-price model. (The long view had said: Cost obsession shows up in capital intensity rather than selling price: management itemises buying an EBCHR furnace for $2.5mn against an $80–90mn list, a VAR for $0.35mn against $4–5mn, and contrasts a INR150–180cr titanium mill against Precision Castparts' $500mn US plant. But there is no evidence of passing savings to customers — realisation per kg is rising, not falling — so this is capital-efficiency, not low-price-plus.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (LIKELY FIT); Market Share Gainers (LIKELY FIT); Friendly Middleman (QUANT SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (STRONG FIT); Cost to Replicate (STRONG FIT); Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Brand Strength (QUANT SIGNAL); Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Revenue is tied to customers' capital programmes — defence capital expenditure, new aircraft platforms, aero engines, oil & gas / LNG / power-generation castings — with management noting an RFI can take seven to eight years to convert and that only one of seven or eight bidders wins; meanwhile the company has itself committed ~Rs. 350 crores of capability capex ahead of revenue and is now scaling capacity further. (The long view had said: Revenue is anchored to customer capital programmes — defence acquisition budgets, new aircraft build rates and platform production runs — rather than to consumables used in customer operations, and management's growth case is built on projected defence capex ($22bn today to $55bn by 2030) and 40,000 aircraft deliveries; the industrial casting legacy business also serves oil & gas, LNG and power. Offsetting this, the demand is long-cycle and programme-locked rather than commodity-priced, and the company invested heavily (~INR 350 crores) through COVID when revenue was flat.)
- **New Entrants**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Management's stated 'parity dharma' is explicitly a screen for businesses with technology gaps no one else in India has filled in 70-75 years, and the qualification/certification burden for aerospace castings plus assets such as one of the world's five largest hot isostatic presses form high entry barriers; avoiding competitor entry is the express reason for the business-selection filter. (The long view had said: An analyst pressed directly on Midhani — a cash-rich state producer already making ~300 tonnes of titanium that the government could direct to expand overnight — and management's answer was essentially dismissive rather than substantive; Precision Castparts is separately building the same EBCHR/VAR capability in the US. Entry barriers are nonetheless real and time-based.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 11 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
