# Fiem Inds (FIEMIND) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Fiem Industries is an Indian auto-parts maker whose main business is designing and manufacturing lighting and related electronics — head lamps, tail lamps, number plate lamps, fog lamps, reflectors and similar parts — that go onto vehicles as they are built. It sells almost entirely business-to-business, directly to vehicle makers (OEMs), overwhelmingly two-wheeler brands: Honda (HMSI), TVS, Yamaha, Suzuki, Royal Enfield and Hero, plus 20-plus electric two-wheeler customers, and more recently passenger-car makers such as Mahindra & Mahindra. It makes money by winning parts on specific vehicle models and supplying them in volume from its own plants (Rai/Sonepat and Tapukara in the north, Hosur in the south, with an EMC/EMI test lab at Gurgaon), so revenue rises and falls with how many units its customers build; management credits its margins to an 'integrated manufacturing model' and operating leverage. Revenue grew from roughly INR 2,014 crores in FY24 to INR 2,790 crores in FY26 with profit after tax of INR 254 crores, EBITDA margin just over 14%, and it describes itself as a net-zero-debt company.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Automotive lighting (two-wheeler)** — The core business — lamps and lighting assemblies fitted to motorcycles, scooters and mopeds for Honda, TVS, Yamaha, Suzuki, Royal Enfield and Hero; management discusses company results essentially as this business, and quotes total two-wheeler industry production (26.7 million units in FY26) as its main demand driver.
- **LED lighting (share of automotive lighting)** — The higher-technology slice of the lamp business that management tracks explicitly and says is steadily replacing conventional lighting — 44% of automotive lighting in FY22, 52% in FY24 and about 60-63% in FY26, with 'most of the new business we are winning in LED'.
- **EV / electric two-wheeler supply** — The same lighting and electronics sold to electric two-wheeler makers — more than 20 EV customers as early as FY23 (INR 18.5 crores of revenue in Q1 FY23), and by FY26 including TVS iQube, Honda's Activa EV platform, Royal Enfield EV initiatives and an approved new Hero EV platform.
- **4-wheeler / passenger car business** — A newer growth line started with an order from a European car maker and then Indian car makers; by FY26 Fiem supplies number plate lamps, rear reflectors and fog lamps to Mahindra & Mahindra across various models, with high-mount stop lamps for Bolero and Scorpio in development — still small but flagged as a major future growth driver.
- **Hub motors and motor controllers (Gogoro project)** — A non-lighting venture to make electric-scooter hub motors and motor controllers for Gogoro; as of May 2024 it had produced almost nothing because Gogoro's launch volumes were tiny (about 5,000 vehicles), so it had made no meaningful contribution to revenue despite capex being planned for it.
- **Exports** — Supply of lamps for models built in India for overseas markets — Yamaha models for Europe and the US, Hero's Harley-linked X440, and development work for TVS-owned Norton — described as a source of growth but lumpy, since fixed export order quantities finishing can dent a quarter.
- **Electronics capability and new products** — In-house electronics manufacturing (a surface-mount / SMT line running since around 2006, plus a new EMC/EMI lab at Gurgaon) supporting products such as USB chargers and sensors and future technologies like laser lighting, touch interfaces and adaptive drive beam; presented as capability that lets Fiem move into higher-value lighting rather than as a separately sized revenue line.
- **Fuel pump module joint venture (exited)** — A former JV making fuel pump modules that Fiem fully exited in June 2022 for INR 26 crores, removing an expected INR 150-200 crores of potential revenue from the portfolio.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Fiem earns its best marks on how it uses money. Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.66 of sales, the balance sheet carries no net debt and ₹276 crores of cash, and the return-on-capital area scores a full 1.0 with capital allocation at 0.75. Management is spending clearly above the ₹40-45 crore level needed just to keep the plants running — ₹86 crores in FY24, ₹108 crores in FY26 and ₹200 crores planned over two years — and guides roughly three rupees of sales for every rupee of that new investment, with revenue moving from ₹1,834 crores in FY23 to ₹2,791 crores in FY26. All of it is funded from internal accruals, alongside a dividend that rose from ₹20 per share after the FY24 1:1 bonus to ₹40 per share (₹105.28 crores) in FY26. The same discipline shows in what they have not done: exiting the Aisan fuel-pump joint venture rather than diversifying, and committing to pre-announce any greenfield plan. This cost-and-efficiency culture — integrated manufacturing, factory efficiency drives, 65% solar at Hosur plus 20% wind — is what earns a partial pass on the Low Price Plus pattern and lifted operating margin from 11.6% in Q1FY23 to a record 14.67% in Q4FY26.

The limit on all of this sits in one fact that management states plainly and repeatedly: prices are set by back-to-back compensation with the carmakers, with raw-material and currency moves passed through in both directions after a lag. That single trait explains almost the entire weak half of the record. It is why Pricing Power and Toll Roads are outright no-fits, why the competitive advantage and industry structure areas both score zero, and why margin gains have to come from mix and efficiency rather than from charging more. The mix shift is real — LED rose from 44% of lighting in FY22 to 63% in FY26 with a fully LED order pipeline, backed by 80-plus development projects in FY23, an in-house EMC/EMI lab in FY26 and programmes in laser, adaptive-drive, matrix and night-vision lighting — but the same shift is also flagged as a watch-level technology risk, because conventional lighting is still around 37% of the mix and analysts have twice asked whether LED prices are falling globally. The pass-through model likewise sits behind two more watch-level risks: Good Enough Goods, since the carmaker chooses the supplier and the end buyer never knows whose lamp is fitted, and New Entrants, since a competitor took LED business at TVS in FY23. Of eleven multibagger patterns, six are partial and five are no-fits, and the pattern score of just 10 out of 100 is what drags the overall rating down.

The biggest single risk, stakeholder concentration, is rated elevated — results visibly turn on a handful of named customers such as HMSI, TVS, Yamaha, Suzuki and Hero, and Yamaha's fixed-quantity export models have swung individual quarters. This is the flip side of Fiem's own strength: the multi-year qualification work that makes it hard to replace (five Mercedes plant visits before global small-lamp approval, 18-24 month development cycles, model-life tooling) is the same thing that ties the company tightly to a few relationships, and the deliberate multi-sourcing by those customers shows up directly in wallet share, with Yamaha tail lamps falling from 82% to 64% and TVS from 77% to 69%. That is why the Market Share Gainers pattern is only partial: the customer list is genuinely widening — 20-plus electric two-wheeler makers, Harley-Davidson, Norton, Yamaha Europe, and a four-wheeler entry with Mahindra, Force and Mercedes guided to ₹200-250 crores — even as share inside existing accounts is contested. Elsewhere the risk picture is calm: overall resilience is sound, fashion risk and shifting consumer preferences are both low because demand is functional and lighting content works on electric and petrol vehicles alike, and the ₹141 crore replacement business gives a modest cushion against an industry that has swung as much as 38.4% year on year. The arithmetic lands at 48 out of 100 — business quality 62, risk safety 70, multibagger fit 10 — a Mixed, two-star verdict: a well-run, well-financed, honestly managed supplier whose economics are set by its customers rather than by itself.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) — PARTIAL
*"Sells once, earns forever"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (2 of 3 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** There is a genuine installed-base annuity in the replacement/aftermarket lamp business that grows as the vehicle parc ages (INR141 crores in FY24), but across all 15 calls there are no licence fees, subscriptions, AMCs, attach/renewal rates or customer advances — and working capital is a use of cash (receivables rose in FY26 after stopping bill discounting).

> "during the current year, we registered a good growth in the replacement market. It's INR141 crores" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ About 46 days of cash is tied up in the trade cycle — customers are not funding the business.
- ✅ Capital spending is about 5% of sales — growth needs little capital.
- ✅ Operating cash was positive in 12 of 12 years — the reliable cash engine recurring-revenue businesses show.

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 1 check supporting).

**What the calls show:** There is a real cost/efficiency culture — integrated manufacturing, factory efficiency drives, ~3x asset turns on capex, and itemised energy savings (65% solar at Hosur plus 20% wind) lifting EBITDA margin from 11.6% (Q1FY23) to a record 14.67% (Q4FY26) — but Fiem does not compete on a low-price, no-frills proposition to win share.

> "At our Hosur facility, around 65% of our energy needs are already met through solar power with a further 20% being added through wind energy." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹39 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 1.7× a year — not the high-turnover, low-margin machine this pattern needs.

### Innovation Dominance — PARTIAL
*"Out-inventing the competition"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 2 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Consistent, named technology pipeline across the years — 80+ development projects (FY23), laser headlamp, night vision, ambient lighting, ADB and matrix lighting, plus an in-house EMC/EMI lab commissioned in Gurgaon (FY26) — and LED share rose 39% (FY20) to 63% (FY26) with a 100% LED pipeline; but R&D spend is never disclosed or quantified as a share of sales or industry, and innovation is largely customer-funded co-development.

> "we are working on three main technologies. One is a laser technology... we propose to be the first in the industry to launch that." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹39 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — limited pricing headroom.
- ❌ Thin retained margin (39% of sales) alongside 12% yearly growth — the flywheel is incomplete.

### Market Share Gainers — PARTIAL
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is per-vehicle content on 2-wheeler production, an operations-linked flow product rather than customer capex, and margins have been remarkably stable (11.6% in Q1FY23 to 14.7% in Q4FY26); but the calls show the industry itself swinging — 38.4% year-on-year volume growth in Q1FY23, and FY26 only just exceeding the FY19 peak after seven years, meaning current record revenue and all-time-high margins are being reported at what is plainly a cycle high.

> "FY26 marked an important milestone as industry production exceeds the pre-pandemic peak achieved in FY19. Production volumes for the year grew about 12% to an all-time high of roughly 26.7 million units." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/operations-linked demand — content per vehicle produced, not customer capex; plus a INR141 crore replacement-market business that grows as the parc ages, and countercyclical capex through the period while remaining net-zero debt*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 2 of the last 11 years with swings of ±12 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.

## What to watch

- Wallet share at the core accounts (Stakeholder Concentration, rated elevated) — whether the Yamaha 82%-to-64% and TVS 77%-to-69% slides stabilise or keep sliding.
- The four-wheeler and export ramp (Market Share Gainers pattern) — whether the guided ₹200-250 crores from Mahindra, Force and Mercedes actually lands and lifts the customer spread.
- Payback on the ₹200 crore capex programme (Capex for growth check) — whether the promised roughly three-times asset turn shows up in sales, given return on capital is the one area scoring a perfect 1.0.
- LED pricing and the remaining conventional mix (Technological Innovation risk, Innovation Dominance pattern) — if global LED realisations deflate while conventional is still around 37% of lighting, the mix-led margin story loses its engine.
- Any crack in the back-to-back pass-through, in either direction (Pricing Power no-fit, Good Enough Goods risk) — margins have held in a ±1.0 point band precisely because escalations are passed through; a lag that is not recovered would show up fast.
