# C.E. Info System (MAPMYINDIA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

C.E. Info Systems, which trades under the brand MapmyIndia (product brand "Mappls"), builds and sells digital maps of India and the software built on top of them — map data, APIs, SDKs, navigation, GIS and tracking tools — that other organisations embed in their own vehicles, apps and systems. Its customers are carmakers and two-wheeler/commercial-vehicle makers (for in-car navigation, e-horizon, ADAS and EV range prediction), consumer-internet and corporate firms (e-commerce, delivery, quick-service restaurants, banks, retail, logistics fleets) and government bodies (central ministries, states, police, defence, smart cities). It makes money mostly by licensing software, map data and APIs on a per-use or subscription basis, plus project-based government work and, in its IoT arm, selling tracking hardware and related services; the company reports a large "open order book" (about INR1,770 crores at December 2025) that converts into revenue over several years. Operations and revenue are overwhelmingly India-focused — foreign revenue mainly comes from global companies buying for their Indian products, with an international software/API push described as an early-stage effort — and full-year FY26 revenue was about INR474 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Automotive & Mobility Tech (A&M)** — Sells map, navigation and connected-vehicle software to vehicle makers — embedded in cars, EVs and two-wheelers, their companion apps and cloud back-ends, including ADAS/e-horizon and EV range features; it grew about 9% in FY26 and management calls it the segment where roughly 2 crore new vehicles a year are the addressable base.
- **Consumer Tech & Enterprise Digital Transformation (C&E)** — Sells APIs, analytics, GIS and digital-transformation projects to consumer-internet firms, corporates (banks, retail, logistics) and government; it is split roughly 50-50 between corporate and government work and declined about 3% in FY26 because government projects slipped to later quarters.
- **Map-led business** — The core, high-margin map/software/API business — in Q1 FY25 it was INR78 crores of INR101 crores total revenue at roughly a 50% EBITDA margin, so it is the bulk of both revenue and profit.
- **IoT-led business (Gtropy)** — The acquired IoT/telematics arm that sells vehicle-tracking devices, video telematics and fleet SaaS to the existing base of roughly 20 crore vehicles on the road; it was INR23.5 crores of INR101 crores in Q1 FY25 at a much lower ~15.7% EBITDA margin, and includes the hardware-sale line.
- **Government business** — Sits inside C&E and covers projects like the Survey of India Integrated Geoportal, Dial 112 police systems, defence mapping, flood modelling and 3D digital twins for states and cities; management puts it at about 20% of total revenue in FY26 (18-19% in FY25) and it was the main cause of FY26's revenue delays.
- **Mappls consumer app ecosystem** — The company's own consumer navigation and location app, described as having 45-plus million cumulative downloads (10-plus million during FY26) and evolving into a broader consumer/developer platform; the calls present it as a strategic and awareness asset rather than a separately sized revenue line.

### The story in depth

The company's strengths all trace back to one thing: it owns the map. Thirty years of building its own field data since 1995, as the only Indian firm doing both the data collection and the platform, lets it keep 87 rupees out of every 100 rupees of sales after direct input costs and earn roughly 50% margins on the map-led business. That same ownership funds a steady stream of new products the company pays for itself — 3D and RealView maps, drone digital twins, driver-assistance and EV software, AI-infused offerings — which is why Innovation Dominance is the one pattern rated a strong fit. The balance sheet reinforces it: no debt, about INR600 crore of net cash, and customers who pay 123 days before suppliers need paying, so the business is effectively funded by its own clients. Management is founder-led, low-key, and picks government orders only where it believes it will get paid.

The patterns and the risks are mostly two readings of the same facts. The premium price the company charges is what makes Pricing Power a likely fit — it refused to match Google's and Ola's price cuts and held 35-45% margins — but that same refusal is exactly what the Good Enough Goods risk points at: it is defending a price premium on the argument of value, against a free product and a cheap one, and no explicit price increase drives revenue anywhere in 18 calls. Being the innovator in a fast-moving field is a strength and the Technological Innovation risk at once, since the ground can shift underneath — analysts have pressed on Google's street view, Ola Maps, and by early 2026 on AI directly. Government geospatial programmes are named as a genuine secular growth market and are also the single highest-rated risk: about 20% of revenue, nine-tenths of it in centrally funded schemes paid out through local bodies, and grants slipping from May-June to October were the main reason FY26 revenue barely moved, from INR463 crore to INR474 crore. Selling through carmakers and app developers underpins the partial fits on Toll Roads and Friendly Middleman while creating the concentration risk, where the Hyundai-Kia programme and the soured Ola relationship each visibly move a quarter.

What holds the overall rating down to 50 out of 100 is not fragility — resilience reads as sound — but the gap between what the company owns and what it has converted into growth and returns. Return on capital and industry structure both score zero in the quality breakdown, and capital allocation and growth score low. The two negative checks say why: international expansion was promised as a one-to-three-year journey with senior hires in 2022 and four years on has produced only an Indonesian joint venture still being built, and the one acquisition, the Gtropy IoT business, came in structurally weaker at a 15.7% margin against 50% for maps, with a funding shortfall and a management transition. Market Share Gainers rates only a likely fit because share is never disclosed and the recent record cuts the other way — automotive revenue grew 15% against 18% industry production, and the enterprise line shrank 3%. So the picture is a genuinely rare asset with strong cash characteristics, currently sitting at a trough it says is turning — Q4FY26 revenue rose 54.8% over the prior quarter and the order book grew — where the deciding question is whether the premium and the growth can be re-established, not whether the business survives.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Roughly 20% of revenue is government, and management confirms ~90% of it sits in centrally funded schemes (AMRUT, Smart City) disbursed via urban local bodies — the single largest cause of the FY26 miss was fiscal grants slipping from May-June to October plus work stalling during Maharashtra and Bihar elections, and Rakesh Verma concedes chronic delayed payments requiring working capital. > "the fiscal grant to the state for the national flagship projects that we are involved in got delayed this time. Something we were expecting in May-June time frame actually got delayed till October" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Government is capped near 20% of revenue with three other vectors (automotive, corporate, IoT); management screens tenders for collectability and reports no write-offs in government business*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
