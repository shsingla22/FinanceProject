# JSW Dulux (JSWDULUX) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

JSW Dulux (formerly Akzo Nobel India, renamed after the JSW Group bought control from Dutch parent AkzoNobel N.V.) makes and sells paints and coatings in India under the Dulux brand. Roughly two-thirds of its business is decorative paint — interior and exterior wall paint, waterproofing, floor coatings and DIY spray paints sold to homeowners and painters through a retail network of about 20,000 dealers across 5,000-plus towns, with around 16,000 tinting machines placed in shops; the rest is industrial coatings sold to businesses such as carmakers, shipowners, oil-and-gas and infrastructure projects, and steel-coil processors, plus about 4,000 B2B customers. It earns money by manufacturing paint at five plants in India and selling it at a margin, running EBITDA of roughly 14-15% on quarterly revenue of about INR 900 crore, with profitability swinging on raw-material costs (raw materials are about 55% of cost) and on how much price it can pass through. Operations are India-focused; a small colorant export business to Southeast Asia and the Middle East, the powder-coatings unit and the international research centre were carved out to the former parent as part of the ownership change, and the head office has moved from Gurugram to Mumbai.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Decorative Paints** — House paint for consumers — premium interior emulsions (flagship brand Dulux Velvet Touch, plus a new luxury-finishes range), exteriors (Max), waterproofing, FloorPlus floor coating and the DIY 'Simply Refresh' range sold partly online; this is the largest part of the company, described in 2022 as about two-thirds of revenue, and it grew 8% by volume in Q3 FY26 and posted record volumes in Q4 FY26.
- **Mass, mid-tier and premium tiers within decorative** — Management splits the paint range by price tier — premium/super-premium, mid-market, and mass-economy (including the 'MEP' mass economy primer where it has been losing to new entrants); it admitted its premium products were priced 5-9% above rivals and has been cutting that premium since September 2025 to win back volume.
- **Decorative B2B** — Paint sold directly to builders, projects and institutional buyers rather than through shops — a smaller piece of decorative that management called out as growing in double digits.
- **Industrial / Coatings vertical** — The non-decorative side, roughly a third of the business, sold to industrial customers and split into automotive and specialty coatings, marine and protective, and coil coatings.
- **Automotive and Specialty Coatings (incl. vehicle refinish)** — Coatings for vehicle makers and for repainting damaged vehicles, including a tie-up with Porsche and new wins with leading Indian OEMs; it was a weak spot in FY25 but grew across the board in Q4 FY26, with mid-market vehicle refinish still under pressure.
- **Marine and Protective** — Paint for ships and for protecting steel structures, with an order book in oil, gas and infrastructure and dry-dock/ship work including jobs for the Indian Navy; management called out one of its highest-ever revenue months in March 2026, and a big Navy order in a prior year created a high base.
- **Coil coatings** — Coatings applied to metal coil used in buildings and infrastructure — a fast-growing line that won marquee projects such as Navi Mumbai Airport, though a couple of coil customers were retained by the former parent in the carve-out.
- **Powder Coatings and International Research Centre (carved out)** — A powder-coatings business and an R&D centre that used to sit inside the company but were moved to an unlisted AkzoNobel entity as part of the ownership change; together with ceased colorant exports (Dramatone/Acotone) this removed about INR 200 crore of annual revenue, roughly INR 25 crore a quarter.
- **Colour and innovation centres** — A global colour centre and a low-cost innovation centre in Bengaluru that developed products for India and other Asian markets under the old parent.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

JSW Dulux comes across as a well-run, well-liked paint company that is not being paid for it. The quality framework's best marks sit with the people and the product: management scores highest of any check for candor, repeatedly owning its own mistakes with specifics — that it had priced itself too high and lost volume, that the mass-economy and MEP end was weak, that October declined. The product side backs that up, with the company keeping ₹41 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs, a record 38% gross margin, a premium mix in the mid-40s against 22% for the industry, a steady launch cadence (Velvet Touch Eterna, Max PU crack-proof, FloorPlus, Simply Refresh, luxury finishes), a short 29-day cash cycle, and consistent cash returns including a special 70th-anniversary interim dividend and a ₹50 per share FY26 final dividend. The multibagger screen picks up the same qualities but keeps stopping short of full credit: Brand Strength, Innovation Dominance, Corporate Culture, Friendly Middleman and Forward Integrators all come back PARTIAL rather than a fit. The reason is consistent across them — the brand, the painter and dealer relationships, the 16,000-plus tinting machines and the training programmes are real, but none of them translated into the ability to hold price. That is the hinge of the whole story.

The single event that connects almost every negative reading is the arrival of a large, well-funded new entrant. Birla Opus came in up to 12% below on list price and, with extra discounts and bigger packs, an effective gap of 12–18%, and Dulux answered with 5–9% premium corrections in September–October 2025 and again in January–February 2026, down to parity with Berger. That one fact does most of the damage on its own. It is why the barriers-to-entry check scores at the bottom of the scale, with margins swinging by ±9.8 points; it is why the pricing, mix and volume check scores at the bottom too, with growth explicitly volume-led against negative price and mix; and it is why four patterns are outright NO FIT — Pricing Power, Cost to Replicate, Toll Roads and Low Price Plus — since a product being visibly negotiated down cannot be any of them. The same fact drives the two most serious entries in the risk review: Good Enough Goods at HIGH RISK and New Entrants at ELEVATED. Note especially that the one pattern rated LIKELY FIT, Market Share Gainers, rests on the very same evidence: the 23% blended volume growth that supports the share-gain case is the arithmetic other side of the price cuts, given the 6.2% revenue growth alongside it, and 9M FY26 decorative volume growth was only 1–2% while the company sits fourth in the market. One trait, read as a strength on the pattern screen and as a wound on the risk screen. Raw materials compound it — Q4 FY26 input inflation of 24–25% forced a 9.7% price increase that still lagged roughly 13.5% of raw-material inflation, with 3–4% still to take, which is the Cyclicality risk at ELEVATED and the source of those margin swings.

The combined rating lands at 41 out of 100 — Mixed, two stars — built from 45% × 50 on business quality, 30% × 18 on multibagger fit, and 25% × 52 on risk safety. The pattern score of 18 is the weakest leg and is honestly earned: one likely fit, six partials, four clear misses. The quality areas split the same way, with management at 0.67 and capital allocation at 0.67 pulling one direction while return on capital at −0.67, competitive advantage at −0.5 and industry structure at −0.5 pull the other. The starkest illustration is that generous dividends are being paid out of a business that generated only ₹4 of operating cash for every ₹100 of capital employed this year. On the other side, resilience reads SOUND and three risks — fashion, technological disruption and shifting consumer preferences — all sit at LOW, because demand is functional and repeat-purchase and the company moved toward DIY, e-commerce and premiumisation early rather than being caught out by them. Two structural items stay on WATCH: the industrial book leans on government and infrastructure work (Navy and coastal orders, oil and gas, Navi Mumbai Airport), and the business has repeatedly been reshaped by one relationship — the former parent's portfolio review took the coil-coating customers, the powder and International Research Centre entities and a roughly ₹200 crore colorant export stream, though the June 2025 outright purchase of the decorative brand and IP did end ₹60–65 crore of annual royalty, which is now being redeployed into branding. So the picture is a durable, honestly managed, cash-generative business in a structurally worse industry than the one it used to operate in, with the burden of proof sitting on whether the brand can win back price.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Market Share Gainers — LIKELY FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and the only computable check supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** Share-gain claims are made repeatedly (Nov 2024 'we continue to gain market share', FY26 23% blended volume growth, industrial wins like Navi Mumbai Airport and Porsche refinish), but 9M FY26 decorative volume growth was only 1–2%, the company sits at #4 chasing an 8% blended share, and recent volume was bought with price cuts that compressed EBITDA — exactly the low-quality share the pattern warns about.

> "as JSW Group in the paints business, I think we are already number four... we should get to number two in 3 to 4 years" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Sales compounding at 14% while margins held or improved (1% → 15%) — growth that was WON, not bought with price cuts.

### Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) — PARTIAL
*"Sells once, earns forever"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls are silent and the numbers are mixed (1 of 3 checks supporting).

- ❌ About 29 days of cash is tied up in the trade cycle — customers are not funding the business.
- ❌ Capital spending is about 33% of sales — growth is capital-hungry.
- ✅ Operating cash was positive in 12 of 12 years — the reliable cash engine recurring-revenue businesses show.

### Friendly Middleman — PARTIAL
*"The expert sells it for you"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (1 of 2 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Management repeatedly describes painters and dealers as the recommending layer and invests in trade training (2,000 youth trained a year, Project Indradhanush), and dealer testimony on quality is cited as the key asset; but roughly half the buying occasion is consumer-led and the company spends heavily on celebrity consumer advertising, so the expert-gatekeeper lock-in is only partial.

> "we use painters, largely 50% of the buying occasion is done through consumers and 50% through painters" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Operating margin of 14% — too thin for a protected franchise.
- ✅ Operating margin moved from about 1% (early years) to 15% (recent years) — the widening-profitability signature of pricing power / lock-in.

### Brand Strength — PARTIAL

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Good Enough Goods, Cyclicality, New Entrants. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

### Good Enough Goods — HIGH RISK
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Classic good-enough dynamics: management admitted Dulux was priced 5-9% above the market and that this caused volume erosion, forcing successive price corrections through Sep-Oct 2025 and Jan-Feb 2026 down to parity with Berger; the weakest area is the mass-economy segment where cheaper entrants are winning, and ~50% of purchase occasions run through painters who control the recommendation.

> "we were usually overpriced between 5 % and 9 % which is what had led to volume erosion" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Genuine product-quality differentiation acknowledged by dealers and a premium mix (mid-40s vs 22% for the market) give some protection at the top end, and management is defending the premium with limited, elasticity-tested price actions rather than blanket cuts*

- ⚠️ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs fell from about 46% to 43% — pricing is being competed away at the gross level.
- ✅ Operating margin (14%) remains near its historical peak (16%) — no collapse in the premium.
- ✅ Operating margin held (about 1% then 15%) — no drift down under competition.

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 3 numeric fingerprints reinforce it.

**What the calls show:** Decorative repaint is a flow/consumer product with stable demand, but the P&L visibly swings on crude-linked raw-material cost cycles (Q4 FY26 RMC inflation 24-25% forcing ~9.7% of price increases, with 3-4% still to take) and the B2B book is tied to customer capex — auto OEM/refinish weakness in FY25, Navy ship orders creating a one-time high base, coil and infra project lumpiness.

> "the RMC inflation is quite elevated, and it's hovering between 24% and 25%" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Majority decorative/repaint revenue is operations-linked consumption rather than customer capex; company kept investing in branding and capex through the FY25 slowdown rather than cutting back*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 4 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from -20% to 16% (swinging ±9.8 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 33% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 3% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.
