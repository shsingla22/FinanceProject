**Checks that improved:**

- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹29 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±3.5 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹29 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±11.0 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±3.5 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Entry barriers are extreme — clearances, environmental caps and lead times so long that NMDC itself took 50 years to open a new Bailadila deposit and is still awaiting clearances for Deposit 13 and Rohne — and pricing is administered rather than discount-driven. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±11.0 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Entry barriers are formidable — clearances, forest diversion and Section 5/7/9/11 sequences meant Deposit 4 took 50 years to open — and NMDC prices at or slightly above domestic peers rather than discounting.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Weak → Good. Now: The Indian merchant iron ore market is a concentrated few-player structure where NMDC can pass through new taxes and duties contractually, indicating customers lack negotiating power. | The long view had said: The customer base is highly concentrated (~70% from three large steel clients) and those customers are integrating backwards into captive mines, so end-customer negotiating power is high rather than absent.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: A clear multi-year 50→100mt vision is being executed with visible milestones (Deposit 4 opened after 50 years, Tokisud, Dubai office), but as a government PSU independence is limited — the RINL land deal required Ministry of Finance approval and related-party support decisions appear policy-driven. | The long view had said: The long-term 100mt vision is real, but strategy is set with the government rather than independently — the mineral portfolio is mandated, a 10% steel-plant stake was taken at the government's instruction, and RINL is supplied on credit for reasons that are not purely commercial.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Weak state-owned and stressed counterparties exist in its orbit — RINL and NMDC Steel both required financial support and could not pay on time — while NMDC captures volume growth as customers expand. | The long view had said: Weak players exist in the ecosystem (RINL's distress, a stagnant secondary/lump segment), but the competitive newcomer named is Lloyds Metals expanding capacity, so there is no clear pool of donators being taken from.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: The customer base is entirely corporate steelmakers, and the benefits offered match that — cost/TCO, contractual tax pass-through, long-term supply and locational logistics — which is the correct fit for corporate buyers. | The long view had said: The customer set is entirely corporate and the benefits offered (reliability, logistics, TCO) are correctly matched, but lock-in has not translated into commercial power — it has instead produced INR7,800cr of receivables and credit provisions.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Its position is explicitly regional-monopoly in character — Lloyds only competes in Raipur (<10% of sales) while NMDC holds locational advantage at Vizag and the West Coast, reinforced by dedicated slurry pipeline and blending-yard infrastructure. | The long view had said: The position is regionally protected by logistics rather than product — a new competitor only touches the ~4mt Raipur market while NMDC's rail/port access locks in Vizag and the West Coast.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: The CMD presents operationally and without personal celebrity, though the tone runs promotional at the margins ('magical figure', 'transformational year', Maharatna status). | The long view had said: Tone is mostly workmanlike and occasionally self-deprecating on capacity questions, but is offset by promotional framing of results as 'spectacularly good', a 'watershed year' and a 'game changer'.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: Capex is explicitly growth capex tied to a 50→100 mtpa expansion, rising from ~INR3,700cr (FY25) to an all-time-high ~INR3,300cr ex-land in FY26 with plans to roughly double, backed by a INR70,000cr five-to-six-year program. | The long view had said: Growth capex has risen steadily (INR2,100cr FY24 → INR3,700cr FY25 → INR3,300cr FY26) against a INR50,000cr sanctioned pipeline to take capacity from ~50mt to 100mt, but execution slips (FY26 guided INR4,200cr, delivered INR3,300cr) and returns on the incremental spend are unproven.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Weak → Poor. Now: NMDC is a large, listed, index-constituent PSU chasing Maharatna status with quarterly analyst coverage from ICICI, Kotak, Goldman Sachs and others — the opposite of an ignored niche. | The long view had said: NMDC is the opposite of under-the-radar — a flagship PSU moving to Maharatna status, covered on these calls by Kotak, ICICI, Motilal, Ambit, DSP and Goldman Sachs.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: Logistics are being built up (slurry pipeline, blending yards near Gangavaram port, ending mine-head sales) but current delivery is constrained — NMDC Steel could not dispatch product for lack of railway rakes, so convenience is an aspiration not yet an advantage. | The long view had said: A deliberate, consultant-backed shift from mine-head selling to stockyards, blending yards and slurry pipelines brings the product closer to customers, though most of this infrastructure is still years from commissioning.
- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Indian steel demand and the national push into coking coal, rare earths and critical minerals underpin the 100mt-by-2030 plan, a secularly expanding domestic end market. | The long view had said: The end market is in clear secular expansion — customers doubling pellet/steel capacity, and management sizing Indian coking coal imports rising from 55-60mt to 150-160mt on blast-furnace additions.

*Unchanged: Geographical expansion; Assurance benefits; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Return on capital (headline); Halo effect (caution); Mini-monopolies; Cost of working capital; Candor; Gain in market share; Asset turn; Cyclical market growth; Merger and acquisition; Disciplined stewards; Technology advantage; People matter (talent development); Intangible benefits; Distribution advantage.*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 9 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
