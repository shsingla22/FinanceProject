### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 3 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Across the Jan-2026 call nearly every segment is described in cycle/commodity-price terms — refrigerant realisations driven by firm global HFC prices and China's quota-led supply restrictions, agrochemical customers deferring offtake with management calling agro cycles 'vicious', and BOPET margins hinging on Chinese capacity cuts. Capex is simultaneously being stepped up to INR2,200-2,300 crore with INR1,500-2,000 crore for the new Odisha site, i.e. heavy investment into a volatile window. (The long view had said: The Jan 2026 call is dominated by cycle language across every segment: agrochemical customers deferring offtake with management calling agro cycles 'vicious', refrigerant earnings swinging on quota-driven global HFC prices, and BOPET/BOPP margins moving with Chinese capacity cuts — revenue and margins clearly fluctuate with commodity pricing and customer inventory cycles rather than steady operational demand.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: In several lines SRF's product is being substituted by cheaper equivalents rather than out-innovated — coated fabric volumes lost to cheaper Chinese imports, Thailand and Hungary films hurt by cheap imports, belting fabrics squeezed by aggressive Chinese pricing, and laminated fabric margins expected to stay strained after import-price protection was withdrawn. (The long view had said: Cheaper substitutes are compressing price across most of the portfolio simultaneously — Chinese pricing in core specialty categories forced SRF to sacrifice margin to hold share, belting fabrics and coated fabrics lost ground to cheap Chinese product, Thailand and Hungary films face cheaper imports, and laminated fabric margins are expected to stay strained after minimum import pricing was withdrawn.)
- **New Entrants**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Chinese producers acting as a structurally different, capital-indifferent competitor are resetting economics in at least four SRF businesses — specialty chemicals, BOPET, belting fabrics and coated fabrics — and management has consciously accepted lower realisations to defend volume, a classic margin-investment-as-competitive-response. (The long view had said: Analysts pressed on new fluorochemical capacity (Tanfac's 20,000 tonne R32 plant) and the risk of price softening, and Chinese producers have entered SRF's core specialty categories with volume-and-price tactics; management responds defensively but points to a structural barrier for the refrigerant business.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL); Fashion Risk (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
