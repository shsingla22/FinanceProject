### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: No sign of the premium being competed away: average realization rose every quarter of FY26 (9%, 7%, 12%, 37% year-on-year) and management never defends pricing against cheaper alternatives; residential product is location-differentiated and bought directly by the end customer, with no intermediary controlling the choice or pushing an own-label substitute. (The long view had said: Management explicitly defends a price premium against local competition — pricing 5-12% above the micro market and, where undifferentiated, trying to 'move away from the competition' — which is the classic posture of a premium product facing cheaper adequate substitutes; homebuilding is not technically complex and every micro market has local developers able to offer a comparable flat for less.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The one preference shift management identifies — buyers moving to premium homes above Rs 10mn, now ~53% of top-8-city sales — is one Puravankara is capturing rather than losing to, with FY26 realization up 21% to Rs 10,213/sq ft (Q4 up 37% to Rs 11,787) on 'better pricing and improved product mix'; housing demand itself is not eroding. (The long view had said: Management tracks a clear migration of the demand base toward premium — homes above Rs.10mn moving from ~50% to ~53% of top-8-city sales — which is a genuine shift away from the mid/affordable Provident format that once carried volumes; the company is pivoting hard into it (average realisation up 21% to Rs.10,213/sq ft in FY26, Malabar Hill/Bandra redevelopment), which works while the shift runs its way but leaves the affordable and Purva Land plotted formats (~Rs.3,000-4,400/sq ft) exposed if preferences rotate back.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (QUANT FLAG); Technological Innovation (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 9 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
