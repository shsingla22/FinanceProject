## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 28 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 5 improved, 11 regressed, 12 unchanged. 3 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them). 1 was newly assessable in the recent view.

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Allocation | Neutral (+0.20) | Good (+0.60) | +0.40 |
| Return on Capital | Good (+0.67) | Neutral (+0.33) | -0.33 |
| Growth | Neutral (-0.33) | Weak (-0.83) | -0.50 |
| Management | Good (+1.20) | Good (+0.80) | -0.40 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±3.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Capital intensity is a hard barrier — a 9 MMT greenfield was re-costed at Rs.36,354cr with a multi-year land and CCEA approval process — and ownership is stable under IOCL, but pricing is set externally rather than rationally by participants. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±4.3 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Capital barriers are enormous (Rs 36,354 cr for 9 MMT), new capacity is planned years ahead against demand, and pricing is formula-based rather than discount-driven, so competition is rational.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: Capex rose from Rs.673cr (FY25) to Rs.856cr (FY26) against a flat maintenance base of Rs.200-250cr, so incremental spend is genuinely growth-directed (Group II/III LOBS, 300 retail outlets), though returns are unproven and the Rs.36,354cr Cauvery JV sits off-balance-sheet. | The long view had said: Capex is dominated by maintenance (Rs 200-300 cr/yr) with growth confined to the Rs 1,600 cr LOBS and Rs 400 cr retail outlet projects plus a 25% stake in a Rs 36,354 cr JV refinery whose incremental ROIC is unproven.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Good. Now: A durable process-engineering edge is evidenced by a GRM premium to Singapore sustained across the window and first-quartile Solomon energy ranking, but it is operational optimisation rather than proprietary multi-product technology. | The long view had said: Process engineering strength is real (DCU utilisation from ~70% to near 100%, distillate yield to 79.1%, first-quartile Solomon) but this is operational optimisation of licensed refining units, not proprietary multi-product technology.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: Expansion is domestic and adjacent rather than geographic-brand led — 300 retail outlet licences being commissioned and the Nagapattinam JV refinery — with exports treated as opportunistic, not a market-building strategy. | The long view had said: No geographic expansion — the company is a single-site Manali refinery whose only new footprint is the Cauvery Basin JV within the same state, and exports are opportunistic rather than a strategy.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: No R&D line item is disclosed, but the calls show a steady cadence of new value-added product introductions (pharma-grade hexane in FY25; pentane, textile-grade MTO and a SAF trial in FY26) that reach commercial dispatch. | The long view had said: No formal R&D line, but continuous process/product development yields new value-added grades (pharma-grade hexane, SAF trial, pentane, textile-grade MTO) — incremental refinery optimisation rather than genuine R&D.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Weak. Now: There is regional presence in Tamil Nadu and a captive relationship with parent IOCL, but nothing in the calls evidences switching costs — realisation is set by OSP formulas and benchmark cracks, not by customer lock-in. | The long view had said: CPCL is the dominant refinery in a structurally deficit southern region with pipeline-connected logistics and an embedded IOCL offtake relationship, creating a regional/captive position rather than true switching costs.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Weak. Now: Reliability is genuine (1,884 fire-free days, stepping up LPG when supply tightened nationally) but it earns no price premium — the buyer of refined product pays the benchmark regardless. | The long view had said: Reliability is genuinely valued — 1,884 fire-free days, first-quartile Solomon operational availability, and stepping up LPG supply when national availability tightened — but the buyer still pays a formula price.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Poor. Now: Output is fungible commodity fuel (HSD, MS, LPG) sold on benchmark cracks, with no aftermarket or mindshare position anywhere in the calls. | The long view had said: No monopoly in the customer's mind — products are commodity fuels sold at refinery transfer price — though niche grades like pharma-grade hexane and MTO give small pockets of specialisation.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Good → Weak. Now: The cycle is near a peak, not a trough — GRM collapsed from $8.64 (FY24) to $4.22 (FY25) and then more than doubled to $9.2 (FY26), with Q4 at $13.75 — so the identifiable move ahead is down, not up. | The long view had said: The cycle troughed in FY25 ($4.22/bbl GRM) and has turned sharply upward through FY26 ($9.2/bbl full year, $13.75/bbl in Q4), with limited new domestic capacity for two years.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Weak. Now: The customer base is bulk/corporate offtake where price is the deciding variable, and the calls show no TCO argument or switching-cost lock-in that would fit the corporate-customer playbook. | The long view had said: Almost entirely a corporate/captive B2B model where reliability of supply matters, but the RTP contract means no TCO premium is captured, and the new retail outlet push is small and unproven.
- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Good → Neutral. Now: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹3.18 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: Utilisation is maxed (112% of installed capacity) which lifts sweating of assets, but refining is inherently asset-heavy — a 9 MMT greenfield costs Rs.36,354cr — and there is no moat protecting the returns. | The long view had said: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹3.18 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: Refining is capital-intensive and asset-heavy, but CPCL sweats an old fully-depreciated asset base at 111-112% utilisation, giving decent turns without a genuine protecting moat.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Neutral → Weak. Now: The refinery already runs above nameplate at 111-112%, so there is no physical runway to take share without new capacity, and no share-capture evidence is offered. | The long view had said: Some share opportunity from the southern MS deficit that CPCL is chasing, but ~90% of output is contractually tied to Indian Oil at RTP, so meaningful share capture is structurally constrained.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Calls are led by the Director (Finance) with technical CGMs, run on operating metrics with no CEO-personality framing, and the company is a low-profile PSU refiner. | The long view had said: Calls are led by the Director (Finance) with technical CGMs, focused on operating metrics, and management explicitly declines to comment on media speculation.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: The 300 retail outlet licences now being commissioned are the first move toward direct customer access, but they are unbuilt and CPCL otherwise reaches end customers only through the parent's channel. | The long view had said: Physical proximity to IOCL's facilities, direct RLNG pipeline connectivity and coastal location on international sea routes give genuine logistical convenience, though no sales force advantage.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Neutral → Weak. Now: CPCL is a price-taker on a benchmark crack; FY26's GRM jump to $9.2/bbl tracked Singapore's move to $5.83, with company-specific gains coming from volume records and crude/energy optimisation rather than pricing power. | The long view had said: Prices are international-parity and outside management control, so growth comes from volume/throughput and modest mix upgrades (distillate yield 76%→79.1%, value-added products), not pricing power.
- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Management quantifies unflattering items unprompted — inventory loss for the year, the GRM fall from $8.64 to $4.22, crude-sourcing disruption — though some questions (export economics, Russian discounts) get deflected into generalities. | The long view had said: Management gives precise numbers (inventory gains/losses, core vs reported GRM, forex loss), explains profit declines by naming cracks rather than excuses, and admits the limits of its own knowledge.

*Unchanged: Cost of working capital; Structural end-market growth; Security by obscurity; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Profit margin; Disciplined stewards; Intangible benefits; Halo effect (caution); Distribution to shareholders; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Return on capital (headline); Distribution advantage.*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 8 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
