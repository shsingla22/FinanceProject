Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. ### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 2 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Core demand is a flow product — electricity sold under long-term PPAs at a stable ~91% EBITDA margin — but roughly 20% merchant/infirm exposure meant Q3 FY26 revenue per MW fell on 'subdued' merchant pricing and weak wind, and the business runs a very heavy self-funded capex programme (INR8,800 crore in one quarter, 5 GW/yr) that is itself an over-investment risk. (The long view had said: Electricity is a flow product consumed in customer operations, not customer capex, and 90%+ of capacity is targeted for 25-year PPAs with stable ~92% EBITDA margins; but the uncontracted merchant/infirm slice behaves like a pure commodity — solar merchant realisations fell to ~INR2.20 and cost INR800–1,000 crores of forgone EBITDA in FY26, plus module-price and wind-resource swings.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: In Q1 FY27 management confirmed that essentially all merchant and C&I capacity — plus the new battery storage — is being contracted to a single related-party offtaker, Adani Energy Limited (~4 GW), on top of an already concentrated DISCOM offtaker base; battery growth is also flagged as dependent on managing a single supply chain. (The long view had said: Counterparty concentration is severe and increasing: sovereign offtakers at 87%, ~half the operating portfolio at the single Khavda site dependent on one evacuation corridor, and by FY27 essentially all merchant, C&I and battery capacity is contracted to related party Adani Energy Solutions on 15–25 year fixed-price arm's-length deals, with module supply partly from the group's own Mundra Solar associate.)
- **Technological Innovation**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The economics of the installed renewable base are being reshaped by storage — management is scaling from 1.4 to 10+ GWh of BESS plus pumped hydro within a year and describes batteries as the hedge for an intermittent, curtailment-prone generation fleet — so the value of unfirmed solar/wind assets depends on keeping pace with storage technology and cost curves. (The long view had said: Innovation here is incremental and value-adding rather than format-destroying — trackers, bifacial modules, 5.2MW turbines, AI/predictive-maintenance O&M, and now BESS and pumped hydro — and the company is consistently an early adopter; existing assets are insulated by 25-year fixed-tariff contracts, so falling new-build tariffs do not strand the installed base.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
