## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 29 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 6 improved, 2 regressed, 21 unchanged. 2 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them). 1 was newly assessable in the recent view.

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Allocation | Good (+0.60) | Neutral (+0.40) | -0.20 |
| Return on Capital | Neutral (-0.33) | Neutral (-0.33) | +0.00 |
| Growth | Good (+0.83) | Good (+1.00) | +0.17 |
| Management | Good (+1.20) | Good (+0.83) | -0.37 |
| Industry Structure | Weak (-0.67) | Neutral (+0.17) | +0.83 |
| Customer Benefits | Neutral (+0.50) | Good (+0.75) | +0.25 |
| Competitive Advantage | Neutral (+0.50) | Neutral (+0.50) | +0.00 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Weak → Good. Now: Blue Star out-grew the industry in every quarter of a down year and gained share, implying weaker competitors ceding ground. | The long view had said: Rather than weak competitors donating share, the evidence points the other way — lower-tier brands gaining at the expense of premium players, and Blue Star's own Malaysian JV losing to competitive pressure.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: The dual B2C/B2B mix worked as designed this year, with reliability-led corporate demand cushioning a collapse in retail room-AC offtake. | The long view had said: The benefit match is only half right — the corporate side correctly sells reliability and TCO on projects and chillers, but the retail RAC side lacks intangible pricing power and competes on price, finance schemes and warranties.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Regulatory barriers exist via BIS energy labelling, but industry channel inventory well above Blue Star's own 65 days sets up a liquidation phase that pressures pricing rationality. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.1 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing rationality is fragile — regulatory label changes trigger industry-wide inventory discounting and down-trading to cheaper brands, with Blue Star's margin gain coming from unilaterally opting out of the discount war.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Room AC is a fragmented multi-brand market where Blue Star holds only ~14%, and the B2B side depends on tender-driven customers with real negotiating power. | The long view had said: The core RAC market is fragmented and hyper-competitive with the leader at only ~13.75% share and lower-tier brands taking share, so the end customer retains substantial negotiating power.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: No switching-cost lock-in or captive aftermarket economics are described; the projects business is competitively tendered with order finalisations routinely deferred. | The long view had said: No switching-cost lock-in or captive aftermarket is evidenced; RAC demand moves with channel inventory, discounting and consumer finance, and B2B projects are re-tendered order by order.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: FY26 was an identifiable cyclical trough (weak summer, GST pause, label change) and the upturn is already visible — record Q4 revenue, order inflow up 35.7%, and a strong summer underway from 13 April. | The long view had said: FY26 was clearly a cyclical trough (weak summer, GST disruption, label-change destocking, PAT down 4.3%) with an identifiable upturn — summer set in on 13 April 2026, pent-up demand, and a record Q4 revenue.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: Growth capex continues in capacity, R&D and digitalization and capital employed jumped from Rs.2,427 cr to Rs.3,258 cr, but FY26 net profit de-grew 4.3%, so incremental returns on that new capital were negative this year. | The long view had said: Sustained growth capex — new Wada and Sri City plants, continued capacity/R&D/digitalization investment, with capital employed rising from ~Rs 1,018 cr (Jun-22) to Rs 3,258 cr (Mar-26) — but incremental returns are modest given segment margins of only 6.5–8.5%.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Growth is organic with no M&A, infra projects are declined on profitability grounds and costs were cut promptly, though working capital swung the balance sheet into net borrowings mid-year before recovering to a thin net cash position. | The long view had said: Consistently organic with prudent leverage (NCDs prepaid in 2022, net cash for most of the period), explicit refusal to chase low-margin infrastructure share, and cost rationalisation taken early in the FY26 downturn.

*Unchanged: Geographical expansion; Cost of working capital; Profit margin; Intangible benefits; Distribution advantage; Distribution to shareholders; Assurance benefits; Out of limelight; Return on capital (headline); Convenience benefits; Gain in market share; Research and development; Asset turn; Mini-monopolies; Halo effect (caution); Technology advantage; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Structural end-market growth; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Security by obscurity; Candor.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Category leadership is claimed in ducted systems and scroll chillers with top-three in VRF and screw chillers, and share edged above 14% in RAC, but the calls contain no heritage/A&P/premium-pricing discussion and revenue still tracked weather and GST rather than brand pull. (The long view had said: Blue Star is described as 'one of the preferred brands with first time buyers in Tier 3, 4 and 5 markets' and sustains A&P at ~1.5-2% of product revenue with confidence that skipping a season's spend does not erode brand image — but the evidence shows share gains of only decimal points and no premium pricing that survives a weak summer.)

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: LIKELY FIT → NO FIT. Why now: The only pricing action in the window is a defensive cost pass-through forced by currency and commodities, taken in a year when volumes fell and channel inventory ran at 65 days versus an ideal 45; the GST cut episode showed demand is highly price-sensitive. (The long view had said: Management passes cost through rather than pricing above inflation: they state 13% of price increase was 'warranted' but only 8% realised by May 2026, and price is explicitly framed as demand-determined ('Price is a factor determined by the market'); the discipline of refusing to discount before the Jan-2026 label change shows conditional, not structural, pricing power.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Share gain is claimed in all four quarters of FY26 (above 14% in Q1, 'gaining market share' in Q2, 'some decimal points' in Q3, 'marginally' in Q4), but the gains are self-estimated and small, achieved in a flat-to-declining market with FY26 Unitary revenue down 5.1% and group PAT down 4.3%. (The long view had said: Share numbers are disclosed and creep upward (13.25% in Q1 FY23, 13.75% in Q1 FY25, 'gained market share marginally' in Q3/Q4 FY26) and leadership is held in ducted AC, deep freezers and water coolers — but the gain is small, ranking slipped from #2 to top-three in VRF/screw chillers, and management deliberately forgoes share in projects for cash and margin.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No single customer, licensor or supplier is named as driving results in any of the four calls; demand is spread across RAC retail channel, buildings, data centres, factories, hospitals, malls, education, pharma and processed food, and management reports continuing dealer-network expansion each quarter. (The long view had said: Customer base is clearly broad (Reliance, L&T, HDFC/Axis, retail, dealers across tiers) with continuing dealer-network expansion, but the calls repeatedly flag input-side dependence on imported copper, compressors and electronics with multi-quarter resolution timelines and semiconductor shortages that cost Segment-III margin.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: FY26 revenue growth collapsed to 3.6% and PAT de-grew 4.3% purely on external swings — an unseasonal monsoon killed the summer, RAC segment revenue fell 5.1% for the year, and Segment-I margins slid from 8.2% to 7.4% on project mix; Segment-I revenue is itself tied to customer capex (data centres, factories, infra) with order inflow for FY26 down 10% and the EMP order book shrinking through the year, while INR depreciation and commodity prices forced price hikes in Q4. (The long view had said: Both engines are cyclical: Segment-I order inflow is explicitly tied to the customer construction/capex cycle (metro, data centres, factories, PLI-driven manufacturing investment), while RAC revenue swings with the summer — Q1FY25 saw ~70% market growth in April-May 2024 versus a washout FY26 where revenue grew just 3.6% and PAT de-grew, after 87% growth in Q1FY23. Margins moved with it (Segment-I 9.9% in Q1FY25 to 6.5% in Q4FY26).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
