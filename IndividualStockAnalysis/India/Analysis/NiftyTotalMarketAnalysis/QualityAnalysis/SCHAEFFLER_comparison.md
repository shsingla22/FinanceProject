### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: There is a visible cost culture — localization raised to 79% driving the first 20%+ EBITDA quarter, cost-reduction work recognised by Sandvik Mining, and deliberate 'cost corrections' in Q1 CY26 — but the calls show margin capture, not low price passed to customers, so the 'low price' half of the pattern is unevidenced. (The long view had said: Cost work is framed purely as internal margin protection (overhead management, portfolio recalibration where they consciously walked away from low-margin industrial revenue in Q1 CY26), not as savings passed to customers; 19% EBITDA and premium technology positioning are the opposite of the low-margin/high-turns model.)

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Leadership has been stable (Kadam as MD/CEO across the window) and management discloses soft patches candidly — the 5.1% sequential revenue fall, a liquidity crunch in some sectors, and moderated capex which it explained rather than buried — while sustaining localization and CSR investment; the 12-month lens is too short to verify multi-year promise-versus-delivery. (The long view had said: Harsha Kadam (MD/CEO) has led throughout the period with promises tracked and delivered — the 30–50% dividend payout policy honoured at 45% in a weak year, e-axle localisation followed through, localisation reaching 80% — while bad news is disclosed promptly and repeatedly: Koovers' continuing losses each quarter, 'earnings quality definitely could have been better', and 'free cash flow... we have work to do here'.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Aftermarket and industrial sales run through distributors and service partners — the Adani Power award cites the distributor network and TVS Mobility is described as a strategic aftermarket partner — but the recent calls describe no trade training programmes or prescriber-style lock-in. (The long view had said: The aftermarket model runs squarely through mechanics and workshops — REPXPERT technical training vans revived in Q2 CY26 and 7,000 Koovers workshops acquired in 2023 — but this channel-through-the-trade dynamic covers only the 12% VLS segment, while the bulk of revenue is direct OEM and industrial contracting.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (LIKELY FIT).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 11 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
