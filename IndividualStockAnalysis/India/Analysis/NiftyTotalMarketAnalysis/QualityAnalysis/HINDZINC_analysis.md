# Hindustan Zinc Ltd. (HINDZINC) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: METALS*

Hindustan Zinc digs zinc, lead and silver out of its own underground mines in Rajasthan, India, and then smelts that ore into finished metal at its own smelters — so it earns money across the whole chain from rock to saleable metal rather than just one step. It sells refined zinc (largely for galvanizing steel and for infrastructure and battery uses), refined lead, and silver, plus some by-products and unprocessed lead concentrate, into both the Indian domestic market and export markets, with prices set by global London Metal Exchange benchmarks in US dollars. Because prices are set by the market, profit comes down to volume and cost: management runs roughly 1.1 million tonnes a year of mined metal and about 1.0-1.1 million tonnes of refined metal, at a zinc cost of production of about $959 per tonne for FY26 (a five-year low, and $851/t in the June 2026 quarter), which they say places them at the low end of the global cost curve. It is part of the Vedanta group, listed in India (BSE 500188 / NSE HINDZINC), with all mining and smelting concentrated in Rajasthan, and it is now branching into fertilizer and critical minerals such as potash, tungsten and rare earths.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Mining / mined metal** — The underground mines — Rampura Agucha, Sindesar Khurd, Zawar, Rajpura Dariba — that produce zinc-lead ore; output crossed 1.1 million tonnes of mined metal in FY26 with reserves and resources of 468.6 million tonnes and 25-plus years of mine life, and management guides to 1,150 KT for FY27.
- **Smelting / refined metal (zinc and lead)** — Smelters at Chanderiya, Dariba and Debari that turn the mined concentrate into saleable refined zinc and lead; refined output was 1,048 KT in FY26 (over 1 million tonnes for four straight years) and this is the core revenue engine, helped by debottlenecking and the new 160,000 tonne-per-annum roaster at Debari.
- **Silver / precious metals** — Silver recovered alongside lead production and sold as refined silver — 627 tonnes in FY26 with FY27 guidance of 680 tonnes — and although it is a by-product by volume, management says the silver portfolio contributes about 45-46% of total profitability, driven by solar, electronics and investor demand.
- **Lead concentrate sales** — Opportunistic direct sales of unprocessed lead concentrate to cash in on high silver prices and clear inventory — 12,000 tonnes sold in the March 2026 quarter and 10,000 tonnes in the June 2026 quarter, described as adding roughly 37 tonnes of silver-equivalent sales.
- **Growth and expansion projects** — A pipeline of capital projects including a 250,000 tonne-per-annum integrated zinc smelter at Debari, a tailings reprocessing plant at Rampura Agucha, and a hot acid leaching plant at Dariba to pull extra lead and silver out of smelter waste (commissioning pushed to Q2 FY27).
- **Fertilizer business (Hindustan Zinc Fertilizer Pvt Ltd)** — A 510,000-tonne fertilizer plant at Chanderiya built to use a by-product of the smelting process, described as a synergistic project along the value chain, with commissioning expected in early Q2 FY27.
- **Critical minerals / multi-metal diversification** — A newer, still pre-production push to become a diversified multi-metal miner — three secured critical mineral blocks in potash, tungsten and rare earths, plus a rare earth and yttrium mining lease at Gundlupet, Karnataka won in the June 2026 quarter.
- **Zinc-based batteries (early stage)** — A small exploratory effort to create new demand for its own metal, via a supply tie-up with US-based AEsir Technologies for nickel-zinc batteries and a research collaboration with the Jawaharlal Nehru Centre for Advanced Scientific Research.
- **Power and cost management** — Not a revenue line but a major profit lever the calls keep returning to — long-term 25-year renewable power deals with Serentica (530 MW capacity contracted) that lock in flat power rates, with renewable consumption at 22% by mid-2026 and power being the company's largest single cost bucket.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Hindustan Zinc scores 57 out of 100 — "Decent", three stars — and the number hides an unusually lopsided company. It earns extraordinary returns: ₹54 of operating cash for every ₹100 of capital employed, ₹54 of operating profit on every ₹100 of sales, margins that wobble by only about ±2.8 points a year, and only about four days of cash tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. Return on Capital scores highest of all the quality areas (1.33), and the money is put to work rather than parked: growth capex — the fertiliser plant and roaster at roughly ₹2,000–2,200 crore in FY23, the 250 KTPA Debari smelter, tailings reprocessing, and a 2x expansion costed at $500–600 million for FY27 — is funded out of free cash flow at 57–59% returns, alongside very large dividends (₹21 a share, or ₹8,873 crore, in Q1FY23; ₹11 a share interim in Q1FY27) paid from a balance sheet that now holds net cash. The root of all of it is one asset: 468.6 million tonnes of ore reserves and resources, over 25 years of mine life, the second-largest zinc reserves in the world, and the only primary-route silver production in India. That is why the "Cost to Replicate" pattern is the single strong fit in the screen and why the barriers check scores a 2 — a competitor cannot buy its way to a comparable orebody.

The same orebody that creates the moat also creates most of the weaknesses, and the report should be read that way rather than as a list of unrelated positives and negatives. Because the deposit sits in Rajasthan, the geographical expansion check scores -1: five years have produced no international footprint, only a rare-earth block in Karnataka, with growth framed entirely around Indian demand. Because a mine runs on leases, approvals and allocations — mining leases and IBM clearances, three state-allocated critical mineral blocks in potash, tungsten and rare earths, the Karnataka rare-earth lease, coal linkage — Government Dependency is the one HIGH risk in the review, and it is high precisely because the assets cannot be moved if a government decision goes the wrong way. And because the output is LME-grade metal sold at exchange-set prices, three patterns come back as no fit at all (Pricing Power, Toll Roads, Brand Strength), the pricing-mix-volume check scores -1, network effects score -2, and Competitive Advantage is the weakest quality area at -0.67. The same trait then reappears as Cyclicality at ELEVATED: management explains results by the zinc and silver price in call after call (revenue up 44% on a 34% move in zinc; revenue up 77% and EBITDA up 109% on supportive prices) and carries a standing hedge book — 71 KT of zinc and 59 tonnes of silver sold forward. Strong moat, no control over price: those are two sides of one fact.

What the company does control is cost, and cost is the hinge connecting the remaining patterns and risks. Cost of production has fallen from $1,264 a tonne in July 2022 to $1,089 in H1FY25, $959 in FY26 and $851 in Q1 FY27, against a stated design target of $1,000 — this is the "Low Price Plus" pattern, and it is also the reason three risk channels come back LOW. A commodity with no brand premium cannot be undercut on price by a cheaper rival when you are already in the global first quartile (Good Enough Goods, LOW); demand is moving toward the company rather than away, with silver drawn by solar and electronics, zinc by infrastructure and galvanisation, lead by batteries (Shifting Consumer Preferences, LOW); and the technology in question is being expanded, not displaced, through incremental work like the fumer, the 160 KTPA roaster, hot acid leaching and tailings reprocessing (Technological Innovation, LOW, and the "Innovation Dominance" likely fit, though no R&D spend is ever disclosed). But the cost advantage has a soft spot that the risk review names: Coal India linkage materialisation swung from 64% in Q4FY26 to 36% in Q1FY27, pushing the company onto costlier imported coal — the reason Stakeholder Concentration sits at WATCH, partly offset by 530MW of 25-year fixed-price renewable power. The overall picture is a business with a rare, defensible asset and genuine operating discipline (delivering on the one-million-tonne pledge for four straight years, beating its own cost guidance, disclosing fatalities openly), whose ceiling is set by things it does not decide: the metal price, the government, and a single geography. Resilience reads SOUND; the middling score reflects a company that is excellent at what it controls and exposed on what it does not.
