### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

**Checks that improved:**

- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Weak → Good. Now: The sector is visibly near a trough — Middle East supply disruption, sulphur/ammonia spikes, phos acid at $1,700 vs $1,360, deficit monsoon and industry production down 21% — with identifiable upside from an expected NBS revision and normalising supply, though the timing rests on government action. | The long view had said: The company sits mid-cycle at an unfavourable point — the West Asia crisis has spiked ammonia/sulphur costs beyond NBS compensation, forcing production cuts to 72% utilisation and margin compression, with no identified upturn other than hoped-for subsidy revision.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Weaker competitors are visibly retreating — industry production and imports fell 21% and 38% in Q1FY27 on unviable subsidy economics — while Coromandel, integrated and raw-material-covered, raised primary share from 18% to 22%. | The long view had said: Coromandel is explicitly taking share from importers (6-7 million tons of imported DAP targeted for NPK substitution) and from weaker players hit by production outages, and NACL itself was a loss-making donator it acquired.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Deliberate, executed expansion out of the southern base into North and Central India (4 lakh tons YTD, +30%, then +24% for FY26) built on multi-year seed marketing, plus upstream Senegal and an SSP facility being evaluated there for international markets. | The long view had said: Credible expansion beyond its southern base into North/Central India (24% growth), retail entering Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu, Nano exports and a Senegal SSP plant under evaluation — though these are early and India-centric rather than a premium brand travelling globally.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. Now: No R&D spend disclosure, but a steady stream of differentiated products (GroPlus, GroAlpha, Urea-SSP, nano, biologicals, unique NPK grades at 35-36% of volume) shows R&D translating into saleable premium products. | The long view had said: R&D output is steady and monetized — 10 new CPC products in FY26 contributing 21% of revenue, new-product share in domestic B2C at 32% vs 23%, plus Nano, biologicals and a new corporate research centre at IIT Chennai Research Park.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Mix is genuinely improving (unique grades 33%→36%, differentiated SSP >50%), but realisations are subsidy/MRP-bound and the only price action taken was cost pass-through that still did not cover input inflation. | The long view had said: Growth is increasingly mix-led — unique grades at 35% of fertilizer volume, differentiated SSP (GroPlus, Urea-SSP) above 50% of SSP sales, and new CPC products at 32% of B2C — but headline fertilizer pricing is subsidy-administered, capping true pricing power.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Some brand-led positions exist (SSP leadership, GroPlus/Urea-SSP, unique grades at ~35%), but the core NPK/DAP product is a subsidised commodity sold against 10 mn tons of imports, so nothing approaching a monopoly in the farmer's mind. | The long view had said: Genuine niche near-monopolies exist — ~60% share in Nano DAP, market leadership in SSP and in neem-based biopesticides — though these are small relative to the core fertilizer business.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: The largest player holds only ~17.5% consumption share and pricing is set by government NBS rates and MRP rather than by industry structure, so this is neither a concentrated nor a price-setting oligopoly. | The long view had said: Phosphatics is a concentrated industry with high capital and raw-material-linkage barriers where Coromandel is the largest player at 17.5% share, but pricing is set by government NBS rates rather than by industry structure.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Neutral → Weak. Now: Beyond safety awards there is no discussion of talent development, and the five-year record shows a revolving door at the top — four different Managing Directors and two CFOs since FY2021 — which argues against a settled internal leadership pipeline. | The long view had said: Safety and operational-excellence certifications suggest systematic capability building, but the excerpts say nothing about talent development or succession pipeline, and MD turnover has been high (Goel → Alagappan → Vellayan family → Sankarasubramanian, with two CFOs).

*Unchanged: Merger and acquisition; Halo effect (caution); Profit margin; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Out of limelight; Assurance benefits; Intangible benefits; Advertisement and promotion; Technology advantage; Gain in market share; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Distribution advantage; Security by obscurity; Return on capital (headline); Cost of working capital; Disciplined stewards; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Barriers to entry and rationality; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Structural end-market growth; Asset turn; Candor; Convenience benefits.*

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these — identical by construction): Persistence of growth (Neutral).*

- **Network effects** — Weak: Nothing in the business model is self-reinforcing with scale — fertilizer is a physical, per-transaction sale, and the emerging data layer (QR traceability, Agri-Stack) is government-owned rather than Coromandel's network.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 8 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Share numbers are disclosed every quarter and trend up on both a full-year and quarterly basis — FY26 consumption share 17.5% making it the largest phosphatic player, Q1FY27 primary share 22% vs 18% and POS share 16%, with 24–30% growth in newly-entered North/Central markets — and the gains came with volume discipline rather than dumping (Q3 share was deliberately let slip to 14% to avoid channel stuffing). (The long view had said: Explicit, rising share numbers over the full period: consumption share 17%→20% in Q2FY25, 17.5% phosphatic leadership and record 4.3mn tons in FY26, primary share 18%→22% and POS share to 16% in Q1FY27, SSP market leadership and ~60% of Nano DAP — with North/Central India expansion growing 24%, and gains achieved while EBITDA and net cash held up rather than through underpricing.)

**That weakened:**

- **Forward Integrators**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Coromandel sells through its own retail centres and delivers a paid drone-spraying service to roughly 3 lakh acres, and is evaluating an SSP plant in Senegal to capture downstream value from its own rock, but no store count, retail revenue share or D2C economics are quantified on these calls. (The long view had said: A sustained, funded move toward the farmer: 45 stores added in H1FY25, 300+ in FY26 including entry into Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu, over 1,200 outlets by Q1FY27 with retail revenue +85% year-on-year and 76% of stores profitable, plus e-commerce, last-mile delivery, drone spraying and in-housing white-label retail SKUs into its own bio plants.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Regular product/process extension is visible — unique-grade share rising 33%→36%, nano and biological/organic ranges, Technical MAP and seaweed granulation plants, new phos-acid technology for optimum rock utilisation — but no R&D spend, share-of-industry-R&D or pipeline economics are disclosed in the last four calls. (The long view had said: Named new products with quantified revenue impact every single year — four combination products in Q1FY23, 10 new products in H1FY25, 10 in FY26 at 21% of CP revenue, new-product share in domestic B2C rising 23%→32% — plus Nano DAP built to ~50-60% market share, five biopesticides and three biofertilisers, patent filings and a new corporate research centre at IIT Chennai Research Park.)

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (QUANT SIGNAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No competing technology platform is discussed defensively in any of the four calls; instead management describes a steady cadence of small, value-adding innovation it is participating in — green ammonia offtake covering up to 20% of ammonia needs, nano fertilisers, drone spraying across 3 lakh acres, biologicals, and new phos-acid technology at Kakinada to optimise rock usage. (The long view had said: The industry is facing several format shifts at once — nano fertilizers substituting granular DAP, green ammonia offtake, coal/lignite gasification feedstock, drone application, biologicals replacing chemistry, and QR-code traceability that could reshape distribution — any of which could strand conventional granulation assets the company is still expanding. But Coromandel is on the offensive side of each: ~50–60% share of Nano DAP, Gromor drone spraying across 3 lakh acres, an IIT Chennai research centre and a steady cadence of small product launches.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Across the four calls management explains results almost entirely through commodity input cycles — phos acid moving $1,250 to $1,290 to a Q2 FY27 settlement of $1,700, sulphur to $550, ammonia spikes from Middle East outages — and in Q1 FY27 deliberately cut output to 72% utilisation because inputs were 'beyond the affordability of any industry player'. Margins swing sharply even though volumes are broadly stable, and heavy capex (Kakinada acid plants, granulation train, Senegal mine) is being committed through this volatility. (The long view had said: Demand is a flow product — farmers consume fertilizer every season and industry consumption held ~24 mn tons flat year on year — but the input side is a pure commodity cycle: phos acid settled at $1,700/t in Q2FY27 vs $1,360, ammonia and sulphur spikes forced management to cut output to 72% utilisation, and EBITDA has oscillated (Rs 687cr Q1FY23, Rs 975cr Q2FY25, Rs 761cr Q1FY27) with margins compressing whenever commodities run ahead of subsidy. The company kept investing counter-cyclically (Rs 3,000cr over two years: Kakinada acid plants, granulation, Senegal mine) through the downcycle.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
