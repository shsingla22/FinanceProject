# Eris Lifesciences Ltd. (ERIS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: PHARMA*

Eris Lifesciences is an Indian pharmaceutical company that sells branded prescription medicines — mainly for long-term (chronic and sub-chronic) conditions like diabetes, heart disease, skin disorders and kidney disease — which it promotes to specialist doctors in India through its own field sales force, tracking productivity as revenue per rep per month (YPM). About 90% of sales come from this Domestic Branded Formulations (DBF) business in India, with the remaining roughly 10% from an exports and contract-manufacturing arm (Swiss Parenterals) selling injectables into Latin America, other emerging markets and, increasingly, Europe on a CDMO basis. It makes money on high gross margins (historically around 80%, more recently 75-86% depending on mix) by owning and pushing well-known brands, and it has grown heavily by acquiring brand portfolios and companies — Oaknet, derma brands from Glenmark and Dr. Reddy's, two tranches of Biocon's India business, Swiss Parenterals — then lifting their margins sharply, funded largely by debt. In FY26 consolidated revenue was about INR 3,129 crore with EBITDA of INR 1,120 crore, and it manufactures in its own plants in Gujarat/Ahmedabad, Sikkim and a Bhopal insulin fill-finish site.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Domestic Branded Formulations (DBF)** — The core India business of branded prescription medicines sold to doctors, worth INR 2,778 crore of revenue in FY26 at a 37% operating margin — roughly 90% of the company and more than 85% chronic/sub-chronic by turnover; management calls it a 'specialty plus super-specialty' business and says Eris now ranks in the top 20 of the Indian market, up from rank 29 at its 2017 IPO.
- **Cardiometabolic (Oral Anti-Diabetes + Cardiac Care)** — The flagship therapy area of diabetes tablets and heart/blood-pressure drugs, which together with vitamins/minerals used to be 80% of revenue and had fallen to about 65% by 2023 as other therapies were added; oral anti-diabetes market share was nearing 6% in FY25, and a new hypertension molecule, Esaxerenone, developed in-house, is its big launch.
- **Insulins / injectable diabesity** — Insulin and injectable diabetes-obesity products (Basalog, Insugen, Xsulin, Xglar), mostly bought from Biocon in 2023-24, where market share in the RHI-plus-Glargine market rose from about 9% at acquisition to 16% by FY26 against a 25% target; Eris also launched its generic Semaglutide brand 'Sundae' in vials and pens in March-April 2026 and claims the number-one volume rank in injectable Semaglutide in its first full month.
- **Dermatology** — Skin medicines built almost entirely by acquisition (Oaknet for INR 650 crore, nine Glenmark brands for INR 340 crore, nine cosmetic-derma brands from Dr. Reddy's for INR 275 crore, plus Biocon's derma portfolio), covering both medical derma and cosmetic products like anti-acne, moisturisers and hair care; it became the fourth-largest therapy at roughly 15% of revenue by 2023, ranks number 3 in its covered market, and grew 14.2% in FY26 versus an 8.6% market.
- **Super-specialty: Nephrology, Oncology and Critical Care** — Kidney, cancer and hospital critical-care products added through the Biocon deals, described as still-growing super-specialty segments; management says critical care 'did not take off as expected' in FY26 and it is discontinuing a basket of low-margin, non-core general injectables worth about 2% of DBF revenue.
- **Other emerging therapies: CNS, Women's Health, VMN** — Central nervous system drugs (entered via the Strides acquisition), women's health, and the vitamins/minerals/nutrition range; together with dermatology these 'emerging' therapies rose from 12% to 28% of revenue by early 2023.
- **International Business / Swiss Parenterals** — The exports arm acquired in FY24 (then under INR 300 crore revenue and INR 80 crore EBITDA), which makes sterile injectables for emerging markets and Latin America and is building an EU contract-manufacturing (CDMO) order book of over INR 1,000 crore; it did INR 348 crore revenue and INR 110 crore EBITDA in FY26 at ~32% margin, is guided to INR 550-600 crore in FY27, and targets INR 1,000 crore by 2029-30, with a third injectable unit due in 2028.
- **Trade Generics** — A small unbranded-generics line that the company wound down and discontinued during FY26, contributing essentially nothing to FY26 revenue.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

**Capital Allocation — Neutral (+0.17)** · 6 of 6 checks had evidence

  > "CapEx for the financial year came close to INR 300 crores, largely on Biologics and Sterile Injectables" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Research and development — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: In-house R&D has repeatedly produced approvals and launches (Dapagliflozin combinations, first-in-India Esaxerenone developed and trialled by their own team, Semaglutide internalisation, Aspart/Degludec pipeline), though several FY26 launches slipped on regulatory delays.
  > "This has been successfully formulated by our own R&D team... We were the first company to develop this in India" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Stakeholder Concentration — ELEVATED
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Customer/channel concentration is genuinely low (fragmented doctor and chemist base served by ~3,700+ MRs), but supplier concentration is real and has already cost money: two consecutive years of ~Rs.50cr insulin sales lost because third-party supply could not meet demand, and the flagship Semaglutide launch runs on a single exclusive supply partnership with Natco. > "we had to take this revenue loss because the supplies were not commensurate with the demand second year running" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate backward integration to remove supplier dependence — derma manufacturing internalised from Jan-2024, RHI vials in-house at Bhopal (5mn+ vials), Semaglutide validation batches at Ahmedabad with capacity doubleable at minimal cost; broad prescriber base with no named customer driving results*

- ⚠️ Customers now take about 71 days to pay, up from 19 — lengthening collection terms are how weak bargaining power against concentrated customers shows up in the numbers.

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** As a branded-generic player Eris sells molecules others can copy, and the calls show the mechanism biting: Zayo collapsed from Rs.5cr to Rs.1.5cr monthly once 'generalization' happened at LOE, tail-end injectable brands were discontinued for low profitability, trade generics wound down, and the biggest new launch was deliberately priced below competition with lower initial gross margins.
