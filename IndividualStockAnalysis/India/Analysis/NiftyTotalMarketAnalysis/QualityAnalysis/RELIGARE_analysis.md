# Religare Enterprises (RELIGARE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Religare Enterprises Limited (REL) is an Indian listed holding company — a "core investment company" (CIC) — that itself has almost no operating business, and instead owns controlling stakes in four financial-services companies that serve Indian retail and small-business customers. Its main business by far is selling health insurance policies through Care Health Insurance (63.2% owned), where money comes from premiums collected minus claims and expenses, plus investment income on the premium float; the rest of the group sells stock-broking services to retail investors and lends money to small businesses and affordable-housing borrowers, earning brokerage fees and interest. On a consolidated basis FY26 total income was INR8,493 crores with a profit after tax of INR73.16 crores, of which the insurance segment accounted for INR7,969 crores of revenue versus INR537 crores from all the financial-services businesses. It operates only in India, through branch networks, agents, partners and digital channels, and the standalone parent earns little on its own (income of just a few crores a quarter, usually a small loss). Management has proposed a demerger that would move the lending, broking and ancillary businesses under Religare Finvest as a separately listed company, leaving REL as a pure health-insurance holding company.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Care Health Insurance** — A standalone health insurance company (63.2% owned by REL) that management calls India's second-largest, selling 40-plus retail and group employer-employee health products through agents, branches and digital channels across 271 branch offices and a 22,000-hospital network; it is overwhelmingly the group's biggest business, with FY26 gross written premium of INR11,417 crores (up from INR9,200 crores), profit before tax of INR539 crores, an investment book above INR10,000 crores, and profitability every year since FY19.
- **Religare Broking (RBL)** — A wholly owned full-service non-bank stockbroker for retail investors, present in over 400 cities with 68 branches, about 12 lakh total customers and 2.5 lakh active clients, which also runs an e-governance services business on a digital platform with a nationwide agent network; it is small relative to insurance, reporting FY26 income of INR373 crores and profit after tax of INR22 crores.
- **Religare Finvest (RFL) — SME/MSME lending** — A wholly owned non-banking finance company with over 15 years in secured SME lending that has cleaned up its legacy problems (fraud tag and corrective action plan removed, now debt-free with capital adequacy of 197.6% and net NPA around 1%) and is described as ready to restart lending rather than as a large earner today — its Q4 FY26 profit of INR89 crores came mainly from recoveries, and it holds over INR591 crores of cash and liquid investments.
- **Religare Housing Finance (RHDFCL)** — The smallest business, an 87.5%-owned subsidiary of Religare Finvest that lends for affordable and mid-income housing in smaller towns and cities, with assets under management of roughly INR245 crores, about 3,200 customers across 8 states, FY26 income of around INR30 crores and a loss of INR18.6 crores.
- **Religare Enterprises Limited (standalone holding company / CIC)** — The listed parent itself, which holds the stakes, allocates capital to the subsidiaries and raises funds (a INR1,500 crore warrant issue split equally between the Burman promoter group and other investors), but has very limited operations of its own — standalone quarterly income of only INR2.4–7.6 crores against higher expenses, so it typically posts a small standalone loss.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

The story starts and largely ends with one business: Care Health. It produced INR7,969 crores of the group's INR8,493 crores of FY26 consolidated income, and it is the part that is visibly working. Care has lifted its share of retail health insurance in every one of the last three quarterly calls — 11.2% of the industry, then 11.4%, then 11.6% (19.2% to 19.7% of standalone health insurers) — growing roughly 9 percentage points faster than the industry, which is why 'Market Share Gainers' is the one multibagger pattern rated a likely fit. It is doing this into end markets that are still small relative to their potential (mortgages at 11% of GDP against 69% in the UK, demat penetration far below the US and China, affordable housing compounding 20-22%), and the group as a whole converted INR100 of capital into INR54 of operating cash this year. Capital is being put to work rather than sat on: an INR1,500 crore warrant issue, INR322 crores into Care whose return on equity rose to 19.9%, and technology builds at the lending and housing arms. Overall resilience reads as sound, and growth is the single strongest area of the quality framework.

The trouble is that the same traits producing the growth also produce most of the weaknesses. Care sells overwhelmingly through intermediaries — agency is about 42% of gross premium with 4.11 lakh agents and roughly 50,000 added in FY26 — which supports the 'Friendly Middleman' pattern as a partial fit, but the daily commission payouts that keep those agents loyal are exactly what makes the 'Good Enough Goods' risk elevated: the person choosing the product is the agent, not the customer, and a combined ratio of about 101.4% shows premiums priced to essentially no underwriting profit. The same channel dependence is what pushes the stakeholder concentration risk onto the watch list. Likewise, the recent demand surge came from an 18% price reduction via the GST exemption — an effective price cut — while the claims ratio rose 2.7% year-to-date, which is why 'Pricing Power' is a clear no-fit and why the framework scores intangible benefits at -1: customers are responding to price, not to the name. Cost discipline, product work, direct branches and hard-to-copy assets like the 22,000-hospital network all register, but only as partial fits, because management never explains why a well-funded newcomer could not assemble the same things. Two patterns fail outright on history rather than economics: the Religare name carries RBI corrective action from 2018 and a bank fraud tag until 2025 instead of heritage value, and the culture record shows funds described as misappropriated and siphoned by former promoters, a contested chairmanship and managing-director turnover.

On the risk side, the biggest exposure is to government and regulator decisions, rated high risk, and it links straight back to the weakest quality scores. Regulatory action has repeatedly set outcomes: the RBI plan froze the lending arm from January 2018 until July 2025, and the IRDAI accounting change reshaped reported premium. The insurance amendment bill permitting far higher foreign ownership, flagged by management, is simultaneously the reason the barriers-to-entry check scores -2 and the reason new-entrant risk is elevated — barriers are falling, not rising, in the group's best business. Profit margins swing by about 23.6 points a year and only INR2 of every INR100 of sales is operating profit, consistent with an industry where key costs sit outside management's hands. The broking arm adds cyclical and technology exposure: FY26 revenue down 2%, profit before tax down 31%, daily cash turnover down 20%, traded clients down 5%, with management openly describing itself as repairing the business and rebuilding trading technology from behind. Shareholders are funding this rather than being paid by it — no dividend or buyback policy is mentioned, and the INR1,500 crore preferential warrant issue plus further promoter creep means dilution. That combination — strong growth and cash returns, weak competitive protection, and heavy regulatory dependence — is what produces the 39 out of 100 overall score and a Mixed, two-star verdict.

The upgrade case rests on Care's share gains continuing while the combined ratio keeps improving, so that growth stops being bought with price; the downgrade case rests on foreign entrants, agent economics or another regulatory action doing to Care what the corrective action plan did to the lending arm.
