### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 3 weakened, 5 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management cites 'strong brand recall' plus pan-India distribution as its core advantage in every one of the four calls, and stretches the name into adjacencies (RP Home series, solar grid-tie inverters, white goods channel), but no A&P spend, premium realisation or share-of-voice evidence is given — and pricing had to follow costs down, not brand up. (The long view had said: Brand and distribution are the recurring, management-asserted core of the franchise from the 2022 call ('75 years… deep customer connect') to the Feb 2026 and May 2026 calls, backed by 100% supply positions on new Tata Sierra and Kia Seltos SKUs and adjacency launches (AGM, Ultra/PowerBox, solar grid-tie) under the same name.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The four calls discuss only product sales across auto OEM, aftermarket, solar, UPS and exports; there is no mention of AMC/service contracts, attach or renewal rates, deferred revenue or customer advances as a funding source. (The long view had said: Across all nine calls there is no discussion of AMC/service contracts, attach rates, renewals, subscriptions or deferred revenue as a funding source; the only adjacent metric is warranty cost, which is an expense, not a recurring revenue stream.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across all four calls management repeatedly absorbed input inflation (antimony, silver, tin, copper, sulphuric acid, rupee depreciation) rather than pricing through it, and passed the full GST cut to consumers while suspending its own price corrections — the opposite of pricing power. (The long view had said: Exide does raise prices (three tranches Jan–Mar FY26 plus ~3% on 1 April, ~5-6% cumulative) without losing volume — Q4 FY26 was its highest-ever revenue quarter — but this is cost-recovery with a lag, not pricing above inflation: management admits price rises trail raw-material moves, that it deliberately forwent a Q3 hike, and that gross margin still fell from 31.6% to 30.1%.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Management repeatedly leans on 'strong brand recall' as the defence while admitting it has not fully passed through input cost inflation to the market and is launching an explicitly economy-tier range alongside its premium one — the profile of a not-especially-complex product where pricing power is being tested, though the calls contain no direct evidence of cheaper challengers taking share. (The long view had said: Batteries are a low-brand-awareness purchase where the mechanic or dealer often picks the product, and Exide competes with unorganized players and a close organized rival; the price-hike discussion in FY26 is framed around 'competitive dynamics' and staged 5-6% corrections rather than free pricing power. In lithium the good-enough attack is explicit — the benchmark is simply the landed cost of an imported Chinese cell, which Exide can only match via scale, yield and subsidies.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Across all four calls results are attributed to broad verticals (~92% of the business growing double-digit) and a pan-India distribution network, with no single customer, licensor or supplier repeatedly driving results; named OEM wins (Tata Sierra Petrol, Kia Seltos) are incremental SKU-level additions, not the base. (The long view had said: The lead-acid base is genuinely broad — no single customer is ever named as driving results, and revenue spans aftermarket, OEM, UPS, solar, railways and exports. The concentration sits upstream and in the new business: cell technology rests on a single partner (SVOLT), all lithium raw material is imported from China, and the cell plant's offtake is described as just '2.5 or 3' large OEM customers under NDA.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Management states outright that lead-acid telecom volumes are structurally declining because of the shift to lithium-ion, and that e-rickshaw is going the same way; the company is simultaneously spending Rs. 4,802 crores of equity on a lithium-ion cell plant that is still only at customer-sample stage — the classic incumbent's forced re-platforming. (The long view had said: The core lead-acid installed base is being displaced by lithium-ion in segment after segment across the nine calls — telecom fell from ~10% of sales to ~3%, e-rickshaw is going the same way, BESS will be lithium-only, and analysts ask defensively whether data centres and other industrial segments follow. Exide's answer is a Rs. 4,802 crore captive cell plant that as of May 2026 has still not shipped a commercial cell, so the hedge is enormous, unproven and cash-consuming.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 8 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
