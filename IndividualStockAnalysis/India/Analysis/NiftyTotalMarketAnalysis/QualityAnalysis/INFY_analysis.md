# Infosys Ltd. (INFY) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: IT*

Infosys is an India-headquartered IT services company (offices at Electronics City, Bengaluru; shares listed on the BSE, NSE and the New York Stock Exchange) that does technology work for large corporate clients under contracts — building, modernising and running their software, data and IT systems, and more recently putting AI into their businesses. It makes money by billing clients for the work of its very large employee base (it hired 21,000 people in a single quarter in 2022 and 15,000–20,000 freshers a year by 2024), so revenue depends on winning deals and profit depends on pricing, utilisation of staff, and the mix of junior-to-senior people (its 'pyramid'); management guides investors each year on revenue growth in constant currency and on operating margin, which has run around 20%–22%. Growth comes largely from 'large deals' — contracts typically above $50mn each, running $1.69bn of total contract value in one quarter of FY23 and $3.6bn–$4.1bn per quarter more recently — often consolidation, cost-takeout and efficiency work where a client hands Infosys more of its IT. Its main markets are the US/North America and Europe, and the company had grown to roughly $20bn of annual revenue by 2026, up from about $10bn a decade earlier.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Financial services / Banking & Financial Services (BFSI)** — IT work for banks, cards, payments and capital-markets clients; repeatedly singled out as the swing factor for the whole company — weak spots like mortgages in 2022, then a 7.9% sequential jump in Q1 FY25 that management credited for its guidance upgrade.
- **Manufacturing** — Technology and engineering services for manufacturers; described in 2024 as a consistently strong sector after a very strong prior year, and reinforced by the acquisition of in-tech (engineering services, about €170mn annual revenue), which was large enough to help lift full-year guidance.
- **Retail and CPG / Logistics** — IT services for consumer goods, retail and logistics clients; run as its own industry group with a dedicated industry head, and described in 2024 as steady but not a standout.
- **Energy, Utilities, Resources & Services (EURS)** — IT services for energy, utility and resources companies plus other services industries; a named segment with its own segment head, characterised in 2024 as tracking in line with earlier commentary.
- **Communication, Media and Technology (including hi-tech)** — Work for telecom, media and technology clients under a dedicated segment head; hi-tech was called out as the softer end of demand in 2024.
- **Digital services (Cobalt cloud)** — The 'new technology' half of the business — cloud migration and digital transformation sold under the Cobalt brand; in Q1 FY23 digital was 61% of total revenue and growing 37.5% a year, with cloud growing faster still, and management called it the main reason it was gaining market share.
- **AI services (Topaz, Topaz Fabric, 'Hexagon for AI')** — Generative and agentic AI work — building agents for business processes, data work, legacy modernisation and AI-assisted coding — delivered through the Topaz brand and a Topaz Fabric platform that lets clients use any foundation model while keeping their own data; it reached 8.2% of total revenue by Q1 FY27, growing double digits quarter-on-quarter, with over 80,000 employees using coding tools and a plan to build 6,000 'frontier engineers'.
- **Large deals** — Not an industry but how management frames the order book — individual contracts typically over $50mn (a few near $500mn), reported every quarter with the share that is 'net new'; these are the company's stated proxy for future revenue, e.g. 34 deals worth $4.1bn in Q1 FY25 and $3.6bn at 61% net new in Q1 FY27.
- **Project Maximus (margin program)** — An internal profitability program rather than a product line, working on value-based pricing, utilisation and the staffing pyramid; it contributed 80 basis points of the 100 basis points of margin expansion in Q1 FY25 and is the main tool management cites for holding operating margin inside its 20%–22% guidance.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Infosys earns its "Decent" 58-out-of-100 rating on the strength of what it does with capital rather than what it can charge. The business turns every ₹1 of assets into ₹1.16 of sales, converts ₹100 of capital into ₹33 of operating cash in a year, and keeps ₹24 of every ₹100 of sales as operating profit with only about ±1.7 points of wobble — which is why Return on Capital scores highest of all seven quality areas (1.67) and why free cash flow hit records of $1.1bn in Q1 FY25 and $955mn in Q1 FY27. Sales have compounded at roughly 12% a year with unusual year-to-year consistency, and the people engine behind it — 50–85k freshers through Mysore training, internal job posting to fill roles, reskilling into AI work, 6,000 Frontier Engineers — scores a full 2. Management scores 0.83 and shows up in the pattern work as the single STRONG FIT, Corporate Culture: bad news is owned on the record (the McCamish cyber incident and regulator notifications in July 2024) and the July 2026 guidance cut is attributed to a specific one-time client decision rather than excused away.

The same asset-light, people-based model that produces those returns is also the source of the report's three ELEVATED risks, and this is the central tension. Because the model sells effort and headcount, margin is defended by cost levers — the pyramid, utilisation, subcontractor reduction, offshoring, automation, 'Project Maximus' at 80bps in Q1 FY25 — rather than by price. That is exactly why Low Price Plus is only a PARTIAL fit (it reads as margin management, not a structural cost edge passed to customers), why Intangible benefits scores −1 with margins pinned in a 20–22% band, and why Pricing Power is a flat NO FIT: pricing is described as 'stable' at best in July 2024, with discount fights and cost-of-living-adjustment negotiations rather than increases, peers complaining of irrational competitive pricing, and clients by July 2026 expecting AI productivity to come back to them as lower prices. Good Enough Goods is ELEVATED for the same reason. Meanwhile the replication barrier management describes — accumulated context from 2,000 deployments, undocumented legacy estates, organisation-wide change management — is real enough to make Cost to Replicate a PARTIAL fit, but it is built out of people and relationships, the very thing the Feb 2026 Investor AI Day describes as facing 'root and branch surgery'. Technological Innovation is ELEVATED because the platform shift threatens the linear delivery model itself; the offsetting evidence is that Infosys is building the new format rather than waiting — Cobalt (2022), Topaz/GenAI (2024), Topaz Fabric and the six-area AI Hexagon, with AI revenue moving from 5.5% of revenue in Q3 FY26 to 8.2% in Q1 FY27 and growing double digits sequentially. That is why Innovation Dominance stops at PARTIAL: the cadence is there, but R&D spend is never disclosed or benchmarked.

The growth record and the risk record are the same set of numbers read twice. Constant-currency growth of 21.4% year-on-year with guidance raised to 14–16% in July 2022 became 2.5% and 3–4% guidance by July 2024, and 1.5–3% by July 2026 with peers said to be catching up. That single trajectory is what turns Market Share Gainers from an early, credible 'leading the industry' claim into a PARTIAL verdict, what makes Cyclicality a WATCH as discretionary spend follows client IT budgets, and what caps Brand Strength at LIKELY — the sub-brands and client-quoted trust at the Feb 2026 AI Day are genuine, but no premium price comes with them. Stakeholder Concentration is a WATCH because the sensitivity is demonstrated rather than theoretical: one client decision partly explains the July 2026 guidance cut, with the offset being 200+ large clients and large deals that are 50–61% net new. Industry Structure is the only quality area scoring below zero (−0.17), matching Toll Roads as a NO FIT — IT services is a heavily negotiated line item that clients actively try to shrink, with Nandan noting 60–80% of client IT spend goes to maintenance they want to cut — and matching New Entrants at ELEVATED, where client-owned Global Capability Centres (Vanguard, Daimler named) and aggressive peer pricing were handled somewhat defensively. Government Dependency and Fashion Risk are both LOW, and overall resilience is judged SOUND. What keeps the multibagger score at 48 is partly structural: at $20bn, triple-listed, with quarterly media conferences and saturation coverage, Security by obscurity scores −2 — there is no undiscovered story here, only a well-run, well-watched business whose returns are excellent and whose pricing is not.
