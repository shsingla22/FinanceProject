# Glaxosmithkline Pharmaceuticals Ltd. (GLAXO) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: PHARMA*

GlaxoSmithKline Pharmaceuticals Ltd. (GSK India) is the Indian arm of the global GSK drug maker, headquartered in Mumbai and listed on the BSE and NSE. It sells branded prescription medicines and vaccines in India — everyday primary-care drugs like antibiotics, skin treatments, painkillers and vitamins, plus preventive vaccines for children and adults, and newer 'specialty' drugs for asthma/COPD and cancer. It makes money by selling these products through pharmacies and doctors in the private, largely self-pay Indian market, with a field force of over 2,000 sales reps calling on doctors face-to-face plus digital outreach (roughly 4 million doctor touchpoints in a quarter); much of its manufacturing sits with contract manufacturers (a fire at one CMO disrupted supply through FY2026). It operates essentially only in India — where management says its products reach more than 270 million Indians a year — and is described as the second-largest contributor to global GSK's emerging markets; quarterly revenue first crossed INR 1,000 crores in Q3 FY2026, with full-year FY2026 revenue up 2%, EBITDA margin about 34% and PAT up 10%.

**The parts of the business:**

- **General Medicines (GenMeds)** — The established primary-care drug business — antibiotics (Augmentin, Ceftum), dermatology (T-Bact), pain/paracetamol (Calpol), vitamins and thyroid (Eltroxin) — still described as the largest single chunk of the top line, with leadership positions in anti-infectives, dermatology, pain and vitamins and the number one spot in self-pay dermatology.
- **Vaccines – Pediatric** — Children's vaccines sold outside the government immunisation programme (Boostrix, Varilrix, Havrix), growing 9-11% and holding the lead in the private vaccines market segments where GSK participates.
- **Vaccines – Adult** — Adult immunisation, led by the shingles vaccine Shingrix, a newer space the company has been building for roughly three years; it was called a key growth driver in FY2026 and is now being promoted using a link between shingles prevention and cardiovascular/metabolic disease.
- **Specialty – Respiratory** — Trelegy Ellipta (a single-inhaler triple therapy for COPD) and Nucala (severe asthma, adding 100-120 new patients a month), the first leg of a specialty business management says was 'almost non-existent' a few years ago.
- **Specialty – Oncology** — The newest addition: Zejula for ovarian cancer and Jemperli for endometrial cancer, launched with their first full quarter in Q3 FY2026; a December approval extended Jemperli to first-line endometrial cancer, widening the eligible patient pool from about 800 to roughly 6,000.
- **Innovative / new portfolio ('freshness index')** — Management's cross-cutting measure of newly launched innovative medicines and vaccines (Jemperli, Zejula, Shingrix, Trelegy, Nucala), which contributed about 6% of the top line at the end of FY2026 against a stated goal of at least 10%.
- **Distributed / tail-end brands** — A portfolio of smaller, non-promoted brands the company distributes; it saw soft demand and was called out as one of the two main drags on FY2026 growth.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Glaxosmithkline Pharmaceuticals scores 78 out of 100 — a Strong, four-star rating built from 73 on business quality, 89 on multibagger pattern fit and 74 on risk safety, with overall resilience judged sound. The engine behind all three numbers is the same one: a prescriber-led commercial model. About 2,000 field representatives, digital contact with roughly 500,000 doctors (4 million touch points in the December 2025 quarter alone) and medicines reaching over 270 million Indians a year give the company reach rivals struggle to match. That reach is why Convenience benefits and Distribution advantage both score full marks, and it is also the direct source of the strongest pattern in the screen, Friendly Middleman, where the doctor rather than the patient is the decision-maker being served. Sitting on top of it are heritage brands — Augmentin, Calpol, Ceftum, T-Bact, Betnovate, Eltroxin — that hold number one positions and, per the FY26 commentary, still grow three to four points faster than their markets, which is what earns Brand Strength a strong fit and Assurance benefits and Intangible benefits their top scores. The financial result is unusually clean: ₹65 of every ₹100 of sales kept after direct input costs, ₹39 of operating cash per ₹100 of capital employed, cash collected four days before suppliers must be paid, no debt, and no large acquisitions in five years.

The same trait that creates the strength creates the main vulnerability, and this is the central tension in the file. Because the prescriber, not the payer, chooses the medicine, the company can charge a brand premium on molecules that are largely off-patent — and that is precisely what the Good Enough Goods risk flags, since cheaper equivalents of Augmentin, Calpol, Ceftum, T-Bact and Eltroxin already exist and still supply the majority of revenue. The screen reads this arrangement as an advantage; the risk review reads the identical fact as an exposure. For now the evidence favours the optimistic reading: market share has been gained across the whole period with evolution indices above 100 on most brands, share was held through COVID and through Trelegy's generic entry, and prices and volumes have grown together (6% each in FY23) rather than price being traded for volume. That is why Good Enough Goods sits at watch rather than higher, and why Gain in market share scores full marks. The same duality shows up in the answer to that risk — the pipeline. Innovation Dominance is a strong fit because of a deliberate, stated shift into Nucala, Trelegy, Shingrix, Zejula and Jemperli, with Blenrep, Bepirovirsen, Efruxifermin and Velzatinib named next and progress tracked against an explicit freshness target. But the very platform being built to escape generic competition is the one exposed to it: eight to ten generic triple-combination versions of Trelegy appeared within ten months of the umeclidinium patent loss, which is why both Technological Innovation and New Entrants sit at watch. Pricing power is likewise real but bounded — NLEM cuts and DPCO ceilings shape what can be charged, which is why Pricing Power is only a likely rather than a strong fit and why Government Dependency, despite revenue being overwhelmingly private self-pay, remains on watch.

The one risk rated elevated is not competitive at all — it is operational and specific. A fire at a single contract manufacturer disrupted Calpol and Cobadex supply for four straight quarters, costing ₹25–30 crore a quarter and taking 3–3.5 percentage points off topline growth, and management had to withdraw its earlier assurance that the supply problem was behind it. That episode is where the three analyses converge most tightly: it is the Stakeholder Concentration risk, and the way it was handled is simultaneously the evidence for a top score on Candor and for Corporate Culture qualifying as a strong pattern fit — bad news quantified and owned rather than buried, with business-continuity planning now being put in place. The remaining risks are all rated low, and for structural reasons: demand rests on disease burden and consumption rather than customer capex or taste, so Cyclicality and Fashion Risk are muted, and the shift in consumer preference toward prevention and adult vaccination runs in the company's favour rather than against it — the same shift that underpins the top score on Structural end-market growth, where private paediatric vaccines reach only about 1 million of a 24–25 million birth cohort and adult immunisation is being built from scratch. The weakest link in the quality framework is Industry Structure, scoring zero, which is consistent with everything above: this is a strong company operating in a structurally difficult industry, where value migrates as molecules go off patent. Two patterns are explicitly rejected — Recurring Revenue, because management describes the business as overwhelmingly acute and one-off with no subscriptions or renewals, and Low Price Plus, because the positioning is premium innovator, with EBITDA margins having moved from 16% to 35.9%. Forward Integrators registers only as a numbers-only screening hint with no supporting evidence from the calls, and Toll Roads and Cost to Replicate are partial.

## What to watch

- Supply concentration (Stakeholder Concentration, elevated): whether the promised business-continuity planning actually removes single-site dependence, or another contract manufacturer outage repeats the ₹25–30 crore per quarter hit.
- Post-patent defence of the new platform (New Entrants / Technological Innovation): whether Trelegy's device differentiation keeps holding share against the eight to ten generic triple combinations, since a loss here undercuts the Innovation Dominance pattern.
- Pipeline delivery against the freshness target (Innovation Dominance, strong fit): whether Blenrep, Bepirovirsen, Efruxifermin and Velzatinib actually launch and scale — this pattern is the stated answer to the Good Enough Goods risk.
- Brand premium versus cheaper equivalents (Good Enough Goods, watch): whether evolution indices stay above 100 and promoted brands keep growing three to four points ahead of market, or the off-patent majority of revenue starts eroding on price.
- Policy pressure on pricing (Government Dependency, watch; Pricing Power, likely fit): whether NLEM cuts, DPCO ceilings, customs-duty decisions on cancer medicines and minimum import prices on amoxicillin inputs continue to be offset by WPI-linked increases and mix.
