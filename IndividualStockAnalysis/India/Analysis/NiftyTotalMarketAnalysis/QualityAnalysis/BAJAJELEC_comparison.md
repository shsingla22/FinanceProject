**Checks that improved:**

- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Weak → Good. Now: Management claims undisputed leadership in mixers, irons and water heaters, backed by an after-sales service network positioned as a core pillar. | The long view had said: No monopoly-like ownership of the customer's mind — the company competes head-on across fans, appliances, lighting and now wires, and is repeatedly benchmarked unfavourably against peers.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Weak → Good. Now: Management accepted a 25% consumer-products revenue decline and negative EBIT to force a structural shift from push-selling to sell-through, sustaining that stance across consecutive quarters. | The long view had said: Strategy continuity has repeatedly broken — the MD who ran the turnaround left mid-plan, the CP head left just before him, and the successor team is now unwinding the prior regime's sales practices and investment choices.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Neutral. Now: New product development is claimed to contribute ~40% of revenue, but no R&D intensity or output metrics are given and innovation investments are now being cut back under cost review. | The long view had said: R&D/innovation was a headline priority through FY23-25 with rising new-product contribution claims, yet by FY26 management concedes the innovations were launched at wrong price points and margins and are now being re-underwritten, indicating poor long-run R&D efficiency.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: Trustworthy after-sales service is named as one of four core fundamentals with continued reinvestment to keep it best-in-class. | The long view had said: Management asserts customers buy Bajaj for trust and quality assurance, including in outsourced wires, but the claim is unproven by results — share is only stable and price remains the operative lever.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: The mix is sensibly matched — retail intangibles via a dual-brand architecture with Morphy Richards, and B2B professional lighting sold on specification with high single-digit margins. | The long view had said: The mix straddles retail consumer products (where the intangible brand pull is weakening) and professional lighting (where reliability sells well and margins hit a record 8.5%), so the benefit-to-customer match is only half working.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: There is little halo risk since reported results are poor, but the claim that fundamentals are intact rests on assertion rather than demonstrated skill, so results and skill cannot yet be separated. | The long view had said: The 2022-24 'turnaround' halo rests on balance-sheet repair and a demerger, while the underlying consumer operation was simultaneously being loaded with channel inventory that has now unwound into losses — results and skill separate poorly.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Out of limelight** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Calls are sober and operationally focused with no personality-driven promotion, but the Bajaj name and the 100-year group celebration keep the company well inside the limelight. | The long view had said: Management deliberately de-personalises the franchise and communicates in plain operational terms, with the Chairman insisting the company runs on strategy rather than any individual.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: Rather than taking share from weak rivals, the company was the one ceding ground this year while peers posted better margins and it corrected its own overstuffed channel. | The long view had said: Weak incumbents do exist in wires where larger peers are ceding share, but over the period Bajaj has itself been the donator in fans and coolers, so the balance is neutral.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 0 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: The four FY26 calls describe after-sales service as a brand fundamental to reinvest behind, but never quantify service/AMC revenue, attach rates, renewals or customer advances as a funding source — there is no evidence either way on a recurring revenue stream. (The long view had said: Across all four excerpted calls (Aug 2022 through May 2026) there is no discussion of AMC/service contracts, attach rates, renewals or deferred revenue; the negative working capital mentioned in Q4FY26 is trade-credit driven, not customer prepayment for a recurring stream.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management claims new product development contributes ~40% of revenues and lists category forays each quarter, but no R&D spend, pipeline detail or industry R&D share is disclosed, and Q3FY26 says innovation investment is now being screened more stringently as capex is cut. (The long view had said: R&D/new-product investment was promised repeatedly (Anuj Poddar in Jul 2024: new-product contribution to 'cross the majority mark'), but by May 2026 management effectively conceded the spend did not convert, saying future innovation must be re-planned for 'the right price point with the right margins'.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Lighting Solutions is gaining share in focus categories (ceiling and outdoor lights) with 9–16% growth, but Consumer Products fell 4% in Q2 and 25% in Q3FY26 with shares merely 'stabilizing' — there is no persistent, quantified share gain across the business. (The long view had said: The Aug 2022 claim of consistent share gains in fans and consumer lighting did not persist — by FY26 the language is 'market shares have remained stable' and 'stabilization of market shares', with fans explicitly flagged as needing work and peers reporting better top lines.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: All four FY26 calls show sales and margins swinging violently with weather-driven seasonality — 45% cooler degrowth and double-digit fan decline in Q1, CP revenue -25% in Q3, and a Q4 CP loss, all attributed to operating deleverage on a seasonal cost base. This is weather/inventory cyclicality in a consumer flow product rather than a commodity or customer-capex cycle, and the non-seasonal book kept growing throughout. (The long view had said: Demand is consumption/flow-linked (fans, geysers, mixers, lighting) rather than tied to any customer's capex, but the calls show repeated, large swings driven by the commodity cycle and by weather-dependent summer products — management tracked an 18-month input-cost cycle in FY23, and by FY26 coolers fell 38-40% and CP EBIT went from 6-7% to negative on operating deleverage, with 3-10% price hikes chasing war-related inflation.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The products are simple, commoditisable appliances sold through a trade channel that controls shelf choice, and management is visibly spending to defend the premium — brand investment raised to 4% of sales from 3%, heavy promotions and trade schemes used to flush channel stock (which itself dented Q3 margins), and price hikes in fans announced into an inflationary quarter. The Morphy Richards dual-brand move is an explicit answer to price-tiered competition. (The long view had said: Consumer Products economics have deteriorated steadily — EBIT of 6-7% falling to 3-3.5% in FY24/25 and negative in FY26 — while management repeatedly concedes it cannot fully pass through input costs and must stay price-competitive; growth had been bought with trade schemes and 'high-cost incremental sales practices' now being flushed out, and when asked what would make a dealer choose Bajaj wires over Polycab or Havells the only answer offered was brand trust on an outsourced product.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Across all four calls no single customer, licensor or supplier is named as driving results; revenue flows through a broad distributor/general-trade base, and the Q3-Q4 channel normalisation was about aggregate inventory health, not dependence on one relationship. Brand ownership is in-house (plus the Morphy Richards brand acquisition), so there is no licence-renewal risk of the Safilo type. (The long view had said: Distribution is broad (deep dealer reach, no named customer driving results), but the Morphy Richards franchise is a licensed brand whose economics depend on a renewable licensor relationship, and the new wires and switchgear lines are being launched on outsourced manufacturing with the sourcing model still undecided.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
