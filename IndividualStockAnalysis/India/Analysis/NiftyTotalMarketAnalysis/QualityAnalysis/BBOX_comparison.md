### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 4 weakened, 3 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: The Jun 2026 CMD describes managed services and professional services bundled around 5,000 active sites supported from 75 delivery centres, and the Wind River tie-up is framed as ~$30m annually for 5 years — but across all four calls management discloses only order backlog and bookings, never attach rates, renewal rates, AMC share of revenue or customer advances as a funding source. (The long view had said: Managed services, warranty/tech support and AMC-type wins appear in deal disclosures (Q1 FY25: managed/on-demand deals >$13mn) and management talks of converting lumpy projects into annuity, but across all 16 calls there are no attach rates, renewal rates or deferred-revenue-as-funding disclosures — and working capital is a cash drain, with debt drawn to fund it (Q1 FY23).)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Management leans on 50 years of heritage, 120+ Fortune 500 customers and decades-long mission-critical relationships, but there is no A&P spend, no premium pricing evidence and no brand-led adjacency — the pull is B2B trust and installed presence rather than brand economics. (The long view had said: The Black Box name carries 50 years of heritage, 120+ Fortune 500 customers and decades-long mission-critical relationships that management calls its most durable advantage — but there is no A&P spend, no premium pricing evidence, and the product arm is explicitly white-label branded, so brand shows as trust/credentialing rather than pricing or share power.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The articulated barrier is embedded, decades-old, mission-critical positions inside hyperscalers, top-5 US banks and healthcare systems across 75 delivery centres and 5,000 sites — real switching friction, but it is a people-and-certification services business a well-funded systems integrator could rebuild, and management itself calls relationships (not assets or IP) the durable advantage. (The long view had said: The advantages cited — global delivery footprint, partner certifications, customer relationships — are exactly what Black Box itself bought rather than built (Black Box Corp in 2019, 2S in Brazil in 2026), and a well-funded integrator can acquire the same; the ~9% EBITDA margin and dependence on third-party fiber, racks and OEM technology confirm no un-replicable barrier.)

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: LIKELY FIT → NO FIT. Why now: No price increase is discussed in any of the four calls; margins are flat in a narrow 8.4-9.0% band, cost inflation from tariffs and fibre/GPU shortages was absorbed as delay rather than passed on, and FY26 revenue guidance was cut from INR6,750-7,000 crore to INR6,325-6,375 crore. (The long view had said: Management repeatedly reprices contracts for labour/input inflation and lifted prices on low-value accounts, and cites 'pricing discipline' in Q3 FY26 — but the volume cost was real (revenue fell 9% YoY in Q1 FY25 from those exits) and gross margin compressed to ~27.7% under inflation in FY23, so this is cost pass-through rather than durable pricing power.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: LIKELY FIT → NO FIT. Why now: No R&D spend, pipeline or named own-product launches are disclosed in any call; gross-margin-funded innovation is absent and management explicitly positions Black Box as an orchestrator of other vendors' technology (Cisco, Wind River) rather than the innovator. (The long view had said: Two R&D centres (Ireland, Bangalore) support market-leading KVM/AV products and INR410cr of the FY25 raise was earmarked partly for innovation in cloud/cyber/AI/IoT, but R&D spend is never quantified or tracked across calls, and the core 80% GSI business integrates other vendors' technology under 'best-of-breed' partnerships rather than proprietary innovation.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Backlog rose from $504m (FY25 exit) to $518m, $555m and then a guided ~$800m by Mar 2026 (+60% YoY) with $626m booked in 9M FY26, plus the 2S Brazil acquisition to scale a sub-scale market — but no explicit market-share figures or comparisons versus named competitors are given, and reported revenue growth was only 5% in 9M FY26. (The long view had said: Backlog compounding from ~$475mn (Jun 2024) to >$800mn (Mar 2026, +60% YoY) on ~$1bn FY26 bookings, four of the top six hyperscalers and four of the top five US banks, plus Brazil entry via 2S, evidence real wallet-share capture — but no explicit market-share numbers versus named competitors, and headline revenue actually shrank in FY25 while guidance was cut in FY26.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Revenue comes from direct large-enterprise pursuits — hyperscalers, US banks, county governments, universities — won by an in-house go-to-market team management repeatedly says it has been hiring, not through prescribers or independent installers who steer a paying end customer. (The long view had said: The 20%-of-revenue Technology Product Solutions arm goes to market entirely through two-tier distribution, VARs and integration partners, with Black Box funding technical enablement and quality support for those partners; but the GSI business (80%) sells direct to enterprises, and no professional-training/certification lock-in of the channel is described.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: Across all four calls technology change is a demand driver rather than a threat: AI adoption is described as requiring 'refreshed new deployment and retrofit of technology infrastructure,' and the company positions itself as a vendor-neutral 'orchestrator' layering partnerships (Wind River, Cisco via the 2S acquisition) rather than defending a proprietary platform or installed format. (The long view had said: As an integrator, Black Box rides platform shifts rather than owning a format — AI, GPU data centres and cloud are presented as tailwinds and it partners with best-of-breed OEMs — but its economics depend on continuously re-skilling into whatever stack customers adopt, and its own TPS product lines (KVM, signal switching, visualization) are exposed to format change, which is why R&D centres in Ireland/Bangalore and cloud-platform products are repeatedly cited.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Results are repeatedly narrated through a handful of named relationships — 'our largest global financial services customer,' hyperscaler extensions, 'four of the top six hyperscalers' — and management is deliberately concentrating further by cutting long-tail accounts to under 1,000 while chasing large deals (two-thirds of Q1 bookings were high-value). (The long view had said: Top 10 customers were ~40% of revenue in FY23 and management has deliberately narrowed from 8,000 accounts to ~300 strategic ones, with named marquee names (Meta, Amazon, Microsoft, four of the top six hyperscalers) repeatedly cited as growth drivers; the transformation partner explicitly notes the data-centre market is concentrated in a handful of hyperscalers. Supplier concentration is also live — fibre, cable, GPU and rack shortages from third-party vendors directly burned $40-50m of FY26 backlog — and the TPS business routes through a few distributors (Ingram, TD Synnex, ScanSource).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (QUANT FLAG); Shifting Consumer Preferences (QUANT FLAG); Fashion Risk (QUANT FLAG).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 10 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
