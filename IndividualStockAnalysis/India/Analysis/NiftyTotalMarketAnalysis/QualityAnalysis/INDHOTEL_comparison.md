### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Long gestation and regulatory hurdles (Taj Bandstand needing full approvals before excavation) plus supply lagging demand support rational pricing, evidenced by RevPAR-led growth rather than discounting. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±15.4 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Structural supply barriers persist (supply growth 2% vs demand 7%, limited incremental supply in key markets), pricing is rational with no discount war evident, and Tata ownership is stable.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹33 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹33 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±15.4 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: Sustained, clearly growth-oriented capex (greenfield Ekta Nagar hotels, Taj Bandstand excavation, Taj Ganges 100-key wing, Frankfurt) with explicit high incremental returns and short payback, funded entirely from internal accruals while ROCE rose 160bps to 17.3%. | The long view had said: Capex has stepped up to INR1,000–1,200 crore annually with roughly half going to greenfield/expansion and demonstrated high incremental returns (Taj Ganges' new 100-room wing PBT-positive in quarter one at 40% EBITDA margin), though a large share remains renovation/maintenance.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: The result is partly attributable to identifiable management actions — the capital-light structural shift, asset management renovations, mid-scale platform build — rather than only a favourable cycle, but a strong post-COVID Indian hospitality up-cycle is clearly flattering the numbers. | The long view had said: A meaningful part of the record run is cycle-driven (supply constraint, post-COVID domestic demand, G20/World Cup years), so results should not be read purely as managerial skill, though the capital-light fee model is genuine skill-based evidence.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Sub-scale independent and regional chains are literally donating share by folding into IHCL's platform — Clarks, ANK/Pride, Brij and Atmantan all migrating onto IHCL brands and distribution. | The long view had said: A large unbranded/independent and weak-operator base is being converted into IHCL's system via licensing and bolt-ons (Claridges, Tree of Life, Brij), giving a visible source of donated share.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. Now: Four small bolt-ons (ANK/Pride, Brij, Atmantan) totalling just over INR500 crores are complementary to mid-scale and wellness and are already growing (Brij +42%, Atmantan +19%), though they are recent and post-deal OPM improvement is not yet proven at scale. | The long view had said: Deals have been small, complementary bolt-ons (INR18 crore for 55% of Tree of Life; ~INR500 crore across four acquisitions) into adjacencies that are already growing and margin-accretive, with Brij up 42% and Atmantan up 19% post-deal and INR250 crore incremental revenue guided.
- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Headwinds, renovation drags, the Frankfurt cost impact and weak Dubai/Sri Lanka fee markets are named specifically with quantified effects, though the framing stays consistently upbeat and no outright mistake is owned. | The long view had said: Management separates exceptional gains from underlying PAT, quantifies adverse impacts (INR40–50 crore West Asia revenue loss, INR15 crore Frankfurt ramp-up), and openly discusses setbacks like the New York pipe burst and a sluggish March/April.

*Unchanged: Geographical expansion; People matter (talent development); Distribution advantage; Return on capital (headline); Customer types (retail vs corporate); Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Cyclical market growth; Structural end-market growth; Network effects; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Asset turn; Advertisement and promotion; Mini-monopolies; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Convenience benefits; Assurance benefits; Security by obscurity; Cost of working capital; Disciplined stewards; Out of limelight; Intangible benefits; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Gain in market share.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 6. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 8 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Portfolio went from 268 operating hotels (Nov 2025) to 382 operational with ~265 in pipeline (Jul 2026), ~24% share of branded mid-scale inventory, 46 signings in H1 FY26 and 20 in Q1 FY27 — and the share gain is accompanied by expanding margins and rising ROCE, not bought with profitability. (The long view had said: Share gain is explicit and persistent: portfolio from ~250 hotels in FY23 to 645 by Q1 FY27 with market share of operational and active supply expanding, a 66% RevPAR premium sustained, TajSATS at 59% (later 50%+) share of in-flight meals, and international expansion (London, New York, Frankfurt, Cape Town, Dubai) — gained on price, not discounting.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is regular new-format and new-brand creation — Qmin at 100+ outlets, amã at 380+ bungalows, Tree of Life at 40+ resorts, the 'Qminization' of Ginger, plus digital and loyalty investment — and the new-business vertical compounded at 31% CAGR over four years, but there is no R&D spend disclosure or industry R&D-share framing. (The long view had said: There is a regular, revenue-bearing cadence of new formats — Qmin from 25 to 100+ outlets, ama to 380+ bungalows, Gateway relaunched targeting 100 hotels by 2030, Loya restaurants, Chambers expansion — with new businesses compounding 31% CAGR over four years, but this is concept/format innovation with no disclosed R&D spend or industry R&D share.)

**That weakened:**

- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Demand is driven directly by guests, corporates, MICE and wedding bookings rather than by an intermediary expert who prescribes the product; the calls describe direct revenue management, loyalty and brand pull. Hotel owners are capital partners, not price-insensitive prescribers for end customers. (The long view had said: the calls are silent and the numbers are mixed (1 of 2 checks supporting).)

*Unchanged: Pricing Power (STRONG FIT); Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Cost to Replicate (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 6 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Hotel demand is inherently cycle-linked and management frames its outlook explicitly on the demand-supply cycle, with results at what look like cycle-high levels (17 consecutive record quarters, standalone EBITDA margin 49.5% in Q4 FY26, RevPAR +14% in Q1 FY27) while committing to INR 1,000-1,200 crores of annual capex; macro sensitivity is already visible in Q1 FY27, where West Asia geopolitics, fuel prices and reduced airline capacity moderated international/long-haul demand and hit TajSATS. Exposure is bounded rather than high because 68% of the operating portfolio and 93% of the pipeline are capital-light, so growth capital is largely owners' rather than IHCL's, and revenue is spread across luxury, mid-scale, business, leisure and 22% international. (The long view had said: Management itself frames the entire 17-quarter run in cycle terms ('up cycle', demand 7% vs supply 2%), and margins have expanded from ~24% pre-COVID to 35% with occupancies at 83-90% and growth now admittedly rate-driven — classic cycle-peak ambiguity; demand shocks (West Asia conflict costing ~INR100 crores enterprise revenue, Dubai/Maldives down, Pahalgam, weather) show sensitivity. Exposure is bounded because revenue is consumer/operations flow (room nights, F&B, weddings), not customer capex, and 68% of the operating portfolio / 93% of pipeline is capital-light with fee annuity income growing 26%.)
- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Pricing is set by the market through revenue management, not by any regulator, tariff or subsidy, and the demand base is corporate, leisure, MICE and weddings; government touchpoints are incidental — construction approvals at Taj Bandstand, an airport project at MOPA, and diplomatic/global events cited as one of several H2 demand drivers. (The long view had said: Government-driven events materially move the numbers — G20 was a large FY24 base effect (Delhi RevPAR grew only 2% in Q2 FY25 against it), government MICE slowed in FY27, and the Prime Minister's appeal to travel domestically is cited as a demand driver, alongside government infrastructure/airport buildout and projects like Ekta Nagar. But no pricing, tariff or subsidy is set by regulators and the revenue base is overwhelmingly private demand.)

*Unchanged: Technological Innovation (LOW); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); New Entrants (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW); Good Enough Goods (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
