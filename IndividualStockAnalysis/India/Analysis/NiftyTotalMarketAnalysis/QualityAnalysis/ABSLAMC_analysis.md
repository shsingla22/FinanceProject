# Aditya AMC (ABSLAMC) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Aditya Birla Sun Life AMC (ABSLAMC) is an Indian asset management company — it runs mutual funds and other investment schemes, pooling money from savers and investing it in shares and bonds on their behalf, in exchange for a fee charged on the assets it manages. Its customers are ordinary retail investors (reached through a network of distributors, banks, digital platforms and its own sales teams across 19,000+ pin codes in India), plus corporates, institutions, and NRI/foreign investors served out of GIFT City. Because it earns off the pool it manages, the numbers management reports each quarter are assets under management, monthly SIP (systematic investment plan) collections, and investor folios: mutual fund quarterly average AUM of about ₹4.28 lakh crore as of June 2026, total AUM including alternates of ₹6.28 lakh crore, and closing AUM crossing ₹10 lakh crore after winning the EPFO mandate. It operates almost entirely in India, is listed on the BSE and NSE, and is part of Aditya Birla Capital, having completed 30 years as an AMC in FY25.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Mutual fund business — equity schemes** — Its main earner: stock-market funds such as Flexi Cap, Balanced Advantage, Multi Asset Allocation, Mid Cap, Small Cap, Multi-Cap, GenNext and Quant, at roughly ₹1.99 lakh crore of quarterly average AUM in June 2026 (about 46.5% of the mutual fund book, up from ₹1.17 lakh crore and 41% in June 2022) — management treats equity as the highest-value part of the mix and ties its growth to fund performance and distributor confidence.
- **Mutual fund business — fixed income / debt schemes** — Bond and money-market funds that make up most of the remaining mutual fund AUM; the calls describe this pool as the more volatile, lower-margin piece, prone to institutional withdrawals when interest rates move (as in FY23, when outflows here pushed the equity share of the mix up).
- **SIP book / retail franchise** — The recurring monthly-investment engine that management calls the cornerstone of the business — about ₹1,085 crore collected in June 2026 from roughly 40 lakh SIP accounts (up from ₹898 crore in June 2022), against total investor folios of 1.11 crore, and pushed through campaigns like 'Har Ghar SIP' and 'Sabse Important Plan' plus distributor-activation programmes such as Sampark and the virtual relationship manager model.
- **Passive business (index funds and ETFs)** — Low-cost funds that simply track an index, described as growing progressively through new product launches and scaling existing ones; smaller than the active book but repeatedly named as a growth area, and index funds were among the schemes seeing improving inflows in FY27.
- **Alternate business — PMS and AIF** — Tailored portfolios for wealthier and institutional clients (portfolio management services and Category II/III Alternative Investment Funds, including credit offerings and the ABSL India Special Opportunities Fund); a key focus area that grew roughly three-fold from ₹11,300 crore in Q4 FY25 to ₹32,570 crore in Q4 FY26, helped by the ESIC mandate.
- **Offshore / GIFT City business** — Funds set up in GIFT City to bring overseas and NRI money into India and to give Indian investors global exposure — including the ABSL Global Emerging Market Fund, Global Blue-Chip Fund, an India ESG Engagement Fund, index-linked funds for inward remittance and an ABSL India Opportunities Fund; still small (the Global Emerging Market Fund was around ₹400 crore while raising) but now run by a dedicated Head of Offshore and PMS.
- **Large institutional / retirement mandates** — Money managed for government-linked retirement bodies, won recently and transformative in size: the ESIC mandate landed in FY26 and an EPFO mandate of roughly ₹6.08 lakh crore was added mid-quarter in Q1 FY27, which is what lifted closing total AUM past ₹10 lakh crore — huge in assets, though management presents it separately from the mutual fund book.
- **Digital and technology platforms** — Not a revenue line of its own but a delivery channel management keeps highlighting — new investor and distributor apps, WhatsApp-based servicing and a Gen AI chatbot that guides investors without human involvement, all aimed at adding customers, selling more products per customer and improving service.

### The story in depth

Aditya AMC is a fee-collecting business sitting in front of a market that is genuinely getting bigger. Industry assets under management more than doubled from ₹37.7 lakh crore in June 2022 to ₹83.14 lakh crore in June 2026, and investor accounts went from 13.6 crore to 29.1 crore — the one check in the whole framework that scores a full +2. The company is well run against that backdrop: it built purely organically with no acquisitions and no borrowings, keeps operating costs guided to 10-12% growth, turns every ₹100 of capital into ₹20 of operating cash, ties up only about ten days of cash between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and pays out roughly 75% of profits as dividend (₹25.5 per share in FY26). It has kept spending on growth rather than upkeep — branches reaching 19,000+ pin codes, a wholly-owned GIFT City subsidiary with a retail licence, new investor and partner apps. Two business patterns fit: it is a textbook Friendly Middleman (distributors and bank channels choose the funds, the investor pays) and a likely Recurring Revenue business, earning an annuity on assets with a sticky systematic-investment book of about ₹76,000 crore across 40 lakh accounts.

The problem is that the very features that make those patterns fit are also the source of the biggest risks. Because distributors pick the fund, about 65% of the total expense charged is paid away to them — and the same intermediary who controls the choice can just as easily point money at a cheap index or ETF product costing roughly 0.08% versus the company's 0.63-0.64% on equity money, which is why 'Good Enough Goods' is flagged as the sharpest risk. Because revenue is an annuity on asset values, it rises and falls with the market: the systematic-investment book fell from ₹87,000 crore to ₹76,000 crore purely on market movement, and quarterly profit dropped to ₹187 crore from ₹228 crore on investment revaluation alone. Because the fee is the customer's whole cost of the product rather than a small input, the regulator and the distributor both push it down — an approximately 0.05-percentage-point mandated fee cut from April 2026 plus telescoping pricing that automatically shrinks the fee as assets grow. That is why Toll Roads and Pricing Power come out as clear non-fits and why the pricing check scores negative: the direction of travel is structural price deflation, not price-setting power. The same government mandates (ESIC ₹28,400 crore, EPFO around ₹6.08 lakh crore) that make the business genuinely hard to replicate are also ultra-low-fee, drove nearly all headline asset growth, and leave a five-year renewal sitting with a single counterparty — one trait producing a strength, a weak revenue mix, and a concentration risk at the same time.

Put together, the picture is a capable, honest management team running a financially clean business inside an industry whose economics are moving against it. Capital allocation (0.75) and management (0.67) are the strong scores; competitive advantage sits at zero and industry structure at -0.83, with profit margins swinging by about 11 percentage points and licensing described as the only real barrier while pricing discipline in the industry deteriorates. Market share is the most consistent negative thread in the record: four straight years of growing slower than the industry (14% then 6% against the industry's 21% and 15%), with management now aiming only to slow the decline rather than gain ground — so 'Market Share Gainers' is a clear non-fit. Brand, innovation, cost discipline, forward integration and culture all score as partial fits: real evidence exists in each (30-year heritage, named campaigns, the Quant Fund launch that gathered ₹2,400 crore, a passive range grown from 28 to 52-54 schemes, ETF assets up 47-68% year on year, a CEO who openly admits share loss), but none has converted into the outcome the pattern requires. The passive and ETF build is simultaneously the defence against the technology-shift and new-entrant risks and an admission that the higher-fee active business is being substituted. Hence the combined score of 45 out of 100 — a Mixed, two-star verdict — with underlying resilience judged sound: the balance sheet and cash generation are not in question, the earning power of each rupee of assets is.

The bull case needs one of two things to change: either the share-loss trend reverses so the growing market actually flows through to this company, or the higher-fee, less-regulated pools (portfolio management, alternative funds, offshore/GIFT City, the long-short platform, private credit) grow from their current roughly 6% contribution into something that offsets fee compression. Until then, an expanding industry and a shrinking share of it largely cancel out.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Friendly Middleman — STRONG FIT
*"The expert sells it for you"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence and the numbers agree (1 of 2 computable checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Across all four years flows are intermediated: MFD and banking-channel distributors choose the funds while the investor pays, ~65% of TER is paid away to distributors, and the company invests continuously in the channel (Sampark distributor onboarding, ~1,900 distributors added FY23, virtual RM model, partner app, CEO 'Yatra' across cities, 19,000+ pin codes). Getting onto bank 'recommendation lists' is repeatedly cited as the gating factor for flows.

> "most of these funds now also feature on the recommendation list of our banking channel partners, which will further support flows" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Operating margin of 64% but swinging ±11.0 points a year — too volatile for a protected franchise.
- ✅ Operating margin moved from about 42% (early years) to 64% (recent years) — the widening-profitability signature of pricing power / lock-in.

### Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) — LIKELY FIT
*"Sells once, earns forever"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and every one of the 3 computable checks supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is an annuity on AUM with a large sticky SIP installed base (SIP book ~Rs.76,000 cr, 40 lakh SIP accounts, 1.11 crore folios by Jun-2026, up from Rs.46,000 cr SIP AUM in Jun-2022), and management explicitly frames SIPs as customer stickiness; but there is no upfront product sale, no attach/renewal-rate or deferred-revenue/customer-advance discussion, and the 'recurring' fee is market-linked (SIP AUM fell from Rs.87,000 cr to Rs.76,000 cr purely on MTM).

> "Building SIP book size is our constant endeavor to ensure the customer stickiness" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Only about 10 days of cash is tied up in the trade cycle.
- ✅ Capital spending is about 2% of sales — growth needs little capital.
- ✅ Operating cash was positive in 9 of 10 years — the reliable cash engine recurring-revenue businesses show.

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Pricing and product launches are set by the regulator throughout the record — the FY23 NFO embargo, budget capital-gains tinkering, and from April 2026 a mandated ~5bps TER cut on equity AUM plus telescoping pricing that automatically compresses yields as AUM grows. ### Cyclicality — ELEVATED

## What to watch
