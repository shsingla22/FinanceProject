# Finolex Cables Ltd. (FINCABLES) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: INDUSTRIAL MANUFACTURING*

Finolex Cables Limited is an India-based manufacturer of electrical wires and cables, run from Pune and listed in Mumbai (BSE/NSE) and Luxembourg. Its bread-and-butter is copper electrical wire and cable sold mainly to Indian builders, homeowners, factories, farms and vehicle makers, alongside a second business making communication cables, including optical fibre cable and the fibre and preform that go into it, sold to telecom operators like Reliance and Bharti and to government broadband projects such as BSNL's BharatNet. It sells almost entirely through a distribution network — roughly 730 distributors and about 4,500 channel partners (around 2,000 buying regularly) — rather than directly to projects, so profits depend on volume plus the gap between copper/commodity input costs and the selling prices it resets every few weeks (14 price changes, about 24%-25% cumulative, in FY26 alone). Sales skew to South India (~45% of revenue) and West India (~30%), with North and East making up the remaining ~25%, and quarterly revenue has grown from about INR 1,222 crores in Q3 FY24 to roughly INR 1,600 crores in Q3 FY26.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Electrical cables (electrical sector)** — The dominant segment and main profit engine — a range of copper wires and cables that hit a record INR 1,697 crores of revenue in Q4 FY26, up about 22% for the full year, historically earning roughly 13.5%-14% margins though product mix has pushed this around.
- **Building wire / construction wire** — House and building wiring, described as still the majority of electrical sales; growth was only marginal in FY26 because repeated copper-driven price hikes met customer resistance.
- **E-beam cured premium wire and solar cables** — New products off an electron-beam plant commissioned January 2025 — a premium halogen-free, flame-resistant building wire rated for 25+ years of life, plus solar cables; management targets INR 500-600 crores of annual revenue from the two, and by FY26 solar was running at 80%-85% of capacity with more capacity planned.
- **Auto cables** — Wiring sold into the automotive industry; one of the fastest-growing lines, up about 42% in volume in Q3 FY26 and about 30% in Q4 FY26, with plant utilisation in the late 70s percent.
- **Industrial cables / flexibles** — Cables for industrial use, a many-SKU business that grew about 28% in volume in Q3 FY26 and about 17% in Q4 FY26.
- **Power cables** — A deliberately small presence — management sizes India's power cable market at INR 23,000-25,000 crores a year against Finolex's exposure of only about INR 150-180 crores — though volumes still grew 21%-22% in FY26.
- **Agricultural applications** — Wires and cables for farm use, the one weak spot in FY26: volumes fell 15%-16% on an unfavourable monsoon, off-season timing and pricing problems.
- **Communication cables** — The second-largest segment, roughly two-thirds optical-fibre-based products and the rest metal-based; revenue is lumpy because it depends on BharatNet tenders and on capex by Reliance and Bharti, and was flat for FY26 overall before a ~30% jump in Q4 as global fibre prices rose from under $3 to about $5.
- **Optical fibre and preform manufacturing (backward integration)** — In-house fibre drawing and a new preform plant commissioned in March 2026, part of a capex programme lifting fibre draw capacity from 4 million to 8 million kilometres by around Q1 FY27, aimed at capturing demand management believes could reach 50 million km a year in India.
- **Extra high voltage (EHV) JV with Sumitomo** — A joint venture in very-high-voltage cables (160kV+) serving a market management sizes at $400-500 million; it turned profitable in FY26 with about INR 21 crores of profit on roughly INR 450 crores of revenue and opened the next year with an INR 380 crores order book.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Finolex Cables comes out of these three reviews as a well-run company in genuinely growing markets that has not yet turned either of those advantages into money for shareholders. The overall score is 44 out of 100 — a Mixed, two-star verdict built from 55 on business quality, 25 on multibagger fit and 46 on risk safety — and the split tells the story. The management checks are the strongest part of the file: candour scores full marks for a team that admits peers outgrew them, admits losing both BharatNet consortium bids, admits channel inventory was inflated and puts a number on the margin dip instead of talking around it; the calls are run by the CFO alone with no promoter showmanship and an explicit refusal to make flattering forecasts. End markets score full marks too — data centre and AI fibre demand, BharatNet and state fibre programmes, solar cables, and an extra-high-voltage market management sees going from roughly $0.5-0.75bn to $4-5bn in three to four years. Against that, the return on capital check is the worst in the framework at minus two: every ₹100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings in the business produced ₹1 of operating cash this year, a 1% cash return. Good people, good markets, poor returns so far.

The reason the returns lag is the same reason the growth story exists, and this is the central knot in the file. The ₹500 crore capex programme — preform plant, e-beam line, fibre draw capacity going from 4 to 8 million km, solar and auto lines — earns credit under both the capex-for-growth check and the disciplined-stewards check, because it was funded from operating cash, taken on without leverage or acquisitions, and committed counter-cyclically while fibre prices sat at unviable lows near $2.5 per km. It is also what is holding the cash return down: optical fibre cable operating profit fell to about 2.5%, the preform project slipped, and projects have been repeatedly delayed. The fibre cycle has since turned hard to around $5 per km on a global shortage, so the new capacity is landing into an upturn — but the bottom is now behind the company and fixed-price annual contracts delay how quickly better prices show up in results. The same backward integration is what earns a partial mark on the innovation pattern, alongside real delivered products: e-beam cured halogen-free building wire with a 25-year life, and solar cables running at 80-85% of capacity within a year of launch. Nothing here is fake; it simply has not paid yet.

The pattern screen is where the weakness shows most plainly: not one of the eleven patterns is a clear fit — five are partial, six are no fit — and the reasons line up almost one-for-one with the elevated risks. Every price move described across nine calls is copper being passed through, twelve revisions in FY25 and fourteen increases worth 24-25% in FY26, with volume explicitly lost as a result and building wire showing only very marginal volume gains; that single fact sinks the pricing-power pattern, drags the pricing-mix-and-volume check to minus one, and drives the elevated cyclicality risk. It also breaks the toll-road pattern, because retail demand fell when prices rose rather than absorbing the increase. The trade channel — 730 distributors and roughly 4,500 partners, of which about 2,000 are active — is the silver lining that keeps government dependency and customer concentration from being worse, since the large electrical-wire business is spread across it; but the same channel is why the friendly-middleman pattern is only partial (no electrician or contractor training is mentioned), why forward integration is a clear no fit (no stores, franchising or direct-to-customer selling), and why the good-enough-goods risk is elevated, since the dealer or electrician controls the end choice and discounted project sales have added 8-10 percentage points to the mix over three years. Management's own honesty is what rules out the market-share-gainer pattern: they conceded lagging peers in FY25 and hold only 11-12% of the optical fibre cable market behind Sterlite, Birla and HFCL. Government dependency is the one high risk — no government business at all for nine months of FY24, tender delays pushing utilisation to 50-55% in FY25, and both consortium bids lost — and it sits directly underneath the same BharatNet and state fibre programmes counted as a structural growth strength. Meanwhile Birla is due into wire production in CY2027 in direct competition and Adani has announced entry, with management's answer being coexistence rather than any barrier, which is why both the new-entrant risk is elevated and the cost-to-replicate pattern fails. Resilience is still judged sound, so the question is not survival — it is whether the money already spent starts earning.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** The communication-cable segment is repeatedly hostage to BharatNet tendering and BSNL orders — 'absolutely no government business during the 9 months' in FY24, tender delays pushing utilisation to 50-55% in FY25, and the consortium failing to win any of the two circles it bid for; management explicitly names government funding as the structural flaw of the industry. Duty changes on Chinese fiber, state-level DPRs, export MROs on defence-linked raw material and the new labour code (INR 6 crore gratuity provision) add further policy sensitivity.

> "bulk of the funding comes from government programs and if that funding is not consistent or sustained over a period of time, then you do have blips" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Communication cables are the minority segment; the far larger electrical-wire business runs through a retail distribution channel, and fiber demand is diversifying toward US/Europe data centres and defence.*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across all nine calls results swing with copper/LME prices and with customer capex cycles — 12 price revisions in FY25 and 14 in FY26, margins dipping in FY25 then recovering, communication-cable volumes falling ~20% in FY24 when telecom operators paused buying and OFC utilisation dropping to 50-55%, then a 35% quarter-on-quarter swing in Q3 FY26 with fiber moving from $2.5 to $5/km. Revenue is tied to Reliance/Bharti/BSNL network capex and to real-estate construction rather than to steady consumption, and management itself refuses to forecast ('very difficult to predict where it will reach and how long it will stay there').

> "There has been a lot of volatility on the commodity side. And that, together with a few changes in the mix has resulted in a lower contribution, lower margin during the year." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Building wire, auto and agri cables are replacement/operations-linked consumables sold through ~4,500 channel partners, and the company kept investing counter-cyclically (preform, e-beam, fiber draw) through the FY25 downturn.*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 2 of the last 10 years with swings of ±12 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 10% and 16% (±2.3 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.

## What to watch

- Return on capital check (-2): whether the 1% cash return on capital rises as the ₹500cr programme fills up — specifically optical fibre cable profitability recovering from ~2.5% and the delayed preform plant reaching its intended run rate.
- Government Dependency (HIGH RISK): the next BharatNet and BSNL tender round after losing both consortium bids, and whether communication-cable utilisation moves up from the 50-55% seen in FY25.
- Pricing Power pattern (NO FIT) and the Pricing, Mix and Volume check (-1): whether the 14 price increases worth 24-25% hold with volume attached, or retail demand keeps falling when prices rise as it did in the Jun 2026 call.
- New Entrants (ELEVATED) and Cost to Replicate (NO FIT): Birla's plant entering production in CY2027 and Adani's timing — the first real evidence of price or share pressure, against management's stated expectation of coexistence.
- Good Enough Goods (ELEVATED) versus Innovation Dominance and Brand Strength (both PARTIAL): whether e-beam and solar lines — solar already at 80-85% utilisation — grow into genuine premium pricing, or discounted project sales keep expanding their 8-10 point share of the mix.
- Cyclical market growth check (+1): whether the fibre price recovery from ~$2.5 to ~$5 per km actually reaches results, given fixed-price annual contracts delay capture and the cycle bottom is already behind the company.
