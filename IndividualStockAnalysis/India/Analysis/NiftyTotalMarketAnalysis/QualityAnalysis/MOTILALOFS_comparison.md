**Checks that regressed:**

- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: No monopoly-like position exists in its core markets — a 2.9% MF AUM share and ~7% cash broking share in crowded asset management and broking industries; the only near-monopoly claims are narrow league-table ranks in IPO/QIP issuance. | The long view had said: It holds leadership only in narrow slices — largest non-bank broker by cash-segment revenue share and #1 in the QIP league table — but with 7.6% retail broking share and 2.9% MF AUM share, nothing approaches a monopoly in the customer's mind.

Now: Distribution reach is a genuine advantage — largest integrated capital markets platform, #1 in IPO/QIP/rights league tables with 39 deals worth Rs 49,000 crore in 1HFY26, a 14.5mn customer base and a distribution book compounding 34–36% — though rivals do replicate parts of it. | The long view had said: The combined internal-plus-external distribution machine covering 98% of PIN codes with 10,800+ advisors is a reach discount brokers and most AMCs cannot replicate, and it directly powers the MF flow-share gains and 41% growth in the distribution book.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 6. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 0 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Across all four calls management frames the shift from transaction-led to annuity-led: ARR rose from 61% of net revenues (Q2FY26) to 65% (Q3FY26) to over 66% of group revenues by Q1FY27, with AUM-linked fees, PWM ARR AUM (~Rs52,000cr) and newly-recognised PE carry accruals all described as structural recurring streams. (The long view had said: Across the 18 calls management steadily re-engineers the model toward annuity/ARR income — trail-based distribution AUM, SIP book, AMC/alternates management fees and NII — with the annuity share disclosed and rising each year (60% of revenues in FY26, 66% by Q1FY27), and wealth-segment ARR up from 33% in FY21 to 57% in Q1FY27. Growth is funded entirely by internal accruals with no equity dilution since 2007.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Evidence of trust and reach — unique PANs crossing 1 crore (16% of all MF industry clients), 91% of AUM beating benchmarks, deliberate brand investment and adjacency launches (private credit, commercial RE, GIFT City) under the same name — but no premium pricing or heritage-driven pricing power is claimed. (The long view had said: Management repeatedly leans on the franchise name to explain headroom in wealth and adjacency launches (AMC, alternates, private credit, housing finance) under one brand, and Q4FY26 shows deliberate brand spend; but the calls give no A&P quantum by brand, and pricing carries no visible premium.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: No call in the window discusses price increases, yield/TER movements or elasticity; revenue growth is attributed entirely to AUM, flows and mix shift rather than pricing. (The long view had said: Pricing is flat to deflationary and largely set by regulation and mix: distribution yields flat year-on-year and quarter-on-quarter (Aug 2024), AMC yields compressing on the shift to passives, TER regulation a live risk, and F&O/transaction-charge changes passed through rather than absorbed. Growth comes from volume, AUM and mix, not price increases.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: No government revenue or subsidy dependence, but the regulator materially moves the P&L: SEBI's F&O changes and higher margin requirements are cited in two consecutive calls as depressing Wealth Management volumes and market breadth, and the new labour code cost ₹14.4 crore in Q3FY26 alone. Pricing and product economics in broking are regulator-determined. (The long view had said: Revenue economics are set by the regulator, not the company: SEBI's F&O contract-size and transaction-charge changes, higher margin requirements, TER revisions and the July 2026 prop-trading rules are discussed in call after call, and management explicitly attributes the FY26 broking decline to them — 'FY25 & FY26 marked regulatory tightening combined with weak markets'.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Active management fees and full-service brokerage face structurally cheaper substitutes — the AMC's own AUM is now described as 'diversified across active, passive, AIF and PMS', and distribution intermediaries control much of the end-client choice. Management does not defend its pricing against cheaper alternatives on these calls; instead it leans on performance and on niche, harder-to-commoditise products. (The long view had said: Both main franchises face cheaper-but-adequate substitutes: zero/low-cost discount broking against full-service brokerage, and low-fee passives against active funds, with the regulator compressing TER on top. Management defends the premium on service and research rather than price, and so far yields have held.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The prevailing preference shift — financialization of Indian savings from deposits/physical assets into equities, SIPs and managed products — runs in the company's favour, and management sizes it repeatedly (equity savings 5% in India vs 40% in US; MF AUM/GDP 20% vs 120%). The portfolio has been pivoted toward exactly that demand base. (The long view had said: The demand base has moved from transactional trading to advice-led investing and from active to passive/SIP products, and management is explicitly pivoting rather than defending: the Aug 2024 call is largely devoted to rebranding Broking & Distribution as Wealth Management because 'today's investors are more informed and seek more than just transactional services'. The passive shift also compresses yields.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Fashion Risk (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
