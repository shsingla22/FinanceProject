# Mahanagar Gas Ltd. (MGL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: ENERGY*

Mahanagar Gas Limited (MGL) is an Indian city gas distribution (CGD) company that buys natural gas in bulk and retails it through its own pipeline network and filling stations in the licensed geographical areas it has been awarded — Mumbai and adjoining areas (including GA-2), Raigad, and, through its wholly owned subsidiary Unison Enviro, additional areas such as Latur. It sells that gas in three forms: CNG pumped into vehicles at its stations, piped natural gas (PNG) piped into household kitchens, and piped gas sold to industrial and commercial users like factories, hospitals, schools, restaurants and canteens. It makes money on the spread between what it pays for gas (a mix of cheaper government-allocated domestic gas — APM, HPHT, NWG and pooled gas — plus term and spot imported LNG, some of it Henry Hub-linked) and the price it charges customers, with management guiding to roughly Rs. 10–12 of EBITDA per standard cubic metre sold; it also keeps spending on infrastructure — pipelines, CNG stations and new household connections — to grow volumes. By FY2026 it was selling about 4.585 mmscmd of gas on average through 8,320 km of pipeline, 518 CNG stations, 3.21 million connected households and 5,924 industrial and commercial customers, earning Rs. 1,451 crore EBITDA and Rs. 847 crore net profit.

**The parts of the business:**

- **CNG (compressed natural gas for vehicles)** — Sells gas as vehicle fuel at its own network of filling stations (518 stations as of March 2026, serving over 1.28 million CNG vehicles registered in its areas); this is by far the biggest business, at about 3.35 mmscmd of the 4.67 mmscmd sold in Q4 FY26, roughly seven-tenths of volumes, and management sees new CNG two-wheelers and scooters as a further growth avenue.
- **Domestic PNG (piped natural gas to households)** — Piped cooking gas supplied to homes — about 3.21 million households connected by March 2026, adding roughly 3.4 lakh new connections in FY26 — a smaller but steady volume block of about 0.605 mmscmd, and one management says is supplied 100% from cheaper domestically produced APM gas even when imports are disrupted.
- **Industrial and commercial (I&C)** — Piped gas sold to factories, hospitals, schools, restaurants and institutional kitchens — 5,924 such customers and about 0.72 mmscmd in Q4 FY26 (roughly a sixth of volumes); it is the fastest-growing segment (up about 15% in FY26) but also the most exposed, since it leans on imported LNG and was curtailed to about 80% supply during the West Asia crisis.
- **Raigad geographical area (GA)** — A licensed expansion area outside Mumbai where MGL has been laying pipeline (424 km as of mid-2024) and building out household connections and CNG stations to create the ecosystem needed before demand there can be unlocked.
- **Unison Enviro Private Limited (UEPL)** — A wholly owned subsidiary running the same CGD business in its own geographical areas; a small but fast-growing add-on that reached its 100th CNG station in January 2026 (up from 53 at acquisition) and contributed about 0.283 mmscmd in Q3 FY26 versus roughly 4.6 mmscmd for the group.
- **Gas sourcing and pricing** — Not a separate product line but the core profit lever management discusses every quarter — blending government-allocated domestic gas (APM, HPHT, NWG, pooled) with term and spot LNG, and adjusting retail prices (for example a Rs. 0.50/kg CNG hike in February 2026) to protect the per-unit margin as allocations and global indices like Brent and Henry Hub move.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Mahanagar Gas earns its 63-out-of-100 "Decent" rating (45% × 63 business quality + 30% × 63 multibagger fit + 25% × 64 risk safety) on the back of one asset: a physical network that nobody can copy. The quality work scores its distribution advantage and its mini-monopoly at the top of the scale — 8,320 km of pipeline, 518 CNG stations, 3.21 million connected households and 1.28 million registered CNG vehicles inside exclusive licensed areas of Mumbai and Raigad, still compounding at roughly 50 stations and 500 km a year. The pattern screen reaches the same conclusion from a different direction, marking both Cost to Replicate and Forward Integrators a STRONG FIT: the barrier is steel in the ground plus a legal licence, and the company owns the whole path to the customer rather than selling through anyone else. Two more quality strengths sit underneath this. Customers effectively fund the business — cash comes back seven days before suppliers have to be paid — and every Rs 100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings threw off Rs 17 of operating cash this year. Sales have grown about 9% a year with unusually little variation, the Rs 30 per share FY26 dividend (Rs 12 interim plus Rs 18 final) was held even in a year when profit fell, and the Unison Enviro purchase looks like a bolt-on that was actually improved after the deal — 47 stations in 23 months and volumes from 0.194 to 0.283 mmscmd. The risk review calls overall resilience SOUND, and this cash-generating, self-funding core is why.

The trouble is that the very things that make the moat also create the biggest exposures. The network is a moat precisely because it is sunk into one place and cannot be moved — and that is the same reason the Technological Innovation risk bites: BEST's municipal fleet went from roughly 3,000 CNG buses at peak to a few hundred as it switched to electric, costing about 1 lakh kg a day, and Mumbai CNG growth is now described as muted at around 2% against 8–9% for the company as a whole. Building that network takes about Rs 250 crore of capex every quarter, which the quality framework credits as genuine growth spending but flags with a warning: each new rupee is buying less, with per-unit EBITDA guidance already slipping. So the Cost to Replicate strength and the stranded-asset worry are the same fact seen from two sides. The second doubled-edged trait is price. The entire reason a customer converts is a 30%-plus mileage-adjusted saving versus petrol — which is why Low Price Plus and Pricing Power register only as PARTIAL, why Brand Strength is a flat NO FIT (no heritage, no advertising, no premium positioning; management concedes customers would be indifferent if the gap closed), and why Toll Roads is a NO FIT too, since fuel is a large, visible line in the customer's budget rather than a rounding error. Regular price increases do stick — Rs 1.50 in July 2024, Rs 0.50 in February 2026, Rs 1 in April 2026, with FY26 volumes still up 8.25% — but only up to the point where the discount to petrol still persuades people.

That price ceiling is where the one HIGH RISK finding lands. Government Dependency runs through all four years of calls: APM allocation formulas, the May 2022 uniform base price guidelines, the monthly price notified by GAIL and vetted by PPAC, and the Kirit Parikh ceiling rising $0.25 per MMBtu from 1 April 2026. Input cost is set by policy, selling price is capped by petrol parity, and the company tends to absorb cost inflation rather than pass it straight through — which is exactly why Cyclicality is a WATCH: volumes have never had a down year (3.45 mmscmd in Q1FY23 to 4.67 in Q4FY26) but margins per unit swing hard. Three other WATCH items round out the picture and each connects back to a pattern. Supply is concentrated in a handful of GAIL/ONGC allocations, a few term LNG contracts and a single pipeline even though 3.21 million households and 5,924 industrial and commercial customers make the demand side well spread — management is diversifying (Henry Hub cut to about 1.3 mmscmd, new Brent-linked volumes) but the dependence is real. Shifting Consumer Preferences captures municipal transport choosing electric, offset by 342,157 new household connections and 118,590 new vehicles in FY26 alone. And the Friendly Middleman pattern (LIKELY FIT) explains who actually controls the growth rate: volume depends on Bajaj, TVS and fleet operators deciding to build or buy CNG variants, not on anything Mahanagar Gas does — which also shows up as Innovation Dominance being a NO FIT, since the innovations that drive demand happen at the vehicle makers. Put together: a protected, cash-generative regional monopoly with a structurally growing end market (gas targeted to rise from 6.5% to 15% of the primary energy mix by 2030), whose returns are set by government pricing and whose growth is decided by other people's product decisions.

*"Owns the shop, not just the product"*

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Across all four years the calls are dominated by government-set inputs: APM allocation formulas and the May-2022 MoPNG uniform base price guidelines, monthly UBP notified by GAIL and vetted by PPAC, the Kirit Parikh ceiling ($0.25/MMBtu rise from 1 Apr 2026), PNGRB zonal unified tariffs (Zone 1 raised 40→54 from Jan 2026), and the 9 March 2026 government pooled-gas allocation order during the Gulf crisis. Priority-sector APM allocation was cut from ~90% (FY24) to ~69% (Q1FY25), directly compressing EBITDA/SCM — a policy change, not a market one, and MGL is a fixed-pipeline utility in a licensed monopoly area.

> "last year almost 90% APM was available... And after that there has been a decrease in APM allocation, and we have been trying to manage that gas cost" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Volumes are a flow product tied to daily transport and household consumption and grew steadily every year (3.45 mmscmd in Q1FY23 to 4.67 in Q4FY26, ~6-9% p.a. with no down years), but margins swing hard with commodity indices — EBITDA/SCM guidance moved from ~Rs.9 to Rs.10-12 to Rs.8-8.5, and FY26 EBITDA fell to Rs.1,451cr from Rs.1,570cr as Brent, Henry Hub and spot LNG moved.
