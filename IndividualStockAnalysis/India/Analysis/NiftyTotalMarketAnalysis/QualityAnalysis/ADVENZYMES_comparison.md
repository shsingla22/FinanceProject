### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: The structural setup invites cheaper substitutes — a single non-complex anti-inflammatory enzyme is ~22% of revenue, sold to pharma formulators who control the choice and behind whose brand the ingredient disappears — though nothing in the four calls shows price defence, and FY26 margins actually expanded (EBITDA 31%, PAT margin 23% vs 21% in FY25). (The long view had said: This is the Nobel Biocare setup: Advanced Enzymes sells an unbranded ingredient to formulators who control the choice and are explicitly hunting cost, so pricing power is weak and repeatedly acknowledged — 'it is tough competitive market' (2022) through 'it is difficult to pass on very competitive world' (May 2026) — with input, freight and energy inflation absorbed rather than passed on, gross contribution eroding, and the 90–95%-share flagship guided to flat growth as cheaper alternatives take share. The defence is productivity and cost efficiency, not brand.)
- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The calls show a steady, small-scale innovation cadence rather than exposure to a platform reset: R&D running ~5% of consolidated revenue every quarter, a new Nashik R&D centre coming on stream, and two EFSA plus three US FDA food-enzyme dossiers filed in FY26. No analyst or management comment in the window frames a competing technology platform as a threat; if anything enzymes are the substituting technology in pharma/nutraceutical processing. (The long view had said: Analysts explicitly probe disruption risk (Feb 2026: monitoring 'disruptive technologies or new entrants in the enzyme space'), and enzyme discovery is a science-driven field where a superior molecule can displace an incumbent product; management's answer is a continuous, incremental cadence — ~50 products in a 1.5–3 year pipeline, single R&D centre split by function, new Nashik R&D centre, two EFSA and three FDA dossiers filed in FY26 — which is small-scale value-adding innovation rather than platform-level exposure.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The demand shift in evidence runs toward the company, not against it: management describes pharma, nutraceutical and biotech sectors moving to wider enzyme adoption, and the company set up Advanced Nutrazyme to sell into the nutrition and wellness range, with B2C US revenue rising ($0.95mn Q1 FY25 to $1.28mn Q1 FY26). (The long view had said: A large slice of revenue is US nutraceutical ingredients, where demand follows consumer health fashions — management has been piloting sugar-management and weight-loss enzyme products for several quarters and in FY26 flags that consumer 'discretionary expenses' are under pressure; the pivot toward the trending categories is itself the response to the shift.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Product concentration is the sharper issue than customer concentration — a single anti-inflammatory enzyme was ~22% of Q1 FY26 top line (up from 18%) — while top-10 customers crept up to 27% in Q1 and Q2 FY26 from 23%/25% a year earlier. Bounded rather than acute: no single named customer or licence is discussed as make-or-break in any of the four calls. (The long view had said: The customer base has broadened steadily over the five years — top-10 share fell from 34% (Q1FY22) to 28–29% (FY23) to 23% in FY26 — and no single customer, licensor or supplier is discussed as driving results; the residual concentration is in a product (the anti-inflammatory enzyme at 18–23% of revenue) and in geography (USA), not in a stakeholder relationship.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (ELEVATED).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 8 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
