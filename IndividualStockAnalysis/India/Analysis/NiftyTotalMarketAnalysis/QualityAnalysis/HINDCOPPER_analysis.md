# Hindustan Copper Ltd. (HINDCOPPER) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: METALS*

Hindustan Copper Limited (HCL) is an Indian copper mining company that digs copper ore out of its own underground mines, processes it at its concentrator plants, and sells the output as copper concentrate — measured as "MIC" (metal in concentrate) — with roughly 30,000 tonnes of MIC expected this year. Its customers are domestic copper smelters and vendors in India (management names Adani, Hindalco, and the shut Vedanta Tuticorin plant as the smelting capacity in the market), and it currently does not export because Indian buyers are short of concentrate and pay faster, via LC or advance. Revenue is priced by a simple formula — the LME copper price minus TCRC (treatment/refining charges) — so earnings swing with global copper prices; cost of production is about $5,000 per tonne of MIC at Malanjkhand and about $6,000 at Khetri and the Jharkhand mines, all-in including mining, beneficiation and overheads. It operates three mining areas under leases valid to about 2040-42 — Khetri/Kolihan in Rajasthan, Malanjkhand in Madhya Pradesh, and the Ghatsila cluster (Surda, Kendadih, Rakha) in Jharkhand — and is a government-linked company that answers to the Ministry of Mines, targeting 12 million tonnes of ore a year by 2030-31 (roughly 80,000-90,000 tonnes of MIC) on about Rs 2,000 crore of its own capex.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Khetri (Rajasthan) — Khetri, Kolihan and Chandmari mines** — The company's original operation (HCL was formed out of Khetri in 1967): two producing underground mines currently doing about 1-1.2 million tonnes of ore, targeted to reach 2.9 million tonnes by 2030-31 (1.5 from Khetri, 1.4 from Kolihan), with Kolihan restarted in April after an accident and prized for its high 0.9 grade, while the third mine Chandmari stays shut since 2001 pending clearance of environmental violations.
- **Malanjkhand (Madhya Pradesh)** — The biggest and cheapest mine — 2.73 million tonnes of ore last year, 2.90 targeted this year and 5 million tonnes by 2030-31 — converted from opencast to underground, with a production cost near $5,000 per tonne of MIC and the bulk of capex (about Rs 900 crore of the Rs 1,300 crore near-term spend) going here for shaft equipping, a new concentrator plant, crusher, fans and power infrastructure.
- **ICC / Ghatsila (Jharkhand) — Surda, Kendadih and Rakha** — Three underground mines around 40 km from Jamshedpur that were shut on statutory clearances and are now reopening — Surda restarted last October and should do about 0.3 million tonnes this year, Kendadih is dewatered and ready once its lease is signed, and together with Rakha the cluster is targeted at 4.3 million tonnes by 2030-31 at roughly $6,000 per tonne cost.
- **Rakha under MDO (mine developer cum operator) mode** — The Rakha mine is contracted out for 30 years (extendable by 10) to Southwest Mining Limited, a JSW company, which invests the whole Rs 2,600-2,700 crore itself over 6-7 years and pays HCL 12.5% of revenue plus statutory dues, with production of up to 3 million tonnes targeted by 2030-31 but nothing expected for roughly 16-24 months from lease signing.
- **Concentrator (beneficiation) plants** — Each mining area has a washing/concentrator plant designed around the ore grade of its own mines, so throughput and MIC output depend on the right mines feeding them — new concentrators are planned at both Malanjkhand and Khetri as part of the expansion.
- **KABIL / Khanij Bidesh (overseas critical minerals) and exploration** — A small, early-stage side interest outside copper: non-invasive exploration is done on five lithium brine blocks in Argentina with a draft geological report pending, and HCL has recently won an exploration licence in India, though management stresses KABIL's mandate is non-copper minerals (lithium, cobalt, nickel, rare earths) and that the company's focus remains reaching 12 million tonnes of copper ore.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 2 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.
