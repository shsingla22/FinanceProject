# Anupam Rasayan (ANURAS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Anupam Rasayan is an Indian specialty chemicals maker that manufactures complex, multi-step chemical molecules to order for large global companies — it does not sell branded consumer products but supplies intermediates and key starting materials that its customers use in crop-protection products, medicines, personal care items and high-performance materials. Most sales come from long-term contracts and LOIs signed with a handful of multinational customers (the top 10 customers were about 77% of revenue in Q1 FY25), priced on a cost-plus basis where raw material, freight, energy and other costs are passed through, which is how management says it keeps EBITDA margins in a roughly 22%-28% band. Its plants are in Gujarat (Sachin and Jhagadia, near Surat), and about 58% of 9MFY26 revenue was exported, with Japan alone around 17% of a recent quarter and the US growing after buying Jayhawk Fine Chemicals. Consolidated revenue was INR 1,730 crores for the nine months to December 2025 (up 84% year-on-year), and the group also owns fluorine chemicals maker Tanfac, has completed the Jayhawk purchase, and has agreed to buy a controlling-sized stake in Bliss GVS Pharma.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Agrochemicals (Agro)** — Custom-manufactured intermediates and molecules for global crop-protection companies; historically the core of the business at 76% of standalone revenue in FY22, deliberately reduced to 55% by FY26, and it went through a sharp downturn in FY25 on customer channel-inventory destocking before recovering during FY26.
- **Pharma** — Key starting materials and intermediates for blockbuster drugs such as Atorvastatin, Sitagliptin, Dapagliflozin and Vonoprazan, pitched as import substitution; the fastest-growing part, up 15-fold from INR 21 crores in FY22 to INR 339 crores in FY26 standalone and 19% of 9MFY26 sales, with 30+ more molecules in R&D and pilot.
- **Polymer / Performance Materials (incl. high-performance materials, electronic materials, defence, semiconductors, EV)** — Advanced materials including fluoropolymers and electronic chemicals sold into defence, electronics, semiconductor, automotive and EV applications; contributed 17% of 9MFY26 revenue, grew 245% year-on-year in that period, and standalone high-performance materials revenue tripled from INR 97 crores in FY22 to INR 305 crores in FY26, with 35+ molecules under development.
- **Personal Care** — A smaller fourth vertical management lists as part of the diversified revenue mix alongside Agro, Pharma and Performance Materials, but which the calls do not size separately.
- **Fluorination chemistry / Tanfac Industries** — A controlling stake bought in 2022 in a fluorine chemicals company that supplies hydrofluoric acid and potassium fluoride — the starting raw materials for Anupam's fluorination products — giving backward integration, supply security and cost stability; Tanfac's numbers are consolidated into group results and it is now adding its own new products and customers.
- **Jayhawk Fine Chemicals (US)** — A US specialty chemicals business bought outright from the CABB Group and completed in FY26, giving Anupam onshore American manufacturing and advanced custom-synthesis capability; it has pro forma annual revenue of about US$76 million but only 1 month and 2 days of it was consolidated in FY26, so the full contribution shows from FY27.
- **Bliss GVS Pharma (announced, not yet closed)** — An agreed purchase of a 43.3%-48.2% equity stake plus an open offer for up to 26% more, funded partly by INR 300 crores of non-convertible debentures, intended to become a dedicated formulations and pharma CDMO/CMO platform sitting alongside the core specialty chemicals business.
- **Captive renewable power** — Not a revenue line but a cost lever management repeatedly highlights — a 12.5 MW solar plant plus later solar/wind additions saving roughly INR 14-15 crores a year in energy costs and targeted at 65% green electricity and energy net-zero by 2027.

### The story in depth

Anupam Rasayan's single biggest strength is that it makes itself indispensable to its customers rather than charging them more. The framework's highest-scoring check is assurance of supply: the company held strategic inventory at customer request through the 2022 supply crisis, bought control of Tanfac to secure its own hydrofluoric and potassium fluoride feedstock, and wins work on supply-chain credibility. That same trait is what makes three of the multibagger patterns fit at all. It underpins the Cost to Replicate case (backward integration plus multi-step fluorination chemistry plus multi-year customer qualification cycles a newcomer cannot shortcut), it is the reason Toll Roads is a partial fit (management describes hard-to-displace intermediates with long qualification cycles), and it drives the strongest pattern verdict, Forward Integrators, through the Jayhawk purchase for onshore US manufacturing and the Bliss GVS stake that extends the company from starting materials toward finished dosage forms. Alongside it, a genuine research effort has converted into revenue rather than staying in the lab: the team grew from 85 professionals in FY23 to a pipeline of 65-plus molecules, 12-plus were commercialised in a single year, and the payoff shows up as pharma revenue and performance materials growing 245% year on year in the first nine months of FY26. This is also why two of the eight risk channels come back LOW: no competing platform technology threatens the installed base, and demand is functional, contracted business rather than anything exposed to taste.

The problem is that the very behaviour that earns customer loyalty is expensive to fund, and the risk review is largely a bill for it. Holding inventory for customers and financing long production cycles shows up directly as the two worst checks in the framework: about 377 days of cash tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and only ₹0.30 of sales from every ₹1 of assets. The pattern screen makes the same point from the other side, rejecting Recurring Revenue outright on the grounds that customers do not fund this business, Anupam funds them by carrying inventory on their behalf. Put those together and it explains the weakest area score of all, return on capital: ₹100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings produced just ₹6 of operating cash this year. The customer relationships are also narrow. Market share gains are real and are the clearest pattern fit, with Japan going from 1% of revenue in 2018 to roughly 17% by Q3 FY26 and new multinational customers added each year, but the top ten customers were 93% of revenue in Q1 FY23 across only 24 products and were still 77% in Q1 FY25, which is why stakeholder concentration is the one HIGH RISK verdict. The FY25 revenue collapse was traced to order deferral by top agrochemical clients, showing a single relationship's ordering pattern can move group revenue by roughly a third. The broadening is genuine and is the mitigant, but the same short customer list that makes wallet-share wins visible is what makes the revenue line swing.

Where the story turns cautious is price and credibility. Contracts are explicitly cost-plus with all inputs passed through, so price moves with cost rather than above it. That makes Pricing Power a clear no-fit and it caps what the growth is worth: sales have compounded at roughly 25% a year but lumpily, and mix improvement has not defended profitability, with operating margins sliding from 28% in Q1 FY23 to 22-23% in FY26. Cost-plus contracts are also cited as the main protection against cyclicality, yet cyclicality still lands as ELEVATED, because revenue still swung from +31% to -34% to +84% across the period as the agrochemical cycle turned. In the more commoditising lines the defence is cost, not differentiation, which is why Low Price Plus registers as a partial fit on the back of an itemised savings programme (a 12.5MW solar installation saving about ₹10 crore a year rising to ₹14 crore, and a ₹59 crore hybrid plant saving about ₹15 crore) at the same time as Good Enough Goods sits on watch, with management answering Chinese pricing dominance by pointing to its own cost curve. New entrants and government dependency are both watch items for the same reason: newer growth such as LiPF6 leans on Western buyers diversifying away from China and on Make in India positioning, in a product where analysts note two or three established global players already exist. Management delivery is mixed rather than poor: promised capex was completed and the diversification pledged in 2022 was delivered, but the 26-28% margin range once called sustainable was not held, which is what drags the management and capital allocation area scores negative. The combined rating of 47 out of 100, blending business quality at 53, pattern fit at 29 and risk safety at 58, lands as Mixed with a sound resilience reading: a durable, well-defended supplier that has not yet shown it can turn that position into a return on the capital it consumes.
