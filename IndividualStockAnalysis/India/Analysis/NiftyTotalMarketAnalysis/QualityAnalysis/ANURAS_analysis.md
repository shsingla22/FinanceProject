# Anupam Rasayan (ANURAS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Anupam Rasayan is an Indian specialty chemicals maker based in Surat, Gujarat (plants including Sachin and Jhagadia) that custom-manufactures complex, multi-step chemical molecules to order for large global companies rather than selling branded products of its own. Its customers are multinational innovators in agrochemicals, pharmaceuticals, personal care and advanced/performance materials (including electronics, semiconductors, defence and EV applications), with the top 10 customers making up roughly 77% of revenue and about 58% of sales exported — Japan alone contributing around 17%. It makes money mainly through long-term supply contracts and letters of intent structured on a cost-plus basis, where raw material, freight, energy and other costs are passed through to the customer, which keeps EBITDA margins in a fairly steady low-to-mid 20s percentage range. Fluorination chemistry is a core capability, backed by owning fluorine raw material supplier Tanfac, and the company has been expanding abroad by buying US-based Jayhawk Fine Chemicals and taking a controlling-sized stake in formulations player Bliss GVS Pharma.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Agrochemicals (Agro)** — Custom-made intermediates and active ingredients for crop-protection companies — historically the core of the business at 76% of standalone revenue in FY22, cut to 55% by FY26 as other segments grew; it went through a sharp downturn in FY25 on customer channel-inventory destocking and recovered through FY26.
- **Pharma** — Key starting materials and intermediates for well-known drug molecules such as Atorvastatin, Sitagliptin, Dapagliflozin and Vonoprazan, pitched as import substitution; it grew roughly 15-fold from INR 21 crores in FY22 to INR 339 crores in FY26 and was about 19% of 9MFY26 sales, with 30+ more molecules in R&D and pilot.
- **Polymer / Performance Materials (incl. electronic materials, defence, semiconductors, EV)** — Higher-value advanced materials including fluoropolymers and electronic chemicals sold heavily into Japan and the US; standalone revenue roughly tripled from INR 97 crores in FY22 to INR 305 crores in FY26, it was about 17% of 9MFY26 sales and grew 245% year-on-year in that period, with 35+ molecules under development.
- **Personal Care** — A smaller part of the diversified revenue mix that management lists alongside Agro, Pharma and Performance Materials, but which the calls do not size separately.
- **Tanfac Industries (subsidiary, acquired 2022)** — A controlling stake in a fluorine chemicals company that supplies hydrofluoric acid and potassium fluoride — the starting raw materials for Anupam's fluorination chemistry — giving backward integration, supply security and better cost stability; its numbers are included in consolidated results.
- **Jayhawk Fine Chemicals (US subsidiary, acquired FY26)** — A 100%-owned US specialty chemicals maker bought from the CABB Group with pro-forma annual revenue of about US$76 million, giving Anupam onshore American manufacturing, advanced custom synthesis capability and closer access to regulated, innovation-led customers; only 1 month and 2 days of it was consolidated in FY26.
- **Bliss GVS Pharma (announced stake acquisition, FY26)** — A definitive agreement to buy a 43.3%–48.2% equity stake plus an open offer for up to 26% more, funded partly with about INR 300 crores of non-convertible debentures, intended to become the group's dedicated pharma platform for finished formulations and future pharma CDMO/CMO work.
- **Captive renewable power** — Not a revenue segment but a cost lever — a 12.5 MW solar plant plus later solar and 9.6 MW hybrid solar-wind additions, saving roughly INR 10–15 crores a year in energy costs and targeting 65% green electricity on the way to energy net zero by 2027.

### The story in depth

Anupam Rasayan comes out of the three reviews as a genuinely useful supplier that has not yet turned that usefulness into good returns for shareholders — an overall 47 out of 100, or Mixed, built from 52 on business quality, 29 on multibagger fit and 58 on risk safety, with underlying resilience judged sound. What the company is unambiguously good at is being relied on: assurance of supply scores the highest mark in the whole framework, evidenced by carrying strategic inventory for multinational customers through the 2022 supply crunch and by winning LiPF6 mandates on the strength of its supply-chain credibility. That reliability has been converted into real geographic and segment expansion — Japan went from 1% of revenue in 2018 to about 13% in FY23 and roughly 17% by the third quarter of FY26, the United States was entered with actual onshore manufacturing through the completed Jayhawk acquisition, and pharmaceutical revenue grew fifteen-fold from Rs 21 crore in FY22 to Rs 339 crore in FY26. This is why two patterns register as likely fits: Market Share Gainers, on those documented wallet-share gains, and Forward Integrators, on the move downstream through Jayhawk and the Bliss GVS stake into finished dosage formulations. Operating profit of Rs 22 on every Rs 100 of sales, moving only about three points a year, says costs are controlled.

The trouble is that the very thing customers value is expensive to provide, and the same trait shows up as a strength in one record and a weakness in another. Holding inventory on customers' behalf is what makes the company indispensable, and it is also why roughly 377 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and why the Recurring Revenue pattern is a clear no-fit — instead of customers funding the business, the business funds the customers. Combine that with a markedly asset-heavy plant base, about Rs 2,500 crore of standalone gross block against roughly Rs 1,676 crore of revenue and only Rs 0.30 of sales per Rs 1 of assets, and the result is a cash return on capital of about 6% — the lowest-scoring area in the whole quality review. That single arithmetic chain also caps the Low Price Plus pattern at partial: the cost culture is real and itemised, Rs 10 crore a year saved from the 12.5MW solar plant and Rs 15 crore a year from the 9.6MW hybrid plant, but poor asset productivity swamps it. Management's own record is dragged down by the same issue — leadership has been stable, with the same managing director and finance chief across FY22 to FY26, and the large capital projects and Tanfac integration were delivered as promised, but the repeated promise to bring working capital back to 2021 levels was not kept, which is why Corporate Culture stops at partial rather than passing.

The risk review points at the same structure from a third angle. The highest-rated risk is customer concentration: the top ten customers were 93% of revenue in the first quarter of FY23 and still 77% two years later, and the FY25 downturn came from a small group of customers deferring purchases. That is the flip side of the deep, validated, multi-year relationships that earn the assurance score — few customers, each large and each hard to replace in either direction. Cyclicality is rated elevated for the same reason: revenue fell 34% year-on-year in the first quarter of FY25 with margins down about five points, then rebounded 84% in the first nine months of FY26 and 65% for the full year, which is exactly the lumpy pattern the growth-persistence check flags despite average growth of about 25% a year. Cost-plus contracts with full pass-through of raw material, freight, energy and inventory-carrying costs are the cushion in a downturn, but they are also the reason Pricing Power is a flat no-fit and why the Toll Roads and Good Enough Goods readings stay unconfirmed — a company that passes costs through does not set prices, and management's defence against cheaper Chinese capacity is cost and process optimisation rather than an argument that customers cannot switch. The backward step into hydrofluoric and potassium fluoride supply through Tanfac and the forward step through Jayhawk build a barrier a newcomer cannot assemble quickly, which is what holds the New Entrants and Good Enough Goods risks at watch rather than higher, but each of those steps also adds more assets to a base that is already producing too little per rupee invested. There is no meaningful technology-obsolescence or fashion risk here, and no reliance on regulated prices or subsidies, so the case rests squarely on whether the demonstrated demand can be served with less capital tied up.
