# Ujjivan Small Fin (UJJIVANSFB) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Ujjivan Small Finance Bank is an Indian bank that lends mainly to low- and middle-income customers and small businesses, funding those loans with deposits it collects from the public. Its original and still largest business is micro banking — small group loans and individual loans to micro-entrepreneurs — which it has been deliberately balancing with secured lending such as affordable housing, micro mortgages, MSME loans, gold loans and vehicle finance; by June 2026 secured loans were 50.4% of a INR42,903 crore gross loan book, against deposits of INR48,129 crore. It makes money chiefly on the gap between the interest it earns on loans and what it pays on deposits (net interest margin of about 8.5%, net interest income of INR1,186 crore in Q1 FY27), plus fee income from loan processing and cross-selling insurance and mutual funds. It operates only in India through a branch network that has grown to 814 branches as of June 2026, spread across roughly 26 states and 326 districts, supported by mobile, internet and the vernacular 'Hello Ujjivan' app.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Micro Banking (group loans and individual loans)** — Small unsecured loans to low-income borrowers, given either to joint-liability groups or as individual loans to good repeat customers who graduate up; it is the single biggest business at INR21,371 crore as of June 2026, about half the loan book, and drives most of the bank's yields and credit costs.
- **Affordable Housing and Micro Mortgages** — Home loans, micro home-improvement and micro business loans backed by property for modest-income households, sold through 581 branches (323 for micro mortgages); the largest secured line at INR11,210 crore in June 2026, growing about 41% a year with very low bad loans (GNPA 1.2% and 0.6%).
- **MSME (including LAP, working capital and supply chain finance)** — Loans to small and medium businesses, both formal and semi-formal, including loans against property, working capital and supply chain finance (jointly about 28% of the segment); INR3,470 crore in June 2026 and one of the fastest-growing lines at 54% year-on-year, also used to bring in current-account deposits.
- **FIG (Financial Institutions Group)** — Wholesale lending to other financial institutions such as smaller lenders; described as 'strategic business' at INR1,800 crore in June 2024 (6% of the asset book) and crossing INR3,000 crore by March 2026.
- **Gold Loans** — Loans against household gold, sourced from 455 branches; small but the fastest-growing product at INR1,020 crore in June 2026, up about 249% year-on-year, lent conservatively at around 75% of gold value at origination.
- **Vehicle Finance** — Loans for two-wheelers and other vehicles, mostly cross-sold to existing customers across 337 branches, with a push into mid-premium and electric vehicles; INR1,036 crore in June 2026, up 85% year-on-year, with GNPA of 1.7%.
- **Agri Banking** — A newer farm-related lending line launched alongside gold and vehicle finance; management groups the three together as roughly 7% of the loan book and 9% of disbursements in Q1 FY27, up from 3% in FY25, with agri growing about 126% in FY26.
- **Retail Liabilities / Deposits (including TASC and CASA)** — The deposit-gathering side — savings and current accounts, fixed deposits, institutional (TASC) deposits and the 'Ivory' programme for wealthier customers — which funds the lending book; deposits reached INR48,129 crore in June 2026, with CASA at INR12,930 crore and cost of funds of 6.86%.
- **Third-Party Products (TPP) and fee income** — Cross-selling insurance and distributing mutual funds through branches and mobile/internet banking, with a co-branded credit card in testing; not a lending business but a meaningful contributor to other income alongside loan processing fees (other income was 2.2% of average assets in FY26).

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Ujjivan Small Finance Bank comes out of all three reviews as a genuine grower with a weak engine underneath, and the combined rating says as much: 49 out of 100, a Mixed two-star verdict built from 49 on business quality and 48 on risk safety. The growth itself is not in doubt. The bank went from a modest 25-branch restart in FY23 to 752 branches across 326 districts and 26 states, and 814 by June 2026, with 581 branches carrying housing and 455 carrying gold loans. FY26 loan growth of 26.6% and deposit growth of 21.4% ran well ahead of system growth of 16% and 13.4%, the loan book was up 28.9% year-on-year and deposits 25% in the more recent period, and the fastest lines were housing at +40.8% and MSME at +54% off a small base. The quality framework rewards this on four separate counts — market share gain, geographical expansion, structural end-market growth and capex aimed at growth rather than upkeep — and the growth area scores 0.6, the best of the seven. But the multibagger screen still only grades Market Share Gainers as a partial fit, because no actual share figures or named-competitor comparisons appear anywhere, and micro banking was contracting for part of the stretch.

The same features that produce the strengths produce the risks, and that is the heart of the story. The high 8.5% margin is what makes Pricing Power a partial rather than a full pattern — but the framework is explicit that this margin comes from pricing for risk, not from an asset-light model or a protective moat, which is why asset turn scores -1 and return on capital scores -1.0, the worst area on the board. The same fact reappears as the Good Enough Goods risk: an analyst noted affordable housing yields had not moved in seven or eight quarters, and management conceded that the market will not allow a price increase. That single trait therefore drives a pattern, a failed check on intangible benefits, and a live risk at once. The deliberate shift to secured lending, from 43.5% to 50.4% of the book, is offered as the silver lining on cyclicality — a more durable book through the cycle — yet it is also the reason the pricing, mix and volume check scores -1, since secured assets yield less, and it pushes the bank deeper into affordable housing and vehicle finance where management itself flags banks and NBFCs crowding in. Meanwhile the branch, AI, cloud and analytics spending that earns the growth-capex point is the same spending that leaves cost-to-income at 65.6% and operating costs at 6.4% of assets, which is precisely why the Low Price Plus pattern is judged NO FIT: this is not a low-cost operator. Cost to Replicate is NO FIT for a related reason — nothing in seventeen calls explains why a well-funded newcomer could not build the same thing.

On the funding and governance side the picture splits cleanly. Stakeholder concentration is the one risk rated LOW, and deservedly: deposits of INR48,129 crore are 70% retail term plus CASA, CASA is 28.6%, 5.4 lakh new borrowers were added in FY26, and no single customer or channel dominates either side of the balance sheet. Management scores reasonably too — the Corporate Culture pattern notes bad news announced early, including pre-flagging the credit cost deterioration in FY25 and disclosing plainly that the RBI returned the universal bank application, and the promised multi-year shift to a secured mix was actually delivered. Prudential buffers look sound, with provision coverage lifted to 85%, liquidity coverage at 132-142% and the credit-deposit ratio held around 85-89%, all built organically with no acquisitions. What pulls the score down is what that growth costs: it is funded by deposits and by repeated equity raises, including a board-approved INR2,000 crore issue, with no buyback or shareholder distribution framework anywhere in the record — so capital allocation scores 0.0 and distribution to shareholders scores -1. Two risks are rated HIGH. Cyclicality is one, and the seventeen-call record is a full cycle in miniature: FY23 recovery and record profit, FY25 stress with rising overdues and contracting micro banking, FY26 return on equity down to 10.9% with credit cost at 2.2% of the loan book. Government dependency is the other, because the regulator effectively sets the terms of trade — the 2022 microfinance rules and income-to-obligation caps, industry guardrails management said would reduce new business, and a single RBI circular that pushed INR110 crore of housing disbursement out of one quarter. Industry structure scores -0.5, resilience is recorded as UNKNOWN, and five of the eight patterns are only partial fits with three not fitting at all — a bank growing fast in an expanding market, without yet showing that it earns durable returns while doing so.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; the numeric fingerprints could not be computed, so the call evidence decides alone.

**What the calls show:** The 17-call arc is a full credit cycle: FY23 opens with recovery from pandemic restructuring and record profit, FY25 sees PAR rise, credit cost climb toward 1.7% and micro-banking contract, FY26 reports ROE collapsing to 10.9% with credit cost 2.2% before recovering to 18.2% ROE and 0.9% credit cost by Q1FY27 — earnings and margins swing violently with the microfinance and rural cycle, and management explicitly monitors El Nino, kharif sowing and geopolitics as drivers of H2 activity.

> "Micro banking assets, GL and IL combined, grew past INR20,000 crores after a large contraction in financial year '25, and also in financial year '24" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate shift to a secured, more diversified book (43.5% to 50.4% of loans) plus gold, vehicle and agri lines; micro-loans are short-tenor working-capital 'flow' credit to household operations rather than capex, and management kept investing in branches and tech through the downturn.*

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Corporate fortunes are directly set by the regulator: RBI's 2022 MFI rules and FOIR caps, MFIN guardrails that management said would cut business generation, an RBI circular that deferred INR110 crores of housing disbursement in one quarter, the RBI-defined retail deposit definition that moved the retail ratio 200bps, and above all the April 2026 return of the universal bank licence application, after which management is deliberately holding micro-banking growth below 10% to reshape its book toward what the RBI wants.

> "The regulator, while returning our application, has acknowledged our ongoing efforts towards diversification of our loan portfolio... we would be growing it at less than 10%" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: No dependence on government orders, subsidies or tenders for revenue; the customer base is private and retail, and diversification into housing, MSME, gold and vehicle is broadening the earnings base.*
