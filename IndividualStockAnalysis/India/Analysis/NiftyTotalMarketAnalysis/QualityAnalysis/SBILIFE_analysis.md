# SBI Life Insurance Company Ltd. (SBILIFE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

SBI Life Insurance Company Ltd. is an Indian life insurance company that sells life-insurance and long-term savings policies — unit-linked (ULIP) investment plans, guaranteed non-participating and participating savings plans, pure protection (term/life cover) policies, credit life cover, and annuity/pension and retirement products — to individual retail customers and to groups such as corporates and lenders' borrower pools. It reaches customers mainly through bancassurance (State Bank of India branches and regional rural banks, with over 59,000–60,000 certified insurance facilitators), a large tied agency force (about 2.57 lakh agents in 2024, with over 1,20,000 added gross in FY26 and new branches opened), and other channels including direct sales, corporate agents, other banks, brokers, online and web aggregators. It makes money by collecting premiums — new business premium plus renewal premium (renewals are 55–58% of gross written premium, which reached INR1,012.9 billion in FY26) — and by investing those premiums, with assets under management of INR5.2 trillion as of June 2026; profit after tax was INR24.7 billion in FY26 and the calls also track value of new business (INR66.7 billion, 27.5% margin) and embedded value (INR807.9 billion). It operates only in India, is regulated by IRDAI, is listed on the NSE and BSE, and describes itself as the private-market leader with roughly 21–23% private market share.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Individual savings — ULIP (unit-linked)** — Market-linked savings-plus-life-cover policies where returns follow investment funds; the largest single product line, at 51% of individual new business premium in Q1 FY23, 58% in Q1 FY25, and INR144.2 billion or 65% of individual APE in FY26 (61% in Q1 FY27).
- **Guaranteed non-par savings** — Savings policies with fixed, guaranteed returns (e.g. the Smart Platina Plus product); roughly 19–23% of individual business over the period, at INR42.7 billion in FY26 and growing 27% in Q1 FY27.
- **Participating (Par) products** — Savings policies that share in the company's profits via bonuses; a smaller but fast-growing line at INR17.3 billion individual APE in FY26 (up 133% year-on-year) and INR2.4 billion in Q1 FY27.
- **Individual protection** — Pure life cover and term-type policies sold to individuals; small in premium terms (INR10.3 billion APE in FY26, INR1.9 billion in Q1 FY27) but management calls it a key growth pillar, with pure protection up 122% in FY26.
- **Group protection** — Life cover sold to groups such as employers and institutions; INR12.1 billion APE in FY26 and INR12.3 billion in Q1 FY27 alone, when it grew 313% year-on-year.
- **Credit life** — Cover that pays off a borrower's loan if they die, sold alongside lending; a steady smaller line at INR4.7 billion new business premium in Q1 FY25 and INR2.9 billion APE in FY26, growing 14%.
- **Annuity and pension / retirement** — Products that convert savings into retirement income, including deferred-annuity options (e.g. SBI Life Smart Annuity Plus); INR86.5 billion of new business in FY26 and INR18.9 billion in Q1 FY27, with individual annuity making up about 90% of annuity business.
- **Group fund management** — Managing pooled retirement and similar funds for group clients; cited at INR15.2 billion with 93% growth in Q1 FY23.
- **Bancassurance (SBI and RRBs)** — Selling policies through State Bank of India and regional rural bank branches — the biggest distribution channel, contributing 60% of total APE in FY26 (47% in Q1 FY27) via more than 59,000–60,000 certified insurance facilitators.
- **Agency channel** — A tied agent salesforce (2.57 lakh agents by June 2024) that produced INR68.6 billion of individual APE in FY26, up 15%, and is being expanded through 'Agency 2.0', new agent recruitment and 120 new branches.
- **Other channels (direct, corporate agents, other banks, brokers, online, web aggregators)** — A mix of non-SBI bank tie-ups (Indian Bank, UCO Bank, South Indian Bank, Punjab & Sindh Bank, Yes Bank) and digital/fintech partners (RenewBuy, PhonePe) that grew to 11% of total APE in FY26 and 28% in Q1 FY27, with the online channel up 47% in FY26.

### The story in depth

SBI Life earns its 'Decent' 62-out-of-100 rating on the back of one dominant asset: distribution. The quality framework gives full marks to the distribution advantage — the captive SBI and regional rural bank branch network with roughly 59,000-60,000 certified facilitators, over 2.57 lakh agents, 120 new branches, non-SBI banks growing 22-31% and other channels up 160% — and calls it reach no private rival can replicate. That reach is reinforced by what the customer actually gets: 99.4% death claim settlement, a 0.02% misselling ratio described as among the lowest in the private industry, an industry-first internal ombudsman, and near-frictionless access through YONO three-click issuance, 99.9% digital proposal submission and 67% automated underwriting. The financial signature of this model is unusually strong: customers pay before the company pays out, so working capital is negative, and every ₹100 of capital in the business produced ₹181 of operating cash this year. Management behaviour matches — no acquisitions in four years, solvency held well above the 1.50 requirement, voluntary pandemic reserving, a refusal to raise commissions to chase growth, and plain admission when protection growth or margin fell short of its own guidance.

Three of the multibagger patterns are simply the same strengths viewed from a different angle. The recurring revenue pattern is a strong fit because the renewal book has grown from ₹57.6bn (51% of gross written premium) in Q1FY23 to ₹587.3bn (58%) in FY26 at 17-19% growth, with 13th-month persistency improving from 85.6% to 87.9% — this is the flow that pairs with the negative working capital and the high cash return on capital. Low price plus is a strong fit because scale over that distribution base drove the operating expense ratio from 7.2% down to 5.3% on a ₹1trn premium base, which management argues is why regulatory surrender-value changes hurt it least. The friendly middleman pattern is a likely fit because sales are almost entirely intermediated through those bank facilitators (47-63% of annualised premium equivalent) and agents, and the growth levers management names are channel investment and training. But the patterns stop short exactly where the quality framework does. Brand strength is only partial because the trust equity converts into distribution reach rather than the ability to charge more; pricing power is an explicit no-fit, with value-of-new-business margin sliding from 30.4% in Q1FY23 to 26.8% in Q1FY25 to 26.2% in Q1FY27 while management twice absorbed cost increases rather than passing them on. Market share gainers is only partial for the same reason — growth beats the industry (12.9% three-year individual APE growth versus 8.5%) yet the actual private-market share has drifted from 24.0% down to 22.4%. The quality scores show the same pattern: growth scores lowest of all areas, and persistence of growth is the one negative check, with roughly 15% average sales growth that arrives lumpily.

The risks are largely the strengths seen from the other side. The regulatory barriers that give the company a high-scoring, rationally priced industry with margins that have barely moved (±1.3 points) are the same barriers that make government dependency the single high risk: the IRDAI surrender-value circular forced product and commission redesign, the GST change cut FY26 profit after tax from ₹31.2bn to ₹24.7bn and dragged margin roughly 110-150bps a quarter, and the revised labour law added further cost. The SBI relationship that makes the business hard to replicate is also the elevated stakeholder concentration risk — bancassurance still supplied around 60% of total APE in FY26, credit life rides SBI's home-loan book, and analysts openly ask what happens if open architecture is mandated, which is precisely why the forward integrators and cost-to-replicate patterns rate only partial despite real progress in agency, online and direct channels. The product mix creates the third overlap: heavy ULIP weighting (65% of individual APE in FY26) ties volumes to equity markets, making cyclicality elevated, and demand rotates between non-par guaranteed, ULIP and par formats in ways management concedes it does not control — the same rotation that makes innovation dominance a partial rather than strong fit, since launches are reactive pivots as much as category creation. Offsetting all of this, technology risk and fashion risk are low, new entrants show no evidence of resetting economics, and the recurring renewal book plus improving persistency cushion the cycle — which is why resilience reads sound. The honest picture is a well-run, low-cost, cash-generative distributor of savings products whose economics are set as much by the regulator and the parent bank as by the company itself, and whose margins are trending down even as volumes grow.

## What to watch

- Value-of-new-business margin: the 'Pricing Power' no-fit rests on the 30.4% to 26.2% slide — a stabilisation or recovery, net of the GST and labour-law drag, would materially change the story.
- Open architecture at SBI: the 'Stakeholder Concentration' elevated risk turns on bancassurance still supplying ~60% of APE — any mandate or shift here tests the 'Cost to Replicate' and 'Distribution advantage' strengths directly.
- Private market share: 'Market Share Gainers' is only partial because share fell from 24.0% to 22.4% despite above-industry growth — a further drift would undercut the distribution moat claim.
- ULIP concentration and persistency: 'Cyclicality' is elevated because ULIP is 65% of individual APE — watch whether 13th and 61st month persistency keeps improving through a weaker equity market, since the renewal book is what funds the 'Recurring Revenue' strong fit.
- Regulatory changes to product, commission or tax: 'Government Dependency' is the only high risk, and the GST hit to FY26 profit shows a single rule change can move earnings more than operating performance does.
- Growth consistency: the 'Persistence of growth' check is the one negative score — evidence that the ~15% average sales growth is becoming steadier rather than lumpy would lift the weakest quality area.
