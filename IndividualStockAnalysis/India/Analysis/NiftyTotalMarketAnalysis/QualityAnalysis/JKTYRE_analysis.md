# JK Tyre & Industries Ltd. (JKTYRE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: AUTOMOBILE*

JK Tyre & Industries is an Indian company that manufactures and sells tyres — for trucks and buses, passenger cars and SUVs, tractors/farm vehicles, two- and three-wheelers, and off-the-road industrial and mining machines. It makes money two ways: fitting tyres onto new vehicles as an approved supplier to auto makers (OEMs such as Hyundai, Kia, Tata Motors, Ashok Leyland, Mahindra, VECV, Renault), and selling replacement tyres to vehicle owners and fleets through a dealer network of brand shops, Steel Wheels centres and pit stops across India, including rural areas. Its plants (Kankroli, Banmore, Laksar and others) ran at roughly 78–85% utilisation, and profitability swings mainly with raw-material costs — natural rubber above all — which management offsets through staggered selling-price hikes, richer product mix and cost cuts. Operations are centred in India, with exports to the US and other markets plus a manufacturing and selling business in Mexico; consolidated revenue reached a record Rs.16,384 crore in FY26 with EBITDA of Rs.2,089 crore.

**The parts of the business:**

- **TBR (truck and bus radial)** — Radial tyres for commercial vehicles, repeatedly called a critical segment for the company and a focus of capacity expansion; Q3FY26 replacement volumes grew 15% and OEM volumes 33% year-on-year.
- **PCR / passenger line** — Tyres for cars and SUVs, the other segment management calls critical, including EV-approved tyres (Hyundai Creta, Tata Punch electric variants, Renault Duster) and newly launched embedded 'smart' tyres; passenger line volumes grew 16–18% year-on-year in recent quarters with premium 16-inch-and-above sizes growing 13% in FY26.
- **Replacement market** — Selling tyres to existing vehicle owners and fleets through dealers — described as the steadier, promising demand driver, growing 11% in Q3FY26 and 19% (TBR) in Q4FY26.
- **OEM business** — Supplying tyres as original equipment to vehicle manufacturers; lumpier than replacement (it fell ~10% in Q1FY24 on pre-buy effects, then grew 24–42% in FY26 quarters), and price increases to OEMs come with a lag.
- **Exports** — Overseas sales from India, running around Rs.560–590 crore a quarter, roughly 15% of revenue, with volumes up 9% in Q3FY26 and 5% for full-year FY26 despite geopolitical disruption.
- **Farm segment** — Tractor and agricultural tyres, including the premium 'Shresth Plus' range; volumes rose 58% year-on-year in Q4FY26 with OE volumes nearly doubling.
- **OTR (off-the-road) tyres** — Tyres for industrial and mining equipment built for harsh conditions; four new types were launched in Q3FY26, a smaller but growing product line.
- **JK Tornel, Mexico** — The overseas subsidiary that makes and sells tyres in Mexico and exports to the US and Latin America, contributing roughly Rs.500–680 crore of revenue per quarter (about Rs.616 crore in Q3FY26) at 8–9% EBITDA margins; described as an earlier successful turnaround acquisition.
- **Cavendish Industries Limited (CIL)** — An Indian subsidiary contributing about Rs.960–1,025 crore of revenue per quarter, whose capacity utilisation JK Tyre lifted from ~30% to over 95%; it was legally merged into JK Tyre during Q3FY26 for operational and financial synergies.
- **Capacity expansion programme** — Ongoing brownfield projects — Rs.1,130 crore already under way (adding ~7% capacity across TBR, ASLTR and PCR) plus a further Rs.4,980 crore approved in phases to 2029 to lift TBR and PCR capacity by 24%.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

JK Tyre comes out of these three reviews as a genuinely well-run operator inside a difficult industry, and the rating of 44 out of 100 (Mixed) reflects exactly that split: business quality scores 52 and risk safety 72, but multibagger fit scores only 10. The strongest evidence sits with management and with the product itself. The 'Disciplined stewards' check is positive because net debt to equity was cut steadily from 1.16x in Q1FY24 to 0.71-0.73x by FY26, using organic brownfield growth rather than large outside acquisitions, and the 'Corporate Culture' pattern independently confirms that promises were kept — the Rs.1,100 crore capex announced in August 2023 was actually commissioned and the Cavendish turnaround and merger were completed as described. 'Independent, long-term and tenacious' rewards the same behaviour from a different angle: premiumisation, radial mix, the Cavendish turnaround and the net-zero commitment were pursued across all eleven calls rather than dropped in weak quarters. Research and development scores positively and converts commercially — UX Green, Levitas Ultra, the XF/XM series, EV tyres approved by Hyundai, Tata and Renault — which is why the 'Technological Innovation' risk lands at LOW and 'Shifting Consumer Preferences' also lands at LOW: the shifts to EVs, SUVs and larger rim sizes are being captured, with premium 16-inch-and-above tyres growing 13% in FY26.

The trouble is that the same product strengths never become pricing strengths, and this single fact links most of the negative findings together. The 'Pricing, Mix and Volume' check is negative because management itself attributes growth to volume with negligible price, and price increases are reactive pass-throughs of raw-material cost. That same admission is why the 'Pricing Power' pattern is a clear NO FIT — in February 2025 management conceded 4-5% of cost increases went unrecovered because it had to 'abide by the competitive market scenario', and in February 2026 the 15% revenue growth was 'majorly volume' with 'very minor' price — and why the 'Good Enough Goods' risk is ELEVATED. The good work on brand, innovation, dealers and OEM approvals therefore only ever reaches PARTIAL: 'Brand Strength', 'Innovation Dominance', 'Friendly Middleman', 'Forward Integrators' and 'Market Share Gainers' all show real activity but stop short, and in the Friendly Middleman case management notes OEM pricing carries raw-material indexation, so even the expert-specifies channel does not confer price setting. 'Cost to Replicate' is NO FIT for a related reason: nothing in the calls explains why a funded newcomer could not catch up, since capacity is buyable — the company is buying Rs.6,110 crore of it — and protection from Chinese, Vietnamese and Thai supply comes from regulation and anti-dumping duty rather than from anything the company owns. That regulatory dependence is exactly what puts both 'New Entrants' and 'Government Dependency' on WATCH, the latter compounded by management anchoring its demand outlook to Union Budget infrastructure allocation and GST reform in nearly every call. It also explains the weakest quality area of all, Industry Structure at -0.67.

Timing is where the strengths and the risks collide most directly. The 'Capex for growth' check is positive precisely because spending is large, rising and genuinely capacity-additive — Rs.1,100 crore in FY24, Rs.1,130 crore announced in Q3FY26 and a further Rs.4,980 crore brownfield programme running to FY29 — yet the 'Cyclical market growth' check is negative for the same reason: this is a cyclical peak, not a bottom, with record FY26 revenue and profit on full utilisation and an 18-20% raw-material increase already flagged for Q1FY27. The 'Disciplined stewards' entry carries this caveat within itself, noting the new Rs.6,110 crore debt-funded programme is being launched at a cyclical high, and the 'Cyclicality' risk is ELEVATED on the evidence that margins swing violently — 12.5% in Q1FY24, 9.1% in Q3FY25 on a natural-rubber spike, 13.8% in Q3FY26. Funding strain shows elsewhere too: about 90 days of cash is tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and 'Distribution to shareholders' is negative because equity was raised through the December 2023 QIP and expansion is being funded with fresh debt, with no buyback or stated distribution policy — which together with the 0.0 Return on Capital reading and 0.17 Capital Allocation score is what holds business quality back despite the good management record. The main structural cushion is the replacement market at 61-63% of the mix, cited as the silver lining under 'Cyclicality' and as the reason 'Fashion Risk' and 'Stakeholder Concentration' are LOW — but the 'Recurring Revenue' pattern is careful to call it NO FIT, since it is repeat consumable demand sold through dealers, not contracted revenue. Overall resilience is judged SOUND; the reason the verdict is Mixed rather than better is that a capable operator with a broad customer base is competing in a business where it cannot set its own prices.

> "we have secured new approvals from India's leading OEMs including Tata Motors, Ashok Leyland, Mahindra, Switch Mobility, VECV, Euler Motors" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Operating margin of 12% — too thin for a protected franchise.
- ❌ Operating margin moved from about 15% (early years) to 12% (recent years) — margins are ERODING, the opposite of pricing power.

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 1 check supporting).

**What the calls show:** Cost optimisation is a persistent theme across the calls — fixed-cost reduction and digitalisation at Tornel, 'sustained cost optimization initiatives' in FY26, interest-cost reduction despite rising rates — and capacity utilisation above 90% shows asset sweating, but there is no itemised savings culture, no no-frills positioning, and the company competes on premiumisation rather than price leadership.

> "Continuous efforts are being made for achieving operational and cost efficiencies through fixed cost reduction" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹38 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 1.0× a year — not the high-turnover, low-margin machine this pattern needs.

### Brand Strength — PARTIAL
*"The name itself sells"*

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.8× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 66% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ✅ Returns on capital held near 16% (was about 18%) — no slide in what the business earns on its capital.

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across the 11 calls, margins swing violently on raw-material and OEM cycles — consolidated EBITDA margin 12.5% (Q1FY24), 9.1% (Q3FY25 on natural rubber spike and carry-forward inventory), 13.8% (Q3FY26), with RM costs now guided up 18-20% in Q1FY27 on the West Asia crisis; demand is repeatedly framed around government/private capex cycles and CV OEM cycles, and the company is committing Rs.6,110 crore of expansion at record utilisation and record profits — textbook peak-cycle over-investment, debt-funded.

> "raw material prices are expected to go up by 18-20% in Q1FY27 from Q4" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Replacement market is 61-63% of the mix — an operations-linked flow/consumable demand base that is structurally steadier than the OEM/capex-linked portion*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 3 of the last 11 years with swings of ±14 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 9% and 16% (±2.4 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 5% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 11%).

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Pricing power is visibly conditional: in Q3FY25 management admitted 4-5% of cost increases remained unrecovered because it must 'abide by the competitive market scenario', and in Q3FY26 the 15% revenue growth was almost entirely volume with 'very minor' price increases. Cheaper Asian imports and, in Mexico, 'more cost-effective products to mass merchandisers' show the pull toward good-enough price points in a category where dealers heavily influence the end choice.

> "There is still some under recovery of 4%-5% yet to be done. We have to abide by the competitive market scenario" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate premiumisation (fuel-efficient XF/XM series, 16"+ PCR, Levitas Ultra, Shresth Plus) plus OEM fitment approvals, which create a specification-led niche that cheap substitutes cannot easily enter*

- ⚠️ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs fell from about 42% to 39% — pricing is being competed away at the gross level.
- ✅ Operating margin (12%) remains near its historical peak (16%) — no collapse in the premium.
- ✅ Operating margin held (about 15% then 12%) — no drift down under competition.

## What to watch
