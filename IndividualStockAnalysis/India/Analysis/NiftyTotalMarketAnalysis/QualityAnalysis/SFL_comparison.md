**Checks that improved:**

- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Weak → Good. Now: Within the window management focused on integrating rather than acquiring, cut debt materially enough to fund a maiden dividend, and capped its Furlenco contribution at Rs.30 crores to limit dilution — though the group still carries scattered stakes (Furlenco, Staqo, Australia, Spain). | The long view had said: The defining act of the period was a large, leveraged, transformational acquisition plus a QIP and a Furlenco/Staqo diversification — the opposite of organic focus and no-big-M&A, though deleveraging and asset sales since show some repair.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Weak → Good. Now: The group holds complementary regional strongholds — Kurlon in south/east, Sleepwell in north/west — which functions as regional brand dominance, but mattresses carry essentially no switching costs or captive aftermarket. | The long view had said: Mattresses are a one-off consumer purchase with no aftermarket, no captive service revenue and effectively zero switching costs — the company itself had to cut Kurlon prices to stay competitive.
- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: Kurlon was a large, distressed acquisition rather than a small bolt-on of an already-strong target, but it is complementary and post-deal margins clearly improved (mid-single-digit EBITDA at acquisition to ~10% combined India core EBITDA), so execution earns credit while the deal profile does not. | The long view had said: Kurlon was a large, complex, debt+QIP-funded deal of a weak acquiree (mid-single-digit EBITDA) that cut PAT ~49% initially, but management delivered the promised turnaround to ~10% margins by FY26, netting out to neutral versus the 'small, simple, already-strong bolt-on' ideal.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: Leadership moves in the window are family succession (Tushaar Gautam elevated to Vice-Chairman and Joint MD) following the professional CEO's exit, and talent development is referenced only as CSR skill-building, so no internal professional pipeline is evidenced either way. | The long view had said: Senior churn rather than a pipeline — an externally hired India CEO exited within two years and the group CFO changed, with key executive roles concentrated in the promoter family; talent development shows up mainly as CSR, not internal succession.
- **Distribution to shareholders** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: A coherent sequence of deleveraging first and then the first-ever dividend since listing shows discipline, but it is a maiden 20% dividend with no buyback and no stated long-term policy. | The long view had said: First-ever dividend only in FY26 after equity dilution via QIP and no buyback history — a nascent, not yet coherent or valuation-disciplined distribution policy.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: The company is coming off a raw-material deflation trough (TDI down to ~Rs.172, polyol ~Rs.107) that suppressed value growth, with management flagging an identifiable upturn in realisations ahead, though rising input costs are a mixed blessing and Middle East supply risk is acknowledged. | The long view had said: The business rides an unpredictable TDI/polyol input cycle that management admits it cannot forecast, and demand recovery is already partly reflected rather than a clean bottom-of-cycle entry.

*Unchanged: Assurance benefits; Candor; Barriers to entry and rationality; Profit margin; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Gain in market share; Return on capital (headline); Halo effect (caution); Advertisement and promotion; Asset turn; Research and development; Geographical expansion; Distribution advantage; Security by obscurity; Share donators; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Cost of working capital; Technology advantage; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Structural end-market growth; Convenience benefits; Intangible benefits; Mini-monopolies; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Out of limelight; Customer types (retail vs corporate).*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Clear and repeated forward integration in the last 12 months: ~6,000 exclusive brand outlets plus 20,000 touchpoints with ~600 net new showrooms added in FY26, first company-owned company-operated stores planned, brand.com D2C up 136% year-on-year, franchised EBOs in each UAE emirate, and 40+ stores now integrated with Furlenco heading to 100. (The long view had said: A consistent, delivered multi-year march toward the customer: exclusive-brand-outlet expansion from ~5,000 outlets in FY23 to ~600 net new showrooms added in each of FY26's periods, first company-owned company-operated stores, D2C brand.com up 136% in FY26 and e-commerce up 53%, Furlenco shop-in-shops in 40+ integrated stores targeting 100, plus own exclusive retail stores across the Emirates.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The core mattress/foam business is one-off product sale with no attach rates, AMC or renewal language anywhere in the four FY26 calls; the only recurring stream is the Furlenco furniture-rental subsidiary, whose subscriber base and run-rate management now reports every quarter (FY26 revenue INR370cr, +60%, PAT ~INR60cr) and is now being cross-sold inside Sleepwell/Kurlon stores. (The long view had said: Across all 19 calls the business is a one-off durable sale (mattress, foam, cushioning) — no AMC, licence, renewal or subscription stream is ever discussed, and there is no mention of deferred revenue or customer advances funding the business; the only recurring-style model is subsidiary Furlenco's furniture rental, which is discussed as a capital-consuming turnaround, not an installed-base annuity.)

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across FY26 realisations were flat to negative — mattress full-year volume grew 12% against value growth of 10%, foam volume 18% against value 14% — and the one price increase taken (end-Q3) was explicitly a reset tied to input costs firming, with management guiding FY27 prices higher because raw material prices will be higher, i.e. pass-through rather than pricing power. (The long view had said: Price increases are taken and passed through without volume loss (FY26: mattress volume +12%, value +10%; ASPs rising in offline, e-com and U2O with explicit premiumisation), and management says a 5% rise 'is not a determining factor' for a mattress buyer — but the increases are inflation/raw-material recovery of 4–5%, and in FY23–FY24 pricing was flat-to-negative with ASP pressure and margin compression, so this is cost pass-through more than pricing power above inflation.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Demand is replacement/consumer-led (a flow product, not customer capex), but reported value growth swings materially with polyol/TDI prices — H1 FY26 foam value grew only 3% on 8-10% volumes as TDI fell from Rs.196 to Rs.172, then Q4 FY26 foam value jumped 36% on 34% volume as inputs firmed. Earnings optics are therefore commodity-linked even though end demand is stable. (The long view had said: Earnings swing with polyol/TDI commodity prices — management repeatedly says direction is unclear and by FY26 describes INR40-50/kg moves multiple times a month, and consolidated EBITDA margins have ranged from ~7.5% to ~11.5% across the period; the industrial/B2B foam book also tracks auto and furniture customers. Mitigated because the core is replacement-driven consumer mattresses, not customer capex.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Management is explicitly chasing the cheap end — new sub-Rs.10,000 models under both brands and a U2O business growing ~100% — and realizations stayed flat through H1 FY26 with the mix shift called out as a drag, in a category where dealers and distributors (paid via schemes and incentives) heavily influence the consumer's choice. Bounded by the fact that price increases taken late in Q3 FY26 held, with Q4 mattress value growth (13%) matching volume growth (13%). (The long view had said: Management itself says ~85% of the Indian market is unorganized/traditional, with cotton mattresses at INR800-1,200 and local operators covering loose foam undercutting branded product — Tarang at ~INR2,000 and FitRest exist precisely to fight that. The MBO/dealer channel compounds it: Kurlon prices had to be corrected to stay competitive and dealer margin and incentive schemes are an explicit lever, meaning the intermediary strongly influences the sale.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Distribution is deliberately broad — ~6,000 exclusive brand outlets, 20,000+ touchpoints, 8,400 U2O dealers across 5,000 towns, plus e-commerce and Dubai franchise/large-format partners — with no named customer, licensor or distributor driving results. The one watch item is chemical sourcing: management flagged Middle East supply risk for polyol/TDI in May 2026 but reported no disruption and diversified sourcing. (The long view had said: Supplier side is the real exposure: a single named TDI supplier (GNFC) sets the reference price and management flags Middle East supply-chain risk to polyol/TDI availability. Customer and channel concentration is genuinely low — 8,400 U2O dealers in 5,000 towns, ~600 net new showrooms a year, and e-commerce platforms plus brand.com.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Government Dependency (LOW).*

*Unchanged: New Entrants (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
