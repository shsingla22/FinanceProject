# HDB FINANC SER (HDBFS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

HDB Financial Services (HDBFS) is an Indian non-banking finance company (NBFC) started in 2007, majority-owned by its parent HDFC Bank and listed on the NSE and BSE since mid-2025. It lends money to ordinary retail borrowers and small businesses — 23.9 million customers as of June 2026, with an average loan of only about ₹1.66 lakh per customer, so the book is 100% retail and very granular; roughly 74% of loans are backed by collateral such as property, gold or the vehicle/equipment financed. It earns mainly on the spread between what it pays to borrow (NCDs, bank loans, commercial paper, external commercial borrowings, sub-debt and perpetual debt) and what it charges borrowers: net interest income of ₹2,509 crores in the June 2026 quarter at a net interest margin of 8.35%, giving a gross loan book of ₹1,21,846 crores and quarterly profit of ₹785 crores. It operates only in India, through about 1,730 branches across 1,161 cities and towns (over 80% of them outside the 20 largest cities, serving what management calls 'aspirational India'), plus 1.6 lakh+ retail and dealer touchpoints, a 38,000-strong field sales force, tele-calling, DSAs and digital channels including its HDB OnTheGo app and a do-it-yourself online platform.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Enterprise Lending** — Secured and unsecured loans to business customers (individuals and firms) and some salaried people for growth and working capital, sold mainly through branches; it is the largest vertical at about 39% of the loan book.
- **LAP + EBL (the mortgage business, within Enterprise Lending)** — Loans against property and enterprise business loans backed by property, which management calls its mortgage book; it grew 13.2% year-on-year in the June 2026 quarter and is described as on a continuing upward trajectory.
- **Gold loans (within Enterprise Lending)** — Small loans against gold delivered through roughly 500 enabled branches; still a small piece but the fastest-growing one, with the book doubling in FY26 and again over the following year.
- **Unsecured business loans (within Enterprise Lending)** — Business loans given without collateral, mainly to MSME/SME-type customers; this was a trouble spot for asset quality that management says it is now 'over', with disbursements starting to accelerate again.
- **Asset Finance** — Financing for income-generating assets — new and used commercial vehicles, construction equipment and tractors — sourced through OEM tie-ups, dealers and an in-house sales team; about 38% of the book, and the slowest-growing vertical recently (CV book +10% and CE +8% year-on-year in Q1 FY27) as management deliberately reworked its used-CV and new-CV product focus.
- **Consumer Finance** — Loans to individuals to buy two-wheelers, cars, consumer durables (ACs, refrigerators and other 'compressor products'), digital products and other lifestyle items, sourced at the point of sale via OEM and dealer partnerships; about 23% of the book and the strongest grower, up 21% year-on-year with the consumer durables book up over 50%.
- **Credit and collections** — An in-house function rather than a product — a 12,500+ person collections team plus tele-callers, scorecard-driven strategies and, more recently, AI bots for early-bucket customers; it is central to the model, with gross Stage 3 (bad loans) at 2.34% of book and credit cost running around 2.3% of the loan book.
- **Treasury / borrowings** — An independent department that raises and manages the money HDBFS lends out, keeping funding diversified (around 39% NCDs and 39% bank loans, rest CPs, ECBs and perpetual/sub-debt) and capital adequacy strong at 21.29%.
- **BPO services** — A small non-lending business providing back-office/outsourced services; it contributed just ₹14 crores, or 1.9% of total pre-tax profit, in the June 2025 quarter.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## The verdict: Strong — 65 out of 100 ★★★★☆

In one breath: the quality framework finds an average business today (55/100); the risk review found nothing severe. Weighing those together gives 65 out of 100 — strong.

**The exact arithmetic:** Overall = 64% × 55 (business quality) + 36% × 84 (risk safety) = 65 out of 100 → Strong (4 stars).

- **Business quality (55/100):** The 34-check quality framework scored the business +0.20 on its −2 (poor) to +2 (excellent) scale, with 79% of checks backed by evidence; mapped onto 0–100 that is 55 points.
- **Multibagger fit (—):** The foundation test was essentially untestable (only 1 of its 3 checks had data) and no pattern shows any evidence — this pillar is left unscored rather than guessed.
- **Risk safety (84/100):** started from a clean 100; 4 worth watching (−16) → 84 of 100.

### The story in depth

HDB Financial Services comes out of the three reviews as a well-run lender whose single biggest strength and single biggest weakness are the same asset: its physical reach. The quality framework gives its top marks (+2 each) to distribution and to customer convenience — 1,730 branches across 1,161 cities, more than 1.6 lakh retail and dealer touchpoints, 38,000-plus staff at the point of sale and a 12,500-person in-house collections team — and that same network is what makes the Forward Integrators and Cost to Replicate patterns register at all, and what earns the Good Enough Goods risk its 'niche-like protection' caveat in deep-geography, small-ticket lending. But carrying a Rs 1.22 lakh crore loan book on 1,730 branches, 80,000-plus employees and a 3.8% cost-to-assets ratio produces only about 2.5% return on assets and 15% return on equity. That is why Asset turn scores -1 and Return on Capital scores the worst possible -1.0, and why an analyst flagged branch and employee productivity as weaker than peers under the Low Price Plus screen, which ends up NO FIT.

The second thread running through all three records is a deliberate choice of price over volume, and it too cuts both ways. Management held rates through a falling-rate cycle, lifting net interest margin from 7.55% in Q4FY25 to 8.35% in Q1FY27 and delivering profit growth of 38% year-on-year on book growth of only 11.3% — that earns a +1 on Pricing, Mix and Volume and is the entire basis for the Pricing Power pattern scoring PARTIAL. The cost of that discipline is written into the weaker scores: FY26 loan growth of 10.9% fell short of the company's own nominal-GDP-plus-6-7% aspiration, so Market Share Gainers stays PARTIAL with no market-share figures ever cited, and Growth scores just 0.6. More tellingly, HDB had to vacate high-value products such as tractor trailers because returns were competed away, implying rivals accept lower risk-adjusted returns. That single fact drives the -1 on barriers to entry, the worst-possible -1.0 on Industry Structure, and both the New Entrants and Good Enough Goods risks being flagged WATCH.

The risk review is nonetheless the more comfortable half of the rating — 84 out of 100 on risk safety against 55 on business quality, blending to an overall 65 and a Strong four-star grade. Four of the eight risk channels are LOW, and each is offset by something the quality work already credited: extreme granularity (23.9 million customers, top 20 exposures at 0.32% of the book, average ticket around Rs 1.66 lakh) keeps Stakeholder Concentration LOW, while the steady technology cadence that earned +1 scores on growth capex and R&D — DIY disbursements up about 2.2 times, collection bots adding roughly 25bps of early-bucket efficiency, an in-house language model cutting response times 20% — keeps both Technological Innovation and Shifting Consumer Preferences LOW. The live worries are cyclical and regulatory: Asset Finance is about 38% of the book and its Stage 3 bad loans went 3.1% to 4.3% before recovering to the 3.6% area, and as an NBFC, funding costs and credit norms sit with the RBI, with repo-rate moves feeding straight into margin. The offsetting evidence is that the cycle appears to have turned — Gross Stage 3 fell 2.81% to 2.44% to 2.34% and credit cost 2.52% to 2.35% to 2.32%, with management having flagged the commercial-vehicle and unsecured-business-loan stress early and then delivered the promised repair, which is why Management scores 0.83 and Corporate Culture registers as a pattern. Two caveats sit outside the numbers: overall resilience is recorded as UNKNOWN, and the company is the opposite of overlooked, scoring -2 on obscurity as a headline HDFC Bank subsidiary IPO covered by five brokerages and under heavy institutional and regulatory attention.
