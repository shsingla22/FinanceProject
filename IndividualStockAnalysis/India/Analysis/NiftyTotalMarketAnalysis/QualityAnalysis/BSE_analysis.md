# BSE Ltd. (BSE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

BSE Limited runs India's stock exchange business out of Mumbai (Dalal Street) — a 150-year-old marketplace where companies list their shares and bonds to raise money, and where investors and brokers buy and sell those securities. Its customers are listed companies and issuers (main board, SME and startup platforms), trading members/brokers and their retail and institutional clients, mutual fund distributors, and data and index users; over 25 crore investor accounts were registered on BSE as of FY26. It earns money mainly from transaction charges on trades it matches and settles (equity cash, equity derivatives, mutual fund orders and clearing-house fees — Rs. 3,795 crores of FY26's Rs. 5,148 crores total revenue), plus listing fees and book-building fees from issuers, data dissemination, co-location and index fees, and interest/treasury income on its own funds and on clearing-and-settlement money it holds. It operates in India under SEBI regulation, with an additional international arm at GIFT City, Gandhinagar, and reported FY26 net profit of Rs. 2,497 crores on 59% revenue growth.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Listing / primary market (main board, SME and Startup platforms)** — Companies list shares, bonds, commercial paper, municipal bonds, REITs and InvITs on BSE's platforms and pay listing and book-building fees; management calls it a core franchise — Rs. 26.9 lakh crores raised across BSE fundraising platforms in FY26, 255 new listings ranked first globally for IPO listings, and the SME platform alone hit its 700th listing in Feb 2026 (Rs. 14,735 crores raised, ~Rs. 1.8 lakh crores market value).
- **Trading — equity cash segment** — The traditional share-trading market where BSE charges transaction fees on turnover; average daily turnover rose from about Rs. 4,057 crores in Q1 FY23 to a record Rs. 7,950 crores for FY26, though management describes cash volumes as being at 'long-term normalized levels'.
- **Trading — equity derivatives (Sensex, Bankex index options/futures and single stock derivatives)** — Futures and options on BSE's own indices (plus single stock derivatives launched July 2024) are the biggest growth engine and the main reason transaction charges jumped 87% in FY26 — average daily premium turnover reached a record Rs. 19,523 crores in FY26 versus Rs. 8,978 crores in FY25, and management flags SEBI's changing derivatives rules and charge structures as the key swing factor.
- **Currency derivatives** — A smaller trading segment in foreign-exchange contracts, with average daily turnover of about Rs. 24,567 crores in Q1 FY23.
- **Clearing and settlement (ICCL, the clearing corporation)** — The back-end arm that guarantees and settles trades, earning clearing fees (Rs. 33.8 crores in Q1 FY25) plus treasury income on the cash it holds from members (Rs. 62 crores in Q1 FY25); it also funds a Core Settlement Guarantee Fund — 5% of transaction-linked revenue is set aside monthly, taking the fund to Rs. 1,202 crores by Jan 2026.
- **BSE StAR MF (mutual fund distribution platform)** — India's largest mutual fund order-routing platform, used by distributors to place investor buy/sell orders, charging per transaction — it processed 14.1 crore transactions in Q1 FY25 (up 72%) for Rs. 48 crores of quarterly revenue, and its fees are counted inside BSE's transaction charges.
- **Data, co-location and index services (including Asia Index / BSE Index Services)** — Selling market data feeds, renting server space next to the exchange's systems, and licensing indices such as Sensex — grouped as 'other operating income' at Rs. 349 crores in FY26, up 59%; BSE bought out S&P Dow Jones Indices' stake in Asia Index to own the index business fully.
- **Investment / treasury income on own funds** — Interest and returns on BSE's own cash and investments, a meaningful but secondary earnings source — Rs. 84 crores in Q3 FY26, up 47% year on year.
- **India INX and India International Clearing Corporation (GIFT City, Gandhinagar)** — BSE's majority-owned international exchange and clearing house at India's offshore financial centre, quoted at about USD 9.9 billion average daily turnover and 92.2% market share in Q1 FY23, with BSE holding roughly 62% and 59% stakes respectively.
- **BSE Technology (wholly owned subsidiary)** — Builds and supplies trading technology — it is the technology provider for the International Bullion Exchange at GIFT City and for BSE's Electronic Gold Receipt segment, and received SEBI in-principle approval to run a KYC Registration Agency (KRA) and a TReDS receivables-financing platform with no extra capital needed from BSE.
- **Electronic Gold Receipts (EGR)** — A then-planned segment for trading spot gold in India, awaiting final SEBI approval as of Aug 2022 and intended to use BSE Technology's bullion-exchange systems.
- **BSE Ebix Insurance Broking (40% stake via BSE Investments)** — An insurance distribution venture integrated with 23 insurance companies, small in scale — Rs. 8.1 crores of premium collected in the June 2022 quarter, though growing fast.
- **BSE E-Agricultural Markets Limited** — A spot trading platform for physical commodities (agri and steel) working with government buyers — very small, with 926 enrolled members and Rs. 17 crores of trades in the June 2022 quarter.
- **Hindustan Power Exchange (22.61% stake via BSE Investments)** — An associate power-trading exchange that began operating in July 2022 across day-ahead, real-time and green day-ahead segments, reaching over 20% share of the contingency market early on.
- **Investor protection, education and awareness** — A cost/regulatory function rather than a revenue line — funded through BSE's Investor Protection Fund, it ran 16,663 investor awareness programmes covering over 8.5 lakh investors in FY26.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

BSE earns a Strong rating of 71 out of 100, built from 72 for business quality, 87 for multibagger fit and only 50 for risk safety, with overall resilience judged sound. The high pattern score and the middling risk score come from the same source, and that is the heart of the story. BSE is one of two Market Infrastructure Institutions in a licensed business: a would-be rival would need a SEBI exchange and clearing licence, a clearing house carrying a Rs.1,202 crore settlement guarantee fund with stress-tested risk systems, 25 crore registered investor accounts and a member connectivity and colocation estate. That is why the Cost to Replicate and Toll Roads patterns fit, why customers buy assurance rather than price, and why exchange and clearing charges are a tiny fraction of a user's cost yet unavoidable. But the identical licence that keeps others out also hands the regulator the controls: the Government Dependency risk is rated high because the transaction charge structure, expiry-day cycles, index derivatives eligibility, SGF and IPF contributions, closing auction method and STT are all set or reset by SEBI or the Union Budget, and management frames quarters around those decisions. The moat and the biggest single risk are the same fact seen from two sides. The quality framework agrees, scoring Industry Structure lowest of all seven areas at 0.33.

The growth engine is share capture, and it too cuts both ways. Starting from a negligible derivatives base, BSE took index derivatives average daily premium turnover up 118% to Rs.19,523 crore in FY26, put Sensex options among the most traded contracts globally, holds about 60% of the SME segment on the way to its 700th SME listing by February 2026, 92% at India INX, and lifted cash average daily turnover from Rs.4,057 crore in Q1FY23 to a record Rs.7,950 crore. That drove the Market Share Gainers pattern to a strong fit and sits alongside a 47% cash return on capital, meaning Rs.47 of operating cash for every Rs.100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings in the business, with revenue of Rs.5,148 crore in FY26 and margins up from 27% in Q1FY23 to 64%. Yet the risk review flags that a disproportionate share of the FY25-FY26 earnings step-up rests on one trend, the retail index options boom, and that growth runs through a single contract family, Sensex and Bankex options, traded by a small set of large members. That is the Fashion Risk and Stakeholder Concentration pair, and the concentration is visible in the clearing arm too, where one Rs.80 crore outstanding from NSE needed a provision. Underneath sits Cyclicality, rated elevated, because revenue is a levered function of market buoyancy, as the muted quarter of Q1FY23 with a 20% net margin against FY26 shows. The quality framework's lower marks for Growth and Return on Capital, both 0.67, reflect that the same numbers can swing the other way.

How BSE won that share creates the third tension. The Low Price Plus pattern is a partial fit precisely because the derivatives franchise was rebuilt substantially on price, with telescopic slabs that subsidise low-turnover members and per-contract charges still below NSE as of May 2026, and the Good Enough Goods risk says the flip side: execution is close to a commodity chosen by the broker rather than the end investor, so analysts keep asking whether the pricing gap will close, and StAR MF realisation per transaction is under similar pressure. Set against that, real pricing power has been demonstrated repeatedly and stuck without volume loss, through the April 2022 listing fee increase, the May 2024 derivatives charge revision and the July 2025 throttle framework that took colocation revenue from Rs.74 crore to Rs.171 crore. The other partial fits temper rather than contradict the strong ones: the Friendly Middleman verdict notes volume arrives through brokers and mutual fund distributors, so management had to engage brokers to get the second-week expiry onto their platforms, and Forward Integrators recognises the move downstream into StAR MF, StAR NPS, insurance broking, KRA, TReDS and BEAM while noting BSE still sits between issuers and investors. Meanwhile the sustained technology spend that underpins glitch-free operation and the re-engineered clearing risk system, scaled from 3,000 to 27,000 trades per second per member, is both a quality strength and the reason Technological Innovation is on watch, since the business runs a latency and throughput arms race and colocation revenue depends on staying competitive. Management's record is the steadying factor: a debt-free balance sheet, no large acquisitions beyond the small Asia Index buy-in that preceded a near fourfold rise in index revenue and 50 new indices, a self-capped SGF policy dialled down once the buffer was met, and a multi-year derivatives revival pushed through repeated regulatory and SGF setbacks in FY24 and FY25 before it delivered in FY26.

### Innovation Dominance — STRONG FIT
*"Out-inventing the competition"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** Named new products land every single year across the 16 calls — EGR and the bullion exchange tech (2022), Sensex/Bankex expiry redesign and single-stock derivatives (2024), StAR MF 2.0, Asia Index buyout followed by 50 new indices and two RBI-approved debt indices, StAR NPS (Apr 2026) — funded by high incremental margins, with technology investment quantified (ICCL throughput re-engineered from 3,000 to 27,000 trades/sec per member). ## What to watch

- Any SEBI or Budget change to transaction charges, expiry-day cycles, index derivatives eligibility or STT — the Government Dependency risk is the one rated high, and it can reset revenue without warning.
- Whether index options premium turnover holds near the Rs.19,523 crore level, since the Fashion Risk verdict ties the FY25-FY26 earnings step-up disproportionately to the retail index options boom.
- Whether BSE closes its per-contract pricing gap to NSE and what happens to volumes if it does — this is the direct test of the Low Price Plus pattern against the Good Enough Goods risk.
- Concentration in the clearing book and among large members, following the Rs.80 crore NSE receivable that required a provision, as flagged under Stakeholder Concentration.
- Continued growth-oriented technology spend and clearing throughput gains — the Capex for growth check and the Assurance benefits check both rest on it, and the Technological Innovation risk turns on falling behind.
- A repeat of a muted-market quarter like Q1FY23, where a 20% net margin sat against FY26's 64% EBITDA margin, which is the Cyclicality risk showing up in reported numbers.
