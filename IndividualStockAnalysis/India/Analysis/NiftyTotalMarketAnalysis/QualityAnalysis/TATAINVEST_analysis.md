### The story in depth

Tata Investment Corporation earns a Mixed rating of 46 out of 100 (2 stars), and the arithmetic behind that number tells you where the tension sits: business quality scored 43, fit with the multibagger patterns scored just 13, and safety from risk scored 92. In other words, almost everything holding the score up comes from the risk side — the eight-channel review turned up no flagged risks at all — while the business-quality and pattern work both came back weak. That is an unusual shape for a company, and it is best read as a business that is not in visible danger but also is not doing much that the quality framework recognises as compounding.

The single fact that drives most of the quality picture is that the company holds a very large asset base against a very small revenue line: every ₹1 of assets produces only ₹0.01 of sales. That one trait shows up three separate times wearing different clothes. It is why the asset-turn check scores at the bottom (-2). It is also why the profit margin looks spectacular — ₹90 of every ₹100 of sales is operating profit, which earns a positive mark — because the sales figure being measured is tiny relative to what the company owns. And it is why the headline return on capital scores -2: for every ₹100 of capital employed, the business generated only ₹1 of operating cash this year, a 1% cash return. So the strongest-looking number in the whole record (the 90% margin) and the two weakest ones are not independent findings; they are the same structural fact seen from different angles, and a reader who takes the margin as a sign of strength without the turnover alongside it will draw the wrong conclusion. The one genuinely clean positive is working capital: only about a day of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, a short and healthy cycle. Growth is the other soft spot — sales have averaged about 5% a year but arrive lumpily, which scores -1 for persistence.

On the pattern screen, only one of eleven registered anything: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) came through as a quantitative signal, consistent with a steady income stream rather than a lumpy one. The other patterns tested — friendly middleman, toll roads, pricing power, brand strength, forward integrators, market share gainers — all came back as no fit. Two caveats matter here. First, every pattern verdict carries the same note: no conference-call judgement was available. So the low multibagger score of 13 reflects missing management commentary as much as it reflects an absent trait, and the same gap leaves three quality areas — competitive advantage, customer benefits, and management — with no score at all. Second, the industry structure check landed at exactly neutral because operating margins swing by about ±5.8 percentage points a year, with the framework noting that key costs appear to sit outside management's control. That volatility is the link between the risk side and the quality side: no specific risk channel fired, yet the resilience assessment still reads STRAINED, and the swinging margins plus the 1% cash return on capital are the most likely reason why. Clean on named risks, thin on demonstrated earning power.
