# Cipla Ltd. (CIPLA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: PHARMA*

Cipla is a 90-year-old Indian pharmaceutical company that makes and sells medicines — branded prescription drugs, cheaper generic copies of off-patent drugs, over-the-counter consumer health products, and active pharmaceutical ingredients (the bulk chemicals other drugmakers use). It sells to doctors, chemists, distributors, hospitals and retail consumers in India, and to wholesalers, pharmacy chains and business partners in the United States, South Africa, and a group of Europe/Middle East/emerging markets it calls EMEU. It earns money by manufacturing drugs in its own plants (in India, the US, Goa, Bangalore, Mumbai, China and elsewhere) and selling them at a gross margin of roughly 62%–67% after raw-material cost, with respiratory/inhalation drugs, complex peptide injections and other hard-to-copy products carrying the best economics. Quarterly revenue was about INR 7,119 crores in the June 2026 quarter, with India the largest single market followed by North America; its plants are regularly inspected by the US FDA, and inspection outcomes are a recurring business risk management discusses.

**The parts of the business:**

- **One-India (branded prescription)** — Doctor-prescribed branded medicines sold in India, concentrated in long-term 'chronic' therapies such as respiratory, diabetes, cardiac and urology; it is the biggest piece of the company's largest market, with 33 brands each above INR 100 crores of sales, flagship inhaler brand Foracort alone above INR 1,000 crores, and chronic drugs making up about 60% of the India mix.
- **One-India (trade generics)** — Unbranded/low-cost generic medicines sold in India through a distributor-and-chemist network reaching over 1.5 lakh chemists; it was re-plumbed into a direct distribution model in FY25 (which briefly dented growth) and has since delivered double-digit growth on the back of new launches.
- **Consumer Health (Cipla Health)** — Over-the-counter wellness and self-care products people buy without a prescription — Nicotex, Omnigel, Cipladine and similar brands, each number one in its segment; described as roughly an INR 600 crore annualised franchise in 2022 growing since via acquisitions (Endura Mass, Astaberry, Inzpera), with operating margins in the mid-teens.
- **North America** — Generic and 505(b)(2) specialty medicines sold in the US, led by inhalers (albuterol MDI, where Cipla is the number one player at about 21% market share, plus the newly approved generic Ventolin) and peptide injections like Lanreotide and Liraglutide; annual revenue was about $780 million in FY26 and it is the second-largest market after India.
- **One Africa / South Africa** — Prescription medicines and over-the-counter brands sold in South Africa and other African markets, where Cipla holds the number one ranking in prescription drugs and consistently grows faster than the local market; it grew 14% year-on-year in the March 2026 quarter and 7% for the full year in dollar terms.
- **EMEU (Europe, Middle East and emerging markets)** — A collection of smaller international markets served through both 'DTM' (Cipla's own front-end selling directly to those markets) and B2B (supplying other companies who sell under their own labels); it crossed $400 million of annual revenue in FY26 despite war-related disruption.
- **API** — Sale of active pharmaceutical ingredients — the raw drug substances — to outside partners, sometimes with profit-sharing on commercial supply; it is a small, lumpy contributor whose swings management has had to explain away in quarterly comparisons.
- **R&D and pipeline** — In-house development plus in-licensing deals (Eli Lilly for obesity drug Yurpeak, MannKind for inhaled insulin Afrezza, rights to Pfizer's established brands) that feed future launches; R&D runs about 5%–7% of revenue (INR 486 crores in the June 2026 quarter) and the US pipeline alone covers roughly 40–50 filings planned over three years, including respiratory, peptides, oligonucleotides and two biosimilars.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Cipla's quality profile rests on one engine that the framework, the pattern screen and the risk review all describe in the same terms: a branded prescription business sold through a distribution network the checks call hard to replicate — the largest India volume player at over 2 billion units, direct coverage of more than 1.5 lakh chemists after distributor layers were removed, the No.1 South African prescription rank and a $400m-plus EMEU footprint. That same trait shows up three times over. It scores +2 on both Distribution advantage and Convenience benefits; it is what makes Friendly Middleman and Brand Strength the two STRONG FIT patterns (a 90-year franchise, 24 brands above INR100cr in FY25 rising to 33, Foracort past INR1,000cr, No.1 consumer positions in Nicotex, Omnigel and Cipladine); and it is why three of the eight risk channels come back LOW. Demand here is functional and repeated — doctors write chronic prescriptions, households rebuy consumer brands — so Cyclicality, Fashion Risk and Shifting Consumer Preferences are all judged benign, with the obesity shift described as something Cipla moved onto through the in-licensed Eli Lilly molecule rather than something that displaced it.

The second engine is the harder-won one, and it is where the strengths and the risks are literally the same fact seen from two sides. The checks give +2 for tenacity: the respiratory franchise was pursued through the Indore OAI, a full tech transfer to US sites and a PAI, ending in the first AB-rated generic Ventolin with four assets queued. That persistence produced the +2 on Gain in market share — US albuterol moving 17% to 19.6% to 21% and No.1, India chronic mix at 60.4%, South African secondaries beating the market in every period cited. But the risk review reads the identical evidence as exposure. New Entrants is a WATCH precisely because the positions being gained are the ones competitors compete away: albuterol share was ceded before it was recaptured, price erosion has been flagged since Q1FY23, and a peptide ANDA has to ramp slowly because share must be taken from someone. Government Dependency is a WATCH for the same reason the tenacity check scores well — regulatory classification, not demand, set the timing, with Indore delaying Advair for years and Goa gating Abraxane. Stakeholder Concentration follows: Revlimid, Lanreotide and albuterol explain most US movement, and FY27 guidance is issued explicitly without Lanreotide. Pricing Power lands only at LIKELY FIT for the same split — calibrated price rises in branded markets, acknowledged erosion in US generics.

Financially the picture is steady rather than spectacular, and the scoring gap between a 70 on business quality and an 87 on pattern fit is where that shows. Sales have compounded around 9% a year very consistently, ₹66 of every ₹100 of sales survives direct input costs and ₹21 reaches operating profit with movement of only about ±2.6 points a year — controlled, but the Growth and Profit margin checks score +1, not +2. The balance sheet is the clearest management strength: four years of near-zero debt at roughly INR600 crores, mostly leases, against INR9,500-10,500 crores of net cash, an explicit preference for organic investment and no large acquisitions, earning +2 on Disciplined stewards. That cash is what funds the defensive spending the Technological Innovation WATCH describes — R&D rising from 5.1% of sales in Q1FY23 to 7.8% in Q4FY26, INR1,974cr in FY26, a 40-50 product filing pipeline, an in-house biosimilars JV against a $200 billion opportunity on a 5-8 year horizon. Against that, Cost of working capital scores -2 with about 202 days of cash stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and the weakest area scores are Industry Structure, Capital Allocation and Return on Capital — the same places the five WATCH risks cluster. The combined verdict is Strong, 78 out of 100 and four stars, with resilience judged SOUND.
