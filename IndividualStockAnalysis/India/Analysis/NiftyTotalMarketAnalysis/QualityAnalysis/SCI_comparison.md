**Checks that improved:**

- **Candor** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Disclosure is unusually specific and non-promotional: the prior quarter's profit was explained away as a one-off tax refund, the liner miss attributed to ECL provisions, four vessels admitted stuck in Hormuz, and the CMD declined to guide on April. | The long view had said: Candid about limits — declining to guide on April, admitting stuck vessels and unquantifiable impact — but weak on specifics (couldn't state the dry-docking schedule) and the prior call's 2-3x revenue/50% margin target was conceded to be a stretch aspiration rather than a plan.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: Management is technocratic and plainspoken and had not held a call 'after a long time', but the narrative leans heavily on PM announcements, Navratna status and TV visibility rather than quiet substance. | The long view had said: Management leans heavily on high-profile national publicity — the PM launching the business plan, India Maritime Week, vessels named on TV — and uses flag-waving rhetoric rather than low-key substance.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Neutral → Weak. Now: No talent-development system is described, and the Nov 2025 board showed directors holding multiple additional charges (Finance, Bulk Carriers) — a thin, vacancy-patched bench rather than a pipeline. | The long view had said: There is a training-institute upgrade with an international ship manager, but the board shows thin bench with directors carrying multiple additional charges and CFO/Finance-director turnover between the two calls.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: The customer base is correctly corporate/state (oil PSUs, Navy, ONGC) and sold on reliability, but there is no switching-cost lock-in — contracts are re-tendered and time charters reprice with the market. | The long view had said: The customer base is almost entirely corporate/government (ONGC, Indian Navy, DRDO, IOC/BPCL/HPCL) served on long-term time charters that cover standing cost, interest and return — the right TCO/reliability match for that customer type.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: The coastal liner and government-managed-vessel business shows captive-like economics (99% utilisation, freight +10% y/y), but ocean tonnage faces no switching costs and is tendered competitively. | The long view had said: Cabotage-protected coastal trade plus captive PSU relationships (ONGC, Navy, DRDO long-term charters; OMC firm commitments into the JV) create a regional/partial monopoly, though switching costs in global shipping remain low.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: The Indian container gap is being filled by strong global lines, not weak donors, and the calls identify no faltering competitor SCI can take share from. | The long view had said: Foreign shipowners currently carry almost all Indian cargo — ~$80bn of annual freight outgo and a negligible Indian container fleet — giving SCI clearly identified donators, backed by policy intent to redirect 25-30% initially.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: Profit margins swing by ±4.9 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Entry needs only capital to buy ships, freight is set by public indices (BDI, worldscale, TD3) rather than by rational industry pricing, and management concedes asset prices inflate in lockstep with rates. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±6.0 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Freight is set by transparent global indices with no pricing discretion, and government subsidy schemes are actively lowering barriers by building new domestic shipbuilding and tonnage capacity.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Weak. Now: No technology edge: an average fleet age of 15.5 years, a single government-funded methanol dual-fuel pilot, and 'digital automation' of routine liner tasks fall well short of a multi-product tech advantage. | The long view had said: Some technical positioning (DP2-class offshore vessels, digital automation in liner, a methanol dual-fuel pilot) but no proprietary, multi-product technology edge — vessels are bought from HHI and MDL like any other owner.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: There is a large stated runway in Indian-flag container tonnage, but no demonstrated capture in the window — liner revenue actually fell from INR1,036 crore to INR784 crore and segment profit more than halved. | The long view had said: Very large runway with policy support — India's freight outgo is ~$80bn and the Indian container fleet is negligible — but share capture is so far only aspirational demand aggregation, not delivered.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Weak → Poor. Now: Global tanker and bulk shipping is a fragmented price-taker market with charterers holding all negotiating power, as management describes directly. | The long view had said: Global shipping is fragmented with many owners chasing the same cargo, and customers price via published global indices and tenders, leaving SCI a price-taker rather than part of a stable oligopoly.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: There is a genuine contracted layer — 7 of 10 offshore support vessels on long-term charters with ONGC/Navy/DRDO (recently repriced ~50% higher for three years), some tankers on time charter with Indian industry, and 40 third-party vessels managed for government organisations — but the calls disclose no renewal rates, attach rates, deferred revenue or advance billing, and the bulk of earnings is spot freight. (The long view had said: Revenue is voyage/spot and time-charter freight, not an installed base with attached recurring streams; management confirmed the 40 managed government vessels contribute almost nothing (cost-plus management fees only), and in May 2026 disclosed ~60% of the tanker fleet is on voyage (spot) with 40% on time charter, with revenue recognised on percentage-of-voyage-completion rather than billed in advance.)

**That weakened:**

- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Nothing here a funded newcomer could not replicate: ships are open-market assets bought via tender from Hyundai/MDL, rates are set by global indices, and management itself notes the whole industry is bidding for the same cargo. The only non-replicable element is government sponsorship, which is a policy allocation rather than an earned barrier. (The long view had said: There is one un-replicable element — sovereign ownership plus cabotage giving an effective monopoly on coastal crude movement and privileged access to PSU demand aggregation and the Maritime Development Fund — but the core earning assets are globally tradeable second-hand ships any funded entrant can buy, and management concedes its 15-year-old fleet earns less than newer competing tonnage.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Innovation Dominance (NO FIT).*

*Not comparable: Low Price Plus (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Named state counterparties recur as the driver of the non-spot book — ONGC, Indian Navy and DRDO on 7 of 10 offshore vessels, oil PSUs for the tanker JV, plus time charters 'with the Indian industry' and liner ECL provisions against 'few customers'; offset by a genuinely spread revenue base across tanker, bulk, liner, coastal and offshore, with the bulk of tanker earnings from the open spot market. (The long view had said: The growth story rests on a handful of named state counterparties — IOC/BPCL/HPCL for the 59-vessel JV (one VLGC already on a five-year charter to IOC), CONCOR and major ports for Bharat Container Shipping Line, and ONGC/Navy/DRDO for seven of ten offshore vessels — while a liner profit shortfall was attributed to ECL provisions on 'few customers'.)
- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No disruption threat is raised on either call; technology appears only as incremental, value-adding steps — digital automation of commercial/day-to-day liner activities and a single pilot methanol dual-fuel PSV ordered from Mazagon Dock under the Green Hydrogen Mission. (The long view had said: Fuel-transition technology is a real but incremental issue: SCI is ordering a methanol dual-fuel diesel-electric PSV under the National Green Hydrogen Mission and discussing green tugs, while its owned fleet averages 15.5 years and management concedes older tonnage fetches inferior charters — an installed base slowly becoming legacy rather than a platform shift.)
- **Fashion Risk**: QUANT FLAG → LOW. Why now: Demand is functional freight haulage of crude, LPG, coal, grain and containers, driven by cargo availability and route economics; nothing in either call ties results to taste, trend or brand appeal. (The long view had said: 1 numeric fingerprint is present but the calls offer nothing either way — numbers alone are only ever a flag to investigate, never a verdict.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (NO SIGNAL); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
