# Prestige Estates Projects Ltd. (PRESTIGE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSTRUCTION*

Prestige Estates Projects Limited is an Indian real estate developer that buys land, builds and sells homes — apartments and plotted developments — to individual buyers, and also builds and holds income-earning commercial property. Most of its money comes from residential sales: it launched over 31 million sq ft in FY26 and booked record presales of more than INR30,000 crores (revenue INR13,196 crores, PAT INR1,312 crores), with revenue recognized only when completed units are handed over. Alongside this, it keeps a growing 'annuity' portfolio — office buildings and shopping malls it leases out, plus hotels it operates — that produces recurring rent and room income. It operates across Bangalore, Mumbai, Hyderabad, Chennai, NCR (Noida, Indirapuram, Gurgaon, Delhi) and Goa, and says it has completed over 200 million sq ft across 300+ projects since inception.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Residential (development / presales)** — Builds and sells apartments, integrated 'Prestige City' townships and plotted developments to home buyers; this is the dominant business, with FY26 sales of over INR30,000 crores across 22.28 million sq ft, average realization around INR14,459 per sq ft, and unrecognized (already sold, not yet booked) revenue of INR61,922 crores as of December 2025.
- **Commercial office** — Develops and leases office space to GCCs, tech companies and domestic corporates, with occupancy above 92%–95%; FY26 exit rental income is about INR829 crores and management projects office annuity income scaling to roughly INR4,000 crores by FY30.
- **Retail (malls)** — Owns and rents out shopping malls, earning rent from tenants and tracking mall footfalls and shopper spending — occupancy near 99%, Q3 FY26 gross turnover of INR702 crores, exit rentals of about INR275 crores for FY26, with 14 more malls in the pipeline targeting roughly INR1,175 crores by FY30.
- **Hospitality** — Owns and runs hotels under brands like Marriott, Moxy and St. Regis, earning room and hotel revenue — FY26 top line of about INR1,050 crores with hotel-level EBITDA near INR440 crores, average room rate around INR14,000 and occupancy above 60%; new Delhi hotels plus an office block are due to open around Diwali.
- **Business development / land acquisition** — The land-buying arm that feeds everything else — it acquires land outright or through joint development deals with landowners; in FY26 it added a project pipeline worth over INR50,000 crores of gross development value, spending heavily on parcels in Hyderabad and Chennai, with about INR4,500 crores budgeted for the next year.
- **REIT holding (Nexus REIT)** — A financial investment the company holds rather than an operating business; its market value swings flow through profit, and a mark-to-market loss on it cut Q3 FY25 PAT to just INR32 crores.

### The story in depth

Prestige comes out of all three reviews as a genuinely good business trading at the top of its own cycle, which is why the combined rating lands at 58 out of 100, or "Decent" — 45% weight on business quality (61), 30% on multibagger fit (55) and 25% on risk safety (56), with overall resilience judged sound. The single strongest thread running through every record is the brand. The quality framework gives full marks to advertising efficiency because projects sell themselves — Ocean Towers moved roughly 40% of its inventory "without any advertising" — and the pattern screen rates Brand Strength a strong fit on the back of three decades, 300 completed projects and 200 million square feet that a new competitor cannot buy. That same brand is what makes the risk review comfortable dismissing New Entrants, Good Enough Goods and Shifting Consumer Preferences as low risks: management was asked directly about competitive intensity in May 2026 and reported no need to compete on terms, and realisations rose every year from INR10,143 per square foot to INR14,459, which is the evidence behind the Pricing Power pattern as well.

The brand also powers the growth story, and the growth story carries its own shadow. Presales rose from about INR17,000 crore to over INR30,000 crore in FY26, a 76% jump, earning top marks for market share gain and geographical expansion — a maiden Mumbai residential completion, a maiden NCR launch, plus Hyderabad, Chennai, Goa and Gurgaon. But that single NCR project, Indirapuram, sold INR9,500 crore on its own, roughly a third of the entire year's sales, which is precisely why the risk review flags stakeholder concentration as a watch item. The trait that produced the year's headline number is the same trait that makes next year's number hard to repeat. Two risks are rated high outright and they are closely linked: cyclicality and government dependency. The FY25 shortfall — presales falling from INR16,333 crore in the first nine months of FY24 to INR10,065 crore a year later, before rebounding to INR22,327 crore in FY26 — was caused purely by approval and RERA delays, with the Chairman flying to Lucknow to lobby an authority and conceding "I can't fight. I have to just comply." The quality framework's high mark for being independent, long-term and tenacious, citing multi-year grinds through Sector 150, Indirapuram and the 4,041-home Jijamata Nagar rehabilitation, is best read as the management response to that exact risk rather than as something separate from it.

The weakest area of the record is what the company is doing with its cash, and here again one decision explains several scores at once. Prestige is building an annuity business it owns and operates: malls at 99% occupancy generating INR702 crore of quarterly transaction value, offices, and hotels it runs under Marriott and St. Regis flags with its own appointed general managers. That earns a likely fit on the Forward Integrators pattern and a partial fit on recurring revenue, with office rentals guided from INR829 crore to about INR4,000 crore and retail from INR275 crore to about INR1,175 crore by FY30 — and it is named as the main cushion against the high cyclicality risk. The cost of building it, though, shows up everywhere else: INR4,000-4,500 crore of annual capital spending and INR2,700 crore of land bought in a single quarter make this structurally asset-heavy, with every rupee of assets producing just INR0.17 of sales, which is the worst score in the framework and drags return on capital and capital allocation into negative or near-zero territory. It is also why management has explicitly deferred any dividend or buyback until the annuity programme is finished. Finally, the framework marks the cycle position down because management itself calls INR30,000 crore the peak and guides to a decelerating 15-20% growth off that high base — so the record's own view is that the strengths are real but the starting point is a good year, not a bad one.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Government Dependency. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Residential development is capex-led and the trajectory across the nine calls shows violent swings: 9M FY24 presales of INR16,333cr (+81%) collapsed to INR10,065cr in 9M FY25 when approvals stalled, then rebounded to INR22,327cr in 9M FY26 (+122%); EBITDA margin drifted from 43% (9M FY24) to 38% (9M FY25) to 34.3% (9M FY26) with one quarter at 22.5%, and management itself concedes lumpiness. Land buying is pro-cyclical — INR2,700cr deployed in a single quarter at the peak, INR50,000cr GDV added in FY26 — and management admits it is now 'at the peak' and must 'take stock'.

> "one quarter will be more, one quarter will be less. Like in one quarter, we had done INR8,000 crores, another quarter, we did INR3,000 crores" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Growing annuity base (office to ~INR4,000cr and retail ~INR1,175cr by FY30, 92-99% occupancy) plus hospitality gives a rising operations-linked, non-development income stream; residential is described as self-liquidating with no structural debt need.*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 6 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 19% and 34% (±4.7 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 19% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 36%).

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Revenue timing is set almost entirely by regulators: the FY25 shortfall was caused purely by RERA/approval delays, with the Chairman flying to Lucknow to lobby an authority and complaining an authority 'is overstepping... I can't fight. I have to just comply'; state elections cost three quarters of approvals, land is increasingly bought from government auctions/corporate deals, and new ventures depend on a Maharashtra government land allotment and the DIAL airport concession.

> "the market is there. The customer is there. Only thing is we need help from the powers" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Diversification across five states and four asset classes means no single regulator controls the whole book, and by FY26 management notes 'a brand-new dispensation, which is talking all positive things' in Tamil Nadu; pricing itself is market-determined, not regulated.*

## What to watch
