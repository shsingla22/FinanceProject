- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹60 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±2.8 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹60 is operating profit. ### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 2 weakened, 3 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: FAIL on the long view, FAIL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Growth AUM has compounded in the low-30s% for several consecutive quarters (34% in Q3FY26, 33% in Q4, 32% in Q1FY27) with disbursements up 44% year-on-year, far above sector growth, and management claims the fastest retail scale-up among NBFCs — critically, the share is not being bought with underwriting: retail 90+ DPD is 0.6-0.8%, unsecured risk at a two-year best, and Wholesale 2.0 has zero NPAs. (The long view had said: Persistent, well-above-industry share gain across all three calls — retail AUM +33-34% y-o-y to INR 85,885cr, growth book +32-34% for many consecutive quarters, customer franchise 5.4mn to 6.0mn, with management explicitly benchmarking the scale-up against peer NBFCs; critically the growth is not bought with underwriting, since retail 90+ DPD stayed in a 0.6-0.8% band for four-plus years, credit cost fell to 1.5-1.6% and Wholesale 2.0 has zero NPAs.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: No licences, AMCs or subscriptions, but the installed base of 6mn customers is monetised through cross-sell that management explicitly says carries better unit economics; cross-sell is ~28-30% of unsecured disbursement with an ambition to raise it materially. There is no discussion of customer advances or deferred revenue funding the business (as a lender it is the opposite). (The long view had said: the calls are silent and the numbers are mixed (2 of 3 checks supporting).)
- **Forward Integrators**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Owns its distribution and is deliberately deepening it — 701 branches at FY26 end rising to 780, with 22 → 67 gold-loan branches targeting 200 by March 2027 and rural branches 136 → 178, plus urban branch consolidation showing active format management. This is own-channel control rather than a manufacturer integrating forward, and there is no franchising or royalty economics. (The long view had said: the calls are silent and no computable check supports the pattern.)

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Yields are flat at 13.2-13.4% with no price increases discussed, total retail income fell 26bps year-on-year, and management concedes that merely holding price firm in a 'tightening competitive environment on rates' cost them disbursement volume in Q3 — the opposite of raising price without losing volume. (The long view had said: Across all three calls they defended price in a falling-rate market — retail PLR never cut through the entire down-cycle, PL yields held above 17% while peers offered single digits, wholesale yield stable at 14.2-14.5% — with volumes still compounding 32-44% and BT-out sub-10%; but this is price defence, not price increases above cost inflation, and management concedes cyclical margin compression is likely if RBI cuts further.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Nothing in these calls is un-replicable by a well-funded newcomer — the advantages cited (AA+ rating, branch count, AI stack, cost ratios) are all buyable or buildable with capital and a few years, and management's own boast that Piramal is 'the youngest NBFC by some margin' in the upper layer, having built an INR85,000cr retail book from INR20,000cr in four years, is itself proof that the barrier to entry here is low. (The long view had said: Real but rebuildable barriers: the AA+ rating (worth 50-80bps of borrowing cost and access to markets previously closed), a 780-branch semi-urban network, a 6mn-customer cross-sell base with lower opex and credit cost, and a proprietary AI stack — yet the company's own history is the counter-evidence, having built this book from INR20,000cr in roughly five years off the DHFL acquisition, which is precisely what a well-funded entrant could attempt.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Lending economics swing with the rate and credit cycle — management tracks MCLR cuts, transmission into cost of borrowing (8.84% → 8.80%), and flagged that Q1 FY27 wholesale funding markets were 'a bit dislocated', while ~13% of AUM is real-estate/mid-market wholesale tied to developer capex; offsetting this, the retail book (85% of AUM) behaves like a flow book with 90+ DPD held in a 0.6–0.8% band for four-plus years. (The long view had said: Lending economics here swing with the rate and credit cycle and with third-party activity levels: management calls margin compression from RBI cuts 'cyclical, not structural', flags Q3 disbursement flattening as 'internal process-driven cyclicality', and describes the fast-growing digital/fintech partnership book as volume-volatile (cut >60% in FY25, now at 'historically high volumes'); the wholesale book lends against developer project capex, where prepayment swings are already a 'major growth headwind'. Offsetting this, the retail book is 85% of AUM, spread over six products and 6mn customers, with 90+ DPD held in a 0.6-0.8% band for four-plus years.)
- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Management explicitly attributed flat Q3 retail disbursements partly to holding pricing firm against rate-based competition, indicating banks and other lenders are competing directly on price in its segments; the AA+ upgrade is the stated answer, opening mass-affluent home loans and larger-ticket LAP where competition is keenest. (The long view had said: Analysts pressed directly on excess capital and mid-sized banks crowding into retail/SME, and management concedes a 'tightening competitive environment on rates' that already dented Q3 disbursements, with single-digit PL rates being offered by others; in wholesale, banks 'taking us out' via refinance is a stated growth headwind. These are well-capitalised incumbent banks and fintechs rather than a wholly new industry format, and the company competes in semi-urban 'Bharat' segments with a rating-driven funding cost improvement in hand.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Credit is a substitutable product with little end-customer brand attachment and DSA/intermediary-influenced choice, so the franchise competes largely on rate — yields sit in a narrow 13.2–13.4% band and holding price firm immediately cost volume in Q3; the defence is cost structure rather than product differentiation. (The long view had said: Credit is a substitutable product and cheaper capital is the good-enough substitute: management is repeatedly defending price, holding PL yields at 17%+ while 'single-digit interest rates' are offered elsewhere, declining to cut PLR through the whole down-cycle, and fielding questions on balance-transfer-out attrition; in wholesale, banks refinance borrowers away. Protection comes from the underserved semi-urban/budget segment and low observed attrition rather than from product differentiation.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (QUANT FLAG); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
