And from the calls: Low barriers and irrational competition are explicit — a slew of new players, NFO-driven distributor commission escalation and telescopic TER regulation that keeps grinding yields down.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Weak → Good. ### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 5 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: AMC economics are annuity-like on an installed base, and management frames the business exactly that way across all four calls — a systematic (SIP) book of INR 37.2 bn/month (~INR 446 bn annualised), 24.1 mn unique investors, and digital initiatives explicitly aimed at re-engaging terminated SIPs to protect persistence; the stream is capital-light and scales with installed-base density, though no upfront product sale or deferred-revenue funding is discussed. (The long view had said: Revenue is genuinely recurring and asset-light — an annualised systematic book of INR 447bn, 24.1mn investors ('1 in 3 mutual fund investors'), and unusually sticky vintages — but there is no upfront product sale, no attach/renewal-rate framing, and no deferred revenue or customer advances funding the business.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Share gain is explicit, quantified and monotonic across all four calls — MF market share 8.51% → 8.65% → 8.89% → 9.04%, equity share 7.13% → 7.38%, ETF share +234 bps year-on-year — and it is not bought with profitability: FY26 operating profit rose 24% and Q1 FY27 PAT 27% year-on-year, with equity net sales share moving to double digits. (The long view had said: Persistent, quantified share gain across all 16 calls without buying it: overall MF share rose 7.40% (Q1FY23) → 8.29% (Q2FY25) → 9.04% (Q1FY27), ETF share 14.1% → 21.4%, SIP share ~6% (Mar-23) → 9.84%, while FY26 PAT grew 19% and Q1FY27 PAT 27% year-on-year — and management explicitly says it does not chase share via NFOs.)

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Long-tenured MD/CEO Sundeep Sikka with a stable senior bench, a capital-light balance sheet returning ~91.5% of FY26 profit as dividend, and 'fastest growing AMC in the Top-10' claims that were repeated and then delivered in each successive quarter; but the excerpts contain no instance of owning a mistake or disclosing bad news, and the CFO seat turned over (Prateek Jain exited FY23). (The long view had said: Sundeep Sikka has led throughout the 5-year window and the calls show promises kept (frugality guidance in FY23, market-share and 'profitable growth' pledges delivered by FY26/FY27), candid bad news (flat AIF/managed-account book, flattening SIP net flows, falling ETF folio share), self-imposed discipline (small-cap cap, voluntary bullion inflow restrictions, no NFOs), and a consistent ~91-96% dividend payout.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Index funds and ETFs are the classic good-enough substitute for active management — the same market exposure at a fraction of the fee — and they are taking a growing share of industry flows while the intermediary (distributor or digital platform) largely controls which product the low-brand-awareness retail investor buys. Management never defends a price premium in these calls; the mix is drifting toward index/ETF/debt-index products where the fee ceiling is low and scale, not brand, decides the winner. (The long view had said: This is the dominant, repeatedly evidenced risk: cheap index funds and ETFs are the good-enough substitute for active management at a third of the fee, telescopic pricing mechanically shrinks realisation as AUM grows, and the distributor — the intermediary who controls the retail investor's choice — captures fee share, with management guiding to 1-2bps of blended yield erosion every year and equity yields having drifted from 58bps (FY25) to 53-54bps (FY27). Fourteen of sixteen calls feature an analyst asking why yields fell.)
- **New Entrants**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Across all four calls management never frames pricing or spend as a competitive response to a new-format entrant, and the outcome data argues the opposite direction: market share rose every quarter from 8.51% to 9.04% (highest since June 2019), NAM was the fastest-growing top-10 AMC in each period, and equity net sales share moved from high single digits to double digits — the incumbent is taking share, not defending it. (The long view had said: Management explicitly attributes structural yield pressure to new AMCs entering and bidding up distributor economics, and a peer's back-book brokerage cut was put to them as a competitive question; fintech platforms are also a structurally different distribution format now shaping SIP behaviour with shorter holding cycles.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Revenue is a fee on AUM that moves directly with equity markets: Q4 FY26 saw the NIFTY fall 14.5% quarter-on-quarter and Mid/Small Caps ~13-14%, pushing total AUM down from INR 8.16 trn to 7.73 trn and turning other income negative, before a Q1 FY27 rebound (Small Cap +24% quarter-on-quarter) lifted AUM to 8.62 trn and PAT to a record INR 5.04 bn — a 31% quarter-on-quarter PAT swing driven by markets, not operations. Reported profitability is being measured at an elevated point in an equity bull cycle, and management gives no way to size how much of the 24% FY26 operating-profit growth is cycle. (The long view had said: Revenue is a fee on market-linked AUM, so both the top line and other income swing with equity markets — Q1 FY23 PAT fell 37% on a market drawdown, and Q4 FY26 saw NIFTY -14.5% quarter-on-quarter with other income turning negative INR 0.34bn — but the underlying demand is operations-like recurring SIP flow rather than customer capex, and the systematic book kept compounding (INR 447bn annualised) straight through the FY26 correction.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Government Dependency (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Technological Innovation (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Fashion Risk (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
