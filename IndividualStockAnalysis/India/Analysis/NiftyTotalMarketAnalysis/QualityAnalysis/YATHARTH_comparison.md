### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 5 weakened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Each call names new clinical technology with revenue relevance (robotic Intelli Hip revision, Rapid AI stroke imaging, Da Vinci in Agra, POEM procedures) that supports higher ARPOB, but this is purchased third-party equipment adoption — no R&D spend, pipeline or share-of-industry-R&D is disclosed. (The long view had said: What is described as innovation is purchased capability — Da Vinci robots, PET/radiation lines, imported machines — all available to any funded competitor; no R&D spend, pipeline, or proprietary development is disclosed in any of the 13 calls.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Growth is well above sector rates and repeated across all four calls (FY26 revenue +36% to INR12,072 million; Q3 +46% with mature hospitals still +33%), with capacity to 3,200 beds and a 5,000-bed target — but no explicit market-share numbers or comparisons versus named competitors are given. (The long view had said: Persistent, measurable share gain across the whole 2023-2026 window: beds from 1,405 to over 3,200 with a 5,000 target, revenue from INR5,203mn (FY23) to INR12,072mn (FY26), Q4FY26 growth of 47% year-on-year with mature hospitals still compounding 33% — well above sector growth — plus stated leadership in Faridabad and entry into Delhi, Agra and Gurugram clusters. The quality caveat: gains are heavily acquisition-led, though new units reach breakeven fast and payer mix improved (government cut toward <30%) rather than being bought with bad-quality revenue.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Growth is explicitly attributed to onboarding star clinicians and super-specialists whose reputation pulls patients, and payment largely comes from third parties (TPA/insurance, CGHS) rather than the patient negotiating price — but the calls show no trade-training programmes or formal referrer channel investment. (The long view had said: Demand is visibly clinician-led — the group repeatedly frames growth as onboarding 'star'/renowned doctors and HODs who bring their clientage, and cites DNB teaching courses as a doctor-retention/attraction device, while insurers, government schemes and patients pay. But there is no formal trade-training or referral-channel programme described, and no attempt to quantify doctor-driven switching costs.)
- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The company owns and operates all its delivery points and is pushing further toward the patient — exclusive hospital partner for the Jewar/Noida International Airport, representative offices in Baghdad and Tashkent, and overseas OPD camps — though there is no franchising, licensing or royalty economics. (The long view had said: Yatharth owns the land, structure and building of its units outright (SARFAESI purchase of Model Town, Faridabad, Gurugram Sector 40 near IGI) and runs everything itself — no franchising, licensing or third-party-funded growth — so it controls pricing and experience but funds all expansion off its own balance sheet, which is the opposite of the franchising variant.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The stated strategy is the opposite of low-price-plus: management repeatedly pushes case mix toward high-value super-specialty work, targets ARPOB above group average at new units, and guides to an INR 50,000+ ARPOB premium asset in Gurugram; no cost-obsession culture or itemised savings are discussed. (The long view had said: There is a genuine cost culture — consumables held at 18-19% of topline versus peers, support services (housekeeping, F&B) kept in-house to save vendor service charges and tax, and low capex per bed (~INR60 lakh) on older assets — and this underpins ~28-30% adjusted EBITDA margins. But the strategy is explicitly upmarket (rising ARPOB, super-specialty mix), not low price plus scale.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The calls themselves show the advantage is replicable with capital: Yatharth built or bought 700 beds plus a 250-bed Agra hospital and turned a new facility profitable within a year, and is buying an under-construction Gurugram asset for ~INR200 crores — a well-funded NCR competitor could do the same; no time-, data- or location-scarcity barrier is articulated. (The long view had said: Some barriers are real and time-based — finite prime-location land in NCR banked over years, a 600+ doctor clinical ecosystem, JCI accreditation, DNB teaching status, and the exclusive Noida International Airport partnership — and management notes a new hospital normally takes ~15 months of cash losses to reach profitability. But nothing here stops a well-funded chain (Apollo, Max, Fortis) from buying land and equipment and hiring the same clinicians in the same catchments; Yatharth itself entered Delhi, Agra and Gurugram exactly that way.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: A material slice of realisation is set by the state: management credits the October 2025 CGHS rate revision — the first in over a decade — for a direct uplift in Q3 top line and margins, meaning that pricing was frozen by policy for ten years, and the group also carries government-facing overhangs (Jhansi land order, income-tax provisional attachment unfrozen in Q2) plus a Jewar Airport partnership. (The long view had said: Roughly 35% of revenue comes from government schemes whose tariffs the state sets — management explicitly credited a CGHS price revision for Q3 FY26 top line and bottom line, and in Nov 2024 had to deliberately shrink government volumes at Noida to protect ALOS and mix, showing fortunes swing with policy decisions the company does not control.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Rather than defending a premium against cheaper substitutes, the group is raising realisation — group ARPOB up 10% year-on-year to INR33,744, Noida Extension at ~INR44,000 with ~70% super-speciality mix, and new hospitals opening above group ARPOB — which is the signature of scale-protected quaternary care that low-cost nursing homes cannot replicate. (The long view had said: Intermediaries fix the price in a large share of the business — government tariffs and pre-agreed TPA rates land below cash rates, and management confirms rates are frozen with no discretion — which caps realisation on roughly two-thirds of revenue; the counterweight is genuine complexity in the case mix rather than brand marketing.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: No single named customer, but results lean on a narrow payer set (cash/TPA insurers plus CGHS) and on individually recruited 'star clinicians' and award-winning specialists whose departure would move revenue; geography is also concentrated in one NCR cluster, so the Noida/Faridabad micro-markets drive nearly all group earnings. (The long view had said: Revenue is atomised across patients and three broadly balanced payer channels (~35% government, remainder split equally between cash and private insurance), across nine-plus hospitals in Noida, Faridabad, Delhi, Jhansi and Agra; no single customer, licensor or supplier is discussed as driving results in any call.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (NO SIGNAL); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (LOW); Technological Innovation (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
