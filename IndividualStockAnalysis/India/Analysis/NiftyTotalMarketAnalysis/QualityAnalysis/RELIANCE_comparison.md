### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 6 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 5. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 7 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Explicit, repeated share numbers rising through the year: broadband subscriber share above 50%, 36.3mn net telecom adds in FY26 to 533mn, largest single-country 5G base outside China, and a stated track to become the second-largest quick-commerce player; the gains are funded by cash profits rather than by degraded credit or underwriting. (The long view had said: Share numbers rise persistently and are quantified against named competition: 45% AGR share and 36% subscriber share in 2022, 170m then 285m 5G users, 70% of new 5G handsets converting to Jio, 78% share of FWA net adds and 65% of incremental home broadband adds by FY27 — gained while EBITDA margin expanded to 57.3%, so the share is not bought with profitability.)

**That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Jio is a subscription business with a large, densifying installed base — 533mn connectivity subscribers, 29mn fixed broadband homes, 285mn 5G users — plus set-top boxes and JioStar/JioHotstar subscriptions layered on the same connection; but across the four FY26 calls management never quantifies attach rates, renewal rates, deferred revenue or customer advances as a funding source, so the recurring-economics disclosure is incomplete. (The long view had said: Jio is a subscription business where the connectivity plan (largely prepaid, billed in advance) recurs off an installed base that management tracks every call — 428m subs in Oct 2022 rising to 533m by Jul 2026, ARPU, churn and per-capita usage — and the AirFiber/set-top-box hardware placed in the home then carries content, cloud, JioPC and managed-service streams on top (digital services growing 20% year-on-year vs 11% connectivity). Enterprise 'managed connectivity' contracts extend the same annuity logic.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Forward Integrators (STRONG FIT); Cost to Replicate (STRONG FIT); Low Price Plus (LIKELY FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Pricing Power (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 3 worsened, 4 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Across these calls Reliance is the value attacker rather than the defender of a price premium: Campa and Independence are the cheaper challengers scaling at 2x, and retail is deliberately competing on 'attractive value to the customer'. No instance of management defending a premium against cheaper substitutes appears in the window. (The long view had said: Connectivity is inherently commoditisable and RIL took no tariff action for twelve months, relying on organic mix to lift ARPU by only Rs.7 — pricing power is bounded by cheaper rivals. In retail the company sits on both sides: it is expanding 3P assortment and its own consumer brands (Campa, Independence) as value propositions, which is the good-enough attack rather than a defence.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: O2C and E&P swing the group: Q1 FY27 topline was up 25% 'primarily because of oil prices' with chemical deltas at three-to-four-year highs, while the prior quarter saw O2C down 4% on crude unavailability, premiums, logistics and insurance costs; E&P EBITDA moves with KGD6 volumes and realisations. The mitigation is real but partial — consumer businesses now carry ~50-55% of EBITDA and S&P cited exactly that in the A- upgrade. (The long view had said: Across all 19 calls O2C earnings swing on crude, product cracks and feedstock spreads — Q2FY23 EBITDA down 40% quarter-on-quarter on margin collapse, FY26 Q4 down 4% amid a supply shock and 'almost doubling' energy prices, then Q1FY27 up 17% on distillate margins '250% more, 300% more'; upstream gas realisations moved from $2.75 to $9.9/MMBTU in a single year. Management explicitly frames results in commodity-margin and cycle language, and capex is heavy and continuous through the cycle.)
- **New Entrants**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Quick commerce has reset Indian retail economics and Reliance is absorbing the hit in margins in every call of the window — retail EBITDA grew 1.3% on 8% revenue in Q3, was 'muted because of the scale up of the hyperlocal e-commerce' for FY26, and fell year-on-year in Q1 FY27 as a stated 'conscious play' funded by 'attractive value to the customer'. (The long view had said: Quick commerce has reset Indian retail economics and RIL is visibly investing margin to respond: FY26 retail EBITDA growth was 'muted because of the scale up of the hyperlocal e-commerce', and Q1FY27 retail EBITDA fell year-on-year with margin down 80bps for the third consecutive quarter as spend on dark stores and delivery infrastructure rose. Management frames this as a deliberate multi-quarter investment, which bounds but does not remove the exposure.)
- **Fashion Risk**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Bounded taste exposure through the fashion and lifestyle retail vertical (called out as soft) and the fast-growing consumer brands portfolio (Campa, Independence), where growth rests on brand appeal; the bulk of group demand, however, is functional and repeat-purchase. (The long view had said: The overwhelming bulk of group earnings rests on functional, repeat-purchase demand — mobile data, home broadband, fuel, polymers and grocery — with per-capita data consumption compounding to 43.7 GB/month and churn falling every quarter. Taste-exposed exposure is limited to the fashion & lifestyle basket and newly built consumer brands, and no growth is attributed to a trend or celebrity moment.)

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
