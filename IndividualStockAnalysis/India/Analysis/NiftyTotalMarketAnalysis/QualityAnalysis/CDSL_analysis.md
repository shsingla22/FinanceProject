# Central Depository Services (India) Ltd. (CDSL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

CDSL is an Indian 'depository' — a market infrastructure institution that holds shares and other securities in electronic (demat) form on behalf of investors, so that buying, selling and transferring them happens as book entries instead of paper. Its customers are three groups: the depository participants (brokers and banks, over 20,700 service locations covering ~98% of India's pin codes) who open demat accounts for investors, the investors themselves (17.27 crore accounts as of Dec 2025, 18.01 crore by March 2026, an ~80%+ share of the industry), and the issuer companies whose securities sit in the system (about 18,798 active companies in mid-2022). It earns fees from these parties — annual charges billed to issuer companies based on their folio counts (billed largely in the first quarter), account and transaction-related charges from depository participants, and per-record fees in its KYC business, where roughly 80% of income comes from 'fetches' (other financial firms pulling an existing KYC record). It operates in India, regulated by SEBI, with a new unit being set up at GIFT City IFSC; FY2025-26 standalone income was INR1,096 crores with net profit of INR468 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Depository (standalone CDSL)** — The core business — holding securities in demat accounts for investors, settling transfers, and servicing issuer companies; it is by far the largest part, with standalone FY26 income of INR1,096 crores and net profit of INR468 crores versus a much smaller subsidiary contribution, and management repeatedly compares it to an infrastructure company that must keep adding technology capacity ahead of volume surges.
- **CDSL Ventures Limited (CVL) — KYC Registration Agency** — Wholly owned subsidiary that creates and stores investor KYC (identity verification) records and lets other financial firms 'fetch' them, described as the first and largest KYC registration agency; income is cyclical with market activity — it grew 65% to INR169 crores of operational revenue in FY24 but fell to INR132 crores in the first nine months of FY26, and management was questioned about pricing risk from the government's CKYC 2.0.
- **Registrar & Transfer Agency (RTA) and GST filing services (within CVL)** — Smaller add-on services run out of the subsidiary — acting as record-keeper for companies (870 companies by June 2022, 30 added that quarter) and processing GST filings (55.48 lakh in Q1 FY23, up 14%).
- **Centrico Insurance Repository Limited** — Subsidiary that applies the same dematerialisation idea to insurance policies, holding them in electronic form; management called it 'work in progress' with a dedicated team in 2024 and it now has its own MD & CEO on the earnings calls, but no revenue figures are given, suggesting it is still small.
- **Countrywide Commodity Repository Limited** — Subsidiary extending electronic record-keeping to commodities; it is named on the calls with its own MD & CEO present, but the excerpts give no size or revenue detail.
- **CDSL IFSC (GIFT City)** — A new unit at GIFT City for which CDSL Ventures received SEBI's no-objection, to be registered with IFSCA as the first KYC registration agency there, aimed at serving international issuers and investors — described as a step for future growth rather than a current earner.
- **Investor education / CDSL IPF** — Not a revenue line but a stated priority — free multilingual services (electronic account statements in 23 languages), a 100-million-view YouTube channel, comic books with Amar Chitra Katha in 12 languages, and the annual Reimagine Symposium, all aimed at bringing more first-time investors into the market that CDSL ultimately gets paid to service.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is levered to market activity across the whole 2022-2026 arc: muted volumes hurt Q1FY23, FY24 income jumped 46% on a 126% turnover surge and record 1cr+ demat openings, then FY26 flattened (consol income INR1,239cr vs INR1,199cr) with consolidated PAT falling to INR455cr from INR526cr and CVL PAT halving to INR55cr, while technology cost kept compounding ~30% through the downturn — the classic peak-investment-into-trough margin squeeze.

> "The combined average daily turnover at BSE and NSE for December 2025 was around INR1 lakh crores, about 8.3% less than the average daily turnover during the same period of the previous year." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Large annuity component — annual issuer charges on 33.76cr folios and custody fees are operations-linked, not capex-linked; management invests countercyclically ('whether we have a great period or a lean period') so capacity is ready when the cycle turns.*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 0 of the last 11 years with swings of ±20 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 40% and 66% (±7.5 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 40% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 6% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Essentially every revenue lever is set by policy: depository pricing is approved by SEBI (an issuer fee hike has been pending ~10 years, with management saying only that the regulator will act 'at the appropriate time'), the unlisted-company revenue stream exists only because of a demat mandate whose definition changed on 1 December 2025, insurance repository revenue waits on evolving IRDAI rules, KRA scope is being reshaped by SEBI consultation papers and a Ministry of Finance circular, and the Securities Market Code 2025 will rewrite the governing statute.

> "the pricing is approved by SEBI in case of depositories. So, it's taken on record and then it's taken forward from there" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Some diversification into subsidiaries (CVL KYC, Centrico insurance repository, commodity repository, GIFT IFSC) — but each of these is itself licence- and regulation-created, so it does not reduce policy dependence.*
