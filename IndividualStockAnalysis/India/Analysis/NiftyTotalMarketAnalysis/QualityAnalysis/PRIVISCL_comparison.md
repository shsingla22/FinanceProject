### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 6 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 6 weakened, 5 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: FY26 growth of 21.7% came on only 6.5% volume growth with input costs 'relatively stable', with price increase named explicitly as a driver and margins expanding to 25%+ for four straight quarters — real evidence of price realisation, though it is disclosed once in aggregate with mix rather than as a repeated, quantified pricing programme. (The long view had said: Repeated, explicit price increases without volume loss: 7-10% on FY25 contracts, ~8% price contribution to FY26's 22% growth alongside 6.5% volume growth, gross margin near all-time-high 52% and EBITDA margin rising 19.6% (9M FY24) → 21.9% (9M FY25) → 25.8% (FY26), with management stating confidence in passing on further increases.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Newly introduced products are repeatedly credited with growth, super-speciality molecules are at lab/pilot stage, and a decade of biotechnology work is said to be converting into patents and biomass-to-value chemistry; but no R&D spend figure, no named products with revenue impact, and no share-of-industry-R&D is ever disclosed in these four calls. (The long view had said: Named new molecules recur every year with revenue impact — Galaxmusk, Prionyl (proprietary process) and Camphor commercialised over FY24-25, then Ethyl Maltol, Ethylene Brassylate and a world-first bio-route Cyclopentanone in the FY26 capex plan, plus biotechnology work management says will create IP/intangibles; only the absolute R&D spend is never disclosed.)
- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Within the year promises were made and kept — the 120-125 day working capital target was hit (124 days, then 117), debt was cut by Rs. 44 crore, PRIGIV was guided to profit and delivered it in Q4, and the labour-code PAT hit was disclosed transparently; against that, the Phase-1 capex date slipped from March 2026 to April and then 30 June 2026 without acknowledgement, the tone is heavily promotional ('visionary Chairman'), and the multi-year record needed to confirm the pattern is not in these excerpts. (The long view had said: The same team (Babani, Rajan, Iyer, Patil) fronts all 12 calls and has delivered what it promised: the Nov 2023 promises of margin normalisation, inventory reduction to ~120-135 days and debt down to ~850-900 crore were met (117-day working capital, INR876 crore net debt, 1.33x net debt/EBITDA), the 20% growth guidance became 22%, bad news is disclosed plainly (shutdown-driven revenue dip, one-time airfreight, Labour Code adjustment, Q4 gross-margin overlap), and the company measures itself on ROE/ROCE of ~22%.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Privi sells aroma ingredients to the top-15 fragrance houses (and a JV with Givaudan) who specify the molecule into formulations that FMCG brands and consumers ultimately pay for — a specifier-driven chain — but the calls never discuss trade training, channel relationship investment or the switching costs that define the pattern. (The long view had said: Privi's ingredients are specified into formulations by fragrance houses and FMCG customers who must approve each molecule before commercialisation, which creates designed-in stickiness, but no call describes trade training, prescriber programmes or channel-relationship investment of the classic kind.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management claims share gains in key products through the tariff disruption and points to global customers reshoring supply chains toward India, but never puts a number on share or names a competitor it is taking share from. (The long view had said: Growth of 16-24% per year with China Plus One positioning and first-in-India molecules implies share gain, and tariff/EU-treaty advantage is claimed, but no explicit share numbers versus named competitors are ever given and management admits rationing capacity and even conceding share on some contracts.)
- **Brand Strength**: QUANT SIGNAL → NO FIT. Why now: This is a B2B ingredient supplier with ~70% exports to 15 industrial customers; across the year there is no A&P spend, no consumer heritage story, no distributor leverage and no adjacency launch under a consumer name — the reputation cited is technical and ESG-based, not brand pricing power. (The long view had said: all 2 computable checks point this way but the calls offer no evidence — numbers alone are only ever a screening hint, never a confirmed fit.)

*Unchanged: Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
