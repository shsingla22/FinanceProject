# Capillary Techno Ind (CAPILLARY) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Capillary sells cloud software that large companies use to run their customer loyalty programmes — the points, tiers, rewards and 'earn and burn' schemes behind things like IndiGo's BluChip, Taj InnerCircle and Tata Neu. Its customers are big enterprises and multinationals (about 115 customers covering roughly 400-410 brands, including 20 Fortune 500 names and two of the Fortune 10), spanning retail, hotels, airlines, healthcare, banks and telcos. It makes money from long-term subscription contracts of three to seven years, billed annually and priced on how many loyalty members or loyalty transactions run through the platform, so revenue grows automatically as customers use it more (plus contracted inflation increases and overage charges); management describes it as 'bank-ledger-like' pricing on a system of record. It operates globally from about 16 offices with roughly 700 staff, serving customers in about 49 countries, and reported revenue of about INR 543 crores for the first nine months of FY26 (~22-23% growth) with adjusted EBITDA margins in the 13-15% range; growth comes from existing customers spending more (110-111% net retention), new client wins, and buying up rival loyalty platforms cheaply (0.5x-1.5x revenue) and migrating those customers onto its own system.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Loyalty stack (core loyalty platform)** — The main product and 'bread and butter' — the system of record that tracks every member, point and reward transaction for a brand's loyalty programme, priced per member or per transaction; management says it is roughly 90% of revenues.
- **Customer Data Platform (CDP)** — The underlying base layer that plugs into every customer touchpoint (point-of-sale, e-commerce, mobile apps, kiosks, NPS surveys) to pull all customer data into one place; management says they don't monetise it much on its own — it exists to support the loyalty product.
- **Engage (Engage+)** — Marketing automation for running campaigns and promotions off the loyalty data, sold as an add-on to loyalty customers; about 5%-7% of revenues and described as the company's 'wedge' into marketing automation.
- **Insights (Insights+)** — Dashboards and reporting on loyalty performance, another add-on to the core platform; management expects the AI product to eventually replace this piece.
- **Rewards** — Powers third-party rewards catalogues for clients, especially banks and telcos, and is also treated as a system of record; around 5%-7% of revenues.
- **aiRA (AI-powered Research Assistant)** — An AI layer running across all the products that lets marketers ask questions in plain English, get analytics, run campaigns and generate creatives instead of hiring an agency — priced per question or analysis; it is a few million dollars of revenue so far, with about a quarter of customers piloting or already paying, and management calls it their fastest-adopted product.
- **M&A / acquired platforms** — Not a product but a stated third engine of growth: Capillary buys competing loyalty platforms cheaply (four done including Brierley, Kognitiv in May 2025, and SessionM bought from Mastercard for $35 million and closed 1 May 2026) and migrates those customers onto its own stack, lifting gross margins from about 30% to 65%-70%; this business runs at 94%-96% net retention while migration discounts wash through, and is expected to take total ARR past $115 million over 12-18 months.

### The story in depth

Capillary sells loyalty software to large companies, and almost everything good about it starts from that one choice. About 97% of revenue comes from big enterprises, roughly 115 of them, running 410 brands across 49 countries, and they buy on reliability rather than appeal: five-nines uptime, a points ledger that is audited like a bank ledger because it holds real customer money, and enterprise-grade governance and privacy. The company has been founder-led for 18 years, spends around 20% of revenue on technology, and was ranked top in the December 2025 Forrester Wave with a perfect score on 22 of 27 criteria. Disclosure is unusually frank — management separates growth won organically from growth bought through acquisition, strips out one-off items such as listing costs, gratuity and a INR25 crore indemnity receipt, and warns in advance that the SessionM acquisition will pull margin percentages down. The scoring reflects this: customer benefits, management and growth are the strongest areas, and the balance sheet holds net cash of INR463 crores.

The patterns that fit best all grow out of the same root, and so do the risks. Because a typical customer wires the platform into seven to nine of its own systems and the ledger becomes the official record, customers rarely leave — that single fact is what makes the Cost to Replicate and Pricing Power patterns strong fits. Contracts carry automatic annual increases of 3-4% in Western markets and 7-8% in higher-inflation countries, plus charges that rise with member and transaction counts, so management says about a third of growth arrives without effort. On top of that sits genuine share capture: new order intake rose from INR53 crores to INR66 crores over nine months, with three to four Fortune 500 wins in the year including a $20 million deal with a Fortune 50 retailer, taken mostly from under-invested legacy platforms and service-heavy agencies. The revenue is contracted on three, five and seven-year terms, billed in advance, and converts well — INR150 crores of operating cash flow against INR107 crores of adjusted profit before interest, tax and depreciation, with existing customers spending 110% of what they spent the prior year (114-115% before acquisitions). But the same stickiness that protects Capillary also protects its rivals, which is precisely why it buys competitors rather than winning them over one deal at a time, and the same narrow enterprise focus that gives it pricing power leaves the whole book resting on roughly 115 names, with analysts asking pointed questions about the health of a single top-five healthcare customer.

The weaknesses are real but mostly known and mostly connected. Return on capital is the one clearly negative area, and it sits alongside the acquisition strategy: when Capillary migrates customers from a bought platform onto its own, it keeps only about 70% of the revenue and hands out one-time discounts — direct evidence that a slice of the market considers cheaper, simpler software good enough. Heavy technology spending is not a luxury either; management itself describes five platform shifts in 18 years that stranded smaller loyalty vendors, and analysts twice pressed on whether a company could now point AI agents at its own customer data instead of buying a platform. The answer was defensible rather than conclusive, so both technological change and new entrants sit on watch. Reputation is another two-sided fact: the Forrester ranking opens doors and shapes which vendors get invited to bid, but it is borrowed standing conferred by analysts and consultants rather than something customers feel directly, which is why brand and the middleman pattern only partially fit. Cost discipline is visible — headcount up 1% against 23% revenue growth, sales spend at 17% of revenue — but none of it is passed on as lower prices. Four of the eight risks score low, resilience reads as sound, and the combined rating lands at 75 out of 100, or Strong, built from 69 on business quality, 78 on multibagger fit and 84 on risk safety.
