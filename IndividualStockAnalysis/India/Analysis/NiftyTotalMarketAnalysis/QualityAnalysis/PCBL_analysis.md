# PCBL Chemical (PCBL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

PCBL Chemical (formerly PCBL Limited) is an India-based chemicals manufacturer whose core business is making carbon black — a black powder made from crude-oil-derived feedstock (carbon black feedstock oil, ~75% of which it buys from the U.S. Gulf Coast) that is mixed into rubber and other materials. It sells mainly to tyre makers, plus to makers of other rubber goods, plastics, inks, coatings and batteries, and earns money on the spread between the oil-linked cost of feedstock and the price it charges per tonne, so profits are usually discussed as 'EBITDA per metric tonne' (around Rs. 20,000/tonne in FY24-25). It runs plants across India (Durgapur, Palej, Mundra, Kochi and a newer Tamil Nadu/Chennai plant), with installed carbon black capacity growing from roughly 7,70,000 tonnes to 8,50,000 tonnes a year, sells about 60% at home and around 40% as exports (Europe, U.S., South Asia, Japan), and has bolted on two newer businesses: the acquired Aquapharm specialty-chemicals business and a battery-materials venture called Nanovace. Alongside this it burns waste process gas to generate power, using some in its own plants and selling the surplus to the grid.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Carbon Black – Tyre** — The largest chunk of the business: standard rubber-grade carbon black sold to tyre manufacturers in India and abroad, making up roughly 64-65% of volumes in FY23 (e.g. 77,650 of 109,377 tonnes in Q1 FY23; 87,945 tonnes in Q1 FY25), much of it on formula-based contracts with tyre majors that pass through raw-material cost with about a quarter's lag.
- **Performance Chemicals** — Carbon black grades sold into non-tyre rubber applications, the second-biggest volume bucket and a fast-growing one — 21,860 tonnes in Q1 FY23 rising to 50,302 tonnes in Q1 FY25 — used by management as a way to lean away from over-dependence on tyres.
- **Specialty Black (Specialty Chemicals)** — The smallest but highest-value carbon black line, sold into inks, coatings, plastics and newer areas like semiconductors, data centres, EV and battery applications; volumes were 9,867 tonnes in Q1 FY23 and 15,671 tonnes in Q1 FY25, with capacity being pushed from 72,000 to 1,12,000 tonnes and described repeatedly as a top strategic focus.
- **Power (captive power plants / external power sales)** — Waste gas from the carbon black process is used to generate electricity — capacity rising from 91 MW toward 134 MW — with a large share sold outside the company (e.g. 116 of 194 million units in Q1 FY25 at about Rs. 4.16 per unit), giving a useful side stream of earnings on top of the chemicals business.
- **Aquapharm Chemicals** — An acquired specialty-chemicals arm (consolidated from FY25) with plants in India and the U.S. and about 1,30,000 tonnes of capacity, making phosphonates — where it claims roughly 24% global share excluding China — plus oil and gas chemicals (under 1% global share) and 'green chelates' such as MGDA and GLDA; it contributed Rs. 359 crores of revenue and Rs. 55 crores of operating EBITDA in Q1 FY25, making it the company's main non-carbon-black earnings leg.
- **Nanovace (battery chemicals)** — An early-stage battery-materials platform run through a joint venture with an Australian technology company, with an 80-tonne pilot plant at Palej due to start up around March 2026 and product validation under way with large battery makers; it contributes nothing yet but is pitched as a high-growth, high-margin future segment.
- **R&D and new products** — Two research centres — near Baroda in India and near Brussels in Belgium — with around 60 scientists and engineers developing new grades (including a patented graphene-based hybrid carbon black), alternate feedstocks such as coal tar and tyre pyrolysis oil, and recovered end-of-life carbon black; described as the engine behind the shift toward higher-value specialty products.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

PCBL is a carbon black and chemicals maker whose genuine strengths sit on the operating side of the business. It spends steadily on research through two centres — roughly 60 scientists in Baroda and Belgium — and that spending has produced actual commercial products: a patented graphene hybrid carbon black, higher-margin non-standard tyre grades, superconductive black and the Nanovace battery-chemicals platform. It has pushed into new geographies with real results, with Europe rising from 14% to 21% of international volume and exports now around 40% of volume, supported by a service-led model in Europe and Japan and new distributors in Africa and Latin America. It is winning share at home, with domestic volumes up 6% year-on-year in Q3 FY26 and 21% in Q4 FY26. It is visibly disciplined on cost — selling captive power externally, running yield, throughput and logistics programmes targeting Rs 200–250 crore, and putting Industry 4.0 and now agentic AI on the shop floor — with 880,000 MTPA of scale behind it. The end markets are growing for durable reasons, including roughly $29bn of expected global tyre investment by 2029, premiumisation and EV adoption in Indian tyres, and new specialty demand from semiconductors, data centres and AI. For every ₹100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings in the business, it generated ₹18 of operating cash this year. The combined rating is 47 out of 100 — Mixed, two stars — built from 55 for business quality, 25 for multibagger fit and 60 for risk safety, with resilience judged SOUND.

The multibagger screen is where the story turns, and it turns for one reason that also drives most of the risk findings: the core product is a price-taking commodity. Not one of the eleven patterns is a clean fit — six come out PARTIAL and five as NO FIT — and the failures cluster around pricing. Management itself describes the strategy as volume-led, with tyre pricing on formula-based pass-through contracts indexed to crude with a quarterly lag, and it conceded in February 2026 that industry overcapacity has removed pricing power even in specialty; the May 2026 price increases were purely cost recovery. That single trait knocks out Pricing Power, Toll Roads (carbon black is a major raw-material line for tyre makers, not a small ingredient they ignore, so customers destock and defer when prices or tariffs move) and Cost to Replicate (the industry is in admitted overcapacity at 75% utilisation against a normal 80%, and PCBL itself adds 60,000–90,000 tonne lines in months). The same trait reappears in the risk review as Good Enough Goods and New Entrants, both rated WATCH, where the threat is not a new business model but simply more capacity, including cheap Chinese supply. The innovation and cost work described above is best read as the company's answer to this problem rather than evidence it has been solved: Innovation Dominance and Low Price Plus both land at PARTIAL, the latter specifically because returns have not held up despite the cost obsession. Market Share Gainers is PARTIAL for a related reason — the domestic and European wins are real, and Aquapharm holds about 24% of the global ex-China phosphonate market, but international volumes then fell 13%. Revenue is one-off tonnage with no subscription or service stream, so Recurring Revenue is a clear NO FIT, and Brand Strength has nothing supporting it at all.

The risk review's one HIGH RISK finding, cyclicality, is the mirror image of the framework's single best score. Results swing with crude, spot spreads and customer destocking: FY26 EBITDA fell to INR1,081cr from INR1,384cr on flat volumes, and the strong 2022 margins were admitted by management to be a war-driven windfall not to be treated as a base. Yet the same cycle is why the framework scores cyclical market growth at +2 — the May 2026 call places the company at an identifiable trough with a visible upturn, with spreads floored, positive exit-quarter momentum, the US tariff cut to 18% and the India-EU FTA. Policy cuts both ways here too: the EU ban on Russian carbon black lifted export volumes in FY25, while the 50% US tariff froze American customers in Q3 FY26, which is why government dependency is a WATCH rather than a clean pass. Concentration compounds it — tyres are consistently 55–65% of carbon black volumes, a significant portion running through formula contracts with a handful of tyre majors, and roughly 75% of feedstock comes from one region, the US Gulf Coast. The weakest part of the record is management and capital allocation, and it traces mostly to one decision: the Aquapharm acquisition, which took on about INR3,800cr of debt for a business earning INR370–380cr of EBITDA at purchase that delivered only INR162cr in FY26, with its CEO resigning. That scores -2 on the merger and acquisition check and pulls Capital Allocation to -0.25 and Management to -0.33. Corporate Culture is PARTIAL for the same mixed reason — management does own bad news on calls and did deleverage, cutting net borrowings by Rs 454 crore in FY26 while funding Rs 750 crore of capex, but the promise record is poor, including an August 2024 pledge of a fivefold increase. Promotional framing — 'best-ever' language, a '$1 billion run rate' callout, a volunteered 5x-profit ambition — costs it a point on the out-of-limelight check. The reassuring items are that demand is functional and repeat-purchase, so fashion risk is LOW, and consumer preference shifts are running in the company's favour, from green chelates with P&G and Henkel allocations to EV and specialty grades. The net picture: a competent operator with real research output and a real cost programme, sitting in a commodity where it cannot set price, carrying an acquisition that has not yet worked, at what management says is the bottom of a cycle.
