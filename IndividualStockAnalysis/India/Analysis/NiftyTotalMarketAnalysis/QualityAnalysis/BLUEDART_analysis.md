# Blue Dart Express Ltd. (BLUEDART) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: SERVICES*

Blue Dart Express is an Indian courier and express delivery company: it picks up parcels, documents and freight from businesses and delivers them across India on a time-definite basis, using its own combined air and ground network. Its customers are mostly businesses — large corporates, SMEs and e-commerce sellers shipping to consumers — with demand spread across metros and increasingly Tier 2 and Tier 3 towns. It makes money by charging per shipment or per kilo (air shipments fetch a higher realisation per kilo than ground, though margins are broadly comparable), and it pushes through an annual general price increase, typically effective 1st January, to offset inflation; fuel is a major cost, since jet fuel (ATF) and diesel prices flow straight into its cost base. In FY2026 it reported revenue of about INR 6,141 crore and profit after tax of about INR 240 crore, moving roughly 1.44 million tonnes and about 404 million shipments in the year.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Air Express (including documents and time-definite air products)** — Overnight/time-critical delivery of documents and lighter, urgent packages flown on the company's own aircraft; air is the larger half of the business at roughly 60% of revenue in FY2026, but management describes these traditional products as growing more slowly than the rest.
- **Ground / Surface Express (B2B)** — Business-to-business shipments moved by road rather than air, typically heavier consignments at a lower price per kilo; management puts it at around 28.6% of revenue in the December 2025 quarter and says it normally runs near 30%, and calls ground a key growth driver.
- **E-commerce (B2C) — split into e-com air and e-com light surface** — Delivery of online-shopping parcels to consumers, either flown or moved as light packages on the ground network; it is roughly 30-31% of revenue and the main driver of both shipment and tonnage growth, with the light-surface piece growing much faster than the air piece.
- **International** — Cross-border shipments, mentioned only as one of the smaller 'other products' that continues to grow but at a slower rate than e-commerce and surface.
- **Own air fleet (aviation capacity)** — The company runs its own dedicated freighter aircraft — six Boeing 757-200s giving about 500 tonnes of capacity per night as of 2022, with two 737-800s being added to lift capacity 15-20% — which is what allows the overnight air product and also exposes earnings directly to jet fuel prices.
- **Hubs and ground infrastructure** — Sorting and linehaul facilities that tie the network together, including a new 'green integrated hub' at Pataudi, Haryana serving North India and a consolidated ground hub at Gurgaon that handles both B2B surface and e-com light surface volumes; capex is described as mostly replacement and renewal with some expansion.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Blue Dart earns its "Decent" 57-out-of-100 rating (three stars) on the strength of two things: the people running it and the physical network they run. Management scores highest of the seven quality areas (0.83), and the reasons are consistent across four years of calls — the finance chief and company secretary host them with no guidance and no showmanship, management admits the new hub will not move company profitability, says ground returns are weaker than air, and refuses to claim a price increase has stuck before it has. The framework flags that there is little "halo" to strip out here, because results are modest (7% revenue growth, Rs.240cr profit, a labour-code exceptional item) and management under-claims rather than over-claims. The second strength is the asset base: an owned overnight freighter fleet moving 450-550 tons a night at 85-90% pallet utilisation, plus all-India ground coverage, scored a full +2 on distribution advantage. That capital works hard — every Rs.100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings threw off Rs.28 of operating cash this year. Growth has also been steady rather than lumpy, averaging about 9% a year.

Those same two strengths are what the multibagger screen picks up, and also what it stops short of. The freighter fleet and pan-India first- and last-mile network are the basis of the Cost to Replicate pattern — expensive and slow for anyone to rebuild — and the 70%-plus share of organised document express is what supports both the Brand Strength and Toll Roads readings. But nine of the eleven patterns come back only PARTIAL, and the reason is usually the same fact seen from the other side. Pricing Power is the one LIKELY FIT, resting on a real January general price increase of more than 4% and a twenty-year fuel and currency surcharge mechanism; yet management calls the hike an inflation offset, says customers take months to accept it, and negotiates lane by lane in "share of wallet" contests — which is exactly why Toll Roads stays partial rather than clean. Market Share Gainers is held back by the company's own numbers: management claims above-industry volume growth but will not quantify it, and 7% FY26 revenue growth sits below the 12-15% industry growth analysts cite. Low Price Plus is a clear NO FIT, because the company deliberately chooses price realisation over volume. That whole picture is why multibagger fit contributes only 33 points into the blended score, against 60 for business quality.

The risk review is the most reassuring of the three inputs at 80, with resilience judged SOUND: no risk is rated high, two (customer/supplier concentration and fashion risk) are LOW, and technology shows no signal at all. What the five WATCH items describe is slow erosion, not sudden breakage — and they trace back to the same trait that produces the strengths. The migration of demand from high-yield documents and air express toward cheaper ground and e-commerce surface is simultaneously the Shifting Consumer Preferences risk, the Good Enough Goods risk, and the reason the pricing-mix-volume check is the one clear negative (-1): growth is volume-led with an adverse mix, so a 4%-plus price rise still leaves realisation roughly flat. The mitigation is that Blue Dart owns the cheaper substitute itself — ground has gone from about 30% to about 40% of revenue at broadly comparable percentage margins — so the trade-down is largely internal. The New Entrants watch covers surface-first players like Delhivery outgrowing it and captive e-commerce fleets; the Cyclicality watch notes the FY23 peak-cycle decision to buy two 737-800 freighters for Rs.300-350 crore; the Government Dependency watch is cost-side, showing up as the labour-code exceptional item and GST-driven shifts in ground tonnage. Set against strong management and returns, the weak spots are structural: growth scored 0.2, industry structure 0.17 and capital allocation 0.0 in the quality framework, which is what pulls a well-run, well-defended business down to a middling overall score.
