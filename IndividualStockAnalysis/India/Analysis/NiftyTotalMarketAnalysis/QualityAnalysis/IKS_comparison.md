### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 4 weakened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Management sets an explicit, repeated share yardstick — growth above the 12% outsourced-TAM growth rate equals share gain — and cleared it in every quarter of the window (24% year-on-year in rupees, 19% in dollars in Q3 FY26), while the TruBridge acquisition adds a 30-40% share position in the 2,200-hospital rural segment. Share is being won on platform breadth, not on discounting or loosened standards; the client count fall from ~900 to ~600 is deliberate tail-pruning of small AQuity accounts. (The long view had said: Management sets an explicit, falsifiable share test — beat the 12% outsourced-TAM growth or you are losing share — and has cleared it every quarter since listing (16% y/y in Q3 FY25 despite deliberate revenue dampening, 22% y/y in Q2 FY26, four consecutive quarters of accelerating outperformance), while share gain is being bought with rising, not falling, margins and by deliberately shedding the small-client tail from 900 to ~600 accounts.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: No price increases, premiumisation or elasticity are discussed in any of the four calls; margin expansion is attributed to AI/tech leverage and AQuity integration, not pricing, and the outcome-based construct makes revenue a function of client volumes rather than rate. (The long view had said: There is pricing-for-value evidence (outcome-based deals, gain-share with Palomar, net-economic-value-add guarantees driving deal momentum) but no evidence of list-price increases above inflation; conversely, FY25 involved deliberate discounts to legacy AQuity customers and management concedes AI will change the SaaS per-seat pricing model, so deflation risk is acknowledged rather than absent.)

**That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Across all four calls management stresses annuity-like repeat revenue (85% in Q2 FY26 rising to 90% by Q4 FY26) and 5-plus year vintage in the top 5/10 clients, but the model is outcome-based fees as a percentage of client revenue — there is no upfront product sale, no attach/renewal rates, and no discussion of deferred revenue or customer advances funding the business. (The long view had said: Across all calls management anchors on annuity economics — 95%+ recurring at IPO (Feb 2025), 85%+ repeat revenue (Nov 2025), 90% repeat with top-5/top-10 vintages of 5–7 years by Q4 FY26 — and growth comes from cross-selling 16 features into an installed base of ~600 enterprise clients / 150,000 physicians. The one missing leg is customer prepayment: IKS actually funds customers upfront (the $16.5m guarantee to Palomar), so working capital runs the other way.)
- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Founder-CEO reports against the same five execution pillars every call, owns shortfalls unprompted ('we were a little slow to get out of the gate' on AQuity cross-sell), pre-announces where the client count will settle (500-600) and explains the decline rather than burying it, and delivered the promised AQuity margin transformation ahead of plan. Only six calls exist since the December 2024 IPO, so the multi-year promise-versus-delivery record this pattern demands cannot be verified from the one-year window. (The long view had said: Founder-led for 18–19 years with a stable Gupta/Balasubramanian team; promises made on the roadshow (early-to-mid-30s EBITDA in 18–24 months) were delivered three to four quarters early, and bad news is owned plainly — the FY26 calls admit the AQuity cross-sell was slow ('first innings of a test match') and then name the specific go-to-market mistake, adding 'the proof will be in the pudding'.)
- **Brand Strength**: LIKELY FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: No heritage, A&P spend, distributor leverage or brand-adjacency language appears; the calls are entirely about platform breadth and enterprise relationships. (The long view had said: There is no consumer brand or A&P engine; what exists is B2B reputation built on third-party validation — Black Book #1 in AI-driven RCM, KLAS top performer in ambulatory RCM, Google Cloud recognition — plus 18-year customer vintages. Credible but category-specific, with no heritage/premium-pricing or adjacency-stretch evidence.)
- **Toll Roads**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Mission-criticality is clear ('inseparable part of our customers' operating environment'), but the defining tiny-cost element is absent: IKS is paid a percentage of client revenue out of a $260bn chore-task cost pool, i.e. a material, negotiated line item, and management itself describes three genres of competitors bidding for that spend rather than a rational oligopoly or certification standard. (The long view had said: Management repeatedly frames IKS as an 'ubiquitous, inseparable part of our customers' operating environment' with very low attrition and multi-year vintages, and TruBridge's rural-hospital system-of-record is genuinely mission-critical infrastructure — but the spend is not a trivial slice: chores are ~15% of physician revenue, and deals are outcome/gain-share priced, i.e. explicitly negotiated on value rather than paid without question.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (STRONG FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 1 worsened, 2 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Management repeatedly foregrounds the vintage and health of the top 5 and top 10 clients, and the client count has been deliberately cut from ~900 post-AQuity to ~600 (heading to 500-600), so revenue is concentrating into fewer, larger physician-enterprise and health-system relationships. No single-name dependency was disclosed or challenged on the calls, which bounds the risk. (The long view had said: Top 10 clients are 45% of revenue (Q2 FY26) and results are repeatedly narrated through a handful of named relationships — Palomar, Radiology Partners, AdventHealth, a top-five health system — while the client count was deliberately cut from ~900 to ~600 to concentrate on large enterprises. Individual deals like the $16.5m guaranteed upside to Palomar are large enough to move reported economics.)
- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Management devoted a section of the Q4 call to mapping three genres of competitors — EHR/system-of-record vendors moving up into workflow, a 'whole bunch' of point-solution systems of action, and platform players — a defensive framing that concedes low barriers for AI-native entrants attacking individual tasks like scribing, coding or prior authorisation. The response has been to build/buy breadth rather than to discount, and margins expanded through FY26. (The long view had said: Management explicitly maps three genres of competitor and concedes that EHR/system-of-record incumbents are structurally different entrants moving onto its turf, while AI-native point solutions have already scaled in ambient scribing. Its response — buying a system of record — is itself a competitive-positioning move against a resetting industry structure.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Chore-task outsourcing — transcription, coding, RCM — is exactly the kind of non-complex service that cheaper challengers can attack, and AI is collapsing the cost of delivering it; management's hedged language about tech leverage giving 'perhaps some margin protection' concedes that pricing is not fully secure. The pruning of the inherited AQuity tail of small clients from ~900 to ~600 also suggests the low-end, price-competed portion of that book was not worth defending. (The long view had said: In the large health-system segment IKS must win task-by-task against cheaper point solutions — management concedes 'you're competing on a point solution basis' and must be 'number one or two or three' in RCM, coding and patient access — and AI is collapsing the cost of exactly those tasks, as its own TruBridge comparison of revenue-per-FTE implies. The niche protection the framework favours is thin where the buyer purchases a single commoditising feature.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The whole FY26 narrative is a defensive-offensive race against generative/agentic AI: a services base of ~13,300 people (largely India delivery, including the acquired AQuity transcription/documentation book) sits directly in the path of automation, and management describes legacy EHR incumbents 'waking up' to become platform systems of action. The April 2026 TruBridge acquisition was explicitly justified as making the moat 'AI-proof', which is an admission that API-level integration alone was not defensible. (The long view had said: The core business was built as human-led labour arbitrage (13,300 staff) doing tasks that generative/agentic AI is now automating, and management spends much of every call defending against this — culminating in a debt-funded acquisition of TruBridge explicitly to make the moat 'AI-proof'. The Q4 FY26 admission that ambient AI scribing point solutions flooded the market and topped out at 50-60% utilisation shows the format is being contested in real time.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 2 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 2 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
