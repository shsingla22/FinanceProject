# CSB Bank (CSBBANK) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

CSB Bank is a small Indian commercial bank that takes deposits from the public (savings, current and term deposits, plus bulk deposits and borrowings) and lends that money out, mainly as loans secured against gold jewellery, alongside corporate/wholesale loans, SME and business-loan-group lending, and a small retail book. It earns most of its income from the spread between what it charges borrowers (yield on advances around 10.6%-11.3%) and what it pays for funds — a net interest margin that ran above 5% in FY2023 and around 3.7%-3.8% by FY2026 — topped up by fee and other income (about 21% of total income in FY2026) from insurance sales, processing and commission fees, LC/BG and trade fees, treasury/bond gains and priority-sector lending certificates. It serves customers through a branch-and-ATM network that grew from roughly 794 branches in mid-2024 to 868 branches and 835 ATMs by June 2026, expanding beyond its home region towards a pan-India footprint to cut concentration risk. Management describes it as being midway through an 'SBS 2030' plan — a build phase of technology and branch investment now moving into a scale phase — with the aim of becoming a full-service, mid-sized bank rather than mainly a gold-loan bank.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Gold loans** — Loans given against customers' gold jewellery — the bank's largest and fastest-growing lending line (24%-26% annual growth in earlier years and about 54% of total advances by Q1 FY2027), described as low-risk with tight loan-to-value and price controls, and targeted to fall to roughly 30% of the book by 2030 as other businesses grow.
- **Wholesale / corporate banking** — Lending to larger companies; management says it 'progressed as envisaged' and by Q1 FY2027 it is, along with gold, one of the two biggest and best-performing parts of the loan book.
- **SME / BLG (Business Loan Group)** — Loans to small and medium businesses; growth has been deliberately held back ('calibrated and cautious') during a weak operating environment, with management saying it will resume scaling when conditions improve.
- **Retail assets** — A comparatively small book of personal and other retail loans including LAS (loan against security) and some unsecured lending; it shrank when the bank deliberately ran off a roughly Rs.2,100 crore gold-repledge LAS portfolio down to about Rs.60 crore, and management plans to relaunch a full retail product range and 'kickstart' retail growth around Q4 FY2027/Q1 FY2028.
- **Deposits / liabilities franchise** — The funding side — savings and current accounts (CASA, about 25% of deposits in 2024 falling to roughly 19-20% by 2026) plus retail and bulk term deposits, supplemented by refinance and overseas/FCY borrowings; deposits grew 20-26% a year recently, faster than the industry, and building granular retail deposits is management's stated priority.
- **Other income / fee business** — Core fees from insurance sales, loan processing, commissions, letters of credit and bank guarantees, plus penal charges, treasury/bond profits and income from selling priority-sector lending certificates (PSLCs); this grew 21% in FY2026 and made up about 21% of total income.
- **Transaction banking and technology platform** — Newer capability being built out — supply chain finance, cash management and trade products on a newly migrated Oracle core banking system with surrounding systems — described as the foundation that will let the bank scale into a full-service bank over the next few years.
- **Branch and ATM distribution** — The physical network used to gather deposits and customers, expanded at roughly 100 branches a year in the early build phase and standing at 868 branches and 835 ATMs by June 2026, with expansion aimed at widening the bank's geographic spread.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

CSB Bank's strongest evidence sits in three places, and they are the same three places where the pattern screen gives partial credit rather than a clean pass. The bank is taking market share from a small base — deposits up 20% against an industry 13.5% and advances up 27% against 16% in FY26, then 26% and 24% against 13.4% and 18.6% in Q1FY27, four quarters of outperformance in a row — which is the one quality check scored at the top of the scale (Gain in market share, 2) and the basis of the Market Share Gainers pattern. Behind it is a management team that scores 0.83 as an area: the SBS 2030 build-then-scale plan set out in 2022 has been followed for four years, including refusing to scale retail until the systems were ready, then completing a full core-banking migration to Oracle with 50-plus surround systems, alongside a branch build from 514 to 868 and a Dubai representative office in process. The conservatism is real and consistent across seventeen calls — accelerated bad-loan provisioning above the regulator's norms, a roughly Rs.200 crore contingency buffer held intact from 2022 to 2026, capital ratios of 20-25%, the same leadership throughout, and no promotional posture. That combination earns Independent, long-term and tenacious a 2, Disciplined stewards and Out of limelight a 1 each, and drives the Corporate Culture pattern to partial.

The problem is that the very thing producing the share gain is also what produces most of the risk. The growth is being bought rather than won: it is funded with roughly 52% bulk deposits at about 6.5% cost of funds, while low-cost current and savings balances have fallen from 35.1% in July 2022 to 19.4% in July 2026, and it is priced to win — management has explicitly cut gold-loan yields to stay competitive. So the same trait shows up as a partial pass on Market Share Gainers and simultaneously as a HIGH RISK on Stakeholder Concentration and a WATCH on Good Enough Goods, because both loan and deposit products are commodities won on rate with no brand protection. The financial trail is unambiguous: net interest margin from 5.17% to 3.66%, yield on advances from 11.25% to 10.65%, gold yield 12% to 11.85%, SME 9.81% to 9.25%, return on assets 1.75% to 1.09% and return on equity 19% to 12.7%, with cost-to-income rising from 48%. That is why Return on Capital scores -1.0, Asset turn and Pricing, Mix and Volume score -1 each, and why Pricing Power, Low Price Plus, Brand Strength and Cost to Replicate all come back as NO FIT — the bank is a price-taker on both sides of the balance sheet, its funding is bought wholesale, and management itself describes the franchise as still to be built. Even the tech overhaul cuts both ways: it supports the partial Innovation Dominance verdict and the positive Capex for growth check, but it is catch-up spending any funded bank can buy, and it is also a named WATCH risk because FY2026 was consumed by it and the old system was the binding constraint on launching anything at all.

Concentration and rules are where the story is most exposed. Gold is 54% of advances, and that single fact ties together three separate findings — it is the engine of the volume growth, it is the reason Government Dependency is a HIGH RISK (the regulator's gold loan-to-value cut from 90% to 75% drove the FY22 bad-loan cycle, the April-2024 penal-charge reclassification took about 40bps off margin in one quarter, and liquidity rules forced further change), and it is the reason New Entrants is a WATCH, since the book competes directly with larger non-bank gold specialists. Growth in gold is now visibly price-led rather than customer-led: tonnage is flat and account numbers have fallen from roughly 6-7 lakh to about 4 lakh, which is the core of the Cyclicality WATCH. The stated fix — taking gold from 54% toward 30% by 2030, building sticky transaction-banking and current-account relationships, and expanding beyond Kerala — is credible and already partly executed on branches, but the record is clear that it is prospective, not delivered, and non-resident deposits at roughly 13% are described by the bank itself as inadequate. Set against genuine offsets — low loan-to-value on gold, non-callable bulk deposits with planned tenors, the intact contingency buffer and countercyclical investment through the cycle — but also against a 91% credit-deposit ratio and roughly 109% liquidity coverage, the combined rating lands at 49 out of 100, or Mixed: business quality 52, risk safety 44, with overall resilience recorded as unknown. In plain terms, this is a well-run, honest, fast-growing small bank whose growth so far has come at the cost of the returns and the funding quality that would make that growth worth paying for.

### Stakeholder Concentration — HIGH RISK
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; the numeric fingerprints could not be computed, so the call evidence decides alone.

*"Out-invented by the industry"*
