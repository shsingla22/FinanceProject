### The story in depth

ITC Hotels comes across as a well-priced, well-run business that has to carry a lot of assets to earn its keep. Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹36 is operating profit, and that margin has wobbled by only about ±0.6 points a year, which the quality framework reads as costs firmly under control and, separately, as a sign of rational pricing with few new entrants pushing in. That is why the Industry Structure area scores the highest of anything measured (2.0 out of a possible range). Working capital is tidy too: only about 20 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. So on the day-to-day economics of running the business, the record is genuinely good.

The catch is that the same asset-heavy shape that keeps competitors out also holds the returns down, and the records show both sides of that single trait. Every ₹1 of assets produces only ₹0.31 of sales, which the framework marks as a negative. Put the fat 36% margin on top of that thin asset turn and you land at roughly ₹9 of operating cash per ₹100 of capital employed — a 9% cash return, scored as neutral, and the reason the Return on Capital area comes in at just 0.33 while Capital Allocation sits at a modest 1.0. The multibagger screen tells a matching story: Brand Strength, Friendly Middleman and Forward Integrators register only as quantitative signals, Toll Roads is a partial fit, and Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) does not fit at all. Every one of those verdicts carries the same caveat — no conference-call judgement was available — so the patterns rest on numbers alone and none has been confirmed by what management actually says.

The combined rating of 50 out of 100, or 'Decent', is best understood as two very different scores averaged together: a multibagger fit of just 9 (weighted 55%) and a risk safety score of a perfect 100 (weighted 45%). The 8-channel risk review returned no risks at all and resilience is marked SOUND, so nearly all of the score comes from nothing having gone wrong rather than from anything going especially right. That deserves a note of caution: four of the seven quality areas — Competitive Advantage, Customer Benefits, Growth and Management — are unscored, and the pattern verdicts lack the conference-call layer. The verdict therefore leans heavily on what was not found. It is a stable, high-margin, low-risk business whose case for outsized returns is unproven rather than disproven.
