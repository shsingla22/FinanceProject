# ACC Ltd. (ACC) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CEMENT & CEMENT PRODUCTS*

ACC Ltd. makes and sells cement in India — it digs limestone, burns it into clinker at kiln plants, then grinds that clinker (often blended with fly ash) into bagged and bulk cement sold under the ACC and Adani Cement brands. It sells through two channels the management always splits out: "trade" (a dealer/retail network serving home-builders, about 74% of volumes by Q4 FY26) and "non-trade" (bulk supply to infrastructure and institutional projects), with demand driven by housing, infrastructure spending and rural construction. Money is made on the spread between the price realised per tonne or per bag and the cost per tonne of fuel, power, raw material and freight — so nearly every call is about price realisation, premium-product mix and cutting cost per tonne (targeting roughly INR3,650–4,000/tonne against INR4,400 achieved in FY26). It operates only in India, plant-by-plant across regions (Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, West Bengal, Jharkhand, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Maharashtra, Gujarat and the South), and since 2022 has been part of the Adani Group's cement business alongside parent Ambuja Cements — the calls are held jointly, and ACC is now being merged into Ambuja under a "One Cement Platform".

**The parts of the business:**

- **Cement (grey cement — trade and non-trade)** — The core and essentially the whole business: bagged cement sold to dealers and homebuilders plus bulk cement sold to infrastructure and institutional buyers, with the group's combined capacity at 109 million tonnes and FY26 sales of 73.7 million tonnes, and trade pricing running up to INR31 per bag above non-trade.
- **Premium cement products (ACC Gold, Ambuja Kawach)** — Higher-priced branded cement grades pushed as a margin lever — management calls them "blockbuster" super-premium products and says they made up 35–36% of trade sales, up 31% year-on-year, among the highest in the industry.
- **Clinker manufacturing and grinding units** — The upstream half of the operation — large kiln/clinker lines (Bhatapara, Jodhpur, Maratha, Ametha) feeding a wider spread of smaller grinding units near markets under a "hub and spoke" model, which management says cuts lead distance and freight; roughly 7 million tonnes of clinker and 10.7 million tonnes of grinding capacity were added in FY26.
- **Captive and green power (waste heat recovery plus renewables)** — Power generated for its own plants from waste heat recovery systems (grown from ~40 MW at Adani takeover toward 218 MW) and ~900 MW of solar/renewables, described purely as a cost lever — green power reached 32–37% of the mix, and surplus that cannot yet be consumed on site is being sold into the market meanwhile.
- **Alternative fuels and waste co-processing (Geoclean / AFR)** — Burning industrial and municipal waste as substitute kiln fuel, presented as both an ESG and a fuel-cost initiative, with the AFR share of the fuel mix rising to 9.5% against a 30% thermal-substitution goal.
- **Ready-mix concrete (RMC)** — A smaller downstream line supplying ready-mixed concrete, including a green variant called ECOMaxX; the calls note it is growing but give no revenue or volume figures, so it is clearly secondary to cement.
- **Captive limestone and coal mining** — Securing its own raw material and fuel — 70 million tonnes of new limestone reserves added in one quarter and active bidding for government coal-mine auctions — done to lower input costs rather than sold as a business in its own right.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

ACC's story is one company doing two things at once: growing fast and paying dearly for it. The growth is real and the end market behind it is genuinely expanding — per capita cement consumption of 275kg against a stated 1-billion-tonne opportunity, with PMAY urban housing worth INR11 lakh crore and sustained infrastructure spend underneath it (Structural end-market growth scores +2). Against that backdrop the company has taken share from roughly 15% to 16.6%, with volumes growing at about twice the industry rate and 19% over nine months, and it is building capacity from 67.5mt toward 109mt with new grinding units, clinker lines, waste-heat recovery and 1,000MW of renewable power. But the Market Share Gainers pattern only PARTIALLY fits, and for a specific reason: the share was largely bought rather than won. Sanghi, Penna, Asian, Tuticorin and Orient were acquisitions of weak assets where turnarounds ran late and extra maintenance capex was needed, the acquired plants still sit at 46-57% utilisation, and stripping out Orient, Q4 FY26 volumes were flat. That is why the Merger and acquisition check scores -1 while the market share check scores +2 — they are two readings of the same event. The bill shows up in the returns: for every INR100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings in the business, it generated INR-7 of operating cash this year, and Return on Capital scores -0.67 across the framework with Capital Allocation at 0.17. FY26 capex of INR7,500 crore is still going in ahead of proof that the last round earns its keep.

The second thread is the attempt to escape being a commodity, and it is half-working. The company keeps INR57 of every INR100 of sales after direct input costs, and premium cement has climbed from 26% of trade sales to 35-36%, growing 31% year on year, behind the Kawach and ACC Gold brands with about INR5 a bag better realisation than peers. Advertising and promotion scores +1 for genuinely building brand equity, and Customer Benefits is the one quality area that scores a perfect 1.0. Yet the Pricing Power pattern lands only at PARTIAL because this is mix, not price: Q4 FY26 realisation was flat at INR254 a bag, and management itself concedes the industry could not pass on an INR400-500 per tonne cost increase. That same admission is what drives the Good Enough Goods risk to ELEVATED — non-trade prices run INR31 a bag below trade and get rolled back, and realisations 'hardly moved' while peers moved 1.5-2%. So premiumisation is the silver lining inside the risk rather than a separate strength. The same doubling-up appears on cost: the Low Price Plus pattern is a consistent, itemised effort — kiln fuel from INR2.84 to INR1.59 per 1000 kCal, waste-heat recovery from 40 to 218MW, 900MW of renewables, dedicated rakes, shorter lead distances — but delivery lags the promises, and cost per tonne rose to INR4,500 in FY26 anyway. The only asset the framework treats as genuinely hard to copy is finite limestone: auctioned blocks in Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan and Assam plus 70 million tonnes added in a single quarter. Everything else management cites can be bought with capex by anyone, which is why Competitive Advantage scores a flat 0.0 and Cost to Replicate stops at PARTIAL. Not one of the eleven multibagger patterns is a full fit — eight PARTIAL, three NO FIT — giving a multibagger score of just 10 out of 100.

Pulling the risk review in, the picture is a strained but not fragile one. Overall resilience reads STRAINED, and the two ELEVATED risks are Cyclicality and Good Enough Goods — both of which trace straight back to the strengths above. EBITDA margin has swung from 6.1% to 14.4% on fuel cost moves alone, and the capex cycle the company chose adds a self-inflicted layer on top of the construction and coal cycles it cannot control. Two risks sit on WATCH. Government Dependency is structural: the same PMAY and infrastructure spending that makes the end market attractive also means demand, coal from government auctions and limestone from government blocks all run through policy — the growth driver and the dependency are the same thing. Stakeholder Concentration is asymmetric — the customer base is genuinely broad, with 74% trade mix and 70,000-plus contractors and influencers, but the input side leans on the promoter group, including a single 16 lakh tonne capped-price coal contract and group adjacencies in coal and logistics. Four risks are LOW, and they matter: no competing technology threatens the plants, consumer preferences moved toward premium and blended cement and the portfolio moved with them, demand is functional and repeat-purchase, and on new entrants ACC is the aggressor rather than the target. Two quality checks score -2 for being high-profile rather than obscure — leading with Adani portfolio identity, awards, and a 300 million-person media reach inside India's most scrutinised commodity sector — which is the cost of the same brand amplification that earns the +1 on advertising. Offsetting all of it, the balance sheet has stayed debt-free at AAA throughout, and the May 2026 call openly owned the cost miss, the three-to-six month capex delays and 'some disappointments', with governance independently reviewed. The combined rating lands at 43 out of 100 — 53 on business quality, 10 on multibagger fit, 64 on risk safety — a Mixed, two-star verdict: a real franchise in a real growth market, buying its position faster than it is currently earning on it.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.0× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ⚠️ Only about 42% of recent operating profit became cash (was 96%) — profits are getting stuck in paper, an early stress signal.
- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 13% to 15% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across the 13 calls the business swings with construction demand, fuel/coal prices and a self-inflicted capex cycle: EBITDA margin moved 6.1%→14.4% (Q3FY23) on fuel cost collapse, then FY26 saw cost per tonne rise to INR4,500 with pricing 'under relentless pressure and not able to pass on', and the 140mt-by-FY28 / 155mt-by-FY28 expansion was pushed to 'FY '30' with capex cut from INR7,500cr to INR6,000-6,500cr — expansion into a peak followed by recalibration is the textbook pattern.

> "the target plans of FY '28, it could move a year or 2, let us say, on a safer side, I would say that FY '30" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Cement is an operations/consumption product with a 65-74% retail trade mix and debt-free balance sheet, which cushions the cycle*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 3 of the last 10 years with swings of ±15 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 9% and 19% (±2.5 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 6% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 13%).

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED

## What to watch

- Utilisation at the acquired plants (Sanghi, Penna, Asian, Tuticorin, Orient) climbing off 46-57% — this is the single test of whether the Merger and acquisition check (-1) and the bought-not-won caveat on the Market Share Gainers pattern get revised upward.
- Cash return on capital turning from INR-7 per INR100 back to positive as the INR7,500 crore FY26 capex starts running — the Return on capital (headline) check at -2 is the heaviest drag on the whole rating.
- Realisation per bag breaking out of the flat INR254 level on price rather than mix — that would upgrade the Pricing Power pattern from PARTIAL and soften the ELEVATED Good Enough Goods risk at the same time.
- Cost per tonne coming back down from INR4,500 and the itemised savings programme landing on schedule — persistent lag keeps the Low Price Plus pattern at PARTIAL and keeps Cyclicality ELEVATED.
- Any change in the promoter-group input arrangements, particularly the 16 lakh tonne capped-price coal contract and group adjacencies in coal and logistics — this is the live edge of the Stakeholder Concentration risk currently on WATCH.
- Continued limestone block wins beyond the MP, Maharashtra, Rajasthan and Assam auctions — limestone is the only asset the Cost to Replicate pattern treats as genuinely hard to copy, and it is what stands between the current 0.0 Competitive Advantage score and a real one.
