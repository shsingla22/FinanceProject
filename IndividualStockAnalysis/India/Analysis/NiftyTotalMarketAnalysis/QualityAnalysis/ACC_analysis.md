# ACC Ltd. (ACC) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CEMENT & CEMENT PRODUCTS*

ACC Ltd. is an Indian cement maker: it digs limestone, burns it into clinker at its kilns, grinds that into cement, and sells it in bags to homebuilders and dealers ("trade") and in bulk to infrastructure and construction projects ("non-trade"), with the calls citing a company-wide trade share of about 74% by the March 2026 quarter. It makes money on the spread between the price per tonne of cement sold and the cost per tonne to make and deliver it — management talks constantly about realisation per bag versus an operating cost per tonne of roughly INR4,400–4,500, with fuel, power and freight the biggest cost lines. It operates only in India, from clinker plants and grinding units spread across the north, west, east and south (Bhatapara, Jamul, Marwar, Farakka, Sankrail, Sindri, Krishnapatnam, Ametha and others), shipping cement short distances into nearby "core markets" — average secondary lead distance of about 47 km. Since 2022 ACC has been owned by the Adani Group and run as part of a combined cement business alongside Ambuja Cements, Sanghi, Penna and Orient Cement; as of the January 2026 call, ACC is being merged into Ambuja under a "One Cement Platform," so most financial figures on these calls are given for the combined group (109 million tonnes of capacity, 73.7 million tonnes sold in FY26) rather than for ACC alone.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Cement (trade and non-trade)** — The core business — bagged cement sold through dealers to individual house builders, and bulk cement sold to infrastructure and institutional buyers; this is essentially all of revenue, and management notes trade pricing runs ahead of non-trade by up to INR31 per bag.
- **Premium cement products** — Higher-priced branded cement such as ACC Gold and Ambuja Kawach, sold at better margins; the calls say premium grew from 26% of trade sales in FY25 to about 35–36% by FY26, and it is repeatedly called a main lever for improving realisation.
- **Blended cement** — Cement where part of the clinker is replaced by fly ash and similar materials — 84% of volumes as of the Oct 2024 call, with the clinker factor down to 65%; it is a cost play, since less clinker means less fuel burned per tonne.
- **Ready-mix concrete (RMC)** — Pre-mixed concrete delivered to sites, including a green variant branded ECOMaxX; the Feb 2023 call describes it as growing but gives no size, and it is never quantified separately in later calls.
- **Captive and green power** — Waste-heat recovery units at the plants (40 MW at takeover, ~196–218 MW by 2025) plus roughly 900 MW of renewables, built mainly to cut the company's own power bill; until site-consumption approvals come through, management says this power is being sold into the open market.
- **Waste co-processing (Geoclean) / alternative fuels** — Burning industrial and other waste as substitute kiln fuel — a service branded Geoclean — with alternative fuel at 9.5% of the fuel mix and a stated target of a 30% thermal substitution rate by 2027; presented as a cost and sustainability initiative rather than a material revenue line.
- **Captive limestone and coal mining** — Securing its own raw material — 70 million tonnes of new limestone reserves added in one quarter of FY25, plus bidding for government coal-mine auctions — so the company controls the two inputs that drive most of its cost per tonne.

### The story in depth

ACC's story starts with real, measurable growth. The company sells into an end market that is genuinely expanding — Indian cement consumption per person is low and the housing and infrastructure runway is long — and it is taking share within that market, moving from roughly 15% to 16.6% with volumes growing at about twice the industry pace and FY26 volume up 16% to 73.7 million tonnes. Capacity has gone from 67.5 million tonnes to 109 million tonnes on the back of INR7,500 crore of growth spending in FY26 alone. Underneath the volume there is a serious cost effort — waste-heat recovery lifted from 40MW to about 218MW, roughly 900MW of green power, alternative fuels, own rail rakes and vessels, digitised operations, and a stated INR3,650 per tonne cost target for FY28. The customer-facing work is also real: more than 70,000 contractors and influencers engaged, 400 workshops, 36,000 certified technology sites, and premium products such as Kawach and ACC Gold that now make up 35-36% of trade sales, growing 31% a year and earning about INR5 more per bag. This is why the scorecard rates customer benefits and growth as the two clear strengths.

The trouble is that almost every one of those strengths is powered by the same engine — buying capacity and spending heavily — and that engine is also the main source of risk. The share gain that scores best on the quality checks is heavily inorganic, assembled from Sanghi, Penna, Orient, Asian and Tuticorin in about two years, which is precisely why the Market Share Gainers pattern is only a partial fit rather than a clean one. The merger and acquisition check scores at the bottom of the range because those deals were large, frequent and involved weak assets needing overhaul, with costs rising after the deals rather than falling. The evidence shows up directly in the numbers: acquired and new plants run at only 46-58% of capacity, the capacity target slipped from FY28 and the 140 million tonne goal moved out to FY30, the Warisaliganj plant slipped a quarter, and for every INR100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings in the business the company generated negative INR7 of operating cash this year. The same pattern repeats on the brand side: advertising and promotion spending is rising deliberately behind the Adani Cement re-badging and the premium push, which did lift premium sales, but the spend visibly hurt margins in the fourth quarter of FY26, and the brand has not yet converted into the pricing power that would justify it. Shareholders are funding this rather than being paid by it — about INR555-560 crore of dividend against INR12,350 crore of growth spending, no buyback, and INR8,300 crore raised through promoter warrants.

What is missing is the thing that would turn all this activity into durable value, namely the ability to charge more. Four of the eleven multibagger patterns are outright non-fits, and they are the ones that matter most: no pricing power, no toll-road-style lock-in, no recurring revenue, and no meaningful cost to replicate. FY26 realisation of INR254 a bag was flat to slightly down against INR255 a year earlier, price increases have historically been rolled back, and management itself said in May 2026 that the industry cannot pass on a INR400-500 per tonne cost increase. The clearest proof on replication cost is the company's own history — a well-funded entrant built a roughly 109 million tonne platform in under four years by buying and building. That commodity character is what makes the Good Enough Goods risk elevated, since dealers, contractors and masons largely steer the end buyer, and it is why industry structure and return on capital are the two weakest areas on the scorecard. Layered on top, demand is repeatedly justified by government programmes while coal and limestone access depends on government auctions, which makes government dependency the single high risk, and the debt-funded capacity race keeps cyclicality elevated. On the other side, the disclosure record is a genuine credit — bad news such as the Penna and Sanghi turnaround delays and the Warisaliganj slippage is reported promptly and specifically, the balance sheet is kept debt-free and top-rated, and related-party dealings get independent review, which is exactly what a company inside a very high-profile promoter group needs given that both the out-of-limelight and low-scrutiny checks score at the bottom. Netting it out — 50 out of 100 on business quality, 10 on pattern fit, 48 on risk safety — gives a combined 38 out of 100, a Mixed two-star verdict with resilience described as strained.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, Cyclicality, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Demand is repeatedly justified by government programmes (PMAY Urban 2 at INR11 lakh crores, budget allocations, road/rail infra), while inputs and economics depend on state action: coal-mine and limestone-block auctions run by government, GST 2.0 reforms shifting demand, Himachal excise-duty drawback and 'additional goods tax', and capex plans being recalibrated around 'the recent railway policies on bulk cement terminals'. The asset base is fixed mining infrastructure requiring public hearings and approvals.

> "we are bidding for coal mines in the auctions being conducted by the government" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Trade (retail/housing) channel is being deliberately raised to ~70-74% of sales, reducing reliance on infra/non-trade government-linked offtake.*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across all 13 calls results swing with cement price/bag, fuel and freight commodity costs, and a debt-funded capacity race from 67.5mt to a 140–155mt target; by May 2026 costs had escalated ~INR400-500/tonne industry-wide, the 140mt goal slipped to 'FY '30', and capex was cut to INR6,000-6,500cr with management admitting they cannot forecast beyond 2-3 quarters. Demand itself is described in cycle terms (monsoon, infra offtake, GDP multiple), and margins have oscillated from 6.1% to 17.5% EBITDA and back.

> "it will be very difficult to provide any long-term estimates for right now till the time things stabilize over the next 2, 3 quarters" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Cement is an operations/consumption-linked flow product rather than customer capex equipment, and the company invested counter-cyclically (Sanghi, Penna, Orient acquisitions) while remaining debt-free with AAA ratings.*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 3 of the last 10 years with swings of ±15 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.

## What to watch

- Utilisation at the acquired plants: the 46-58% run rates in the Capex for growth check are the bridge between the spending and the returns, and the negative 7% cash return on capital will not turn positive until they fill up.
- Realisation per bag: the Pricing Power non-fit rests on INR254 versus INR255 a year earlier and on rollback-prone hikes, so a sustained increase that sticks through a cost cycle would be the first real evidence of a moat.
- Premium mix beyond 35-36% of trade sales without further margin damage: this is where the Brand Strength partial verdict and the rising advertising spend flagged in the Advertisement and promotion check either pay off or do not.
- Any further slippage in the capacity timeline after FY28 moved to FY30, which would compound both the Merger and acquisition check score and the elevated Cyclicality risk from the debt-funded capacity race.
- The trade channel's rise toward 70-74% of sales, since that is the stated offset to the high-risk Government Dependency on infrastructure offtake, coal and limestone auctions.
