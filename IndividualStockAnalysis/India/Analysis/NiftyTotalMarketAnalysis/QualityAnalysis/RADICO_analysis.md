# Radico Khaitan Ltd (RADICO) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSUMER GOODS*

Radico Khaitan is an Indian alcoholic-spirits company that makes and sells branded liquor — what the calls call IMFL (Indian Made Foreign Liquor) — including whisky, vodka, gin, rum and single malts. It earns money by selling cases of these bottled spirits through India's state-controlled liquor markets (each state has its own excise policy and pricing approvals), through the military canteen (CSD) channel, through bars and restaurants (the 'on-trade' channel), and by exporting; it was described as the largest exporter of IMFL from India in Q1 FY23. Profits come from a mix of volume growth and 'premiumisation' — steadily shifting sales toward higher-priced brands, which lifts revenue per case and margins, while raw-material costs (grain, ENA/alcohol, glass and packaging) drive margins the other way. The company is backward-integrated into distilling (its Rampur distillery in Uttar Pradesh, a Rampur dual-feed plant and a greenfield distillery at Sitapur), operates across Indian states plus international/duty-free markets, and in FY26 crossed INR 6,000 crores of net revenue and INR 1,000 crores of EBITDA on roughly 9.5-9.75 million IMFL cases per quarter.

**The parts of the business:**

- **IMFL business (branded spirits)** — The core business of selling bottled branded spirits by the case; it accounted for about 79% of net revenue in Q1 FY23 and is the segment management reports volumes, realisations and margins on.
- **Prestige & Above (P&A) category** — The premium end of the IMFL range (brands such as Magic Moments vodka, Morpheus, 8PM Premium Black, Royal Ranthambore, After Dark, 1965), which management says drives growth — it rose from about 26% of IMFL volume in Q1 FY22 to 43% by Q1 FY25, and grew 26-28% by volume in the FY26 quarters.
- **Luxury portfolio** — The top-priced brands — Rampur Indian Single Malt (including Asava, Trigun and 1943 Virasat), Jaisalmer Indian Craft Gin, Sangam World Malt and The Spirit of Kashmyr vodka — priced from roughly INR 4,000 to over INR 10,000 a bottle; it delivered INR 475 crores of sales value in FY26 with guidance of 25% value growth in FY27.
- **Regular / Popular category** — The lower-priced mass-market IMFL brands; still a sizeable chunk of volume but deliberately rationalised at times, and swung sharply with state policy — up 33% in Q3 FY26 on the Andhra Pradesh route-to-market change, down in Q4 FY26 on a high base and Maharashtra/Karnataka policy changes.
- **Non-IMFL business** — The rest of revenue outside branded IMFL (roughly a fifth of net revenue in FY23), where management repeatedly says commodity inflation hit gross margins hardest and price increases came later than in IMFL.
- **International business** — Exports and duty-free/travel-retail sales, run under a dedicated President - International Business; it includes travel-retail-exclusive launches like Rampur TRIGUN at $200 a bottle and Sangam World Malt, and Rampur single malt being served on Air India first and business class.
- **Distillation and manufacturing assets** — Own distilleries — the Rampur distillery (operating since 1943), a Rampur dual-feed plant and the Sitapur greenfield project, on which INR 165 crores had been spent by Q1 FY23 — plus expanded gin distillation capacity and a new wholly-owned Scotland subsidiary approved in Q3 FY26 to secure matured malt supply; this backward integration is cited as a source of long-term margin expansion.

### The story in depth

Radico Khaitan earns a "Decent" 3-star rating (60 out of 100: 45% x 70 on business quality, 30% x 55 on multibagger fit, 25% x 48 on risk safety), and the story behind that score is a single one: the company has spent the period moving itself up-market. The quality framework scores full marks on customer benefits, growth, industry structure and return on capital — it keeps ₹68 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs, generates ₹27 of operating cash for every ₹100 of capital employed, and rides an Indian spirits market that is premiumising because of demographics and aspiration. The same up-market push is what produces the three strongest multibagger patterns. Brand Strength is a strong fit on the back of the Rampur "since 1943" heritage, the Jim Murray tasting and the Air India first/business listing, all supported by advertising and promotion held to a disciplined 6-8% of IMFL revenue every year. Innovation Dominance is a strong fit because named launches arrive every single year with disclosed revenue impact — Trigun and Royal Ranthambore, Rampur Asava, Sangam World Malt and Jaisalmer Gold, Virasat and Spirit of Kashmyr. Market Share Gainers is a strong fit on hard numbers: Andhra Pradesh share up from about 15% to 26% to become the leading player, the highest share in UP country liquor, more than half of India's luxury gin held by Jaisalmer, and the largest IMFL exporter position.

The uncomfortable part is that several of those same strengths are also where the risk sits. The premium, taste-led end of the portfolio that drives the brand and innovation patterns is exactly what makes Fashion Risk elevated — Jaisalmer, Rampur, Virasat, Kashmyr, D'YAVOL tequila, flavour extensions and event tie-ins all depend on staying fashionable, softened only by the fact that there are many brands across price tiers and a large repeat-purchase base such as Magic Moments at 8.6m cases and After Dark at 3.1m. The licensing and distillery rules that earn a top score on barriers to entry, and that keep margins within a narrow ±2.1 point band, are the very same state excise machinery that makes Government Dependency the single highest risk: price increases have to be granted state by state, which is why the Pricing Power pattern is only a partial fit — roughly 3% realisation impact in Q1FY23, only 150-170bps guided for FY25, about 60bps of the FY27 margin bridge — and why gross margin still fell from 47.1% to 41.5% on grain inflation before recovering to 48%. Policy also manufactures competition it cannot outspend: Maharashtra Made Liquor, open only to locally-owned producers, cut that state's market from 2.4m to 1.8m cases a month with the industry down about 20% in Q3 FY26, which is why New Entrants sits on watch despite management's view that nobody is entering the luxury tier. And government corporations acting as sole buyer in several states produce the Telangana receivables that have dragged for two years — feeding both the Stakeholder Concentration watch and the framework's worst score, a 177-day gap between paying suppliers and collecting from customers.

What holds the middle together is management and what still limits the score is proof of durability. Stewardship scores full marks — growth has been organic only, capex was funded straight through the inflation downturn, net debt came down ₹329cr in FY26 with a stated path to debt-free, and there were no large acquisitions in five years — and Management is the highest-scoring quality area. Even so, the Corporate Culture pattern is only partial, because alongside stable promoter-family leadership, long-horizon capex into Rampur dual-feed and Sitapur, and a new 20% minimum dividend policy, the flagship promise slipped. Growth itself scores minus on persistence: about 24% a year on average, but lumpy from year to year, matching a Cyclicality risk that is elevated because gross margin swings with agri and packaging cycles even though volumes rose every year from 6.82m to 9.75m cases. The framework's weakest area is competitive advantage at zero, which sits consistently with the patterns that do not fit at all — Toll Roads (a discretionary drink bought by end consumers, with no switching or certification lock-in), Low Price Plus (management explicitly refuses to cut price for share) and Forward Integrators — and with the Good Enough Goods watch, where the regular and country-liquor half of volumes competes purely on price against cheap local alternatives. Only the luxury end has a genuine time barrier: stock laid down five or six years ago reaching market from FY24, a 1943 lineage, and a Scotland subsidiary to secure matured malt, which is why Cost to Replicate is a partial rather than a full fit. Overall resilience is judged sound: a company converting a real consumer trend into real share, with its margins, cash timing and even its right to sell still decided by state governments.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, Cyclicality, Fashion Risk. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.2× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 58% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 13% to 18% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Pricing, route-to-market, distribution and even the right to sell are set by state excise policy in every quarter of the record: price increases must be granted state by state, Telangana receivables are owed by government and delayed for two years, Maharashtra's MML policy shrank the industry ~20% in a quarter, Andhra's route-to-market change swung regular volumes 33% up then negative, plus surrogate-ad rules, duty rationalisation, Bihar prohibition and US tariffs/UK FTA.

> "The collection issue has been going on for the last two years... hopefully, the entire old outstanding should be cleared within a month" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Wide multi-state and export footprint so no single policy regime dominates; management notes several states are proactively rationalising duty*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 2 numeric fingerprints reinforce it.

**What the calls show:** Demand is a repeat-purchase consumption product rather than customer capex, but gross margin swung materially with agri/packaging commodity cycles across the 16 calls — 47.1% (Q1FY23) to 41.5% (Q1FY25) on grain inflation, then back to 48% (Q4FY26) on benign inputs — and the FY23-25 period carried a large Rampur Dual-Feed/Sitapur greenfield capex build that pushed net debt up before deleveraging.

> "Grain price inflation had a negative impact of 335 basis points on a year-on-year basis on gross margin" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/consumable demand (IMFL volumes grew through the whole period, 6.82m to 9.75m cases); backward integration and premiumisation dampen input swings; capex now only maintenance and the company is near debt-free*

- ⚠️ Year-to-year sales growth swings by ±52 percentage points — boom-and-bust revenue, not a steady compounder.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 11% and 17% (±2.1 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 23% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 4% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

## What to watch

- Government Dependency (high risk): more Maharashtra-style local-producer rules, or slower state-by-state price approvals, would directly cap the already-partial Pricing Power pattern.
- Telangana government receivables and the 177-day cash cycle (Cost of working capital check, Stakeholder Concentration): further slippage turns a low score into a cash problem, faster payment would relieve both.
- Gross margin holding near the 48% seen in Q4FY26 rather than sliding back toward 41.5% (Cyclicality risk) — this is the test of whether premiumisation, not benign grain prices, is doing the work.
- Demand staying with the taste-led luxury brands — Jaisalmer, Rampur, Virasat, Kashmyr (Fashion Risk, Innovation Dominance pattern): a cooling in any of them hits the strongest patterns and the highest-value volumes at once.
- Follow-through on the debt-free path after the ₹329cr FY26 reduction, and no repeat of the slipped flagship promise (Disciplined stewards check, Corporate Culture pattern) — this is what keeps Management the strongest area.
