# Housing & Urban Development Corporation Ltd. (HUDCO) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

HUDCO is an Indian government-linked lender (an NBFC that RBI has since mandated to become an infrastructure finance company), sitting under the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, that makes long-term loans for housing and urban infrastructure projects. Its customers are mainly state government entities, state-owned corporations and urban local bodies building things like metros, roads, water systems and housing — HUDCO itself does no capex, it only lends to those who do. It earns money on the gap between what it pays for funds (bank loans, repo-linked loans, domestic bonds, external commercial borrowings including Japanese-yen loans, FCNR deposits and an RBI forex window) and what it charges borrowers, running a spread of about 2% and a net interest margin of about 3%. It operates across India in every state and calls itself 'sector agnostic' — free to fund anything on the government's harmonized infrastructure list — with the loan book growing from roughly INR80,000 crore in FY23 to about INR1.34 lakh crore by Q1 FY26 and a stated target of INR3 lakh crore by 2030.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Infrastructure project finance (lending to state government entities)** — The core business: sanctioning and then disbursing long-term loans to state entities for capital-intensive projects, and by far the biggest driver — sanctions went from about INR25,000 crore in FY23 to INR83,000 crore in FY24 to roughly INR1.24–1.27 lakh crore in FY25, with disbursements rising from INR8,400 crore to about INR40,000 crore over the same period.
- **Mobility / transport financing** — Funding metros, rapid rail (RRTS), state road transport corporations, e-mobility, ring roads and multi-modal corridors, plus refinancing metros' existing foreign-currency loans with cheaper domestic loans — described as a newer, fast-expanding segment rather than a separately sized one.
- **Water and sanitation / basic urban services** — Loans for water supply and water-body rejuvenation projects tied to central government programmes like AMRUT 2.0 and Swachh Bharat Mission, which management says is drawing heavy demand from states.
- **Roads** — Financing state-level HAM (hybrid annuity) and state-owned EPC road projects, cited as one of the capital-intensive areas the company moved into as it diversified beyond housing.
- **Housing** — The company's founding purpose and still in the name — lending for housing projects including PMAY-linked work, though on recent calls management talks about it far less than urban infrastructure.
- **Consultancy / advisory to states and urban local bodies** — Advising state counterparts on structuring and understanding projects (for example under AMRUT 2.0) alongside the lending — presented as a support activity, with no revenue figure given on the calls.
- **Urban Invest window / Urban Challenge Fund role** — A recently launched platform meant to be a one-stop shop taking a city project from concept to bankability, financial closure, investors, a HUDCO loan and central-scheme funding — positioned as the vehicle to capture the government's INR1 lakh crore Urban Challenge Fund subsidy plus roughly INR2 lakh crore of institutional finance, and still awaiting final guidelines as of the Feb 2026 and Jul 2026 calls.
- **Borrowing / treasury** — Not a revenue line but central to how it makes money: raising funds cheaply across banks, bonds, ECBs and FCNR (borrowing about INR20,000 crore at ~6.32% in one quarter) — and the source of a one-off INR470 crore fair-value loss on FCNR borrowings in 9M FY26 that the company says has now been exited.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** HUDCO is a Navratna PSU under MoHUA whose loan book is almost entirely to state governments and state entities, with more than 70% guaranteed by state guarantee or budget provision; across all seven calls management frames growth off central schemes and policy (AMRUT, Jal Jeevan, Swachh Bharat, SASCI loans, the Urban Challenge Fund, Finance Commission grants) and even seeks GoI liability support, so pricing, volumes and status (Navratna/Maharatna) are set by policy rather than the market.

> "considering over 70% of our book is either guaranteed by state or through budget provisions" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Sector-agnostic mandate plus a nascent, deliberately untargeted PPP/private-sector division (2 projects, ~INR6,000-7,000 crore sanctioned in Q1 FY27) and a shift toward 'bankable' cash-flow-backed projects — but this is early and small.*
