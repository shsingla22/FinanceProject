### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 5. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 8 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Toll Roads**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Delhi's regulated aero tariff reset lifted aero revenues 127%/166%/173%/161% year-on-year across FY26 while the airport still handled record quarterly traffic of 20.8m — airlines cannot route around the gateway. Management itself notes ATF is 55–60% of airline opex, framing airport charges as the small-but-critical slice, and the TDSAT/AERA process shows regulatory-anchored economics. (The long view had said: Airport charges are a small slice of an airline's cost base (management notes ATF alone is 55-60% of airline opex) yet the asset is indispensable, and Delhi/Hyderabad operate as concession-anchored local monopolies; but the pricing is set by AERA rather than by a rational oligopoly, and there is no certification/standards angle.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: GMR's operator reputation is repeatedly cited via global accolades and it is winning new mandates under the same name (Nagpur, Bhogapuram, Crete, Delhi cargo city), but the calls disclose no A&P spend, heritage/provenance story, or evidence that the brand itself commands a price premium — the pricing comes from regulation and location. (The long view had said: Repeated award citations and 'global benchmarks' language, plus a track record that helps win new concessions (Nagpur, Bhogapuram, Crete, Goa), but there is no A&P spend, heritage story or evidence of consumers choosing an airport by the GMR name.)

**That weakened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No R&D spend, pipeline, or named innovations are disclosed in any of the four calls; the capital cycle is construction-led (Bhogapuram, Crete, cargo city, Aerocity real estate) and funded by debt rather than by high gross margins recycled into R&D. (The long view had said: Named operational-technology launches (AI digital twin, next-gen Airport Predictive Operation Centre) recur across calls, but no R&D spend is ever disclosed, no pipeline economics are quantified, and no revenue impact is attributed to innovation.)

*Unchanged: Forward Integrators (STRONG FIT); Cost to Replicate (STRONG FIT); Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Demand is operations-linked flow (passengers, retail spend per pax) rather than customer capex, but the last 12 months show real swings — traffic fell 3.5% year-on-year in Q2FY26 on airspace closures, then +2.5%, then +1%, and Q4FY26 opened with management flagging an ATF price spike consuming 55-60% of airline opex, route suspensions and a fare-driven traffic slowdown; airline fleet availability (Airbus/Boeing delivery delays) is an external supply cycle GMR does not control. (The long view had said: Demand is operations-linked passenger flow rather than customer capex, and tariffs are RAB-based, but the trajectory across FY25–FY26 shows traffic swinging from 10% year-on-year (Q3FY25) to 2.5% and then 1% on aircraft-supply constraints, the Air India crash, airspace closures and an ATF price spike — while the company itself is deep in an investment cycle (Bhogapuram, Delhi, Crete, Nagpur) with net debt of INR340bn at 5.5x EBITDA.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Delhi alone contributed roughly half of group income (INR20.2bn of INR40.4bn in Q4FY26) and 20.8mn of 31.9mn quarterly passengers, so one concession asset and one regulatory counterparty (AERA/TDSAT) drive results; on the customer side, a handful of named airlines — IndiGo, Air India, Akasa — dictate capacity and hence traffic, and the cargo concession showed counterparty fragility when a single operator's security clearance was terminated. (The long view had said: Delhi alone contributes roughly half of total income and its EBITDA swing drove the whole FY26 result, while system traffic is repeatedly explained by the fleet decisions of two or three airlines — Air India, IndiGo and Akasa — with management quantifying next-year growth off their delivery schedules and blaming flat traffic on the Air India crash and IndiGo disruption.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Good Enough Goods (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); New Entrants (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
