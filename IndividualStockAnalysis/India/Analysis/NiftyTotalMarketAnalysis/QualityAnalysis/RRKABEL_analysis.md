# R R Kabel (RRKABEL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

R R Kabel is an Indian consumer-electrical company that makes and sells electrical wires and cables — the kind used to wire homes, offices, factories and power infrastructure — plus a smaller range of household electrical goods such as fans, lights, switches and appliances. It sells mainly through a large branded retail and distribution network in India (management cites over 124,000 retailers and more than 325,000 electricians influencing sales), alongside institutional/B2B customers, and it exports to roughly 65 countries; exports have run at about 27%–29% of revenue and management calls the company one of India's largest wire-and-cable exporters with about 9% of that export market. It earns money by converting copper, aluminium and PVC into finished wire and cable and selling it at a branded margin, so profits swing with metal prices and with how quickly it can pass price changes through. It was described at IPO as the fifth-largest branded wire-and-cable player in India with about 7% market share by value (FY23); full-year FY26 revenue was INR 9,722 crores with EBITDA of INR 789 crores and profit after tax of INR 492 crores, and it operates its own manufacturing plants with a INR 1,200 crore capex programme running FY26–FY28.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Wires & Cables (W&C)** — The core business — house/building wires and heavier power and other cables sold under the RR Kabel brand to retail, institutional and export customers; it is about 90% of revenue (INR 8,764 crores in FY26) and essentially all of the profit, and management calls it the company's primary growth engine.
- **Wires (within W&C)** — Building/housing wire sold largely into real estate — homes, offices, hotels, restaurants — historically the bigger half of the segment and the more retail, brand-driven part.
- **Cables (within W&C)** — Power and industrial cables tied to infrastructure and electrification projects; a smaller but faster-growing piece that management has been pushing from about 30% to 35% of segment revenue, and where most of the current capacity expansion is aimed.
- **Exports** — Not a separate segment but a major channel — wire and cable shipped to about 65 countries, roughly a quarter to nearly a third of total revenue, dependent on international quality certifications and exposed to shipping and geopolitical disruptions such as the Red Sea and Middle East conflicts.
- **FMEG (Fast-Moving Electrical Goods)** — The consumer-appliance arm — fans (the largest contributor), lighting, switches, switchgear and appliances sold under the RR and Luminous brands, plus professional lighting under Arraystorm — about 10% of revenue (INR 959 crores in FY26); it has been loss-making since the 2022 acquisition of Luminous's home electrical business, though losses are narrowing and management targets breakeven in FY27.
- **Manufacturing and backward integration** — Own plants that also produce inputs such as copper wire rod, PVC compound and electron-beam (E-beam) treated cable, which management says helps control cost and quality; capex of about INR 1,200 crores over FY26–FY28 is going mainly into cable and wire capacity and is expected to add roughly INR 4,000–4,500 crores of annual revenue potential.
- **Project RRise (strategic roadmap)** — Management's three-year plan, referenced in the FY26 calls, targeting roughly 18% annual growth in Wires & Cables and 25% in FMEG, adding up to about 2.5x EBITDA growth over the period.

### The story in depth

R R Kabel comes out of these three reviews as a genuinely good distribution business inside a commodity-priced industry, and the combined rating of 60 out of 100 — 'Decent', built from 62 on business quality, 44 on multibagger fit and 74 on risk safety — reflects exactly that split. The quality framework's strongest marks all cluster in one place: reach. The company sells through 124,000-plus retailers and runs a digitalised loyalty programme covering 325,000-plus electricians, sells in 65 countries with about 90% of exports under its own brand, and management repeatedly credits this network for its outperformance. Around that sit an asset-light balance sheet (₹1 of assets producing ₹2.10 of sales), a ₹1,200cr FY26-28 brownfield capex programme aimed roughly 80% at cables at a stated 3-3.5x revenue conversion, and an end market growing at about twice GDP as buyers shift from unbranded to branded product (57% branded share in FY15 to 72% in FY23). With only about 7% of the branded wire and cable market as the fifth-largest player, there is plenty of room left to grow into.

The pattern screen essentially re-describes that same distribution strength, which is why the two strongest pattern verdicts and the sharpest risks trace back to one shared fact: the electrician, not the household, picks the brand. That fact makes 'Friendly Middleman' a likely fit and makes the 23-year network plus country-by-country US and European certifications genuinely hard to copy. The same fact drives the 'Good Enough Goods' risk, because the product is not complex and cheaper unorganised wire remains on the shelf — the loyalty of the middleman is the thing holding the premium in place. A second trait cuts the same way. Wire and cable is a 'C category' item at 1-1.5% of a project's cost, so volumes hold up when prices rise, which produces the toll-road-like language in the calls; but prices themselves are pure pass-through of copper moves, taken several times a quarter, not increases above cost. So 'Pricing Power' only partially fits, and 'Cyclicality' is the one elevated risk: FY25 saw domestic wire volumes flat to down, exports down about 5.6% and segment margin fall to roughly 6.4% on volatile metals, while Q3 FY26 revenue jumped 42.3%. Growth in volumes has beaten the 14-15% industry rate in good quarters (28% in H1 FY24, 30%-plus in Q3 FY26) but only about 6% over nine months of FY25, which is why 'Market Share Gainers' is judged uneven rather than proven. And the capex that funds growth is being added by every competitor at once, which is the 'New Entrants' watch — offset for now by industry-wide pricing discipline and phased six-month capacity tranches.

The clear weak spot, and the one place the three reviews agree without qualification, is the move beyond wires and cables. The Luminous home electrical acquisition of May 2022 built the FMEG segment, but that segment has stayed loss-making across the whole window and scores -1 in the quality framework; the promised breakeven has slipped four times, from FY25 to early FY26 to March 2026 and now FY27. That single record drags on three separate places at once: it holds 'Brand Strength' to a partial fit because the brand has not stretched profitably beyond its core, it holds 'Corporate Culture' to partial despite capex being delivered on schedule and shortfalls being admitted plainly, and it sits alongside two changes in the MD/Chairman seat in three years. Industry Structure is the only quality area scoring below zero. Against that, the risk review is reassuring on the things that usually break companies: no platform or substitute technology risk, no fashion exposure with about 90% of FY26 revenue in functional wires and cables, consumer preferences moving toward branded and compliant product rather than away, and no single customer or licence to lose — though management did concede that the Middle East export geography is large enough that a disruption there dents revenue, and European shipments already caused visible softness in Q3 FY25. Overall resilience is judged sound. The investment case, put plainly, is a strong distribution franchise with real runway in its core, priced against a commodity input it cannot control and a diversification effort that has not yet worked.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across the 11 calls, revenue and margins swing with copper/aluminium: FY25 saw wire volumes fall (domestic wire flat to -9%, 9M volume growth only 6%) and W&C segment margin drop to ~6.4% on 'volatile metal prices', while FY26 Q3 revenue jumped 42.3% and EBITDA 86% 'mainly due to higher commodity prices' after a ~20-25% copper move in one quarter. Demand is also tied to construction/infrastructure and to channel stocking-destocking, and the company is committing INR1,200cr of capex into a period every player is expanding capacity.

> "prices have moved by almost 20%, 25% just in a single quarter, so there were pressures on working capital at our channel side" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/consumption-linked demand — wire & cable is a C-category item at 1-1.5% of project cost, so 'consumption will not be reduced just if prices are increasing'; continuous price pass-through kept the Q3 FY26 margin impact to ~0.5%; diversified domestic + 65-country export mix.*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 0 of the last 6 years with swings of ±18 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 6% and 9% (±1.1 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.

## What to watch
