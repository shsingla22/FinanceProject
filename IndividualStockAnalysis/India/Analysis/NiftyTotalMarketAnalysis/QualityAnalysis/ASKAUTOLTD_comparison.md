**Checks that improved:**

- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Not obscure — the company has been listed since 2023 and its calls draw questions from CLSA and Nuvama, though it remains a fairly plain mid-cap ancillary rather than a crowded story. | The long view had said: Post-IPO the company is actively covered by numerous institutional brokerages (ICICI, Nuvama, JM, IIFL, YES, Narnolia, Monarch) and runs PR-hosted calls — no longer an ignored niche.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing is rational and formula-based (raw material cost plus value addition, accepted by OEMs) and GST 2.0 is formalising the market against unorganised entrants, though the persistence of grey-market duplicators shows entry into the aftermarket is not hard. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Some barriers from OEM validation and technology tie-ups, and pricing behaviour is rational (cost pass-through, no discount war), but parts of the portfolio are commoditised enough to carry very low margins.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Good → Weak. Now: The two-wheeler cycle is at or past its all-time peak, not near a bottom — FY26 production of 26.7 million units surpassed the previous FY19 peak — so the cyclical tailwind is largely spent. | The long view had said: Entered the period near a cyclical trough with two-wheeler volumes still below the FY19 peak and rode the recovery, but that upturn has now been fully harvested with FY26 surpassing the prior peak.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: The record is strong but not cleanly separable from a cyclical upswing — industry production grew 11.8% to an all-time peak in the same year, and much of the margin gain came from mechanically shedding a loss-making business rather than from operating skill alone. | The long view had said: Results are partly a cyclical and policy windfall (GST 2.0, two-wheeler recovery, aluminium pass-through), but management itself separates these from underlying performance, making the skill-vs-luck read reasonably robust.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: OEM qualification for safety-critical braking parts implies some switching cost and the independent aftermarket grew 24.7%, but there is no evidence of a captive lock-in strong enough to price independently — management must still ask OEMs to absorb input costs. | The long view had said: Real switching costs from safety-critical validation cycles and just-in-time online supply to OEMs, plus a captive independent aftermarket channel, though customers demonstrably can and do reallocate business.
- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Excellent → Good. Now: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.63 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: This is a capital-intensive casting/component business — Rs. 500 Cr capex on ~Rs. 4,200 Cr revenue and multi-quarter plant ramp-ups — so it is not asset-light, though returns are high at 26.9% ROACE. | The long view had said: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.63 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: Roughly 2:1 asset turn on new aluminium capacity with ROACE of 26.9% and ROAE 25.3% in FY26, though this is a capital-heavy casting business kept deliberately under-utilised (Karoli at 65%) to hold spare capacity.
- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Management quantifies unflattering effects rather than hiding them (alloy denominator drag on margin, 30bps and 80bps), openly owns the missed export target, and corrects an analyst's misunderstanding by pointing back to prior guidance — though the habit of restating growth ex-wheel-assembly and ex-aluminium consistently flatters the headline. | The long view had said: Consistently strips out pass-through and business-exit effects to show real growth, quantifies self-inflicted losses, admits missed export targets, and refuses to answer from memory rather than guess.

Now: A modest, consistent dividend of Rs. 1.85 per Rs. 2 share against FY26 EPS of Rs. 15.08 (~12% payout) with no buyback — coherent retention given 26.9% ROACE, but no evidence of opportunistic or disciplined capital-return policy. | The long view had said: Coherent and steadily rising dividend (50% → 75% → 92.5% of face value) at a conservative payout with cash prioritised for growth capex, but no buybacks and no articulated valuation-linked policy.

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

**That eased:**

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 9 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
