# Welspun Living (WELSPUNLIV) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Welspun Living (formerly Welspun India) is an Indian home-textiles manufacturer that makes and sells things people put in and around the home — bath towels, bedsheets, pillows, rugs and carpets, plus specialised non-woven fabrics. Most of its money comes from bulk supply to large overseas retailers, above all in the United States (its biggest market, though management says nearly 40-41% of business now sits outside the US, across Europe, UK, India and rest of world), where India holds roughly 45% of US cotton terry towel imports and 60% of cotton sheets. Alongside that business-to-business export base, it has been shifting to also sell directly to consumers under its own and licensed brands, in India and abroad — the name change to 'Welspun Living' was made to signal becoming a complete 'home solutions' provider. Manufacturing is mainly in India (Anjar, Vapi), with newer pillow plants in Ohio and Nevada in the US; consolidated revenue was about INR 9,468 crores in FY26, down from a higher base after a year of US tariff disruption.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Home Textile Exports (core business)** — Bulk manufacture and supply of terry towels and bedsheets to big international retailers, mostly in the US — this is the largest part of the company and the main revenue driver, and its FY26 revenues fell about 11% on tariff and weak-demand pressure.
- **Domestic Consumer Business** — Selling home textiles to Indian shoppers under the 'Welspun' brand (the most widely distributed home textile brand in India, present in 600+ towns and over 15,500 outlets) and the mass-premium 'Spaces' brand through general trade, modern trade and quick commerce; FY26 domestic revenue was INR 657 crores, up 9%, and it reached EBITDA breakeven in Q4 FY26.
- **Global Brands** — Branded sales outside India, including the company's own luxury heritage brand Christy (UK-led, expanding to US, Middle East, Africa) and licensed names like Martha Stewart, Disney and COCOCOZY; management says global brands were about 12% of the business in FY26, with Christy growing 15% that year.
- **Flooring (domestic and global)** — Carpets, area rugs and hard flooring sold to homes, hotels, offices and institutions — the India side has grown steadily (18% in FY26, including big projects such as the wall-to-wall flooring for Bharat Mandapam at the G20 Summit), while the export flooring side fell about 25% in FY26 on shipping disruption and tariffs.
- **Advanced Textiles** — Technical non-woven fabrics (spunlace) used in hygiene, personal and home care, wipes, medical, femcare, protective apparel, insulation and filtration; a smaller unit at INR 452 crores of FY26 revenue, down 20% that year.
- **US pillow manufacturing (Ohio and Nevada)** — Company-owned plants making utility bedding and pillows inside the US — Ohio started commercial production in September 2024 and reached 60% utilisation with the pillow business doubling to USD 27.5 million in FY26, and a new Nevada facility began production in March 2026.
- **Welspun Hospitality (institutional)** — Supplying home textiles to hotel chains such as Hyatt, IHG, Wyndham and Marriott, described as a bridge between the B2B business and consumer brand visibility; India's religious tourism and pilgrimage infrastructure is also called out as a growing institutional demand source.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Welspun Living is a large home-textile manufacturer that has spent years doing sensible, visible things with its money. It has kept marketing spend at roughly 10% of domestic sales every year and is only now seeing the payoff, with the Welspun brand up 44% and Spaces up 19% year-on-year in Q4 FY26 and the domestic business reaching EBITDA breakeven. It has put money into growth rather than mere upkeep — a ₹860 crore FY25 capex plan, the Anjar towel and Jacquard lines, 40 TPD more spinning, and greenfield pillow plants in Ohio and Nevada. It has widened its geographic base so that non-US sales are now 41% of the total, taken Christy into the Middle East, US and Africa (+31% year-on-year) and Welhome into Japan, the UK, China and Taiwan. It has won documented shelf share in the US, growing bed-sheet dispatches 18% against a 5% market, and holding towels flat against a 2% market decline. Its research work turns into sellable product — innovation runs at 20–22% of revenue on a 50-patent base including HygroCotton, GX Pillow and Wel-Trak. On the headline measure it turned ₹16 of operating cash for every ₹100 of shareholder funds plus borrowings, a 16% cash return. These same strengths are what put four of the multibagger patterns — Brand Strength, Innovation Dominance, Forward Integrators and Market Share Gainers — on the board as partial fits, along with Cost to Replicate, where the 50 patents, world-leading Jacquard capacity, traceability and the world's top S&P Global sustainability score of 90 in textiles now act as a sourcing gate for retailers.

The trouble is that every one of those eleven patterns stops at partial or no fit, and the reason is the same trait that produces several of the strengths: this is a cotton converter selling other people's shelf space. Over 80% of the business is FOB supply where the retailer owns the customer relationship and the end consumer does not know Welspun is involved — which is precisely why the Good Enough Goods risk is rated HIGH, why Pricing Power and Toll Roads are outright no-fits, and why Network Effects scores the worst mark in the whole framework at -2. Scale in this model buys cost advantage, not a self-reinforcing position, so Low Price Plus registers as a genuine but unprotected cost culture: overhead rationalisation, procurement work, plant productivity and automation delivered a 313bps sequential margin recovery and ₹956 crore of free cash flow in the FY26 downturn, but it did not stop margin falling from 15.4% in Q2 FY24 to 7.7% in Q3 FY26. The cyclicality check and the cyclicality risk are literally the same fact read two ways: being visibly at a cycle bottom with an identifiable upturn scores +2 as an opportunity — FY26 revenue down 11.5% on a 9.1% margin, then Q4 revenue up 7.7% quarter-on-quarter with margin up 313bps — while the same violent swings, from 6.3% to 15.4% to 7.7% to 10.8% EBITDA margin across eleven calls, make cyclicality a HIGH risk. Government dependency works the same way. The Ohio and Nevada plants and the push to 41% non-US revenue are credited as geographic expansion strengths, but they exist because US reciprocal tariffs moved from 10% to 25% to 50% and back toward 18%, and because FY26's revenue fall and margin halving are attributed to those tariffs. Meanwhile the customer base itself is a WATCH item: a handful of large US retailers set the volumes, and around 12% of the business sits in licensed brands — Martha Stewart, Disney, COCOCOZY — that carry renewal exposure.

Put together, the picture is a competent operator in a structurally hard place, and the scoring says so plainly: business quality 57, multibagger fit 10, risk safety 18, giving 33 out of 100 and a Weak, one-star grade with resilience marked STRAINED. Within the quality areas, customer benefits (0.75) and growth (0.67) are the bright spots, capital allocation is decent at 0.6, but competitive advantage and management both land at zero and industry structure is negative at -0.33 — the framework is telling you the company does good work in an industry that does not let good work compound. The defences being built are real and are aimed at exactly the right weaknesses: patented ingredient platforms at 22% of revenue and the sustainability ranking push back on Good Enough Goods; own brands at 19.3% of FY26 revenue, a 15,511-outlet Indian network, 125 distributors, quick commerce up 8x and Christy's e-commerce growth push back on retailer concentration and on the forward-integration gap; geographic spread pushes back on tariff policy. Management behaviour supports this reading — Dipali Goenka has led across all eleven calls, guidance cuts were made openly (FY25 EBITDA 15% to 14%), FY26 declines were owned as external shock absorption, the deleveraging promise was delivered, and the first real shareholder distribution — a ₹252 crore buyback at ₹175 a share plus a 10% dividend — was timed at the earnings trough, which is disciplined even if there is no long-run payout record behind it. What is missing is proof that any of it converts into durable pricing or returns. Growth capex remains unproven with FY26 revenue down 11.5% and FY27 capex shifting; about 108 days of cash stays locked between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, scored -1; and the share gains that ran through FY24–FY25 reversed enough in FY26, with global flooring down 25% and advanced textiles down 20%, that persistent compounding cannot be claimed.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Government Dependency, Good Enough Goods, New Entrants. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.5× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 69% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ⚠️ Returns on capital slid from about 24% to 12% — the business is earning less and less on the money invested in it.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across the 11 calls results swing violently with input commodities and retailer restocking cycles rather than with any stable flow demand: EBITDA margin ran 6.3% (Q2FY23) to 15.4% (Q2FY24), down to 7.7% (Q3FY26), back to 10.8% (Q4FY26), and FY26 revenue fell 11.5% after FY24's 19% growth quarters. Management frames the business around cotton, coal, container, freight and crude costs, and the FY24 peak came with 80-98% capacity utilisation and a debottlenecking/capex push (INR595cr YTD FY25, INR860cr guided) that landed just as demand rolled over.

> "cotton prices have gone up by 10%. The man-made fibers have gone up by more than 20% and the crude by 40%" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Home textiles are replenishment household consumables rather than customer capex equipment, and FY26 showed countercyclical discipline — free cash flow of INR956cr, net debt down 52%, and capex redirected to productivity 'rather than volume-led expansion'.*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 3 of the last 11 years with swings of ±13 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 8% and 27% (±5.9 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 18% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 7% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Corporate fortunes are visibly set by policy: US reciprocal tariffs moved 10% to 25% to 50% and back toward 18%, a Russian-oil-linked levy was imposed and removed, and FY26's 11.5% revenue decline and margin halving are attributed to tariffs; conversely the entire FY27 growth case rests on India-US, India-EU, India-UK, Japan and Australia FTAs plus GST reform. The company also carries government export incentive receivables and depends on OTEXA-tracked trade access.

> "It started from March, 10% and then it moved to 25% and then 28th August onwards, it moved to 50%. And then the next February, it came back to 10%" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate geographic diversification — non-US share expanded to 41% in FY26 — plus onshore US manufacturing (Ohio, Nevada pillow plants) to sit inside the tariff wall.*

### Good Enough Goods — HIGH RISK
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 3 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** The Nobel Biocare mechanism is present: over 80% of business is FOB supply where the retailer owns the shelf and the end consumer has no awareness of Welspun, the product is substitutable cotton textile, and cheaper origins take share the moment relative cost moves. Pricing power is visibly weak — tariff and commodity costs are negotiated as roughly 50/50 sharing with customers rather than passed through — and FY26 EBITDA margin fell to 9.1% from 13.6%.

> "we talked about a 50%, 50% sharing. So here are also conversations that are happening with the customers" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Genuine premium protection is being built — patented ingredient platforms (HygroCotton, GX Pillow, Wel-Trak) at 22% of revenue, a top-ranked sustainability/traceability moat that large retailers underwrite, and a branded portfolio at 19.3% of revenue that carries its own consumer pull.*

- ⚠️ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs fell from about 55% to 49% — pricing is being competed away at the gross level.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has collapsed from a peak of 27% to 8% — the Nobel-Biocare-style fingerprint of a premium being competed away.
- ⚠️ Operating margin drifted down from about 25% to 11% — steady profit erosion, the mark of rising competitive pressure.

## What to watch

- Does the Q4 FY26 turn hold? The Cyclical market growth check (+2) rests on one quarter — revenue +7.7% q-o-q, margin +313bps — while the Cyclicality risk stays HIGH until several quarters confirm it.
- Where do US tariffs settle? The Government Dependency risk (HIGH) turns on rates staying near 18% and on the India-US/EU/UK trade agreements landing; another move back toward 50% would undo the recovery case.
- Do own brands keep outgrowing FOB? Brand Strength and Forward Integrators sit at PARTIAL and the Good Enough Goods risk stays HIGH while over 80% of sales are FOB; own brands moving well past 19.3% of revenue is the signal that changes both.
- Can growth capex earn its keep? The Capex for growth check (+1) is explicitly flagged as unproven — the Ohio and Nevada pillow plants and Anjar expansion need to show incremental returns, not just deferred FY27 spend.
- Does share capture resume outside the US? Market Share Gainers stalled with global flooring -25.1% and advanced textiles -20% in FY26; renewed share gains, plus the 108-day working capital cycle shortening, would lift both the pattern and the Return on Capital area.
