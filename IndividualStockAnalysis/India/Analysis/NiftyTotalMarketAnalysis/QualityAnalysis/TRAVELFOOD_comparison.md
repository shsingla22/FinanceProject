### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Demand is flow-type — travellers eating at airports, not customer capex or a commodity price cycle — but FY26 showed how sharply exogenous travel shocks hit volumes: India-Pakistan conflict, the Air India crash groundings, FDTL crew-rest curtailment at the largest carrier, and the Middle East conflict left network passenger traffic up only 1.2% for the year and down ~3.5% in Q2, with growth carried by new units rather than underlying traffic. (The long view had said: Demand is a flow of passenger footfall rather than customer capex, but FY26 showed how event-sensitive it is: traffic at TFS airports grew just 1.2% for the year (vs 8-9% historically) after the India-Pakistan conflict, the Air India crash groundings, FDTL crew-rest curtailment and the Middle East conflict, with Q2 traffic actually declining ~1%. Sales and PAT still compounded ~25% and ~21% on unit additions and LFL, showing the swing is in the demand driver, not in a commodity price or a customer's investment cycle.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Results depend on a small set of relationships: JVs with 'two of the largest multi-airport operators', heavy weighting to Delhi and Mumbai, licensed brands (Gordon Ramsay, Nando's, Wagamama) that can move to rivals as in the Safilo case, and lounge volumes routed through a handful of banks and card networks; a single airline's FDTL curtailment was enough to moderate a quarter. (The long view had said: Results hinge on a handful of counterparties: master concessions with two large multi-airport operators (GMR JV, Adani-linked airports) and JV structures, with Delhi and Mumbai the dominant hubs; Delhi T3 is on an extension to 30 September and goes to open tender with management unable to say who will win, and analysts pressed on both that renewal and Kolkata potentially moving to Adani. The lounge half of the business (41% of consolidated revenue) additionally depends on banks and card issuers who are unilaterally tightening lounge access.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No volume erosion is attributed to changing habits; the underlying demand base (air passengers) is structurally expanding, and the portfolio is being actively pivoted toward current preferences with regional menus, boba tea, wellness pods and value combos. (The long view had said: The lounge demand base is being reshaped by banks metrically tightening card-funded lounge access — an 18-month running trend on ~41% of revenue — and on the QSR side management is continuously re-cutting the portfolio for changing tastes (regional menus, boba tea, value-led combos, premiumisation). The shift is being managed, not ignored, but the underlying access economics are set by third parties and consumer habit.)

**That worsened:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Airport F&B carries a visible price premium that management is now partly defending with 'value-led combo offers', and on the lounge side the choice is controlled by an intermediary — banks and card networks — that can renegotiate or trim access economics, the classic setup where the intermediary captures the margin. (The long view had said: Airport real estate is a scarce, access-controlled niche where the concession structure — not shelf space — determines who sells, so cheaper unbranded substitutes cannot simply appear next to the outlet; gross margin actually expanded to 84.7% for FY26 while value-led combos were added deliberately, and price increases have been taken broadly in line with 3-5% food inflation rather than defended against discounters.)

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); New Entrants (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
