# Caplin Point Laboratories Ltd. (CAPLIPOINT) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: PHARMA*

Caplin Point Laboratories is an India-based generic drug maker that manufactures affordable medicines — mainly sterile injectables, ophthalmics, tablets/capsules and soft-gelatin capsules — and sells them under its own labels through its own subsidiaries and warehouses in Latin America, and as approved generics in the US and other regulated markets. Its original and still-largest engine is Latin America (Central America since 2002, plus Mexico, Chile, Peru, Ecuador, Colombia, Brazil), where it supplies thousands of small independent pharmacies in the 'private market' and also wins large government institutional tenders; management says it holds about 41% of shelf space in independent Central American pharmacies and keeps roughly six months of stock sitting close to the customer, which is how it earns its cash. The second engine is Caplin Steriles, a US FDA-approved injectables plant that sells in the US both through partners and, since about 2025, through its own front-end label (Caplin Steriles USA), plus contract manufacturing (CMO) for other firms — US-side revenue splits roughly 75% product sales and 25% milestone and profit-share payments. The group has grown revenue from about INR 1,085 crore five years ago to about INR 2,300 crore in FY26, holds INR 2,726 crore of liquid assets and INR 1,471 crore of free cash, and is spending heavily on new factories (17 injectable lines planned over two to three years), its own APIs, and product registrations.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Latin America / LatAm (branded and unbranded generics, private market + tenders)** — Sells finished generic medicines through Caplin's own subsidiaries, warehouses and distribution in Central America (Guatemala, El Salvador, Honduras, Nicaragua, Dominican Republic), Mexico, Chile, Peru, Costa Rica, Ecuador and Colombia, split between everyday private-market sales to independent pharmacies and lumpy government tenders (e.g. USD 6.2m in El Salvador, USD 14.5m in Ecuador); management repeatedly calls this the mainstay that generates the group's cash flow and profits, with Mexico now its biggest LatAm market and Chile the newest (135 registrations, ~USD 10m of supply booked over 18 months).
- **Caplin Steriles Limited (regulated-market injectables and ophthalmics)** — The US FDA-approved sterile plant that develops, files and manufactures injectables, ophthalmics and pre-filled syringes for the US, Canada, Mexico, South Africa, Australia, China and Europe — it went from roughly break-even in FY23 to being the company's main growth story, with about 65 ANDAs approved or near-approval under Caplin's name and consistent high growth expected.
- **Caplin Steriles USA / own US front end** — Caplin's own US sales label, launched around 2025, which sells its approved products directly to US wholesalers rather than only through partners; it reached nearly USD 10 million of revenue since inception and management calls the decision to go direct a good one in hindsight.
- **US partnering, licensing and CMO work** — A 'hybrid' model where Caplin files its own products and licenses them to partners, and also contract-manufactures for other companies — this is what produces the milestone and profit-share income that makes up roughly 25% of US revenue.
- **Acquired ANDAs / source-and-outsource distribution** — Using its cash pile to buy ready-made US product approvals (about 14 ANDAs bought recently, four of them oncology) and to source products from other CMOs in India and China for resale to US wholesalers, so its US label has more to sell than it can make itself.
- **API business (Vizag plant and R&D centres)** — A bought-and-refurbished active-ingredient plant in Vizag plus two API R&D centres, meant mainly for backward integration — making the key raw materials for Caplin's own products and filing its own DMFs; three critical APIs were scaled up by early 2026 with the first DMF filing expected by end of that year.
- **Caplin One Labs (oncology injectables, oral solids, onco API)** — A set of new factories under construction covering oncology injectables, oral solid dosage and oncology APIs; the onco injectable plant began exhibit batches in early 2026 with about 12 organic products in development, and completing these units also brings an ~8% tax benefit.
- **Soft gelatin capsules (Pondicherry)** — An older formulations division whose capacity was doubled from about 2 crore to 4 crore capsules a month in FY23 because of strong demand and market shortage.
- **Amaris Clinical (CRO wing)** — The in-house clinical research arm that runs bio-equivalence and clinical studies for Caplin's filings; it holds a US FDA EIR and an approval from Chile, and supports work such as the 40-plus bio-studies being run for Chilean products.
- **New sterile capabilities under build-out (BFS, PFS/cartridges, ophthalmic ointments, hormones, inhalers)** — Ongoing investment to cover 'pretty much the complete gamut' of sterile forms — a blow-fill-seal line ordered from Rommelag with 14 products in development, a pre-filled-syringe and cartridge line, ophthalmic ointments, plus R&D into hormone injections and inhalers, with first approvals expected FY27-FY28.
- **Digitalisation and Video/Visual SOP** — Not a revenue line but a capability management stresses heavily — patent-filed video-based standard operating procedures, automation and in-house software logbooks/BMR-BPR, used to keep regulatory compliance clean (EU GMP and Saudi FDA audits passed with no critical observations) and pitched as a bargaining chip to win product-sourcing tie-ups with Chinese and Mexican firms.

### The story in depth

Caplin Point comes out of the three reviews as a high-quality, self-funded generics maker that has grown by going where bigger money does not bother to look. The quality framework gives top marks to the unglamorous niches it has chosen — bottom-of-the-pyramid Latin America and small US wholesalers and independent distributors rather than the big three — and to the steady build-out that followed: 135 registrations and a $10m order book in Chile, tender wins and a planned plant in Mexico, owned warehouses in Chile and Guatemala, and a US front end with licences in 45-50 states. Profitability is strong and stable: ₹34 of operating profit on every ₹100 of sales, ₹60 left after direct input costs, gross margins rising from 55.3% to 59.6%. R&D crossed 5% of turnover and ₹100cr for the first time in FY26 and is visibly productive — 60 ANDAs, 10 approvals in four quarters, approvals coming through in 14-15 months against roughly 20 for the industry. The balance sheet is essentially debt-free with ₹1,471cr of cash, every project paid for out of the company's own earnings. Across the eleven-pattern screen, four patterns come out as strong fits — Innovation Dominance, Forward Integrators, Market Share Gainers and Corporate Culture — which is why the multibagger portion of the rating scores full marks, and the combined verdict lands at 84 out of 100, Outstanding, with resilience judged Sound.

The interesting part is how often one trait is doing double duty. Owning its own distribution is the clearest strong-fit pattern (Forward Integrators — a US label that went from 45 state licences in 2024 to 30 products and about $11m of revenue by FY26, with no B2B cannibalisation), and it is also the main answer to the biggest risk flagged, Stakeholder Concentration, which is rated Elevated because a persistent quarter of Caplin Steriles' US revenue depends on a handful of named licensing partners and single tenders visibly move the reported numbers. Tenders themselves cut both ways: eight of eight won in Mexico is part of the Market Share Gainers evidence, while the same tender business — $6.2mn in El Salvador, $14.5mn in Ecuador, an oncology tender in FY26 — is what puts Government Dependency on Watch, with management conceding tender margins are structurally lower and deliberately pushing towards private markets and branded generics. Most striking, the very thing the quality framework scores highest — certainty of supply and compliance, backward integration, EU GMP and Saudi FDA audits with no critical observations, revenue won on shortages rather than price cuts — exists precisely because the product itself carries no pricing power. The screen returns No Fit on both Pricing Power and Toll Roads: no call in the series describes a price increase, margin gains are put down to mix and lower input costs, and the sources are switchable. Management says outright there is no marketing for a generic, only distribution. That is the Good Enough Goods risk in plain form, and reliability is the moat standing in for a brand.

The financial picture has one clear soft spot, and it is the same one from two directions. About 194 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, with receivables running 121-136 days — the one strongly negative check in the framework, and the reason the model shows up as No Fit on Recurring Revenue, since working capital is funded by the company rather than by customers. The ₹1,471cr cash pile and zero debt are what make that long cycle affordable, and management frames the cash as strategic oxygen for counter-cyclical factory building through volatile periods; but of the seven quality areas, Capital Allocation scores lowest by a wide margin and Return on Capital is the second weakest, against much stronger marks for customer benefits and competitive advantage. Two patterns land only partially for related reasons: Low Price Plus, because the affordability story is real but the margins are high rather than thin, and Cost to Replicate, because twenty-plus years on the ground in Latin America is a genuine time barrier but the regulatory side is not. The remaining risks are mild — Cyclicality, Technological Innovation and Fashion Risk all rated Low on the strength of essential, repeat-purchase medicines and a steady cadence of incremental capability additions, with no signal at all on shifting consumer preferences. The one live competitive worry is New Entrants, on Watch because management itself describes 'virtual companies' with no ANDA base sourcing and reselling in volume, and acknowledges the price erosion that resets economics in generic injectables — a format Caplin is partly copying itself through asset-light sourcing from China, Korea and Turkey.
