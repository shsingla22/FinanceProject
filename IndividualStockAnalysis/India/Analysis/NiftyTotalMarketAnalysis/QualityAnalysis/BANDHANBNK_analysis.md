# Bandhan Bank Ltd. (BANDHANBNK) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

Bandhan Bank is an Indian commercial bank, headquartered in Kolkata, that lends money to customers and takes deposits from the public. Its historic core is very small "microcredit" loans made to groups of low-income borrowers and small entrepreneurs — the segment management calls EEB (Emerging Entrepreneurs Business) — collected in person at group meetings by relationship officers, and it has been adding home loans, loans to NBFCs and small businesses, and ordinary retail loans (personal, gold, two-wheeler, auto) to diversify away from that base. It makes money mainly on the gap between the high rates it charges borrowers and what it pays depositors (margins of about 6.2% in Q1 FY27), plus fee income from things like card machines, payment gateways and corporate internet banking. It operates across India through a network of roughly 6,300 banking outlets serving over 3.35 crore customers, concentrated in the east and in rural/underserved areas — West Bengal alone was about 24% of loans, and the top five states (West Bengal, Maharashtra, Bihar, Gujarat, MP) about 60%.

**The parts of the business:**

- **EEB (Emerging Entrepreneurs Business) / microcredit** — Small unsecured group loans to low-income entrepreneurs, collected at physical group meetings via tablets, with options like 18- and 36-month tenures and bi-weekly or monthly repayment; it was about 50% of gross advances as of March 2024 and is the bank's oldest and highest-yielding business, though also the one that caused most of its bad-loan problems and is now being deliberately grown cautiously.
- **Housing finance** — Home loans, a secured business the bank has pushed hard as part of diversification — growing 27% year-on-year in the June 2022 quarter and making up about 24% of gross advances by March 2024.
- **Commercial banking** — Lending mainly to NBFCs, small enterprise loans and SME/SMA borrowers; it grew 81% year-on-year in mid-2022 and 34% in FY24, reaching roughly 22% of gross advances by March 2024.
- **Retail assets (other than housing)** — Personal loans, gold loans, two-wheeler and auto loans sold to individual customers, the smallest piece at about 4% of advances in March 2024 but growing fast (roughly 65% year-on-year on an adjusted basis that year) and pushed through branch cross-selling.
- **Liabilities / deposits franchise** — Current and savings accounts (CASA) and term deposits from retail and bulk customers, which fund the lending book — deposits reached about INR 1.65 lakh crore by June 2026, with retail deposits plus CASA at 74% of the total and CASA alone at 29.4%, and management deliberately shrinking costly bulk deposits.
- **Digital, merchant acquiring and payments** — Corporate internet banking, POS terminals that plug into customers' ERP systems, payment-gateway tie-ups (BillDesk, Cashfree, Razorpay) and an in-app mobile collection tool, described as a fee-and-service capability serving corporate, government, utility and education clients rather than a large reported loan segment.
- **Stressed-asset resolution / ARC sales** — Not a product line but a recurring activity management highlights: recovering or selling off bad microfinance loans — for example in the December 2025 quarter it sold INR 3,707 crore of written-off loans at about 9% of value and INR 3,165 crore of NPAs at about 18%, which directly moved reported profits and NPA ratios.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Bandhan Bank scores 46 out of 100 — a Mixed, two-star rating built from 45 for business quality and 48 for risk safety, and the two halves tell the same story from different angles. The strongest marks come from where the bank sits rather than what it owns: Indian retail credit and financial inclusion are still expanding, the bank has brought 3.35 crore customers into the formal system, and it appears to be past the worst of the microfinance credit cycle, with credit cost down to 1.8% from 3.4% in FY24, slippages falling and the shrinking microfinance book beginning to turn. It has also executed a genuine, multi-year move out of its West Bengal home base, spreading deposits and branches nationally. But every scored measure of earning power is negative — Return on Capital sits at the floor of the scale and Industry Structure is close behind — because the bank competes in a fragmented, price-driven market with no evident barrier protecting what it earns.

The single decision that shapes everything is the pivot from high-yield microfinance to secured lending, and it cuts both ways at once. On the risk side it is the main reason the Cyclicality and Stakeholder Concentration channels have a credible improvement story: the book is now 57% secured, non-microfinance lending has grown 27% to two-thirds of advances, and bulk deposits have been cut from 32% to 26%. On the quality side the same move is what pushes margins down — net interest margin fell from 8% in early FY23 to roughly 6% by FY26-27, and in the latest quarter interest income grew only 6% even though advances grew 16%. That is why the Pricing Power pattern is a clear no-fit and the asset-turn check scores negative: the bank is buying safety with yield it cannot recover elsewhere. The deposit side shows the same weakness in reverse — cutting the savings rate immediately pushed balances out, CASA has slid from 43.2% in mid-2022 to around 27-29%, and the Low Price Plus pattern fails on cheap deposits, operating costs and loan quality all at once.

Of the eleven multibagger patterns, none is a full fit; only Brand Strength and Innovation Dominance rate a partial. Retail term deposits growing 36% and a 74% retail mix show a real franchise, but the 4% drop in current and savings balances right after a rate cut says those deposits follow price, not loyalty — the same evidence behind the Good Enough Goods risk and the negative intangible-benefits check. The technology work is steady and useful (core banking migration, tablet-based collections, payment integrations) but is catch-up rather than advantage, which is also why the technology-disruption risk is the one rated Low. Market Share Gainers fails because the earlier growth was underwritten in a way that produced a Rs 3,852 crore write-off and Rs 6,872 crore of bad-loan sales, and those same swings make Cyclicality a High Risk. Corporate Culture fails on delivery gaps — 2.5% credit-cost guidance against 3.4% actual — and connects to the negative people check, since the whole senior team including the chief executive was hired from outside in 2024. The second High Risk, Government Dependency, is separate and largely outside management's hands: RBI microfinance rules, roughly Rs 650 crore of priority-lending certificate income with Rs 400-500 crore at risk, and a government guarantee scheme covering losses on the micro book. With overall resilience still unknown, this is a recovering, de-risking bank rather than a compounding one.

*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**What the calls show:** Fortunes are repeatedly set by policy: the RBI's April 2022 microfinance rules directly suppressed a quarter's disbursement; PSLC income of ~Rs 650 crores hinged on an RBI observation with Rs 400-500 crores at risk plus a forced RIDF deposit; the CGFMU government guarantee scheme drove claims of Rs 1,150-1,200 crores and an audit whose outcome management openly hoped would be positive; and the Nov 2025 labour codes forced a Rs 120 crore gratuity provision, repeated at Rs 61 crores in Q1FY27.
