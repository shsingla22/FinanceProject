# Avanti Feeds (AVANTIFEED) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Avanti Feeds is an Indian company built around shrimp farming, run from Hyderabad with its registered office in Visakhapatnam, Andhra Pradesh. Its main business is manufacturing shrimp feed — made mostly from fish meal, soya bean meal and wheat flour — which it sells to shrimp farmers in India, earning money on the gap between feed selling prices and volatile raw-material costs (feed sales were about 5,62,060 metric tons in FY26). Its second business, run through subsidiary Avanti Frozen Foods, buys and processes shrimp and exports frozen and cooked shrimp products, chiefly to the United States and other overseas markets (about 16,976 MT exported in FY26). A third, newer venture, Avanti Pet Care, is a pet care project whose progress management reports separately on each call. In FY26 the group reported consolidated gross income of about INR 6,279 crores and profit before tax of about INR 882 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Feed Division (Shrimp Feed)** — Makes and sells shrimp feed to Indian aquaculture farmers, and is clearly the core of the company — FY26 gross income of about INR 4,538 crores and PBT of INR 715 crores out of a consolidated INR 6,279 crores and INR 882 crores, with margins driven mainly by fish meal, soya bean meal and wheat flour prices.
- **Shrimp Processing and Export Division (Avanti Frozen Foods Private Limited)** — Processes shrimp and exports frozen and value-added cooked shrimp, mostly to the US, contributing roughly INR 1,100–1,200 crores of gross income a year (INR 1,117 crores in FY24) — the clear second business, and the one hit by the 2021 USFDA salmonella recall for which the company provisioned about INR 33 crores.
- **Pet Care Project (Avanti Pet Care Private Limited)** — A newer venture with its own CFO that appears on calls from around FY26 onward, presented as a project whose 'status' is still being reported rather than as a division with meaningful revenue in the excerpts.

### The story in depth

Avanti Feeds earns its best marks for how efficiently it turns money into sales and how straight management talks. Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.42 of sales, and every ₹100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings generated ₹15 of operating cash this year — a 15% cash return, and the strongest of the seven quality areas (Return on Capital, 0.67). The business gets there on modest plant: ₹6,279 crore of consolidated FY26 revenue off facilities where a 1.75 lakh MT feed plant cost only about ₹125 crore. Management scores next best (0.5), and the one multibagger pattern rated a clear fit is Corporate Culture: the company disclosed the Salmonella recall in painful quarterly detail — provisions, claims settled, uninsured destruction costs — until it closed at NIL in FY24, cut guidance explicitly, warned about margin pressure before it showed up in results, and delivered its promised capex on schedule (the Varadapuram feed plant, new pre-processing and cold storage, and the Krishnapuram processing plant commissioned March 2024, all self-funded). Sales have compounded at roughly 12% a year, and the end market is genuinely growing: Indian shrimp feed consumption rose from 10.5 lakh MT in FY21 to an estimated 11–12 lakh MT, seafood exports hit an all-time high, and PMMSY policy support reinforces the trend.

The catch is that the very traits behind those strengths also create the weaknesses. The asset-light economics that produce the high return on capital mean a rival can buy its way in for the same small sum — a competitor can add 1,75,000 MT of capacity for about ₹125 crore — which is exactly why Cost to Replicate rates only a partial fit and New Entrants sits on the watch list, with imported feed undercutting domestic product until the 15% customs duty was restored. Likewise, the dominant position that looks like an advantage is also the reason growth has stalled: at roughly 45–50% of the Indian shrimp feed market there is little runway left, and feed volumes went 5.41 lakh MT in FY22 to 5.13 lakh MT in CY23 to 5.62 lakh MT in FY26 while the industry pool grew to 11–12 lakh MT — so the Market Share Gainers pattern is holding, not gaining, and the framework marks share erosion outright. Forward integration cuts both ways too: moving from feed into own processing and export lifted shipments from 12,497 MT in FY23 to 16,976 MT in FY26, but it anchored the division on the United States, where volumes fell 17.9% in FY26 under countervailing duties, an import alert and tariffs — hence Stakeholder Concentration on watch, partly offset by the same push into the EU (+36%), China (+24%), Japan and Korea that the framework credits as credible geographic diversification.

What the company cannot do is set its own price, and this is where the risk review and the pattern screen say the same thing in different words. Feed selling prices need Andhra Pradesh government permission — a ₹2.56/kg increase was rolled back in August 2022 — while wheat and soya availability turns on export bans and MSP, and the import duty on feed is a policy lever too; that is why Government Dependency is the single high risk. It is also why four patterns fail outright: Pricing Power (FY22 hikes left ₹1.63/kg of raw-material inflation unabsorbed, and FY26 gross income fell on a lower sale price), Toll Roads (feed is the farmer's largest input, not a rounding error he pays without arguing), Low Price Plus (no itemised cost culture; margins move entirely with fish meal, soya and wheat), and Friendly Middleman (the calls never describe channel investment). Competitive Advantage is accordingly the weakest area at −0.33, and Good Enough Goods rates elevated. On top of that sits the cycle: fish meal doubled from ₹100 to ₹240 per kg in two months, and the company enters an explicitly challenging FY27 straight off a peak year (FY26 PBT ₹882 crore, up 20%) — nearer a top than a bottom. The newer answers to all this are real but small: value-added shrimp exports rose 142% in FY24, and the Avant Furst pet-food venture sells direct on Amazon and Supertails, but at ₹1.36–1.51 crore of quarterly sales in FY26 it is immaterial, and no R&D spend is ever disclosed, so Brand Strength and Innovation Dominance both come out partial. Netted out — 45% weight on business quality at 55, 30% on multibagger fit at 40, 25% on risk safety at 48 — the score is 49 out of 100: Mixed, two stars, with resilience judged sound. A well-run, cash-generative, honestly managed leader in a market where the leader does not get to set the terms.
