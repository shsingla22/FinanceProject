# Kaynes Tech (KAYNES) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Kaynes Technology is an Indian electronics contract manufacturer — what the industry calls an ESDM/EMS player — that builds electronic assemblies for other companies' products rather than selling anything under its own brand. Its core work is designing and assembling printed circuit boards (PCBAs) and complete electronic boxes for customers in automotive and EV, railways, industrial, aerospace, defence and medical-type end markets, described by management as 'high mix, high value' work with variable volumes, plus support across the whole life of a product from concept through post-manufacturing and end-of-life. It earns money by winning firm, non-cancellable customer orders (order book about INR 90,000 million as of Q3 FY26) and recognising revenue as it builds and ships against them; FY26 consolidated revenue was INR 3,626.4 crore, up 33% year-on-year, at a 15.8% EBITDA margin and 10% PAT margin. It is headquartered in Mysore with plants across India (Mysore, Manesar, Chamarajanagar, Hyderabad, Sanand, a new PCB plant at Chennai), sells to 300-plus customers in 24 countries, and has bought its way into North America via August Electronics.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Core EMS / PCBA business** — The original and still-dominant business: assembling printed circuit boards and electronic sub-systems to customer designs across automotive, EV, industrial, railway and aerospace verticals — management says nearly all current order-book additions are EMS orders, and the flagship Mysore plant alone crossed INR 10,000 million of revenue in FY26.
- **Smart meters / power distribution products** — Makes electricity smart meters (and is targeting gas and water meters) for AMISPs — the companies that win state utility metering contracts — built up via the October 2024 acquisition of Iskraemeco India and a new Hyderabad factory; management said it was roughly 10% of sales at the time, possibly 12–15% at peak execution, and it carries a heavier working-capital cycle than core EMS.
- **OSAT (semiconductor assembly and test)** — A new chip packaging and testing plant at Sanand, Gujarat, backed by government incentives under the India Semiconductor Mission (FSA approval received) — Unit 1 is operational and ramping, Unit 2 commercialises in Q2 FY27, with management citing revenue visibility of over INR 25,000 million across the next five years.
- **HDI PCB manufacturing** — A roughly INR 1,500 crore plant coming up at Chennai to make the bare high-density, multi-layer circuit boards themselves — a backward-integration move aimed at defence, aerospace and complex industrial customers, which management argues can pull through about INR 13,500 crore of follow-on EMS work from the same clients.
- **August Electronics (North America)** — An acquired US operation being integrated to give Kaynes a North American customer base and higher-margin specialist capability such as RF and microwave assemblies.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, Cyclicality, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.2× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ⚠️ Only about -48% of recent operating profit became cash (was 54%) — profits are getting stuck in paper, an early stress signal.
- ✅ Returns on capital moderated from about 17% to 14% — lower, but still well clear of the deep slide this check looks for.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Large parts of the business are policy-anchored: the Iskraemeco acquisition (Oct 2024) was explicitly to chase the government's 110mn+ awarded and 110mn unawarded smart meters via PGCIL/West Bengal AMISPs, railway Kavach is a mandated programme whose INR3bn order deferral forced a guidance cut in Feb 2026, and the Sanand OSAT economics hinge on ISM-framework FSA approval and central/state capital subsidy. FY26 revenue missed INR4,500cr guidance, ending at INR3,626cr, with management blaming delayed government orders and approval cycles.

> "we were quite confident in the last quarter of getting 2 government orders where the products have been done and the product testing have been approved... it is a delayed project" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Metering is being capped at ~INR300cr of FY26 revenue and converted to a device/ODM model, with ~40% ex-metering growth showing the diversified EMS base still carries the company*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is tied to customer project readiness rather than customer operations — management repeatedly explains shortfalls as 'customers don't take our products unless their entire project is aligned' (Feb 2026, ~20% shortfall to plan) and West Asia/Russia-Ukraine driven deferments (May 2026) — while simultaneously committing heavy countercyclical capex (INR1,500cr HDI PCB, two OSAT units), so the capital is going out while the top line slips. Margins have nonetheless been stable at ~14-16% EBITDA across all 17 calls, so this is not a commodity cycle.

> "Since our revenues are closely linked to customer project readiness and approval cycles, some revenue recognition has shifted despite the orders remaining valid" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Non-cancellable ~1.5-year rolling order book (INR9,000cr), 300+ customers across automotive, industrial, railway, aerospace, and continued investment through the soft patch*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 0 of the last 7 years with swings of ±26 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 10% and 16% (±2.3 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 35% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 8% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.
