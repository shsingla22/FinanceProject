**Checks that improved:**

- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: The company just cycled through the weakest summer in its 27-year history and enters FY27 with signals of a strong summer and pre-built cooling inventory — a textbook position near a cycle bottom with an identifiable upturn. | The long view had said: FY26 saw the weakest summer since inception depressing the high-margin cooling category, and management has stocked aggressively against clear signals of a normal-to-strong summer ahead — a plausible cyclical trough with an identifiable upturn.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Good. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹9 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹9 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±3.8 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Expansion is credible and calibrated — Chhattisgarh entered ahead of plan, MP targeted, UP scaled to 53 stores — but Bihar still contributes 75% of revenue and the new-geography stores are visibly diluting margins while they mature. | The long view had said: A methodically sequenced contiguous-state expansion (Bihar 1999-2021, Jharkhand FY22, Eastern then Central UP, Chhattisgarh ahead of plan, MP next) executed with zero store closures since inception.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: The company holds a first-recall position in its core geography, present in all 38 Bihar districts and 22 of 24 Jharkhand districts, which functions as a mind-share monopoly locally though not a structural one. | The long view had said: In Bihar the company holds ~60% share with an entrenched position in the customer's mind ('Sambandh Bharose Ka') plus a captive after-sales service arm, in a market with no organized competition.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: The customer base is purely retail, but the benefits delivered are convenience and affordability rather than the intangible/brand pull the framework wants for retail customers, so the match is only partial. | The long view had said: The customer base is purely retail and the benefit set is correctly matched — trust, hospitality, service and physical accessibility — rather than corporate-style TCO selling, though the retail intangible is service-based rather than aspirational.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: The target markets are described as underserved, implying weak unorganised incumbents, and AVL's ability to pre-stock through BEE-norm and supply disruptions is a capability smaller dealers cannot match. | The long view had said: The competitive field is small dealers who cannot match AVL's inventory buying or price positioning, and organized peers are absent from these states, leaving abundant share to take.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: Consumer-durables retail here is fragmented rather than oligopolistic, and the discussion of price hikes flowing through to end consumers across all retailers indicates no collective pricing power over the customer. | The long view had said: The structure is not an oligopoly but one organized player against a long tail of small unorganized dealers, and management notes rising OEM competition rather than a stable few-player market.
- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Weak. Now: After direct input costs, the company keeps ₹16 of every ₹100 of sales — thin pricing power; customers likely buy on price. And from the calls: Gross margin of 15-16% and full pass-through of GST cuts and OEM price hikes show no image- or taste-driven pricing power; the offer is affordability, not aspiration. | The long view had said: After direct input costs, the company keeps ₹16 of every ₹100 of sales — thin pricing power; customers likely buy on price. And from the calls: Gross margin is a thin ~15-16% and entirely mix-dependent — it fell when mobiles and laptops grew — indicating the retailer has little independent pricing power from image or brand.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: The proposition is explicitly trust and reliable availability built over 27 years, with stocking strategy aimed at never being out of product during peak demand. | The long view had said: The entire proposition is trust and reliability — the tagline 'Sambandh Bharose Ka', a dedicated grievance call centre, and zero store closures in 27 years including COVID — with customers buying certainty of service rather than lowest price.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. Now: Capex is overwhelmingly growth-oriented — 102 stores added in three years versus 105 in the prior 23-24 years, with deliberately larger, costlier UP formats — but management flags margin drag from immature stores and never quantifies incremental store ROIC. | The long view had said: Capex is overwhelmingly growth-oriented — 102 new stores added in three years versus 105 built in the prior 23 years — with store-level economics (6-month breakeven, 3-year payback) and ROCE/ROE of 28%/60% indicating high incremental returns, though a portion goes to renovation of older stores.
- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Excellent → Good. Now: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.82 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: The model is lease-based and store-light, but working capital is heavy and deliberately rising — INR840 crores of inventory against INR2,672 crores of FY26 revenue — which structurally caps asset turn even as the cluster density provides some protection. | The long view had said: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.82 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: Returns are high (ROCE 28%, ROE 60%) on a leased-store model, but the balance sheet is inventory-heavy by design (INR840 crores inventory on INR2,672 crores revenue, ~80 days) and debt-funded, so it is high-return rather than genuinely asset-light.
- **Advertisement and promotion** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: A&P is treated as a flexible cost lever — cut in Q1 FY26 to defend margins, then stepped up in UP in Q3 and topped with a celebrity brand ambassador — so it reads as tactical support rather than a consistently compounding brand-equity investment. | The long view had said: A&P is rising deliberately to seed new geographies (UP/Lucknow marketing, loyalty program 'Buy and Win', onboarding Pankaj Tripathi as brand ambassador) and is described as investment for market depth, but it has visibly compressed margins with returns still unproven.

*Unchanged: Convenience benefits; Out of limelight; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Barriers to entry and rationality; Structural end-market growth; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Distribution advantage; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Security by obscurity; Gain in market share; Halo effect (caution); Disciplined stewards; Return on capital (headline); Candor; Cost of working capital.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 0 weakened, 9 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Promises made in the Aug 2025 call were delivered by May 2026 (25-30 stores and '200-plus by year-end' → 207 stores), management explicitly owns bad news rather than burying it (SSSG of minus 4%, margin moderation of 42/33 bps quantified, an INR1.5 crore labour-code exceptional provision flagged), and the founder-family leadership (Yashovardhan Sinha as CMD with Yosham Vardhan as Whole-Time Director throughout FY2022-FY2026) de-levered quickly when Q1 went against them. (The long view had said: Founder-led since 1999 with stable leadership (Yashovardhan Sinha CMD throughout FY22-FY26), ESOPs granted to 300+ employees down to cashiers and store managers, store-economics discipline (breakeven in 6 months, 3-year payback), and store-count promises delivered — 150 by FY25 and 200+ by FY26 both met (207 at March 2026). Against that, the repeated 30-35% growth aspiration was not delivered (FY26: 18% revenue, 11% PAT), leverage has been carried throughout, and management declines several legitimate disclosure requests (Q4 SSSG, state-wise margins, capex per store).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Low Price Plus (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Innovation Dominance (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: As a multi-brand retailer, product-level technology change passes through from OEMs rather than stranding AVL's own assets — the January 2026 BEE energy-norm transition, raised as a risk by analysts, was absorbed without write-downs and was in fact turned into an opportunistic buying window at OEM discounts. No call in the window discusses AVL's format or systems becoming legacy. (The long view had said: The business is a bricks-and-mortar format whose infrastructure could become legacy if the channel shifts; management has repeatedly declined to build an online capability ('Not at the moment') and answered the AI question with a wait-and-watch stance. Product-level tech change (BEE norms, memory-chip pricing) passes through to prices and inventory but is small-scale, not disruptive to the retailer.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Across the four calls the demand base is expanding rather than eroding, and it is moving toward AVL's higher-value categories: post-GST premiumisation, washing machines and panel TVs each growing ~30% in Q3 FY26, mobiles +20%, and management calling out commercial refrigeration as a new opportunity. No volume decline in the window is attributed to changing habits, and no channel migration away from physical durables retail is discussed. (The long view had said: The structural shift toward online shopping is the live question in these calls, and management's defence is a claim about regional habit ('people prefer seeing goods and feeling it... a family affair') rather than evidence of a portfolio or channel pivot. Offsetting this, the underlying demand base is expanding — rising rural incomes on good monsoons and premiumisation — rather than eroding.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
