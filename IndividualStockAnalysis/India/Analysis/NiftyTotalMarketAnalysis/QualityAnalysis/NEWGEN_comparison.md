### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 5. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 3 weakened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Third-party validation is repeatedly cited — Forrester Wave strong performer, Gartner Magic Quadrant (as a niche player), Forbes Asia Best Under A Billion twice, Great Place to Work twice — which builds enterprise credibility, and wallet share deepened (customers billing over INR5cr rose 87 to 101). But no heritage narrative, premium pricing, or brand-led share capture is evidenced in these calls. (The long view had said: Reputation assets are cited consistently — Gartner Market Guide inclusion, a commissioned Forrester TEI/ROI study, CRISIL A1, two consecutive Great Place to Work certifications — and the NewgenONE name is stretched across verticals, but there is no evidence of premium pricing or A&P-driven brand pull.)
- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Leadership is long-tenured and succession was internal (Tarun Nandwani, 33 years at Newgen, promoted to CEO), the balance sheet stays net-cash with dividends and INR302cr operating cash flow, and management discloses bad news plainly — weak India/EMEA, elongated deal cycles, the INR35cr labour-code hit and INR42cr exceptional items separated out rather than buried, and an explicit admission that the prior quarter's problem was not fixed. (The long view had said: Founder-led with exceptional tenure (Nigam, Varadarajan, Jeet through FY22-26; incoming CEO Tarun Nandwani with 33 years) and candid disclosure — US weakness, DSO deterioration, INR42 crore exceptional items, and refusal to guide in FY27 — but the repeated 20% growth and US/GSI acceleration promises made from 2022 onwards were not delivered, with FY26 at 6%.)
- **Toll Roads**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The platform is mission-critical in regulated, process-intensive sectors and management describes the base as extremely sticky, but these are large, negotiated, board-level purchases — not a sub-1% line item bought without haggling — as shown by repeatedly elongated large-deal cycles in India and EMEA. No certification or standards anchoring is claimed. (The long view had said: the calls are silent and no computable check supports the pattern.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Gains are visible in new geographies — 47 new logos in FY26, US +17% FY26 and +27% in Q1 FY27, APAC +14% — and wallet share within accounts is expanding, but management gives no market-share numbers or named-competitor comparisons, and its two largest regions (India and EMEA) were flat-to-weak all year, so overall growth stayed at 6-7%. (The long view had said: Deepening penetration is evidenced (47 new logos in FY26, customers billing over INR5 crores rising 87→101, US growing 17-27% off a small base), but management gives no market-share numbers versus named competitors, and the four-year record shows 24-30% growth decaying to 6% in FY26 as large deals stalled.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Deals are won directly from end customers — banks, insurers, governments — and Newgen funds its own front-end, spending 21-26% of revenue on sales and marketing; there is no prescriber/installer intermediary choosing the product. The only channel-like items are a developer community and a new ecosystems remit, not a trade that specifies on the buyer's behalf. (The long view had said: System integrators/GSIs and technology partners (Coforge, Anabatic, Finastra) recommend and implement Newgen into enterprise clients and contribute a steady ~20% of revenue, but management admits it has little control over these deals and describes no trade-training or professional-certification lock-in.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: the calls are silent and no computable check supports the pattern. (The long view had said: Management repeatedly points to an India-centred cost base letting it spend ~20-26% on S&M versus 40-45% for US peers and stay profitable at ~$100m scale, and in FY26/FY27 shows itemised cost discipline (7% headcount optimisation, AI-led engineering) — but it does not describe passing savings into lower price as a strategy.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (STRONG FIT); Pricing Power (QUANT SIGNAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Large licence deals are tied to customers' discretionary technology-investment decisions, and across all four calls management flags elongated decision cycles, customers 'holding off' on large deals and geopolitical uncertainty in India/EMEA slowing closures and collections; the offset is that annuity revenue is now 62% of the total and growing steadily, which behaves like flow revenue rather than a capex cycle. (The long view had said: Revenue is tied to banks'/insurers' discretionary technology programmes and lumpy upfront licences, which behaves like customer capex: licences compounded 30-40% for two years and then large deals stalled in FY26, cutting growth from 25% (Q1 FY25) to 6% for FY26 and forcing cost optimisation. The growing annuity/subscription base (62% of revenue, SaaS +36-44%) is an operations-linked flow component that damps the swing.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Across the window management never defends pricing against cheaper substitutes and no discounting or margin investment is framed as a competitive response; adjusted net margins held at ~21% for FY26 while the product is positioned in complex, regulated, audit-heavy workflows where a cheap substitute is hard to swap in, and the buyer is the enterprise itself rather than an intermediary who could pocket the spread. (The long view had said: The core low-code/ECM layer is a category where cheaper platform and now AI-native alternatives can be 'good enough', and management acknowledges customers are pausing to re-evaluate whether the value justifies the spend; the FY26 licence shortfall came with no pricing power evident. Protection comes from verticalised, regulated-industry depth — policy administration, trade finance, auditability and governance frameworks — which is a genuine niche rather than a commodity screw.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Newgen sells ECM/BPM/low-code platforms into an industry being reset by generative and agentic AI: AI dominates every call in the window, management is re-architecting the platform around agents and governance, analysts question directly how AI agents will be monetised, and management concedes that traditional people-based engagements are getting harder to scale — the classic incumbent-infrastructure problem. (The long view had said: Enterprise content/process automation is in the middle of the AI platform shift: management concedes customers are deferring large deals because of 'uncertainty around the AI' and whether the purchase is still the right value, and analysts repeatedly probe how AI agents are monetised. The company is re-architecting its installed low-code/ECM base around agentic AI, which is exactly the incumbent's forced-migration problem, though its cadence of incremental releases (LumYn, vertical AI modules, 67 patent filings) and AI-driven delivery efficiency show it is riding rather than ignoring the wave.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (LOW); New Entrants (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
