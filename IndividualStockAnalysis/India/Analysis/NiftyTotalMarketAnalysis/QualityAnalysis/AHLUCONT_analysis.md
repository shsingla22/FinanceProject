# Ahluwalia Contracts (AHLUCONT) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Ahluwalia Contracts (India) Limited is an Indian construction contractor — management describes it as being "only in buildings and factories," not roads or other civil infrastructure. It is hired by clients to build structures: hospitals, railway station redevelopments (CSMT Mumbai), government complexes (Central Vista), commercial and institutional buildings, industrial parks and residential towers for private developers such as DLF, Signature Global, SmartWorld and Emaar. It makes money by winning contracts through competitive bidding (it talks about being "L1," i.e. lowest bidder), building up an order book, and then billing clients as work is executed — FY26 turnover was INR4,565 crores on a closing order book of INR21,096 crores, with EBITDA margins running around 9-10% and PAT margin near 5-6%. Work is spread across India (in FY23 split roughly East 38%, North 34%, West 18%, South 5%, plus about 6% in Nepal), with NCR/Delhi alone around 44% of the order book by FY26, which is why Delhi pollution shutdowns, monsoons, elections and labour shortages repeatedly hit quarterly execution.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Government / public-sector projects** — Building work for government and public bodies — hospitals, railway station redevelopment, airports, Central Vista — which was about 81.5% of the order book in FY23; management says competition here is intense and rates are worse than private work.
- **Private-sector projects** — Building work for private clients and developers, about 18.5% of the order book in FY23, but the stated focus area from FY25 onward because there are few capable contractors, competition is lower and the rates are "far better than the government projects."
- **Hospitals / healthcare buildings** — The single largest end-use category in the FY23 order-book split at about 32%, including projects like Tata Memorial Hospital.
- **Residential buildings** — Apartment and housing construction for developers (DLF, Signature Global, SmartWorld, Emaar), about 13% of the FY23 order book; repeatedly described as "not a focus area," and by FY26 management said it had deliberately slowed down bidding for further residential work, especially in NCR.
- **Commercial buildings** — Offices and similar commercial structures, roughly 8% of the FY23 order book, and one of the categories management says it is now steering toward.
- **Institutional buildings** — Education and other institutional structures, plus airports and hotels — not given a separate percentage, but named from FY23 onward as a growth area on the private side and, by FY26, the stated focus alongside commercial work.
- **Large marquee / design-build projects** — A handful of very big, named jobs that drive revenue in any given year — CSMT (targeted at INR300-350 crores in FY26 and ~INR700 crores the next year), Central Vista (INR2,600 crores, awarded January 2026), and the Gem & Jewellery Park / IJPM (starting Q1 FY27 at an expected 20-25% of project value in that year).
- **Mechanization / equipment capex** — Not a revenue line but a stated strategic shift: because individual orders are bigger, buildings more complex, timelines squeezed and labour chronically short, the company has been investing heavily in machinery — which has raised depreciation and held back return ratios, with management arguing the margin payoff comes over the next couple of years.
- **Real estate inventory** — A small legacy holding of INR59.73 crores mentioned once in the FY23 balance-sheet data points; never discussed as an operating business.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 1 check supporting).

**What the calls show:** Some genuine cost traits — geographic clustering of sites to spread overheads (Aug-2024 call), consolidation into fewer larger tickets, and a deliberate multi-year shift to mechanisation to offset labour cost — but the model fails the test on returns: asset turns are falling (capex INR274cr FY26, rising depreciation, order book doubled while revenue grew ~9-11% CAGR) and management explicitly refuses to compete on aggressive pricing.

> "within a radius of about 15 to 20 kilometers, we would be doing nearly 20 million square feet. So, obviously the overheads can be spread, the cost can be reduced." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹52 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 1.1× a year — not the high-turnover, low-margin machine this pattern needs.

### Brand Strength — PARTIAL
*"The name itself sells"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (1 of 2 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** A real reputation asset in building EPC — repeat marquee clients (DLF, Adani, Google, Tata Memorial, Central Vista, CSMT) and prequalification credibility that management says limits competition in complex private work — but there is no premium pricing (margins compressed), no A&P, and no consumer/heritage brand economics.

> "There is greater demand for timely delivery , quality delivery. There are only a handful of contractors in the private sector side. The competition is less." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹52 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ❌ Operating margin of 10% — too thin for a protected franchise.

### Market Share Gainers — PARTIAL
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 1 check supporting).

**What the calls show:** Order book nearly tripled from INR7,588cr (Nov-2022) to INR21,096cr (Mar-2026) with FY26 inflow of INR10,257cr, and average ticket size rose deliberately (Central Vista ~INR2,600-3,000cr) — but revenue grew only ~11% in FY26 and margins fell over the period, so backlog share gain has not converted into profitable execution share.

> "our order book has doubled over the past 2 years, but our revenues have grown at less than 9% CAGR over the past 2 years" — *management, on an earnings call*
