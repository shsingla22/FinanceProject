### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 8 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: The entire model is an annuity on an installed base: fees are charged as basis points on AUM (58bps equity, 28bps debt, 13bps liquid, restated identically across Q2FY26 and Q1FY27), and the SIP/STP book is an auto-renewing, pre-authorised recurring stream that grew to INR48.1 billion a month across a 17.1 million investor base with negligible incremental capital. (The long view had said: AMC fees are annuity-like on an installed base — 17.1 million unique investors, 30 million accounts, and a systematic (SIP/STP) book that grew from INR12.8bn/month in Jun-22 to INR48.1bn/month in Jun-26, which management calls sticky habit formation. But there is no upfront product sale, no attach/renewal-rate or deferred-revenue/customer-advance discussion, and revenue is mark-to-market dependent rather than contractually locked.)

**That weakened:**

- **Friendly Middleman**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Distribution runs substantially through MFDs, banks and national distributors who recommend schemes to investors, and commissions are a managed lever — but the channel is weakening as an exclusive route, with direct plans at ~31% of equity AUM and 97% of transactions digital, so end-customer self-selection is now material. (The long view had said: Across all 17 calls the flow engine is the intermediary — MFDs, national distributors, banks (notably HDFC Bank), fintechs — who recommend while the investor pays via TER; management repeatedly describes commission structures, dedicated channel teams, branch-to-branch mapping with HDFC Bank and service/training investment in the trade rather than end-customer advertising.)
- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The company has moved toward the end investor with 280 owned branches (50 opened in two years, many in B30 towns), a self-described 'phygital' model, and direct plans now ~31% of equity AUM with 97% digital transactions — real disintermediation of the distributor, though no franchising or royalty economics. (The long view had said: Real movement toward the end customer — direct plans now ~31% of equity AUM, 97% of transactions digital (up from 69% six years back), 24 new mostly B-30 branches opened with quick-breakeven unit economics, and a stated 'phygital' strategy — but the majority of assets still come through third-party distributors, so control of the customer relationship is only partial.)

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
