# Aurionpro Solutions (AURIONPRO) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Aurionpro Solutions is an India-headquartered technology company that builds and sells its own software products and platforms — plus, in some cases, the hardware and systems around them — mainly to banks and financial institutions, and to transit authorities, governments and data-centre operators. It makes money by winning project-based contracts (licences, implementation and build-out work) that it books into an order book, followed by ongoing maintenance and support contracts that are not counted in that order book; it spends about 9-10% of revenue on R&D to keep the products proprietary. Revenue is reported in two segments, Banking & Fintech and the Technology Innovation Group (TIG), which were roughly the same size in the first nine months of FY26 (TIG Rs.471 crore out of Rs.1,066 crore total, both growing 26%). It sells in India and internationally — the US, Central and Latin America, Africa, Australia, the UK, Singapore and the Middle East — with FY26 revenue of Rs.1,411 crore (up 20.2%), a 20% EBITDA margin, a 15% PAT margin and an order book above Rs.1,800 crore entering FY27.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Banking and Fintech** — Sells software platforms that banks run their core operations on — lending, transaction banking, cash management, trade finance — and was roughly Rs.595 crore of the Rs.1,066 crore nine-month FY26 revenue, growing 26%, with the Middle East described as an increasingly important growth engine (and the source of the FY26 shortfall when war there delayed deals).
- **Lending (Integro)** — Loan origination, collateral management and limits management software — the calls note Aurionpro was placed in the leaders quadrant of Chartis's RiskTech rankings in all three categories, and won a large lending-modernisation mandate from a Singapore-headquartered bank across its international subsidiaries.
- **Transaction banking / cash management (iCash pro)** — The platform banks use to run corporate cash-management and transaction-banking services; management says a marquee Indian public sector bank picking it has made it close to the market standard for new deals, with CSB Bank also signing on.
- **Enterprise AI (Arya.AI, Lexsi Labs, AurionAI)** — An acquired-and-built AI arm making AI products for banks and insurers — including a domain-specific enterprise AI platform, an AI-native agentic trade finance application and in-house foundation models — described as the focus of a heavy, concentrated investment cycle rather than a large revenue line today.
- **Technology Innovation Group (TIG)** — The non-banking half of the company, made up of three slices — smart mobility/transit payments, data centres, and smart cities — at Rs.471 crore in the first nine months of FY26, up 26%, and historically the larger share of the order book (about 65% versus 35% for banking in 2024).
- **Smart Mobility / transit payments** — Supplies the fare-collection and related systems that metros and transit agencies use — automated fare collection for Delhi Metro's Bhopal and Indore projects, platform screen doors via Titagarh for Mumbai Metro Line 5, plus wins in the US (including a State of California tender), Latin America, Africa, Australia and the UK, and partnerships with Mastercard and Vix; management says it competes by owning the full stack of software, systems and hardware.
- **Data centres** — Designs and builds data centres as a single-window provider — including complex jobs like high-performance computing for IIT Guwahati and IIT Bombay, a tier-four design for the RBI, and a project for IDBI Bank — a business that grew over 50% a year for three years and in FY26 landed one of India's largest AI-focused data centre mandates from a hyperscaler, which requires heavy upfront investment and working capital.
- **Smart cities** — The original third leg of TIG, serving government institutions, but deliberately slowed down by management as a planned de-emphasis rather than a growth driver.
- **Payments (AuroPay)** — An RBI-authorised online payment aggregator used to bundle payments with Aurionpro's own software — transit fares, lending, SME SaaS — rather than compete as a plain commodity consumer gateway; a newer business that has absorbed meaningful capitalised investment.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Aurionpro is, on the evidence here, a genuinely differentiated business sitting on a weak financial engine. The quality framework gives it full marks on Competitive Advantage and Customer Benefits: it sells mission-critical certainty to regulated banks, RBI tier-four data centre design and safety-critical platform screen doors, where trust and references decide the sale; it holds very high switching costs in core banking with effectively perpetual maintenance annuities and captive positions in metro fare collection; and it has kept R&D at 9-10% of revenue for years while actually converting that spend into products and wins — Omnifin, Arya.AI, AurionAi, Orion-MSP, an AI-native trade finance platform, and Chartis leaders-quadrant placement as the only Asian vendor in three quadrants. All three of its end markets — enterprise AI for banks, Indian data-centre capacity, and metro/transit fare systems — are structurally expanding. Against that, the single worst score in the whole framework is return on capital: for every ₹100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings in the business, it produced ₹3 of operating cash this year. That gap is why the combined rating lands at 50 out of 100, or 'Decent' — 45% weight on business quality scoring 62, 30% on multibagger fit scoring 47, and 25% on risk safety scoring only 34 — with resilience judged SOUND but nothing better.

The pattern screen tells the same story from a different angle, and its one unambiguous result follows directly from the R&D check. Innovation Dominance is the only STRONG FIT: R&D is disclosed, growing, and explicitly prioritised over margin expansion, with named launches every year. Everything else is a shade weaker. Brand Strength and Forward Integrators are LIKELY rather than confirmed — the reputation is real but works as business-to-business reference capital (marquee metro and public-sector-bank wins, 23 new logos in one quarter) rather than a consumer brand, and the move toward the end customer is narrow, limited to owning the transit fare contract end-to-end and replacing third-party gateways with its own licensed AuroPay. Toll Roads and Recurring Revenue come out only PARTIAL, and the reason is instructive: the lifetime maintenance mechanic is exactly what the switching-costs check rewards — contracts with 15-to-25-year maintenance tails that management deliberately excludes from the order book because they run 'for life' — but the screen wants more than the calls provide. Market Share Gainers is likewise PARTIAL: growth has plainly outrun the industry, with a five-year growth rate of 32-33%, revenue roughly quadrupling and expansion into the US, LatAm, UK, Australia, the Middle East and Southeast Asia, yet the company never quotes actual share numbers. Pricing Power and Friendly Middleman are flagged only by the numbers with no supporting evidence from the calls, which makes them screening hints, not findings. Low Price Plus is a clean NO FIT, and deliberately so — management declines plain-vanilla payment gateway work and stays selective in data centres to protect margins, which is the same instinct that earns the assurance-benefits and technology-advantage marks.

The risk review is where the strengths turn around and bite, because in several places the exact same trait produces the pattern and the risk. The R&D intensity that makes Innovation Dominance a strong fit also drives the ELEVATED technology risk: management calls AI the largest inflection it has seen, is rebuilding every product line as 'Software 2.0' in a 'humongous' multi-quarter effort, and has capitalised roughly ₹200 crore of intangibles — the classic incumbent problem of paying to rebuild while still running the old stack, and a plausible contributor to that 3% cash return on capital. The transit and data-centre engines that supply the structural growth score are also the source of two HIGH risks: revenue is order-book and project-execution driven, tied to customers' capex programmes (MMRDA ₹250cr, Delhi Metro ₹150cr, Titagarh ₹100cr, hyperscaler builds), and a large share of that demand is set by governments and policy. The same hardware and integration work sits in tender-priced, commoditisable territory, which is the Good Enough Goods watch flag — and it is precisely the part of the business the switching-cost moat does not cover. Concentration is a live watch too: the data-centre business is capped by one strategic relationship, the FY26 inflection rests on a single very large hyperscaler mandate, and the Q4 miss traced to one region. New Entrants is on watch because analysts asked directly whether large AI platform companies threaten the banking suite and management's answer was defensive while simultaneously conceding the whole stack must be rebuilt AI-native. The one genuine offset is behavioural: management owns the FY26 miss, calls its own result below par, and had disclosed working-capital and execution problems back in 2022 — the candor check scores full marks even though the broader Management area scores only a third. On balance, the durability is real but concentrated in the software annuities, while the growth and the risk both sit in the lumpier project businesses.
