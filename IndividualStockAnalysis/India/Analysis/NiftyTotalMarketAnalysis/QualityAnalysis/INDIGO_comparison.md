### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

| Return on Capital | Good (+0.67) | Good (+0.67) | +0.00 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Candor** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Management owned the December disruption explicitly and quantified the damage beyond the accounting entry, disclosing an additional 15-16 billion rupees of impact on top of the 5.8 billion exceptional item. | The long view had said: Management explicitly owned the December operational failure and quantifies adverse items (forex MTM, labour code provisions, the 15-16bn hidden disruption cost), though it declines to disclose OEM compensation and gives limited forward guidance.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Q1FY27 growth was almost entirely pricing and mix — yield up 21.3% and unit revenue up 19% on ~1% passenger growth and a 1.3-point lower load factor, with premium Stretch mix expanding to 65 aircraft. | The long view had said: FY27 growth is almost entirely price/mix-led — yields +21.3% with load factors down 1.3pts and passengers up only ~1% — plus premium (business class), Lite Fare and ancillary mix, though this is cost-pass-through pricing rather than brand pricing power.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Organic-only growth with no M&A, deliberate capacity rationalisation in soft quarters and explicit cost discipline (deferred senior increments, grounding older CEO aircraft), offset by a very large committed order book and ~$10bn of dollar liabilities. | The long view had said: Organic focus and no big M&A, with counter-cyclical investment through the downturn and 516bn cash, but leverage is very high (777bn total debt incl. leases) and the growth commitment is enormous and largely irrevocable.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: The market behaves as a concentrated, capacity-disciplined structure where carriers cut supply together in weak quarters and fuel costs were passed through, but it is closer to a duopoly with IndiGo plus the Air India group and end customers can still shop on price. | The long view had said: Indian aviation has consolidated into a rational duopoly-plus structure where discipline is holding — IndiGo pushed yields +21% and guided +25% PRASK while loads barely moved, evidence the end customer lacks negotiating power.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. Now: Heavy, clearly growth-oriented capex — A350 order doubled from 30 to 60, first A321XLR, a new Bengaluru MRO, 13 aircraft inducted in Q1FY27 and a 1,000-engine LEAP MoU — but incremental returns are currently poor with FY26 EBITDAR margin down to 27.3% and a net loss. | The long view had said: Massive, sustained growth capex — a 500-aircraft Airbus order, 30 A350s, ~1,000-aircraft pending orderbook to 2035, plus aircraft ownership via GIFT City, an integrated corporate campus and a 1,000+ LEAP engine MoU with MRO — clearly growth rather than maintenance, though incremental ROIC is cyclical.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Some effective regional monopolies on thin routes (Jalandhar, Purnea, Hindon) and first-mover status at Navi Mumbai, but customer switching costs are minimal and BluChip lock-in is still nascent. | The long view had said: Effective route-level monopolies on thin regional and new-airport routes (Deoghar booked solid at launch, 97 domestic destinations) give local pricing power, though airline customers face essentially no switching costs.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 6. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 1 weakened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Management leans on a 19–20 year consistency promise ('reliability, accessibility, discipline and customer focus'), 850m cumulative customers, sector-leading OTP for nine consecutive months, and stretches the name into premium (Stretch), loyalty and long-haul adjacencies. The December 2025 meltdown — 2,500+ cancellations — is an explicit, self-acknowledged test of that trust rather than a demonstration of it, and no A&P spend or share-premium data is given. (The long view had said: Management leans on a 20-year consistency/reliability promise, 123mn customers and repeated industry awards, and has stretched the name into premium (business product, BluChip, Lite Fare); but the December 2025 operational collapse showed the trust is testable and there is no A&P-led premium-pricing story.)
- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: The December disruption was disclosed promptly and owned without deflection in two consecutive calls ('fell short of the standards we set for ourselves'), with refunds, vouchers beyond regulatory compensation, and a stated process review; promises also convert — the Nov 2025 call promised the A321 XLR and Athens 'in the new year' and the Jan 2026 call delivered both, and long-term capital is committed through cycles (A350 doubled to 60, MRO, 1,000 LEAP engines) even while reporting an FY26 loss. Offsetting flag: CEO Pieter Elbers departed and Willie Walsh was appointed within months of the crisis, against founder-MD Rahul Bhatia's continuity. (The long view had said: Bad news is owned promptly (Bhatia on the December disruption, forex and exceptional items quantified separately, dividend withheld) and the balance sheet is deliberately conservative with ~INR 390bn free cash and aircraft loan prepayments; against that, leadership has churned heavily — Dutta to Elbers to Willie Walsh, plus repeated chairman/CFO changes — so long-tenured stability is not demonstrated.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is a visible cadence of named launches with commercial intent across the four calls — Stretch business cabin, BluChip, India's first A321 XLR with hot meals and seat power, A350 order doubled to 60, Navi Mumbai first-mover, MRO facility — but this is fleet and product rollout, not R&D-funded dominance; no R&D spend, share of industry R&D, or high gross margin funding engine is disclosed (margins in fact fell). (The long view had said: Gross margins are thin and no R&D spend or pipeline is ever disclosed; the 'innovation' cited is network/product/ancillary (Stretch, Lite Fare, XLR routes) and bought-in third-party technology, not proprietary R&D dominance.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: IndiGo is clearly the largest and most connected Indian carrier — 94 destinations, 600+ routes, 123m passengers in FY26 (highest ever), over 10m domestic passengers in May 2026 alone — but no explicit market-share percentage or comparison versus named competitors appears, and FY26 passenger growth was only ~4% against 9.5% ASK growth, so this window shows scale held rather than share visibly gained. (The long view had said: Clear multi-year expansion — 74 domestic destinations in 2022 to 97 domestic plus 45 international by FY26, 107mn to 123mn passengers, weak competitors ceding capacity — but explicit market-share numbers are never given and FY26–FY27 growth stalled (passengers +4% then +1%, capacity guidance cut to single digits).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (LIKELY FIT); Low Price Plus (LIKELY FIT); Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Supplier concentration is structural and repeatedly named: the single-aisle programme is explicitly 'anchored by the Airbus A320 and A321 Family', widebodies are Airbus A350 (order doubled to 60), and July 2026 added an MoU with a single engine maker for over 1,000 LEAP-1A engines — one airframer and one engine family carrying the entire future fleet. It is bounded rather than acute because no supplier relationship was described as at risk in these calls, and the customer side is atomised across 123 million passengers. (The long view had said: Extreme supplier concentration: a single-airframe Airbus fleet with a ~1,000-aircraft orderbook, and Pratt & Whitney engines whose powder-metal defect grounded a peak of mid-70s aircraft, forcing expensive damp leases and lease extensions for multiple years, with compensation negotiated bilaterally and management admitting it has no OEM visibility beyond the near term. The new 1,000-engine CFM MoU concentrates the next decade on another single supplier.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: An airline seat is a price-transparent, largely undifferentiated product bought through intermediaries with limited brand loyalty, and this window showed the limit: a 21% yield increase came with load factors down 1.3 points and still produced a Q1FY27 loss, so cost pass-through is not free. The exposure is bounded because IndiGo is itself the low-cost incumbent — it occupies the good-enough position rather than defending a premium against it. (The long view had said: Air travel is a price-transparent commodity where the intermediary (OTAs, fare aggregators) frames the choice and brand loyalty is thin, so pricing power depends on industry discipline rather than product differentiation — visible when management can only 'keep testing high levels of yields' against cost shocks; the new premium/business push moves IndiGo into exactly the value-added territory a good-enough rival could attack.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
