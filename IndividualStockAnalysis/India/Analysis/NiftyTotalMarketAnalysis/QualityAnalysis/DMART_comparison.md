## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 27 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 5 improved, 3 regressed, 19 unchanged. 3 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them). 1 was newly assessable in the recent view.

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Allocation | Good (+1.00) | Good (+1.33) | +0.33 |
| Return on Capital | Neutral (+0.33) | Neutral (+0.33) | +0.00 |
| Growth | Neutral (+0.50) | Neutral (+0.50) | +0.00 |
| Management | Excellent (+1.67) | Excellent (+1.50) | -0.17 |
| Industry Structure | Neutral (+0.17) | Neutral (+0.00) | -0.17 |
| Customer Benefits | Neutral (-0.50) | Neutral (+0.00) | +0.50 |
| Competitive Advantage | Neutral (+0.00) | Neutral (+0.33) | +0.33 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Weak → Neutral. Now: Customers trust DMART for consistent value and stock availability, but the calls give no evidence that price is secondary to trust — price is the entire proposition. | The long view had said: Price is the proposition rather than secondary to trust; the customer's assurance is that DMart will be cheapest, which is precisely the benefit type the framework treats as weakest.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: The customer base is purely retail and the benefit offered — everyday low price — is correctly matched to that value-seeking shopper, delivering 35.3 crore bill cuts, though it is a price rather than intangible-based match. | The long view had said: Coherently matched to a mass retail customer through everyday-low-price value, but it delivers none of the intangible/brand benefits the framework prefers for retail customers and has no switching-cost lock-in.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: Growth capex is clearly expansionary and accelerating — 40 stores (FY23), 41 (FY24), 50 (FY25) with ~1.8–2.0 mn sq ft added annually and a stated intent to keep opening 10–20% of the base every year, funded entirely from internal cash flow with no debt. | The long view had said: Heavy, self-funded, rising growth capex on owned stores (50 stores and 2mn sq ft in FY25, targeting 10-20% of store base annually) but incremental returns are moderating (ROCE 19.1% to 17.8%, fixed asset turn 3.6 to 3.4).
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Good → Excellent. Now: The cluster-based store and supply-chain density is the core structural advantage — it compounds operating leverage with each added store and is explicitly why capital is preferentially deployed in existing markets. | The long view had said: Cluster-based store density plus owned distribution centres and the Align Retail packing subsidiary (INR2,800 crores) give a low-cost supply chain rivals struggle to match, though management concedes supply chain is the one hard part of new-state entry and last-mile capacity is a known weakness.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: DMART's owned-property, low-cost operating model is genuinely hard to replicate, but the window shows active new entry and price competition compressing gross margins rather than rational pricing. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.6 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Trajectory has deteriorated from 2022's benign view of discounting to persistent price wars and heavily funded new entrants by 2025, with capital flowing freely into the category.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Weak → Poor. Now: The opposite holds — the category is the most crowded and capital-attracting space in Indian consumer, with everyone from Zudio to quick commerce piling in. | The long view had said: The opposite of obscure — a heavily covered large cap whose annual call draws Jefferies, Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, Macquarie, CLSA and Nuvama, in a category attracting large pools of venture capital.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Reported metrics have softened on nearly every axis over the window (ROCE 19.1%→17.8%, EBITDA 8.7%→7.9%, LFL 9.9%→8.4%, e-commerce loss widening to INR247 crores), so the quality reputation is not currently being validated by improving results — the judgement rests on process discipline, not on a halo of outcomes. | The long view had said: The assessment holds up reasonably because management itself separates skill from outcome — attributing weak stores to its own execution rather than competition — while disclosing moderating ROCE, falling margins and widening e-commerce losses.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: India's vast unorganised kirana trade and weak legacy modern-retail chains remain the structural donors of share to value formats, implied by the repeated framing of untapped headroom across population strata. | The long view had said: Abundant weak competitors to take share from — unorganised kirana and roadside sellers dominate the base, and large incumbent FMCG/retail players are visibly losing ground to newer formats.

*Unchanged: Out of limelight; Mini-monopolies; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Profit margin; People matter (talent development); Convenience benefits; Candor; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Asset turn; Gain in market share; Disciplined stewards; Return on capital (headline); Technology advantage; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Cost of working capital; Intangible benefits; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Geographical expansion; Structural end-market growth.*

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these — identical by construction): Persistence of growth (Neutral); Cyclical market growth (Neutral); Network effects (Neutral).*

**Newly assessable in the recent view:**

- **Merger and acquisition** — Good: Growth is entirely organic — new formats (Food Plaza, pharmacy, Align Retail packing) are built in-house as subsidiaries rather than acquired, and no M&A is mentioned anywhere in the window.

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 1 worsened, 6 unchanged; 1 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Structurally different competitors are the recurring theme of the window: in 2023 management concedes a wave of new sub-Rs.1,000 apparel players (Zudio, Myntra) took share — 'earlier, in that segment you had fewer players... when they have more choices, obviously it has an impact for us' — and GMA mix was permanently reset down, with management guiding it will never return to 27-28%. ## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
