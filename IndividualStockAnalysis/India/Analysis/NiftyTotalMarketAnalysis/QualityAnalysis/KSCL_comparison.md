**Checks that improved:**

- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Good. Now: FY26 growth was clearly realization- and mix-led — maize revenue +40.17% on volume +18.84%, hybrid rice revenue +18.3% on flat volumes — but management flags that this pricing lever is exhausted for FY27. | The long view had said: Pricing power is structurally weak — cotton MRP is government-controlled, cost inflation could not be passed on for multiple years, and FY27 growth is explicitly expected to be volume-led with no price increase.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Breeding gestation is a real barrier for new entrants, but industry-wide high inventory and the resulting inability to raise prices signal irrational supply behaviour this season. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.6 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Real technical barriers exist in germplasm and breeding lead times, but these are offset by recurring discount wars, illegal BT seed in Gujarat, and industry-wide over-production.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Management claims dominance in a few non-cotton segments but the business faces open competition in vegetables and cotton, with no aftermarket or mind-share monopoly evident. | The long view had said: No monopoly position in any crop — roughly 16-17% share in cotton and 10-11% in maize in a market crowded with organised, small and illegal players.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Good → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹24 is operating profit. **Checks that regressed:**

Now: Growth is entirely organic and self-funded with net cash on the books (Rs 309 crore), no debt discussion and no M&A, and capex was made counter-cyclically during a weak cotton cycle. | The long view had said: Debt-free with net cash throughout, growth is entirely organic and R&D-led, no acquisitions in four years, and inventory/production was built counter-cyclically ahead of an expected cotton upturn.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Kaveri is gaining from the varietal-to-hybrid shift in maize and rice, but in cotton it is the donator rather than the recipient of share to illegal seed sellers. | The long view had said: Illegal cotton seed and weaker unorganised players are ceding ground to organised companies, and a pending Seeds Bill is expected to formalise the market further.

Now: R&D of ~Rs 60 crore/year (5-10% of revenue including capex) is visibly converting into products — new cotton hybrids went from 10.3% to 30.05% of cotton volumes and a 200+ hybrid portfolio — though vegetables R&D has underdelivered for two years. | The long view had said: R&D has compounded 15-20% a year to 5-10% of revenue and demonstrably converts into products: new-product share rose from 40%to 52% in maize, 67.67% to 75.79% in bajra, and 10.3% to 30.05% in cotton by FY26.

Now: Distribution runs through subsidiaries and a dealer channel with advance-based placements, but no evidence of superior accessibility versus peers is offered. | The long view had said: Broad multi-state dealer reach plus a digitalised farmer database and social/digital engagement tracking demand generation through to harvest gives genuine customer intimacy.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Non-cotton pricing power is real — FY26 revenue growth far outran volume in maize (+40% rev vs +19% vol) and hybrid rice (+18.3% rev on flat volume) — but cotton is price-capped, Q3 FY26 gross margins hit multiyear lows on unpassed cost, and management guided to no price increase in FY27. (The long view had said: Genuine pricing came through in FY26 (price-led revenue growth, maize revenue +40% on +19% volume) and in Aug 2024 for maize/rice, but the four-year trajectory shows the opposite as often: 2022 discounting, government-capped cotton prices, gross margins sliding from ~49.5% to 47%, and FY27 guided with no price increase at all.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Sales run through a dealer/channel network with placements, incentives and schemes rather than end-farmer marketing, but the farmer both chooses and pays, and no trade-training or professional-certification programme is described in the FY26 calls. (The long view had said: The farmer both chooses and pays; dealers are a discounting channel rather than a prescribing professional, and Kaveri markets straight to farmers with its own digital database and demand-generation tracking (Feb 2026). Price sensitivity is demonstrably high, not low — they lost 35% of cotton volume in FY25 rather than cut realisations.)

**That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Seed is a one-shot consumable sale with no licence, AMC, subscription or attach-rate revenue discussed on any of the four FY26 calls; the one customer-funding element (advances) actually shrank and management deliberately discouraged it. (The long view had said: Seeds are an annual consumable with no licence/AMC/subscription stream or installed-base attach rates discussed across the 17 calls; the only trait present is customer advances funding the season, and management is actively shrinking that (FY26 other current liabilities fell INR600cr to INR470cr on lower advances because the incentives cost margin).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (STRONG FIT); Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 3 worsened, 5 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Cheap unapproved illegal BG-II RRF seed is repeatedly cited across every FY26 call as the reason cotton volumes fell (-15% in Q1) and margins compressed; management estimates ~20% of the market is illegal and defends its premium on farmer-education grounds ('they don't know what sort of seed they are putting in'), i.e. low end-customer brand awareness with the dealer channel able to push the cheaper substitute. (The long view had said: This is the clearest structural threat: once hybrids age, cheaper players sell equivalent genetics and Kaveri must discount — realisations were cut in FY23, cost inflation could not be passed on in FY25/FY26, and profitability has ground down (gross margin from ~49.5% to 47%, EBITDA margin from ~28-29% in FY20-21 toward ~24%, Q3FY26 gross margin at multi-year lows). The buyer is a price-sensitive farmer with the dealer influencing choice, and management admits it absorbed cost rather than lose volume.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: No single named customer or supplier drives results, but Punjab is described as the largest hybrid rice market and its restriction has capped that segment's volumes for multiple years, while cotton production depends on contract growing areas where management flagged competition for acreage. (The long view had said: Revenue comes from millions of farmers through a broad dealer network across many states with no named customer driving results; management states government sales are 'an insignificant portion' of receivables and points to a diversified crop and geographic mix. The residual is the single external trait licensor (Bayer/Monsanto) for BT cotton.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The demand base itself is shifting between crops and seed types rather than merely cycling: management notes the rice market grew but 'majority of the market was in this varietal seed and not in the hybrid segment', cotton acreage has drifted down for two seasons, while maize, mustard, sunflower and millet acreage expand. (The long view had said: The demand base shifts between crops (cotton acreage falling, maize rising on ethanol policy, sunflower on edible-oil prices, mustard and hybrid rice hybridisation rising), but the shifts move within Kaveri's own portfolio rather than eroding it — maize revenue +40% in FY26 and mustard/sunflower growth offset multi-year cotton decline.)

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Cyclicality (WATCH); Technological Innovation (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
