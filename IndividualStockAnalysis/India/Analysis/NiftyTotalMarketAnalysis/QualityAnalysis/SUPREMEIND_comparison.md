**Checks that improved:**

- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Weak → Good. Now: Exclusive access to Wavin's piping technologies for India and SAARC creates a partial technology lock, though customer switching costs in commodity pipes remain low. | The long view had said: Almost no switching costs — sales run through independent distributors who destock aggressively and demand price protection when resin prices move, indicating buyers switch or defer freely.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: A narrow monopoly-like position exists in gas piping, where certification makes it the sole domestic supplier able to offer both pipes and fittings. | The long view had said: Strong brand recall ('People who know plastics best') and a leading position in organised plumbing, but roughly 85% of the market sits elsewhere and no aftermarket/service lock-in is described.
- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: End markets are in secular expansion — housing, agriculture, a threefold increase in central budget allocation for drinking water, and plastics substituting conventional materials across 45+ piping systems. | The long view had said: End markets — housing, agriculture irrigation, plumbing, infrastructure water schemes — are in secular expansion with plastics substituting other materials, supporting management's confidence to keep adding capacity through a downturn.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Certifications (DVGW), technology licences and scale provide some barriers, but imitators exist and raw-material price volatility repeatedly forces industry-wide price resets rather than rational pricing. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.9 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Low entry barriers and evidence of irrational pricing — competitors publicly accuse Supreme of price cutting, dealer price protection is routine, and look-alike entrants proliferated in packaging films.
- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: Wavin was a small, complementary bolt-on (~Rs 310 cr, 71,000 MT, same products) that was fully integrated within two quarters and brought 180 customers plus an exclusive 7-year technology licence, though consolidated OPM fell rather than improved post-deal. | The long view had said: Wavin is a genuinely complementary, cash-funded bolt-on (71,000 MT, 266 dealers, 120 salespeople) but the acquiree was weak/loss-making and diluted margins initially, so it fails the 'acquiree already strong, OPM improves' test even if the fix appears underway.
- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Good. Now: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.34 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: The business turns roughly Rs 10,000 cr of revenue while self-funding a Rs 1,200 cr capex programme entirely from internal accruals and remaining net-cash, indicating decent asset productivity, though no balance-sheet turnover detail is given. | The long view had said: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.34 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: Business is moderately asset-heavy and getting heavier — Rs1,031cr capex in 9M FY26 drained cash surplus from Rs944cr to Rs49cr and working capital rose, with fixed-cost absorption explicitly blamed for margin fall.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: The company grew piping volume 6% in FY25 while the country's piping market degrew about 6%, and accelerated to 16-17% volume growth in Q2/Q3 FY26 — clear, sustained share capture in a fragmented market. | The long view had said: Long runway (organised share only ~15%) with repeated above-industry growth — 16-17% piping volume growth and 30% CPVC growth in 9M FY26 in a weak market — though management refuses to quantify share and rivals allege price-led gains.
- **Candor** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Management openly reports misses and their causes — a shortfall at the composite cylinder division, monsoon-driven agri washout, inventory losses from falling PVC — and gives specific, revisable guidance that Q3's 16% delivery supported. | The long view had said: Mixed: they do quantify inventory losses (Rs100-120cr in 9M) and openly cut margin guidance from 15.5% to 13.5-14%, but repeatedly stonewall analyst questions on mix, market share and segment margins and defended unmet targets until very late.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: This is a widely followed large-cap with brokerage-hosted quarterly calls and multiple analysts probing guidance quarter by quarter — the opposite of an ignored niche. | The long view had said: Management style is low-key, but the company itself is a large, heavily covered listed name with quarterly calls hosted by brokers and questions from CLSA, Jefferies, Kotak and others — not an ignored niche.
- **Distribution to shareholders** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: A 550% (Rs 11/share) interim dividend was declared with stated reasoning tied to business prospects, but there is no buyback or explicit valuation-linked distribution policy discussed. | The long view had said: Consistent, well-articulated dividend policy (interim dividend of 550%, i.e. Rs 11 on Rs 2 face value, explicitly tied to business prospects) funded alongside capex from internal accruals, but no buyback discipline evidence.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Excellent → Good. Now: Distribution reach is a real and widening advantage — the Wavin deal was justified chiefly on market reach and regional service economics, supplemented by new plants closer to demand regions. | The long view had said: Distribution is the clearest durable advantage — 36 manufacturing sites across 15 states placing product close to demand, direct distributor relationships, 319 showrooms, and a network further deepened by Wavin's 266 dealers and 120 salespeople.

*Unchanged: Capex for growth (not maintenance); Geographical expansion; Halo effect (caution); Share donators; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Technology advantage; Research and development; Out of limelight; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Assurance benefits; Cost of working capital; Return on capital (headline); Profit margin; Intangible benefits; Cyclical market growth; Convenience benefits; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Disciplined stewards.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

Why now: FY25 piping volumes grew ~6% while the industry de-grew ~6%, and FY26 piping accelerated to +17% (Q2) and +16% (Q3) with 13% for 9M against a weak agri/infra backdrop; the Wavin acquisition added 71,000 MT capacity and over 180 acquired customers. The caveat is that share is being won alongside falling realisations and a 22% PAT decline, so some of it is coming at the cost of profitability. (The long view had said: Volume growth is consistently above the company's own read of the industry (Q1 FY23 pipes +55% vs country +27%; CPVC +30% in 9M FY26; capacity to 1mn MT plus the Wavin acquisition), but management repeatedly refuses to quantify share and even calls its own share gain 'conjecture'.)

- **Low Price Plus**: LIKELY FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. (The long view had said: The model is volume/turns-led with rivals accusing Supreme of price aggression (Jul 2024), and post-Wavin management cut acquired staff and overhead costs, but no itemised cost culture or structural cost advantage is articulated on the calls.)

Why now: The four FY26 calls discuss only one-time product volumes (MT sold) and capacity; there is no mention of AMC/service revenue, attach rates, renewals, installed-base monetisation or customer advances as a funding source. (The long view had said: Across all excerpted calls (Jul 2022–Jan 2026) management discusses only one-time product sales of pipes, packaging and cylinders; there is no mention of licence fees, AMC/service contracts, attach rates, renewals, deferred revenue or customer advances.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 3 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: All four FY26 calls show earnings swinging on PVC/polymer resin prices and monsoon rather than on volume: FY25 saw resin prices change 14 times since July'24, Q1 FY26 carried inventory losses, and despite 6-10% volume growth, value growth was negative or flat with consolidated PAT down 26% (Q1), 24% (H1) and 22% (9M). Demand is further tied to agriculture seasonality, housing and government infrastructure capex cycles. (The long view had said: Across all four windows the calls are dominated by polymer-price swings driving inventory gains/losses and violent margin movement — 12.24% operating margin in Q1FY23 on falling PVC, 15%+ in FY25, back to 12.1% in 9MFY26 with a Rs.100-120 crore raw-material hit and guidance cut from 14.5-15% to 13.5-14%; demand is further hostage to monsoon, agri season and channel destocking, with management repeatedly unable to forecast quarterly margins.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The composite cylinder division's FY25 shortfall was explicitly caused by one customer (IOCL) not ordering as expected, and orders since remain a handful of PSU LOIs; the industrial components division openly works to reduce dependence on a few large customers, and the new piping technology edge rests on a single licensor relationship with Orbia/Wavin. (The long view had said: The calls affirmatively show a broad base — eight product segments, direct distributor sales, 319 furniture showrooms, 36 sites across 15 states, and the Wavin deal adding 266 distributors/dealers, 180 customers and 120 salespeople; the only named-customer dependence is IOCL/BPCL in the composite cylinder division, which at roughly Rs.54 crore per 2-lakh-cylinder order is immaterial to group revenue.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: NO SIGNAL → WATCH. Why now: The consumer furniture business faces an eroding demand base that management attributes to the category itself rather than the cycle — plastic furniture demand is described as stagnant nationally, with FY25 consumer volumes down 3%. Elsewhere preference shifts run in the company's favour (plastics substituting conventional materials in piping). (The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and the only computed fingerprint is clean.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
