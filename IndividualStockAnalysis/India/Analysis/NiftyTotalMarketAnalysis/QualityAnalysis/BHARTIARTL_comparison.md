Now: Management flags four consecutive quarters of growth achieved without any tariff repair, implying the tariff cycle sits at a trough with an identifiable industry price-hike upturn still ahead. | The long view had said: The company sits late rather than early in the tariff-repair cycle — wireless growth fell below 10% for the first time in years and management concedes no repair is in sight while energy and FX costs rise.

Now: FY26 India capex of ~Rs.31,000 Cr is explicitly aimed at growth (11,000 5G sites and 11,000 km fiber in Q3, 2m home passes, 1 GW data centre ambition) while India EBITDAaL still grew ~18% and OFCF hit Rs.41,500 Cr, indicating high incremental returns rather than maintenance spend. | The long view had said: Sustained heavy growth capex — 5G pan-India, 43,000 km of fiber and 8mn home passes in FY26, plus a 1GW data-centre ambition — funded from operating cash while leverage fell from 2.52x to 1.1x, though telecom's capital intensity caps incremental ROIC.

Now: The only deal in the window is a large related-party share swap for an additional 16.3% of Airtel Africa — simple and in an already-strong asset (Africa margin 38.8%, net debt/EBITDAaL 0.7), but it is a big consolidation rather than a small complementary bolt-on, with no post-deal OPM evidence yet. | The long view had said: Explicit bolt-on-only doctrine in digital adjacencies plus spectrum trading/M&A, and the one large FY26 deal (16.3% Airtel Africa) is a no-cash intra-group swap in an asset already owned and understood.

Now: Convenience is a stated growth lever: simplified international roaming plans driving 30%+ roaming revenue growth, NBFC lending integrated into Airtel Thanks, and IPTV positioned on experience and convenience. | The long view had said: Exceptional accessibility built over the period — LCO-partnered home passes, fibre in 1,500+ cities and FWA in 3,200, all channels selling all services, plus expanded B2B sales capacity into SMBs.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 7. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 4 strengthened, 2 weakened, 2 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Every one of the four calls claims industry-leading revenue growth with rising share of 5G handset shipments, record broadband net adds (939k → 951k → 1.2m) and 4.4m mobile revenue-earning adds in Q3 — and crucially the share gain came alongside margin expansion (51.4% → 52%) and deliberate exit of low-margin B2B revenue, not bought with profitability. (The long view had said: Share gain is claimed and evidenced consistently across all four years and every segment — #1 B2B player (2022), postpaid base ~45% larger than nearest competitor, DTH share gains through an industry decline, rising 5G shipment share, B2B core-connectivity share, culminating in lifetime-high mobility revenue market share in FY2026 — and it was won alongside rising ARPU and margins (India EBITDAaL margin 51%→51.7%), not bought with price.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Telecom subscription base is inherently recurring and the calls repeatedly frame the front-end device/CPE sale plus the annuity behind it — removing set-top-box subsidies (customer funds the box upfront) while IPTV/FWA CPE builds an installed base; installed-base metrics are disclosed each quarter (13m connected homes, 3m FWA, 181m 5G users). Deferred revenue/customer advances are not explicitly discussed as a funding source in these excerpts, but prepaid-in-advance economics show up as consistently strong operating free cash flow (Rs.41,500 Cr+ in FY2026). (The long view had said: The core business is subscription/installed-base driven — 13m connected homes, 181m 5G customers, convergence cutting churn ~50% — and Indian prepaid is billed in advance, but across the 16 calls management never discusses attach rates, renewal rates or deferred revenue/customer advances as a funding source, so the classic product-upfront-plus-annuity framing is absent.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Named launches with revenue impact recur each quarter (all-in-one OTT pack, IPTV, FWA to 3,000+ cities, AI-enabled RAN energy engine, sovereign Airtel Cloud with 24 deals) and the in-house tech stack is being exported to Africa, Payments Bank and Indus — but no R&D spend, pipeline or share-of-industry-R&D disclosure supports full innovation dominance. (The long view had said: There is a consistent, named innovation cadence — self-optimising AI network (2022), FWA and 5G SA for uplink (2024), industry-first AI anti-spam and voice bots, AI writing ~30% of code (2026) — but R&D spend is never disclosed or quantified as a share of industry, and the innovation is operational/cost-led rather than a high-gross-margin R&D flywheel driving pricing.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: The replication barrier is concrete and stated in the calls: a 500,000 km domestic fibre network (130,000 km laid in three years), ~11,000 5G sites added in a single quarter covering 74% of population, 38,000 solarised sites, and Rs.31,000 Cr of annual India capex — plus finite licensed spectrum. A well-funded newcomer cannot rebuild this in a few years. (The long view had said: Some assets are genuinely hard to rebuild — the largest mid-band spectrum pool accumulated 'through auctions, M&A and trading over the last five years', 1,43,000 km of fiber in three years, 45 million home passes, subsea cables and Nxtra's gigawatt data-centre build — but this is a licensed duopoly whose economics were themselves reset by a well-funded new entrant within a few years, and the calls give no articulated reason a comparably funded challenger could not close the gap again.)

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: ARPU rose every quarter (250 → 256 → 259) with simultaneous customer additions, and premiumisation is the core stated strategy — but management explicitly and repeatedly states there has been no tariff increase, so the ARPU lift is mix and upgrade-led rather than headline price increases; genuine pricing power remains regulator/competition-gated. (The long view had said: ARPU rose from Rs.183 (Aug 2022) to Rs.211 (Aug 2024) to Rs.259 (Q3 FY26) while the company simultaneously added customers every quarter (4.4m revenue-earning adds in Q3 FY26), with an explicit premiumisation playbook — feature-to-smartphone, prepaid-to-postpaid, data monetisation, international roaming up 30% y/y; the qualifier is that step-changes depend on industry-wide tariff repair, and Q4 FY26 ARPU slipped to Rs.257 with management saying they were 'not happy'.)
- **Brand Strength**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: The Airtel name is stretched into adjacencies (Airtel Finance, Airtel Money NBFC, Airtel Cloud, Airtel Payments Bank, Airtel Thanks) with a stated 'right to win' filter, and premium positioning is consistent — but the calls disclose no A&P spend, heritage narrative, or brand-driven premium pricing evidence. (The long view had said: The Airtel name is repeatedly stretched into adjacencies — Airtel Black, Payments Bank, Airtel Finance/Airtel Money NBFC, Airtel Cloud, Airtel IQ, Nxtra, One Airtel — while simultaneously commanding premium pricing and taking share, which is the brand test; A&P spend itself is never disclosed on the calls.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
