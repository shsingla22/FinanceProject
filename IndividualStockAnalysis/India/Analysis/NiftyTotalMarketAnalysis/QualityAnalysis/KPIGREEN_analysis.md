# KPI Green Energy (KPIGREEN) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

KPI Green Energy is an Indian renewable power company (part of the KP Group, with in-house EPC expertise since 2008) that builds and runs solar, wind and hybrid solar-wind power plants, mainly in Gujarat. It makes money two ways: it builds plants for industrial and commercial customers who want their own captive power — earning a one-off construction/sale fee of roughly Rs. 4.25–4.50 crore per MW plus an ongoing land-lease and operations-and-maintenance fee of about Rs. 10 lakh per MW per year — and it owns plants itself, selling the electricity under 25-year power purchase agreements to state utilities such as GUVNL, which gives it steady, high-margin annuity income. Alongside this it takes third-party EPC and balance-of-plant construction orders (for example from GSECL and Adani Green) and is moving into battery storage, floating solar, green hydrogen and power trading. Revenue has grown fast — from Rs. 647 crore in FY23 to Rs. 2,742 crore in FY26, with PAT of Rs. 509 crore in FY26, an installed capacity of about 1.62 GW and an installed-plus-upcoming portfolio of roughly 6.26 GW.

**The parts of the business:**

- **CPP (Captive Power Producer)** — Builds solar/wind/hybrid plants that are sold to industrial and commercial clients for their own captive use, then leases them the land and runs the plant for a recurring O&M fee of about Rs. 10 lakh per MW per year; this was historically the bigger revenue earner (about 85% of FY23 revenue, Rs. 550 crore) and stands at roughly 3.69 GW of installed plus upcoming capacity by March 2026.
- **IPP (Independent Power Producer)** — KPI owns and operates its own solar, wind and hybrid plants and sells the electricity under 25-year PPAs with state utilities like GUVNL — a smaller share of revenue but far higher margin and the source of long-term annuity income; the portfolio is about 2.57 GW installed plus upcoming and is expected to generate over 390 crore units of electricity a year.
- **EPC / Balance-of-Plant contracts for third parties** — Uses its engineering and construction arm to build plants for outside customers — recent examples being a 152 MW floating solar contract from GSECL at Kadana Dam and balance-of-plant supply and site services for Adani Green totalling 534 MW at Khavda (later cited as 1.13 GW), sitting within an order book of about Rs. 6,000 crore that carries price-escalation clauses to protect margins.
- **Battery Energy Storage (BESS)** — A newer line: a 445 MW / 890 MWh standalone battery project awarded by GUVNL with viability gap funding needing roughly Rs. 1,000–1,100 crore of investment at an expected 13–14% IRR, plus a further 120 MW / 240 MWh project — described as a key future growth segment rather than a current earnings driver.
- **Land bank and power evacuation infrastructure** — Owns a large land bank (6,275 acres as of June 2025) and transmission/evacuation capacity of over 3.2 GW at 11 kV and 66 kV, which management presents as the enabling asset base that lets it site and connect both CPP and IPP projects quickly; land carried at only about Rs. 83 crore in the books versus a stated much higher current value.
- **Power trading** — Recently obtained an interstate trading licence from CERC and an intrastate licence from GERC, allowing it to buy and sell power on exchanges and through bilateral contracts across India for better realisations — described as new and still small.
- **Emerging segments (green hydrogen, EV charging, floating solar, offshore wind, pumped storage)** — Early-stage areas backed by three MOUs with Delta Electronics India covering storage, green hydrogen, EV charging and solar inverters; management lists these as future capability-building rather than material revenue today.
- **Sundrops Energia (subsidiary)** — A subsidiary that did Rs. 586 crore of revenue with Rs. 130 crore PBT and Rs. 97 crore PAT in FY26 and is being prepared for its own IPO (DRHP stage).
- **International expansion (Botswana)** — An MOU with the government of Botswana for up to about 5 GW of renewable generation and storage (cited as roughly Rs. 36,000 crore of investment), with an initial 500 MW targeted within a couple of years — a stated ambition, not yet an operating business.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

KPI Green Energy has grown at a pace that is hard to ignore — sales up about 92% a year and profits about 104% a year over five years, with the portfolio going from 312 MW in FY23 to 6.26 GW installed-plus-upcoming in FY26, on a stated bid win rate of roughly 80–90%. It sells into an end market that is clearly expanding: India's 500 GW renewable target with about 45 GW still to be tendered and conventional plants being replaced, which is why the structural end-market check is one of the few strong positives (+2) and why shifting customer preferences register as a LOW risk rather than a threat. Customers get a real, simple benefit — captive power cheaper than the grid, delivered in 3–6 months against peers' 18, with an 85% generation guarantee — and that shows up as the only positive quality area, Customer Benefits at +0.75. But the multibagger screen found no pattern that fully fits: five are outright NO FIT and six only PARTIAL, and the framework grades the business at 28 out of 100, or Weak, with resilience marked STRAINED.

The reason the growth does not convert into quality is that the same features producing the growth also produce the weaknesses. Growth is purely volume-driven — more megawatts — because tariffs are locked for 25 years under PPAs and captive power is deliberately sold at a discount to the grid, with price-variation clauses used only to defend margins, never to expand them. That single fact scores Pricing, Mix and Volume at -2 and is exactly why the Pricing Power pattern is a NO FIT. The same 25-year GUVNL contracts that create the genuine annuity layer behind the PARTIAL verdict on Recurring Revenue — plus O&M and lease income of about ₹10 lakh per MW per year on the captive side — are also what make Government Dependency a HIGH RISK and Stakeholder Concentration ELEVATED, since the 250 MW solar, 370 MW hybrid, 150 MW wind and 445/890 MWh battery awards all hinge on one counterparty. Buying growth this way is expensive: every ₹1 of assets produces only about ₹0.27 of sales (FY26 revenue of ₹2,742 crores sitting on ₹9,882 crores of assets), roughly 356 days of cash is tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and the business generated only ₹5 of operating cash per ₹100 of capital employed. Return on Capital is the worst quality area at -1.0, with Capital Allocation at -0.75 close behind, and the headline growth itself scores -2 for persistence because it arrives in lumps.

The one durable advantage anyone can point to is physical scarcity: 6,275 acres of contiguous Gujarat land bank and 3+ GW of granted power evacuation, which management calls the real bottleneck for competitors. That is the entire basis for the PARTIAL on Cost to Replicate and the only silver lining offered against the Good Enough Goods risk — and it is also, unavoidably, what makes the balance sheet so asset-heavy in the first place. Strip that away and the offering is undifferentiated engineering and construction using bought-in panels, won through competitive tenders where returns are set by bid economics (13–14% IRR on the GUVNL battery award, a stated floor of 12%), against far larger bidders like Adani, NTPC and SJVN, with GUVNL as a monopsony buyer setting tariffs. Hence -2 on both Oligopoly and Network Effects, and profit margins that swing by ±9.1 points. Management's track record is genuinely mixed rather than bad — growth guidance of 50–70% was promised and met across FY24–FY26 and the 250 MW GUVNL block came in ahead of schedule, and hard questions on falling returns, the interest burden and the SBI pledge are answered candidly, which is what keeps Corporate Culture at PARTIAL. Against that sits a steady stream of headline MOUs (Botswana, South Korea, Delta, Gujarat) with little follow-through and growth commitments made on television, scoring -2 on Out of Limelight; the same MOU-driven pivots into batteries, green hydrogen, floating solar, offshore wind and pumped storage are what put Technological Innovation on WATCH, alongside a project redesign from tracker to fixed-tilt that added roughly ₹700 crore of cost.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) — PARTIAL
*"Sells once, earns forever"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 3 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** There is a genuine annuity layer — 25-year GUVNL PPAs on the IPP side and O&M/lease income of ~Rs.10 lakh per MW per year on CPP, described consistently from the Jun-2023 call ('annuity income at 325 per annum... for 25 years') through May-2026 ('strengthening the company's recurring revenue base'). But it fails the capital-light, customer-funded test: growth is funded by debt and equity capex (interest peaking near Rs.300 crores, inventory of Rs.1,400 crores), not by advances or deferred revenue.

> "Our expanding IPP portfolio is expected to generate more than 390 crore units annually, strengthening the company's recurring revenue base" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ About 356 days of cash is tied up in the trade cycle — customers are not funding the business.
- ❌ Capital spending is about 113% of sales — growth is capital-hungry.
- ❌ Operating cash was positive in 5 of 8 years — not fully consistent.

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**What the calls show:** The captive proposition is cheaper-than-grid power delivered fast (3–6 months vs peers' 18) with an 85% generation guarantee, and margins hold up (PAT 16–17%) — but across all eight calls there is no cost-obsession culture, no itemised savings, and no evidence the cost edge is hard to copy rather than a function of Gujarat land and policy timing.

> "people are taking 18 months but we are doing in three to six months" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹48 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 0.3× a year — not the high-turnover, low-margin machine this pattern needs.

*"Small cost, can't skip it"*
