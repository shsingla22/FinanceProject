### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 6. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 5 strengthened, 1 weakened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Every call in the window claims a majority or 'significant share' of customers' incremental rollouts plus the transition of competitors' existing sites onto Indus, and Q4 FY26 adds an explicit leadership position in IBS with DAS gaining in metros, tunnels and highways — share gained on service quality and execution rather than on price concessions or credit. (The long view had said: Market share is the first-listed strategic pillar in every call from FY24 onward and the delivery is verifiable: macro towers grew from 186,474 (Q1 FY23) to ~264,500 (Q4 FY26), tenancy ratio held at an industry-leading 1.62–1.66, IBS leadership achieved in Q4 FY26, plus Africa entry (Zambia licence secured, Uganda/Nigeria pending) with an explicitly local-adaptation cost model.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: The entire model is long-term recurring rental from an installed base, and every call in the FY26 window frames growth as co-locations added onto existing towers at incremental tenancy well above the portfolio average — i.e. recurring revenue that thickens with installed-base density at low incremental capital. Loading revenue from 5G equipment on already-built sites is repeatedly cited as the growth driver, though the excerpts never discuss customer advances or deferred revenue as a funding source. (The long view had said: The business is genuinely recurring — 10-year MSA renewals give long-term rental visibility, revenue compounds with installed base (186k towers/336k co-locations in Aug 2022 to 264.5k/428k by May 2026), plus loading revenue and annual escalations. But it fails the funding test: customers pay in arrears and the Vodafone Idea receivables crisis dominated FY23–FY25, and 70% of capex is growth capex, so this is capital-hungry, not customer-funded.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: The FY26 calls contain no discussion of brand heritage, A&P spend, premium pricing from brand, or category stretch — this is a B2B infrastructure contract business and the evidence is silent on brand economics. (The long view had said: the calls are silent and the numbers are mixed (1 of 2 checks supporting).)
- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Management flagged bad news promptly (Q1 FY26 diesel up 10% year-on-year, Q2 co-location additions moderating on a major customer's slower rollout) and then delivered the fix it promised — diesel moved from +10% to +3% to -4% to -7% year-on-year across the four calls, and the lithium-ion battery rollout was executed 'as indicated in the previous earnings call'. Leadership has been stable since FY2023 under Prachur Sah with disciplined capex governance and a INR 14 final dividend, though the two-MD churn in FY2022 sits just outside this window. (The long view had said: Positives are real — bad news disclosed bluntly (Bimal Dayal's "elephant in the room" on receivables, Rs 12.3bn prudent provision taken proactively), ROCE/RoE reported every single quarter, and multi-year promises delivered (collections restored, then buyback in FY25, then the Rs 14 dividend in FY26 as flagged). Against that, three MDs across FY22–FY23 and a promoter selling down 18% argue against long-tenured, owner-minded stability.)
- **Toll Roads**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Indus is a critical, near-oligopoly supplier of passive infrastructure with 99.97%+ uptime that operators cannot run networks without, but tower rental is a large opex line for telcos rather than <1-2% of cost, and the calls show customers actively moving sites between towercos — the opposite of 'don't switch once converted'. (The long view had said: Tower rental is a large, negotiated line in a telco's cost base, not a sub-1% invisible input: the Aug 2022 MSA renewal forced a discount of roughly Rs 500 per tenancy and gave customers an option to exit up to 9% of sites without exit charges. Customers demonstrably do negotiate.)

**That weakened:**

- **Cost to Replicate**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The pan-India base of ~264,500 macro towers and 442,000 co-locations, built through state-by-state RoW permissions and reaching finite, hard-won locations like Ladakh at 4,500m, Lakshadweep and Tawang forward posts, is not something a funded entrant rebuilds quickly. But it is capital-replicable rather than time- or data-locked, and the same calls show tenancies migrating between towercos, so the barrier is scale and site rights rather than an un-crossable one. (The long view had said: A pan-India grid of ~264,500 towers on finite, right-of-way-approved sites with landlord contracts and 99.977% uptime is not rebuildable by a funded newcomer in a few years, and management articulates the switching barrier concretely — moving a grid degrades the operator's own end-customer experience and costs real money. The caveat is that this replication moat has not converted into pricing power against a concentrated customer base.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (LIKELY FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Incremental growth is explicitly tied to operator network-expansion capex — management repeatedly attributes quarterly tower/co-location swings to customer rollout pace (Q2 co-locations 'moderated due to slower rollouts by a major customer'; Q3/Q4 pickup as a major customer's capex revived), and notes 5G rollout momentum has tapered from ~487k to ~531k BTS over the year. This is bounded by the annuity nature of the installed base: ~442,000 co-locations on long-term rentals plus loading revenue continue regardless of new build. (The long view had said: Tower and tenancy additions track customers' capex programmes — 5G rollout drove an 'abnormal year of expansion' in FY25 and management concedes 'the pace of incremental rollouts has moderated' — and an analyst directly asked how growth sustains once the customer's catch-up capex phase ends; reported EBITDA/PAT have swung violently (-34% then +29% then -36% y-o-y) on provisions.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The asset base is physical macro towers whose value depends on the prevailing radio architecture, and management's own commentary shows the growth driver (5G base-station build) already maturing — 'large-scale deployments stabilize' — leaving loading rather than new technology cycles as the driver. No disruptive threat is discussed defensively in these calls; innovation shown is incremental (IoT devices, AI/ML outage prediction, AI voice agents, computer vision, GFRP research with IIT-Madras, lithium-ion migration). (The long view had said: Each technology generation has increased rather than obsoleted the asset: 2G-to-4G-to-5G migration and 5G densification drive loading revenues on existing towers, and the company's own innovation is incremental (lithium batteries, solar, IoT sensors, AI-led fault detection) rather than a platform bet; the only mild negative is 'network optimization by our customers' weighing on growth.)
- **New Entrants**: NO SIGNAL → LOW. Why now: The calls show Indus gaining rather than ceding ground against existing tower peers: customers are transferring already-built sites onto its portfolio and it claims a majority share of new rollouts, with no structurally different competitor format discussed in any of the four calls. (The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and all 2 computed fingerprints are clean.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (HIGH RISK); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
