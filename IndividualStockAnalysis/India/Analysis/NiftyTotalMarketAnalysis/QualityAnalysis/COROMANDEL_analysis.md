# Coromandel International Ltd. (COROMANDEL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FERTILISERS & PESTICIDES*

Coromandel International is an Indian company that makes and sells farm inputs — mainly phosphate-based fertilizers such as DAP, NPK complexes and single super phosphate (SSP), plus crop-protection chemicals — to farmers, largely through a dealer channel and its own retail centres. It earns money by buying raw materials (rock phosphate, phosphoric acid, ammonia, sulphur, mostly imported and dollar-priced), converting them into finished fertilizer at its own plants, and selling at government-controlled prices topped up by a government subsidy under the Nutrient Based Subsidy scheme, so profitability depends on the gap between raw-material costs and the subsidy rates New Delhi announces each season. Its home market is southern India (Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and neighbouring states), where the calls say it is the largest phosphatic player with roughly 17.5% national market share and record sales of about 4.3 million tons of DAP/NPK in FY26, and it is now pushing into north and central India. It is also buying its way backwards up the supply chain — a rock phosphate mine in Senegal (71.5% owned) and new phosphoric/sulphuric acid plants at Kakinada — and its results swing with the monsoon, since demand follows rainfall and sowing.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Nutrition and allied business (fertilizers)** — The core and by far the largest business — manufacturing and selling DAP and NPK complex fertilizers (record 3.5 million tons produced and 4.3 million tons sold in FY26 across plants at Kakinada, Vizag and Ennore), which drives most of the revenue and is the segment management spends nearly all the call time on.
- **Single super phosphate (SSP)** — A cheaper phosphate fertilizer where the company says it is the market leader, selling a record 8.4 lakh tons in FY26, increasingly in higher-priced 'differentiated' versions like GroPlus, GroAlpha and Urea-SSP that now make up over half the volume.
- **Crop protection (agrochemicals)** — Pesticides and related chemicals sold both in India and exported; the calls treat it as the clear number-two business, and it has been volatile — hit by global destocking and weak domestic demand in muted seasons, with exports recovering by FY26.
- **Specialty nutrients and organic products** — Higher-value plant nutrition products including water-soluble/specialty fertilizers and organic and seaweed-based inputs, described as a strong-growing but smaller business, with new plants (technical MAP, seaweed granulation) being built to expand it.
- **Bio products** — Biological crop inputs, run under the same executive as crop protection and retail; management flags a global and Indian shift toward biologicals as a growth driver but gives no size.
- **Nano fertilizers (Nano DAP)** — A newer liquid nano-nutrient line launched around November 2023 and described as well received, but still a small, emerging part of the mix.
- **Retail and drone spraying services (Gromor Drive)** — Company-run farm retail centres plus a drone crop-spraying service using drones from group company Dhaksha, which covered about 3 lakh acres in FY26 — a small service business used mainly to build farmer relationships.
- **Backward integration / mining** — Not a selling business but a cost lever: the Senegal rock phosphate mine (3.5 lakh tons output in FY26, stake raised to 71.5%), a majority stake in BMCC, and new sulphuric and phosphoric acid plants at Kakinada intended to cover 60%-70% of its own acid needs and secure raw material supply.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Coromandel comes out of the three reviews as a genuinely well-run company that happens to sit in a business it does not fully control. The 34-check framework gives it top marks on the things a company can decide for itself: a decade-long backward-integration plan announced in 2022 and delivered on schedule by 2026 (Senegal rock stake lifted from 45% to 71.5%, Kakinada phosphoric and sulphuric acid plants commissioned in March 2026), over INR 3,000 crores of growth capex funded from net cash of INR 4,214 crores, working capital so tight that cash comes back one day before suppliers have to be paid, and management that volunteers bad news plainly — the drone-business impairment, the Nano bans in Uttar Pradesh and Maharashtra, the earlier fall in market share. That same self-help energy shows up as real share capture, with consumption share rising from about 17% to 20% and primary share reaching 22% against 18%. But the area scores tell the other half of the story: capital allocation and competitive advantage score well, while return on capital scores lowest of all — the company is executing hard inside an industry whose economics it does not set.

The pattern screen sorts neatly along that same line. Everything Coromandel controls scores well: Forward Integrators is the one strong fit, built on 1,200-plus owned rural stores growing 85% year-on-year with three-quarters of them profitable, plus nutri clinics, drone spraying and last-mile delivery — the same asset the quality framework flags as a distribution moat and a convenience benefit. Low Price Plus, Innovation Dominance and Market Share Gainers all come in as likely fits on the strength of backward integration, ten new crop-protection products contributing 21% of that revenue, and repeatedly rising share numbers. Everything the government or the commodity market controls fails: Pricing Power is a clear no-fit because subsidy rates 'do not reflect the sharp increase in raw material prices' and margins compressed rather than expanded, and Toll Roads is a no-fit because fertiliser is one of the farmer's biggest cost lines, not a small indispensable input. Brand Strength, Cost to Replicate and Friendly Middleman all land at partial for the same underlying reason — the core product is a commodity that imported DAP and generic NPK compete against on price alone. One flag deserves discounting: Recurring Revenue registers only as a quant signal, meaning the numbers hint at it but the eighteen earnings calls offer no supporting evidence.

The risk review then names the single fault line the other two analyses circle around. Government Dependency is the one high risk, and it is the direct cause of the Pricing Power and Toll Roads no-fits, of the weak return-on-capital score, and of the quarterly swings in subsidy receivables between roughly Rs 1,700 and Rs 3,800 crore. Four risks sit on watch, and each pairs with a strength that is also a partial answer to it: raw-material concentration through the Strait of Hormuz is exactly what the Senegal mine and captive acid capacity were built to blunt, which is why the same backward-integration trait powers the Low Price Plus pattern and mitigates the Stakeholder Concentration risk; the Good Enough Goods risk of a commodity product is answered by unique grades at about 35% of volume and the owned retail chain, the very things that keep Brand Strength and Cost to Replicate at partial rather than strong; and the Technological Innovation watch — nano fertiliser, biologicals, green ammonia, drones — cuts both ways, since Nano DAP leadership at roughly 60% share feeds Innovation Dominance while the Nano bans management disclosed show the same shift can bite. Cyclicality is a cost-side rather than demand-side problem, with phosphoric acid settling at $1,700 a ton against $1,360 while farm consumption stayed flat. The clearest bridge out of the dependency is the non-subsidy business — retail, crop protection, specialty nutrients and bio — now carrying about 52% of EBITDA. Overall resilience reads sound, and the combined score of 70 lands the company at Strong: high-quality execution, a real and rare forward-integration story, and a ceiling set by someone else's subsidy formula.

> "60% to 70% of our captive acid requirements will be met internally, giving us a lot of operational flexibility and raw material security" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Across all 18 calls the economics are set by government: NBS subsidy rates, DAP special packages, PM-PRANAM, freight subsidy on SSP, subsidy receivables of Rs 1,700–3,800 crore swinging quarterly, and in FY26–FY27 an explicit margin squeeze because announced NBS rates did not cover post-Middle East raw material inflation, with management 'representing to government' for relief. > "These rates do not fully compensate for the increase in global raw material costs, which happened subsequent to Middle East crisis." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Non-subsidy segments (crop protection, specialty nutrients, bio, retail — retail +85% year-on-year, CP EBIT +44%) now carry ~52% of EBITDA in Q1FY27, a genuine diversification away from policy-driven revenue*

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Demand is a flow product — farmers consume fertilizer every season and industry consumption held ~24 mn tons flat year on year — but the input side is a pure commodity cycle: phos acid settled at $1,700/t in Q2FY27 vs $1,360, ammonia and sulphur spikes forced management to cut output to 72% utilisation, and EBITDA has oscillated (Rs 687cr Q1FY23, Rs 975cr Q2FY25, Rs 761cr Q1FY27) with margins compressing whenever commodities run ahead of subsidy. > "We have taken a conscious call to moderate production to 6.9 lakh tons compared to 8.4 lakh tons, roughly representing 72% of capacity utilization" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Consumable/operations-linked demand (not customer capex), plus countercyclical backward-integration capex and record 4.3 mn ton sales while peers cut output*

## What to watch

- Non-subsidy share of EBITDA — if it stalls below the roughly 52% reached in Q1FY27, the Government Dependency high risk stays the binding constraint and the Forward Integrators fit loses its payoff

- New-product contribution and Nano regulatory treatment — the 21% of crop-protection revenue from ten new products and the ~60% Nano DAP share carry the Innovation Dominance fit, while further bans like those in Uttar Pradesh and Maharashtra would tip the Technological Innovation watch risk against the company
