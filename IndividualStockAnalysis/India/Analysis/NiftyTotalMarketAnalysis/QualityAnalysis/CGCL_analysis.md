# Capri Global (CGCL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Capri Global Capital (CGCL) is an Indian non-bank lender — it borrows money (from banks and the debt market) and lends it out at higher rates, keeping the difference. Every loan it makes is backed by collateral: gold jewellery, a house, a shop or other property, or a residential project under construction. Its customers are mostly small, self-employed retail borrowers and small businesses, plus mid-sized property developers, served through a branch network that reached 1,429 locations and roughly 6.9 lakh (690,000) customers by March 2026, spread across India with heavy recent expansion into the south and east (Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Odisha). Alongside interest income (yields around 16.3% and a spread of about 7% in Q4 FY26), it earns fee income from distributing car loans and insurance for other institutions and from co-lending arrangements with banks; total loan book (AUM) was INR36,623 crores in March 2026, up 60% year-on-year, with full-year profit of INR949 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Gold Loan** — Small loans against household gold jewellery, made through dedicated branches; launched around 2022 and now the biggest business at INR16,965 crores AUM (grew 111% in FY26), with very low bad loans (0.3% gross NPA) and about INR17 crores of loans per branch.
- **Housing Finance (affordable home loans)** — Home loans in the affordable segment, increasingly to self-employed borrowers (74% of the book), at INR7,447 crores AUM and growing 43% year-on-year.
- **MSME lending (including Micro LAP)** — Secured business loans to micro, small and medium enterprises — INR6,465 crores AUM, up 23% — including a newer 'Micro LAP' sub-vertical of smaller-ticket loans to emerging self-employed borrowers (INR824 crores across 151+ branches).
- **Construction Finance** — Project loans to mid-sized and small residential developers in metro and Tier-1 cities, run with escrow-controlled cash flows; INR5,708 crores across 282 active projects, and management deliberately caps this at or below about 20% of the total book.
- **Co-lending (with partner banks)** — Loans originated by Capri but funded jointly with banks, so Capri earns fees and keeps only part of the loan on its own balance sheet — a capital-light growth route that had reached INR7,138 crores, about 23.5% of AUM, by December 2025.
- **Car loan distribution** — A fee-only business where Capri sources and originates car loans for other lenders rather than lending itself — an early and steady contributor to non-interest income (e.g. INR11 billion of originations and INR186 million of net fees in a single quarter of FY23).
- **Insurance distribution** — Selling insurance to customers for fee income; described as a newer stream expected to contribute from FY25 onward.
- **Indirect / wholesale lending** — A smaller legacy wholesale book grouped with construction finance in reporting (together about 20% of AUM in FY24), historically with near-nil bad loans.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Capri Global rates a 53 out of 100 — "Decent" — and the three analyses agree on why: the company is very good at growing and only modestly protected once it has grown. The quality framework scores Growth at 1.2 and Customer Benefits at 1.0, its two best areas, on the back of a gold-loan business built from zero to INR16,965 crores, consolidated assets under management of INR36,623 crores compounding at 60% year-on-year, a branch network taken to 1,429 with 318 added in FY26, sub-30-minute gold loan turnaround and 85 lakh app engagements. Management scores 0.83 for promising a 1,500-branch chain in Aug 2022 and an INR300 billion FY27 asset target and effectively hitting both a year early, and for building all of it organically with a 100% secured book, conservative loan-to-value ratios, escrow-based construction finance, a self-imposed 20% cap on construction finance and leverage of 2.8-3.3 times. The multibagger screen confirms the same picture from a different angle: the only two strong fits are Market Share Gainers and Innovation Dominance — growth and the technology behind it — while the moat-shaped patterns (Pricing Power, Toll Roads, Friendly Middleman) are outright no-fits. That is why the multibagger fit contributes only 30 out of 100 to the blended score even though business quality contributes 63.

The single trait that explains most of the record is that Capri Global competes by building and spending rather than by owning something others cannot copy, and this shows up as a strength and a weakness at the same time. The four-year in-house technology programme — INR112 crores in FY24 alone, LOS/LMS, SPARK, Orion, Pegasus and a proprietary small language model — earns a +2 on the research and development check and the Innovation Dominance strong fit, and it produced hard output: cost-to-income compressed from about 70.5% in Q4FY24 to 49.4% in Q4FY26 and disbursements up 83% on only 19% more staff. But the Low Price Plus pattern is only a partial fit precisely because that efficiency is kept as margin instead of being passed to borrowers as a lower rate, so the cost advantage never turns into customer lock-in. The same happens with the physical network. The 1,429 branches drive the +2 capex check, the +2 convenience check and the partial Forward Integrators fit — and are simultaneously the reason the Technological Innovation risk is flagged as a watch item, because that brick-and-mortar base is capital-heavy if origination moves digital, and management's answer to fintech competition ("fintechs do not have the balance sheet") was read as defensive. Meanwhile the industry itself offers no help: the barriers-to-entry check is the framework's worst at -1, dragging Industry Structure to -0.17, because banks are pushing down-market, fintechs are entering origination, and pricing power had to be regained rather than being structurally held. This is the direct cause of the Pricing Power no-fit — in FY24 management conceded it could not pass on a 50 basis point rise in funding cost on fixed-rate loans, and the FY26 yield improvement came from product mix, not from the ability to charge more.

The risk review reinforces rather than contradicts this, and the one high risk sits on top of the company's own solution to its main competitive problem. Because gold and loan-against-property lending is close to a commodity where borrowers shop on rate — the Good Enough Goods watch item — Capri Global leans on co-lending with banks to match cheaper bank pricing. That same co-lending channel is roughly 21-23% of assets and about half of fee income, and it is exactly what the new RBI guidelines effective Jan 2026 hit, which is why Government Dependency is the only high risk; the earlier gold-loan cash-disbursement circular showed the same regulator-sets-the-economics dynamic. The pivot to direct assignment is the stated substitute. Cyclicality is a watch item for a related reason: gold loans are now about 46% of the book and grew partly with gold prices, with analysts explicitly asking how much of the growth was tonnage versus price — so the business line that drove the market share gain is also the one carrying price exposure. On the other side, three risks are low — no single customer, lender or partner drives results across 6.9 lakh customers and 35-plus lenders, demand is functional rather than taste-driven, and the shift to digital channels has already been met. The remaining gap is capital: the distribution-to-shareholders check is -1 because the company is a net capital raiser (an INR1,200 crore rights issue and an INR2,000 crore equity infusion in Q1FY26) paying only a token 15% dividend on INR1 face value with no buyback, and Return on Capital scores 0.0 with resilience recorded as unknown. In short, shareholders are currently funding a land-grab in structurally expanding markets — MSME credit at over 30% of GDP and affordable housing at 11-12% penetration — executed well but without proof yet that the position, once taken, earns a protected return.
