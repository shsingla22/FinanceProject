# Ashapura Minechem (ASHAPURMIN) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Ashapura Minechem is an Indian mining and minerals company that digs up rocks and either ships them out raw or processes them into higher-value industrial materials. Its biggest business by far is in Guinea, West Africa, where it holds mining concessions for bauxite (the ore used to make aluminium) and iron ore, and exports bauxite by sea, mainly to Chinese alumina refineries — it shipped about 8 million tons in FY26 versus 3.5 million the year before, and built its own roads and three ports to do it. Its older, smaller India business mines bentonite clay and other minerals in Gujarat and converts them into value-added products such as bleaching clay, kaolin, organoclay and ceramic materials, sold into edible oils, foundries, oil and gas, paints, construction and other industries and exported to more than 80 countries. In FY26 the group did INR 5,237 crores of revenue with INR 674 crores of EBITDA, of which Guinea supplied roughly INR 4,200 crores of revenue and INR 561 crores of EBITDA — so the company is essentially a bauxite exporter with a specialty-minerals arm attached.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Guinea operations (bauxite)** — Mines bauxite from three concessions holding over 700 million tons of reserves and ships it to customers (priced around $70/ton CIF China, roughly $10.5 per ton of EBITDA in Q3 FY26); it is the dominant part of the group, about 76% of Q3 FY26 revenue and about INR 4,200 crores of FY26 revenue, with port capacity being expanded from 15 to 20 million tons and a long-term volume target of 15 million tons by FY27-28.
- **Guinea iron ore** — A licensed iron ore resource of over 300 million tons that is currently only sold locally to a nearby beneficiation plant after trial supplies; management calls it small today but expects meaningful volumes and is studying building its own beneficiation plant within three to four quarters.
- **Bentonite & Allied Minerals and White Performance Minerals** — The core India vertical, mining and processing bentonite (700,000 tons capacity, largest in India and third largest globally) and kaolin into industrial products for foundries, detergents, animal feed, oil and gas and construction; together with the white minerals it generated about INR 998 crores of revenue and INR 112 crores of EBITDA in FY26, and is the main target of a planned INR 150 crores of India capex.
- **Specialty Adsorbent Solutions (Ashapura Perfoclay Limited, 50% JV)** — Makes bleaching clay — a bentonite derivative used to purify edible oils, turning a roughly $40/ton raw mineral into a $400/ton product — from what management calls the world's largest single-location plant at 200,000 tons; it contributed positively in FY26 but was hurt by a sharp rise in sulfuric acid, its key raw material.
- **Advanced Ceramic Materials (stake in Orient Ceratech Limited)** — Produces industrial ceramic materials including proppants from what the company says is the first plant of its kind in India; it is a smaller contributor but was the India segment's bright spot, delivering encouraging growth versus the prior year.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

The story here starts with one decision and everything else follows from it. Over nine to eleven years, Ashapura self-funded roughly $135 million to build a bauxite business in Guinea with no help from the state — three wholly owned ports with 16mt of capacity, 300km of heavy-haul road, and 700mt of concessions — and came out the only non-Chinese company with established standing there. The quality framework rewards this twice, scoring the company well for being independent, long-term and tenacious, and for growth capex that actually converted into output: exports went from 3.5mt in FY25 to about 8mt in FY26, with a 15mt target for FY27-28, alongside record FY26 EBITDA. That same build is what the multibagger screen picks up as a partial fit on Market Share Gainers and Cost to Replicate. Underneath it sits an unusually comfortable funding position: cash comes back from customers 22 days before suppliers have to be paid, so the business is partly financed by its own customers rather than by lenders — the single highest-scoring item in the whole framework, and the reason capital allocation and management both score well.

The problem is that the exact same trait that creates the strengths also creates the risks. Guinea is about 76-80% of both revenue and profit, it is priced off an index rather than negotiated, and management confirms the bauxite goes almost all to China. So the growth is entirely volume-led, and volume alone cannot protect the profit: realisation fell from roughly $70 to $60 CIF, EBITDA per tonne collapsed from $10.5 in Q3 FY26 to $5.9 in Q4, and group margin dropped from about 14% to about 7% in Q4 despite the record volumes. This is why price, mix and volume score badly and why the industry structure area is negative — Ashapura ships 8mt into a 200mt-plus export market where Chinese majors ship 70-80mt each, so it has no say over price and no supply discipline to lean on. The multibagger screen reaches the same conclusion from the other direction: Pricing Power is a clear no-fit, and so is Toll Roads, because bauxite is a large negotiated input to the customer's cost base, not a small critical ingredient. The risk review then flags cyclicality and government dependency as high, and customer/geography concentration as elevated — all three are simply the Guinea bet described in different words. Mining, logistics and transshipment are outsourced to Chinese partners as well, so the concentration runs through the cost side too.

The counterweight is the smaller Indian business, roughly 24% of revenue, and it is genuinely different in character. A 12-acre research centre with 75 scientists has produced named, commercialised products — organoclay, proppants, detergent-grade and animal-feed bentonite — with a pipeline into paints and foundries, and ₹150 crores of India capex committed to more value-added products. This is the one place the screen finds something close to a real edge, rating Innovation Dominance a likely fit, and it is also the stated silver lining against the 'good enough goods' risk, since it exports to 80-plus countries across diversified end uses rather than into one market. But it is small relative to the commodity, no R&D spend is disclosed, and the brand claim rests on being the cheaper China-Plus-One alternative rather than on premium pricing. That is why the combined rating lands where it does — 41 out of 100, a Mixed two-star verdict, built from 57 on business quality, only 18 on multibagger fit, and 38 on risk safety. Resilience is judged sound, and management is straightforward about its own misses, but returns on capital and customer benefits both score at zero, and growth has averaged only about 10% a year in a lumpy pattern. In plain terms: a well-run, hard-won, cash-efficient business that has bought itself a real position in a market that will not let it keep the profit from that position.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 2 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Roughly 76-80% of revenue is index-priced Guinean bauxite sold on spot-linked contracts; within three quarters realisation fell from ~$70/t to ~$60/t and EBITDA/t collapsed from $10.5 to $5.9, with FY26 group margin dropping from ~14% to ~7% in Q4 despite record volumes, on top of ~$135m of front-loaded infrastructure capex.

> "our long-term contracts' volumes may be fixed, but the prices are depending on the index conditions... there are no fixed prices going into multiple years" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 4 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from -14% to 15% (swinging ±8.3 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
