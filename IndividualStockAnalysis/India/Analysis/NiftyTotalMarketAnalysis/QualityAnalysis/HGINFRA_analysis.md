# HGIEL (HGINFRA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

H.G. Infra Engineering Limited (HGIEL / HGINFRA) is an Indian construction and infrastructure company, in business for over 23 years, that builds highways, expressways, railway lines and stations, metro works, and more recently solar plants, battery storage and power transmission lines. Its customers are mainly government bodies — NHAI, the Ministry of Road Transport, Indian Railways, DMRC, MSRDC, state DISCOMs, GUVNL/NVVN — plus large private developers such as the Adani Group, IRB, Welspun and DLF. It makes money in two ways: as a contractor paid a fixed price to build a project (EPC), and by putting in its own equity to build and hold projects for a period under HAM (Hybrid Annuity Model) or as power/storage assets, later selling those completed assets to infrastructure funds to recycle capital. Work is spread across many Indian states — Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Haryana, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Jharkhand and Delhi — and the size of the business is tracked by an order book that ran from about ₹11,508 crore (June 2022) to a peak of ₹15,642 crore (June 2024) and ₹10,147 crore (March 2026), with annual revenue guided at around ₹5,000 crore in FY23 and quarterly revenue of roughly ₹1,500 crore by FY25.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Roads & Highways — EPC** — Builds highways and access-controlled expressways for a fixed contract price, with no ownership stake — examples include the Ganga Expressway for Adani (a single ₹4,971 crore order, now 100% complete and handed over), Delhi UER-2, Delhi-Vadodara packages, Nelamangala-Tumkur and the ₹3,940 crore Pune-Shirur job from Welspun; this is the company's oldest and biggest activity, and together with HAM roads it made up 53%-64% of the order book in FY26 and virtually all of it in FY23.
- **Roads & Highways — HAM (Hybrid Annuity Model)** — Builds road projects through wholly-owned project companies (SPVs) into which HGIEL invests its own equity and takes bank project loans, then gets paid over time by the government — projects include Raipur-Visakhapatnam OD-5/OD-6/AP-1, Khammam-Devarapalle 1 and 2, Karnal Ring Road, Chennai-Tirupati, Varanasi-Kolkata and Narol-Sarkhej; management said HAM was 36% of the order book in FY23 and 28% in FY25, with total equity commitments of about ₹1,137 crore (FY23 estimate) rising to ₹1,461 crore across 10 HAM projects by FY25.
- **HAM asset monetization** — Sells completed road SPVs to infrastructure funds to get its invested equity back and fund new work — three projects (Gurgaon-Sohna, Rewari-Ateli, Ateli-Narnaul) were sold to Highway Infrastructure Trust in November 2023 for about ₹315 crore received plus ₹60 crore pending, Rewari Bypass was expected to bring roughly ₹130 crore against ₹75.7 crore of equity invested, and a further five HAM subsidiaries were being sold to Neo Infra Income Opportunities Fund under agreements signed in 2025.
- **Railways & Metro** — Builds railway lines, station redevelopment and metro civil works — the DMRC metro project, Bilaspur (Himachal Pradesh) railway, Kanpur and New Delhi railway stations, Dhule-Nardana, Karanjaon, Gaya-Son Nagar, Thane Metro and two Adani thermal-plant rail projects at Anuppur (₹340 crore) and Mirzapur (₹440 crore); this segment was started as a diversification, grew to about ₹2,498 crore of order book in FY25 and ₹2,825 crore (27% of the total) by March 2026 across around 10 ongoing projects.
- **Solar energy** — Builds and owns solar power plants — around 700 MW DC of KUSUM-C scheme projects worth about ₹1,763 crore of EPC value, of which 350+ MW was executed by December 2025 and physical progress was 96.3% by March 2026, delayed by heavy Rajasthan monsoons and transmission-line land problems; HGIEL had put in ₹731 crore of its own equity by December 2025 and sells the power to state DISCOMs, though only ₹72.88 crore had been billed and ₹56.33 crore collected at that point.
- **BESS (Battery Energy Storage Systems)** — Develops and will own large battery storage projects under binding agreements with GUVNL and NVVN — three projects totalling 735 MW / 1,470 MWh at sites including Banaskantha and Dholera, worth about ₹1,620 crore of order book in December 2025 and ₹1,527 crore in March 2026, which management expects to generate roughly ₹225 crore of annual revenue once all are commissioned.
- **Power transmission** — A newly entered line building transmission infrastructure, with two projects backed in Uttar Pradesh and Jharkhand worth roughly ₹1,200 crore combined; it is still small, showing about ₹318 crore in the March 2026 order book with SPVs formed and long-lead equipment ordered.
- **New areas being evaluated** — Management said it is actively looking at tunnels, water infrastructure, buildings and other specialised EPC work for future diversification, but the calls report no order book or revenue from these yet.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Every check, area by area

**Capital Allocation — Neutral (+0.00)** · 3 of 6 checks had evidence

- **Cost of working capital — Excellent** *(from the financial statements)*: Cash comes back 171 days BEFORE suppliers need to be paid — customers effectively fund the business (negative working capital, a rare strength).
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance) — Weak** *(from the conference calls)*: Equipment capex is small and largely replacement-driven (Rs 75-135 cr/yr, with old assets phased out and sold), while the genuinely large capital deployment went into HAM/solar/BESS SPVs (Rs 1,903 cr HAM equity, Rs 851 cr solar equity) whose returns have not materialised — solar commissioning slipped to FY27 and consolidated PAT margin fell from 10% to 6.3%.
  > "it is only Rs. 15 odd crore which has been added in Q1. But not much during the entire year, we are expecting that Rs. 75 odd crores would be the total number" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Merger and acquisition — Weak** *(from the conference calls)*: The only acquisition-like move is the MoU to take over 116 MW of KUSUM solar projects from Ultravibrant, which fed into a solar portfolio that has been chronically delayed and equity-hungry (commissioning deadline pushed to March 2027, equity at Rs 851 cr including a Rs 99 cr forced loan repayment), so post-deal margins worsened rather than improved.
  > "we have entered into MoU with Ultravibrant Solar Energy Private Limited for take over and development of 116-Megawatt DC of KUSUM solar projects" — *management, on an earnings call*

*"Owns the shop, not just the product"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and every one of the 2 computable checks supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** The company does move down the value chain into asset ownership — HAM SPVs, KUSUM-C solar IPP (700MW), BESS (735MW/1,470MWh) and 35-year transmission assets — but it recycles rather than retains them, monetizing HAM SPVs to Highway Infrastructure Trust and Neo Infra, so this is capital recycling, not customer-facing forward integration.

> "we successfully transferred 100% of shareholding of KD -2 and 49% of KD -1" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Operating margin moved from about 12% (early years) to 20% (recent years) — the widening-profitability signature of pricing power / lock-in.
