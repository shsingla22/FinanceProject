# Akums Drugs & Pharmaceuticals (AKUMS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Akums is an Indian pharmaceutical manufacturer whose main business is making finished medicines on contract for other drug companies — it is described on the calls as the largest India-focused pharma CDMO (contract development and manufacturing organisation) with over 30% market share, serving 1,500+ clients a year including 26 of the top 30 pharma companies in India. It makes tablets, capsules, liquid orals, injectables, creams, eye drops, nasal sprays and 60+ other dosage forms across 11-plus formulation plants (49 billion units of annual capacity), and charges clients on a cost-plus basis, so its revenue moves with raw-material (API) prices as well as volumes. Alongside this it sells its own branded medicines in India through Akumentis and in about 65 overseas markets through Unosource, sells cheaper unbranded 'trade generic' medicines through distributors, and manufactures APIs (bulk drug ingredients, mostly cephalosporins). Group revenue was about Rs 4,359 crore in FY26 with adjusted EBITDA of Rs 522 crore, and it is extending the contract-manufacturing model abroad via a European supply contract (for which it received €100 million upfront) and a Zambia partnership.

**The parts of the business:**

- **CDMO (contract development and manufacturing)** — Develops and manufactures finished formulations for other pharma companies on a cost-plus basis; by far the biggest part of the group at roughly three-fourths of revenue historically and Rs 3,485 crore of FY26 revenue with Rs 467 crore EBITDA, and it is the segment being extended into Europe and Zambia.
- **Domestic branded formulation (Akumentis)** — Sells Akums' own branded medicines in India through a 1,500-plus field force across 140-plus brands, focused on gynaecology, cardio-diabetics, orthopaedics and paediatrics with about 70% of sales in chronic and sub-chronic therapies; small but profitable at Rs 446 crore revenue and Rs 90 crore EBITDA in FY26.
- **International branded formulation / exports (Unosource)** — Sells branded and generic formulations in around 65 export markets across CNS, anti-infectives, analgesics and gynaecology, backed by 700-plus product dossiers; a small segment (about Rs 50 crore of revenue in Q3 FY26) that management calls a focus area for growth.
- **Trade generics** — Sells unbranded generic medicines through distributors in India; the smallest segment (about Rs 25 crore a quarter) and loss-making, which management has been deliberately shrinking, with losses narrowing and the segment turning EBITDA-positive by Q4 FY26.
- **API (active pharmaceutical ingredients)** — Manufactures the bulk drug ingredients that go into medicines — 737 MT of capacity with roughly 85% from cephalosporins plus respiratory, CNS and anti-diabetic APIs — contributing over 5% of revenue (about Rs 54 crore in Q3 FY26) and still loss-making because of falling cephalosporin prices, though losses are being cut through cost and portfolio actions.

### The story in depth

Akums earns a Decent rating of 64 out of 100 — the blend of 63 for business quality, 48 for multibagger pattern fit and 84 for risk safety — and the story behind that score is a company that is very good at the thing it does but has chosen a business model that caps what winning is worth. The clearest strengths are research spending that actually produces output (over ₹100 crore a year for three straight years, about 2.6% of sales, four centres and 400 scientists, converting into 1,000 cumulative DCGI approvals, with 27 approvals in a single quarter against 31 in the whole prior year and 24 patents filed in one quarter) and a strong cash return on capital, with ₹34 of operating cash generated this year for every ₹100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings. Those two feed directly into the one pattern rated a STRONG FIT — Market Share Gainers — where volumes grew 14% against a flattish industry in Q1FY25 and at double-digit rates in the second half of FY26 while the Indian pharma market grew 1–1.5%, all on a base that already holds over 30% of India's contract manufacturing and serves 26 of the country's top 30 pharma companies. Innovation Dominance is rated a LIKELY FIT on the same evidence.

The trait that wins the volumes is also the trait that limits the profit from them. Akums manufactures under its customers' labels on a fixed cost-plus percentage of roughly 15%, and that single fact explains most of the weak spots at once. It is why two patterns are outright NO FIT: Toll Roads, because contract manufacturing is the customer's main product cost rather than a small sliver collected regardless of conditions, and Pricing Power, because realisation is a pass-through of raw material prices rather than a management decision. It is why the Pricing, Mix and Volume check scores negative — a roughly 20–25% fall in active ingredient prices flowed straight through into an average price variance of about –3.5%, leaving mix as the only positive lever. It is why Cyclicality is flagged WATCH, since that same pass-through held reported contract manufacturing revenue to 2–4% growth through FY26 despite the volume gains, with cephalosporin ingredients down more than 30%. And it is why the Good Enough Goods risk is flagged WATCH: the end consumer has no awareness of Akums, and the pharma client sitting in between controls the choice and keeps the brand economics. The company's own answer to this — its branded businesses — remains small and stalled, which is why Brand Strength, Friendly Middleman and Forward Integrators all come out only PARTIAL: the domestic branded arm is about 10% of revenue at ₹446 crore and grew just 2.9% in FY26 with growth explicitly deprioritised in favour of margin, and the international brand line was flat at ₹143 crore.

The risk review is the reason the overall score holds up: resilience is rated SOUND, four of the eight risk channels come out LOW (technology shift, new entrants, changing consumer preferences and fashion), and the four WATCH items are mostly the flip side of things already counted as strengths rather than separate threats. Regulation is the clearest example — tighter enforcement is credited with pushing volume toward compliant manufacturers like Akums and keeps New Entrants at LOW, yet the same dependence on regulators is why Government Dependency is WATCH, since DCGI approvals shape the product mix and EU GMP, ANVISA and Russia GMP clearances gate the entire international plan. Stakeholder Concentration is similar: the base is genuinely broad at 1,500-plus clients and 4,000-plus commercialised formulations, so the exposure sits in the growth story rather than today's revenue. The real soft spot is capital allocation, the weakest quality area alongside industry structure, and it is one specific decision — the Parabolic/Pure and Cure active-ingredient acquisition, a distressed asset that has lost roughly ₹40 crore of operating profit in FY23, ₹44 crore in FY25 and ₹40 crore in FY26 with a turnaround repeatedly promised and repeatedly missed. Against that sit genuine positives on the same axis: borrowings cut from ₹418 crore to ₹213 crore, ₹1,682 crore of net cash, dividends paid, and disclosure that improved from refusing a segment split in September 2024 to a five-segment breakout. Both cycles the company rides — ingredient prices and industry volumes — appear to be turning up off a trough, which is what would let the volume wins finally show up in reported numbers.

> "the largest India-focused pharmaceutical CDMO with over 30% market share" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Sales compounding at 15% while margins held or improved (8% → 9%) — growth that was WON, not bought with price cuts.

### Innovation Dominance — LIKELY FIT
*"Out-inventing the competition"*

## What to watch

- Active ingredient prices: the Cyclicality risk and the negative Pricing, Mix and Volume check both hinge on prices continuing to tick up off their trough after two years of erosion — that is what converts double-digit volume growth into more than 2–4% reported revenue growth.
- Parabolic/Pure and Cure: the –2 scoring Merger and acquisition check and the bottom-ranked Capital Allocation area rest on this one asset; an actual end to the ~₹40 crore annual operating losses would lift both.
- Europe and other export approvals: the Geographical expansion check, plus the Government Dependency and Stakeholder Concentration risks, all track the same milestones — the EUR 35 million a year contract running to 2032 with EUR 100 million received upfront, further dossier approvals, ANVISA and Russia GMP.
- Own-brand growth: Brand Strength, Friendly Middleman and the Good Enough Goods risk would all improve if the domestic branded arm moves beyond 2.9% growth on ₹446 crore and the international brand line beyond flat at ₹143 crore.
- New capacity ramping: the Capex for growth check notes slow ramp-ups at the injectable, Penem, Baddi and Jammu facilities, and the Low Price Plus pattern depends on utilisation lifting the 12–15% margin band.
