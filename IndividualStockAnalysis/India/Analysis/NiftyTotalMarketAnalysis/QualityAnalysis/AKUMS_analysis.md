# Akums Drugs & Pharmaceuticals (AKUMS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Akums is an Indian pharmaceutical manufacturer whose main business is contract development and manufacturing (CDMO): other pharma companies hire it to develop and make finished medicines — tablets, capsules, liquids, injections, creams, eye drops, nasal sprays, gummies and more — which those clients then sell under their own brands. Management calls it the largest India-focused CDMO with over 30% market share, serving 1,500+ clients a year including 26 of India's top 30 pharma companies, and it earns money largely on a cost-plus basis, so its billing rises and falls with raw-material (API) prices while profit tracks volumes and product mix. Alongside this it sells its own branded medicines in India (through Akumentis) and in about 65 export markets (through Unosource), sells unbranded trade generics through distributors, and makes bulk drug ingredients (APIs, mostly cephalosporins). Manufacturing and R&D are based in India (units including Baddi and a planned Jammu expansion, 11+ formulation plants, ~49 billion units of annual capacity, four R&D centres), with FY26 revenue of Rs 4,359 crore and a growing regulated-market push into Europe and a Zambia partnership.

**The parts of the business:**

- **CDMO (contract development and manufacturing)** — Develops and manufactures finished dosage medicines for other pharma companies to sell under their brands; by far the biggest piece — about three-fourths of revenue at listing and Rs 3,485 crore of FY26's Rs 4,359 crore, with Rs 467 crore of EBITDA, and the segment management treats as its core growth engine including new European supplies from FY28.
- **Domestic branded formulation (Akumentis)** — Akums' own branded medicines sold in India through a field force of 1,500+ reps across roughly 140 brands, focused on gynaecology, cardio-diabetics, orthopaedics and paediatrics with ~70% chronic/sub-chronic sales; roughly Rs 446 crore of FY26 revenue and Rs 90 crore EBITDA, so a small but profitable second leg.
- **International branded formulation / exports (Unosource)** — Sells Akums-branded generics in about 65 overseas countries across CNS, anti-infectives, analgesics and gynaecology, backed by 700+ product dossiers and regular new approvals; small in size (about Rs 50 crore in the December 2025 quarter) but described as a focus segment for the group.
- **Trade generics** — Unbranded generic medicines sold through distributors in India; a loss-making segment management has deliberately been shrinking for years (about Rs 25 crore of quarterly revenue, down 18% year-on-year), which finally turned EBITDA-positive in the March 2026 quarter and is expected to stay small and profit-focused.
- **API (active pharmaceutical ingredients)** — Makes bulk drug ingredients — around 85% cephalosporins, plus respiratory, CNS and anti-diabetic molecules — with 737 MT of capacity; contributes over 5% of revenue and has been loss-making throughout the period on falling cephalosporin prices, with management cutting losses through cost control and shifting toward higher-margin non-cephalosporin products and regulated markets.

### The story in depth

Akums is India's largest contract drug manufacturer, and its record reads as one of a company that is winning work rather than winning price. The quality framework's strongest marks sit on the customer side: the whole Customer Benefits area scores full marks, led by an "assurance" proposition — regulatory compliance, audit-passing quality systems and 15-plus years of meeting multinational clients' standards — and management credits its recent demand pickup directly to buyers moving toward manufacturers they trust to stay compliant. That same trait is what drives the one pattern the screen calls a STRONG FIT, Market Share Gainers: more than 30% of the contract-manufacturing market, 26 of India's top 30 pharma companies as clients, and volume running more than 25% ahead of an industry growing barely 1%-1.5%. Research spending backs it up rather than sitting idle — 1,000 cumulative regulatory approvals, 27 in a single quarter against 31 in the whole prior year, 24 patents filed in one quarter — which is why Innovation Dominance is judged a LIKELY FIT. And the money works: every ₹100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings in the business produced ₹34 of operating cash this year.

The catch is that the single feature that makes Akums the safe choice for its clients — cost-plus contract manufacturing — is also the source of most of what holds the record back, and it shows up on all three lists at once. Because prices are passed straight through, growth is volume-led with structurally negative pricing (the framework marks this down outright), which is exactly why the screen returns NO FIT on Pricing Power, and why Toll Roads, Cost to Replicate and Low Price Plus stall at PARTIAL despite genuinely high barriers: twenty years of approvals, 4,000 formulations, 11 plants, 24-month client audit cycles and 15-year relationships make customers slow to leave, but slow-to-leave customers still set the price. The risk review names the same thing from the other direction: Good Enough Goods is on WATCH because the client owns the brand and the choice, with margins capped around 13%-15% at the contract-manufacturing level, and Cyclicality is on WATCH because falling ingredient prices — down 7%-12%, then roughly 20%-25% across the year — mechanically cut revenue and held contract-manufacturing growth to 4%-5% even while volumes rose. The owned-brand businesses that might have escaped this do not: domestic branded grew 2.9%, below the industry, with better margins coming from overhead and receivables cleanup rather than premium pricing, so Brand Strength is a NO FIT and Friendly Middleman only PARTIAL on the roughly 10% of revenue it covers. Industry Structure is the weakest quality area on the board at 0.17.

What is left is a decent business at what may be a decent moment, with capital allocation the open question. The ingredient price cycle that hurt is now turning — prices up high single digits, the cephalosporin decline easing — which the framework logs as an identifiable upturn ahead, and the international build-out is being delivered milestone by milestone: EU manufacturing approval for two plants, a first European dossier approval, a EUR 35 million-a-year contract running to 2032 with EUR 100 million already received upfront, a $25 million Zambia order, Brazilian and Russian clearances. But the record also shows the cost of getting this wrong before: the Parabolic/Pure and Cure ingredients acquisition was a distressed asset that has drained profit for years — that division still lost ₹40 crore at the operating level this year — and management itself called it a miss. New capacity in injectables, Penem, Baddi and Jammu is ramping but its returns are still unproven, and the Capital Allocation area scores just 0.33. Two more WATCH flags connect directly to the growth story: the client base is genuinely broad at 1,500-plus, yet the *incremental* growth now leans on a handful of named contracts, and market access at every step is gated by regulators — the same strict enforcement that handed Akums its volume gains is a permission it does not control. Set against that, the balance sheet is conservative (debt cut from ₹418 crore to ₹213 crore, ₹1,682 crore of cash), disclosure has been candid including on bad news, and resilience is judged SOUND. The combined rating of 61 out of 100 — Decent, three stars — is built from solid business quality (64) and comfortable risk safety (72) pulled down by a weak multibagger fit (48), which is a fair description of a strong operator inside an economic model that caps what strength can earn.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

*"Winning customers from rivals"*

> "the largest India-focused pharmaceutical CDMO with over 30% market share" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Sales compounding at 15% while margins held or improved (8% → 9%) — growth that was WON, not bought with price cuts.

### Innovation Dominance — LIKELY FIT
*"Out-inventing the competition"*

**What the calls show:** Consistently disclosed and delivered across the period: INR100+ crore R&D each of the last three years, 400 scientists, cumulative 1,000 DCGI approvals (27 in Q1FY26 alone vs 31 in all FY25), 24 patents filed in one quarter, named launches (Trelagliptin, Relugolix, Rivaroxaban EU approval) and first commercialisation of triple-layer tablets — and management ties the niche-dosage mix directly to the gross margin improvement from ~36.6% to 37%+.

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 1 check supporting).

**What the calls show:** Scale-plus-low-margin economics are evident (largest CDMO, 49bn units, 12-13% group EBITDA on cost-plus pricing) and cost discipline is repeatedly itemised across FY25-FY26 — portfolio rationalisation, overhead control, SAP S/4HANA, yield improvement — but the cost culture is presented as loss-repair in API/trade generics rather than a hard-to-copy structural advantage passed to customers.

> "Our business is based on cost plus margin. API prices impact our realization from our clients." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹42 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 0.8× a year — not the high-turnover, low-margin machine this pattern needs.

### Forward Integrators — PARTIAL
*"Owns the shop, not just the product"*

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is mechanically tied to the API price cycle under a cost-plus model — API prices fell 7-12% in FY25, ~10-12% in FY26 Q1 and ~20-25% for the full year, holding CDMO revenue growth to 4-5% despite volume growth, then reversed upward on geopolitics in FY26 Q4; the API segment itself is a pure commodity (cephalosporin erosion >30%) and has run EBITDA-negative for eight straight quarters. Offsetting this, the core CDMO product is a flow/consumable (make-to-order medicines consumed in patient demand, not customer capex), percentage margins are contractually preserved through the cycle, and management kept investing (Baddi, Penem, injectables, >INR100 cr/yr R&D) through the weak H1 FY26.

> "The whole of the year, the API prices crashed by about 20%, 25% as a basket." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/consumable, make-to-order demand not tied to client capex; full cost pass-through preserves percent margins; countercyclical capex and R&D through the FY26 downturn*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 0 of the last 8 years with swings of ±17 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between -2% and 12% (±4.4 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
