**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.4 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing behaviour was rational with no discount war — prices held stable through three quarters of soft demand — and entry is gated by customer qualification and complex chemistry, though no direct evidence on new entrants is given. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±3.4 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Technical barriers exist, but pricing rationality clearly does not — realisations fell 30-35% on Chinese dumping and Aether was a price-taker in both directions, up and down.
- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Poor → Neutral. Now: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹0.36 of sales — a fairly asset-heavy business. And from the calls: The business is structurally capital-intensive — roughly INR160-180 crores of capex per production block for a targeted 1.75x asset turn only at maturity — so protection comes from customer lock-in rather than asset-lightness. | The long view had said: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹0.36 of sales — a fairly asset-heavy business. And from the calls: The business is structurally asset-heavy and slow-turning — five capital-intensive sites running at 49-76% utilisation with a 179-day working capital cycle producing only ~INR1,160cr of FY26 revenue on years of multi-hundred-crore capex.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Management frames India versus China/West at country level but gives no evidence of a stable few-player structure in its own niches, and large corporate customers like Baker Hughes and Milliken retain substantial negotiating power despite long contracts. | The long view had said: This is not an oligopoly — Chinese dumping set prices, customers renegotiate contract pricing every year, and Aether's own management describes competition with China as the reason it will not name molecules publicly.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: Large, clearly growth-oriented capex across Site 3++ and Site 5 with Rs 245 cr deployed in 9M FY26, plants commissioned on a customer-contracted basis and an explicit 1.75x asset-turn target at maturity, though incremental ROIC is still unproven as blocks only started commercial production. | The long view had said: Relentless, almost entirely growth-oriented greenfield capex (Sites 3, 3+, 3++, 4, 5 plus R&D/pilot expansions, INR3,838mn in FY26 and INR3,000-3,500mn guided for FY27), but incremental returns have lagged badly — Site 3 cost ~INR200cr for a promised INR400cr revenue yet sat at 49% utilisation, and Site 4's potential was cut from INR400cr to INR250cr before finally reaching INR220cr in FY26.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Switching costs are high and demonstrable: dedicated sites per customer, validation batches, and 50-plus customer certification audits plus customer pre-audits of Site 5 before a single kilo ships. | The long view had said: The CEM model creates real switching costs — customer qualification audits, validation batches and multi-year extendable contracts mean a customer cannot cheaply re-source, evidenced by 50+ certification audits and pre-audits for Site 5.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Rather than sitting near a cycle bottom, Q4 earnings are being flattered by a conflict-driven feedstock spike that management itself calls unprecedented, which is a peak-type condition of uncertain durability. | The long view had said: Management called the bottom in mid-2024 during the China dumping trough and the upturn has now materialised, with FY26 revenue +38%, EBITDA +53% and margins recovering from 11% (Q4FY24) to 31%, though the company enters FY27 nearer mid-cycle than trough.
- **Candor** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Disclosure is granular (segment splits, per-client sales, pricing) but commissioning guidance slipped repeatedly across the year — Site 5 Phase 1 moved from end-Q3 FY26 to start-Q4 to 'shortly' to end-May/June — without any explicit acknowledgement of the delay, alongside recurring unquantified promises of imminent large contracts. | The long view had said: Management discloses inconvenient facts plainly — sub-50% utilisation, a Site 4 potential cut from INR400cr to INR250cr, lost quarters from the fire, one-off insurance income inflating Q3FY26 — but tends to reframe slippage as timing rather than owning the repeated over-promising.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Explicit succession-building in the technical organisation, grooming next-generation R&D leaders via institute networks to staff the new facility, though this is stated only in the most recent call with no system-wide talent programme described. | The long view had said: Systematic and specific talent architecture — ESOPs across all levels, a stated 13-year retention focus, an explicit programme to groom next-generation R&D leaders using ICT Mumbai/NCL Pune links, and senior lateral hires (CTO Jim Ringer, Guenter Stevens).

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
