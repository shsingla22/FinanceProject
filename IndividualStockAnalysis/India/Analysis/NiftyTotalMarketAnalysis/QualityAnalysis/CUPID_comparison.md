### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 8 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is product extension — 16 DCGI-certified IVD kits now selling (INR0.9cr in Q2 FY24 vs INR0.2cr prior quarter) and first-mover status in female condoms — but no R&D spend is disclosed, and the move into polyisoprene/polyurethane condoms is to be bought via acquisition rather than developed in-house. (The long view had said: No R&D spend is ever disclosed and the one diversification — IVD kits — has serially missed its own targets (INR30–40cr guided in FY21, INR10cr guided for FY23, INR40 lakhs actually delivered, with a INR1.4cr loss), while product 'newness' amounts to flavour variants and bundling deodorant sprays.)

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: LIKELY FIT → NO FIT. Why now: Management has actively avoided brand building — the prior MD said domestic promotion would cost several crores for lower margins, and the new MD concedes B2C brand creation is expensive in a price-conscious market. Only 5-11% of sales are domestic and there is no A&P spend, premium pricing or distributor leverage evidenced. (The long view had said: There is a genuine 30-year heritage, own-brand selling (Cupid male/female condoms, Cupisure) and a first-mover claim in Indian female condoms, but brand equity is unmonetised: 89–94% of revenue is B2B export tender, A&P is under 10% of spend with no brand ambassador, and consumer reach was only ~1,000 outlets by Jan-2024.)
- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The new Halwasiya management is stating a forward-integration intent — building a B2C sales force in India and in export geographies, distribution presence, bundled product packs — but as of the Jan-24 call this is announced plan, not delivered: domestic remains ~11% of sales including IVD kits. (The long view had said: A deliberate forward move began only under the new Halwasiya management from Nov-2023 — own-brand B2C, zonal/area sales managers, website sales and ~1,000 retail outlets — but it is embryonic, unquantified and explicitly capped by unwillingness to spend, with no store count, franchise royalty or D2C revenue share to point to.)

*Unchanged: Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Market Share Gainers (NO FIT); Corporate Culture (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 6 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Condoms sold into public-health tenders are awarded largely on price against WHO-prequalified peers, and management concedes the Indian consumer market is intensely price-driven and that domestic margins are structurally lower — brand pull is weak, with the 'first female condom manufacturer' certification claim being the main differentiator management offers. (The long view had said: The product is not complex and is bought on price: domestic margins are 5-7% versus 15-20% on exports purely because low-cost manufacturers undercut, the export book itself is won through competitive government tenders, and new management concedes Indian consumers are 'very price conscious' while spending under 10% on promotion with no brand ambassador — brand awareness at the point of sale is low and the retailer/tender buyer controls the choice.)
- **New Entrants**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Management describes a supply-constrained industry where entry into the tender market requires a three-year manufacturing licence, WHO certification and CE certification — a multi-year regulatory moat that Cupid itself is still climbing on the IVD side. No structurally different competitor format is discussed defensively in any of the four calls. (The long view had said: Management concedes that four or five low-capacity domestic manufacturers price well below Cupid, capping Indian margins at 5-7% versus 15-20% on exports, and analysts raise both Mankind and new-format entrants like PeeSafe in the female-contraceptive space; the response is to build a Cupid B2C brand and distribution force rather than to defend an existing moat.)

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (the only stress check that could be computed is clean.) vs Sound on the one-year view (the only stress check that could be computed is clean.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
