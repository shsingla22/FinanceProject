### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 5 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: The four calls disclose only transaction-volume-driven revenue; there is no discussion of annual membership/AMC fees, attach rates, renewals, deferred revenue or customer advances as a funding source. (The long view had said: Revenue is per-unit transaction fees that recur automatically with an installed base of 9,000+ registered participants (all DISCOMs, all merchant RE generators), and members pre-fund trades with advance payment/margin money — a negative-working-capital model — but management never discusses AMC/licence attach rates, renewal rates or deferred revenue as a funding source.)

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management leans on an 18-year trust/relationship record with counterparties as the reason share should survive coupling, but there is no A&P spend, premium pricing or adjacency-launch evidence in these calls — it is institutional reputation, not consumer brand. (The long view had said: Management leans on 18 years of trust and customer loyalty as the reason share will survive market coupling, and the IEX name has been stretched into IGX, ICX and now Indian Coal Exchange — but there is no A&P spend, heritage/provenance story or premium pricing to go with it.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Exchange volumes grew 16.1%, 12% and 14.3% (9M FY26) while national electricity demand was flat, so IEX is gaining share of the total power pool from bilateral/PPA routes; but no share-versus-competitor numbers are given and the Jul 2026 call frames share as something to defend under market coupling rather than to grow. (The long view had said: Exchange share of national consumption roughly doubled (from ~4–5% to over 8% of India's electricity by FY26) and IEX's own volumes compounded, but its share of the exchange market has been flat-to-slightly-down across the four years (85% in Q1 FY23 vs '80-85%' in Jul 2026), so this is share defence plus category growth rather than persistent share gain.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (QUANT SIGNAL); Forward Integrators (QUANT SIGNAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Toll Roads (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The threat to IEX in these calls is regulatory, not technological; on technology the record is steady small-scale, value-adding innovation — customer API integration for bidding and back office, data analytics, readiness for a 5-minute RTM auction window, carbon-credit and CfD products — with no competitor platform or format shift described defensively. (The long view had said: The threat is not a rival technology but a mandated architecture change: market coupling moves the matching/price-discovery engine — the exchange's core infrastructure — to Grid India, reducing exchanges toward order-collection utilities. Alongside this, the market keeps re-granularising (1 hour → 15 min → proposed 5-min RTM with gate closure cut from 75 to 50 minutes), which IEX has so far led rather than followed.)

**That worsened:**

- **New Entrants**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Market coupling would pool order books across all power exchanges and set one uniform price, structurally resetting the economics that gave IEX its liquidity moat and letting rival exchanges compete on equal terms; management repeatedly addresses this defensively across all four calls and has pursued appeal and 'legal recourse'. (The long view had said: HPX entered in July 2022 alongside PXIL and both used promotional/fee incentives in TAM and REC, with analysts pressing on whether DAM/RTM share could be chipped away; the durable answer has been ~80-85% share held for 18 years and 9,000+ registered participants. The structurally different entrant is market coupling itself, which would neutralise the liquidity moat — management concedes a possible 20-40% DAM impact.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Cyclicality (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 12 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
