# Sharda Cropchem (SHARDACROP) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Sharda Cropchem sells crop-protection chemicals — herbicides, insecticides, fungicides and biocides — but does not manufacture them; it buys the products from third-party suppliers (sourcing is in dollars, with China named as a supply source) and markets and distributes them to a wide global customer base. Its core asset is regulatory paperwork: it prepares detailed product dossiers and obtains product registrations in its own name in each country, which is what lets it sell there and keeps competitors out; registrations stood at 3,011 as on 31 March 2026 with about 1,004 more applications pending, and it spends roughly INR 450-500 crores a year of capex on building this registration base. Money is made on the gap between the sourcing cost and the selling price — gross margin was 35.9% in FY26 and EBITDA margin 19.7%, on revenue of INR 5,268 crores. Its main markets are Europe, NAFTA (including the US) and Latin America; sales are largely in euros while purchases are in dollars, so the euro-dollar rate moves reported results.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Agrochemical business** — The core business — marketing and distributing herbicides, insecticides, fungicides and biocides worldwide off its own product registrations; it is the overwhelming majority of the company at INR 4,717 crores of FY26 revenue (about 90% of the total) and grew 25% year-on-year.
- **Non-agrochemical business** — A much smaller side business the management discusses only in passing (in one call described as competing against local production in the US market); it contributed INR 551 crores in FY26, roughly 10% of revenue, and was broadly flat year-on-year at +1%.

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across all five calls the business is described in classic commodity-cycle terms: a 2023-24 glut of Chinese production crushed prices (some products trading at 25% of prior levels) and caused the FY24 collapse (PAT INR32cr) and a 2022 inventory write-off, followed by a violent recovery (FY25 PAT +854%, FY26 PAT +124%, Q4FY26 EBITDA margin 24.8% vs 15.8% FY25). Realisation, not volume, drives the swing — FY26 full-year price/mix was -1.8% with FX +10.3% — so current peak-looking margins are cycle- and currency-flattered rather than structurally earned.

> "there have been very excessive production in China in the year 2024... Many of the products today are being traded at a price of 25% of what it was being traded in the previous years" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Crop-protection chemicals are consumed in the farmer's annual operating season (a flow/consumable product, not customer capex), and the company stayed net-debt-free with INR400-500cr p.a. registration capex through the downturn*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 11 years with swings of ±18 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 10% and 23% (±3.7 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 9% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 12%).
