# IRB Infrastructure Developers Ltd. (IRB) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSTRUCTION*

IRB Infrastructure Developers is an Indian road company that builds highways and then charges vehicles a toll to use them. Its customer on the building side is the government — mainly the National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) and state authorities — which awards it road contracts under BOT (build-operate-toll), TOT (pay upfront for an existing road and collect its toll) and HAM models; on the revenue side its real customers are the millions of motorists and truckers who pay at its toll plazas on corridors such as Mumbai–Pune, Ahmedabad–Vadodara, the Hyderabad Outer Ring Road and the Ganga Expressway. Money comes in three ways: daily toll collections (which rise with traffic and with annual tariff hikes linked to wholesale inflation — e.g. an 18% hike on Mumbai–Pune from April 2023), construction (EPC) and long-term operations-and-maintenance fees billed to its own road projects and its two InvIT trusts, and distributions plus fees from its stakes in those trusts. It also recycles capital by selling mature roads into its listed trusts — management calls this the B.E.S.T. strategy (Build, Execute, Stabilise, Transfer) — which released about Rs. 4,900 crores of equity in FY26 and helped grow the asset base from Rs. 80,000 crores to Rs. 94,000 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **BOT segment (toll roads owned by IRB)** — Highways IRB built or upgraded and now tolls directly on its own balance sheet, including the flagship Mumbai–Pune and Ahmedabad–Vadodara corridors; it brought in Rs. 712 crores of income in Q4 FY26, up 11% year on year, and is the steady cash-generating core of the company.
- **Construction segment (EPC)** — The in-house road-building arm that constructs and widens highways — both for IRB's own projects and for the InvIT-owned ones — and is the largest single revenue line (Rs. 815 crores in Q4 FY26, though down 32% year on year), with margins guided at roughly 18–20% and an EPC order book that has shrunk to about Rs. 2,100 crores.
- **InvIT & related assets segment** — IRB's income from its two infrastructure investment trusts — a Private InvIT (IRB Infrastructure Trust, 51%-owned, with GIC affiliates as partners) and a Public InvIT (~17% effective holding) — through distributions and related fees; it is the fastest-growing line, up 31% to Rs. 401 crores in Q4 FY26, with the Private InvIT alone collecting about Rs. 11.79 crores of toll per day.
- **O&M (operations and maintenance) order book** — Multi-decade contracts to maintain and run the highways held inside the InvITs, which give IRB very long-dated, visible revenue — the O&M book was roughly Rs. 33,600 crores as of August 2025 and makes up the bulk of the ~Rs. 45,000 crore total order book reported in May 2026.
- **TOT projects (toll-operate-transfer)** — Existing government highways IRB buys the right to toll for a long period by paying a large sum upfront — such as the Hyderabad Outer Ring Road bought for Rs. 7,380 crores over 30 years — and management says this, rather than new BOT construction, is now the main route for growing the portfolio toward a targeted Rs. 1,40,000 crore asset base.
- **HAM projects (hybrid annuity)** — Roads built for the government where the authority pays IRB in instalments rather than IRB collecting toll; management describes these as lower-margin than BOT work, and one HAM asset was sold to the Public InvIT in FY26 for about Rs. 500 crores of equity value.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

### Cyclicality — WATCH

*Silver lining: Toll collection is operations-linked flow revenue with 20-30 year concessions, contracted tariff escalation and long-dated O&M order book (~Rs. 33,600 crore), which grew steadily (Private InvIT +30% year-on-year in Q4FY26) right through the EPC downturn*
