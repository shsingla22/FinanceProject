# DCM Shriram Ltd. (DCMSHRIRAM) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSUMER GOODS*

DCM Shriram is an Indian diversified manufacturer that makes and sells industrial chemicals, plastics, sugar and ethanol, farm inputs, and branded windows and doors. Its customers are mostly other businesses — alumina, paper, textile, detergent and pharma makers who buy caustic soda; PVC and plastics processors; oil marketing companies that buy its ethanol under India's fuel-blending programme; and Indian farmers who buy its seeds, crop-protection products, specialty nutrients and subsidised urea — plus retail homeowners and real-estate projects that buy Fenesta windows. It makes money by processing raw materials (salt and power into caustic soda and chlorine, sugarcane into sugar and ethanol, natural gas into urea) and selling at prevailing commodity prices, so earnings swing with caustic/chlorine 'ECU' realisations, PVC prices, sugarcane costs and government policy on sugar, ethanol and import duties. Operations are entirely India-based — chemical complexes at Kota and Bharuch, sugar and distillery units in Uttar Pradesh (Ajbapur, Loni), window fabrication plants including Hyderabad and Bhubaneswar — with a small overseas seed business and some exports; quarterly net revenue was around Rs 2,700-2,900 crore in FY23-FY25 rising to Rs 3,811 crore in Q3 FY26.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Chemicals (Chlor-Alkali)** — Makes caustic soda and chlorine plus downstream products — hydrogen peroxide, epichlorohydrin (commissioned Oct 2025), aluminium chloride and calcium chloride — and management calls it a core growth engine, with revenue up 30% year-on-year in Q3 FY26 and the bulk of the Rs 3,500 crore capex programme (new 850 TPD caustic capacity, 120 MW captive power, green power projects) directed here.
- **Vinyl (PVC and carbide)** — Produces PVC resin and calcium carbide sold into agriculture and construction end-uses; it is the most volatile part, repeatedly squeezed by cheap Chinese imports and 'suboptimal' at low PVC prices — revenue fell 53% year-on-year in one quarter (Q2 FY23) — and is reported together with Chemicals as the combined 'Chloro-Vinyl' segment, the company's largest revenue driver.
- **Sugar and Ethanol** — Crushes sugarcane at its Uttar Pradesh mills (473 lakh quintals in season 2025-26 at 10.8% recovery) into sugar sold domestically and exported, and distils ethanol sold to oil marketing companies for petrol blending; roughly comparable in revenue to chemicals (Rs 617 crore in Q2 FY23) but margins have been under sustained pressure because state-set cane prices rise faster than sugar and ethanol realisations.
- **Fenesta Building Systems** — The consumer-facing arm that makes and installs branded UPVC and aluminium windows and doors, and has moved into glass façades, selling to homeowners and real-estate projects; management describes it as a steady growth business expanding geographies and product range, with an aluminium extrusion plant at Kota being commissioned.
- **Shriram Farm Solutions (Agri-Inputs)** — Sells seeds, crop-protection chemicals and specialty plant nutrition to Indian farmers, backed by an R&D centre in Ludhiana and tie-ups with global technology partners; it is a consistent double-digit growth contributor, with research wheat seed described as a market-leadership product.
- **Fertilisers (Urea)** — Runs a natural-gas-based urea plant whose economics are set by government subsidy and gas costs; management describes it as a stable, low-drama business where the focus is energy efficiency and cost control, though subsidy receivables ballooned when gas prices spiked in FY23.
- **Bioseed** — Breeds and sells hybrid seeds for crops such as corn, paddy, wheat and mustard in India and in international markets; it is the smallest and weakest agri unit — management said in 2022 it should 'turn around over a period of next 2 years' — and its sales track farmer income and monsoon conditions closely.
- **Advanced materials / Shriram Polytech and Hindusthan Speciality Chemicals** — Subsidiary-level businesses extending the chemicals chain — Hindusthan Speciality Chemicals in epoxy resins (helped by anti-dumping duty from Nov 2025) and Shriram Polytech in plastic compounding, in which the company sold a 50% stake to form a joint venture with Teknor Apex in FY26; described as a growing 'glycerine to ECH to epoxy' value chain rather than a large current earner.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

DCM Shriram comes out of these three reviews as a competently run, genuinely growing company that happens to sell mostly things it cannot price. Sales have compounded at roughly 8% a year with unusually steady year-to-year growth, the family leadership team has been on every earnings call from 2022 to 2026, net debt is modest at Rs 1,767 crore against Rs 13,538 crore of revenue, and dividends have been raised right through a heavy building programme — from 230% interim in FY23 to 560% (Rs 174.66 crore) for FY26. Alongside that, a large multi-year capital programme has been consistently funded: about Rs 3,500 crore in chemicals plus new ECH, hydrogen peroxide, epoxy, aluminium extrusion and green power lines, with fresh board approvals of Rs 217 crore and Rs 101 crore. The bright spots are the two consumer-facing arms: Fenesta grew 28% to cross Rs 1,112 crore with its order book up 15%, and research wheat set record sales while strengthening its lead. Innovation is real and regular too — 5 new products in Q2FY23, 6 in Q1FY25 and 13 in FY26 of which four came from in-house R&D. This is why the quality scorecard gives middling-positive marks to management, capital allocation and growth.

The trouble is that almost none of this converts into the ability to charge more, and the same traits that create the few positives also create the main weaknesses. Growth is overwhelmingly volume- and capacity-led while price and mix pull the other way: ECUs down 4%, PVC prices hit by Chinese dumping, ethanol realisations down 15% on mix, and Fenesta's own margins falling on adverse mix. That is exactly why the multibagger screen rates Pricing Power, Toll Roads and Cost to Replicate as outright no-fits, and why the quality framework scores Competitive Advantage and Return on Capital at zero. The cost obsession that makes the Low Price Plus pattern a partial fit — 120MW captive power, 44MW then 68MW of renewables, the power savings that kept Vinyl profitable in Q3FY26 — is also the tell that the company competes on the cost curve rather than on differentiation, and it is the same reason the New Entrants risk stays on the watch list as Indian chlor-alkali capacity crosses six million tonnes and Bangladeshi hydrogen peroxide floods an already oversupplied market. Fenesta shows the same double edge: the rising advertising and promotion spend that earns a positive mark on the brand check, and that supports the Brand Strength and Forward Integrators patterns, is currently depressing margin and has not bought premium pricing — which is precisely the evidence the Good Enough Goods risk cites when it flags margin compression at the one branded franchise the group owns.

The risk review then adds the ingredient that ties the whole picture together: policy sets the economics of nearly every segment. Urea realisation and its Rs 189 crore of outstanding subsidy, cane cost fixed by the UP government's SAP, sugar export quotas, and ethanol volumes rationed by oil marketing company allocation (sugarcane-based share cut from 34% to 28%) make Government Dependency the single high risk, and it is the same policy cap that holds back the otherwise-positive structural growth check. Ethanol also concentrates a large distillery investment on effectively one buyer group, which is why Stakeholder Concentration sits on the watch list. Layered on top is genuine cyclicality — Vinyl segment profit swinging from +Rs 156 crore to -Rs 10 crore to +Rs 44 crore across three quarters — while about 128 days of cash stays tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, so the working capital cycle is long at the very moment the capital programme is heaviest. The offsets are honest but partial: Fenesta, Shriram Farm Solutions and Bioseed dilute the policy and commodity exposure without removing it, and technology disruption and fashion risk are genuinely low because the products are functional, repeat-purchase staples. Netting it out, business quality scores 55, multibagger fit 33 and risk safety 44, giving 46 out of 100 — a Mixed, two-star verdict with resilience judged strained: a well-managed, cash-generating spender in an industry structure that scores negative, where the returns depend on prices the company does not set.

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*
