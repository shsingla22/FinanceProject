# Lumax Auto Tech (LUMAXTECH) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Lumax Auto Technologies is an Indian auto-parts maker (a "Tier 1" supplier, which it now wants to grow into a "Tier 0.5 system integrator") that designs and manufactures components and complete sub-systems fitted into vehicles built by others — passenger cars, two- and three-wheelers, and commercial vehicles. Its customers are the vehicle makers themselves, named on the calls as Maruti Suzuki, Mahindra, Tata Motors, Volkswagen, Toyota, Honda Motorcycle, TVS, Bajaj, Volvo Eicher and Daimler India, plus a separate aftermarket (replacement-parts) business sold to consumers/workshops and some exports. It makes money by winning nominations to supply parts for specific vehicle models and then selling them over the model's life, so revenue tracks Indian vehicle production plus the rising value of content it puts in each vehicle (premiumisation); it reports an order book — INR 1,450 crore as of FY26 — as forward visibility. Much of the group is run through joint ventures and subsidiaries with foreign technology partners (Japanese, Italian, German, Israeli), and operations are in India, with plants including Bengaluru and Bangalore facilities. Scale grew from INR 422 crore of quarterly revenue in Q1 FY23 to a full-year FY26 record of INR 4,870 crore revenue, INR 705 crore EBITDA (14.5% margin) and INR 337 crore PAT.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Standalone entity (Integrated Plastic Modules, aftermarket, chassis & swing arm for 2-wheelers, trailing arm for 3-wheelers, 2-wheeler lighting)** — The parent company's own factories making plastic modules, metal frames/chassis for two- and three-wheelers and two-wheeler lamps, plus the replacement-parts business — the single biggest piece, at 70% of consolidated revenue in Q1 FY23 and still 47% in FY24 (with plastic modules ~21%, aftermarket ~19%, chassis ~15% and lighting ~10% of group revenue in Q1 FY23).
- **IAC India (75% subsidiary, acquired; being merged into the parent)** — A Tier 1 car-interiors supplier (consoles, door and interior trim) to Mahindra, Maruti Suzuki, Volkswagen India and Volvo Eicher, added by acquisition and immediately the second-largest business at 31% of FY24 consolidated revenue; it drove the 53% revenue jump in FY24 and has since been folded into the parent's standalone accounts.
- **Lumax Mannoh Allied Technologies (55% subsidiary)** — Makes manual, AMT and automatic gear-shifter systems for cars, where management says it is the market leader, contributing 15% of consolidated revenue in Q1 FY23 and 12% in FY24, and also exporting automatic shifters for a global platform.
- **Lumax Cornaglia Auto Technologies (50% subsidiary)** — Makes air intake systems and urea (emission-control) tanks with 100% share of business at Volkswagen and Tata Motors, a smaller but steady 7% of revenue in Q1 FY23 and 6% in FY24.
- **Lumax Alps Alpine India (50% subsidiary)** — Makes automotive electronic devices, components and related software — the smallest reported piece at 2% of revenue in Q1 FY23 and 1% in FY24, with an INR 110 crore order book and an award from Maruti Suzuki for local design and development.
- **Lumax Metallics / Lumax Ancillary (100% subsidiaries, since merged into the parent)** — Metal seat frames and related ancillary manufacturing, about 6% of consolidated revenue in Q1 FY23, both later merged into the parent company to simplify the structure.
- **Lumax Ituran Telematics (50% subsidiary)** — Supplies vehicle telematics parts, described as small but newly commercial after starting supplies to Daimler India in FY24, with revenue expected to grow sharply thereafter.
- **Greenfuel Energy Solutions** — The alternate-fuels business (CNG-related parts such as ferrule-less tubes and fittings, supplied for Maruti Suzuki's Celerio CNG), acquired and merged into a group SPV in FY26 and named by management as one of the growth engines and order-book verticals.
- **Aftermarket business (including the Bluechem Group partnership)** — Sells replacement parts and, via a tie-up with Germany's Bluechem Group, car-care products in India; roughly 19% of revenue in Q1 FY23 and repeatedly cited in FY26 as a strong momentum driver, with its own business head on the calls.
- **Lumax JOPP Allied Technologies (50% JV) and Lumax FAE Technologies** — Two smaller joint ventures being restructured in FY26 — the board approved selling the entire 50% JOPP stake back to the German partner and buying out the remaining 15.97% of Lumax FAE to make it wholly owned.
- **Product verticals as reported today: Advanced Plastics, Mechatronics, Alternate Fuels, Structures & Control Systems** — The four groupings management now uses to describe the order book, with Advanced Plastics the largest share, followed by Mechatronics, Alternate Fuels and Structures & Control Systems out of the INR 1,450 crore order book (roughly 25% to be delivered in FY27, 54% in FY28 and 21% in FY29).

### The story in depth

Lumax Auto Tech is, on the evidence, a well-run parts supplier that is winning more of its customers' business and is spending money to grow rather than just to stand still. Capital spending rose from about INR78 crore in FY24 to INR233 crore in FY26, aimed at capacity expansion at IAC and Lumax Alps and at new land, and it has been matched by results: revenue up 34% in FY26 and operating margin up to 14.5%. Only about 24 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and every INR100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings produced INR19 of operating cash. The two acquisitions were digested well — IAC India in FY24 and Greenfuel in FY25 lifted group margin from 12.1% to 14.5-15%, IAC itself runs near 20%, and the loss-making JOPP joint venture was exited. This is the same engine behind the two strongest pattern matches. 'Market Share Gainers' is a strong fit because the company keeps growing faster than the industry it sells into (FY26 revenue +34% against industry production growth in the teens), with the order book compounding from INR600 crore in August 2022 to INR900 crore in FY24 to INR1,450 crore. 'Corporate Culture' is a strong fit because the same two people, Anmol Jain and Vikas Marwah, have led across all 19 calls, they explained candidly in May 2024 why growth excluding acquisitions was only 8% against 15-20% guidance, and they turn down revenue that fails their cash-collection test.

The awkward part is that several of the traits producing the growth also produce the risks. Growth is coming from a better sales mix rather than from charging more: premium interiors and higher value content per vehicle, with passenger vehicles now 53% of revenue. Management describes recovering commodity, wage and energy inflation as a back-to-back pass-through from carmakers with a three-to-six-month lag — which is why 'Pricing Power' scores no fit at all, and why 'Low Price Plus' only partially fits, since the fixed-cost programme that lifted standalone margins from 8-9% to roughly 11% came from an outside consultant hired in FY26 rather than from a long-embedded cost culture. That cost-plus position feeds directly into the 'Good Enough Goods' risk: the largest segment, Advanced Plastics at INR2,566 crore and about 53% of FY26 revenue, is relatively undifferentiated moulding work where carmakers run continuous price-down programmes, and the company's own trophies are for cost management and value engineering. Likewise, being sole-sourced on specific platforms is what earns the partial 'Toll Roads' and 'Cost to Replicate' credit — Cornaglia holds 100% of the business at Volkswagen and Tata Motors, Mannoh leads in gear shifters — but that identical fact is the Stakeholder Concentration risk, rated elevated, alongside IAC drawing more than 90% of its order book from Mahindra. The research effort has genuinely produced new business (the SHIFT tech centre, localised 'industry-first' ferrule-less CNG tubes, mechatronics revenue up about 150% in FY26), yet much of the underlying technology is licensed through partners, which is why 'Innovation Dominance' stays partial.

Timing and outside forces are the other qualifier. One quality check is explicitly negative: the company is riding a demand peak rather than sitting near a low point, with Q3 FY26 passenger vehicle production up 19% and two-wheelers up 15%, helped by GST rationalisation, rate cuts and record festive demand, making FY26 the best year on record — and Government Dependency is on watch precisely because policy has moved the numbers before, most visibly when a gazette notification pushed OBD-II emission norms from April 2023 to April 2025 and stalled the start of production behind Lumax FAE's INR135 crore order book. Cyclicality is also on watch, given the four-to-six quarters of semiconductor shortage described in the August 2022 call and the demand moderation flagged in the first half of FY26. Technology change sits in the middle: part of the older portfolio is tied to petrol and diesel engines — gear shifters at 12-15% of revenue, air intake systems, urea tanks, emission parts and oxygen sensors — while the newer side is genuinely repositioned, with electric vehicles at about 40% of the FY24 order book and 9% of FY26 revenue and Greenfuel adding CNG. Taste-driven and fashion risks are low because demand is functional and contractual with an order book scheduled to FY29, and there is no evidence at all about new entrants. Put together, the scoring lands at 62 out of 100 — 64 for business quality, 55 for multibagger fit, 66 for risk safety — a 'Decent' three-star verdict, with overall resilience judged strained. The honest reading is a strong operator with real execution, whose competitive protection is narrower than its growth rate suggests, and which is being measured at the best moment in its history.

## What to watch

- Whether the Advanced Plastics segment (53% of FY26 revenue) can hold margin against carmakers' continuous price-down programmes — the core of the 'Good Enough Goods' risk and the reason 'Pricing Power' scores no fit.
- Progress in reducing customer concentration — IAC's more than 90% dependence on Mahindra and Cornaglia's 100% share at Volkswagen and Tata Motors — the Stakeholder Concentration risk rated elevated.
- Whether growth holds up when the demand peak fades: the negative 'Cyclical market growth' check and the Cyclicality risk both rest on FY26 being the best year ever, aided by GST rationalisation, rate cuts and festive demand.
- Conversion of the INR1,450 crore order book into revenue on schedule, since the 'Market Share Gainers' pattern depends on it — and policy timing can stall it, as the OBD-II delay did to Lumax FAE's INR135 crore book under the Government Dependency risk.
- Whether the roughly 11% standalone margin from the FY26 consultant-led cost programme sticks once the consultant is gone — the weak link in the partial 'Low Price Plus' fit.
- The pace at which mechatronics, EV nominations and CNG replace petrol- and diesel-linked products such as gear shifters (12-15% of revenue), the balance behind the Technological Innovation risk.
