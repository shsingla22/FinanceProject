- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Good. Now: R&D is consistently ~9-11% of revenue rising to INR750-800cr with steady output (270 ANDAs, 4-6 US launches a quarter, peptide lab), though management concedes it was late to the biggest peptide opportunity. | The long view had said: R&D has run at 8-15% of sales for years with INR350cr of Aleor R&D written off, projects cut because returns fell, and management admits IRR thresholds have been lowered while US growth stayed single-digit — low long-run R&D efficiency.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Good. Now: Large new capacity (Indore, injectable/onco blocks) built in prior years is now ramping and driving operating leverage, but returns are still pending and utilisation is only 'improving', so incremental ROIC is unproven. | The long view had said: A large FY21-23 growth capex cycle (4 new facilities, ~INR300cr/yr of fixed cost incl. depreciation) delivered poor incremental returns — F2/F3 were still only 40-60% utilised three years later — and capex has since dropped to maintenance/debottlenecking of INR300-350cr.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Weak → Good. Now: Share is being captured in US products and ex-US markets (20% for FY26), but the India branded business has grown below market for four straight quarters, i.e. donating share at home. | The long view had said: India branded has persistently ceded share (growth below IPM for 3-4 years with catch-up repeatedly deferred), partially offset by genuine US ophthalmic share gains.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹15 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹15 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±6.0 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Neutral. Now: Utility Therapeutics was a genuinely small, complementary bolt-on ($4m upfront, milestone-linked), but the acquiree was pre-launch rather than already strong and the deal depresses near-term margins with a high royalty outgo. | The long view had said: No bolt-on acquisition track record; the one consolidated venture (Aleor) required successive write-offs of capitalised R&D totalling ~INR350cr before merger, and growth deals since are in-licensing (Pivya) with high royalty outgo.

Now: Animal health and the neglected UTI niche are genuinely under-contested pockets, but the bulk of the business sits in the highly-scrutinised, capital-saturated US generics arena. | The long view had said: This is a heavily covered, well-capitalised listed generic space (HSBC, ICICI, IIFL, Motilal, Dolat on every call) contesting the same US ANDA opportunities as dozens of Indian peers — the opposite of an ignored niche.

Now: End markets — Indian branded pharma, animal health, and a stable 30m-prescription US UTI market with no innovation for a decade — are structurally durable rather than declining. | The long view had said: India branded and animal health sit in structurally growing markets, but the largest exposure (US generics/API) is a deflationary, over-supplied end market, so end-market tailwind is neutral overall.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: There is no identified weak competitor being harvested; if anything Alembic is the donator in India, growing below market for four consecutive quarters. | The long view had said: Rather than facing weak competitors to take share from, Alembic has been the donator in India — peers expanding field forces into Tier 2/3 while it holds back and grows below market.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is a branded India franchise with 'focus brands' growing ahead of the portfolio, an animal-health brand performing well, and a new US branded entry (Pivya) — but the India branded business grew only 4-6% each quarter, below market, so brand strength is not producing share gain plus premium pricing. (The long view had said: There is a real India branded franchise with focus brands, specialty therapies and animal health outperforming, and a stretch attempt into US branded specialty (Pivya) — but no heritage story, no A&P disclosure, and brand strength is contradicted by four years of sub-market India growth.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Volume-led share gains are claimed in the US ('picked up market share in some other products') and ex-US grew 20% for FY26 (up to 36% in Q3), but India — a third of revenue — grew 5% for the year, admittedly below market growth, with the catch-up date pushed out to Q1 FY27. (The long view had said: The trajectory is share loss, not gain: India has grown below market for 3-4 years with the catch-up promised in FY24 still unfulfilled in FY26 (4-6% vs market), and the only share claim is ophthalmics in the US.)

**That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Nothing in the four FY26 calls addresses recurring revenue, attach rates, renewals, subscriptions or customer advances as a funding source; working capital is discussed only as receivables/inventory build. (The long view had said: Across all three calls there is no discussion of licence fees, AMCs, subscriptions, attach rates, renewals or deferred revenue as a funding source; working capital is discussed only as a rising absorber of cash (INR ~3,000 crores).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Cost to Replicate (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 2 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **New Entrants**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Management describes competitive intensity and price erosion as a permanent structural feature rather than a passing phase — every quarter's release flags pricing pressure in US generics and API, and the FY27 outlook explicitly assumes no relief, which is the signature of continuous new entry into each molecule as it genericises. (The long view had said: The defining theme across all 15 calls is a flood of competing generic suppliers resetting economics — 'too much supply and too many people supplying, especially from India' — producing high-single to low-double-digit price erosion every year, falling R&D IRR thresholds, and management's own FY27 assumption of no easing in competitive intensity.)
- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls demand is operations-linked prescription pharma — chronic/acute therapies, animal health, API — with revenue growing steadily (10%, 16%, 11%, 4%) and gross margin held inside a stated 70-75% band despite pricing headwinds; management frames the target market as stable rather than cyclical. (The long view had said: US generics behaves like a semi-commodity: persistent double-digit price erosion, one-off/short-term supply-shortage 'bump ups', a Sartan windfall that faded, and a large capex cycle (four new facilities, ~INR300cr/yr of costs) commercialised into a weakening price environment — margins swung from 75%+ gross and 20%+ EBITDA down and back. But demand is prescription-driven flow demand (chronic/acute drugs consumed in patient treatment, not customer capex), which caps the severity.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The GLP-1/peptide platform shift is the recurring analyst question across Q1 and Q2 FY26, and management concedes it missed the first wave of semaglutide in both the US and Canada, so it is playing catch-up on the industry's biggest new modality while relying on Tirzepatide to be in the first phase. (The long view had said: Across 15 calls no analyst or management discussion of platform-level disruption of generics; innovation is incremental and self-directed — moving R&D from oral solids toward injectables, complex injectables, derm, ophthalmic, inhalation, oncology and peptides, i.e. small-scale value-adding cadence rather than an installed base being made legacy.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Good Enough Goods (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
