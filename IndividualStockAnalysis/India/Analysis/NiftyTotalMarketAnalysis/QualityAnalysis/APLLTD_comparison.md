**Checks that improved:**

- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Good. Now: New facilities (including Indore) commissioned and now ramping are driving operating leverage, but incremental returns are still unproven as utilization is only now improving and net debt rose through the year. | The long view had said: The FY22-24 growth capex on four new facilities was large but returns lagged badly — INR300cr/yr of costs hit P&L with minimal revenue for years, and by FY26 capex is only maintenance/debottlenecking at INR300-350cr with F2/F3 still at 40-60% utilisation.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Weak → Good. Now: There is no halo to strip away — results are modest and management itself attributes outcomes to execution rather than any special advantage, making the assessment robust. | The long view had said: The exceptional pre-2020 record cited by management rested substantially on the Sartan windfall and peak US pricing which they themselves called unsustainable, so past results overstate durable skill.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹15 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹15 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±6.0 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Weak → Good. Now: A large India field force, a 20%-growing ex-US footprint expanding into Europe and Asia, and out-licensing/manufacturing partnerships give meaningful reach, but not one rivals cannot match. | The long view had said: US sales run through third-party logistics and distributors — a change of 3PL alone disrupted a quarter — and the India field force is being held flat while competitors extend reach.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Some weak incumbents exist in the UTI niche (older genericized products, over-priced new ones) and Alembic gained share in select US molecules, but in India Alembic is the one donating share. | The long view had said: In India the company is itself the donator, ceding share to peers who are expanding rep teams into Tier 2/3 towns, and in US generics competitors are strengthening rather than weakening.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Persistent multi-quarter price erosion across US generics and API is evidence of low entry barriers and irrational competitive pricing, which management expects to continue. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±6.0 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Barriers are low and pricing is irrational — competitive intensity keeps rising and shrinking the opportunity set, with price erosion persisting every year from FY23 to FY26.

- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: Share gains are claimed in select US products and ROW, but the India branded business has run below market growth all four quarters and is only hoping to converge in FY27. | The long view had said: Genuine share capture exists in US ophthalmics, but the India branded business has been losing relative share by growing below market for years, with the promised catch-up repeatedly deferred.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: R&D is high and rising (8-11% of sales, guided up to INR750-800cr for FY27) with a steady output of 4-6 US launches per quarter and 270 cumulative ANDAs, though the company admits being late on key peptides like semaglutide. | The long view had said: R&D at 8-9% of sales reliably produces filings and launches (ANDAs 237 to 270, ~25 US launches/year), but management concedes project returns have fallen since 2020 and the IRR hurdle has been lowered, so long-term efficiency is deteriorating.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Good. Now: Sustained ~9-11% of revenue R&D directed at complex injectables, peptides, oncology and NCE-1/First-to-File shows a real technical build, though the company was late to the first semaglutide wave. | The long view had said: R&D is genuinely shifting toward complex injectables, peptides, ophthalmics and First-to-File/Day-1 filings, but this is an aspiration still awaiting approvals rather than a demonstrated multi-product technology edge.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 1 weakened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is a branded India franchise with 'focus brands' growing ahead of portfolio and a deliberate strategic pivot into US branded (Pivya), but no heritage/premium-pricing story, no A&P scale argument, and India brand growth has trailed the market all year. (The long view had said: There is a real India branded franchise with focus brands, specialty segments and animal health outperforming, plus a first US branded launch (Pivya) in FY26 — but no heritage narrative, no A&P disclosure, no premium pricing, and India growth of 4-7% has trailed market growth for 3-4 years, which is the opposite of brand-led share gain.)
- **Low Price Plus**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Cost obsession is repeatedly and consistently articulated — cost improvement programmes in manufacturing, cost efficiency offsetting price erosion, operating leverage from new facilities lifting EBITDA-before-R&D margin from 23% to 25-26% — but there is no itemised savings culture or scale-based structural cost edge described that a peer generics maker could not copy. (The long view had said: There is genuine cost discipline (R&D trimmed from INR 722cr to 475cr in FY24, 'cost improvement programmes', facility utilisation), but the stated strategy is explicitly the opposite of low-price scale — chase higher-margin products, not volume — and the company competes on quality/compliance rather than price.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: There is a real time-and-capital barrier in the 270 cumulative ANDA filings, 226 approvals, USFDA-compliant facilities and a new peptide lab; but management itself describes an environment of persistent price erosion and competitive intensity, which is the signature of an advantage a well-funded generics rival can and does replicate. (The long view had said: Nothing in the record is un-replicable by a funded entrant: the assets are FDA-approved Baroda plants and an ANDA pipeline that competitors also build, and management itself says competitive intensity is shrinking the opportunity set and development IRR thresholds have had to be lowered.)

**That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No discussion of attach rates, AMC/service revenue, renewals, installed base or customer advances funding the business anywhere in the four calls; working capital is discussed only as a rising drag (net working capital ~INR3,000 crores, net debt INR1,213-1,361 crores). (The long view had said: Across all three calls there is no discussion of attach rates, AMC/service contracts, subscriptions, renewals or deferred revenue as a funding source; working capital is discussed only as a receivables/inventory drag (INR ~3,000 crores net working capital in FY26).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls demand is described as prescription/volume-led and operations-linked (formulations, API, animal health), not tied to any customer capex programme or commodity price cycle; revenue grew ~10-16% each quarter on volumes and new launches, and management explicitly attributes outcomes to execution rather than market cycles. The only capital-intensity flag is new facilities (Indore, injectables/onco) whose utilisation is still ramping, which is company-specific rather than cyclical. (The long view had said: US generics behave cyclically for Alembic — the Sartan windfall peak gave way to price erosion, EBITDA collapsed to INR 9 crores in Q1FY23 and recovered to INR 263 crores by Q4FY24, and a large 2020-23 capex cycle (four new facilities carrying ~INR 300 crores of annual cost) landed just as US returns fell, the classic over-investment-at-the-peak pattern; management itself calls the US 'a little cyclical'. Bounded because the products are consumed medicines (flow demand, not customer capex), India branded and animal health provide stable repeat volume, and by FY26 capex is back to INR 300-350 crores of maintenance only.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: Across four calls no single customer, licensor, distributor or supplier is named as driving results, and no analyst asked about a relationship renewal; management describes growth as broad-based across India branded, US generics, ex-US and API, with 226+ approvals and expansion into new territories across Europe and Asia. The only single-relationship item is the Utility Therapeutics in-licence for Pivya, which is small (USD 4mn upfront) and additive rather than load-bearing. (The long view had said: US revenue swings materially on the ordering behaviour of a small set of consolidated buyers — a single logistics switch caused buyers to pull forward inventory, taking one quarter to $70 million and the next to $47 million — and API showed 'slower offtake from some customers' in FY24. No single named customer or licensor is discussed as make-or-break, and the base is broadening across ROW territories, so exposure is real but bounded.)
- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: US generics and API face continuous entry by additional filers on each molecule, which management treats as a permanent condition — Q4 FY26 explicitly assumes no easing of pricing pressure or competitive intensity in FY27, and the response is cost programmes and sharper product selection rather than price defence. Exposure is bounded because the company is itself the entrant in several markets and is deliberately moving to harder-to-enter categories. (The long view had said: Repeated across the four years: a widening field of Indian suppliers entering the same US molecules has structurally reset economics, with management stating opportunities are 'becoming little less' and R&D threshold IRRs having been lowered because returns fell versus pre-2020. Bounded rather than high because the entrants are same-format generic peers rather than a structurally different business model, and Alembic is responding by moving into harder-to-enter formats.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The GLP-1/peptide platform shift is the recurring analyst question across Q1, Q2 and Q4 FY26, and management concedes it missed the first wave — it will not be in the first phase of semaglutide in either the US or Canada — so it is retooling with a dedicated peptide lab/block and an FY27 R&D step-up to INR750-800 crores. This is a modality change the incumbent must build new infrastructure for, though it is a pipeline-timing risk rather than an installed base going obsolete. (The long view had said: Across fifteen calls no analyst or manager raises a platform shift, format obsolescence or disruptive competitor technology; innovation is incremental and continuous — 270 cumulative ANDA filings, 25-27 launches a year, and a measured extension from oral solids into injectables, ophthalmics, derm, oncology and peptides funded at a steady 8-9% of revenue. That is small-scale, value-adding innovation, not the kind that strands an installed base.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (QUANT FLAG).*

*Unchanged: Good Enough Goods (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
