## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

**Checks that improved:**

- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: There is a regional density advantage — Hyderabad plus AP/Telangana are ~87% of revenue with deep hub-and-spoke coverage — but as a 92% B2C walk-in business there are essentially no contractual switching costs. | The long view had said: There is regional density in Hyderabad/AP-Telangana, but B2C walk-in customers face essentially no switching costs and management concedes it must match market rates to retain them.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing behaviour is rational with no discount war evident — realisation per test held steady at INR487-488 and they absorbed input inflation rather than cutting — and radiology hubs with PET-CT impose real capital barriers, though pathology entry remains easy. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±3.6 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Advanced radiology capital intensity and clinical credentials deter entrants, promoter ownership is stable, and Vijaya refused to join the discount war while the aggressive entrants' pricing proved unsustainable.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: The stated proposition is trust and quality rather than price, and customers are moving to Vijaya from cheaper unorganized labs, but the calls give no explicit evidence of price-insensitivity being tested. | The long view had said: The stated competitive answer to discounting is reliability and trust rather than price, backed by quality as the first pillar, high-end equipment and clinical talent.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Talent is an explicit pillar — headcount from ~2,000 to 3,500 in five years and an internal elevation of Siva Rama Raju to COO — but the CFO, CTO, and Lab Director slots were all filled externally in one quarter, which is thin evidence of an internal pipeline. | The long view had said: Talent is named as a core pillar with the workforce doubling to 3,500, a full senior bench built out (CFO, COO, CTO, Lab Director) and explicit second-line development in new geographies.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Good → Neutral. Now: Technology is purchased capability — automated lab tracks, PET-CT, a new genomic testing menu and a newly hired CTO — competitively available to any well-capitalised rival, with no proprietary edge or R&D intensity claimed. | The long view had said: Real technology depth for the segment — 3T/204-channel MRI, dual-source cardiac CT, PET-CT, an automated lab track system and now Genomics — but it is procured equipment rather than proprietary, R&D-built advantage.
- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: They dissected the weak Q2 into three specific causes rather than deflecting, and stayed deliberately conservative on Pune breakeven even while Bengaluru outperformed. | The long view had said: Management gives granular numbers, volunteers unflattering facts (the April revenue dip from the PH system migration, flat PH growth, no GLP-1 traction yet) and says when it lacks data rather than improvising.

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 6 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Across the four calls the technology discussion is incremental and self-directed — an automated track lab at Panjagutta, PET-CT with cardiac CT at Bannerghatta, planned genomic testing, a newly hired CTO — with no analyst question about AI/platform disruption and no defensive discussion of a competitor's new technology or of Vijaya's installed base becoming legacy. (The long view had said: The business is anchored in heavy fixed radiology infrastructure (PET-CT, gamma camera, 3T MRI, 160-slice CT at INR 12–19 crores per hub) plus recurring replacement capex, so a platform shift in imaging or AI reads would be costly to absorb — and analysts do raise disruption directly, which management dismisses rather than quantifies. Against that, the innovation cadence visible in the calls is incremental and value-adding (automated track lab at Panjagutta, genomics build-out, a new CTO), which is the benign form of innovation, not the perilous form.)
- **New Entrants**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Management describes taking share rather than defending it — footfall shifting from the unorganized sector, Hyderabad growing ~15-20% year-on-year purely from existing capacity with no new hubs, and margins expanding 379bps in Q4 — and at no point in the four calls is discounting or margin investment framed as a response to a new-format or online-first competitor. (The long view had said: Structurally different entrants are a recurring theme from the first call onward — digital/online aggregators running deep-discount customer-acquisition pricing in FY23, and by Q3 FY26 large organised pathology chains moving into radiology — and management addresses both defensively. But exposure is bounded by the outcome: Vijaya took no price cuts on wellness packages, and EBITDA margin rose from 38.2% (Q1 FY23) to 43.5% (Q4 FY26) with double-digit volume growth in the core Hyderabad market throughout.)

*Unchanged: Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Government Dependency (LOW); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
