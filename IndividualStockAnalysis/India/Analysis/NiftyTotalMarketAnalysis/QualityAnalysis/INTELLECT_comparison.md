### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 4 weakened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Cost to Replicate**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management articulates a concrete replication barrier — ten years of pre-revenue AI investment since 2016, 700 microservices, patented accuracy architecture, and 60 marquee banks sitting on a shared backbone that makes cross-sell easy and rip-out costly — but enterprise banking software faces well-funded incumbents (Temenos, FIS) and hyperscaler-backed AI rivals, so the barrier is time-and-integration based rather than un-replicable. (The long view had said: A concrete replication cost is articulable and repeatedly stated: ~25 years of banking domain modelling compressed into 329 (now 700) microservices, 500+ events and 1,700 APIs, two million R&D hours a year, 125 patents, plus Tier-1 live references in 62 countries — the reference base itself being the barrier, since Manish notes rivals 'try to extend that out from a core banking' but Intellect built 54 bank installs of real liquidity competency.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Clear evidence of winning ground — first US core/payments/liquidity wins in Q1, Americas the fastest-growing region, 53 new customers and 82 go-lives LTM, 23% LTM growth explicitly 'despite the industry slowdown' — but no named market-share numbers versus competitors are given. (The long view had said: Consistent customer accretion and incumbent displacement (Vancity taken from an entrenched credit-union vendor whose Backbase implementation failed, 53 new customers in LTM Q3 FY26, 500+ institutions, Americas grown to 27% of revenue with no market below 13%), but management never cites its own market-share percentage rising against named competitors, so the share-gain claim rests on wins and rankings rather than measured share.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Sales are direct enterprise engagements with banks' own decision-makers — the buyer is the payer; the only intermediaries mentioned are 14 'value discovery partners' and consulting hires, which is channel support, not a prescriber economy. (The long view had said: The bank, not an intermediary, pays and chooses, but from FY24 onward Intellect deliberately built a partner/GSI channel — Accenture, EY, KPMG, Deloitte, PwC signed for Purple Fabric, weekly enablement sessions per GSI, partners certifying themselves on iTurmeric/Purple Fabric, and 14 'value discovery partners' plus one large GSI in Q3 FY26 — so influencers increasingly source deals (120 leads in a quarter vs 50 historically).)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The FY26 calls describe deliberately raising cost (₹130 Cr / ~$15m Purple Fabric spend, 27 senior leaders hired, a 7.25 lakh sq ft campus) and accepting a 3–3.25% EBITDA margin drag; the pitch is business impact and TCO for the client, not a low-cost operating culture. (The long view had said: Management explicitly leans on Indian cost arbitrage in R&D — two million engineering hours a year that would cost ~$100m in the US — and platform economics where 7–20 customers add only 10–20% incremental cost, but there is no no-frills/low-price-to-customer positioning; they sell to Tier-1 banks on value, and EBITDA margin has stayed in the 16–24% band rather than compounding on turns.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (LIKELY FIT); Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Revenue depends on large bank technology-transformation budgets (a form of customer capex), which management itself acknowledges is in a slowdown, and quarterly margins swung sharply within the year — EBITDA margin fell from ~24% in Q1/Q2 to 16% in Q3 while PAT dropped to Rs 28 Cr. This is bounded by the shift toward recurring license-linked revenue and AMC, which now exceed half of revenue. (The long view had said: Revenue is tied to banks' discretionary technology transformation budgets — management repeatedly flags lumpy licence deals, including a Rs.30 Cr deal deferred in Q1 FY25 purely because 'the bank's financial budget limits are over', and itself directs investors to LTM rather than quarterly numbers; the offset is the deliberate shift to subscription/platform revenue (ARR Rs.1,118 Cr, +60% in LTM to Q3 FY26) and countercyclical investing through the FY24-25 'wintering period'.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Management devoted most of the FY26 call to a platform-level AI reset — rebuilding every product 'AI-first' in 18 months, filing 15 patents in six months, and positioning Purple Fabric against Palantir and C3.ai — and Arun Jain openly states AI will destroy IT jobs and put India 'in some trouble'. This is exactly the large-scale, infrastructure-changing innovation that creates more victims than victors, and Intellect's own eMACH.ai installed base is being re-architected mid-flight while it also sells against banks' legacy mainframes. (The long view had said: The company sells into an industry where the underlying platform is being reset twice over — cloud/composable, then generative AI — and management is explicit that the shift is destructive ('jobs will go away from the IT world, and India will be in some trouble'), while its own older licensed product estate must be rebuilt as eMACH.ai (300 to 700 microservices) and every product re-architected 'AI-first' in the last 18 months; analysts probe LLM disruption directly.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Results are driven by a small number of very large, lumpy engagements — 11 'multi-million-dollar destiny deals' in Q2, three US wins framed as the quarter's headline in Q1 — so individual Tier One bank decisions materially move a quarter, and the funnel is described in terms of a handful of large accounts to cross-sell into. The base is nonetheless visibly broadening. (The long view had said: Across the 18 calls no single customer, licensor or channel drives results: 500+ institutional customers in 62 countries, 53 new logos in LTM to Q3 FY26, and revenue spread across markets at 13%-27% each so management could pivot from a weak Europe to the Americas without losing the year. The residual risk is deal lumpiness (a single Rs.30 Cr slip swung a quarter), not relationship dependence.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Government Dependency (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: New Entrants (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 2 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 2 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
