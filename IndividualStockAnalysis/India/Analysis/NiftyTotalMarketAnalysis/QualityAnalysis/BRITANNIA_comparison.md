## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 30 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 8 improved, 2 regressed, 20 unchanged. 3 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them).

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Allocation | Good (+1.00) | Good (+1.20) | +0.20 |
| Return on Capital | Excellent (+1.67) | Excellent (+2.00) | +0.33 |
| Growth | Good (+0.83) | Good (+0.83) | +0.00 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Weak → Good. Now: A deep functional bench presents on every call and internal promotions are visible (Siddharth Gupta GM to VP Marketing, Jayaraman GM to VP), though the CEO was hired externally and a CMO was brought in from outside. | The long view had said: The top of the house has churned repeatedly — MD Varun Berry exited, CEO Rajneet Kohli exited, and the CEO/MD role was filled externally by Rakshit Hargave, alongside a new CMO — indicating a weak internal succession pipeline despite good frontline salesforce upskilling.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: Trust and quality reassurance are central to the positioning — nutrition claims (no maida, no palm oil, no added sugar, DHA-enriched) and the Nutrition Foundation work — though for a low-ticket consumer staple price remains a live consideration. | The long view had said: Quality and reliability investments exist (farmer milk quality programmes, best-in-class fill rates for organized trade) but the customer is buying taste and price, not certainty.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Commodity inflation (palm oil +45%, cocoa +35% year-on-year in Q1) has largely receded through the year and GST 2.0 plus grammage additions are framed as a demand inflection, suggesting the company sits at a favourable point in the input-cost and consumption cycle. | The long view had said: The commodity cycle that crushed FY23 margins has already normalised and margins have recovered to ~17%, so the company is mid-cycle rather than positioned at a bottom, with fresh fuel/laminate inflation now emerging.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Good → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹18 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹18 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±2.3 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Regional strength exists (dominance ambitions region by region) but there are no switching costs in biscuits — consumers and modern trade shift on price, and regional competitors are actively taking share. | The long view had said: There are no switching costs — retailers and consumers divert instantly on a price-point arbitrage, and regional players win pockets on local flavour preference.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: The unorganised/non-compliant trade is a genuine donor pool under GST 2.0, and Britannia notes it is outgrowing other organised national players, though the regional insurgents are gaining rather than donating. | The long view had said: Fragmented adjacent categories (wafers with a ~20% leader, cheese with many 2-3% players) offer donators, but in the core biscuit business the flow has reversed with regional players taking share from Britannia.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Weak → Neutral. Now: Product-formulation capability is evident (millet cookies, DHA fortification, cheese-dipped crackers) but this is recipe and manufacturing know-how, not a deep or defensible multi-product technology edge. | The long view had said: R&D is recipe, format and process work supporting regional customisation, not a deep multi-product technology edge; competitive threats are met by copying regional players' flavours faster.
- **Advertisement and promotion** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: A&P is explicitly being stepped up as a stated strategic priority with named campaigns (Good Day, 50-50 with Ravi Shastri, Milk Bikis/Gukesh, NutriChoice with Aamir Khan) and management links it to brand moats and market-share gains. | The long view had said: A&P is being stepped up deliberately as brand-equity building and shows sales payback — Cheeze Dipped became #2 in sandwich crackers within three months of a TV/IPL-backed launch — though spend follows category defence against regional players as much as premiumisation.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: The structure is fragmenting rather than consolidating, with numerous small and regional entrants attracted by the profit pool and national players resorting to discounts, so it is not a stable oligopoly. | The long view had said: A national oligopoly exists but is not stable — regional unlisted players are proliferating beyond the East, and the trade acts as a 'gatekeeper' with real negotiating power over the INR5/INR10 packs.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Domestic Hindi-belt expansion is executing well and the Kenya JV is progressing, but the international business was materially hit by the West Asia conflict with Oman/Dubai manufacturing and Strait of Hormuz disruption, making the geographic record mixed this year. | The long view had said: The seed-then-manufacture playbook is credibly executed — Nepal grew from INR20cr to ~INR180cr and Mundra/Oman were flexed to keep serving North America — though West Asia exposure proved fragile in Q4 FY26.

*Unchanged: Mini-monopolies; Return on capital (headline); Pricing, Mix and Volume; Security by obscurity; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Gain in market share; Asset turn; Structural end-market growth; Convenience benefits; Halo effect (caution); Distribution advantage; Intangible benefits; Barriers to entry and rationality; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Candor; Cost of working capital; Out of limelight; Research and development; Disciplined stewards.*

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these — identical by construction): Merger and acquisition (Neutral); Network effects (Poor); Persistence of growth (Excellent).*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 3 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Every call is organised around named heritage brands (Good Day, Marie Gold, 50-50, Tiger, Milk Bikis, NutriChoice, Pure Magic) with sustained celebrity-led A&P and adjacency launches stretched under the same names (Toastea rusk, Fudge/Layer cake, Doodh Marie); both CEOs commit to stepping up brand investment. (The long view had said: Heritage brands (Good Day, Marie Gold, 50-50, Little Hearts, NutriChoice, Treat, JimJam) carry sustained A&P — Aamir Khan on NutriChoice, IPL and World Cup activations — command a premium over competitors, and stretch repeatedly into adjacencies (croissant, cake, rusk, wafer, cheese, ghee, drinks) under the same name; management explicitly frames brand investment as moat-building.)
- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Forward integration shows only through direct digital channels — e-commerce salience rose from 4% to 6% of domestic sales in FY26 (management claims >12% adjusted), with quick commerce ~70% of that and exclusive/D2C offerings planned — but there are no own stores, franchising or licensing revenues. (The long view had said: A clear, sustained move toward the customer via own-channel e-commerce — 8x FY20 levels by Aug 2022, 4% of sales by FY25, 6% (12% adjusted for ₹5/₹10 packs) by FY26 with 70% via quick commerce, deliberately B2C-only, and planned digital-first brands — but no own stores, franchising or royalty streams.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Share gains are real but uneven across the year: 5 of 7 regions gained and the Hindi belt added 65bps in Q1 FY26, yet overall Nielsen share was 'flattish' and by Q2/Q3 FY26 management concedes share leakage to regional players, making it a named strategic priority to fight them. (The long view had said: Share gains are the most consistently repeated claim across the record — 36 straight quarters as of Aug 2022 and still 'continuing to gain share' in Aug 2024 — earned alongside price leadership rather than discounting, with direct reach expanded to 28.2 lakh outlets and 30,000 rural distributors; the caveats are cheese (Amul is 6x Britannia's share) and regional competitors that by FY26 required a dedicated strategic pillar.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (QUANT SIGNAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
