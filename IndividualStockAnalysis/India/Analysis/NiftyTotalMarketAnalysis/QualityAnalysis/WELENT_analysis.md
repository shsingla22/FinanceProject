# Welspun Enterprises (WELENT) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Welspun Enterprises is an Indian infrastructure construction company that builds large public works for government clients — mainly water and wastewater plants for city and state bodies (like Mumbai's municipal corporation, BMC/MCGM) and highways and bridges for road authorities (NHAI and state governments). It mostly works on an EPC basis, meaning the client pays it to design and build the project, so the money comes from construction contracts rather than from owning assets; on top of that it earns steadier, recurring fees from long-term operations & maintenance contracts (e.g. a 15-year O&M phase after the Dharavi sewage plant), and from toll or annuity payments on the few road assets it develops itself. Management deliberately runs an "asset-light" model — it sold six road assets to Actis in 2022 and plans to monetize more — leaving it debt-light with large cash reserves (~INR1,700 crores at FY26). Work is concentrated in India, heavily in Mumbai and Maharashtra plus projects in Uttar Pradesh, Bihar and Tamil Nadu; FY26 consolidated revenue was INR3,615 crores with a consolidated order book of about INR20,000 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Water / Integrated Water vertical (EPC)** — Designs and builds large water-treatment and sewage-treatment plants and water-supply schemes — Dharavi 418 MLD sewage plant, Bhandup 2,000 MLD and Panjarpur 910 MLD water treatment plants, and UP Jal Jeevan Mission household-water works — and management calls it the biggest part of the business, at roughly 70% of the EPC order book in 2024 and about INR11,000 crores of order book by Q3 FY26.
- **Transport vertical (roads, EPC / HAM / BOT)** — Builds highways, expressways and bridges for NHAI and state governments under EPC, hybrid-annuity (HAM) and build-operate-toll models — past work includes the Delhi-Meerut Expressway and Mukarba Chowk-Panipat, current ones Aunta-Simaria and Sattanathapuram-Nagapattinam, and the newly awarded Pune-Shirur Elevated Road at about INR7,300 crores, the single largest recent win.
- **Welspun Michigan Engineers Limited (WMEL) — subsidiary** — A specialist engineering arm acquired in 2023 doing niche urban-infrastructure work — micro and segmental tunneling, sewer rehabilitation with polymer liners, marine works, pumping stations and bridges — which earned INR874 crores of revenue in FY26 at a ~21% EBITDA margin, making it a small but high-margin piece of the group.
- **Operations & Maintenance (O&M) contracts** — Long-duration contracts to run and maintain the plants and roads it has built, described by management as providing stable, recurring cash flows; roughly INR5,400 crores of the consolidated order book sits in O&M.
- **Oil and gas — Adani Welspun Exploration Limited (AWEL), 35% associate stake** — A minority stake in a joint venture holding offshore gas blocks (Mumbai, B-9, C37, ~1.1 Tcf gas in place) that produces nothing yet and has already caused a write-off — management says it was never counted in the business plan, so it is a side option rather than a real earnings source.

### The story in depth

Welspun Enterprises comes out of the three reviews as a genuinely well-run contractor operating in a difficult industry, which is why the combined rating lands at 48 out of 100, or Mixed. The strongest marks are for the end-market and the balance sheet: water supply, sewage treatment and urban infrastructure are described as being in permanent expansion, backed by the Jal Jeevan Mission, the 100-cities programme and multilateral funding, with a stated addressable pool of about 4.5 lakh crore rupees. The company funds itself unusually cheaply within that market, collecting cash 418 days before it has to pay suppliers, so customers effectively finance the work. Management has used that position conservatively: net debt of 43 crore against 1,728 crore of cash, a CRISIL AA- rating with the outlook raised to positive, and the asset-light promise made in 2022 actually delivered through the Actis monetisation. The risk review calls overall resilience SOUND, and the Corporate Culture pattern is the single STRONG FIT, partly because bad news gets disclosed promptly and specifically, including the Aunta-Simaria delays and the Kutch write-off booked as an exceptional loss.

The patterns that fit best are the ones about winning more work, and they rest on the same foundation that creates the biggest risks. Market Share Gainers is a LIKELY FIT on the back of an order book that grew from 11,900 crore in 2022 to roughly 20,000 crore in FY26, with water built from nothing to about 70 percent of the engineering and construction book in six years, and marquee wins at Dharavi, Bhandup, Panjarpur and Pune-Shirur. Innovation Dominance is also a LIKELY FIT, resting on trenchless and micro-tunnelling work, tunnel boring machines through the WMEL acquisition, specialised linings, the multi-level 418 MLD Dharavi plant and the SmartOps tool. That technical edge is showing up in the numbers: FY26 revenue fell 2 percent while operating profit rose 16 percent and margin widened 350 basis points to 23 percent against guidance of 18 to 20 percent, driven by a 37 percent increase in higher-margin tunnelling and rehabilitation. But those same wins are all lowest-bid public tenders, so Pricing Power, Toll Roads and Friendly Middleman are all recorded as NO FIT, and Low Price Plus, Brand Strength and Cost to Replicate only get partial credit. The strength is execution and prequalification, not the ability to name a price.

The risks are the direct consequence of that structure rather than separate problems. Because every contract is won on price and awarded by a government body, Government Dependency is HIGH RISK, with essentially the whole book coming from NHAI, MSRDC, BMC and MCGM and the Uttar Pradesh Jal Jeevan Mission, and Stakeholder Concentration is ELEVATED, since BMC and MCGM alone anchor most of the roughly 14,000 crore water book. Because revenue is tied to project spending rather than steady demand, Cyclicality is HIGH RISK, visible in swings from plus 96 percent in the first quarter of FY23 to minus 12 percent in the third quarter of FY26, and in FY23 margins collapsing from 11.7 percent to 7.2 percent on commodity inflation the company could not pass on. Those same swings explain the two weakest quality checks: margins that move by roughly 12.6 points, which the framework reads as a sign of an unstable industry, and lumpy sales growth despite a respectable 14 percent annual average. The most serious gap is the return on capital, where every 100 rupees of shareholders' funds and borrowings produced only about 2 rupees of operating cash this year. Two things partly offset this: the operations and maintenance book, which grew from about 1,500 crore of an 11,900 crore order book in 2022 to about 5,400 crore, giving a steadier, advance-funded revenue layer tied to plants already built, and the WMEL bolt-on, which scaled from 144 crore of quarterly revenue to 874 crore in FY26 at a stable 21 percent margin. Against that sits the newer risk of the 7,300 crore Pune-Shirur build-operate-transfer commitment and a speculative oil and gas joint venture, which pull in the opposite direction to the asset-light discipline the culture pattern rewards.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Government Dependency, Stakeholder Concentration, Fashion Risk. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 2 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.7× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 3 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is entirely contract/capex-linked and has swung violently across the period — +96% year-on-year in Q1FY23, +28% in Q1FY25, then -12% in Q3FY26 and a full-year decline — while FY23 margins collapsed to 7.2% standalone EBITDA on the commodity cycle (bitumen, steel, cement, Jharkhand mining seizure) before recovering to 23%; order inflow and execution depend on the government capex cycle, not customer operations.

> "this is primarily due to the unusually high commodity price cycle, and as we see the inflation normalizing... we believe that this pressure will ease off" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Growing operations-linked O&M book (~INR5,400cr, 15-year Dharavi/Panjarpur contracts) plus toll annuities give a flow-type revenue layer; asset-light monetisation and ~INR1,700cr cash allow countercyclical bidding*

- ⚠️ Year-to-year sales growth swings by ±86 percentage points — boom-and-bust revenue, not a steady compounder.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from -25% to 21% (swinging ±12.6 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 49% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 2% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

## What to watch
