### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: Demand is everyday consumer footwear — school shoes, sneakers, slippers sold ~26 million pairs a year through a distributor and online base, with no linkage to customer capex or commodity price cycles; the only swings discussed in FY26 were self-inflicted (SAP/warehouse transition) or policy-timing related (GST festive shift), and the company kept investing in Paonta Sahib and Pant Nagar capacity through a soft demand patch. (The long view had said: Demand is consumer-discretionary and highly seasonal/weather-driven rather than commodity- or customer-capex-driven: Q1 FY25 revenue fell on heatwaves and lower footfalls, FY24-25 saw a 'challenging market environment' and channel destocking, and an analyst in Feb 2026 flagged 'quite a volatility in terms of the quarterly performances'. Offsetting this, footwear is a repeat-purchase flow product with no tie to customer capex programmes, and management kept marketing spend up through the downturn.)
- **Fashion Risk**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Management credits growth in every FY26 call to trend-led drivers — the sneaker category, celebrity campaigns with Siddhant Chaturvedi and Kriti Sanon, a new logo and repositioning as a 'culture-led brand spanning fashion, sports and everyday lifestyle' — so a meaningful share of the current growth rests on taste that can move. The counterweight is a broad, functional, repeat-purchase base. (The long view had said: Management explicitly positions the company on taste — 'we are a fashion forward organisation' bringing global trends to India fastest — and credits recent growth to a celebrity campaign ('You Go, Girl' with Kriti Sanon) and a trending sneaker silhouette, alongside a full brand/logo refresh; taste can move without warning. The base is nonetheless everyday functional footwear sold at ~26 million pairs a year across many SKUs.)

**That worsened:**

- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The legacy demand base is genuinely eroding — Q1 FY26 volumes fell 11.6% on lower entry-price DIP school shoes, slippers and sandals, and Q3 noted a lower mix of open footwear — but the pivot toward the new preference is working, with sneakers doubling in volume, ASP up 7% for FY26, women's and kids' mix rising to 22%, and an athleisure apparel launch in January 2026. (The long view had said: The consumer shifts visible in the calls — casualisation/athleisure, sneakers, online buying, women's participation — have been pivoted into rather than suffered: sneaker volumes roughly doubled year-on-year, women/kids mix rose from 16% (FY22) to 22%, online grew 18-19% after the marketplace pivot, and athleisure apparel launched in Jan 2026.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 2 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 2 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
