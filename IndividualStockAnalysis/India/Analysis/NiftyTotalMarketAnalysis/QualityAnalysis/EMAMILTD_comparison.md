**Checks that improved:**

- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Debt-free balance sheet, working-capital tightening and continued brand investment through a weak year, tempered by a steady stream of small acquisitions and strategic investments rather than a purely organic focus. | The long view had said: Prudent on leverage (debt-free, working capital tightened) and willing to invest counter-cyclically in A&P, but the period is marked by repeated inorganic bets — Dermicool, Helios/TMC, Brillare, Axiom, IncNut — rather than an organic focus.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Management persists with multi-year turnarounds (Fair & Handsome to Smart & Handsome, Kesh King repositioning, The Man Company) and explicitly rejects short-term framing, though results from that tenacity are still unproven. | The long view had said: There is tenacity in multi-year projects (Project Khoj rural expansion, 50-year franchise, persistent Kesh King revival that finally produced two double-digit quarters), but strategy is visibly outsourced to consultants — BCG for brands, KPMG for supply chain, a Bain partner hired as Chief Growth Officer.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: Reformulation and NPD are yielding visible results — Kesh King Gold ('Ayurveda plus science') drove 10% and then 14% growth in successive quarters, and 12 new Smart & Handsome SKUs plus Dermicool spray were launched — but there is no disclosed R&D intensity or long-run efficiency metric. | The long view had said: There is a steady stream of line extensions (Dermicool Her, Dermicool soap, Smart & Handsome sunscreen/deo, digital-first Zandu SKUs) and two WHO-GMP units, but these remain small (digital portfolio ~INR25 crore) and R&D has not created a needle-moving new franchise in four years.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: No weak competitor is identified as a share donor; the only structural share shift discussed is Emami's own move from wholesale (down to 27%) into organized channels. | The long view had said: Weak unorganised players are visibly ceding share as costs rise, and multinationals divesting sub-scale brands (Reckitt selling Dermicool) provide another source of donated share.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Neutral → Weak. Now: The one strong quarter was volume-led (11% domestic on 9% volume) and the company passed GST cuts straight through to lower MRPs, so pricing/mix is not the growth engine even though it aided gross margin. | The long view had said: Growth is predominantly volume- and season-led rather than price/mix-led — 8.7% volume within 10% value in Q1FY25, explicit reluctance to take price, and GST cuts passed through as grammage increases on ~20% of the portfolio; premium mix (TMC/Brillare/IncNut) is still only ~21% of business.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 5. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 0 weakened, 5 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Q3 and Q4 FY26 both credit 'judicious price hikes'/'judicious pricing actions' for gross margin expansion (+30bps to 70.6%, then +250bps to 68.4%), and management points to consumer stickiness in talc; but pricing power is not demonstrated on volumes — talc fell 17%, then steeply, then 40% in Q4, and FY26 revenue declined 1%. (The long view had said: Management repeatedly cites 'judicious price hikes' with gross margins expanding (62.6% in Q1FY23 to 69.9% for FY26, +130bps), but pricing is deliberately restrained in some years and volume in core brands (Kesh King, hair oil, summer portfolio) depends heavily on consumer/trade promotions and grammage increases rather than pricing-for-value.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Innovation is named and quantified each quarter — Dermicool prickly heat spray, 12 new Smart & Handsome SKUs, Kesh King Gold reformulated with Gro Biotin and plant Omega 369, digital-first Zandu Care launches — but no R&D spend, share of industry R&D, or revenue-from-new-products disclosure, and the flagship Male Grooming relaunches have repeatedly failed to convert into growth (-9%, then -4% in Q4). (The long view had said: Named launches appear every year (Dermicool Her, Zandu digital-first SKUs, Brillare rosemary oil, Smart and Handsome NPDs) and R&D/WHO-GMP capability is claimed, but R&D spend is never disclosed or quantified and innovation has not translated into sustained volume or pricing dominance — FY26 revenue declined 1%.)
- **Low Price Plus**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management repeatedly cites 'rigorous cost discipline' and 'cost leadership' and passed the entire GST 2.0 cut through to MRPs, and is pushing shampoo sachets for rural affordability — but with gross margins of 68-71% this is a premium-margin model, not a low-price/high-turns one. (The long view had said: Cost discipline is mentioned, but the model is the opposite of low-price-plus: gross margins of 67.7% (Aug 2024) rising to 70.6% (Feb 2026), EBITDA margins of 33.4%, explicit premiumisation and price hikes rather than passing savings through on volume/turns.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (QUANT SIGNAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (QUANT SIGNAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 5 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Not a commodity or customer-capex cycle — the portfolio is repeat-purchase consumables — but earnings swing sharply with weather-driven seasonality: talc/prickly heat fell 17% in Q1 FY26 and 40% in Q4 FY26, dragging consolidated Q4 revenue -4% and full-year EBITDA -6%, with Q2 EBITDA down 29% on operating deleverage. (The long view had said: Not a commodity or customer-capex cycle — these are repeat-purchase consumer staples — but the portfolio is heavily weather/season-levered, and a weak summer alone drove a 22% decline in the summer portfolio (talc -40%), a 4% Q4 sales decline and a 1% full-year FY26 revenue decline with EBITDA down 6%. Management repeatedly frames growth outcomes around season (hottest summer in 14 years in FY25, 'extremely poor' summer in FY26, extended winter, El Nino).)
- **Good Enough Goods**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No discussion across the four calls of defending price against cheaper substitutes, retailer own-label pressure, or intermediaries steering choice; pricing power is affirmatively visible in gross margins expanding to 68.4% in Q4 (+250bps) and 69.9% for FY26 (+130bps) on 'judicious pricing actions', and management explicitly attributes the Q4 decline to weather rather than competition. (The long view had said: The portfolio is mass-market, low-complexity and price-sensitive — talc, balms, hair oils — where growth is admittedly promotion-driven ('depending on the consumer promotions, the market behaves like that'), analysts asked whether Kesh King's weakness was consumers 'down trading', and Patanjali/unorganized players compete on price. Management's own explanation for the FY26 hair-oil recovery is that cheaper unorganized supply became uneconomic, i.e. the relief is cost-driven and reversible rather than a durable moat.)

**That worsened:**

- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Two of Emami's legacy demand bases are eroding on taste, not cycle: management states outright that face cream demand has structurally declined (driving the Fair & Handsome rebrand), and Kesh King had to be repositioned as 'Ayurveda plus science' because consumers now demand proof of efficacy. The IncNut acquisition is explicitly framed around consumers shifting to efficacy and personalization. (The long view had said: Core demand bases have visibly eroded: the premium hair-fall oil market was 'on a decline for quite some time', Kesh King fell 15% and male grooming declined across multiple quarters, and the fairness franchise had to be rebranded from Fair and Handsome to Smart and Handsome and extended into sunscreen/deodorant/body wash. Management explicitly acknowledges consumer migration toward efficacy, customization and online channels.)

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
