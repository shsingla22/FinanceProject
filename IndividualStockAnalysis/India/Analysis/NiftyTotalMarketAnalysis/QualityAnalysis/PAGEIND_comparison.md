### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 29 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 5 improved, 4 regressed, 20 unchanged. 3 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them).

**Checks that improved:**

- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Weak → Good. Now: The exclusive territorial licence for the Jockey brand functions as a regional monopoly on that brand, but consumer switching costs in innerwear are negligible, so the protection is at the brand-rights level rather than the customer level. | The long view had said: There are no switching costs or captive aftermarket in innerwear — consumers trade freely, entry-level price points are losing to competition, and channel mix is shifting offline-to-online outside the company's control.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing behaviour is rational rather than promotional — no discounting war is referenced, prices were held then raised in a calibrated way on select styles, and ownership/management is stable. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.5 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing rationality has improved since the FY24 discount war and brand/distribution scale is a real barrier, but the periodic discounting bouts and heightened competitive intensity at entry price points show the industry is not fully disciplined.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: Genuine capacity growth capex is underway — Odisha plant commissioned in Q1 FY26 and the KR Pet facility funded by a subsidy-linked term loan — but with FY26 volumes up only 3.9% the incremental return on this new capacity is not yet visible. | The long view had said: Capex is calibrated and modest — the Odisha plant (subsidy-backed) is the only major addition while planned capacity stays ~200mn pieces with no further facilities, and capex-light efficiency gains rather than expansion have driven recent output.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Results and skill are separable here: the record shows weak topline alongside genuinely self-generated margin and working-capital gains, and management itself attributes Q4 partly to macro recovery rather than claiming full credit. | The long view had said: Stripping the halo, much of the franchise value is the licensed Jockey brand and legacy distribution rather than current management skill, and FY24-FY26 delivery (4-6% revenue growth, repeatedly deferred double-digit target) argues for neutrality.
- **Candor** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Unusually candid throughout — management labelled its own results subpar against internal targets, explained the mechanics of volume volatility and distributor destocking without deflecting, and disclosed the Rs 350 million labour-code provision clearly. | The long view had said: They own the misses plainly — naming weak demand, competitive pressure at entry price points, the self-inflicted inventory problem, and refusing to promise a double-digit timeline — and disclose one-off labour-code provisions transparently.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: The calls frame the value proposition around product features, price stability and consumer sentiment rather than customers paying up for certainty, and demand proved clearly sensitive to the macro rather than trust-anchored. | The long view had said: The proposition is consistency and trust — refusing to alter raw-material mix to protect quality even under severe cotton inflation, and 150 years of Jockey heritage backing the reliability promise.
- **Advertisement and promotion** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: A&P was sustained and even stepped up (higher marketing spend compressed Q2 EBITDA), but through three of four quarters it failed to lift revenue beyond 3-6%, so the spend has not demonstrably converted into sales in this window. | The long view had said: A&P is a disciplined, budgeted 4-5% of net revenue used for brand-building and consumer activation, and management is choosing to raise it back to 5% even at the cost of margin, though the payoff in sales growth has been slow.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Management concedes revenue performance was below its own plan for most of the year with no claim of share capture; the Q4 rebound is attributed to demand recovery and the end of distributor destocking rather than to taking share. | The long view had said: Penetration remains low (18-20% men's innerwear, 7-8% athleisure) leaving genuine runway, and management believes leadership lets it gain in adversity — but three years of 4-6% growth means share capture is asserted more than demonstrated.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Internal elevation of Karthik Yathindra to CEO points to a pipeline, but the only people commentary this year is a recruitment freeze and a ~2,500-3,000 headcount reduction via lean/automation, with no talent-development programme discussed. | The long view had said: A visible internal pipeline — Karthik Yathindra rose from CMO/SVP in FY25 to CEO by FY26 — alongside productivity-led lean initiatives, though a recruitment freeze and ~2,500-3,000 headcount reduction temper the development story.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 3 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Real brand assets are cited — Jockey's 150-year heritage, the 'Licensee of the Decade' award for a second term, sustained marketing investment and adjacency stretches (JKY Groove, outerwear, bonded-tech bras) — but the brand is licensed from Jockey International rather than owned, and the FY26 record shows neither premium pricing nor demonstrated share gain, with revenue up only 6.3% and prices deliberately frozen. (The long view had said: Repeated across the record: 150-year Jockey heritage, 'Licensee of the Decade' from Jockey International for a second consecutive term, sustained A&P at 4-5% of net revenue every year, refusal to change product 'recipe' to cut cost, and stretch into juniors, athleisure and outerwear under the same name.)
- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management discloses bad news promptly and owns underperformance on the calls — Karthik conceding revenue was 'below par' to plan, and naming the two-year distributor-inventory problem that only closed in Q4 — and the balance sheet stays conservative (a single Rs.40 million subsidy-linked term loan); against that, the repeated double-digit growth aspiration went undelivered for three straight quarters before demand, not execution, turned it. (The long view had said: V.S. Ganesh has fronted all 16 calls with an internally promoted CEO (Karthik, CMO to CEO), the company stayed debt-free with working capital tightened from 73 to 52-56 days, and bad news is disclosed promptly and owned — the two-year distributor-inventory correction, the missed double-digit growth target admitted repeatedly, and the Rs.350mn labour-code provision that cut PAT 7.4% explained upfront rather than buried.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Management explicitly took no price increase in Q1, Q2 or Q3 FY26, passed the September GST cut through to consumers, and absorbed cotton inflation via sourcing and cost control rather than price; only in Q4 was there a 'calibrated price increase in select styles', and the Q3 ASP rise was attributed to mix, not price — this is the absence of pricing power over the last twelve months. (The long view had said: Premiumisation is real and repeated (premium ranges outgrowing mid-premium; ASP up 4% on mix in Q3 FY26), but the core test fails on the trajectory: after July 2022 the company took no price increase for roughly three and a half years, and the January 2026 hike was only ~2% weighted average taken to recover cotton inflation, not to lead it.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 5 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Pricing power looks constrained: no price increase was taken for four straight quarters despite cotton and other input inflation, with margin held only through sourcing, lean/automation and a recruitment freeze, and volume growth stuck at 1.4%-2.5% until the Q4 destocking-driven rebound — the classic signature of cheaper alternatives capping price. No specific private-label or discount challenger is named on the calls, which bounds the read. (The long view had said: Analysts have pressed since FY25 on the widened price gap to smaller brands stalling the natural upgrade to Jockey and asked for a flanker brand; by FY26 entry-level products — in men's innerwear, the largest category — are the weakest part of the book, and management's defence has been holding prices for 24 months and launching tighter price-point athleisure styles under the Jockey name.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Essentially the entire business is a licence: Page manufactures and sells under the Jockey brand owned by Jockey International, and the Q4 FY26 call was framed around that relationship (150-year brand milestone, 'Licensee of the Decade' award for a second term) — the Safilo failure mode of losing a licence sits squarely on this model. The customer side is a distributor-led wholesale structure where reported sales are primary sales to distributors, though the retail base itself is broad. (The long view had said: The entire franchise rests on licensed brands — Jockey and Speedo — owned by Jockey International, and management has ruled out building any owned brand alongside it, so a licence loss would be a Safilo-style event; management repeatedly foregrounds the relationship ('licensee of the Decade Award for the second consecutive term', Jockey's 150 years).)
- **Government Dependency**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Revenue is not government-set, but policy repeatedly moved the P&L within the year: the new labour/wage codes forced an exceptional Rs. 350 million gratuity and leave provision that turned Q3 PAT negative (-7.4%), the September 2025 GST rate cut had to be passed through to consumers, and a Karnataka state capital-subsidy-linked term loan supports the KR Pet plant. (The long view had said: Revenue is B2C and unregulated; policy shows up only in costs and incentives — a one-time Rs.350mn labour-code provision in Q3FY26, a GST change management called negligible, and Rs.40-50 crore of Odisha state subsidies expected in FY27.)

*Unchanged: New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Technological Innovation (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
