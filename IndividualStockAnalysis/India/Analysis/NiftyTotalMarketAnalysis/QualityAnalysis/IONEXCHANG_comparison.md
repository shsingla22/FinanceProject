### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The engineering segment (roughly 60% of FY26 revenue) is project/EPC work tied to customers' capacity expansion — ultra-pure water for solar and pharma plants, refinery packages, semiconductor and data-centre opportunities — and its margins swung violently within the 12 months (EBIT margin called 'all-time low at 4.8%' in Q2, consolidated Q4 FY26 EBITDA margin 2.31% vs 10.75% in Q1). Offsetting this, the chemical division is explicitly consumption-linked and the consumer division grew 24–34% y/y through the year. (The long view had said: Roughly two-thirds of revenue is EPC engineering tied to customers' capacity expansion (solar ultra-pure water, semiconductor fabs, refineries, data centres, textile parks), and segment margins have swung hard across the 16 calls — engineering EBIT ~6% in Q1FY23, recovering to 11%+ group EBITDA in FY25, then FY26 group EBITDA down 29% with Q4FY26 EBITDA margin at 2.31%. The chemicals/consumer businesses are consumables sold into customer operations and grew through the same period, which bounds the damage.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The resin business faces price-based competition that management addresses through quality differentiation rather than brand — Roha is explicitly positioned as 'an industry benchmark in terms of product quality' and needed third-party WQA certification to access international markets, while Q4 profitability was hit by input costs requiring pricing action to be passed through. The consumer division has grown revenue strongly for four straight quarters yet remained loss-making every quarter, consistent with a competitive, price-sensitive end market. (The long view had said: Ion exchange resins are a globally traded product where price competition is visible: the company declined awarded semiconductor packages on price, must repeatedly 'pass on' input-cost increases with a lag (chemical EBIT fell 18% y-o-y in Q3FY26 despite 16% revenue growth), and management explicitly frames the R&D agenda as moving to value-added products to hold a competitive edge — the posture of a firm defending a premium rather than one immune to cheaper substitutes.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL); Fashion Risk (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
