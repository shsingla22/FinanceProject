# Birla Corporation (BIRLACORPN) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Birla Corporation is a more than 100-year-old Indian company, part of the MP Birla Group, whose main business today is making and selling cement — management describes it as roughly a 20 million tonne capacity company, going to 27.5 million tonnes once its Maihar Line-II and linked grinding units come up around FY29. It makes money by mining limestone and manufacturing cement at integrated plants and grinding units, then selling it mostly through dealers to individual house-builders (the 'trade' segment, 77% of volumes in FY26) rather than to bulk industrial buyers ('non-trade'), with a deliberate tilt toward higher-margin blended cement (88% of volumes in FY26) over plain OPC, sold under brands positioned at premium (Perfect Plus), mid (Samrat Advanced) and popular price points. Its plants and markets are concentrated in Central India (Maihar in MP, Kundanganj in eastern UP), the North (Chanderia in Rajasthan, its single biggest unit) and Maharashtra/Vidarbha (Mukutban), with only a very small presence in the East. Profitability swings with regional cement prices, freight (average lead distance 337 km in FY26), fuel costs (pet coke, diesel) and state incentive accruals; FY26 volumes grew about 4% with EBITDA near INR 800 a tonne, and net debt was about INR 2,300 crore as of June 2026.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Cement** — The overwhelming bulk of the business and essentially all of what management discusses on these calls — manufacturing and branded selling of cement across Central India, the North and Maharashtra, with capacity of about 20 million tonnes rising to a planned 27.5 million tonnes by FY29.
- **Jute** — The company's original business — management says it runs the first Indian jute mill, still operating, and is one of the largest players in the jute sector, but the MD explicitly says it is 'not the real focus' for investors, implying it is small relative to cement.
- **Mukutban (Maharashtra/Vidarbha operations)** — A newer plant in a market where the company had no prior presence, ramped from cash-negative to a profit and growth engine — volumes rose to 27.7 lakh tonnes in FY26 at capacity utilisation in the high 60s, though it sells at lower net realisations than the older markets.
- **RCCPL Private Limited (the acquired Reliance cement business)** — The cement business bought from Reliance in 2016 — the Maihar integrated plant and Kundanganj grinding unit — which roughly doubled capacity, was rebranded into Perfect Plus, and is run as a subsidiary with its own CFO; management treats its successful integration and premium repositioning as the company's proof point.
- **Trade vs non-trade / blended vs OPC mix** — Not separate legal segments but the split management manages the business by — it deliberately concentrates on dealer (trade) sales of blended, value-added, premium-branded cement and has de-focused OPC and industrial sales, which helped in FY25–FY26 but hurt in Q1 FY27 when price increases came through in non-trade and OPC instead.
- **Wider MP Birla Group activities** — Mentioned as context rather than as part of the listed company's earnings — other group companies in cables and guar gum, plus social-sector institutions such as Bombay Hospital, Belle Vue Clinic, South Point School and the Birla Planetarium.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

**Capital Allocation — Good (+0.60)** · 5 of 6 checks had evidence

  > "if they make money, that money invested in brand building will give them far more lasting you know benefits than short-term price cuts" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance) — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: Large, clearly growth-oriented capex programme (INR4,000-4,500cr taking capacity 20→27.5mt by FY29, Kundanganj Line 3 commissioned, Maihar Line II, Prayagraj/Gaya grinding units, WHRS, captive Bikram coal block) executed without over-announcing, though returns are diluted by soft Central India pricing and an FY26 volume growth of only 4%.
  > "we have got about INR4,000 crores, INR4,500 crores capex plan undergoing at this juncture" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Merger and acquisition — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: The one major deal (Reliance Cement, 2016) was a complementary bolt-on whose plants were ramped from low utilisation to near-max and whose brand was successfully repositioned upward, and management has since stayed away from further acquisitions.
  > "we are not really looking at acquisitions, expansions" — *management, on an earnings call*
