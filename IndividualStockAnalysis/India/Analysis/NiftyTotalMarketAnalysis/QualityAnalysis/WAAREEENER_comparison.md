**Checks that regressed:**

- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Weak. Now: The technology position is fast-follower — standard TopCon/G12 cell formats and automation scale — with no evidence in these calls of proprietary, multi-product technical differentiation or R&D intensity. | The long view had said: Waaree runs current-generation technology (TOPCon, G12/G12R, HJT, BIPV/flexible) but concedes the technology wave turns every three years and equipment is depreciated over three years — it follows the cycle rather than owning a proprietary edge.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Tier-1 status and the Waaree retail brand give some standing in the customer's mind, but modules remain a substitutable product with no aftermarket or services lock-in described. | The long view had said: Waaree holds a strong position in the Indian installer's mind — 50+ certifications, largest cell plant, and retail counters that pay a per-panel premium — though it is not a monopoly and lacks a captive aftermarket.

Now: A rapid burst of five-plus acquisitions in twelve months (Racemosa smart meters 76%, Kotsons transformers 64%, Meyer Burger's distressed US assets, United Solar Oman, Associated Power Structures 55% for INR1,225 crores) that push into new, unrelated segments rather than small complementary bolt-ons of already-strong businesses, with no post-deal margin evidence yet. | The long view had said: Deals are individually small and complementary and some are cheap (1 GW Arizona HJT line for ~$20mn, below its own greenfield cost), but they are numerous, rapid and push into unrelated segments (transformers, smart meters, T&D, polysilicon) with an acquiree like Meyer Burger being a distressed asset, so post-deal OPM benefit is unproven.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Neutral → Weak. Now: Leverage is genuinely prudent and capex is demand-linked, but the year featured multiple sizeable acquisitions and simultaneous diversification into batteries, transformers, hydrogen, glass, meters and T&D — the opposite of organic focus and no-big-M&A. | The long view had said: Leverage discipline is real and consistently claimed (debt/equity below one through heavy capex, 'book and build'), but this is offset by five-plus acquisitions in two quarters and simultaneous entry into eight new product lines — the opposite of organic focus.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: Bankability and certainty of supply are central to the pitch — 39 quarters of Tier-1 status, EPD certification, EcoVadis gold, and a fully traceable non-Chinese polysilicon chain that customers require. | The long view had said: The core customer purchase is bankability and 25-year reliability, built on 50+ certifications and multi-year field validation that management explicitly frames as an entry barrier competitors cannot replicate.

Now: A genuine distribution/accessibility edge in Indian retail solar that is being explicitly reused to push adjacent products such as inverters, alongside online expansion. | The long view had said: A 370+ store franchise network grown into 'India's deepest solar retail engine' delivering INR5,515 crores (20.8% of revenue, +84%) gives direct customer access rivals lack, and the same channel now carries inverters and storage at no incremental go-to-market cost.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Excellent → Good. Now: Reach that rivals struggle to match — a retail/B2C franchise generating INR5,515 crores, EPC and O&M through WRTL, and now local US manufacturing for local supply — though service-criticality is only moderate in modules. | The long view had said: The franchise/counter network is the one asset management itself judges unreplicable, and it is validated by 20.8% of revenue at premium realisations plus its reuse as the channel for adjacent products.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 3 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Forward Integrators**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Waaree is clearly moving toward the customer — retail/B2C at INR5,515 crores (+84%, ~20.8% of FY26 revenue), EPC at 11.6%, in-house O&M, online sales and its own PPAs/connectivity — but no own-store count, franchise royalties or D2C logistics economics are given in these calls. (The long view had said: Consistent across all four calls: franchisee/channel-partner store network (370+ in Nov 2024) built into 'India's deepest solar retail engine', with retail revenue of INR5,515 crores in FY26, +84% year-on-year and ~21% of sales, plus own US manufacturing and direct servicing of the US market; management calls the channel a distribution asset competitors cannot convert.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Growth (revenue +70%, +118.8%, then +84% FY26; production +77%) is far ahead of industry capacity additions and management claims global rank ('largest non-Chinese module manufacturer in the world', first Indian maker past 1 GW/month) with US footprint expanding to 4.2 GW, yet no explicit market-share percentages or named-competitor comparisons are provided. (The long view had said: Module output rose 4.8GW (FY24) to 12.6GW (FY26, +77%), capacity 12GW to ~26GW, order book INR40,000cr to INR53,000cr, and Waaree claims to be the largest non-Chinese module maker globally while holding ROCE at 32–42% — share gained with rising, not sacrificed, profitability.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Solar modules are a structurally deflationary product and management frames falling prices as the demand driver; across all four calls there is no mention of price increases, premiumisation or waiting lists — growth is explicitly volume- and capacity-led (12.6 GW produced, +77%). (The long view had said: Retail counters fetch a stated 1–1.5 cent/panel premium and exports/DCR carry 300–350bps higher spreads, with EBITDA margin expanding from 17% to 25% over FY25–FY26; but this is mix and cost management ('we are always managing Waaree at the gross margin level'), in an industry with structural price deflation and a GST-driven price cut — not repeated price increases above cost inflation.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 0 worsened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
