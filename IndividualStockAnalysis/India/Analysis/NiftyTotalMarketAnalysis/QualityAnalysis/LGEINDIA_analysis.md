# LG Electronics (LGEINDIA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

LG Electronics India sells household electronics and appliances under the LG brand — washing machines, refrigerators, air conditioners, dishwashers and televisions — mainly to Indian consumers through a wide network of distributors and retailers, and increasingly to business customers such as government infrastructure projects, hotels, corporates and IT parks that buy commercial air conditioning and large information displays. It makes most of its money selling this hardware, and is adding steadier income from services, chiefly annual maintenance contracts (AMC) sold to existing customers after the sale. It manufactures in India (two existing plants, with a third being built at Sri City, Andhra Pradesh, for a planned INR 50 billion investment, making air conditioners and compressors), with a localisation rate of 55.2% in FY26, and has recently started exporting India-made premium products to neighbouring countries, the US and Europe. In FY26 it reported revenue of INR 246.05 billion, EBITDA of INR 24.08 billion (9.8% margin) and net profit of INR 16.85 billion; it listed on the Indian stock exchanges in October 2025.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Home Appliance & Air Solution (H&A)** — The largest business by far — washing machines, refrigerators, room air conditioners and dishwashers — contributing roughly two-thirds of revenue (INR 39.48 billion of INR 61.74 billion in Q2 FY26 and INR 65.16 billion of INR 80.54 billion in Q4 FY26), with management claiming category leadership at 33.4% share in washing machines, 29.9% in refrigerators, 17.4% in room ACs and 43.2% in premium side-by-side refrigerators.
- **Home Entertainment** — Televisions plus the B2B 'information display' screens business, contributing about a third of revenue (INR 22.6 billion in Q2 FY26, 12.6% EBIT margin, the higher-margin of the two reported segments), with TV market share of 27.5% and growth skewed to premium large-screen and OLED sets, while information display was hurt by US tariffs and weak infrastructure spending.
- **Two-track product strategy: Premium and LG Essential Series** — Not a separate segment but how management splits its product range — a premium line (OLED TVs, French-door and side-by-side refrigerators, 5-star ACs, AI front-load washers) that drives higher selling prices and margins, and the newly launched 'Essential Series' aimed at first-time and value buyers in under-penetrated regional markets.
- **AMC (annual maintenance contracts) / non-hardware business** — Service contracts sold to existing consumers after the point of sale, described as a small but fast-growing, high-margin recurring revenue stream that management is building out with a dedicated AMC organisation; no revenue figure is given in the calls.
- **B2B business** — Sales to institutions rather than households — commercial air conditioning for IT parks, commercial real estate and institutional buyers, plus government infrastructure, corporate and hospitality orders, and an ambition to serve data centre cooling; called a growing contributor to the FY26 revenue mix, again without a disclosed figure.
- **Exports / 'Make India Global'** — Shipping India-made products abroad — large-capacity refrigerators to advanced markets and Essential Series to neighbouring countries — currently small but the top priority of the FY27 'EXCEL' plan, with management aiming to roughly double export value helped by lower India-US tariffs and the India-EU trade agreement.
- **Manufacturing / localisation ('Make in India')** — In-house production including compressors, run from two existing plants plus the new Sri City facility (compressors due in Q3 FY27, room ACs in Q4 FY27), with localisation at 55.2% and a target of rising 1-2 percentage points a year to cut costs and reduce exposure to a weakening rupee.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

LG Electronics India comes out of these three reviews as a genuinely good business that is not a hidden one. The quality framework scores it best where it earns its money: every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.80 of sales, maintenance capital spending is only 2-2.5% of sales on ₹246bn of revenue, and every ₹100 of capital in the business threw off ₹30 of operating cash this year. Only about 30 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. Sales have compounded at roughly 8% a year with unusual steadiness, into an end market that management describes on every call as expanding on low household penetration, urbanisation and rising incomes. The balance sheet matches the story — no acquisitions, no debt, ₹44.8bn of cash, and the entire ₹50bn Sri City expansion paid for out of the company's own earnings while demand was weak. Management also talks straight: it named the Q3 shortfall against its own internal targets and broke the Q4 margin decline into its parts (1.1% from channel promotion, about 1% from currency, 0.2% from e-waste rules). The combined rating of 61 out of 100 — 'Decent', three stars — reflects business quality of 75 pulled down by a multibagger fit of only 34, and the single clearest reason for that gap is the 'security by obscurity' check scoring -2: this is the category leader in every major segment, the most-subscribed Indian IPO since 2008 with ₹4.4 lakh crore of bids, and a company under heavy analyst and government attention. Nothing here is undiscovered.

The patterns and the risks are largely the same facts read from two directions, and that is the most important thing to understand about this company. The distribution and service network is the standout structural advantage in the quality work, and it is what powers the 'Market Share Gainers' pattern — refrigerators at 29.9% (up 1.0 point), room air conditioners at 17.4% (up 0.5), televisions at 27.5% (up 1.4), side-by-side refrigerators at 43.2% (up 4.5), all held through a demand downturn. But holding that network is exactly what shows up as the 'New Entrants' risk at ELEVATED: the same calls describe incremental festive go-to-market spending, channel promotion investments that cost about 1.1% of the Q4 margin, and temporary credit support that lengthened payment days to trade partners. Share is being bought as well as earned. The same doubling applies to cost. Deep backward integration into compressors, heat exchangers, PCBs and panels up to 86 inches, plus localisation rising from 45.1% in FY22 to 55.2% in FY26, is what makes the 'Low Price Plus' pattern likely and what creates real 'Cost to Replicate' barriers — yet the risk review notes those are capital barriers a well-funded rival could clear, and the sub-₹20,000 Essential range exists precisely because 'Good Enough Goods' is rated ELEVATED and cheaper brands attack this terrain. Brand does the same double duty: leadership across categories, 60-63% of OLED and 43% of side-by-side, sustained advertising including Cricket World Cup marketing, and successful moves into dishwashers make 'Brand Strength' a likely fit — and that brand is also the main thing standing between the company and the good-enough attackers.

Where the record is thinner, the assessments are honest about it, and that is why several patterns land at PARTIAL rather than full marks. Price increases are frequent and led (2-3% on washers and refrigerators in November 2025, 7-10% on air conditioners for the new efficiency ratings, 12-13% cumulatively on aircons, with competitors following), but the review reads these as defensive pass-throughs of cost and currency rather than true pricing power, since margins still moved against the company. The annual maintenance contract business is called out in all three calls as a deliberate high-margin recurring stream with its own organisation created in Q3 FY26, but no attach rates or renewal rates are disclosed, so it stays a promise rather than proven. Product launches are constant and revenue-relevant, and the company won the National Energy Conservation Award, but no research spending figure is given. Three patterns do not apply at all — the dealers are ordinary retail distribution rather than expert prescribers, the products are the customer's purchase rather than a small critical input, and nothing in the calls supports forward integration. On the risk side, resilience is judged SOUND, with technology change, shifting consumer taste and fashion all rated LOW because demand is functional and the preference shifts named on the calls (premium, larger screens, energy efficiency, dishwashers) run in the company's favour. The live pressures are the ones that move quarterly numbers rather than break the business: weather-driven seasonality that swung EBITDA margin from 12.4% to 8.9% in Q2 and 7.7% to 4.8% in Q3 before rebounding to 11.7% in Q4; policy that repeatedly moves the P&L through the GST rate cut, efficiency-rating resets, e-waste targets rising from 60% to 70% and the new Labour Code; and a structural dependence on the Korean parent for brand, technology and royalty arrangements covered by the tax agreement. The debt-free balance sheet and internally funded expansion are what let the company invest through those swings rather than be forced by them — which is the practical link between the capital allocation strength and the cyclicality risk.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

**Overall:** the company partly passes the foundation every multibagger shares (steady cash + high returns on capital + growth), 2 of 3 testable checks passing, and fits 3 of the 11 patterns: Low Price Plus, Brand Strength, Market Share Gainers.

### Low Price Plus — LIKELY FIT
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and the only computable check supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** The Essential series is deliberately aggressive on price (sub-INR 20,000 washing machines/refrigerators) and management runs a genuine cost programme — localisation from 45.1% in FY22 to 55.2% in FY26, in-house compressors, global procurement scale — but returns are mid-single-digit PAT margins with moderating ROE/ROCE, so the low-price-high-turns engine is not yet demonstrated.

> "these prices, we have very aggressively priced these products, and we have given a very compelling reason to entry-level customers to upgrade" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹30 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 1.8× a year with 36% returns on capital — thin margins made up on volume, the low-price-winner profile.

### Brand Strength — LIKELY FIT
*"The name itself sells"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern, but the numbers push back (0 of 2 checks supporting) — held one step below a full confirmation.

**What the calls show:** Across all three calls brand is the stated reason for resilience — leadership in every major category, 60-63% OLED share, 43% side-by-side refrigerator share, plus sustained A&P (Cricket World Cup marketing) and successful stretch into new categories (dishwashers doubling, chest freezers) under the same name.

> "We are proud of the consistent trust that Indian consumers and trade partners place in our brand and we remain firmly committed to protect and grow this leadership in every category." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹30 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — limited pricing headroom.
- ❌ Operating margin of 10% — too thin for a protected franchise.

### Market Share Gainers — LIKELY FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern, but the numbers push back (0 of 1 check supporting) — held one step below a full confirmation.

**What the calls show:** Explicit, quantified share gains are reported every quarter across categories — refrigerators 29.9% (+1.0%), RAC 17.4% (+0.5%), TV 27.5% (+1.4%), side-by-side 43.2% (+4.5%) — and were sustained through a demand downturn; the caveat is that part of the gain was bought with festive/channel promotion spend that compressed margins.

> "In refrigerator, we stood at 29.9%, an increase of 1% compared to YTD September '24" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Sales compounding at 8% a year — below the 12% pace the share-gainer pattern looks for.

### Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) — PARTIAL
*"Sells once, earns forever"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (2 of 3 checks supporting).

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are New Entrants, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.1× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 65% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 33% to 45% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### New Entrants — ELEVATED
*"A new player changes the rules"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Management repeatedly frames spending as competitive response — 'incremental festive go-to-market investments', channel promotion investments costing ~1.1% of Q4 margin, and 'temporary credit support' extending payment days to trade partners — and defends price positioning against unnamed peers, while itself launching the Essential series at sub-INR20,000 to defend the value zone against cheaper Chinese and Indian brands. No specific new-format entrant is named or discussed defensively in these calls.

> "The single largest contributor was our channel promotion investments, which impacted margins by approximately 1.1%" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: The company is building its own value-segment format (LG Essential) pre-emptively rather than reacting, and gained share in every major category through the pressure period*

- ⚠️ Operating margin drifted down from about 14% to 11% — steady profit erosion, the mark of rising competitive pressure.
- ⬜ Insufficient sales history.

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Home appliances are exactly the terrain where cheaper good-enough brands attack, and LG's own actions show the pressure: heavy channel promotion and go-to-market investments, careful language about keeping 'price positioning remain competitive', and the launch of an aggressively priced sub-INR20,000 Essential range to defend the entry tier. Offsetting this, brand awareness is high (consumers choose LG directly, no intermediary controls the sale), share rose in every category through the downturn, and price hikes of 2-3% on appliances and 7-13% on ACs stuck with competitors following.

> "we were the first one to take this bold decision and others also followed us. And this is why our competitive price positioning has not changed in the market" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Strong end-consumer brand awareness and category leadership (33% washing machines, 30% refrigerators, ~17% AC, 27.5% TV); price leadership that peers follow; scale-based cost protection via in-house compressors, heat exchangers and PCBs plus 55.2% localization — advantages a sub-scale good-enough challenger cannot replicate*

- ✅ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs held near 31% (was 34%) — no gross-level price erosion.
- ✅ Operating margin (10%) remains near its historical peak (16%) — no collapse in the premium.
- ⚠️ Operating margin drifted down from about 14% to 11% — steady profit erosion, the mark of rising competitive pressure.

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Demand is weather- and season-driven rather than customer-capex driven — a cooler summer, early monsoon and post-Diwali softness swung H1 FY26 revenue flat and cut EBITDA margin from 12.4% to 8.9% (Q2) and 7.7% to 4.8% (Q3), before Q4 rebounded to 11.7% on a hot summer; management itself notes 55% of AC business lands in Q4. This is seasonal/consumer-discretionary volatility in a flow-type replacement-and-penetration market, not a commodity or customer-capex cycle, and commodity exposure runs through input costs (copper, aluminium, FX) rather than selling prices.

> "Q3 is traditionally the smallest quarter in our sales cycle, amounting is around 16% to 17% of our total revenue, and the revenue softness during this period had a direct impact on operating leverage" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Consumer replacement/penetration-led demand rather than customer capex; countercyclical INR5,000 crore Sri City investment funded from internal accruals while demand was weak; growing recurring AMC and B2B revenue streams smoothing the mix*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 6 years with swings of ±8 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 10% and 16% (±2.9 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 2% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 5%).

## What to watch

- Whether the channel promotion and trade credit support flagged under the New Entrants risk keeps growing — if the 1.1% Q4 margin cost repeats or rises, the Market Share Gainers pattern is being paid for rather than won
- Whether the AMC business ever discloses attach or renewal rates — that is the single missing fact holding the Recurring Revenue pattern at PARTIAL, and proof would add a genuinely different, higher-margin income stream
- Whether price increases start sticking as margin gains rather than passing through cost and currency — that would move Pricing Power from PARTIAL to a real fit, and would also blunt the ELEVATED Good Enough Goods risk
- Whether margins recover to the earlier level after the seasonal swing under the Cyclicality risk (12.4% to 8.9%, 7.7% to 4.8%, then 11.7%), or whether the low points become the new normal, which would undercut the Return on Capital and barriers-to-entry checks
- The parent relationship under Stakeholder Concentration — any change to the brand, technology or royalty arrangements covered by the tax agreement would hit the Brand Strength pattern and the Cost to Replicate barriers at the same time
