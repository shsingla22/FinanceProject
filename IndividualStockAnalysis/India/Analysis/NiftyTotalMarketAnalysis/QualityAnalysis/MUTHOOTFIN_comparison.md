**Checks that improved:**

- **Candor** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Management quantifies the one-off components of yield each quarter when challenged (INR300 crores, INR500 crores, INR85 crores) rather than letting headline yields stand, though the CFO could not supply disbursement figures after that slide was dropped from the presentation. | The long view had said: Management candidly volunteered the loss of ~15 lakh small-ticket customers and explained NPA forbearance in detail, but repeatedly deflected on opex growth, disbursement numbers and yield composition.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: Headline results are substantially cycle-flattered — record gold prices inflating ticket sizes and enabling NPA liquidations — so the 95% PAT growth overstates managerial skill, even though branch execution and the Muthoot Money turnaround are genuine. | The long view had said: The spectacular FY26 optics (PAT +95%) are substantially exogenous and one-off — record gold prices, INR500cr of legacy-NPA interest write-backs, auction and ARC income — so results overstate incremental managerial skill.
- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: The end market is in genuine secular expansion — formalisation of an estimated 25,000-30,000 tonnes of idle household gold, supportive RBI rules and tighter unsecured-credit norms redirecting borrowers to gold loans. | The long view had said: Formalisation of gold-backed credit is a genuine secular trend, supported by 25,000–30,000 tons of idle household gold and an increasingly supportive regulator removing branch-licensing constraints.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Growth is entirely organic with no acquisitions, collateral discipline is conservative (average LTV 57% versus 75% permitted, ornaments only, no bullion), and surplus capital is used to strengthen subsidiaries. | The long view had said: Consistently organic, conservative guidance, no large M&A, capital supplied to its own 100% subsidiary, and prudent collateral discipline (actual portfolio LTV 57% against a permitted 75%).
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Gold loans carry minimal switching costs — a borrower can redeem and re-pledge elsewhere — and there is no captive aftermarket; stickiness rests only on branch proximity and habit. | The long view had said: Switching costs are minimal — the book churns roughly every four months and the company has already lost lakhs of small-ticket borrowers to rivals — so there is no captive lock-in.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: New entry is actively accelerating and is well-capitalised, with an analyst noting AAA-rated deep-pocketed NBFCs entering at finer funding costs and a peer signalling rate cuts; management's only defence is operational intensity. | The long view had said: Operational intensity, in-house appraisal and security infrastructure are real barriers that have repeatedly driven entrants out, yet the sector has seen recurring discount waves with rates offered as low as 9% and continual new entry.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Clear share capture — 50-54% gold-loan AUM growth far above industry, INR57,000 crores added in FY26 and 4.24 lakh new customers a quarter — but tempered by already being the dominant specialist player. | The long view had said: Clear runway (banks hold ~INR13 lakh crore of gold loans vs ~INR3 lakh crore for all NBFCs, and 25,000–30,000 tons of household gold is unpledged) with demonstrated capture — 50% gold loan AUM growth in FY26.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Weak → Poor. Now: The opposite of obscure — a INR1 trillion market cap, heavy sell-side coverage and management's own description of gold loans as the flavour of the market, drawing in capital and competitors. | The long view had said: The opposite applies — a mass-advertised household brand, an upper-layer NBFC under close RBI scrutiny, covered by a large analyst pack and attracting deep-pocketed new capital into its niche.
- **Advertisement and promotion** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. Now: No A&P spend is disclosed, but the brand outcome is measurable and durable — 10 consecutive years as India's most trusted financial services brand, repeatedly cited by management as a growth driver. | The long view had said: Sustained and rising A&P — the 'Put Your Goal To Work' campaign in FY23 through a deliberately growing ad budget in FY26 — has translated into measurable brand equity and volume growth.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Employer-quality signal is real (Great Place to Work for a 5th year) but every executive seat on the call is held by a family member, with no evidence of a non-family leadership pipeline. | The long view had said: In-house appraiser training, a 1,000-strong audit cadre over 5,000 branches, 'Great Place to Work' certification five years running and low attrition despite competitor poaching, though the home-finance CEO was hired externally.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: The gold-loan market is fragmented and opening further, with banks, multiple NBFCs and unorganised lenders competing, so it is not a stable oligopoly. | The long view had said: The focused gold-NBFC space is concentrated and retail customers have no negotiating power, but banks dominate the wider market and a fresh wave of AAA-rated entrants undermines structural stability.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Reported yield expansion is repeatedly explained by one-off NPA recovery and ARC interest rather than pricing, with core yield admitted flat at ~18-18.5%, and AUM growth driven mainly by higher gold collateral value while tonnage softened. | The long view had said: Recent growth is led by price and mix — a 50–100bp rate hike, yields rising 20.34%→20.76%, and a deliberate shift into higher-ticket loans — while tonnage and customer counts actually declined, which is favourable on this parameter but signals volume weakness.

*Unchanged: Customer types (retail vs corporate); Mini-monopolies; Merger and acquisition; Out of limelight; Geographical expansion; Cyclical market growth; Asset turn; Distribution to shareholders; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Distribution advantage; Intangible benefits; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Convenience benefits; Share donators; Assurance benefits.*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 1 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
