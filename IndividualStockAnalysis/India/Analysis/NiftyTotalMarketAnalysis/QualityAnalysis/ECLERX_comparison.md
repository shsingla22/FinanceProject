**Checks that improved:**

- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Delivery-footprint expansion is credible and well-executed — Lima and Cairo went live within the year, Cairo hit top-quartile performance four months into production and already has multiple clients signed, with Manila and Fayetteville next. | The long view had said: Delivery-geography expansion is credible and already converting — Cairo signed by existing clients and going live, with Manila, Fayetteville and Peru in motion — though this is cost/capacity diversification rather than a premium brand travelling into new demand markets.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Clear share capture from a small base against a weak macro — 18% USD growth, top-10 concentration falling from 63-64% to 59%, non-top-10 outgrowing top-10 every quarter, and a rising count of $1m/$3m/$5m clients. | The long view had said: eClerx is a sub-USD-500 million player in a vast outsourcing market and is demonstrably capturing share — 17.9% FY26 USD growth against a flat industry, with top-10 concentration falling 63–64% to 59% as non-top-10 clients and USD 1m/3m/5m client counts rise.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Good → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹26 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±1.4 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹26 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±4.8 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: Growth is substantially volume/headcount-led (bottom-of-pyramid hiring, seat additions, higher headcount pressuring Q4 margin) with no pricing power cited; the offsetting positive is genuine mix improvement toward higher-value Analytics & Automation and longer-term deals. | The long view had said: Growth is explicitly volume/headcount-led rather than price- or mix-led, with management confirming headcount is the leading indicator of revenue because 80% of work is FTE-based, and onshore mix additions actually carry lower margins.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Systematic, large-scale capability building — 8,000 employees (40% of headcount) upskilled on GenAI with TU Munich, 3,000+ trained on agentic AI and vibe coding, firm-wide Copilot rollout, GPTW certification and a third consecutive talent-development award — alongside attrition below historical levels. | The long view had said: Systematic large-scale reskilling (3,000+ employees trained on Agentic AI and vibe coding), repeat talent-development awards, and attrition falling from ~39–45% in FY23 to ~21%, offset by the fact that the senior leadership layer was bought in externally rather than promoted.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Good. Now: A multi-continent delivery and service network (India, Egypt, Peru, Philippines, US) with 90+ clients above $0.5m and expanding sales capability gives useful reach where service execution is decisive, though rivals of scale can match it. | The long view had said: Delivery reach is broadening usefully (India, Cairo, Manila, Peru, Fayetteville) and the Adobe Gold partnership adds a channel, but this footprint is readily matched by larger BPO rivals and is not a reach competitors cannot replicate.
- **Candor** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Consistently candid: unpacked the weak Q1 cash flow into DSO, gratuity and bonus drivers, attributed most of Q2's margin beat to FX and pre-warned Q3 and Q4 softness, disclosed the offshore-call NPRM risk, and switched to the operating-EBITDA metric investors asked for. | The long view had said: Communication is clear and pre-emptive — they flagged a softer Q4 a quarter in advance and it materialised, disclosed the Personiv earnout overshoot and expense treatment openly, and adopted an operating-EBITDA metric analysts asked for — though explicit ownership of mistakes is rare.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Good → Weak. Now: No monopoly-like hold on the customer: roughly 15-20% of the top line rolls off every year, indicating work is recompeted rather than owned. | The long view had said: Pockets of mind-share monopoly exist — 'pole position' on Agentic AI at certain clients and entrenched trade-lifecycle/KYC processes — but this is client-by-client incumbency, not a category-wide monopoly.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Some switching-cost stickiness exists — tenured relationships, an AI-native KYC orchestrator embedded across multiple client systems, and steady upsell/cross-sell — but it is offset by persistent double-digit roll-offs and no captive aftermarket. | The long view had said: Real switching costs exist through embedded, largely non-discretionary FTE operations with very long-tenured Fortune 500 clients, but the annual 15–20% roll-off shows the lock-in is partial rather than captive.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Headline results are flattered by factors outside management skill — 200 of the 270bps Q2 margin expansion came from FX and growth leans on headcount addition — so the record year should not be read as pure execution alpha, even though client diversification and new-centre ramps are genuine skill signals. | The long view had said: The assessment survives halo-stripping because the sequence was strategy-stated-first then delivered, and management attributes weakness honestly to exogenous factors (China luxury, geopolitics) rather than claiming credit for everything.

Now: A coherent package was executed — INR 300cr buyback completed with 625,000 shares extinguished, promoters abstaining, plus a 1:1 bonus and INR 1 dividend — but the buyback was priced at INR 4,500 versus a market price near INR 4,400 after a strong run, i.e. not at a depressed valuation. | The long view had said: A consistent, repeated buyback policy (INR 3,850 million in July 2024, another completed January 2026 with 625,000 shares extinguished) plus bonus issues in 2022 and 2026 and a token dividend shows a coherent distribution habit, though no evidence buybacks are timed to low valuations.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 3 weakened, 7 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Four consecutive quarters of 16-20% constant-currency year-on-year growth in a weak IT-services macro, with FY26 at $469m (+17.9% USD), deal wins stepping up ($32m→$46m→$45m→$46m), rising $1m/$3m/$5m client cohorts and top-10 concentration falling from 63-64% to 59% — and the share is not bought with profitability, since EBITDA rose 29% and full-year margin expanded 132bps. No explicit market-share percentages are given, but growth is well above sector and geographically broadening (Lima, Cairo, Manila). (The long view had said: Persistent, broad-based share gain across the record: FY26 revenue up 17.9% in USD against a fashion/luxury industry guided to low-single-digit growth, top-10 concentration falling 63-64% to 59%, a steadily rising count of $1m/$3m/$5m clients, ACV rising each year, and international expansion with local delivery (Cairo, Manila, Peru, Fayetteville) — gained through cross-sell and new logos, not discounting, with EBITDA margin held in-band and PAT up 30%.)

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: There is real B2B reputational equity — Personiv recall among SMBs, Market360 named in the Gartner Market Guide for Digital Shelf Analytics, Everest Leader/Star Performer placements, GPTW — but no heritage story, no sustained A&P spend disclosure, and no evidence brand commands a price premium; recognition supports win rates, not pricing. (The long view had said: Some brand equity exists — Personiv's recall with SMBs, 25 years of delivery, and analyst/award validation (Gartner Market Guide, Everest Group, Chartis) — but there is no heritage premium, no A&P leverage, and no evidence the name commands price; recognition is used as credibility in B2B sales rather than pricing power.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: All four calls describe direct enterprise selling — deal wins of $32-46m per quarter, own S&D at 12-14% of revenue, onshore consulting and new-logo acquisition — with no prescriber, installer or professional channel choosing on the end-customer's behalf; the Adobe Gold partner status is an ecosystem badge, not an expert intermediary who specifies the product. (The long view had said: No evidence either way — sales are direct enterprise relationships with client procurement/business heads; the only third-party channel mentioned is the Adobe Solution Partner programme, which is a technology alliance rather than a professional intermediary prescribing to a paying end customer.)
- **Forward Integrators**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The business is already direct B2B enterprise services with no intermediated channel to integrate through — no owned stores, franchising, licensing or D2C; the capex discussed is delivery seats (750 in Airoli, 400 in Pune, 400 in Chandigarh) and computing equipment, which is delivery capacity, not a move toward the end customer. (The long view had said: Silent — eClerx is a B2B services provider with no owned stores, franchising, licensing or D2C channel; delivery-centre expansion (Egypt, Peru, Manila) is capacity, not forward integration toward an end consumer.)

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 2 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Top 10 clients still account for 59% of revenue and management discloses annual roll-off of 15–20% of the top line, so a single relationship loss is material; however, concentration fell from 63–64% to 59% over FY26 and non-top-10 growth outpaced top-10 growth in Q1, Q2 and Q3. (The long view had said: Top-10 clients have accounted for 59-64% of revenue across the entire four-year window (60% in Aug 2022, 62-64% mid-period, 59% by Q4FY26), and analysts repeatedly probe single-client effects — one European luxury client's distress was visible in reported numbers, and Kapil declined to comment on it. A concentration of this magnitude means one relationship loss materially resets the P&L.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Much of the delivery book is outsourced operations where clients are explicitly buying cost reduction, which invites cheaper substitutes and in-house GCCs; management's answer is to move up into productised, differentiated offerings rather than defend price on the base work. (The long view had said: Outsourced operations is a scale, not a niche, business: onshore/incremental work is admitted to carry lower margins, ~15-20% of revenue rolls off annually, and the margin band has been held down to 24-28% versus the 28-32% guided in FY23 — consistent with price pressure from cheaper delivery alternatives. Management defends the premium on domain plus owned IP rather than on any structural barrier.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The business is labour-linked outsourced operations, and agentic AI is discussed in every FY26 call as the axis on which the model is being rebuilt — 8,000 employees GenAI-upskilled, a targeted 25% coding productivity gain, agentic pilots "with all clients across the board" — meaning the same technology that wins deals also compresses the FTE-based revenue base. (The long view had said: The core economics are headcount-arbitrage — Srini confirms 'given that 80% of our work continues to be FTE' — while Agentic AI is being pushed by clients into exactly those processes; analysts ask repeatedly about cannibalization and AI-driven deflation, and Kapil concedes 'there isn't a history to say what the outlook would be in 12, 24, 36 months.' Management is building its own platforms and winning agentic deals, but by May 2026 admits AI revenue 'in terms of what percentage… that's still small' relative to the FTE base it could displace.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (WATCH); New Entrants (QUANT FLAG); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
