# Sonata Software Ltd. (SONATSOFTW) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: IT*

Sonata Software is an India-headquartered IT services company that builds, modernizes and runs software, data and AI systems for large corporate clients, which management calls "modernization engineering" powered by its own Platformation framework, proprietary "lightning" tools and IP. It runs two quite different businesses: an international services arm that bills clients (mostly in North America, plus UK/Europe, India and Australia) for project and multi-year engagements delivered by roughly 6,300 employees from India and offshore/nearshore centres, and a domestic India business that resells and supports software and cloud subscriptions, largely tied to Microsoft. Revenue comes from services contracts — large multi-year deals, new logos and expansion of existing accounts, tracked via order book and book-to-bill — plus low-margin product/licence resale in India. In FY2026 consolidated revenue was about Rs.10,701 crore with PAT of Rs.464 crore; the international arm did $328.4 million (Rs.2,948 crore) and the domestic arm Rs.7,772 crore of revenue but only Rs.289 crore of gross contribution, so the two arms earn roughly comparable profits despite very different sales figures.

**The parts of the business:**

- **International Services** — The core IT services business — modernizing clients' legacy platforms and building digital, cloud, data and AI systems under multi-year contracts; it earned $328.4 million (Rs.2,948 crore) in FY2026 with about 20% EBITDA margins and Rs.293 crore PAT, and is the growth engine management talks about most, with North America now over 70% of its revenue and the top 10 clients about 54%.
- **Domestic business (Sonata Information Technology Limited, SITL)** — The India-facing arm that resells and supports software licences and cloud subscriptions (heavily Microsoft-linked) — it books huge revenue, Rs.7,772 crore in FY2026, but management judges it on "gross contribution" of only Rs.289 crore because the resale pass-through is low margin; it still contributed Rs.172 crore of profit and is named as one of the company's four strategic pillars.
- **AI and agentic AI offerings (AgentBridge, Harmoni, GenAI programs)** — Selling AI-led modernization work — rebuilding legacy applications with AI, agentic AI platforms and Microsoft AI co-sell/consumption deals; management says AI-led work was $49 million of FY2026 order book (about 18% of the total, up from 10% a year earlier) with a $280 million AI pipeline across 100-plus clients, and it is also used internally to lift delivery productivity.
- **Cloud and data (including Microsoft Fabric)** — Building clients' data platforms, analytics and cloud migrations, with Sonata positioned as an official Microsoft Fabric partner and hundreds of data engineers; cloud-and-data opportunities are described as roughly half the total pipeline, and cloud/data rose from 49% to 59% of international revenue during FY2024.
- **Microsoft Dynamics and Microsoft platform services** — Implementing and modernizing Microsoft Dynamics ERP, SaaS transitions and low-code/no-code work as a long-standing Microsoft partner (Microsoft Inner Circle partner) — repeatedly cited as a key revenue driver and a leadership position the company defends.
- **Focus verticals (Healthcare & Life Sciences, BFSI, Retail/Manufacturing & Distribution, Technology/Media/Telecom)** — The four industries the company deliberately concentrates on, with the heaviest investment in Healthcare & Life Sciences and BFSI — together those two grew from about 13% of revenue three years ago to roughly 30-31% by FY2026.
- **Acquisitions (Quant, Encore, GBW, Scalable)** — Bolt-on companies bought to add capability, clients and geographies, which management says over-performed and were folded into a single Sonata entity and way of working during FY2024; M&A is one of the stated levers for diversifying the client base.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Sonata Software earns its living without needing much capital, and that is the clearest thing the three reviews agree on. Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹2.15 of sales, the business generates ₹20 of operating cash for every ₹100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings, and reported returns are high (RONW 30-32%, domestic ROCE 43%). Customers effectively fund the operation — cash comes back 19 days before suppliers need to be paid, a genuinely rare strength. Sales have compounded at about 18% a year with unusually steady year-to-year growth, helped by adding four delivery geographies in FY24, scaling North America from roughly 54% to over 70% of revenue, and shifting the mix toward banking and healthcare clients (13% to 31% of revenue in three years). Bolt-on acquisitions (Quant, Encore, GBW, Scalable) are described by management as over-delivering. Capital allocation and return on capital both score full marks in the quality framework, and a formalised dividend policy — quarterly interims plus a final — was maintained straight through the FY26 downturn, though there is no evidence of buybacks when the shares were cheap. The catch is that none of this rests on anything hard to copy: the competitive-advantage area scores only 0.33 and industry structure is negative, and the 'Cost to Replicate' pattern is rejected outright because the stated advantages — a Microsoft partnership, certified people and AI tooling — are available to any competitor with money to spend.

The single trait that most defines Sonata is also its biggest exposure: it sells largely through Microsoft rather than directly to end customers. That relationship is what makes the 'Friendly Middleman' pattern a partial fit — Inner Circle partner, Fabric launch and feature partner, co-selling through Microsoft's programme, with its own sales dollars aligned to Microsoft's annual plan. The same arrangement is what makes the 'Good Enough Goods' risk elevated: the domestic licence-reselling business is a low-value-add position the vendor can simply step around, and it did — an OEM partner took large customers direct, and gross contribution shrank 3.5% in FY26 even as revenue grew 5.9%. Client concentration compounds this. The top ten customers have stayed at 54-55% of revenue, and three of them — the largest banking client, the largest technology-media client and a large retail client — began ramping down at the same time, which is why the concentration risk is rated elevated and why the 'Market Share Gainers' pattern is only partial: the mix shift was real, but the gains stalled and international dollar revenue actually shrank 2.1% in FY26. The same client weakness undermines 'Pricing Power' as well. Price increases were used as a margin lever, but they arrived alongside volume loss rather than in spite of it, with flat 0-1% sequential quarters, so the test of raising price while holding volume is not met. What offsets this partly is disclosure quality: management flagged the FY24 'blip', the client ramp-downs and the OEM going direct promptly and by name, reports returns every quarter, and has long-tenured leadership.

The AI shift plays both sides of the ledger in exactly the same way. Sustained spending on proprietary tools — Platformation, the lightning tools, Harmoni.AI, AgentBridge, and status as a Microsoft Fabric launch partner — has converted into visible commercial results: AI reached 18% of the order book and a $280m AI pipeline by the fourth quarter, with over 90% of the workforce AI-certified. That is what earns 'Innovation Dominance' a partial verdict, though R&D spending itself is never disclosed. The identical facts make technological innovation an elevated risk, because the same technology attacks a business model where revenue has historically tracked headcount: staff numbers fell from 6,649 to 6,283 while revenue stayed flat, margins rose on 'AI-led productivity', and the Board changed the CEO. Cost discipline shows up as a likely fit for 'Low Price Plus' — offshore mix moved from 53% to 68%, utilisation reached 91.8%, and EBITDA margin rose from 17.3% to 20.2% within FY26 — but the reviews read this as keeping the savings rather than passing them on to win volume, which is why margins improved in the same year revenue fell. The company is visibly at a cyclical low point: elongated decision cycles, discretionary client budgets under pressure, three large accounts ramping down, with an AI-modernisation recovery building in the pipeline. Toll Roads and Forward Integrators find no support at all. Netting it out, the combined rating is 57 out of 100 — 63 for business quality, 41 for multibagger fit, 66 for risk safety — a 'Decent' three-star verdict with resilience judged sound: a high-return, cash-generative, well-run operator whose economics are excellent but whose protection against competitors is thin, and whose next leg of growth depends on the AI pipeline converting before the legacy client and reselling revenue erodes further.'

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

> "For SITL, we continue to scale our business -- India business with a sharp focus on annuity business" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Customers effectively fund the business — cash arrives 19 days before suppliers are paid.
- ✅ Capital spending is about 1% of sales — growth needs little capital.
- ✅ Operating cash was positive in 11 of 12 years — the reliable cash engine recurring-revenue businesses show.

### Low Price Plus — LIKELY FIT
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and the only computable check supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** There is real cost discipline — offshore mix 53%→68%, utilization to 91.8%, pyramid optimisation and AI-led delivery efficiency lifting EBITDA from 17.3% to 20.2% within FY26 — but this is margin capture, not a low-price-plus-scale strategy; savings are kept, not passed to price, and Sonata lacks the scale the pattern requires.

> "Our offshore revenue mix improved to 68% from 63% in Q3 2026. We also benefited from pyramid optimization and price increases." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹27 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 2.1× a year with 39% returns on capital — thin margins made up on volume, the low-price-winner profile.

- ❌ Operating margin moved from about 9% (early years) to 7% (recent years) — margins are ERODING, the opposite of pricing power.

> "In FY2026 our international services dollar revenue stood at $328.4 million, a degrowth of 2.1% year-on-year" — *management, on an earnings call*
