And from the calls: Pricing behaviour is rational and orderly — mid-single-digit increases in line with the market in India and Brazil across all four quarters with no discounting — though entry barriers in generics are clearly low. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±5.2 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Repeated evidence of low barriers and irrational pricing: base-portfolio price erosion in the US made products 'non-lucrative to launch', and Brazil erosion has ranged to 90% depending on entrants.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Good → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹33 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹33 is operating profit. Now: R&D is visibly converting into first-mover launches — No.1 generic semaglutide with 38% generic share, first generic Brexpiprazole after own clinical trials, Resmetirom Phase 3, ~10 US launches and 58-65 Brazil filings — with spend reallocated rather than inflated. | The long view had said: R&D efficiency is improving but uneven: the US pipeline went stale for years while India R&D reallocation produced first-to-market oral Semaglutide (100% oral share), first generic Brexpiprazole and a Resmetirom Phase 3, plus ~58 molecules filed in Brazil.

Now: Capability is formulation and first-to-file speed rather than a deep technology edge — Torrent has no injectable GLP-1 fill-finish capacity and must partner it across every territory. | The long view had said: No deep technology edge — the US filing pipeline went stale, GLP-1 injectable capacity is entirely outsourced to partners, and new India products come from clinical-trial timing and in-licensing rather than proprietary technology.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 6 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 6. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 7 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Persistent, quantified outperformance in both branded markets: India grew 11/12/14/15% against IPM growth of 8/8/10/10%, Brazil grew 14-17% secondary against market growth of 5-7%, and semaglutide took 38% share among generic players — all achieved while EBITDA margin held at ~32.7%, so the share is not bought with profitability. (The long view had said: Persistent, quantified outperformance in every call: IPM rank went 8th to 6th, India grew 12-15% against IPM 8-10% quarter after quarter across FY23-FY26, Brazil grew 13-17% on IQVIA against a 6-12% market, and the gain is funded organically with margins rising (29.2% to 32.7%) and leverage falling, not bought.)

**That weakened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The model is the opposite of low-price-plus: 32.7-32.9% EBITDA margins held every quarter, mid-single-digit price increases in both India and Brazil, and no cost-obsession or itemised-savings language; the only cost programme discussed is one-off JB merger synergies. (The long view had said: There is genuine, itemised cost discipline (Q3FY25 broke down freight, energy and BRL savings rupee-by-rupee; Germany 'cost optimization to improve our competitiveness in the tender segment'; US 'stop the bleeding'; INR400-450cr JB synergies tracking ahead of plan), but the business is a 76% gross margin / 32.7% EBITDA branded model, not a low-price high-turns one.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 2 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: A single third-party supplier's disruption has depressed the German business in constant currency for four consecutive quarters (+1%, -5%, -6%, -1%) with management repeatedly refusing to guide and admitting it is outside their control, compounded by the structural fact that 75% of the German business is third-party sourced; an alternate supplier takes 3-4 quarters to onboard. The entire GLP-1 injectable franchise is likewise dependent on an undisclosed partner in every territory. (The long view had said: Supplier and single-relationship dependency has visibly cost revenue twice in the window: one third-party supplier's regulatory shutdown drove German constant-currency revenue down 6% for multiple quarters with management admitting no visibility and a 3-4 quarter switch time, and the single insulin CMO contract went to nil revenue for a quarter (2% of group topline, near-100% flow-through to EBITDA).)
- **Technological Innovation**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The GLP-1 shift is a large-scale therapeutic platform change and Torrent lacks the capability in-house — it has no fill-and-finish capacity for injectables and must partner across all territories, and in Germany biosimilars are now automatically substitutable and tendered, a format shift where Torrent has only just launched its first product. It is currently a winner of the shift (38% generic semaglutide share, 100% of the oral format), but on partnered, not owned, infrastructure. (The long view had said: The one large innovation wave in the period — GLP-1s — was captured rather than suffered: Torrent planned 'day one' launch from Jan-2025, and by May-2026 held 38% of generic Semaglutide share in India including 100% of the oral format, plus first-to-launch Brexpiprazole and a Phase-3 Resmetirom programme; it also launched its first biosimilar into Germany's new automatic-substitution regime rather than being displaced by it.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
