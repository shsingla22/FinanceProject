### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 7 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: FAIL on the long view, FAIL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The lender variant is partly present — cost of borrowing fell 7.69% (Q2) to 7.50% (Q3) on bank negotiations and repo cuts, asset quality is high (GNPA 0.93%, negative credit cost of 45 bps in FY26), and the in-house Infinity platform is credited with cutting operating costs — but ROE of only 12.73% and a spread compressing to 2.12% fall short of the high-returns-through-turns test, and no itemised cost culture is described. (The long view had said: Opex discipline exists (opex/ATA guided 1%–1.1% through FY26), but there is no cost-of-funds or price advantage — cost of borrowing is 7.35% versus banks, management concedes it must match rather than undercut, and the loan book historically carried 6.35% GNPA, the opposite of the cheap-funding/high-quality-loan bank variant.)

**That weakened:**

- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Sourcing does run partly through direct selling agents (Apr 2026 call notes onboarding DSAs to the Infinity platform), but the core mechanism — low end-customer price sensitivity — is explicitly contradicted: management repeatedly describes rate-sensitive borrowers and balance-transfer attrition in prime. (The long view had said: Sourcing does run partly through intermediaries — DMA/APF/DSA relationships in the Aug 2022 call and third-party sourcing alongside in-house in Jul 2024 — but the defining trait of low price sensitivity is absent: the company repeatedly discusses balance-transfer attrition and rate competition, and by Apr 2026 is fighting 'BT pressure in prime business'.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Cost to Replicate (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 6 unchanged. Financial resilience: Unknown on the long view, Unknown on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: A home loan is a largely undifferentiated product bought on rate through DSA intermediaries, and management repeatedly defends its pricing against cheaper alternatives — customers 'more sensitive to interest rate movements', Prime held flat 'in line with our strategic approach to profitable growth', and balance-transfer attrition compressing spreads — but the damage stayed bounded, with NIM inside the 3.65-3.7% guidance all year and ROA at 2.66%. (The long view had said: The product is undifferentiated and the customer buys on rate: management admits interest rates are 'almost at the same' as banks for good customers, blamed a 100-150bps differential for heavy balance-transfer runoff, and by Q4 FY26 spreads were again compressing 'due to lower incremental yield and BT pressure in prime business' with yields falling every quarter; a large share of sourcing runs through DSAs who steer the borrower. The strategic answer — moving down the pyramid to informal-income affordable/emerging — is itself an admission that the premium cannot be defended in prime.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The book is affirmatively de-concentrated: 99.6% retail across 3.6 lakh live accounts and 393 branches, with the corporate book — the one place single names mattered — shrunk to INR 272-401 crores of an INR 87,347 crore book, and the one large corporate foreclosure that moved yield by 10bps shows how small that residual exposure now is. (The long view had said: The retail book is highly granular (3.6 lakh live accounts, 393 branches), but single relationships still move the P&L on the corporate side — one large account foreclosure cost 10bps of yield, one Rs.275cr account was declared a fraud in Jan-2026, and the FY26 corporate restart was essentially one Mumbai case of ~Rs.360-370cr; funding also leans on banks and a PSU promoter whose participation needs regulatory clearance.)

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Cyclicality (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
