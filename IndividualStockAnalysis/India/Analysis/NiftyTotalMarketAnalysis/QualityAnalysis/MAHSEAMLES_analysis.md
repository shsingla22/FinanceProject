# Maharashtra Seamless (MAHSEAMLES) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Maharashtra Seamless makes steel pipes and tubes in India, mainly 'seamless' pipes (made in one piece, without a welded joint) and a smaller line of ERW pipes (made by rolling steel and welding the seam). Its main customers are oil and gas companies that use the pipes for drilling and production wells — management says roughly 70% of exposure is oil and gas, with the rest going to boiler makers and other industries; state-owned ONGC and Oil India alone have accounted for 33% to 60% of the order book. It earns money by converting steel into pipe and selling it by the tonne, with profit tracked as EBITDA per tonne (seamless has run around INR 15,000-30,000 per tonne, well above ERW), and it earns more on 'value-added' pipes such as cold drawn pipes, cylinder pipes, drill pipes and sour-service subsea pipes. It sells mostly in India plus exports (the US has been a key export market), runs plants including a Telangana unit acquired as United Seamless and later merged in, works off a short order book of only three to four months, and also earns large 'other income' from a treasury of about INR 3,115 crores of investments.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Seamless pipes** — The core business — jointless steel pipes across the full diameter range for oil and gas drilling and other industry; it generated about 84% of FY24 EBITDA, ships roughly 4 lakh tonnes a year against 5.5 lakh tonnes of usable capacity, and management calls the company the domestic market leader and the only listed player focused solely on seamless pipes.
- **ERW pipes** — The smaller welded-pipe line, earning far less per tonne than seamless (about INR 7,315 versus INR 25,793 per tonne in FY24) and contributing only around 16% of FY24 EBITDA, though management noted margins improving with a better product mix in Q3 FY26.
- **Value-added products (within seamless)** — Higher-margin specialty pipes — cold drawn pipes, cylinder pipes, drill pipes, sour-service subsea seamless pipes, and premium connections — of which the company makes four; drill pipes are a tiny national market (8,000-9,000 tonnes a year) but high margin, and premium connections are not yet made, with production expected about six months out under a royalty deal with a foreign partner.
- **United Seamless / Telangana unit** — The acquired seamless plant that management called the biggest driver of growth in FY23, with higher margins from newer equipment and value-added products; it was merged into Maharashtra Seamless, and an INR 850-odd crore capex plan centres on a Telangana finishing line to unlock 1 lakh tonnes of already-built but unusable production capacity.
- **Treasury and investments** — A large pile of cash, bonds, mutual funds and deposits — INR 3,115 crores as of September 2025 — that throws off substantial 'other income' (INR 235 crores in H1 FY26, and boosted in Q3 FY26 by gold and silver markets); management keeps it deliberately, funds all capex from it, has cleared inter-corporate deposits and corporate guarantees, and has quadrupled the dividend over a few years.
- **Drilling rig (rental)** — A rig earning rental income denominated in US dollars, mentioned mainly as a natural hedge against the company's dollar loan rather than as a growth driver.
- **Renewable / captive solar power** — One of the four segments management said it was focusing on alongside pipes; concretely it involves setting up a solar plant for the company's own use to cut power costs, part of an earlier INR 530 crore outlay.

### The story in depth

Maharashtra Seamless comes out of all three reviews as a well-run company sitting in a difficult business. The strongest marks are for the people and the balance sheet: management scores 0.8 on quality, having discharged every inter-corporate deposit and corporate guarantee to unrelated parties six months ahead of the timeline it committed to in 2022, prepaid debt, and funded capex without borrowing, while defending an explicitly long-term, survival-first capital policy against sustained shareholder pressure across multiple calls. The company is the acknowledged 35-year market leader, the only player in its industry never to go bankrupt, holds API certification and the full size range, roughly 5.5 lakh tonnes of active capacity and a net-cash treasury of INR3,115 crore. Those same facts are why the Corporate Culture and Cost to Replicate patterns register as partial rather than absent, and why the risk review calls overall resilience SOUND even while flagging cyclicality as a high risk — the cash pile is precisely what lets the company sit out a downturn. But the combined rating is 34 out of 100, a Weak one-star, because business quality (52) and risk safety (32) are dragged down by an almost complete absence of multibagger traits (10).

The reason so little translates into the patterns is that this company sells through competitive government tenders, and that single fact defeats most of the screen at once. Four patterns fail outright on it: there is no subscription, licence or installed-base revenue because production only starts after an order is won and the order book runs just three to four months (Recurring Revenue); sales go straight to ONGC, Oil India, city gas distributors and export buyers with no prescriber or trade channel in between (Friendly Middleman); there is no heritage story, advertising spend or premium pricing (Brand Strength); and price is discovered in the tender rather than set by the company (Pricing Power). The Toll Roads pattern gets partial credit for a genuinely niche, mission-critical product — drill pipes in a national market of only 8,000-9,000 tonnes, plus import-substitution items where the company claims majority share — but fails the defining test for the same tendering reason. The very same tender dependence shows up as risk: Government Dependency is rated HIGH because demand is anchored in government oil and gas spending, and Stakeholder Concentration is ELEVATED because a handful of PSU buyers drive the results, with a single INR674 crore ONGC order moving revenue and profit from one quarter into the next.

The second thread running through everything is Chinese imports, which now run at 20-25% of India's roughly 9 lakh tonne production. This shows up in the quality framework as a minus on pricing, mix and volume, where profit per tonne fell from INR25,793 in FY24 to a guided INR10,000-15,000 range by FY26; in the pattern screen as the outright failure of Pricing Power; and in the risk review as an ELEVATED New Entrants rating, with management itself conceding the existing antidumping duty is no longer prohibitive and does not cover value-added products introduced after 2016. The defence is the niche certified tail of the range, which is why Good Enough Goods sits at WATCH rather than higher, and why the in-house development programme — first-in-India subsea sour-service pipes, drill pipes, cylinder pipes, cold-drawn benches — earns a positive research mark and partial credit on Innovation Dominance. Growth, though, has nowhere obvious to come from: the end market grows only 2-3% a year on management's own account, exports collapsed from 32% of the order book in FY23 to 5-6% of dispatches by FY24 and had not revived by FY26, volumes are flat at around 4 lakh tonnes, and the INR852 crore capex plan has repeatedly slipped, with the INR184 crore Telangana line announced in May 2024 still incomplete in January 2026 and only INR90 crore of purchase orders issued. So market share is being defended rather than gained, and the same conservatism that earns high marks for stewardship also means INR3,115 crore — roughly half the market value — sits idle while management refuses buybacks or any cap on cash retention, which is why capital allocation scores negative and 153 days of cash stays tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers.

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 1 check supporting).

**What the calls show:** Cost discipline is real and recurring — captive solar/wind covering ~70-75% of power, back-to-back raw material booking to lock margins, expenses curtailed in the FY26 downturn, and scale economics worth ~5% of margin at full utilisation — but there is no evidence of deliberately passing savings into price to win volume, and asset turns are modest with capacity underutilised.

> "if we increase the production through economy of scale the cost will come down. So, it can make a 5% difference in the margin" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹36 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 0.6× a year — not the high-turnover, low-margin machine this pattern needs.

## What to watch

- Antidumping duty expiry in October 2026 — the Government Dependency risk names this as the main pricing protection, and the New Entrants risk notes it is already not prohibitive and excludes post-2016 value-added products.
- Profit per tonne against the guided INR10,000-15,000 range — a recovery toward the INR25,793 seen in FY24 would be the first evidence against the Pricing Power failure and the negative mark on pricing, mix and volume.
- Whether the INR852 crore capex plan and the Telangana line actually get built — continued slippage keeps the capex-for-growth check negative and keeps Market Share Gainers stuck at defence rather than gain.
- Any use of the INR3,115 crore treasury — a buyback, a cap on cash retention, or the distressed acquisition management says it wants would move the distribution check and test the Corporate Culture pattern.
- Export share of dispatches, still 5-6% versus 32% of the order book in FY23 — a revival is the clearest available route out of the 2-3% domestic end-market ceiling flagged under structural growth and geographical expansion.
- PSU concentration and the three-to-four month order book — the Stakeholder Concentration risk turns on ONGC and Oil India, whose share has already fallen from 60% of the FY24 order book to 33% in Q3FY26.
