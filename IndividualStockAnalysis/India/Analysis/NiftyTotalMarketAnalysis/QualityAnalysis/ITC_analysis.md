### The story in depth

ITC comes out of these three reviews as a solid, steady business rather than a spectacular one: 59 out of 100, or "Decent" (three stars), built from 67 on business quality, just 16 on multibagger fit, and 96 on risk safety. The engine of the quality score is pricing and stability. The company keeps ₹59 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs, turns ₹35 of every ₹100 of sales into operating profit, and has held those margins within about ±1.6 points year after year. The framework reads that steadiness two ways at once — as "Intangible benefits" (customers willingly pay the price) and as "Barriers to entry and rationality" (few newcomers are disturbing the pricing), which is why Industry Structure is the single strongest area at +2. Alongside that, sales have compounded at roughly 7% a year with very consistent year-to-year growth, and ₹100 of capital in the business generated ₹25 of operating cash this year.

Those same traits are what the pattern screen is picking up. "Toll Roads" and "Brand Strength" both register as QUANT SIGNAL, and "Pricing Power," "Recurring Revenue," "Friendly Middleman," "Innovation Dominance" and "Forward Integrators" all come out PARTIAL — the numbers point toward a durable, well-priced franchise, but every one of the eleven patterns carries the same note: no conference-call judgement available. That evidence gap is the reason the multibagger contribution is only 16 out of 100. It also shows up in the quality framework, where Competitive Advantage and Management are left unscored, and in the risk review, where the sole item raised — "Good Enough Goods" — is explicitly a QUANT FLAG: one numeric fingerprint is present, the calls say nothing either way, and the record is clear that numbers alone are a prompt to investigate, never a verdict. The high risk-safety score of 96 and the SOUND resilience reading should therefore be understood as "nothing has been contradicted," not "everything has been checked." It is also worth noting that the fat margins that earn the Brand Strength and Pricing Power signals are exactly what would attract a cheaper, good-enough competitor, so one trait is driving both the best pattern reading and the only risk flag.

The clear soft spot is how the money moves through the business. About 164 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, scored at −2, and that alone drags Capital Allocation to −2 — the weakest area on the board. Asset turn is neutral at zero: every ₹1 of assets produces only ₹0.84 of sales, a moderately asset-heavy setup. Put together, a company that earns excellent margins is also holding a lot of capital in assets and in the working-capital cycle, which is what keeps a 25% cash return on capital at a score of +1 rather than higher, and what caps overall business quality at 67. In short: strong pricing and a stable industry structure on one side, a long cash cycle and moderate asset productivity on the other, and a large amount of unverified ground in the middle where the call-based evidence would normally settle the argument.
