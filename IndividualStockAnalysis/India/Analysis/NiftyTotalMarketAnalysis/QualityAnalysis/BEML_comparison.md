### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 2 weakened, 7 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is heritage (established 1964, Schedule A defence PSU), third-party awards and a 1,400-unit fleet across 73 countries that opens export doors, but no premium pricing, no A&P spend and no consumer-style brand stretch — the 'brand' is really PSU/MoD standing. (The long view had said: There is genuine heritage (1964, Mini Ratna, PSU-of-record status in metro and HMVs, awards and accolades cited in Jun-26) and it opens doors — sole bidder, nomination orders, DMRC export partnership — but it converts to neither premium pricing nor A&P-driven share, and 65-70% of sales are through open competition.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Named new products land every year with revenue impact (Vande Bharat sleeper, indigenous 12x12 HMV, LAMV, 21 cu m rope shovel, 550 HP grader, 35T electric dumper) and R&D spend is stated at an all-time high, with TBM and maritime cranes next — but this is import substitution funded by a shrinking margin, not high gross margins funding a dominant R&D share. (The long view had said: Named new products appear year after year — 12x12 HMV, LAMV, 21 cu.m rope shovel, 550 HP motor grader, 35T electric dumper, Vande Bharat Sleeper, driverless metro, and now TBM and maritime cranes — with R&D and capex at all-time highs in FY26; but the innovation is import-substitution funded by low gross margins, not high margins funding R&D that then earns pricing power.)

**That weakened:**

- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Sales go directly to institutional buyers (Coal India, MoD, metro corporations) via tender; where an intermediary does exist — the Mine Developer Operators — it works against BEML, pushing toward OpEx, lower-end machines and second-hand equipment. (The long view had said: The calls are silent on any professional intermediary; sales go directly to Coal India, MoD, metro corporations and MDOs through tenders, with no discussion of trade training or channel influence.)
- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Management discloses bad news candidly (margin dip, legacy balance-sheet correction, Q4 shortfall) and invests long-term, but the promise-versus-delivery record over these four calls is poor: order book guided to Rs22-23k crore in Jun-25 and Rs20k crore twice thereafter closed at Rs15,900 crore, the +150bps EBITDA guidance became a 38% EBITDA decline, and chairman turnover has been high. (The long view had said: Management does disclose bad news promptly and without spin — the INR80 crore metro provision in Feb-26, the INR250 crore legacy correction and missed INR20,000 crore order-book target in Jun-26 — but the Dec-23 promises of 30-40% defence growth and sustained double-digit margins were not delivered (FY26 PAT down 50%), chairmanship has rotated repeatedly, and a 2018 contract was left uncorrected on the books for years.)

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 2 worsened, 3 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: In the segments where the method is changing — surface mining replacing drill-and-blast, continuous underground mining, aluminium car bodies, driverless metro — BEML does not own the technology and is dependent on partners it has not fully secured, while its installed base is diesel dump trucks facing EV conversion. This is method-level change in its core mining franchise, not incremental. (The long view had said: Mining method is shifting away from BEML's installed base — drill-and-blast to surface miners and underground continuous mining (25 MTPA to a projected 200 MTPA) — segments where BEML admits it has no product and is still hunting partners (Tesmec tie-up, continuous-miner partner 'not zeroed upon anybody'). In rail, management concedes a competitor's acquisition of propulsion capability made it 'very, very competitive', prompting BEML to build its own TCMS.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The MDO transition is a textbook good-enough attack on BEML's high-end position: contractors buying on opex economics choose lower-specification machines BEML doesn't make, or second-hand equipment altogether, and BEML's answer is to wait for a Coal India policy change rather than to defend on product. (The long view had said: Management explicitly describes cheaper substitutes taking share in its core mining market: under the MDO model, operators choose OpEx over CapEx and buy lower-end equipment BEML does not make, or second-hand machines — a classic good-enough attack on its high-end excavators and shovels. Metro tendering shows the same commoditisation, with price gaps to rivals under 1%.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Revenue is tied to customer capex cycles and tender timing rather than steady operations — the CMD himself calls the order book "cyclical every year", mining demand hinges on Coal India's acquisition capex, and the year is monsoon- and tender-skewed with Q4 alone jumping to ~INR1,800 crores; FY26 showed the damage, with revenue at an all-time high while EBITDA fell 38% and PAT halved. (The long view had said: Across all three calls the revenue base is customer-capex equipment (Coal India overburden machines, metro corporations' rolling-stock programmes, MoD platforms) and management repeatedly describes a hard-wired intra-year cycle plus a lumpy multi-year one — FY25 rail & metro collapsed from 38% to 19% of revenue on execution gaps, FY26 mining orders slipped on monsoon, and Q4 alone carried 40-45% of sales, dragging working capital and debtors. Margins swing violently with it (FY26 PBT -51%, EBITDA -38%).)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Management states outright that mining is largely dependent on Coal India and its subsidiaries and that moving away from that over-dependence is a stated objective; on the defence side, collections stalled on a single counterparty (MoD) and had to be recovered in April–May. The order book is also concentrated in a handful of programmes — 68% Rail and Metro at Q3 FY26, with MRVC alone worth more than INR30,000 crores of potential ticket size. (The long view had said: Management names the dependency itself repeatedly: mining revenue rests on Coal India and its subsidiaries, defence on a single customer (MoD), and rail on a handful of metro corporations — and it flags Coal India's acquisition-model change as a direct threat to its own product fit. Collection shortfalls in FY26 were attributed to delayed MoD payment, showing single-counterparty cash risk.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
