# Bajaj Finance Ltd. (BAJFINANCE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

Bajaj Finance Limited (BFL) is an Indian non-bank lender, headquartered in Pune, that makes loans to ordinary consumers and small businesses across India — everything from financing a shopper's purchase of consumer durables at the point of sale, to personal loans, home loans, gold loans, small-business (MSME) loans and vehicle finance. It reaches customers through a very large physical and digital footprint: roughly 4,200 locations, a customer franchise that grew from 66 million in late 2022 to 119.3 million by March 2026, and the Bajaj Finserv app (about 67 million customers as of early 2025), with management running what it calls 32 different lines of business. It earns money mainly on the spread between what it charges borrowers and its cost of funds (7.41% in Q4 FY26), plus fee and distribution income; it funds itself with market borrowings and customer deposits (INR 68,533 crore, 16% of consolidated borrowings in Q4 FY26). Assets under management grew from about INR 2.31 lakh crore in Q3 FY23 to roughly INR 5.10 lakh crore in Q4 FY26 (22.4% growth), with FY26 profit after tax growing 26.7% in the quarter and return on equity of about 19%.

**The parts of the business:**

- **B2B / sales finance (consumer durables financing)** — Lends to shoppers at the point of purchase for consumer discretionary goods; B2B disbursements were INR 16,026 crore in Q3 FY23, and management tracks it closely as a seasonal, demand-sensitive volume driver and a key source of new customers.
- **B2C / consumer lending (including unsecured personal loans)** — Direct loans to individual customers, largely unsecured; management flagged customers with 3-plus unsecured loans as a risk area and deliberately pruned this exposure back to pre-COVID levels by March 2025.
- **Mortgages / Bajaj Housing Finance Limited (BHFL)** — A separately managed housing finance subsidiary doing home and property loans; in Q3 FY23 it was the main reason overall AUM growth fell short, because of intense pricing pressure in mortgages.
- **Gold loans** — Loans secured against gold, run through a dedicated branch network that crossed 1,000 branches by early 2025; the fastest-growing line, up 115% and 3.5% of total AUM in Q4 FY26, expected to exceed 5% of AUM by FY27.
- **MSME lending** — Loans to micro, small and medium businesses; grew only 6% in FY26 (versus 22.5% for the company) because management cut exposure by about 30% after this book drove a meaningful share of credit costs, with a return to double-digit growth expected in FY27.
- **Two-wheeler and three-wheeler financing (captive Bajaj Auto book)** — Financing of Bajaj Auto vehicles, discontinued after Bajaj Auto set up its own captive finance company; now under 1% of AUM but still 13% of gross NPAs in Q4 FY26, and being wound down to under INR 1,500 crore by September 2026.
- **Deposits** — Fixed deposits taken from customers as a funding source rather than a lending product; the book stood at INR 68,533 crore in Q4 FY26, or 16% of consolidated borrowings, after being 21% of the balance sheet back in Q3 FY23.
- **Co-branded credit cards** — Cards issued with RBL Bank and DBS where BFL sourced customers; new sourcing was stopped by mutual agreement in early 2025, but BFL continues to earn distribution fees and revenue share on the existing arrangement.
- **Digital platform / Bajaj Finserv app and cross-sell** — An app and web platform used to sell and service products to the existing customer base; 31 million net app installs in Q3 FY23 rising to nearly 67 million customers by Q3 FY25, with a 'cross-sell franchise' of 61.56 million customers that management treats as a core growth engine.
- **Partnerships (Bharti Airtel)** — A five-year strategic partnership announced in Q3 FY25 to distribute BFL products through the Airtel Thanks app, with two products live at the time and nine planned by March 2025.
- **Microfinance (MFI)** — A small-ticket lending business mentioned only in passing; together with gold loans it accounted for half of the roughly 1,800 employees added in Q4 FY26, suggesting it is in a build-out phase.
- **FinAI transformation (BFL 3.0)** — Not a lending product but the company-wide operating programme management spends the most airtime on — a 203-person AI unit expanding to about 363 by June 2027, 27 live voice and text bots, and 600-plus autonomous agents planned for FY27 to cut cost and manual effort across all business lines.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*
