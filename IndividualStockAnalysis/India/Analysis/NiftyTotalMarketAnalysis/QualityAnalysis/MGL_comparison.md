**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.8 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Entry is barred by PNGRB licence exclusivity plus the sunk cost of 8,320 km of pipeline and 518 stations, and pricing is calibrated and administered rather than discount-driven — a single ₹0.50/kg move all year. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±5.0 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: PNGRB licence exclusivity and huge sunk pipeline investment keep new entrants out and pricing is administered rather than discount-driven, but EV adoption by BEST shows the barrier does not extend to substitute technologies.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: Accessibility is the core benefit and it is compounding fast — 518 CNG stations and 3.42 lakh new household connections in FY26, versus the inconvenience of LPG cylinders or petrol refuelling. | The long view had said: Accessibility is the core customer benefit and is being deliberately deepened with very large stations to cut queuing, though Mumbai land scarcity limits it.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Good. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹18 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±2.8 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹18 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±5.0 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Candor** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: The CFO volunteered a fully quantified bridge for the margin fall — gas mix ₹0.70–0.75/SCM, FX ₹0.50, uniform base price ₹0.20–0.22, opex ₹0.15–0.20 — and management similarly quantified volume lost to the pipeline outage and to Hormuz curtailment rather than deflecting. | The long view had said: Management discloses granular sourcing mix, quantifies self-inflicted and external volume losses, admits Mumbai growth is muted and price hikes incomplete, and even issues a written correction to a misstated figure.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: During the Hormuz LNG disruption MGL delivered 100% supply to domestic PNG and CNG and protected buses, hospitals, schools and canteens first — customers are buying uninterrupted supply, and some industrials volunteered to pay a premium to keep it. | The long view had said: Reliability is a genuine selling point — MGL kept 100% supply to domestic PNG and CNG through the Gulf crisis and prioritised hospitals, schools and public transport, backed by ISO 45001 safety certification and safety awards.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: Results are dominated by exogenous variables management does not control — APM/NWG allocation, Brent, Henry Hub, rupee, and government curtailment orders — so quarter-to-quarter swings (PAT ₹324 cr → ₹193 cr → ₹202 cr → ₹132 cr) should not be read as evidence of managerial skill either way. | The long view had said: Results track exogenous drivers — APM allocation falling from ~90% to ~69%, Brent/Henry Hub swings and government pooling circulars — more than managerial skill, so strong past profits should not be read as durable competence.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 1 weakened, 9 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Forward Integrators**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: MGL owns the last mile to the customer — 8,320 km of its own steel/PE pipeline into 3.21mn homes and 518 CNG stations (52 added in FY26; UEPL's 100th station at Latur, 47 added in 23 months) — which is real forward integration, but the calls show no franchise royalties, D2C revenue disclosure or own-store-share metrics, and a chunk of CNG still retails through OMC outlets on trade margin. (The long view had said: MGL is a distributor that owns the entire path to the end customer — its own retail CNG network (292 stations in Aug 2022 → 518 by Mar 2026, plus UEPL's 100th station) and direct piped connections to 3.21m homes and 5,924 I&C customers — and the Feb 2026 call is explicit about defending prime locations (Bombay Port Trust land, Western/Eastern Express Highway, a 60-point station at Wadala).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (LIKELY FIT).*

*Unchanged: Cost to Replicate (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT); Innovation Dominance (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 3 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The calls affirmatively show the demand base expanding, not eroding: 3,42,157 domestic households connected in FY26 alone, 872 new I&C customers, and government-led acceleration of LPG-to-PNG conversion after the supply crisis, which management frames as a structural positive. (The long view had said: The demand base's fuel preference is migrating: municipal transport is choosing electric over CNG, and the CNG-versus-petrol economic gap narrowed enough in FY23 for analysts to ask whether new customers still have a reason to convert. Against this, customer additions have held up and the FY26 LPG crisis prompted a government push toward PNG that MGL says it will seize.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Sourcing is concentrated in a handful of administered/contracted channels (APM via GAIL, NWG, HPHT, Henry Hub, spot RLNG) and physical delivery in one pipeline corridor — a single pipeline damage in Q3 shut CNG outlets across Mumbai and GA-2 and cost ~1% of CNG volume, while the Strait of Hormuz disruption curtailed I&C supply to ~80% in Q4; CNG retail also runs through OMC outlets, evidenced by the one-time OMC trade-margin reversal that distorted Q1 EBITDA. (The long view had said: Customers are highly diversified (3.21 million households, 5,924 I&C, 1.28 million vehicles) but supply is severely concentrated: gas comes through a handful of allocations and contracts controlled by GAIL/ONGC and a few term LNG suppliers, and a single pipeline incident in Q3FY26 shut CNG stations and cut industrial supply for days. In Q4FY26 term contracts went to force majeure and I&C supply was curtailed to ~80%, showing how little supplier optionality exists.)

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Cyclicality (WATCH); Technological Innovation (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
