### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 0 weakened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 2 could not be compared.

*Foundation test: FAIL on the long view, FAIL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Across all four calls management restated an identical numbered list of 12 priorities and then reported delivery against each: credit cost guided at 1% +/- 10bps and delivered at 0.8% FY26 and 0.8% in Q1 FY27; DA income deliberately cut from INR66 crores to INR7 crores and even taken negative, with the earnings hit disclosed rather than hidden; unsecured book cut from 10% to under 0.6%; branch openings delivered as guided. They also name the ST LAP problem openly rather than burying it. (The long view had said: Under Parvez Mulla the calls follow a disciplined promise-then-scorecard format — 12 priorities listed at the start of FY26 and each reported against — with candid admission of the ST LAP delinquency 'injury', the DSA-income crutch and stubborn opex, and delivery on the credit-cost guidance (guided ~1%, delivered 0.8%); offset by heavy discontinuity, with the entire FY24 board and MD exiting and True North fully selling out in Q1 FY27.)
- **Low Price Plus**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: A genuine cost culture is visible and itemised — 70 ST LAP branches co-located into gold branches to release premises, 'frugal cost structure' repeated as a standing priority every quarter, and opex actually declining 2.4% sequentially in Q1 FY27 while NII grew — but the customer-facing leg is missing: this is a high-yield gold/LAP lender with wholesale (ECB, sub-debt) funding, not cheap deposits, so there is no low-price-to-customer proposition. (The long view had said: Fedfina is a high-yield lender (15.7-17.5% yields, gold at 19%+) funded at ~8.7%, and management itself concedes a poor cost record; the FY26-27 cost improvement is a repair story, not an entrenched low-cost culture, and the bank-variant test fails since it has no deposit franchise.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 1 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Unknown on the long view, Unknown on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: The RBI directly reset the product's economics mid-year: from 1 April 2026 onboarding LTVs on bullet gold loans must be computed on principal plus interest at maturity, forcing FEDFINA to redesign to a periodic interest-due structure and to warn that reported overdues will stay elevated. Labour-code regulation also cost INR3.9 crores in one quarter. Revenue is not from government contracts or subsidies, but the regulator sets the binding product parameters. (The long view had said: Regulator action repeatedly and materially reshapes the P&L and the product itself: the Nov-2023 RBI risk-weight change raised cost of funds and dented capital adequacy, draft RBI guidelines on bank-NBFC overlap with parent Federal Bank left the company making representations and sitting at 'status quo' on its own structure, and the April-2026 LTV/bullet-loan rewrite forced a redesign of the core gold product with reported Stage 2 rising 2.2%-2.7% purely on the rule change while co-lending transactions broke down in transition. PSL classification also governs which borrowings are cheap.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The book is granular retail secured lending across 750+ branches with AUM per branch of only INR16-18 crores; no single customer, licensor or counterparty is discussed as driving results. Management also deliberately cut its dependence on direct assignment counterparties, with DA income down 89% to INR7 crores in FY26 and negative INR13 crores in Q1 FY27, absorbing that hit while core net income still grew 33%. (The long view had said: The borrower base is granular (35,000 new gold customers in a single quarter), but the capital model leans on a small set of institutional counterparties: co-lending and direct-assignment partners are the mechanism for conserving capital, and when those partners hit regulatory transition issues in Q1 FY27 the business fell back on balance sheet, pushing leverage from 4.6 to 4.89 and CRAR down from 22.4% to 20.71%. Promoter Federal Bank also drives the regulatory overhang, and 75%+ of borrowings are bank funding.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: The lead product is gold loans, where AUM is levered to the gold price: AUM grew 77% year-on-year in Q1 FY27 while tonnage grew only ~1% quarter-on-quarter/12% year-on-year, and management flagged a 15% decline in domestic gold prices between 31 Jan and 30 Jun 2026 as a headwind — so much of FY26's 'growth' was commodity price, not volume. Portfolio LTV also drifted from 59.3% (Q3 FY26) to 67.9% (Q1 FY27) as prices fell, and the ST LAP book has been through a two-year credit-cost cycle requiring an ARC sale and policy rebuild. (The long view had said: Gold is now ~50%+ of AUM and management explicitly attributes growth swings to bullion prices — 76% gold AUM growth in FY26 on price tailwinds against only 12% tonnage, then a 15% domestic price fall between Jan and Jun 2026 that flipped the same driver into a headwind; credit costs also swung with the MSME cycle (0.7% FY24 to 1.8% FY25 back to 0.8% FY26). Bounded rather than high because the underlying demand is operating-capital lending to small self-employed borrowers, not customer capex, and tonnage has compounded ~10-12% for five years independent of price.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

*Not comparable: Shifting Consumer Preferences (NOT ASSESSED → LOW — could not be assessed on the long view).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
