# HDFC Bank Ltd. (HDFCBANK) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

HDFC Bank is an Indian bank that takes deposits from savers and lends the money out, operating across India through a branch network that has grown to about 9,700 branches serving roughly 100 million customers. It sells everyday banking to individuals — savings and current accounts, fixed deposits, credit cards, car and two-wheeler loans, gold loans, home loans and wealth management — and banking to businesses, from small shopkeepers and MSMEs up to large corporates, including cash management, trade finance for importers and exporters, and settlement services for capital-market clients. It makes money mainly on the gap between what it pays depositors and what it earns on loans (net interest income, with a margin around 3.4%-4%), plus fees and commissions from cards, merchant payments, and distributing third-party products like insurance and mutual funds, and some income from its bond and derivatives book. In FY26 it reported deposit growth of 14.4%, loan growth of 12%, gross bad loans of 1.15% and a return on assets of 1.9%; it merged with mortgage lender HDFC Limited around 2023 and also owns lending subsidiary HDB Financial Services.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Retail liabilities / deposits (CASA and term deposits)** — Current, savings and fixed-deposit accounts gathered mostly through branches — the bank's core funding, described as 84% sourced from retail branches, with deposits of about Rs 16 lakh crore back in mid-2022 and management repeatedly calling this the 'granular and sustainable' franchise it prizes most.
- **Retail advances (including mortgages and 'wheels')** — Loans to individuals — home loans, car, two-wheeler and transportation finance, personal and gold loans — where management says it is a top-2 mortgage bank and the top 'wheels' bank in India; retail loans were growing ~22% year-on-year in 2022.
- **Payments business — credit cards and merchant acquiring** — Issuing credit cards and processing card payments for shops; in 2022 it had a 17.6 million card base and 3.2 million payment acceptance points, and by FY26 it accounted for roughly 21%-22% of card issuance, 26%-28% of card spends and 35%-36% of merchant acquiring in the country — a major fee generator.
- **Commercial and rural banking (MSME / priority sector)** — Lending and banking services to small and medium businesses and rural borrowers, offered in 640 districts as of 2022 and growing ~29% year-on-year then; management says it is among the top 2 MSME banks in India.
- **Wholesale / corporate banking** — Loans and services to large companies, plus trade finance handling 18%-20% of India's exports and 13%-15% of import flows; growth here is deliberately selective because corporate lending spreads are thin, and the book grew ~16% in 2022 even after letting go of Rs 40,000-50,000 crore of low-priced assets.
- **Transaction banking — cash management and capital markets settlement** — Handling companies' collections and payments and settling capital-market and share-issue money, where the bank claims market leadership with 35%-40% of capital-market account settlements and 40%-50% of banker-to-issue escrow settlements.
- **Wealth management and third-party product distribution** — Selling investment and insurance products of other providers to customers through a hub-and-spoke model — offered in 357 locations in 2022 with a plan to reach 900+ — and one of the fastest-growing fee lines, up 32% year-on-year in one 2024 quarter.
- **Treasury / investments** — The bank's own portfolio of government securities, corporate bonds and derivatives, which produces trading gains or losses depending on interest rates — for example a Rs 1,312 crore mark-to-market loss in the June 2022 quarter when bond yields spiked.
- **Digital banking and technology platforms** — Mobile and net banking, the PayZapp payments app, SmartHub Vyapar for merchants and the UPI-linked 'Zapp' wallet — now serving over 60 million registered mobile app users, with 97% of payment and service transactions and 92% of new customer acquisitions done digitally; tech spend has more than quadrupled to around $1 billion.
- **HDB Financial Services (subsidiary)** — A separately RBI-licensed non-bank lender within the group that serves a different, more hands-on customer segment through its own branches, and which regulation required to be listed by September 2025.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## The verdict: Mixed — 49 out of 100 ★★☆☆☆

In one breath: the quality framework finds a good business today (65/100); it strongly fits 1 of the 11 patterns long-term winners share; the risk review found 1 high risk (Government Dependency). Weighing those together gives 49 out of 100 — mixed.

**The exact arithmetic:** Overall = 45% × 65 (business quality) + 30% × 15 (multibagger fit) + 25% × 60 (risk safety) = 49 out of 100 → Mixed (2 stars).

- **Business quality (65/100):** The 34-check quality framework scored the business +0.60 on its −2 (poor) to +2 (excellent) scale, with 82% of checks backed by evidence; mapped onto 0–100 that is 65 points.
- **Multibagger fit (15/100):** the foundation test did not pass (+0); 1 pattern fit strongly (Cost to Replicate) (+15) → 15 of 100.
- **Risk safety (60/100):** started from a clean 100; 1 high risk (Government Dependency) (−20); 5 worth watching (−20) → 60 of 100.

### The story in depth

HDFC Bank scores best where it is biggest. The quality framework gives full marks to its distribution advantage (9,700 branches, 100 million customers, best-in-class business of INR330 crores per branch), to the convenience that comes with combining those branches with 97% digital adoption, to the certainty customers buy — depositors added INR2.45 lakh crore in the very quarter of the chairman resignation and Dubai branch shock, against asset quality tested over three decades at 1.15% GNPA — and to an end market still in permanent expansion, with MSMEs only 20-25% banked and 42% of its customers under 40. This is the one place the multibagger screen also says STRONG FIT: Cost to Replicate. The same branch network, customer base, cycle-tested underwriting and licence-gated deposit franchise that earn the top quality scores are exactly what a rival cannot buy or build quickly. It is also why the risk review rates Stakeholder Concentration LOW — roughly 100 million customers, 6-8 million added a year, retail at 82-84% of deposits, and small retail liabilities up to 47% of net deposit accretion mean no single depositor or borrower can hurt the bank.

The trouble is that this scale converts into safety far better than it converts into profit, and often the identical trait shows up as a strength on one page and a risk on another. Being India's largest private bank and an index heavyweight scores -2 on the 'security by obscurity' check and sits directly underneath the only HIGH RISK finding, Government Dependency: RBI repo moves drive the margin, priority-sector quotas force the bank to source sub-economic assets that are merely 'available at a price', and an RBI draft circular threatened HDB. Brand trust is real enough to hold deposits through a governance shock — that is what carries the Brand Strength pattern — but the framework only scores it a 1 because it does not turn into price: mortgage pricing sits within 10bps of peers. That single fact is why Pricing Power is the screen's one NO FIT, why the Good Enough Goods risk stays on WATCH with management conceding 'there is a sensitivity to price there', and why Low Price Plus is only partial — the cost side improved from 40.5% to 39.5%, but the cheap-deposit leg has weakened. The Shifting Consumer Preferences risk closes the loop: the deposit engine that is the bank's best asset is drawing on a pool management describes as growing at 8-9%, one of the lowest of all RBI segments, with customers steadily moving from current and savings accounts into time deposits.

The HDFC Ltd merger is the hinge between the good and the mediocre parts of this record. It scores -1 on the acquisition check — the opposite of a small bolt-on, and followed by a worse borrowing mix (8% to 21%), a loan-to-deposit ratio at 110% and margins down from 4.0% to about 3.4%. That is the clearest reason Return on Capital scores 0.0 and Capital Allocation only 0.33, why the growth-capex check earns just a 1 despite the branch network nearly doubling and technology spend quadrupling to about $1bn (return on assets is flat at 1.9% and payback on the technology build is still prospective), and why Market Share Gainers is only partial — deposit share kept rising 30-50bps a year, but the bank deliberately gave up advances share for three years to repair the ratio. The redeeming read is Corporate Culture: management pre-announced in October 2024 that it would grow slower than the system in FY25, at system in FY26 and faster in FY27, and largely delivered. So the story is a business that is genuinely hard to replicate, sells certainty and convenience into a market with decades of runway, but does not price for it, is steered by regulation as much as by competition, and is still digesting a very large deal — which is how the combined score lands at 49 out of 100, or Mixed. The record does not settle the resilience question either way.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Fortunes are repeatedly set by policy rather than the market across all 17 calls: RBI repo moves drive NIM, priority-sector lending quotas force sub-economic asset sourcing (PSLC/IBPC/PTC purchases 'available at a price'), an RBI draft circular threatened HDB's model while another circular mandated HDB's listing by Sept-25, AIF provisioning was created and released purely by regulatory fiat, and FY27 growth levers named are the FCNR swap window and the government's ECLGS 5.0 scheme. The March 2026 event required explicit statements of support from the Government, RBI and SEBI.

> "The ECLGS scheme 5.0 is also a very good opportunity and you will see a fair amount of growth that we will pick up in the mid-market segment" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.
