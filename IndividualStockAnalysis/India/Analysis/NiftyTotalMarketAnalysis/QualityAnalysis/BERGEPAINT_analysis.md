# Berger Paints India Ltd. (BERGEPAINT) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSUMER GOODS*

Berger Paints India is an India-based paint and coatings maker that sells decorative paints for homes and buildings (emulsions, exterior and interior premium products, enamels, putty, distemper, primers, textures and wood coatings) plus waterproofing and construction chemicals, and industrial coatings for infrastructure, factories and vehicles. It makes money by manufacturing and selling these products through a very large dealer and retail network — management talks about adding roughly 2,000-2,200 retail touch points a quarter, installing 2,000-2,500 tinting 'colour bank' machines a quarter, and running more than 1,800 branded stores across the country — with growth driven by volume, periodic product price changes, and a shift in mix toward premium, higher-margin products. Management describes itself as the number two player in Indian decorative paints with a market share above 20%, running gross margins around 40-41% and an EBITDA margin guided to 15-17%. Beyond India it operates through overseas subsidiaries in Poland (Bolix) and Nepal (BJN), plus Indian subsidiaries and joint ventures in specialty, construction-chemical and automotive coatings.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Decorative business** — The core business: paints for walls and homes sold to retail consumers and painters through dealers, running high single-digit volume growth in most quarters, with premium and luxury products growing double digits while mass products like putty, distemper, primer and enamel are flat or declining.
- **Construction chemicals and waterproofing** — A fast-growing add-on range — waterproofing coatings, tile adhesives, admixtures, roof and damp-proofing products such as Roof Cool & Seal and DAMShield — which management repeatedly calls a strong double-digit grower and says now covers about 80-85% of the major products in the category.
- **Wood coatings** — Paints and finishes for wood, consistently described as having robust performance and strong double-digit volume growth, though it is a smaller line than decorative paints.
- **Protective and infrastructure coatings** — Industrial coatings sold to infrastructure and construction projects, where management says Berger holds a leadership position; it grew strongly when government infrastructure spending was high (FY25) but had gone muted by the December 2025 quarter.
- **Automotive, general industrial and powder coatings** — Coatings sold to vehicle makers, appliance makers and other factories; growth was flat or muted through FY25 on weak two-wheeler and commercial-vehicle demand, and had turned steady by Q3 FY26.
- **Bolix (Poland subsidiary)** — An overseas subsidiary in Poland doing project-linked exterior/insulation-type work, which delivered solid top-line growth throughout but saw profits dented by one-off project costs in FY25 before showing strong sales and operating profit growth in FY26.
- **BJN Nepal (subsidiary)** — The Nepal paints subsidiary, which shrank during a local credit crunch in FY25, rebounded sharply afterwards and even paid a dividend up to the parent, then turned muted again in FY26 amid political disruption.
- **STP Limited (subsidiary)** — A subsidiary in construction chemicals and admixtures whose results have been choppy — hurt by raw material costs and weak admixture sales in FY25, and by a temporary plant shutdown at Jamshedpur that normalised from January 2026.
- **SBL Speciality Coatings (subsidiary)** — A smaller specialty coatings arm with mostly flat to modest growth, hit in FY26 by weak industrial demand, tariffs and start-up costs from a new factory near Chandigarh.
- **Berger Becker Coatings (joint venture)** — A coatings joint venture that management consistently reports as delivering healthy or robust growth in both sales and profitability.
- **Berger Nippon Paint Automotive Coatings (joint venture)** — A joint venture supplying coatings to the automotive industry, which moved from small top-line growth with strong profit gains in FY25 to strong double-digit revenue and profit growth in FY26 as auto demand improved.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Berger Paints comes out of these three reviews as a well-run, well-defended business that is currently being paid very little for how much more it sells. The quality work scores it highly on the things a careful owner cares about: management candour (it owns a missed forecast, breaks the volume-versus-value gap into its parts, and volunteers market share loss rather than waiting to be asked), disciplined stewardship (a purely organic strategy, walking away from the large Akzo deal, and a balance sheet that improved to net cash of Rs.918cr), and steady economics — about ₹49 of every ₹100 of sales left after direct input costs, ₹15 of operating profit per ₹100, roughly 10% annual sales growth, and a 20% cash return on the capital employed. The overall rating lands at 64 out of 100, or 'Decent', built from 69 on business quality, 48 on multibagger fit and 74 on risk safety, with resilience judged SOUND.

The single trait that ties almost everything together is distribution reach. Management itself names it as the source of its outperformance and its main strategic lever, and the numbers behind it — roughly 2,000+ retail touchpoints and 1,800 to 2,500 colour-bank tinting machines added per quarter, more than 1,800 experience stores — earn top marks on both the distribution and convenience checks. That same reach is what makes several patterns fire: it is the delivered promise behind Forward Integrators (the urban store plan announced in Nov 2024 was actually built out), the barrier behind Cost to Replicate (decades of dealer density and a hundred-year brand, with serial multinational entrants never proving disruptive), and the installed base behind the Recurring Revenue read, where tinting machines at the dealer counter pull repeat business without any subscription or contract economics. Strong Corporate Culture fit rests on the same honesty the candour check rewards. But the reach is bought and maintained, not free: growth capex on dealer touchpoints, colour-bank machines, a new office and a new plant near Chandigarh is going in while incremental returns on it remain unproven, which is why the capital allocation area scores only 0.67.

The uncomfortable part is that the very push behind that reach is also the source of the biggest weakness and the biggest risk, and they are the same story told twice. Growth is almost entirely volume — 8.5% volume against 0.4% value in Q3 FY26 — with price cuts and a shift into economy emulsions, textures and tile adhesives actively working against value; this scores the worst mark in the whole quality framework (-2 on pricing, mix and volume) and it is exactly why Pricing Power is graded NO FIT, alongside Toll Roads and Low Price Plus. On the risk side, the deep-pocketed new entrant (Grasim/Birla Opus) is discussed defensively in every call, with management quantifying a permanent 2.5-3% hit to its own growth rate and paying higher painter incentives purely as defence, a 1.5% drag on realisation — the only ELEVATED risk on the board. So the earlier Market Share Gainers claim (share 'in excess of 20%' in Feb 2025) has softened to PARTIAL, with the share slide dropped from the deck by Feb 2026 and a marginal loss conceded when prompted. Set against that, the defences are real and working: gross margins of 40-41%, a 15-quarter high, held through a price war; operating margin inside the guided 15-17% band every quarter from Q2FY25 to Q3FY26; a steady cadence of new products (DampShield, Color Plus, Silk Metallics, anti-dust, Easy Clean) that supports the Innovation Dominance read and lifts mix; and a refusal to chase putty volumes at negative gross margin. Five of the eight risk channels sit at WATCH or LOW, and the weakest quality areas are industry structure (0.17) and growth (0.33) — that is, the arena, not the operator.

> "we would like to be playing it safe. We don't want to say that... we will grow at double digit and then land up with 4-5%" — *management, on an earnings call*

Consumable pull-through, not contracted recurring revenue.

> "installed more than 2,000 color bank machines. In this quarter" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Friendly Middleman — PARTIAL
*"The expert sells it for you"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 2 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Demand is clearly intermediated — painters, contractors and builders are the route to market, and Berger raised painter incentives defensively in FY26 (a 1.5% drag on realisation) and cited contractor/builder relationships as the gating factor in metros.
