# Puravankara (PURVA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Puravankara Limited (stock code PURVA) is an Indian property developer, based in Bengaluru, that buys land and builds homes on it — apartments, affordable housing and plotted land parcels — and sells them mainly to individual home buyers, alongside some commercial/office development. It makes money by selling units in its projects ("presales"), collecting the money in instalments as construction progresses, and booking that as revenue in its profit and loss account when the finished homes are handed over, which is why its reported revenue and profit swing sharply from quarter to quarter with the timing of handovers. It grows by continually adding land — outright purchases, joint development agreements with landowners, joint ventures, and society redevelopment deals in Mumbai — and funds itself with a mix of customer collections and debt (net debt around Rs. 1,900-2,500 crores across the calls, at a cost of roughly 11-12%). It is strongest in South India, especially Bengaluru (over half of sales), plus Chennai, Hyderabad, Kochi, Coimbatore, Mangalore and Goa, and has been pushing into West India (Mumbai/MMR and Pune); by FY26 it reported record annual presales of Rs. 7,407 crores on 7.25 million square feet, total income of Rs. 3,846 crores and a return to profit.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Puravankara** — The flagship premium/mid-market residential brand (and commercial projects), where management targets projects with a minimum top line of about Rs. 700-1,000 crores each; it is the largest sales contributor — for example Rs. 585 crores of the Rs. 1,128 crores sold in Q1 FY25.
- **Provident** — The affordable-housing brand, run at larger scale to drive cost efficiencies, with a minimum project size of about a million square feet of saleable area; it is the second-biggest contributor, at Rs. 449 crores of Q1 FY25 sales, having grown 109% year-on-year on launches such as Provident Botanico.
- **Purva Land** — The plotted-development business, which buys big land parcels of roughly 50-100 acres and sells plots rather than built homes; it is the smallest of the three selling arms, contributing Rs. 95 crores in Q1 FY25 and heavily dependent on new launches.
- **Starworth Infrastructure and Construction Limited (SICL)** — The group's construction/EPC contracting arm, focused on precast, buildings (commercial, data centres, residential) and special projects, strengthened in 2022 with senior hires from Katerra and Tata Projects; it is a supporting business rather than a main revenue driver in these calls.
- **West & Commercial Assets** — The Mumbai/Pune and commercial-property push, headed by its own CEO in the later calls, built largely through society redevelopment deals (Chembur, Malabar Hill, Pali Hill) and launches in Andheri/Lokhandwala and Thane; management said Mumbai and Pune together made up 47% of the planned launch pipeline in FY25.
- **Land bank and business development** — The pipeline of land the company owns or has tied up — about 57 million square feet across South and West India in 2022 — replenished each year through acquisitions and joint developments, with FY26 alone adding over 12 million square feet carrying an estimated gross development value of about Rs. 15,200 crores.

### The story in depth

Puravankara comes out of the three reviews as a genuine growth story built on borrowed money, which is why the combined score lands at 35 out of 100 — a "Mixed" verdict, made up of 44 for business quality, just 8 for multibagger fit, and 50 for risk safety. The growth itself is real: bookings rose from about Rs.2,400cr in FY22 to a record Rs.7,407cr in FY26, with Rs.11,200cr guided, while the average selling price climbed 21% to Rs.10,213/sqft (Rs.11,787 and +37% in the March quarter). Two things drove that — a move out of the southern home market into Mumbai and Pune (Thane, Andheri/Lokhandwala, Chembur, Malabar Hill, Bandra), and a brand that management says lets it price 5-12% above the surrounding market across three labels (Puravankara premium, Provident affordable, Purva Land plots). This is the one place where the checks, the patterns and the risk review agree: "Gain in market share", "Geographical expansion" and "Pricing, Mix and Volume" all score positively, Brand Strength is the only pattern rated a likely fit, and Customer Benefits is the single quality area scoring at the top of its range.

The trouble is that the same engine producing the growth is what makes the balance sheet fragile, and the reviews keep landing on the same fact from different sides. To keep launching, the company must keep buying land — Rs.762cr in a single quarter, 12.76mn sqft and roughly Rs.15,200cr of future sales value added in FY26 — and it funds that with debt costing about 11%. Net borrowings climbed from roughly Rs.1,889cr in Q1FY23 to about Rs.2,482cr by Q3FY26, sitting at 1.31 times shareholders' money and consuming around Rs.600cr of interest a year. The "Capex for growth" check gives credit for spending on expansion rather than upkeep, but flags in the same breath that it is debt-funded with thin returns; the risk review turns the identical behaviour into its worst rating, calling Cyclicality a HIGH RISK precisely because the company kept deploying into land through the good part of the cycle. What comes back is modest: every Rs.1 of assets produces only Rs.0.22 of sales, and every Rs.100 of capital employed generated Rs.5 of operating cash — the reason Return on Capital is the weakest quality area of the seven, and why the whole set is judged STRAINED. Shareholders are not being paid out of this either: across sixteen calls there is no dividend or buyback discussion, and the direction is the reverse — a Rs.1,000cr equity raise approval on top of rising debt.

That leaves a business with visible strengths but few of the traits that compound on their own, which is why the multibagger screen scored only 8. One pattern fits, six are partial and three plainly do not: revenue is one-off project sales booked lumpily on handover (total income swung from Rs.334cr in Q3FY25 to Rs.1,104cr in Q3FY26), so there is nothing recurring; the product is a customer's largest purchase rather than a small unavoidable input; and despite the affordable Provident brand, the group positions itself as a premium seller with no sign of a cost-cutting culture — borrowing costs have stayed at 11-11.6% throughout. The same lumpiness shows up as a negative "Persistence of growth" score, with sales averaging about 8% a year but jumping around, and it traces back to a real dependency: the launch calendar that produces bookings is gated by government approvals, and management repeatedly blames slippage on administrative change such as e-Khata and the new Greater Bengaluru Authority. The brand premium is likewise double-edged — it is the basis of the Brand Strength and Pricing Power patterns, but the risk review rates Good Enough Goods ELEVATED for exactly the same reason: defending a price above adequate cheaper rivals is a posture that has to be re-won every launch, and management itself concedes much of the price gain came from mix rather than genuine pricing power. On the reassuring side, demand is functional shelter bought by thousands of individual end users across six cities, so Fashion Risk and customer concentration both rate LOW, and the buyer shift toward homes above Rs.10mn (from about 50% to 53% of top-city sales) is one the company is already pivoting toward.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Residential development is a capital-cycle business and the 16 calls show it plainly: net debt rose from ~Rs.1,889cr (Q1FY23) to ~Rs.2,482cr (Q3FY26) while the company kept deploying into land during the up-cycle (Rs.762cr in one quarter alone, ~12.8mn sq ft added in FY26), and reported results swung from a Rs.94cr loss in Q3FY25 to Rs.58cr profit in Q3FY26, with EBITDA margin moving 47% (Q1FY23) to 22-23% and presales from Rs.5,900cr to Rs.5,000cr to Rs.7,407cr. Revenue is recognised on handover, so income jumped 230% year-on-year in one quarter — an inherently lumpy, cycle-levered earnings base.

> "repaying debt versus creating value, there is a significant gap and upside... we are very comfortable with the current number, and we would like to aggressively grow the business" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: End-user (not investor) driven demand, sustenance sales of ~Rs.1,000-1,200cr p.a. providing a base, and an FY27 guidance of Rs.750cr debt reduction*

## What to watch

- Debt path: management has guided to Rs.750cr of debt reduction in FY27 — actual delivery against that, versus more land buying at ~11% cost, is the single clearest test of the HIGH RISK Cyclicality verdict.
