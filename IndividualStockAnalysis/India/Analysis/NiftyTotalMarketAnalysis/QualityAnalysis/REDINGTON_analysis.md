# Redington (India) Ltd. (REDINGTON) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: SERVICES*

Redington is a technology distributor: it buys hardware, software and cloud subscriptions from big technology vendors (Microsoft, AWS, Autodesk, Red Hat, Fortinet, Palo Alto and similar OEMs) and resells them through a network of channel partners, resellers and retailers who in turn serve enterprises, small and mid-sized businesses, government, education and consumers. It handles the supply chain in between — stocking inventory, warehousing, extending credit to partners, and increasingly adding services like migration, renewals and professional services — which management calls "supply chain orchestration." Because it is a middleman, margins are thin and money is made on volume plus tight control of working capital and interest cost: FY26 revenue was about INR 1,19,347 crore (~$13.5 billion, up 20%) but profit after tax was only about 1.3%–1.4% of sales, with working capital around 25–30 days and ROCE about 22%. It operates mainly in India (its largest market and the No. 1 IT distributor there per VAR India), the Middle East (UAE, Saudi Arabia and smaller GCC markets), Africa, Turkey (through subsidiary Arena) and South Asia, with newer expansion into ASEAN, CIS countries and South Africa.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Mobility Solutions Group (MSG / mobility)** — Distributes mobile phones and related devices, including direct-to-retail selling in India; the biggest single line at roughly 33% of quarterly revenue in Q4 FY26 (up 19% year-on-year), but management describes it as volume-driven and traditionally the lowest-margin part of the mix.
- **Endpoint Solutions Group (ESG)** — Distributes PCs and other end-user devices, including AI PCs (41% of India revenue in Q4 FY26 came from AI PCs above 40 TOPS); about 30% of top line and grew 28% year-on-year in Q4 FY26.
- **Technology Solutions Group (TSG)** — Sells enterprise infrastructure — data centre, networking, servers and storage, plus Platform-as-a-Service and Subscription-as-a-Service — typically through large lumpy deals (over INR 1,100 crore of large deals in Q4 FY26); about 19% of top line, up 34% year-on-year.
- **Software Solutions Group (SSG)** — Formed as a global vertical about a year before June 2026, it bundles software (40% of SSG), cloud/hyperscaler subscriptions (33%), security products (27%) and a small professional-services piece (under 1%); a $2.2 billion business at 17% of group revenue (up from ~12% two years earlier), growing 29% in dollar terms with about 74% recurring revenue and higher gross margin and profit than the group average.
- **Cloud / CSG (now inside SSG)** — The hyperscaler and subscription-software business previously reported separately as CSG — a standout grower (54% year-on-year in Q2 FY25, and consistently 40%–50% growth more recently) supported by the in-house Cloud Quarks platform for digital lifecycle management and automated subscription renewals.
- **Professional services** — A small but deliberately growing services arm (about $10-plus million of revenue) offering productized services around the customer lifecycle, with gross margins above 20% — far higher than distribution — and flagged as a future profitability driver.
- **Arena (Turkey subsidiary)** — The Turkish distribution business, a persistent drag: high inflation, high interest rates and 15%–20% IT market decline led to losses (INR 44 crore loss in Q4 FY26, INR 22 crore Redington's share) and an exceptional impairment of the Arena trade name hitting group PAT by INR 75.2 crore.
- **AI initiatives and platforms** — Newer capability investments rather than a reported segment — an AI lab at the Chennai headquarters, an "AI Exchange" marketplace with over 200 AI agents linking software vendors and customers through the partner network, and five AI learning centres in Tier 2 cities under CSR.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Redington is, at heart, a very large and very efficient middleman, and almost everything good and bad about it flows from that one fact. The quality work scores its distribution reach and channel intimacy at the top of the scale — number one in India and the Middle East and Africa across some forty countries, tens of thousands of transacting partners, up-country reach growing 45% — and it turns ₹1 of assets into ₹3.52 of sales while holding working capital at 25-30 days. That same profile is what makes two of the eleven multibagger patterns a strong fit: Low Price Plus, built on an explicit cost-and-turns culture with costs growing slower than revenue, and Market Share Gainers, evidenced by the number one India ranking and a move to number eight globally while growing 20% a year with only one global peer faster. Sales growth of about 13% a year, steady rather than lumpy, and a management team the framework rates full marks for candour — volunteering margin declines, Saudi provisions and Turkey losses unprompted, with the same finance director present across all eighteen calls — round out the strongest part of the picture.

The catch is that the trait driving the strengths also drives the weaknesses, and the records are explicit about it. After direct input costs the company keeps only about ₹5 of every ₹100 of sales, which earns the lowest score on pricing power and simultaneously explains why three patterns are outright no-fits: there is no Pricing Power (management concedes margin normalisation rather than claiming increases), no Brand Strength (it distributes Microsoft, AWS, Autodesk, Red Hat, Fortinet and Palo Alto, not its own name), and no Toll Road (large deals are knowingly accepted below normal margins, with group net margin around 1.3-1.4%). The risk review reaches the same place from the other side: Good Enough Goods flags distribution itself as a thin, substitutable service with gross margins in steady multi-quarter decline. Two whole quality areas score zero — industry structure and return on capital — and the return figure is worth stating carefully: the headline check measures ₹2 of operating cash for every ₹100 of capital employed this year, a 2% cash return, sitting alongside the 22% ROCE management cites on the calls. Structurally, the business sits between vendors who can change how they sell and partners who buy on price; the risk review's Stakeholder Concentration channel notes Apple's go-to-market change being questioned directly and Dell, Lenovo and Acer changing theirs too.

The bridge between the two halves is the software and cloud shift, and it is where the patterns and the risks are literally describing the same thing. The end market is rated a clear structural grower with its addressable size tripling, and the software and cloud arm has gone from 12% to 17% of group revenue in two years, now $2.2bn growing 29%, with 74% of it recurring and better margins than the group. That single pivot is what makes Recurring Revenue, Forward Integrators and Innovation Dominance partial rather than absent — the platform build-out (Cloud Quarks 2.0, a Chennai AI lab, an AI marketplace with 200-plus agents, automated renewals) is real, but there is no disclosed research spend and the annuity layer is still only about a sixth of revenue. The very same evidence appears as the silver lining under three separate risks — Technological Innovation, Shifting Consumer Preferences and New Entrants — because the migration away from boxes is both the opportunity and the thing eroding the legacy revenue pool, with vendor and hyperscaler marketplaces potentially routing around distributors altogether. Add cyclicality that has already bitten (Turkey's PC market down 18-20%, pushing Arena into losses and a trade-name write-down) and government demand that has twice moved the numbers (Saudi IT reprioritisation forcing inventory provisions, delayed Indian government receivables forcing one-off provisions), and seven of the eight risk channels sit on watch with overall resilience marked strained. The combined score of 56 — 63 on business quality, 40 on pattern fit, 64 on risk safety — lands the company at Decent: a genuinely well-run share gainer in a structurally poor economic position, where the outcome depends on the pivot outrunning the erosion.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

**Overall:** the company partly passes the foundation every multibagger shares (steady cash + high returns on capital + growth), 2 of 3 testable checks passing, and fits 2 of the 11 patterns: Low Price Plus, Market Share Gainers.

### Low Price Plus — STRONG FIT
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence and the numbers agree (1 of 1 check computable supporting).

**What the calls show:** Across all four years management describes an explicit cost-and-turns culture: a consolidated shared-services centre processing 300,000+ orders a quarter (Aug 2022), opex growing slower than revenue in FY25 and FY26, best-in-class working capital of 25 days in Q2FY25 and 30 days in Q4FY26, interest cost cut 59% ex-Arena, all delivering 22% ROCE on ~1.3% net margins — thin unit economics rescued by asset turns and cost discipline.

> "we are cranking ourselves to be very, very efficient, and very maniacal focus on our cost" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹5 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 3.5× a year with 18% returns on capital — thin margins made up on volume, the low-price-winner profile.

### Market Share Gainers — STRONG FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence and the numbers agree (1 of 1 check computable supporting).

**What the calls show:** Share gains are explicit and repeated across the period: VAR India ranked it the No. 1 IT distributor in India for FY24, and by June 2026 it is No. 8 globally on Omdia data, No. 1 in India and Middle East-Africa, growing 20% y/y with only one global peer faster — while SSG grew from 12% to 17% of revenue and India grew 50% in Q4FY26. Critically, this is not share bought with credit: working capital fell to 25-30 days and deals failing ROCE tests were let go.

> "we are number 8 globally, but we are number 1 in India and number 1 in Middle East, Africa" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Sales compounding at 13% while margins held or improved (2% → 2%) — growth that was WON, not bought with price cuts.

### Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) — PARTIAL
*"Sells once, earns forever"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (1 of 3 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** The June 2026 investor event quantifies a genuine annuity layer inside SSG — 74% of SSG revenue recurring (up from 72%), automated subscription-renewal platforms and Cloud Quarks 2.0 lifecycle management — but this is only ~17% of group revenue, the rest being transactional hardware distribution, and management never frames deferred revenue/customer advances as a funding source (customer advances appear only as a one-off in Turkey).

> "About 74 percentage of our last year revenue is recurring... And this used to be 72% in the previous year. We expect this trend to keep going up." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ About 34 days of cash is tied up in the trade cycle — customers are not funding the business.
- ✅ Capital spending is about 0% of sales — growth needs little capital.
- ❌ Operating cash was positive in 10 of 12 years — not fully consistent.

### Friendly Middleman — PARTIAL
*"The expert sells it for you"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 2 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Redington sells almost entirely through resellers/channel partners rather than end customers, and invests in the channel (11,000+ partners transacting monthly on its cloud platform in FY23, an SSG 'academy' training partners and sales force, Tech Citadel partner events). But the reseller is a price-and-credit-sensitive commercial buyer, not a trusted prescriber whose reputation locks in the end customer, and margins are compressing rather than holding.

> "our own academy, which is the training and skill building programs for both our sales force as well as our partners" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Operating margin of 2% — too thin for a protected franchise.
- ❌ Operating margin moved from about 2% (early years) to 2% (recent years) — no widening trend.

### Innovation Dominance — PARTIAL
*"Out-inventing the competition"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 2 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** There is a visible, repeated capability-build cadence — cloud platform with 11,000 partners (FY23), Cloud Quarks 2.0, an AI lab in Chennai, an AI Exchange marketplace with 200+ AI agents launched March 2026, automated renewals — but no R&D line is disclosed, gross margins are ~5-6% and falling, so innovation is not funded by or converted into pricing power.
