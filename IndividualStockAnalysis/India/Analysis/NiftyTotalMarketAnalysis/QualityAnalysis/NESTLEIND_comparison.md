## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 29 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 5 improved, 2 regressed, 22 unchanged. 2 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them).

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|

| Return on Capital | Excellent (+1.50) | Excellent (+1.50) | +0.00 |
| Growth | Good (+0.80) | Good (+1.20) | +0.40 |

| Customer Benefits | Excellent (+2.00) | Excellent (+2.00) | +0.00 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Good. Now: In the most recent inflationary period growth was materially pricing-led — Q4 2022 volume was only 3-4% of ~14% growth — but management frames pricing as defensive cost pass-through and repeatedly states the strategic priority is volume, not price. | The long view had said: Management explicitly and repeatedly subordinates pricing to volume/penetration — pricing was deliberately held below cost inflation (6.3% price vs 15% commodity inflation) — which is the opposite of pricing/mix-led growth, only partly offset by a 16% CAGR premium portfolio.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: A structured succession plan was executed with the incoming CMD introduced to investors alongside the outgoing one, and 'future ready plans for our people' plus 'people' as one of the five Ps show systematic talent focus. | The long view had said: Talent is treated as a core 'P' with future-ready people plans and an orderly, pre-announced CMD succession, but the successor was brought in from outside rather than from an internal pipeline.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Management explicitly refuses to manage for the quarter, has executed a single penetration-led strategy for seven-plus years, and demonstrably rebuilt the business from the MAGGI crisis of 2015. | The long view had said: Demonstrated tenacity over a 9-year arc — rebuilding a million lost outlets after the Maggi crisis and holding one penetration-led strategy across cycles — though strategic direction is set globally by the parent, limiting true independence.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Barriers are high — 113 years of presence, 10,000+ distributors, 5.3 million outlets and nine factories — and pricing behaviour described is cost-recovery rather than discounting, but the excerpts do not rule out new entrants. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.6 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing behaviour is rational (price taken well below input inflation, no discount war), but management openly flags an entrepreneurial wave of new food entrants, so entry barriers are not high.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Packaged food carries habit-based rather than contractual switching costs and no regional monopoly or captive aftermarket is described in these calls. | The long view had said: There are no switching costs or captive aftermarket in packaged food; management itself concedes credible regional competitors are taking their shot at the core.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Advertisement and promotion** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. Now: The company self-describes as a brand-marketing company whose core brands sustain propositions over decades and consistently pairs innovation with brand building, though no explicit A&P spend or efficiency figures are given in these excerpts. | The long view had said: A&P has been redirected to digital (51% of media spend, +126% since 2020) and shows up as measurable brand equity — Maggi household penetration up 50% and coffee up 130% between 2016 and 2024.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Good → Neutral. Now: There is a dedicated Nestlé R&D Centre India and a data-and-analytics transformation programme, but the advantage described is recipe/formulation and process capability rather than a deep proprietary multi-product technology edge. | The long view had said: There is real applied food-science depth accessed via the Nestlé R&D Centre and Purina's 90 years and 500+ scientists, but it is parent-owned capability in a low-R&D-intensity category rather than a proprietary multi-product tech edge in the listed entity.

*Unchanged: Research and development; Candor; Mini-monopolies; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Asset turn; Merger and acquisition; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Distribution advantage; Geographical expansion; Gain in market share; Convenience benefits; Halo effect (caution); Structural end-market growth; Cyclical market growth; Out of limelight; Intangible benefits; Profit margin; Security by obscurity; Disciplined stewards; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Cost of working capital; Assurance benefits.*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Demand is packaged-food consumption (flow/consumable), not customer capex; across the four calls management reports 17 of 22 quarters of double-digit domestic growth, 8%+ volume-led CAGR and operating margins held in the historic 20-21% band even through the worst input inflation, with margin recovering to 21.1% in Q4 2022. Commodity inflation dents input costs but the demand base and margin band are affirmatively stable, and the company invested ~INR 5,800 crores of capex 2020-2025 while others held back. (The long view had said: Demand is flow/consumable and household-driven (penetration-led volume growth, 5.3m outlets, two of three Indian households), not tied to any customer's capex; but input commodity cycles visibly swing margins — 2022 commodity inflation ran 5x the prior CAGR and cut H1 profit from operations from 21% to 18.2%, and commodity inflation is still listed as a live challenge in Feb 2025. Management also invested countercyclically (INR 5,800 cr capex 2020-25, 35%+ capacity added, 10th factory) through the demand slowdown.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Government Dependency (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
