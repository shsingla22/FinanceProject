### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 4 strengthened, 0 weakened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Realisations rose through the year (price gain 4% in Q1, 3% in Q2, +INR5/bag in Q3) alongside volume growth 2-5x the industry, and management claims hikes are sticking; but Q4 shows cost escalation of ~INR25/bag not passed through, and pricing is described as market-driven region by region rather than company-set. (The long view had said: Premiumisation is genuine and rising (premium cement 26% of trade sales in Q2 FY25 to 35–36% by FY26, Kawach/ACC Gold, +INR5/bag realisation in Q3 FY26), but this is mix, not price: Q4 FY26 realisation was flat at INR254/bag and management concedes the industry cannot pass through cost inflation.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: TRA 'most trusted cement brand' for a fourth year, marquee-project references (Chenab bridge, Navi Mumbai airport), and premium products (Ambuja Kawach, ACC Gold) at 35-36% of trade sales support a real premium; offsetting this, the ACC identity is being folded into an amplified Adani Cement brand and branding spend was a named cost overrun in Q4. (The long view had said: ACC/Ambuja carry genuine heritage (ACC incorporated 1936) and management leans on it plus large activations reaching 300 million individuals, yet the brand is simultaneously being transitioned to 'Adani Cement' at meaningful one-off cost, and branding spend is cited as a cost drag rather than a pricing lever.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is product-mix innovation (premium SKUs, 13 blenders being installed) and digital/AI investment (CiNOC operations centre, AI cost-optimiser tools), but no disclosed R&D spend, no share-of-industry-R&D claim, and R&D is mentioned mainly in an ESG-scores context. (The long view had said: There is credible process innovation — CiNOC AI operations centre, first commercial-scale Coolbrooks RotoDynamic kiln electrification, IIT Bombay/Sweden carbon capture pilot, and 'R&D-backed customized cements' flagged for FY27 — but no R&D spend is ever disclosed and no named product is tied to a revenue number.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Share gains are quantified in every call — 15.5% (+2%) in Q1, 16.6% (+1%) in Q2, volumes at 2x industry in Q3, FY26 volume +16% vs an industry growing ~4-5% — and margins improved alongside (EBITDA/t up 28-32% YoY), though much of the gain is inorganic (Orient, Penna, Sanghi) rather than purely won in the market. (The long view had said: Share has risen from ~15% (Q2 FY25) to 16.6% (Q3 FY26) with volumes at 2x industry and 16% FY26 volume growth, but the gain is largely bought — Sanghi, Penna, Orient, Asian, Tuticorin — and ex-Orient Q4 FY26 volumes were flat, with acquired assets still at 57%/46% utilisation.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 5 unchanged. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Cement is a substitutable commodity where the non-trade channel is openly price-sensitive and prices have historically been 'rollback-prone', so the premium is not structurally protected; the company is defending it through branding spend that itself pushed Q4 costs up rather than through product complexity. (The long view had said: Cement is substitutable and the calls show pricing power is fragile — non-trade prices run INR31/bag below trade and are rolled back, realizations 'hardly moved Q-o-Q versus for peers... up 1.5% to 2%', and in FY26 the industry absorbed INR400-500/t of cost without passing it on; the dealer/contractor intermediary largely steers the end-customer's choice.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No single customer, licensor or supplier is named as driving results in any of the four calls; demand comes through a broad dealer/channel network with 10% trade volume growth, and management describes deepening engagement across many channel partners, CREDAI, BAI and NAREDCO rather than any one relationship. (The long view had said: Customer base is broad (dealer network expansion, 70,000+ contractors/influencers, trade at 74%), but the input side leans hard on the promoter group and a few counterparties — a single 16 lakh tonne capped-price coal contract, group 'adjacencies' in coal, logistics and renewable power, fly ash sourced from thermal power plants, and repeated analyst questioning on related-party structure requiring a Grant Thornton RPT review.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls demand and pricing are described in cycle terms — monsoon, elections, geopolitics, infra offtake — and costs/margins swung materially (cost per tonne INR4,200 in Sep-25, INR4,500 in Dec-25, FY26 exit target of INR4,000 missed at INR4,400, plus a March cost spike of ~INR25/bag from the West Asia war), all while the group commits to a large capacity build from 109 to 155 MTPA by FY28 with fuel, freight and packing costs outside its control. (The long view had said: Across the 13 calls the business swings with construction demand, fuel/coal prices and a self-inflicted capex cycle: EBITDA margin moved 6.1%→14.4% (Q3FY23) on fuel cost collapse, then FY26 saw cost per tonne rise to INR4,500 with pricing 'under relentless pressure and not able to pass on', and the 140mt-by-FY28 / 155mt-by-FY28 expansion was pushed to 'FY '30' with capex cut from INR7,500cr to INR6,000-6,500cr — expansion into a peak followed by recalibration is the textbook pattern.)

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); New Entrants (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
