**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Profit margins swing by ±5.7 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: New entrant capacity is actively arriving in the main revenue segment and pricing has been irrational enough to drive salt realisations down ~10% year-on-year. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±8.9 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: New entrants and capacity additions are explicitly acknowledged in both bromine domestically and salt in Australia and the Middle East, with resulting price erosion of ~10% — the opposite of rational, protected pricing.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Switching costs are real — customers run multi-month qualification and certification processes and business runs on long-term bilateral contracts with relationships of 5-7 years. | The long view had said: The captive brine reserves and port proximity give a regional cost position, but customers are large global traders buying on renegotiable annual contracts with no switching costs — contracts were reopened and repriced in both directions.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: The customer base is entirely corporate (chlor-alkali majors, oil majors, PTA producers) and the value proposition is correctly matched — reliability, purity and qualification-based lock-in rather than brand intangibles. | The long view had said: The customer base is entirely corporate (52 clients, large trading conglomerates), which is the right context for reliability-based selling, but ACI has not built the switching-cost lock-in that model requires — contracts are repriced to market.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Weak competitors exist to take share from — a distressed peer (Oren Hydrocarbons) was acquired out of insolvency, and India ran as a net bromine importer creating import-substitution headroom. | The long view had said: India being a net bromine importer offers some import-substitution share to take, but no weak incumbent competitors are identified — the newcomers described are expanding, not retreating.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Weak. Now: Accessibility has been a liability rather than an advantage this year, with logistics failures costing ~250,000 tons of shipments and road construction doubling effective transport distance from plant to port. | The long view had said: Port proximity and a 300-truck stockyard fleet give real logistical access, but that same logistics chain has been the chronic failure point — monsoon, Cyclone Asna and the Kutch road diversion that doubled haul distance.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Weak. Now: Technology is a weakness, not an edge — the SOP process requires a full re-engineering after repeated failures, and bromine plant efficiency problems suppressed production for multiple quarters. | The long view had said: The green natural-brine SOP route is a real process differentiator with REACH certification, but ACI has been unable to operate it reliably for four years, and its semiconductor technology is licensed via a 21% minority stake rather than owned.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Weak. Now: Growth capex is large and rising (INR2,067 crore semiconductor phase 1, ~INR190 of INR250 crore spent on bromine derivatives, SOP plant rebuild) but incremental returns are so far absent — derivatives sit at 30-45% utilisation with margins under pressure and SOP delivered only 644 tons / INR3.5 crore in FY26. | The long view had said: Capex has been unambiguously growth-oriented (bromine 28.5k→42k tpa, Rs.250cr Jhagadia derivatives, brine-field expansion, SiC fab), but incremental returns have been poor — ROCE fell from 40%+ in FY23 to consolidated FY26 PAT of INR105cr with EBITDA down 17%.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 0 weakened, 7 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: NO FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: No heritage story, A&P spend, distributor leverage or brand-led adjacency appears in the FY26 calls; the company sells commodity and intermediate chemicals on specification. (The long view had said: A commodity export model selling ~75% overseas to trading conglomerates and large industrials, with price set by global contract/spot dynamics; across 14 calls there is no A&P spend, heritage story, consistency promise or brand-led adjacency — only product specs and tonnage.)
- **Corporate Culture**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management does disclose bad news promptly and specifically (the September 2025 income-tax search, plant technical failures, logistics and volume shortfalls), but the promise-versus-delivery record inside the window is weak — SOP slipped from 'meaningful contributions in H2 FY26' to H2 FY27 at just 644 tons, derivatives never reached the repeated '>50% by year-end' target (45%), and leadership has churned (MD, ED and CFO all changed across FY24-FY26). (The long view had said: Promises made in early calls were not delivered: derivatives 'commercial production and billing before FY2024 end' only reached 30-45% utilisation by FY26; SOP has been 'meaningful contributions next year' since Feb 2023 yet produced 644 tons (~INR3.5 crore) in FY26; bromine at ~20,000t against a 42,000t plan. Leadership is unstable (MD Pendurthi to EVC and Swaminathan in FY26, two CFO changes, Kannan exit). Management is candid about cyclone losses and technical failures, but candour without delivery does not meet the pattern.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The legacy brine business is not technology-threatened, but management has bet large capital on two high-innovation-rate industries where it has no incumbency — a compound semiconductor fab in Odisha and zinc-bromide batteries via an 18.14% stake in Offgrid Energy Labs, the latter competing in a lithium-dominated storage market; separately the SOP process has failed repeatedly at scale, requiring a full re-engineering. (The long view had said: The core marine-chemicals process is stable and low-innovation, but management has voluntarily placed large capital into two high-innovation-rate industries — a UK silicon-carbide wafer fab/MOSFET business and zinc-bromide batteries competing against entrenched lithium chemistries — with 2-3 year concept-to-commercialisation roadmaps and no revenue yet across five quarters of updates.)
- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: New supply rather than a new business format is resetting salt economics — management flags rising competitive intensity from capacity additions in Australia and the Middle East, with realisations down about 10% year-on-year and market-share defence named as an explicit Q4 objective. (The long view had said: Analysts pressed on new Indian bromine capacity being built by other players, and by FY26 management concedes competitive intensity is high in salt with new capacity in Australia and the Middle East, contributing to salt pricing down ~10% year-on-year; these are conventional capacity additions rather than a structurally different format, so the reset is to price, not to the industry model.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: These are unbranded industrial inputs where price is the principal lever — salt pricing fell ~10% against incoming low-cost capacity, and in derivatives management notes end-users are 'struggling to pass on the cost increase', limiting the ability to defend a premium; the counterweight is specification rather than brand. (The long view had said: Bromine and industrial salt are undifferentiated commodities where the buyer awards contracts on price — management admits it cannot fully pass bromine cost increases through to derivative customers and had to withdraw volumes from products where cost impact was highest, evidencing thin pricing power rather than a defended premium.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (QUANT FLAG).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
