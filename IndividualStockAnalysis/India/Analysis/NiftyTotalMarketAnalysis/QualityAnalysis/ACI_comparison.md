Now: There are weak players to take from — India remains a net importer of bromine (import substitution runway) and ACI literally acquired a failed competitor's assets out of NCLT. | The long view had said: The only weak competitor evident is the distressed Oren Hydrocarbons acquired through NCLT; elsewhere the competitive set is strengthening and adding capacity rather than donating share.

(The long view had said: There is a genuine structural cost advantage in SOP/bromine from natural sea brine and a stated cost focus that intensified under the new MD in FY26 (aggressive cost reduction, logistics capability, technology/automation lever), but FY26 shows costs running away — 50% fuel inflation, INR14-15 crore logistics hit, EBITDA margin falling from 45% (Q3FY23) to 21.8% (Q4FY26) — so a hard-to-copy cost culture is not yet demonstrated.)

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The core salt/bromine franchise faces no disruption discussion, but management is betting large capital on step-change technologies outside its base: a ₹2,067 crore phase-1 compound semiconductor fab and a zinc-bromide battery investee targeting a gigafactory, while the SOP process itself has failed to scale, requiring 're-engineering of the entire product system and the manufacturing process' after years of trials. (The long view had said: The core marine chemicals business is a low-innovation extraction process, but from Nov 2024 management deliberately moved capital into two of the most innovation-brutal industries there are — silicon carbide semiconductors (GBP 15m in Clas-SiC, plus the SiCSem fab in Odisha) and zinc-bromide batteries (USD 12m in Offgrid, competing against lithium-ion) — with a self-stated 2-3 year concept-to-commercialisation gap and no revenue yet after ~18 months.)
- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: New industrial salt capacity in Australia and the Middle East is being added into the company's core market, and management links this directly to high competitive intensity and a ~10% y-o-y price decline, with 'market share growth in industrial salt' now framed as a defensive priority. The entrants are conventional producers rather than a structurally different format, which bounds the risk. (The long view had said: By Nov 2024 analysts were pressing management on domestic bromine players adding large capacity, and by May 2026 management conceded competitive intensity is high in salt with new capacity in Australia and the Middle East — which shows up directly as ~10% lower salt realisations. This is incremental capacity from conventional competitors rather than a structurally different format, so it resets pricing but not industry economics.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Industrial salt — ~70% of standalone revenue — is a bulk commodity where the only claimed differentiation is purity, and with new low-cost capacity arriving prices fell ~10% and management resorted to 'commercial and pricing actions' and 'aggressive cost reduction' rather than holding a premium. Bromine derivatives face the same squeeze, with margins pressured and end users 'struggling to pass on the cost increase'. (The long view had said: The core products are undifferentiated commodities with no brand premium to defend — pricing is negotiated against spot benchmarks and management admits it cannot pass costs through in derivatives, having to 'adjust volumes' on products where cheaper competition bit. The FY26 collapse in derivatives contribution margin at 45% utilisation shows limited protection; the answer being attempted is niche specification (15 new products, certifications, REACH-certified green-route SOP) rather than price defence.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH).*

*Not comparable: Shifting Consumer Preferences (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
