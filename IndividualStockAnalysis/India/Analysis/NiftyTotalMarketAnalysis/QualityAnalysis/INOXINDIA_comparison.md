### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 5 weakened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

**That strengthened:**

Why now: Margins expanded through FY26 (Q2 EBITDA +18% on 16% revenue growth; FY26 EBITDA margin 23.8% at or above guidance) and US orders were won despite tariffs, implying customers absorbed cost rather than switching — but no call in the window discusses explicit price increases, premiumisation pricing or elasticity. Mix-led premiumisation is real (high-value aerospace, non-standard kegs at higher margins), but no evidence of repeated above-inflation price rises.)

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Within the window promises were kept — the 10x LNG capacity build flagged in Q1 progressed to a commissioned automated line at Kalol by Q3, FY26 EBITDA margin landed at or above earlier guidance, and management disclosed the LNG truck softness and tariff headwinds rather than glossing over them; but the one-year lens gives no view of multi-year promise-keeping or capital-allocation discipline, and the chairman roster shows recent churn. (The long view had said: Promises made are delivered: the FY25 call guided 18-20% revenue and 22-24% EBITDA and FY26 landed at 21.2% growth with 23.8% margin; Savli was promised at IPO, funded from internal accruals and crossed INR200cr in year one. Leadership (Acharya CEO, Logar CFO) is unchanged across all ten calls, the balance sheet stayed debt-free with net cash throughout, and bad news is disclosed plainly — keg value flat despite 31% volume growth, LNG truck adoption 'near-term challenges', MSRTC delays, weak Q3 FY26 fuel-tank offtake.)
- **Toll Roads**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Strong certification/approval anchoring — Heineken, AB InBev and Molson Coors approvals covering >40% of global beer volume, DNV IMO-ISO certification, DOT approval in the US, PESO clearance in India, and repeat ITER qualification — which are classic 'once converted, don't switch' gates; but management never quantifies the product as a tiny share of customer cost or describes negotiation-free pricing. (The long view had said: Strong certification/standards anchoring recurs across the record — IATF 16949 for cryogenic fuel tanks (May 2025), AB InBev 98% global certification, Heineken/Molson Coors and Paulaner approvals, PESO/DOT/PNGRB regulatory approvals, and repeat ITER qualification — and the equipment is mission-critical. But it is capital equipment, not a sub-1% consumable, and no 'customers don't switch once converted' economics are shown.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls management discusses order backlog, dispatches and one-off equipment orders; there is no discussion of AMC/service revenue share, attach rates, renewals, installed-base monetisation or customer advances as a funding source. (The long view had said: Across all ten calls the discussion is entirely order-book and project-execution driven; management never quantifies AMC/service revenue share, attach rates, renewals, installed-base monetisation, or customer advances/deferred revenue as a funding source.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The FY26 calls position the business as high-value, tailor-made and technologically complex with a 23.8% EBITDA margin and rising mix-led margins — the opposite of a low-price, low-unit-margin, high-turns model; no cost-obsession culture or itemised savings passed to price is described. (The long view had said: Management repeatedly cites cost competitiveness against Chinese IMO container makers and the ability to hold US disposable-cylinder share despite Feb-2025 tariffs, but this is India-cost arbitrage rather than a codified cost culture, and the economics are high-margin (23-25% EBITDA), not low-margin/high-turn.)
- **Forward Integrators**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The FY26 route to market is explicitly OEMs, shipyards, industrial gas majors and appointed third-party distributors — no own stores, franchising, royalties or D2C revenue is mentioned anywhere in the four calls. (The long view had said: Only a mild move toward the customer: own distribution partnerships in Germany, Poland and the UK for kegs and a Brazil/Netherlands service-distribution footprint. No own stores, franchising, royalties or D2C revenue share anywhere in the ten calls.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: The calls affirmatively show the premium holding: FY26 EBITDA margin of 23.8% 'in line with or better than guidance' with margin expansion in H1, US orders won repeatedly 'despite the tariff' (7 lakh disposable cylinders in Q3, 2 million units in FY26), and >85% share in Indian LNG semi-trailers. The products sit behind hard qualification barriers — DNV/IMO certification, PESO, DOT approval, ITER site track record, brewery-by-brewery approvals — which is the scale/niche protection that cheaper challengers struggle to replicate. (The long view had said: Part of the portfolio is genuinely commoditisable — disposable cylinders, standard IMO containers and standard kegs — where management defends position on price competitiveness against Chinese and US manufacturers and explicitly contrasts itself with 'commodity-oriented players'. The protection is real but partial: the premium rests on niche, complex, low-volume work rather than on end-customer brand pull.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The business is levered to which clean-fuel format wins — LNG as a 'transition fuel' is explicitly a bet with a finite life, and management already concedes 'the adoption of the LNG truck segment has witnessed some near-term challenges' while simultaneously building 10x LNG fuel tank capacity. The cadence of innovation shown is small-scale and value-adding rather than platform-threatening, and the company is hedged across the possible winners (LNG, green hydrogen, liquid hydrogen storage, ultra-high purity ammonia, CO2 battery storage as an alternative to lithium-ion), which keeps this bounded. (The long view had said: Cryogenics is a mature, physics-constrained engineering discipline and across ten calls no analyst or competitor platform is discussed defensively; instead the company shows a steady cadence of small-scale, value-adding innovation — three patents (FY24), Gen-2 LNG fuel tank, first indigenous 4K helium cryostat, ultra-high-purity ammonia ISO tank, IATF 16949 certification, and an early-stage MoU for liquid-nitrogen data-centre cooling. Energy-transition technology shifts (LNG, hydrogen, fusion, SMR) are positioned as demand drivers rather than threats to an installed base.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
