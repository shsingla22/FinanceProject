# Kirloskar Pneumatic (KIRLPNU) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Kirloskar Pneumatic (KPCL) is a Pune-based Kirloskar Group engineering company that designs and manufactures industrial compressors and the complete packaged systems built around them — air compressors, refrigeration compressors and cooling packages, and gas compression packages for oil, gas, CNG, biogas and hydrogen. It sells business-to-business to industrial customers such as dairy, food processing, fisheries, cold chain and ice plants, pharma, chemicals and fertilizers, cement, metals, mining, power plants, textiles, tyre makers and city gas/CNG station operators. Revenue comes mostly from building and shipping equipment and large engineered packages against a project order book (roughly INR1,900 crores of orders on hand entering 2026, on FY26 total income of INR1,786 crores), plus a growing annuity-like operations-and-maintenance service business on its installed base. It is overwhelmingly a domestic Indian business — exports were only about INR140 crores in FY26, mainly CNG packages to the Middle East/North Africa — and management stresses in-house manufacturing at Hadapsar, Nashik and Saswad, own patents (128+ IPs filed) and import substitution as its edge.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Air Compressor Division (ACD)** — Makes air and gas compressors — the Tezcatlipoca centrifugal compressor (the standout product, 115 ordered in FY26, sold into metals, power, pharma, tyres and textiles), reciprocating and screw compressors, plus the new Hydrino oil-free machine; management says it is roughly 18-20% of the business.
- **Refrigeration Compressors and Systems** — The largest part at roughly 40-45% of the business: ammonia reciprocating compressors (KC/KCX range) that dominate cold chain and ice plants, Khione refrigeration packages sold to dairy, pharma, fisheries and concrete, the new Tyche semi-hermetic compressor replacing European imports, and large engineered process-refrigeration projects.
- **Process Gas Systems (PGS) / Gas Compression** — Roughly 35-40% of the business, supplying upstream, midstream and downstream gas compression packages, CNG station and booster packages (including exports to the MENA region), and biogas (Jarilo) and hydrogen packages — the most volatile part, hit by nearly two years of slow oil-and-gas order finalisation before picking up in early 2026.
- **O&M (operations and maintenance) service business** — Services the company's own installed base, notably managing over 1,000 CNG filling stations across India; described as steadily growing with the installed base, though no revenue figure is given.
- **Precision Engineering Division** — A new unit set up in FY26 selling forgings, castings and precision-machined components made on spare capacity at the Nashik and Hadapsar plants; still small and a non-reportable segment, with management declining to say much until it proves it is not one-time work.
- **Zephyros commercial air conditioning (new segment)** — An in-house-developed commercial air conditioning package aimed at an Indian market management sizes at over INR5,000 crores, applied for under the government PLI scheme, with demonstrator units running at its own plants and commercialisation planned for the first quarter of FY27.
- **Systems & Components (India) Private Limited (subsidiary)** — A process-refrigeration company in which KPCL bought a 55.26% stake in early 2025; small — about INR9 crores of sales in a quarter — and only recently turned profitable, meant to widen KPCL's reach in the process refrigeration market.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Kirloskar Pneumatic's story starts with its engineering. The quality framework gives top marks to research and development, technology advantage, and out-of-limelight, understated management: 57 new intellectual property filings in FY26 took the total past 128, alongside CII recognition as a top-30 IP-driven corporate and a steady stream of revenue-earning launches such as Tezcatlipoca, Khione, Tyche, Janus D, Hydrino and the patented ammonia Zephyros architecture. That same evidence is what makes Innovation Dominance the one pattern rated a strong fit, and it feeds two more: Pricing Power, where FY26 margins genuinely expanded (raw material to sales down 3.1 percentage points, EBITDA of 21.7% against 19%) on the back of less-contested, IP-led products, and Cost to Replicate, where roughly 200 engineers plus in-house forging, foundry and machining form a barrier a copycat would need years and heavy spending to assemble. Behind it sits a conservative balance sheet — debt-free with INR460 crores of net cash, self-funded capex of about INR100 crores in FY25 and INR90 crores in FY26 plus a INR320 crores PLI commitment, all directed at new capacity rather than upkeep — and a management team scored highly for candour, having opened a weak quarter by naming its own misjudgement, disclosing an INR2 crores bad-debt write-off and telling investors not to extrapolate. Management scores highest of all the quality areas at 1.67 out of 2.

The awkward part is that the very choice which produces the good margins also produces the biggest realised loss. Management explicitly refuses the low-price route — which is why Low Price Plus rates only partial despite a real cost programme in backward integration, solar power and import substitution — and in gas distribution that refusal cost the company market share to cheaper rivals plus the INR2 crores write-off, with Calana boosters shipped in record numbers at near-zero margin and screw compressors described as a laggard for the year. So the Good Enough Goods risk is rated elevated and New Entrants a watch, and Market Share Gainers lands only at likely fit: real gains in some lines (Tezcatlipoca past 100 units and now the industry standard, Tyche taking share from over 1,000 imported compressors, air compressors still at only about 5% share) sit next to admitted losses in others. The pattern screen also finds nothing to lock customers in — Toll Roads is a clear no fit, because compressors are large capital purchases for the buyer, not a cheap consumable, and the October 2025 call documents customers trading down rather than staying put. Brand Strength and Friendly Middleman rest largely on the Kirloskar group name and one go-to-market plan rather than any measured channel or distribution advantage, which is why the industry structure area scores lowest of all at 0.33.

The rest of the risk register comes back to who pays the bills. Revenue depends on other companies' capital spending — packages, CNG stations, refinery and oil-and-gas projects — so Cyclicality is rated high risk, and this is the same fact behind the negative persistence-of-growth check: sales have averaged around 20% a year but arrive in lumps, as shown when 27% order-book growth in FY25 gave way to a weak first half of FY26. Stakeholder Concentration is a watch for the same reason, since roughly INR600 crores of large packages sat in the FY25 order book and their absence drove the miss, although the FY26 order book of INR1,939 crores contains no such large orders and spreads across cement, metals, pharma, tyre, textiles, dairy and fisheries. Government Dependency is the other high risk: CNG city-gas distribution, gas pricing, biogas and hydrogen programmes are policy-set, and the PLI application actually gates the Zephyros ramp — the same PLI commitment counted as growth capex on the strength side. The offsets are the growing service annuity on more than 1,000 CNG stations under maintenance, which is the basis of the partial Recurring Revenue rating, and process gas at 35-40% of revenue sitting at a five-year trough with a turn beginning, scored as a cyclical tailwind into FY27. That balance is what the combined rating of 63 out of 100 reflects: business quality at 71 and multibagger fit at 72 are pulled down by risk safety of 38, with overall resilience judged sound and the verdict Decent.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Government Dependency, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 2 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is tied to customers' capex — packages, CNG stations, refinery/oil-and-gas projects — and the FY26 trajectory shows exactly the swing this channel warns about: after 27% order-book growth and 18.6% YTD EBITDA in FY25, H1 FY26 saw order booking, sales and profit all fall year-on-year, with Tezcatlipoca units delivered but sitting uncommissioned at customer sites. Management repeatedly concedes lumpy, package-driven revenue that cannot be judged quarter-on-quarter.

> "Capex spend, except in few government-led sectors is still very hesitant." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Growing operations-linked flow business — O&M on 1,000+ CNG stations, aftersales, and the deliberate shift to short-cycle 'Equipment business' targeted at 20-25% of sales; consumption-driven food/dairy/pharma demand kept 'booming' while project work stalled; debt-free with INR460 crores net cash, so capex continued (INR165 crores over two years) through the downturn.*

- ⚠️ Year-to-year sales growth swings by ±85 percentage points — boom-and-bust revenue, not a steady compounder.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 7% and 20% (±4.2 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 10% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 4% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** A large share of demand sits on policy-determined infrastructure: CNG city-gas distribution, gas availability and pricing, biogas and hydrogen programmes whose scale-up management repeatedly defers, and a PLI application that gates the Zephyros ramp; management itself says capex is hesitant 'except in few government-led sectors' and flags 'uncertainty around several green initiatives'. Biogas is the textbook case — orders won, then 'a period of quiet' pending unresolved generation economics.

> "There is uncertainty and a halting progress in several sectors coming from array of reasons, weakening rupee, oil price, gas availability, uncertainty around several green initiatives, et cetera." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate diversification away from policy-driven revenue: PGS dependence reduced, refrigeration now 40-45% of business anchored in food/dairy/cold chain, plus the new Precision Engineering division.*
