### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 5. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 3 weakened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: Across all four calls there is no discussion of realised price increases, rate cards, billing-rate movement or elasticity; the margin step-up is attributed to back-end automation and G&A right-sizing, so there is no evidence either way on pricing power. (The long view had said: Management is explicitly pivoting to outcome-based pricing ('priced on outcomes, not hours') and claims the highest-margin agentic work, and margins did expand sharply; but across 14 calls there is no instance of price increases taken without volume loss — margin gains are attributed to SG&A leverage, FX, third-party cost and ESOP reduction, and the AI shift is inherently deflationary to effort-based billing.)

**That weakened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Named assets recur every quarter with disclosed spend and client counts — ForgeX, CodeInsightAI, Integration Studio, XtenderAI, Encora's AIVA, and Nuuron in Q1 FY27, with ~$58m invested in AI innovation in FY26, 8 AI platforms and 22 AI assets deployed across 54 clients — but at ~3% of revenue this is not outsized R&D funded by high gross margins, and no share-of-industry-R&D claim is made. (The long view had said: Every call names new capability with revenue impact and quantified spend — 6-lever AI strategy and 100+ deployed solutions (Jul-23), 200+ solutions and the Quasar marketplace (May-25), OneAI/CodeInsightAI/ModSquads (May-26), Nuuron plus '$58mn invested in AI innovation', 8 AI platforms, 22 AI assets, 11,000 data/AI practitioners (Jul-26) — and 86% of revenue now comes from that AI/data/cloud core.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Revenue is won by direct enterprise selling — a proprietary 'industry solutioning and AI specialist cadre' crafting proactive large deals with CIO-level buyers — not through independent professionals or installers who specify the product for a paying end customer. (The long view had said: Some third-party endorsement dynamics exist — Pega Global Elite Partner, Tier-1 MSSP for Zscaler, ServiceNow CoE, Duck Creek/Guidewire ecosystem leadership and Everest/ISG leader ratings shape buyer shortlists — but the buyer is the client CIO/procurement itself, deals are negotiated and competed for, so the classic prescriber-pays-nothing structure is absent.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Management explicitly positions away from cheap labour arbitrage and toward premium AI-led engineering, and the FY26/FY27 story is margin expansion (EBIT 12.3%→16.6% in Q4, 16.0% combined in Q1 FY27) from automation and right-sizing rather than passing savings into price. (The long view had said: There is a real cost engine — offshore mix at 51% as 'structural margin support' (Jul-23), G&A capped in absolute terms via AI, 40% reduction in Encora G&A, planned exit of a $20m low-margin India portfolio — but the savings are retained for margin expansion (EBIT 12.3%→16.6% exit) rather than passed to customers as a low-price proposition.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Government exposure is small and shrinking by choice — 'Government Outside India' is 6.0% of Q1 FY27 revenue and management announced the planned closure/exit of the India government business (and the data centre business), taking a reported growth hit to do so; no pricing, tariff or subsidy dependence is discussed anywhere in the window. (The long view had said: A named "Government outside India" vertical runs ~6% of revenue and has been a called-out growth driver (27.1% in FY25, 17.5% in FY26), one of the top 10 clients is a public-sector client, and UK public framework agreements are repeatedly flagged as a future revenue stream tied to a trade deal — policy-driven revenue that management cannot control.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Every call in the window is dominated by AI resetting the industry's delivery and pricing model — management itself says the industry must pivot from 'scale-of-people' to 'scale-of-intelligence' and that disruption will create 'outsized winners and legacy laggards', and it paid $2.35bn for Encora largely to buy AI-native engineering DNA rather than build it. This is large-scale, not incremental, innovation, and the incumbent effort-based revenue model is the thing being displaced. (The long view had said: This is the single dominant theme across the calls and it is industry-level, not company-level: management states outright that "labor as a default has been disrupted" and that firms "that continue to bill hours are getting left behind" — the effort/headcount business model that IT services was built around is the infrastructure being obsoleted. The offsetting response is genuine and large (OneAI/Nuuron platforms, $58m AI investment, 11,000 AI practitioners, 30% of engagements AI-enabled), but the outcome of a platform shift of this scale is unknowable, and Coforge is adding headcount into it (46,228 people).)
- **New Entrants**: NO SIGNAL → WATCH. Why now: Management describes a structurally different competitor set — AI-native firms and 'digital native innovation velocity' outpacing 'legacy SIs' — and explicitly positions Encora as an 'AI-led engineering disruptor' that is 'not a labor arbitrage play'; the response was to buy the new-format player rather than defend the old one. (The long view had said: The calls contain no discussion of structurally different competitors entering the market, no defensive commentary about new-format rivals, and no discounting framed as a competitive response.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
