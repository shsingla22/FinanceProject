- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: Bata is a widely covered listed household brand with multiple broker-hosted calls, the opposite of an ignored niche. | The long view had said: Bata is a heavily covered large-cap consumer name with calls hosted by ICICI, YES, B&K and 360 ONE and questions from Goldman Sachs, Fidelity, JM and IIFL — the opposite of an ignored niche.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: The customer base is retail consumers and management matches it correctly with brand campaigns, price-point architecture and store experience rather than TCO arguments. | The long view had said: The customer base is retail, where intangible brand pull is the right lever, yet Bata's brand equity is eroding with younger buyers; the corporate/industrial (I&D safety) leg with 2,000+ KRO reach is growing but still small.

Now: Spend is skewed to remodelling existing stores (ZBM) and asset-light franchise/SIS rather than large new-store growth capex, with an analyst explicitly flagging almost no net retail additions during the year. | The long view had said: Capex has been steady on store renovations, franchise/SIS expansion, ERP and moulds across all four years, but incremental returns are poor — revenue was flat-to-negative through FY24-FY26 and the company has been monetising land and closing plants rather than adding productive capacity.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Weak → Neutral. Now: Technology claims are limited to consumer-facing comfort features (Easy Slide, Stamina) described as democratizing rather than proprietary, with no R&D intensity disclosed. | The long view had said: Technology is applied (ERP/MS Dynamics, ZBM, Bata app, Power product tech) but it is catch-up operational IT rather than a proprietary edge, and the ERP was a large expense charged off rather than a differentiator.

Now: The market is highly fragmented with roughly 20,000 multi-brand outlets carrying competing brands and consumers freely trading across price points, not a stable oligopoly. | The long view had said: Management explicitly describes the market at both the value and premium ends as highly fragmented rather than consolidated.

Now: Management names weak quarters plainly, admits misjudging GST disruption, and itemises four exceptional items to bridge a reported 94% PBT decline to an 11% like-for-like figure. | The long view had said: Management openly labels its own results disappointing, discloses negative same-store sales, walks analysts line-by-line through four exceptional items, and concedes shortfalls — though it declines to share channel- and brand-level growth data.

Now: Certain products own a place in the consumer's mind under the Bata name and brand consideration hit its highest level, though this is brand salience rather than a locked position. | The long view had said: Bata owns a genuinely iconic position in the Indian consumer's mind, but that mindshare is ageing into 'legacy' among younger buyers and confers no monopoly-like economics.

Now: The articulate initiative narrative outruns the financial result — flat-to-3-5% growth and gross margins that have not yet reflected lower discounting — so process quality should not be read as proven skill. | The long view had said: The narrative quality (ZBM, product funnel, brand consideration) far outruns the financial outcome, so the strong storytelling should be discounted — turnaround has been promised since 2022 and delivered only 3-5% growth by FY26.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 1 weakened, 7 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Heritage (incorporated 1931), sustained and deliberately rising A&P — two-plus consecutive quarters of double-digit marketing growth — a rising brand-consideration score, and a stable of stretchable sub-brands (Power, Hush Puppies, Floatz, Victoria Ballerina); but the pattern's other half, premium pricing plus share gain, is not evidenced in the window. (The long view had said: Heritage and reach are real and repeatedly evidenced — highest-ever brand consideration of 66, high and climbing unaided awareness, sustained A&P at 250-300bps with two consecutive quarters of double-digit marketing growth (Feb/Jun 2026), and stretch into Floatz, Power Apparel and Nine West. But the brand does not convert into the pattern's proof points: no share gain and no premium pricing, and management is directly challenged on Bata being seen as a legacy brand by younger consumers with the answer still 'in works'.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Named launches each quarter with disclosed revenue traction (Power Easy Slide at 4,000 pairs/week targeting 10,000, Power Stamina at Rs.4,000+, Hush Puppies iconic collection to Rs.20,000, Floatz +30%) and a 'product funnel' redesign promised and then detailed; but no R&D spend disclosure, no industry R&D share, and gross margins are not high-and-rising to fund it. (The long view had said: Named launches recur each year with revenue traction — Sneaker Studios (125 in 2022 to 740 by 2024), Floatz at ~4.5% of retail, Power technology launches, Nine West, and the reimagined product funnel plus zero-based merchandising scaled 400 to 550 stores by Jun 2026. But no R&D spend is ever disclosed, innovation has not yet lifted growth or gross margin, and management concedes most of the product investment is not yet visible.)
- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Same three initiatives tracked with the same metrics across all four calls, with promises delivered: ZBM guided at '50 stores a quarter' from ~200 then reported at 400 and 550; a promised deeper disclosure on the product funnel delivered the following quarter; weak quarters and the GST disruption owned plainly, and Q4's four exceptional items itemised to a like-for-like PBT bridge rather than buried. MD Gunjan Shah has led since FY2022. (The long view had said: Bad news is disclosed plainly and accounting is conservative — the disappointing quarter owned in Aug 2024, full charge-off of ERP costs, exceptional items itemised in Jun 2026 — and Gunjan Shah has led continuously since FY2022 delivering the franchise, inventory and ZBM promises made in earlier calls. Against that, the headline double-digit growth aspiration has gone undelivered for years, the finance seat turned over three times (Vidhya Srinivasan, Anil Somani, Amit Aggarwal) and the board list churned heavily.)

**That weakened:**

- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Demand is driven by direct consumer marketing — influencer-led campaigns (Prajakta Koli, Vir Das), brand consideration tracking and price-point checkouts — not by a professional prescriber choosing on the buyer's behalf; the MBO/KRO trade programme is a distribution reward scheme, not expert lock-in. (The long view had said: No prescriber/installer/professional intermediary is described anywhere; sales are direct-to-consumer through own stores, franchise, MBOs and e-commerce. Franchisee and MBO engagement programmes are trade distribution, not an expert recommender who chooses on the customer's behalf.)

*Unchanged: Forward Integrators (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Market Share Gainers (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 0 worsened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

Why now: Two structural shifts run through the calls — casualisation/athleisure away from traditional formal footwear, and the migration of buying online — and management is explicitly rebuilding the portfolio and channel mix around both, with e-commerce called the fastest-growing channel and casual/premium brands outpacing the core. (The long view had said: The demand base under Bata's legacy formal/dress franchise has migrated to casual, sneakers and athleisure and from stores to online, and every call from Aug 2022 to Jun 2026 is a running account of pivoting to chase it — sneaker studios from 125 to 740, casualization, Power athleisure, Floatz, Nine West, e-commerce; the pivot's growth has not offset the erosion, with four years of roughly flat revenue and a sneaker proposition still 'in progress' in Jun 2026.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (ELEVATED).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Fashion Risk (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
