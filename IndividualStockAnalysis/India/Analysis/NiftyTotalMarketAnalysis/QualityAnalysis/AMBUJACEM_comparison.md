Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.4 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. Now: The Indian market is consolidating toward a top-two structure and pricing discipline is visibly improving, though end customers still retain negotiating power in non-trade. | The long view had said: The industry is consolidating meaningfully — top-5 share from ~45% in 2018 to 60% — but customers still hold pricing power, as evidenced by rollback-prone pricing and the inability to pass on costs.

- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Evidence of an early-cycle pricing upturn — January hikes sticking after years of rollbacks, GST 2.0 tailwinds — though Q4 flagged cost inflation and a moderated 5-5.5% industry growth outlook. | The long view had said: Pricing is at a cyclical trough but management guides to a further softening — industry growth cut to 5-5.5% with weak monsoon and inflation — so no identifiable upturn is yet in sight.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: Growth is predominantly volume-led (20% YoY) with only 3-4% price gain; mix is a genuine positive (premium at 35% of trade sales, up 31% absolute) but does not yet outweigh volume as the driver. | The long view had said: Growth is overwhelmingly volume- and capacity-led, with pricing essentially flat — INR254/bag versus INR255 a year earlier and only INR1 QoQ — and management conceding the industry cannot pass on cost increases.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 0 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Share rose from ~13.5% to 15.5% in Q1 FY26 and to 16.6% in Q2, with volumes growing 20% YoY versus ~4% industry (5x) and 2x industry in Q3; crucially the gain came with EBITDA/t up 28-32% YoY and a debt-free balance sheet, not bought with profitability — though a large part is acquisition-led (Orient, Penna, Sanghi) rather than organic. (The long view had said: Share numbers rise explicitly and repeatedly — 14% with a 20% FY28 target (Aug 2024) to 16.6% (Q3 FY26), capacity 67.5mt to 109mt, volumes at 2x industry — but much of it is bought (Sanghi, Penna, Orient), acquired-asset utilisation is still 46-57%, and analysts noted Q4 FY26 volumes were roughly flat ex-Orient.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Four consecutive years as TRA's most trusted cement brand, marquee-project supply (Chenab arch bridge, Navi Mumbai airport), premium sub-brands Ambuja Kawach and ACC Gold at 35-36% of trade sales growing 28-31% YoY, and acquired Orient/Penna/Sanghi volumes were immediately rebadged under Ambuja/ACC with customers accepting it — a direct test of brand stretch that passed. (The long view had said: Genuine heritage brands (Ambuja, ACC) plus premium SKUs Kawach and ACC Gold at 35-36% of trade sales, sustained A&P (300 million reach, brand-transition spend that itself inflated Q3 FY26 cost), but the brand has not converted into premium realisation at the aggregate level — realisation was flat Q-o-Q while peers rose 1.5-2%.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: FY26 showed price gains of 4% (Q1) and 3% (Q2) with 20% volume growth and premium cement at 35-36% of trade sales, plus Jan-26 hikes of INR15-20/bag in the South holding without rollback; but cement pricing is regional and cyclical, realisation gains (INR5/bag) lagged the INR25/bag March cost spike, and management could give no forward pricing guidance. (The long view had said: The trajectory shows the opposite of pricing power: FY26 Q4 realisation was ₹254/bag versus ₹255 a year earlier, hikes have historically been rollback-prone, and in May 2026 management said a ₹400-500/t industry cost increase could not be passed through. Premiumisation (35% of trade) has only partly offset this.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The entire margin thesis rests on a premiumisation spread that must be actively defended — trade pricing sits up to INR31/bag above non-trade, and Q4 FY26 costs rose partly on 'higher branding cost' as trade sales were pushed, i.e. the premium is sustained by marketing spend against commodity-grade cement that dealers and masons can substitute; management also concedes competitors currently hold a power-cost advantage. (The long view had said: Cement is a near-commodity where the premium is hard to defend: non-trade realisations run up to INR31/bag below trade, pricing is described as 'rollback-prone', the industry 'is not able to pass on' a INR400-500/t cost increase, and Q4FY26 realisation was flat QoQ while peers gained 1.5-2% — and the buying decision is heavily intermediated by contractors and masons, which is why branding and sales-promotion spend rose enough to be named as a cost driver.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Results are driven by a broad dealer/retail channel rather than any named customer, licensor or supplier; management describes widening channel engagement (CREDAI, BAI, NAREDCO, CEO Samvaad, Dhanvarsha) and no analyst question on a single relationship's renewal appears in the excerpts. (The long view had said: No single customer matters — sales run through a broadening dealer/trade network (trade share 65%→74%) — but the input side leans on the promoter group and analysts pressed on it from the first call: a 16-lakh-tonne coal contract, and explicit reliance on Adani Group 'adjacencies' for coal, logistics, ports and renewable power, with related-party approvals between Ambuja and ACC raised to Rs 3,000 cr.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
