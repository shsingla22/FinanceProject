### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Repeated across all four calls: fourth consecutive year as TRA's most trusted cement brand, marquee-project references (Chenab bridge, Navi Mumbai airport), premium products Ambuja Kawach and ACC Gold at 35-36% of trade sales growing 28-31% Y-o-Y, acquired Orient/Penna volumes migrated onto the Ambuja and ACC brands within days, and stepped-up branding spend in Q4 — brand is delivering both share gain and price gain. (The long view had said: Ambuja and ACC are genuine legacy brands with heritage, large activation spend (outreach to 300 million individuals) and premium sub-brands (Kawach, ACC Gold) at 35-36% of trade sales; but brand strength has not converted into premium realisation — Q4 FY26 realisations were flat versus peers up 1.5-2% — and branding cost is repeatedly cited as a cost headwind rather than a margin source.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Realisations rose across the window (price gain of 4% in Q1, 3% in Q2, +INR5/bag in Q3) alongside volume growth, with management claiming hikes are sticking unlike prior years; but Q4 FY26 shows roughly INR25/bag of cost escalation the company could not offset, so pricing power is cyclical rather than proven above cost inflation. (The long view had said: The FY26 calls explicitly document the absence of pricing power: realisations rose just INR1/bag QoQ to INR254 while industry costs rose INR400-500/tonne, prices are described as 'rollback-prone', and Q4 FY26 admits the industry cannot pass on cost — the INR5/bag YoY gain is a premium/trade mix effect, not price increases above cost inflation.)

**That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: the calls are silent and no computable check supports the pattern. (The long view had said: Across all 16 calls the revenue discussion is entirely bagged/bulk cement volume, realisation per bag and cost per tonne; there is no mention of licence fees, AMC/service contracts, subscriptions, attach rates, renewals or deferred revenue as a funding source.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The calls describe capacity being assembled with capital and acquisitions — Orient, Penna, Sanghi bought and integrated within the window, 15 mt of debottlenecking at $48/t, 104.5 to 155 mt targeted by FY28 — which is precisely the demonstration that a well-funded entrant can build this position in a few years; no time-based, data-based or location-scarcity barrier is articulated. (The long view had said: The only genuinely hard-to-replicate assets cited are finite and licence-gated — limestone blocks won at auction (275mt added in one quarter alone, new Assam block) and plant/grinding locations — but everything else management points to (WHRS, green power, rakes, digital, premium SKUs) is capex a well-funded rival can and does buy, which is precisely why the top-5 share is only ~60% and pricing remains contested.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Cement is a low-complexity product where the intermediary (dealer, contractor, mason) heavily shapes choice, and the calls show the premium having to be actively bought: branding and sales-promotion costs rose materially in Q4 specifically because the company pushed trade and premium mix, and non-trade realisations lag trade by up to INR31 a bag — evidence the price premium only holds in the branded retail channel. Prior-year 'rollback-prone' pricing suggests limited pricing power against cheaper regional supply. (The long view had said: Cement is a near-commodity where the intermediary (contractor, mason, dealer) largely picks the bag: non-trade realisations run up to Rs 31/bag below trade, prices were historically 'rollback-prone', and by Q4 FY26 realisation barely moved (Rs 254 vs Rs 255 a bag Y-o-Y) while costs rose — showing limited pricing power against cheaper alternatives. The defence is an explicit, sustained premiumisation and branding push, whose cost itself dented Q3/Q4 FY26 margins.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No customer, licensor or supplier is named as driving results in any of the four calls; revenue runs through a broad dealer/channel network with 74% trade sales, and institutional engagement is spread across CREDAI, BAI, NAREDCO and CONCOR rather than concentrated in one relationship. Fuel sourcing is discussed as a cost line (kiln fuel INR1.60-1.65/1000 kcal, rising AFR share) with no single-supplier dependency flagged. (The long view had said: No customer concentration — sales run through a wide dealer network and a 65-74% trade mix — but the supply side is anchored on the promoter group: the first call was dominated by analyst questioning of a 16 lakh tonne coal contract and Adani 'adjacencies' in coal, fly ash and logistics, and the 1,000 MW renewable programme sits within the group ecosystem.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
