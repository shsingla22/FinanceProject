# Crompton Greaves Consumer Electricals Ltd. (CROMPTON) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSUMER GOODS*

Crompton Greaves Consumer Electricals is an India-based consumer electricals company with an 85-plus year history that sells everyday household electrical and home products under the Crompton brand — ceiling and other fans (it calls itself the world's number one ceiling fan brand), water pumps for homes and farms, water heaters, air coolers, kitchen and small domestic appliances such as mixer grinders, air fryers and juicers, and LED lighting. It makes money mainly by selling these products to ordinary households through a pan-India dealer and retail network, plus faster-growing 'alternate' channels such as e-commerce, modern retail and rural, and it also sells lighting and solar projects business-to-business (street lighting, industrial projects, state government tenders). Much of the product is manufactured or outsourced to third parties and sold on brand, distribution and after-sales strength; profitability is driven by pricing actions against commodity-cost swings, premiumisation and cost savings, with heavy advertising spend behind the brand. Operations are essentially all in India (head office in Mumbai), and results are reported both standalone and consolidated with its majority-owned subsidiary Butterfly Gandhimathi Appliances; consolidated quarterly revenue was around Rs 1,860 crore in mid-2022 and Rs 2,283 crore in the March 2026 quarter.

**The parts of the business:**

- **ECD (Electrical Consumer Durables)** — The largest part of the company — fans, pumps and appliances sold to households; it drives most of the revenue and profit (standalone quarterly revenue was about Rs 1,959 crore in Q1 FY25 with ECD growing 21%, and ECD EBIT margins were run at roughly 19-20% excluding advertising spend).
- **Fans** — Ceiling and other fans, the flagship category where management says Crompton is the world's number one ceiling fan brand and has steadily gained market share; BLDC (energy-efficient) fans are the current growth push, growing 30%-plus, and the category went through the BEE 2.0 energy-rating regulatory change from January 2026.
- **Pumps** — Water pumps for homes (residential) and farms (agri), plus a newer solar pump business sold largely into government/B2B orders — solar pump execution was around Rs 21 crore in a single quarter in FY25 and was called out again as a growth driver in FY26.
- **LDA (Large Domestic Appliances)** — Water heaters (geysers) and air coolers — a seasonal business that benefits from both hot and cold weather; management says Crompton is now the number 2 water heater brand nationally in general trade, and air cooler volumes grew 68% in one strong summer quarter.
- **SDA (Small Domestic Appliances)** — Small kitchen gadgets led by mixer grinders, plus newer launches like infrared cooktops, air fryers and high-end juicers; described by management as arguably the fastest-growing kitchen appliance business of its size in India and now a 'material' business with a step-jump in profitability.
- **Large kitchen appliances / built-in kitchen** — A newer, still-small incubating line of chimneys, built-in ovens, built-in microwaves and dishwashers sold partly through 'Crompton Signature Studio' exclusive stores — entered in 2022 targeting a roughly Rs 2,200 crore market, but only about Rs 14 crore of revenue in a quarter and still loss-making at the EBITDA level as of FY25.
- **Lighting** — LED lighting split into B2C (battens, panels, downlighters, ceiling lights sold to consumers) and B2B (street lighting, industrial and large project orders); it was restructured around FY24 after years of price erosion and recovered to what management called industry-leading 14% growth and industry-leading margins by Q4 FY26.
- **Butterfly Gandhimathi Appliances (subsidiary)** — A majority-owned (81%) kitchen appliance company acquired in 2022 selling mixer grinders, gas stoves, pressure cookers and similar products, historically strong in south India with a plan to expand to non-south markets — running at roughly Rs 245-253 crore of revenue per quarter with EBITDA margins around 8-10%, and the carrying value was written down (impaired) in FY26 even as growth and profits recovered.
- **Solar rooftop** — A recently launched business installing rooftop solar systems for homes, initially off state government tenders (about Rs 18-19 crore booked in Q3 FY26) and expanding into direct-to-consumer, with roughly 5,000 homes installed by May 2026.
- **Residential wires** — The newest launch, announced February 2026 — house wiring products sourced from third-party manufacturers and sold through Crompton's existing dealer network, targeting a market management sizes at Rs 36,000-37,000 crore, with essentially no revenue yet as products only reached select markets in FY26.
- **Alternate channels (e-commerce, modern retail, rural)** — Not a product line but a route to market management tracks closely — about 17% of Crompton's sales and growing roughly 30% a year, with e-commerce alone delivering over Rs 100 crore per quarter.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Crompton earns a 'Decent' 3-star rating (64 out of 100, from 65 on business quality, 52 on multibagger fit and 78 on risk safety), and the record tells a fairly consistent story about why. The engine is an 85-year-old brand attached to a pan-India dealer network, run on an asset-light footing: outsourced manufacturing, ₹1.33 of sales from every ₹1 of assets, a 23% cash return on capital, and cash collected 24 days before suppliers have to be paid, so customers effectively fund the business. Those traits show up in the scores (Return on Capital, Growth and Customer Benefits all at the top of their range) and in three of the strongest pattern verdicts. Brand Strength and Market Share Gainers are both rated LIKELY FIT and are really the same fact seen twice — advertising and promotion has been stepped up year after year (doubled in Q1FY23, up 30% in Q1FY25, more behind lighting and Butterfly in FY26) and is matched by third-party-audited share gains: +2pp in fans, water heaters moving from roughly fifth or sixth in general trade to second, plus gains in air coolers. Low Price Plus is the third LIKELY FIT, resting on the named Unnati cost programme, value engineering, alternate raw materials and the VRS-led restructuring of the Baddi and Baroda lighting plants.

The awkward part is that the same design choices that make the returns look good also thin out the protection around them. Outsourced manufacturing and simple, mass-market products are exactly why the asset turn and cash returns are high — and exactly why Cost to Replicate lands only at PARTIAL, since the record notes Crompton's own playbook shows a newcomer can rent much of the same capability. Competitive Advantage scores zero as an area: Network effects is scored -2 because these are standalone durables sold through trade with no self-reinforcing loop, and Toll Roads is a NO FIT because fans, pumps and lighting are discretionary purchases where price decides. That is the direct source of the one ELEVATED risk, Good Enough Goods: pumps lost growth for years when Crompton's prices ran 20-25% ahead of unorganised rivals, and lighting suffers chronic price erosion — the same erosion that forced the restructuring counted as a cost-culture strength. New Entrants sits at WATCH for the same reason. It also explains why Pricing Power stops at PARTIAL: price increases are frequent and management calls itself industry-leading on pricing (four straight quarters of hikes by Q1FY25, 7-8% in fans in FY26 alongside record March volumes), but these read as passing on commodity costs rather than charging more because the brand allows it. Brand and distribution are doing real work here, but as partial shock absorbers rather than a wall.

Where the strengths compound most cleanly is in extending the distribution asset into new places. Deep dealer relationships are the stated right-to-win for entering wires and solar rooftop, alternate channels have grown from 9% to 17% of sales, e-commerce is claimed as number one in its segments, and Crompton Signature Studios went from 17 exclusive stores at launch in July 2022 to 100 carrying large kitchen appliances by August 2024 — enough for Forward Integrators and Friendly Middleman to score PARTIAL, and enough for Stakeholder Concentration to rate LOW since no single customer or supplier drives results. Management and Capital Allocation both score well, backed by tenacity on multi-year projects (the three-year lighting turnaround, solar pumps scaled from ₹20 crore to well past ₹200 crore) and by plain disclosure of bad news — the Butterfly impairment and its effect on returns, the LKA losses, the supply glitches that capped air cooler sales. But the newest engines carry a new exposure: solar rooftop revenue currently comes from state tender orders with a roughly ₹500 crore order book from Andhra and Telangana programmes, so Government Dependency sits at WATCH even as the core business has cut its government-contract share by about 500 basis points. Cyclicality is also a WATCH — weather seasonality and the commodity cycle swing results, partly offset by selling into both warmer and cooler seasons. Technological Innovation, Shifting Consumer Preferences and Fashion Risk are all LOW, and overall resilience reads as SOUND: a business that is genuinely well run and well positioned in distribution, but whose products are ordinary enough that it has to keep earning its share every quarter.

## What to watch

- Good Enough Goods (ELEVATED): whether the price gap to unorganised rivals widens again the way it did in pumps, where a 20-25% premium cost years of growth — this is the single biggest threat to the share gains.
- Pricing Power (PARTIAL): whether price increases stick when commodity costs fall, which would show brand-led pricing rather than the cost pass-throughs the record currently sees.
- Market Share Gainers plus the Advertisement and promotion check: whether steadily rising A&P keeps producing audited share gains, or spending rises while the gains stop.
- Government Dependency (WATCH): the roughly ₹500 crore solar rooftop order book from Andhra and Telangana state tenders — any stall in scheme flow hits a growth engine, not the core.
- Cost of working capital and Asset turn: the 24-day negative working capital and asset-light model are what generate the 23% cash return on capital, so any move toward carrying inventory or funding channels would reset the return profile.
