- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Good → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹21 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹21 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±3.5 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Multi-year Dahej backward-integration strategy, sustained EDFD campaign since 2024 and deliberate sequencing of Tarapur as a learning step show long-term tenacity, but strategic independence is limited since products and plant mandates flow from the parent SCC. | The long view had said: The long-term execution is evident (multi-year capex, decade-long pipeline), but independence is structurally limited — the product pipeline, trademarks and technicals come from the parent, and SCIL is now agreeing to start paying royalty to SCC.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: SCIL is a listed large-cap MNC subsidiary with regular analyst calls and IR advisers, so it is well covered rather than under the radar, but it also avoids hype. | The long view had said: SCIL is a large listed MNC subsidiary with regular analyst-heavy earnings calls (Elara, Vallum, Awriga and others), a dedicated IR adviser and industry award recognition — the opposite of an ignored niche.

And from the calls: Gross margins of 42–43% held through the worst kharif in years with complete pricing integrity and a rising branded share of domestic sales, evidencing genuine brand-based pricing power. | The long view had said: After direct input costs, the company keeps ₹48 of every ₹100 of sales — pricing power customers are happy to pay for. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. Now: Farmers face no meaningful switching cost and there is no captive aftermarket; the only lock-in cited is brand preference and channel relationships. | The long view had said: Exclusive distribution of parent-innovated molecules under global trademarks creates a protected niche, but farmer-level switching costs are low and the Glyphosate PCO notification showed how fast regulation can dissolve such positions.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 2 weakened, 8 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: FY25 saw realisations fall ~10% in both domestic and export (price deflation, the opposite of the pattern), but through FY26 management held 'complete pricing integrity' with stable realisation and record margins in a weak season — price discipline and defence, not demonstrated ability to raise prices above cost inflation. (The long view had said: Across all four calls management passes through cost inflation without losing price — 2022 'freely pass on the rising cost', 2025 'price realization remained broadly stable', FY26 'complete pricing integrity' — with gross margin rising from 38.4% (Q2 FY24) to 42.6% and 43.1%, and FY27 guided as calibrated product-by-product price increases.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Sales do run through ~17,000 channel partners/distributors whose relationships management invests in heavily, but the company simultaneously spends most of its energy on direct end-customer (farmer) demand generation rather than relying on a prescribing professional, so the classic middleman lock-in is only half present. (The long view had said: Sales run through ~16,000 distributors and 31,000 channel partners whose product knowledge is deliberately built through trainings and EDFD meetings, but the farmer is both payer and the direct target of mass demand-generation, so the classic 'expert chooses, someone else pays' dynamic is only half present.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Regulatory approval is a direct determinant of what can be sold: biostimulant regulatory constraints suppressed the PGR/biological categories for much of FY26 and hurt subsidiary Barrix, while the upside case for Excalia Max rested on a fast-track approval window. Management also flags export bans and agri policy as shaping the sector. Pricing itself is not regulator-set and there is no PSU-tender or subsidy dependence, which bounds the exposure. (The long view had said: Policy repeatedly and materially moves the business: the Oct-2022 Glyphosate notification restricting spraying to pest control operators hit a key product overnight, biostimulant regulatory constraints suppressed the PGR/biological categories for most of FY26, and launches plus capex commercialisation are gated on registrations and environmental clearances.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The product pipeline and the manufacturing growth plan are both anchored on a single relationship — parent Sumitomo Chemical Co: the blockbuster INDIFLIN/Excalia Max is 'a Japanese innovation by our parent Company', and the Rs.300cr+ Dahej greenfield is explicitly to make SCC-innovated products and serve SCC's global diversification. That is technology-, supplier- and offtake-concentration in one counterparty. The customer side is the opposite: over a million farmers and ~17,000 channel partners reached via the EDFD campaign. (The long view had said: The business is structurally anchored on one stakeholder — parent Sumitomo Chemical Co.: proprietary molecules, trademarks, technical-goods supply, export orders and the entire Make-in-India/Dahej capex programme run through the parent, and in FY26 management disclosed a new royalty arrangement being negotiated, showing the parent can reset the economics unilaterally.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (QUANT FLAG).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
