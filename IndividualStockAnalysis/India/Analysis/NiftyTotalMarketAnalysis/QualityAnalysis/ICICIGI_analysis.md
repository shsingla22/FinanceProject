# ICICI Lombard General Insurance Company Ltd. (ICICIGI) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

ICICI Lombard is an Indian general (non-life) insurance company — it sells policies that pay out when something goes wrong with your vehicle, your health, your property, your cargo or your business, rather than life insurance (management explicitly says term/life insurance is 'not the business we are in'). Its customers span individuals (car, two-wheeler and retail health cover), companies and SMEs (fire, marine, liability, group health), and government schemes, all within India, and it describes itself as the largest private-sector general insurer in the country. It makes money two ways: underwriting profit — collecting Gross Direct Premium Income (₹247.76 billion in FY2024) and paying out less in claims and expenses than it takes in, measured by a combined ratio of 103.4% in FY2026 versus 117.8% for the industry — and investment income earned on the float, with an overall portfolio return of about 7.98% in FY2024 on a book that is mostly fixed income with roughly 11% in equities. It reaches customers through agents, its own website and app ('IL TakeCare'), digital ecosystem partners, and bancassurance tie-ups including ICICI Bank.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Motor** — Insurance on cars, two-wheelers and commercial vehicles, split between Own Damage (damage to your own vehicle) and Third Party (legally mandated cover for injury or damage caused to others); it is a core retail line where the company holds market leadership, with FY2026 mix of 52.8% private car, 25.4% two-wheeler and 21.8% commercial vehicle, and a motor combined ratio of 106.6% versus 128.0% for the industry.
- **Health (Retail Health)** — Individual and family medical/indemnity policies sold largely through an agency force the company has been deliberately building out (1,101 agency managers as of Q1 FY2023); it is the fastest-growing part of the book, up 51.1% in FY2026 with market share rising from 3.3% to 4.1%, and 42.1% of new retail health premium now coming from long-term policies.
- **Health (Group Health)** — Medical cover sold to employers and other groups for their members; management runs it 'with a disciplined approach' focused on managing the book rather than chasing volume, and it grew 12.8% for the industry in FY2026 versus the much faster retail line.
- **Mass Health** — Government-sponsored/mass health schemes, treated as a lumpy, low-margin line that management routinely strips out (alongside Crop) when quoting 'underlying' growth — for example FY2026 growth of 7.0% overall versus 10.2% excluding Crop and Mass Health.
- **Commercial lines / Property & Casualty** — Corporate and SME cover for factories, offices, goods and business risks — the P&C segment was ₹19.54 billion of premium in Q1 FY2023, and the company says it holds leadership in the Liability and Marine Cargo lines, though FY2026 growth of 5.4% lagged the industry's 12.2% because management refused to match competitors' price cuts.
- **Fire** — Property/fire cover for commercial premises, the largest single piece of the commercial book (₹11.43 billion in Q1 FY2023); it has recently been the most price-competitive line in the market, with the industry seeing 27.8% de-growth in Q1 FY2027 as rates collapsed at the April renewal cycle.
- **Crop** — Agricultural/crop insurance, another lumpy government-linked line that management excludes from its 'like-for-like' growth comparisons rather than treating as a core growth driver.
- **Investment book** — The pool of premium money held before claims are paid, invested mainly in fixed income (yield to maturity ~7.4%) with about 11% in equities, producing a realised return of 7.98% in FY2024 plus capital gains of ₹5.51 billion — a large and reliable second profit engine alongside underwriting.
- **Distribution — agency, bancassurance and digital** — Not a product but how the company sells: an agency force for retail health, bancassurance and Key Relationship Groups including ICICI Bank (up 49.4% in Q1 FY2023), and a 'Digital One' team covering its own website, the IL TakeCare app (~1.7 million downloads by mid-2022) and ecosystem partners, contributing ₹2.10 billion or 3.9% of GDPI in that quarter.
- **Reinsurance and CAT exposure** — The company cedes part of its risk to reinsurers and absorbs catastrophe losses when they hit — CAT losses cost ₹1.37 billion in FY2024 (worth 0.8 percentage points of combined ratio) — and softer FY2027 reinsurance renewal rates are flagged as a cost tailwind.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

ICICI Lombard comes out of the three reviews as a Strong business (65 out of 100, four stars) whose whole story runs through one trait: it competes on reliability and discipline rather than on price. The quality framework's highest marks sit with management (1.67) and the customer promise — 98.8–99.0% of retail health claims paid within 30 days against an industry 82.1%, health and motor customer-satisfaction scores of 73 and 69, 94,000-plus agents, bancassurance, aggregators and an app with 22.1mn downloads handling 67–82% of claim intimations. That same trait is what makes two multibagger patterns fit outright. Corporate Culture is a strong fit because promises made on old calls were kept (the pledge to reach roughly 102% combined ratio by FY2025 was met at 102.6%, FY2026 at 102.4%) and bad news is quantified rather than smoothed — the ₹1.65bn Supreme Court third-party hit, ₹0.63bn of fire losses, the 46% profit decline, and management's own statement that growth is running below industry. Recurring Revenue is the other strong fit: renewal and advance premium fund the business, with ₹34.71bn of advance premium, motor advance premium up more than 15%, and advance-premium share outside motor rising from 9.6% to 12.5%. That same prepaid structure is why cash arrives before suppliers must be paid, which the framework flags as a rare working-capital strength.

The strengths and the weak spots are two sides of the same decision. Because the company refuses to buy volume, the Market Share Gainers pattern is only a likely fit rather than a certain one: retail health share climbed 3.3% → 4.1% → 4.5% with 51.1% then 69.5% growth and motor leadership at 10.5%, yet headline premium grew below industry, commercial lines were deliberately shrunk 13.8% during a price war, and crop exposure was capped. The quality scores show the cost of that choice plainly — growth scores 0.4 and return on capital 0.33 against management's 1.67. The upside of the same discipline is that rivals are doing the opposite: an industry combined ratio of 119.3% against the company's 103.4%, industry return on equity of 4.0% against 19.1%, and peers struggling with expense-of-management limits. That is the Share donators strength — a large pool of loss-making competitors to take business from — and it is why the ten-year average combined ratio of 102.9% against an industry 115.3% matters more than any single year. Several patterns land only partially for the same underlying reason: Low Price Plus, Brand Strength, Forward Integrators, Innovation Dominance and Cost to Replicate all have real evidence behind them (expenses held at 29% against a 30% cap, do-it-yourself digital servicing at 69–71% of engagements, the IL TakeCare app producing ₹5,170.7mn of premium in FY2026 versus ₹2,237.2mn, a motor combined-ratio advantage over industry that widened from 10.3 points in FY2020 to 21.4 points in FY2026), but none of them yet converts into the ability to charge more.

The risk review confirms where that conversion fails, and it is the same place in every case: price. Pricing Power and Toll Roads are outright non-fits — motor third-party rates are set by the government and described as 'beyond our control', fire rates fell 27.8%, April renewals came in at a discount, and a Supreme Court judgement forces a 12–15% third-party reserve adjustment. That is precisely why Government Dependency is the single high risk and why the industry-structure score is the only negative one at −0.17. The elevated Good Enough Goods risk is the commercial mirror of it: at the point of sale the product looks the same to the buyer, the intermediary picks the winner, and cheaper capacity keeps taking share even while industry motor combined ratios sit at 128%. The company's answer is service rather than price — the claims speed and satisfaction scores that earned top marks on assurance benefits — and the widening motor cost advantage suggests it is working, but it is a defence, not a moat with a price tag on it. The three watch-level risks all trace back to distribution: the ICICI Bank relationship and parent brand named in every call (Stakeholder Concentration), aggregators and digital-first health players resetting how policies are sold (New Entrants), and the underwriting cycle that collapsed commercial pricing in H2 FY2026 and Q1 FY2027 (Cyclicality). Technology, consumer preferences and fashion risk are all low — the shifts underway are tailwinds the company pivoted into rather than threats. Overall resilience reads as sound, with the honest caveat that the framework marks it down on 'security by obscurity': this is the largest private general insurer in India, listed and covered by every brokerage, so nothing here is a secret.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Large parts of economics are set outside the company: motor third-party pricing is fixed by the Ministry of Road Transport/IRDAI ('beyond our control'), the absence of a TP hike is cited as a direct drag on industry growth, EOM caps, Use & File, 1/n accounting and Ind AS are all regulator-mandated, crop is a state-tender/PMFBY scheme (Maharashtra's 80:110 redesign), and a June 2026 Supreme Court order is expected to raise industry motor TP loss ratios 12-15%, forcing a ■1.65bn provision.

> "with respect to what happens to motor third party. Frankly speaking, this is beyond our control" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate, opportunistic and shrinking exposure to policy-driven crop and mass health (crop ~4.1% of GDPI; company growth reported excluding crop and mass health) and a decisive pivot to retail health, which grew 51.1% in FY2026 and 69.5% in Q1 FY2027*

## What to watch

- Whether retail health keeps compounding at the pace behind the Market Share Gainers pattern (3.3% → 4.5% share, 51.1% then 69.5% growth) — if it slows while headline premium still lags industry, the growth score of 0.4 stops being a deliberate choice and starts being a limitation.
- The combined ratio versus the ten-year 102.9% average and the FY2026 102.4% — this is the promise-kept evidence behind the strong-fit Corporate Culture pattern, and a miss would undercut the single most reliable signal in the record.
- Motor third-party pricing and the 12–15% reserve adjustment from the Supreme Court judgement — the core of the high Government Dependency risk and the reason Pricing Power is a non-fit; a rate hike or its continued absence moves earnings regardless of execution.
- How long the 21.4-point motor combined-ratio advantage keeps widening while fire premiums fall 27.8% and renewals price at a discount — this is the only working defence against the elevated Good Enough Goods risk and the underwriting-cycle Cyclicality risk.
- The mix between ICICI Bank bancassurance and everything else — non-parent banca, the 94,559 agents and POS, and the IL TakeCare app's ₹5,170.7mn of premium — since this is simultaneously the Friendly Middleman and Forward Integrators patterns and the Stakeholder Concentration and New Entrants risks.
