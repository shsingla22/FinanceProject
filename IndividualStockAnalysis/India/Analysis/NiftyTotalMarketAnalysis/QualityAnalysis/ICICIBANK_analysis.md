# ICICI Bank Ltd. (ICICIBANK) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

ICICI Bank is an Indian bank that takes deposits from customers and lends the money out, earning most of its income from the gap between what it pays on deposits and what it charges on loans (net interest income, at a net interest margin of roughly 4.3%), plus fees on services (about ₹6,779 crore of fee income in the March 2026 quarter). It serves individuals, farmers and rural households, small shopkeepers and SMEs, and large Indian companies, offering home loans, auto loans, personal loans, credit cards, farm credit, working-capital and business loans, current and savings accounts, term deposits and money transfers. It operates mainly across India through a large branch network — 7,511 branches and 12,087 ATMs and cash recycling machines at March 31, 2026 — plus mobile and internet platforms such as iMobile for individuals and InstaBIZ for businesses, and a small set of international branches serving mostly Indian companies and non-resident Indians (about 2.4% of loans). Management runs it for what it calls 'risk-calibrated profitable growth', reporting profit after tax above ₹50,000 crore for FY2026 and a consolidated profit of ₹54,208 crore including subsidiaries.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Deposits (current, savings and term accounts)** — The bank's funding base — money customers park in current accounts, savings accounts and fixed-term deposits — which grew 11.4% year-on-year at March 31, 2026 and which management repeatedly calls its key franchise strength, with among the lowest cost of deposits in the system.
- **Retail loans** — Loans to individuals — home loans (mortgages), auto loans, commercial vehicle and equipment loans, personal loans and credit cards — the single largest chunk of lending at about 41.7% of the total portfolio including non-fund exposures at March 2026.
- **Mortgage portfolio** — Home loans, the biggest single retail product, growing 13.2% year-on-year in FY2026 and consistently cited as a core growth driver.
- **Personal loans and credit cards** — Unsecured lending to individuals, which was about 9.7% and 4.4% of the total loan book respectively in mid-2024 after very fast growth, but had slowed sharply by end-2025 (personal loans up 2.4%, cards down 3.5% year-on-year).
- **Rural portfolio (including kisan credit card)** — Farm and rural lending, notably the kisan credit card given to farmers, which the calls flag as seasonally lumpy — it reliably produces higher bad-loan additions in the first and third quarters of each year (about ₹7.4 billion in the December 2025 quarter).
- **Business banking** — Lending and banking services to small businesses and merchants, sourced through branches, cross-sell to current-account holders and digital tools; consistently the fastest-growing segment (up 44.7% year-on-year in mid-2022, 35.6% in mid-2024, 24.4% in FY2026).
- **SME portfolio** — Loans to small and medium enterprises, grown through the branch network and digital platforms, expanding at 23-32% year-on-year in the earlier calls.
- **Domestic corporate banking** — Lending and working-capital services to large Indian companies, growing around 5-14% year-on-year across the period, with 71.9% of the book rated 'A- and above' at March 2026; includes ₹791 billion of exposure to non-bank finance and housing finance companies (4.3% of advances) and a ₹681 billion builder/real-estate book (another 4.3%).
- **International / overseas branches** — A small offshore book, down to about 2.4% of total loans by end-2025, made up almost entirely of Indian corporates and India-linked trade finance rather than genuinely foreign lending (about 92% Indian corporates in 2024).
- **Digital platforms (iMobile, InstaBIZ, OneSCF, Money2India)** — The bank's apps and online channels — iMobile for individuals, InstaBIZ for businesses (including instant digital current accounts and overdrafts open to non-customers), OneSCF for corporate supply-chain finance and Money2India/iMobile Global for non-resident Indians — used to onboard customers, cross-sell and cut cost of service rather than reported as a separate profit line.
- **Subsidiaries** — Separately managed group companies whose results are discussed on each call and which lift group profit above the bank's own — consolidated FY2026 profit of ₹54,208 crore versus above ₹50,000 crore standalone — and which also pay dividends up to the bank.
- **Treasury** — The bank's own investment and markets activity, explicitly carved out of the profit measure management steers by ('profit before tax excluding treasury', ₹65,021 crore in FY2026) because it is seen as lumpy rather than core.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## The verdict: Decent — 58 out of 100 ★★★☆☆

In one breath: the quality framework finds a good business today (64/100); it strongly fits 1 of the 11 patterns long-term winners share; the risk review found 1 high risk (Government Dependency). Weighing those together gives 58 out of 100 — decent.

**The exact arithmetic:** Overall = 45% × 64 (business quality) + 30% × 40 (multibagger fit) + 25% × 68 (risk safety) = 58 out of 100 → Decent (3 stars).

- **Business quality (64/100):** The 34-check quality framework scored the business +0.58 on its −2 (poor) to +2 (excellent) scale, with 82% of checks backed by evidence; mapped onto 0–100 that is 64 points.
- **Multibagger fit (40/100):** the foundation test (steady cash + high returns on capital + growth) passed in full (+25); 1 pattern fit strongly (Corporate Culture) (+15) → 40 of 100.
- **Risk safety (68/100):** started from a clean 100; 1 high risk (Government Dependency) (−20); 3 worth watching (−12) → 68 of 100.

### The story in depth

ICICI Bank comes through these three reviews as a well-run, heavily fortified franchise whose main asset is reach and trust rather than pricing muscle. The quality work gives its best marks to distribution and convenience — 7,511 branches and 12,087 ATMs by March 2026, with 528 branches added in just the last twelve months, layered over digital channels such as iMobile Pay, InstaBIZ and Money2India — and to what the framework calls assurance benefits: safety, liquidity and provisioning that customers pay for by accepting lower deposit rates, which is how a bank turns trust into a real funding cost advantage. Management scores equally well: the same strategic language appears word-for-word from July 2022 to April 2026, contingency provisions were built up from Rs 85 billion to Rs 131 billion and deliberately left untouched even in a weak quarter, and capital was kept strong at 16.35% core equity with a Rs 12 per share dividend for FY2026. Those same traits show up in the pattern screen as the one unqualified hit — Corporate Culture, a STRONG FIT — and in the risk review as the reason three of the eight risk channels come out LOW.

The catch is that the very things that make the bank safe are also what keep it from scoring higher. Because lending and deposits are largely interchangeable products, price is where the fight happens: the pattern screen rates Pricing Power a clear NO FIT, citing margins falling from 4.78% in the first quarter of FY2024 to about 4.30% by FY2026, aggressive mortgage price competition, and management stating outright in April 2026 that pricing is not the bank's to set. That same fact drives the Good Enough Goods and New Entrants risks to WATCH, with the cost of deposits climbing from 3.46% to roughly 4.9% — in other words, the cheap-funding moat that earns the bank its highest quality marks is being re-priced by rivals in real time. Likewise, the deliberately cautious, granular loan book that makes Stakeholder Concentration a LOW risk (weakest-rated corporate exposure down from Rs 82 billion to Rs 34 billion, about 0.2% of advances, largest single borrower under Rs 5 billion, bad loans down from 0.70% to 0.33%) is the same restraint that holds the Growth score to 0.4 and leaves Market Share Gainers only a PARTIAL fit, with profit growth sliding from roughly 50% year-on-year in mid-2022 to single digits by FY2026. Eight of the eleven patterns land as PARTIAL for the same underlying reason: the strengths are real but the calls never show them converting into premium prices or measured share gains.

Regulation cuts both ways too, and it is the sharpest example of one trait producing both a strength and a hazard. Licensing, capital rules and rising compliance costs are what keep new competitors out — no new entrants appear anywhere in four years of calls — yet the same regulator sets the bank's economics directly, which is why Government Dependency is the single HIGH RISK finding: a supervisory review forced a Rs 12.83 billion provision on a Rs 200-250 billion priority-sector agriculture book in January 2026, and the cost of priority-sector certificates has steadily risen and lifted operating expenses. There is also a structural drag no amount of good management can undo: the return-on-capital area scores minus one, because a bank is the opposite of asset-light, earning a roughly 2.3% return on assets and a 4.3% margin off a leveraged balance sheet resting on Rs 3.4 lakh crore of net worth. That mix produces the composite: 64 out of 100 on business quality, only 40 on multibagger fit, and 68 on risk safety, blending to 58 and a Decent, three-star grade. The honest read is a durable, conservatively run compounder with a strong culture and a hard-to-copy network, but with its pricing contested, its growth deliberately restrained, and its economics partly written by the regulator — and the resilience question left unresolved as UNKNOWN.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
