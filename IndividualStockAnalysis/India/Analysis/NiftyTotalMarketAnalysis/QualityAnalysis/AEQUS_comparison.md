## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 30 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 6 improved, 0 regressed, 24 unchanged. 1 check carries the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them). 1 was newly assessable in the recent view.

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |

| Capital Allocation | Neutral (+0.00) | Neutral (+0.25) | +0.25 |
| Return on Capital | Weak (-1.00) | Weak (-1.00) | +0.00 |
| Growth | Good (+0.83) | Good (+1.00) | +0.17 |

| Competitive Advantage | Neutral (+0.50) | Neutral (-0.33) | -0.83 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: The entire proposition is certainty — NADCAP-certified processes, rigorous qualification per SKU, and carrying 4-6 weeks of extra inventory (pushing working capital to 151 days) purely to protect delivery commitments. | The long view had said: Customers buy certainty and qualified reliability — parts stay for years once approved, and management carries 125% of quarterly material at working-capital cost to protect deliveries — though it competes as a value-oriented supplier, so price is not irrelevant.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.4 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: High and explicit entry barriers from NADCAP certification, years of qualification investment and long approval cycles, though nothing is said about pricing rationality among incumbent suppliers. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±3.4 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Entry barriers are genuinely high (NADCAP certification, multi-year qualification, decade-long relationships), but multiple Indian groups are now entering commercial aerospace and Chinese consumer-electronics precedent points to long-run margin compression.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: Results are hard to separate from tailwinds and subsidies — SEZ status, MeitY PLI, an aerospace supercycle — and with the company loss-making at PAT level and only two calls of listed history, the strong aerospace ROCE cannot yet be attributed to management skill. | The long view had said: The IPO glow and headline 33-51% revenue growth mask a full-year PAT loss of ₹1,133mn, a widening consumer EBITDA loss (-₹783mn), a lost anchor toy customer, and 45-50% FY27 growth plus doubled EBITDA that is entirely promised, not yet delivered.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Purely organic growth with no acquisitions and leverage cut to 0.23x net debt/equity post-IPO, but the ~INR4,750cr of new capex commitments are aggressive for a company still guiding to consolidated PAT break-even only by H1 FY28. | The long view had said: Focus is organic with no large M&A and leverage improved to 0.23x net debt/equity post-IPO, but committing ₹500cr of fresh consumer capex at 23% utilisation funded partly by new borrowings, with consolidated PAT negative to H1 FY28, is aggressive rather than prudent.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Sells globally with ~93-94% of revenue USD-linked and support units in Cholet (France) and Paris, Texas, but the FY26 expansion is capacity in India (Hosur, Belagavi, Hubballi) rather than new geographies. | The long view had said: Overseas units exist in Cholet (France) and Paris, Texas, but all announced expansion is domestic Indian capacity serving export customers, with no evidence of new-geography market entry being a growth driver.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: R&D investment is real and productive — a new advanced-materials R&D ecosystem at IIT Dharwad plus 433 new qualified parts added in Q4 taking the portfolio to 5,654 SKUs (+26% YoY) — though no R&D spend or efficiency metric is disclosed. | The long view had said: An advanced materials R&D ecosystem at IIT Dharwad was set up, but as a contract manufacturer Aequs owns no product IP and shows no track record of R&D-generated products.

*Unchanged: Pricing, Mix and Volume; Convenience benefits; Gain in market share; Out of limelight; Intangible benefits; Security by obscurity; Technology advantage; Profit margin; Distribution advantage; Return on capital (headline); Mini-monopolies; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Share donators; Independent, long-term and tenacious; People matter (talent development); Candor; Structural end-market growth; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Cyclical market growth; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Cost of working capital; Asset turn; Merger and acquisition; Capex for growth (not maintenance).*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 0 weakened, 10 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Management articulates a concrete, time-based replication barrier in aerospace — the only precision manufacturer with full vertical integration inside a single Indian SEZ, NADCAP-certified special processes, and thousands of individually qualified SKUs each requiring a multi-year approval cycle — repeated consistently across both FY26 calls; the consumer segment notably fails this test, as the Hasbro exit showed. (The long view had said: The aerospace side offers a concrete replication barrier — 15 years of NADCAP-certified surface treatment, forging and machining qualifications inside one SEZ, delivering 100–150 new parts per month, which a funded entrant cannot compress. The consumer electronics enclosure business has no comparable barrier: it is a capacity race against Chinese incumbents, dependent on Chinese equipment, at 23% utilisation with no proven returns.)

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **New Entrants**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Management describes no new-format competitor and no discounting or margin investment framed as a competitive response; instead the calls stress structural entry barriers — long qualification cycles, NADCAP certification and single-SEZ vertical integration — and aerospace segment margins actually rose (segment EBITDA up 76%, ROCE 20% from 14%), which is not the signature of entrant pressure. (The long view had said: Analysts twice probed structurally different competition — 'many Indian groups are trying to build commercial aerospace manufacturing in India' (with Dynamatic winning a large door order) and established Chinese precision suppliers in consumer electronics — and management answered defensively by pointing to its moat rather than to pricing or share data.)

**That worsened:**

- **Fashion Risk**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The consumer vertical — toys for Mattel and formerly Hasbro, plus consumer electronics programmes — is tied to specific customer product cycles whose demand rests on taste, and the segment is where all the new capital is going (INR500 crore FY27 capex, 23% utilisation targeted to 40-50%). The exposure is bounded because aerospace still supplied 85% of FY26 revenue and the toy work is component/contract manufacturing for a diversified brand owner rather than Aequs's own trend-exposed brand. (The long view had said: Toy exposure is real but small and indirect — Aequs is a pure contract manufacturer with no product IP, so brand fashion risk sits with Mattel/Hasbro, and toys are a fraction of a consumer segment that is only 15-17% of revenue while 83-85% is functional, contracted aerospace hardware.)

*Unchanged: Stakeholder Concentration (HIGH RISK); Cyclicality (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

*Not comparable: Shifting Consumer Preferences (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 1 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
