### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: The calls affirmatively document deliberate de-concentration: top-5 and top-10 client shares fell 6.2% and 9% over eight quarters while the large accounts still grew, $5m+ accounts rose from 26 to 39 year-on-year, and FY26 added 47 new logos including 24 strategic ones. (The long view had said: Single relationships still visibly move the numbers — 'our top client' shifting processes onshore-to-offshore was repeatedly flagged as a growth headwind, one large UK banking client's renewal and one large comms client's in-house estate are discussed by name, and CMT results swing on work-packet timing at a couple of consumer tech accounts. The trajectory is clearly improving, with management quantifying de-concentration each quarter.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The core proposition — labour-based process execution — is being repriced by agentic AI in real time, and management itself frames FY26 demand as clients 'redesigning their operating models for AI' moving 'from manual execution to assisted and increasingly automated operations'; headcount is essentially flat (34,495→36,205 including two acquisitions) while revenue grew ~14.6% in dollars, i.e. the FTE-linked revenue model is already breaking. Pricing is migrating to platform-fee-plus-per-interaction constructs whose economics are unproven at scale. (The long view had said: The entire FTE/labour-arbitrage BPO model is being reset by AI and agentic operations, and management itself frames the industry as fractured — launching relAI (FY25), 'UnBPO' (FY26) and then declaring a new category, with pricing moving to platform fee plus per-interaction and value measured per employee rather than headcount. This is large-scale, infrastructure-changing innovation of exactly the kind that produces more victims than victors, even though FSL is currently on the right side of it.)
- **Fashion Risk**: NO SIGNAL → LOW. Why now: Demand is contracted, functional and repeat — multi-year sole-source outsourcing of claims, billing, servicing and collections — with no growth attributed to a trending product or brand moment across any of the four calls. (The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and all 2 computed fingerprints are clean.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 9 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
