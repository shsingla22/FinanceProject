# Himadri Speciality Chemical Ltd. (HSCL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CHEMICALS*

Himadri Speciality Chemical is an Indian company that started in 1990 as a small coal tar distillation unit and now turns coal tar and related feedstock into a range of industrial chemicals and advanced materials. Its core money-makers are coal tar pitch (sold mainly to aluminium and graphite makers), speciality carbon black (a black pigment/filler used in plastics, inks, paints, coatings, wires and cables, fibres, rubber and tyres) plus speciality oils and naphthalene-based products; it earns revenue by making these at low cost from an integrated, backward-linked plant base and selling to industrial customers in roughly 56 countries. Manufacturing is in India, including a large integrated carbon complex, a speciality carbon black site at Singur and an anode plant at Mahistikry in West Bengal, with liquid coal tar pitch exported through dedicated terminals at Haldia and Mangalore. On top of this base it has added a tyre business (Birla Tyres) and is investing heavily in lithium-ion battery materials; in the quarter ended June 2026 the group reported consolidated revenue of INR 1,432 crore, EBITDA of INR 313 crore (22% margin) and PAT of INR 228 crore.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Coal Tar Pitch** — Distills coal tar into pitch for aluminium and graphite customers and is described as the business that 'anchors the company', with 600,000 MTPA of coal tar distillation capacity, over 65% domestic market share, and the world's largest single-location fully integrated carbon complex, now also exporting liquid pitch via the Haldia and Mangalore terminals.
- **Speciality Carbon Black** — Makes high-value carbon black grades (over 70 grades) for plastics, inks, high-performance coatings, paints, batteries, wires and cables, fibres, rubber and EV tyres; total carbon black capacity is 250,000 MTPA including 130,000 MTPA of speciality carbon black after the Singur expansion, which management calls the world's largest single-site speciality carbon black plant.
- **Super Speciality Carbon Black** — A newly announced higher-purity, premium-priced version of the above for lithium-ion batteries, engineered plastics, fabrics, fibres, coatings and conductive black, being created by converting 6,000 MTPA of existing capacity at a capex of about INR 170 crore.
- **Speciality Oils and Naphthalene (including derivatives SNF and PCE)** — Sells oils and naphthalene-based chemicals derived from the same coal tar stream, described as part of the core along with pitch and carbon black, and now including a consumer-facing branded naphthalene ball, 'Durofresh', which management says has had an encouraging response in retail.
- **Anthraquinone and Carbazole (forward integration)** — A new plant making ingredients for dyes, pigments, pharmaceuticals and electronics from Himadri's own oils, billed as India's first commercial facility of its kind and an import substitute, with total capacity of 5,300 MTPA — an initial 2,600 MTPA phase from Q2 FY27 and full commissioning targeted by FY28.
- **New Energy / Lithium-ion Battery Materials — Anode** — Commissioned its first anode material plant at Mahistikry, West Bengal in April 2026 with an initial 200 MTPA, using technology developed fully in-house and fed by specially engineered high-purity coal tar pitch made by Himadri itself; still small in volume but described as the most transformational part of the company's future, with samples going to cell makers (OEMs) for approval.
- **New Energy / Lithium-ion Battery Materials — LFP Cathode Active Material** — A planned lithium iron phosphate cathode business where a first 2,000 MTPA milestone is due in Q3 FY27, building to 40,000 MTPA under Phase 1 by around FY29 and a long-term ambition of 200,000 MTPA (enough for roughly 100 GWh of batteries), which management says would make Himadri the first commercial-scale LFP cathode plant outside China.
- **Carbon Nanotube (CNT)** — A newly developed in-house technology with a 200 MTPA plant planned for Q4 FY27 at about INR 70 crore capex, aimed at lithium-ion batteries, semiconductors, electronics, sensors, polymers, coatings and aerospace; small in size today but positioned as a long-term growth option.
- **Birla Tyres (tyre manufacturing)** — A tyre business Himadri entered as a strategic partner alongside resolution applicant Dalmia Bharat Refractories, which restarted commercial operations on 29 May 2025 and now sells over 80 products through 49 distributors and more than 1,000 dealers in India, Asia, the Middle East and Africa, with capacity being shifted from truck-and-bus bias to off-the-road tyres and a passenger car radial plant for EV/SUV tyres targeted by FY28; notably, Himadri says it does not sell carbon black to Birla Tyres.
- **Strategic investments and partnerships (Sicona, International Battery Company, Invati Creations)** — Minority stakes and licensing tie-ups rather than operating businesses — an exclusive licence to Sicona's silicon-carbon anode technology, a stake in US-headquartered cell maker IBC (used to validate and deploy Himadri's materials via IBC's South Korea plant), and joint research with Invati on advanced electrode materials.
- **Research & Development** — Not a revenue line but repeatedly presented as the engine of the business, with over 180 scientists and technologists including 28 PhDs drawn from Japan, South Korea, Australia, the US, Europe and China, and INR 120 crore spent in FY26.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Himadri Speciality Chemical is, at its core, a very strong industrial franchise attached to an unproven new business. The strongest evidence in the records is about position and capability: over 65% of the domestic coal tar pitch market, run from what is described as the world's largest single-location integrated carbon complex, plus India's first anthraquinone/carbazole plant and first indigenous carbon nanotubes — the "mini-monopolies" check scores at the top of the scale. Behind that sits real technical depth: ₹120cr of FY26 R&D, 180 scientists and 28 PhDs, a decade of in-house lithium-ion work that produced a self-developed, unlicensed anode plant, and 70+ specialty carbon black grades. Management has also done what it said it would: the FY25 promise to double profit in three years was delivered in two, the carbon black expansion (60k→130k MTPA) and pitch debottlenecking (500k→600k) were commissioned as promised, and the balance sheet stayed net-cash through it. That track record is why the "Corporate Culture" pattern is the single STRONG FIT in the screen and "Innovation Dominance" a LIKELY FIT, and why growth today is coming from a better mix rather than more tonnes — in 9M FY26 volumes rose only 3% while EBITDA per tonne rose 15% and profit 41%.

The awkward part is that the very traits that create the strengths also create most of the risks. The cost advantage in pitch rests on coal tar feedstock and on end-markets — aluminium smelters, graphite, tyres — that are commodity-linked, which is exactly why "Cyclicality" is the one ELEVATED risk; management itself blames raw-material price swings for volume and revenue constraints, and the 'other' segment's operating profit swung from ₹25cr to ₹1cr. Management's own framing of competing at the "most economical cost globally" while climbing from commodity to specialty to "super specialty" is, read honestly, an admission that the lower rungs get commoditised and margin has to be re-earned by moving up — the "Good Enough Goods" risk. The same is true of the growth story: the battery materials push (anode, a 200,000 MTPA LFP cathode ambition, silicon-carbon, CNT) is what makes "Structural end-market growth" a top-scoring check, with global lithium-ion demand projected to go from about 1.6 TWh in 2025 to about 6.8 TWh in 2035 — and it is simultaneously the source of three separate WATCH risks. The chemistry is unsettled (management discusses NMC versus LFP versus sodium-ion itself), the demand case leans on policy such as customs-duty exemptions, GST reduction and EV30@30, and the entire payoff depends on qualification by a small, unnamed group of cell makers plus single technology partners. This is also why nine of the eleven multibagger patterns land only at PARTIAL: the business is straight product tonnage sales with no recurring or service revenue, brand and channel work is young and largely acquired, the 65% share is described as maintained rather than gained, and better realisations come from mix shift, not from documented price rises held without losing volume.

The financial scorecard is where enthusiasm has to be tempered. Return on capital is the weakest area in the framework (-0.33), with roughly ₹7 of operating cash generated per ₹100 of capital employed, and about 103 days of cash tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. Capital allocation is barely positive (0.17), pulled down by the Birla Tyres purchase — a distressed insolvency asset in an unrelated, competitive industry that needs multi-year revival and further spending, the opposite of a small bolt-on that already works — alongside several venture-style commitments. So the same management that delivered every operating promise it made has a mixed record on where it puts the money, and the heavy, self-funded growth spending is currently depressing the very return figure that would prove the spending was worthwhile. The combined rating reflects that split: 45% × 65 on business quality, 30% × 33 on multibagger fit and 25% × 74 on risk safety gives 58 out of 100 — "Decent", three stars — with resilience assessed as SOUND. In plain terms: a genuinely hard-to-replicate core business with real technology, earning modest cash returns while spending ahead of a payoff that has not yet arrived, and whose biggest new bet carries most of the identified risk.
