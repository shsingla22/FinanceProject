**Checks that improved:**

- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: NHPC is a large listed PSU with a regular broker-hosted analyst call, so it is not under-the-radar, even though the hydro niche attracts little private capital. | The long view had said: Far from under-the-radar: a large listed PSU with a broker-hosted quarterly call, PM-led foundation stones and ministerial site visits.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Communication is a plain, low-key PSU recital of physical progress and costs by functional directors, with no celebrity framing, though management does invoke the Prime Minister's vision. | The long view had said: Management is technocratic and low-key in style, but commentary repeatedly attaches itself to political occasions and leaders rather than substance alone.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Weak → Neutral. Now: Real heavy-civil engineering capability (31.5 km HRT daylighting, 673-tonne rotor lowering) but it is execution know-how, not a multi-product R&D-backed edge, and geology repeatedly defeats it. | The long view had said: Rather than a technology edge, the calls document repeated technical failures — tunnel cavities and water ingress at Parbati-II, severe geology at Teesta-VI's HRT and Rangit-IV — with no R&D intensity disclosed.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Indian renewable/hydro build-out is a secular end market, evidenced by a large approved pipeline (CCEA approval of Rs 26,070 Cr for Kamala, 5-6 projects/~10,000 MW to be started) and solar diversification. | The long view had said: End market is in permanent secular expansion — clean/RE power, plus an 18 GW pumped-storage pipeline and ~10,000 MW of new hydro starts planned, all driven by India's decarbonisation and grid-balancing needs.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Poor. Now: All FY26 growth is volume/capacity-led with regulated levelized tariffs fixed per project — revenue +12% and generation +16% attributed entirely to new units commissioned, with no pricing or mix lever. | The long view had said: All growth is volume-led — FY26 revenue +12% attributed entirely to new capacity commissioning — while tariffs are CERC cost-plus, not company-set pricing.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Neutral → Weak. Now: Focus is organic but capital discipline is weak: repeated upward cost revisions and multi-year schedule slippage across Subansiri, Teesta-VI and Ratle, funded on a very large balance-sheet commitment. | The long view had said: Focus is organic and M&A is small, but stewardship is undermined by debt-funded capex pushing finance costs from ₹1,189cr to ₹1,423cr and by repeated large cost escalations on flagship projects.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: Private hydro developers have failed outright and NHPC has absorbed their assets — Lanco's Teesta-VI via NCLT and Jal Power's Rangit-IV — a direct transfer of position from weak competitors. | The long view had said: Private hydro developers have failed outright and NHPC has literally absorbed them out of bankruptcy — Lanco Teesta and Jal Power both acquired through the NCLT route.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: Hydro offers grid reliability value, but NHPC's own reliability record is weak — Teesta-V shut since Oct'23 and only restarting June'26, with PAF down for two straight years. | The long view had said: The product sold is assured availability — earnings include explicit PAF-based availability incentives and long-tenor contracted supply, with buyers valuing dispatchable peaking/balancing power.
- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Weak → Poor. Now: Electricity is a pure commodity sold at regulator-determined levelized tariffs (Rs 3.92–6.73/unit by project) with zero image- or taste-driven pricing power. | The long view had said: No image- or taste-based pricing power; tariffs are cost-plus determined by CERC and vary purely with project capital cost (₹3.92 to ₹6.59 per unit), so cost overruns pass into price rather than margin.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Neutral → Weak. Now: Leadership continuity is poor — four chairmen in five years, CMD changed again between calls (Chaudhary to Bhupender Gupta), and the Q4 FY26 call was led by the Director (Projects) with no CMD present. | The long view had said: No systematic talent-development discussion; internal elevation exists (R.P. Goyal from Director-Finance to CMD, Uttam Lal's long Director-Personnel tenure) but the top seat changed three times in five years (Singh → Vishnoi → Goyal → Gupta).

*Unchanged: Mini-monopolies; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Candor; Geographical expansion; Halo effect (caution); Capex for growth (not maintenance); Independent, long-term and tenacious; Merger and acquisition; Barriers to entry and rationality; Asset turn; Return on capital (headline); Gain in market share; Cost of working capital; Profit margin; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Oligopoly (vs duopoly).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 1 unchanged; 5 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Revenue is regulated and operations-linked rather than commodity-priced, but output and profits swing materially with hydrology and force-majeure events: FY25 generation fell 9%, PAF fell to 73.94%, and PAT dropped 17% on lower water availability and the Teesta-V shutdown, while FY26 generation rose 16% and PAT 25% on new commissioning. Swings are hydrological/asset-specific, not a demand cycle, and the company keeps investing counter to conditions. (The long view had said: Revenue is regulated cost-plus and electricity is a flow product with stable demand, but generation, PAF and revenue swing materially with hydrology and outages — FY24 generation fell 12%, PAF from 88.75% to 77.60% and revenue 10%, while FY26 rose 16% on new commissioning; meanwhile capex is being pushed to Rs 13,000-15,000 Crore a year into long-gestation projects with large cost overruns (Subansiri Rs 19,992 Crore to Rs 30,072 Crore).)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Management repeatedly volunteers tariff comparisons and singles out lower-tariff projects as 'very attractive' (Rs. 4.44–4.46/unit) while flagship projects carry Rs. 5.60–6.73/unit levelized tariffs, implying awareness that cheaper generation alternatives set the benchmark for discom offtake. The calls do not show a defended premium, only cost-plus pricing on high-cost assets. (The long view had said: Power is an undifferentiated commodity and the price-sensitive intermediary (the state discom) chooses the cheapest source, so NHPC's Rs 4.4-6.59/unit hydro output competes against solar it is itself selling at Rs 2.66/unit — a cheaper 'good enough' electron with no end-customer brand awareness.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Stakeholder Concentration (ELEVATED); New Entrants (ELEVATED); Technological Innovation (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 12 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
