# Central Bank of India (CENTRALBK) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

Central Bank of India is an Indian public-sector bank, over 114 years old, that takes deposits from the public and lends that money out — its total business (deposits plus loans) stood at about INR 8.33 lakh crore as of June 2026, split between INR 4.79 lakh crore of deposits and INR 3.54 lakh crore of loans. It sells everyday banking to households, farmers, small businesses and large companies through a pan-India branch network of 4,605 branches (65% of them in rural and semi-urban areas), 3,820 ATMs, 13,890 business-correspondent outlets and 22,346 total touch points, plus a new IFSC banking unit at GIFT City for overseas business and 159 NRI desks. It earns mainly from net interest income — interest on loans (yield ~8.8%) and on its investment book, minus what it pays depositors (cost of deposits ~4.6%) — which was INR 14,171 crore for FY26, helped by a very high share of cheap current and savings deposits (CASA around 47% of deposits, one of the best in the industry). A smaller slice of income comes from fees, treasury/investment gains, forex and bank guarantees, and from recovering money on loans it had already written off (e.g. INR 1,062 crore in one quarter). The bank also meets government-mandated priority-sector lending targets (58% of lending versus a 40% requirement) and holds joint ventures in home finance and financial services, with approved stakes of 25.18% in Future Generali's life insurance and 24.91% in its non-life insurance arms.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Deposits / CASA** — The funding side of the bank — current and savings accounts plus term deposits from retail and government customers; deposits were INR 4.79 lakh crore in June 2026, with CASA at 46.61% (savings alone crossed INR 2 lakh crore) which management repeatedly calls the bank's single biggest strength, and it markets specific deposit products such as Cent Queen (INR 1,457 crore mobilised) and Salary Cent Prestige (INR 787 crore).
- **RAM (Retail, Agriculture and MSME)** — Management's core 'growth engine', making up roughly 68% of the loan book against a guided 65:35 RAM-to-corporate mix, and growing about 21% a year.
- **Retail** — Home, auto, education and personal loans to individuals — about INR 1.05 lakh crore in June 2026 and roughly 30% of total advances, with housing loans alone making up around 63% of retail; management says retail needs constant monitoring and follow-up, so it uses call centres, bots and 'feet on street'.
- **Agriculture** — Farm and allied lending, INR 64,274 crore in June 2026, which also helps the bank clear its mandated targets for agriculture and for small and marginal farmers (11% versus a 9.5% norm).
- **MSME** — Loans to micro, small and medium enterprises, INR 71,308 crore in June 2026 and growing ~18%; lending to micro enterprises alone is over 15% versus a 7.5% regulatory threshold.
- **Corporate** — Large-company and project lending, INR 1,12,770 crore in June 2026 after 46.5% growth off a low base (the bank had deliberately shrunk this book earlier), concentrated in renewable energy, data centres, HAM road projects and commercial real estate, with new corporate and mid-corporate branches being opened.
- **Gold loan and SHG verticals** — Two newly carved-out dedicated divisions reporting to an Executive Director — the gold loan book is about INR 36,000 crore with management saying there is still a lot of untapped scope, especially in South India — flagged as future credit growth engines.
- **Treasury / investment book** — The bank's portfolio of investments (largely securities), which earns interest income of around INR 2,547 crore a quarter at a ~6.9% yield plus trading gains that swing a lot — treasury income fell from over INR 300 crore in one quarter to INR 9 crore the next on mark-to-market.
- **Non-interest income (fees, recovery in written-off accounts, forex, guarantees)** — Fee-based earnings plus cash recovered from loans already written off — INR 1,647 crore in one quarter, of which INR 620 crore was write-off recovery — and the bank has set up a centralised bank-guarantee cell and a centralised forex cell to push this line higher.
- **Co-lending** — Loans originated jointly with partners, described as a newer stream that scaled from INR 355 crore outstanding in Sept 2021 to INR 3,362 crore a year later and called 'very robust' in later calls.
- **Joint ventures and insurance stakes** — Two existing JVs in home finance and financial services, to which the bank added approved acquisitions of 25.18% of Future Generali's life insurance arm and 24.91% of its non-life arm.
- **Branch network and overseas/GIFT City unit** — The distribution engine — 4,605 branches with 65% in rural and semi-urban India, business correspondents, customer acquisition centres at 35 places and government business centres at 9, plus one new GIFT City IFSC banking unit opened June 2026 that management intends to use as its platform for overseas business.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## The verdict: Decent — 56 out of 100 ★★★☆☆

In one breath: the quality framework finds an average business today (50/100); the risk review found 1 high risk (Government Dependency). Weighing those together gives 56 out of 100 — decent.

**The exact arithmetic:** Overall = 64% × 50 (business quality) + 36% × 68 (risk safety) = 56 out of 100 → Decent (3 stars).

- **Business quality (50/100):** The 34-check quality framework scored the business +0.02 on its −2 (poor) to +2 (excellent) scale, with 82% of checks backed by evidence; mapped onto 0–100 that is 50 points.
- **Multibagger fit (—):** The foundation test was essentially untestable (only 1 of its 3 checks had data) and no pattern shows any evidence — this pillar is left unscored rather than guessed.
- **Risk safety (68/100):** started from a clean 100; 1 high risk (Government Dependency) (−20); 3 worth watching (−12) → 68 of 100.

### The story in depth

Central Bank of India comes out of these three reviews as a repaired bank rather than a superior one, and the rating reflects exactly that split: 50 out of 100 on business quality but 68 on risk safety, blending to 56 and a "Decent" three-star grade. The repair is real and management-driven. Bad loans fell from 15.52% of the book in September 2021 to 2.60% by June 2026, credit cost fell from 2.21% to 0.40%, the bank left the Reserve Bank's prompt corrective action framework in 2022, and it has now put together more than fourteen straight profitable quarters ending in a FY26 net profit of INR4,369 crores, with provision cover around 96% and capital at 18.28%. The Corporate Culture pattern rates this a partial pass precisely because most of the multi-year promises were kept — bad loans guided below 10% and taken far lower, the loan-to-deposit ratio guided to 70% and delivered at 74% — and because uncomfortable items such as the INR632 crores deferred-tax hit and weak treasury income were disclosed rather than buried. That same track record is what earns the strong scores on management and capital allocation, alongside starting work on the new expected-credit-loss accounting two years ahead of the 2027 deadline and declining to raise capital when it did not need to.

What the reviews will not concede is that any of this has become an economic advantage, and the reason is a single trait that shows up as a strength, a pattern and a risk at once. The bank's low-cost current and savings deposits, at 47-51% of deposits and described as among the best in the industry, sit behind a 114-year-old network of 4,605 branches and 22,346 touch points with 65% rural and semi-urban reach. That franchise is what makes the Cost to Replicate pattern a partial pass — a newcomer cannot cheaply rebuild it — and it is the stated cushion under both the Good Enough Goods and Cyclicality risks, since cost of deposits has fallen to 4.60%. It is also why customer and counterparty concentration is rated the lowest risk of the eight: 85% of deposits are under INR3 crore, retail, agriculture and MSME lending is 68% of advances, and the customer base runs to about 8 crore. But the cheap funding never reaches the bottom line. Operating costs stayed at 55-58% of income against an October 2022 promise to get below 50% — the specific reason Low Price Plus is only half-present — return on assets remains below 1%, and that combination is what drives the worst score in the whole framework, a maximum negative on return on capital.

The growth story and the pricing weakness are likewise the same fact seen from two sides. Advances grew 18.76% in FY26 and 28.58% in the June 2026 quarter, with retail, agriculture and MSME up 21% and total business up 15.6%, comfortably ahead of the system — yet Market Share Gainers is only a partial pass because management itself puts the 46.5% corporate growth down to a low starting base, and the Pricing Power pattern fails outright because the yield on advances slid from 8.84% to 7.78-7.89% and the interest spread narrowed from 3.44% to about 3.06% over the same stretch. The decision to walk away from roughly INR7,000 crores of low-yield corporate loans rather than match competitors' rates counts as genuine discipline in the checks and as direct evidence of commoditised pricing in the risk review; the planned shift toward gold loans and self-help-group lending is an attempt to claw the yield back toward 8%, and it is the only pricing lever identified anywhere in the records. Two further gaps compound this. Fee income is thin and undifferentiated — commission and brokerage of INR119 crores within INR1,647 crores of other income, bancassurance at INR44 crores — so Recurring Revenue does not fit, even though the bank has bought 25.18% of Future Generali life and 24.91% of the non-life business and has near-zero insurance penetration across 5.6 crore active customers to sell into. And with no research spend disclosed and a direct question on technology spend deflected to an offline answer, Innovation Dominance does not fit either, which is the same evidence the risk review reads more kindly as steady incremental technology rather than a scramble to catch a disruptor. Sitting over all of it is the one high risk: majority government ownership, with policy and the regulator setting the terms at nearly every level, including priority-sector lending at 58% against a 40% requirement, and today's clean profits resting at an unusually benign point in the credit cycle.
