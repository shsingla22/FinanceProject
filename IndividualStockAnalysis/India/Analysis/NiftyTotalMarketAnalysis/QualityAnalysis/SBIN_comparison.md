**Checks that improved:**

- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: Brand trust demonstrably converts into a cheap, sticky liability base (CASA 39.46%), but management explicitly refuses to monetise that trust through pricing, and NIM at 3.03% is thin — image drives volume, not margin. | The long view had said: There is franchise/trust value but no image-based pricing power in the P&L — NIM is capped at ~3% and margin is set by repo pass-through, not brand.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Indian banking is concentrated at the top but far from a stable oligopoly — management repeatedly cites intensifying liability competition and pressure from market-linked savings alternatives. | The long view had said: Indian banking is fragmented and price-competitive on both deposits and floating-rate lending, with management repeatedly citing competitive pressure and NIM being dictated by repo pass-through rather than industry rationality.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Attrition below 1%, an explicit career-for-life proposition with the sector's largest learning ecosystem, and a leadership bench entirely filled internally (every MD is a promoted insider). | The long view had said: Internal pipeline is demonstrably strong — Setty rose from MD to Chairman and the MD bench is filled internally each cycle — with repeated emphasis on training and upskilling, though disclosure is generic.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: Growth is predominantly volume-led (credit +16.87%, business up Rs 11 trillion) with domestic NIM slipping from 3.12% to 3.03%, offset by a deliberate mix shift toward high-RORWA segments and stated pricing discipline. | The long view had said: Growth is clearly volume-led — 15-17% loan book growth against a NIM that is guided flat-to-down at ~3% and compressed on rate cuts, so pricing is a headwind rather than the growth driver.
- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Weak → Neutral. Now: A bank of Rs 76 trillion balance sheet earning ~1% ROA is structurally asset-heavy, not asset-light, but the low-cost CASA franchise is a genuine protecting moat that keeps returns high (ROE 18.5%). | The long view had said: Structurally asset-heavy: a Rs 76 trillion balance sheet generating a ~1% ROA means very low asset turn, and returns depend on leverage rather than asset-light economics, even though the franchise moat is real.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Communication is clear on strategy and unusually direct about uncomfortable topics — the valuation gap, deposit financialization, and AI-enabled cyber risk becoming systemic — though there is little explicit ownership of specific misses. | The long view had said: Management gives explicit numeric guidance, is held to it, openly admits when its own internal call was wrong, and refuses to give a flattering ECL number rather than guessing.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Stated horizon is genuinely long (Viksit Bharat 2047, multi-year calibrated shift, YONO 2-3 year scaling), but as a government-controlled bank with short, rotating chairman/MD tenures — four MDs changed across these four calls — independence and personal tenacity are structurally constrained. | The long view had said: A stated multi-year repositioning (Project Saral, Analytics 2.0, YONOisation) has been executed consistently across two chairmen, though as a government-majority PSU its independence is inherently constrained and it aligns strategy to state objectives.

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 1 worsened, 6 unchanged; 1 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Unknown on the long view, Unknown on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Deposits and floating-rate loans are commoditised products where the intermediary-free customer can switch on rate alone, and SBI's domestic NIM of 3.03-3.12% sits well below private peers, with management explicitly declining to price up and choosing to 'de-emphasize' segments where capital is not compensated. The unusual feature is that SBI is itself the low-price incumbent rather than the premium product being undercut, which caps the risk. (The long view had said: Deposits and loans are commoditised products, yet the calls affirmatively show SBI retaining pricing power rather than defending a premium: it took the savings rate down to 2.5%, deliberately avoided aggressive high-cost and differential-rate wholesale deposits, and still grew savings balances 10.6% with CASA improving — while savings market share rose from 20.2% (2004) to 26.27% (2024) as competitors lost share.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
