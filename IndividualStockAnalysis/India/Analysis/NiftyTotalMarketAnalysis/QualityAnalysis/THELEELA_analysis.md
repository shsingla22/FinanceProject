# Leela Palaces Hotels (THELEELA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Leela Palaces Hotels & Resorts Limited (formerly Schloss Bangalore Limited) runs a chain of luxury hotels, palaces and resorts in India under The Leela brand, which management describes as India's only listed pure-play luxury hospitality company, backed by sponsor Brookfield and listed in June 2025. It sells expensive hotel rooms, restaurant and banquet dining, weddings and events, and spa/wellness experiences to well-off Indian and foreign travellers, and it earns most of its money by owning and operating the hotels itself — filling rooms at high nightly rates (FY26 same-store RevPAR up 14%, ADR up 13%) plus food and beverage sales. A smaller, capital-light slice comes from managing hotels owned by other people for a fee (hotel management agreements, or HMAs), plus a 25% equity stake with a management contract on a Dubai asset. It operates across Indian luxury destinations — city hotels in Delhi, Bengaluru, Chennai, Mumbai and Hyderabad, and leisure properties in Udaipur, Jaipur, Kovalam, Coorg and elsewhere — with FY26 operating EBITDA margin of 49% (Q4 55%), FY26 PAT of INR 403 crores, and a stated target of INR 2,000 crores EBITDA by FY30.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Rooms / owned palaces (same-store business)** — Renting luxury rooms at its own hotels — the core earnings engine, with five owned palaces all delivering double-digit RevPAR growth in FY26 and 'retail' (individual, directly-booked guests) described as the dominant segment at about 55% of total business.
- **Food & Beverage (restaurants and banqueting)** — Restaurants, bars and banquet/wedding catering inside the hotels, which management calls a key differentiator and 'important pillar' — roughly 39% of revenue in Q1 FY26, growing 15% in FY26, with non-resident (walk-in, non-staying) guests now 54% of covers at city hotels.
- **Hotel Management Agreements (HMA) — capital-light expansion** — Running hotels owned by third parties for management fees without putting up the capital, such as The Leela Hyderabad, a signed 80-key hotel in Jaisalmer and properties in Sikkim and Mumbai; fees grew 20% year-on-year in Q1 FY26 and this is described as one of four growth pillars.
- **The ARQ — invite-only membership club** — A new ultra-luxury paid membership club launched inside existing hotels (starting at The Leela Palace Bengaluru, expanding to Delhi, Chennai and Mumbai), targeted at 2,000-plus paying members at stabilisation and pitched as high-margin because it reuses existing hotel space and cost.
- **The Leela Luxury Residences** — Branded ultra-luxury serviced apartments, starting with 60-plus keys in Mumbai with a 22,000 sq ft club, due to open in FY27; branded residences are also how the company plans to recover its USD 70 million Dubai investment within 2-3 years.
- **Development pipeline / greenfield expansion** — Building or buying new owned hotels — Mumbai BKC (250 keys), Coorg (71-key all-villa resort acquired), plus construction underway in Agra, Srinagar, Ranthambore, Bandhavgarh, Ayodhya and Sikkim — taking the chain from 13 hotels toward a contracted 21, with FY26 the fastest key-addition year at 23% growth (966 additional keys of visibility).

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Luxury hospitality here is a high-operating-leverage, asset-heavy business whose results swing with travel cycles and shocks: Q1 FY26 was hit by Operation Sindoor, Q4 FY26 RevPAR growth collapsed from ~20% to 6% on the West Asia conflict, and MICE bookings were cancelled — meanwhile the company is expanding at its fastest-ever pace (23% key growth, nine greenfield projects, ~Rs 1,650 crore outlay) at a point where margins are at record highs (49% FY26, 55% in Q4), the classic set-up for over-investment at a peak. Demand is nonetheless consumer/operations-linked rather than tied to any customer's capex, and leverage remains low at 1.6x net debt/EBITDA.

> "While occupancy in March took a hit due to international travel being disrupted, we continued to grow ADRs in double digits." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow-type consumer demand (not customer capex), 60%+ incremental revenue flow-through with conservative 1.6x net debt/EBITDA, and countercyclical asset enhancement/acquisition (Coorg, Dubai) funded from a de-levered balance sheet*

- ⬜ Not enough sales history.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 44% and 48% (±1.7 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⬜ Insufficient capex data.
