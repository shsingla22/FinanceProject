# Alkyl Amines Chem (ALKYLAMINE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Alkyl Amines Chemicals is an Indian chemical maker that produces aliphatic amines — mainly methylamines and ethylamines — plus amine derivatives and specialty chemicals such as acetonitrile. It sells these as bulk industrial inputs to other manufacturers, chiefly pharmaceutical (API) makers and agrochemical companies, with a newer product aimed at dyes, pigments and electronics; management notes it is usually only a small 'C item' in a customer's total input cost. It makes money on volume and on the spread between selling prices and raw material costs (ammonia, methanol and other alcohols, plus coal that generates about 80% of its power/fuel cost as steam), passing raw material increases through to customers where it can. It runs three plants in India (including a site at Kurkumbh) at roughly 60%–85% utilisation, selling largely into the domestic Indian market — where it competes with Balaji, RCF, the newly entered Aarti Drugs and aggressive Chinese imports — with some direct exports including to the US.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Methylamines** — Its larger, more export- and competition-exposed amines line; management sizes the total Indian market across the four producers at roughly 80,000–90,000 tons against installed capacity of over 150,000 tons, so it describes methylamines as a 'long' (oversupplied) item, and it was the part most hurt by US-related trade disruption and Chinese pricing in FY26.
- **Ethylamines** — A mainly domestic-focused amines line in an Indian market of about 30,000–35,000 tons, less affected by global disruption because competition is local, though selling prices fell enough in H1 FY26 to squeeze contribution per kg; a high-capacity new ethylamine plant was commissioned around May–June 2023.
- **Acetonitrile** — A specialty solvent sold mainly to pharma, made via the acetic acid route (versus imports made as an acrylonitrile by-product), addressing an Indian market of roughly 30,000–35,000 tons where imports had been about 40%; an anti-dumping duty effective end-June 2025 is expected to lift its volumes and prices from around Q4 FY26.
- **Amine derivatives and new import-substitute products** — R&D-developed products, largely import substitutes for the same pharma and agro customers, run partly on capacity freed up by the ethylamine expansion; management in 2022 put 25,000–30,000 tons of new capacity at Rs. 600–800 crore of eventual annual sales potential.
- **New Kurkumbh project** — A roughly Rs. 120 crore plant for a product going mainly into dyes and pigments, electronics and other uses, with no other Indian producer; mechanical completion slipped to about end-June 2026 with commissioning shortly after, and management expects about 1.5x sales-to-asset turnover.

### The story in depth

The core of this business is a well-run, self-financed chemicals maker that earns good money on the capital it uses. Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹26 is operating profit, and margins have moved only about ±3.8 points a year, which says costs are genuinely under control. For every ₹100 of capital in the business — shareholders' money plus borrowings — it generated ₹30 of operating cash this year. Management scores at the top of the framework on four separate counts: they are candid (they listed four distinct ways US policy hurt them and admitted missing their own volume guidance, and owned up to weak results in DEK and Monoisopropylamine), they are disciplined (all growth organic with no acquisitions, projects delivered well within budget, and they kept investing counter-cyclically through a three-year downturn), they think in decades (plant decisions on a decade view, 4-5 year product gestation, technology developed in-house, and a refusal to enter DMF or upstream ammonia just because peers did), and they stay out of the limelight (they will not name unlaunched products or disclose volumes and customer-wise sales, and the same executive director and CFO handle the calls soberly). That combination is why Management scores 1.5 and Return on Capital 1.33 in the area scores, and why the overall verdict lands at 60 out of 100 — 'Decent', three stars — with resilience judged SOUND.

The same traits that produce the strengths also produce the ceiling, and this is the central tension in the record. The insistence on organic, self-developed growth with no acquisitions is a top-marks stewardship trait, but it shows up on the other side of the ledger as a -1 on geographical expansion: the export mix has sat at roughly 20% for years with no expansion programme, and the only international exposure discussed, the US at about 1% of the top line, was a source of damage rather than growth. Growth and Industry Structure score just 0.17 and Capital Allocation 0.33. Three multibagger patterns come out as likely fits, and each one is qualified by a matching risk. 'Toll Roads' fits because management explicitly describes itself as a tiny, non-negotiated line in customers' cost base, and amines and acetonitrile have few players — but the toll keeps getting broken by Chinese imports and by a fourth entrant in methylamine. 'Market Share Gainers' fits because share was retained and 'perhaps even slightly increased' in FY26 and acetonitrile share rose after the anti-dumping duty — but methylamine volumes were lost to that fourth entrant and monoisopropylamine share has been under Chinese pressure for years. 'Innovation Dominance' fits because everything since the original US licences is in-house R&D with a running pipeline of import substitutes — but conversion is slow, which is why the R&D check scores only 1: the ₹600-800cr of new-product revenue promised back in 2022 has not shown up, and the newest named product, at Kurkumbh for dyes, pigments and electronics, is a ₹120cr opportunity flagged in November 2025.

Where the strengths run out, the risks take over, and they are mostly the same fact seen from the other side. Pricing Power is a clear no-fit and the pricing/mix/volume check is -1, because this is a commodity where price changes are raw-material pass-through: FY23's top-line fall was value-driven not volume-driven, FY25 realisations fell 5-6% against a 3-4% raw material decline, and FY26 saw both price and volume fall about 1% each. That is the same condition the risk review calls 'Good Enough Goods' at ELEVATED — buyers switch on price alone, management concedes 'both routes are acceptable to the customers' for acetonitrile, and DMF is not worth making because imports are cheaper. Cyclicality is the one HIGH RISK, with results explained across all four years by swings in ammonia from about ₹50/kg to over ₹100/kg, in coal, and in acetonitrile realisations from about ₹200 down to ₹140-150 and back. New Entrants is ELEVATED and is the mirror of the 'Cost to Replicate' no-fit: Aarti Drugs commissioned a methylamine plant in FY26 into a market with roughly 150k tons of capacity against 80-90k tons of demand, and Chinese producers sell at or below cost and redirect US-blocked volumes into Asia. Even the good news carries a string: the anti-dumping duty that lifted acetonitrile share is why Government Dependency sits at WATCH, since profits in two product lines now lean on trade policy. On the other side, the flow-through nature of demand cushions all of this — amines go into customers' ongoing production, not their capital spending — and Technological Innovation and Fashion Risk are both LOW, with around 200 pharma customers, life sciences at 65-70% of sales, and an 80:20 domestic-to-export split spreading the customer base. Supply is the concentrated end: ammonia is imported, cannot be stored, and is bought month to month.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across all four years management explains results in terms of commodity price swings — ammonia moving from ~Rs.50/kg to over Rs.100/kg, coal, acetic acid, acetonitrile realisations falling from ~Rs.200 to Rs.140-150 and back — with revenue flat while volumes move, and FY26 topline/bottomline flat, spreads in ethyl/methyl described as at 2-decade lows. Demand is repeatedly framed as pharma/agro destocking and inventory cycles rather than secular growth, and management itself calls the business subject to 'normal cycles, up and down'.

> "the top line has been flat because prices have been subdued... all businesses settled into their normal cycles, up and down" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/consumable demand — amines go into customers' ongoing API and agrochemical production, not their capex, and management notes 'we are actually a very minor cost in their cost of material. It's what you might call a C item', which supports pass-through of RM inflation; capex is continued counter-cyclically through the downturn (Kurkumbh Rs.120cr project, new ethylamine plant).*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 0 of the last 11 years with swings of ±11 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 11% and 26% (±3.8 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 22% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 8% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

*"A new player changes the rules"*

## What to watch

- Whether the ₹600-800cr of new-product revenue promised in 2022, and the ₹120cr Kurkumbh product named in November 2025, actually reach the sales line — this is what separates the 'Innovation Dominance' pattern from the R&D check's score of 1 for slow conversion.

- Methylamine volumes and share against Aarti's new plant, in a market carrying roughly 150k tons of capacity against 80-90k tons of demand — the 'New Entrants' risk directly tests the 'Cost to Replicate' no-fit verdict.
- Any realisation increase that is not simply raw-material pass-through — after FY26's ~1% fall in both price and volume, this is the only thing that would move 'Pricing Power' off no-fit and lift the -1 on the pricing, mix and volume check.
- Ammonia sourcing, which is imported, cannot be stored and is bought month to month — the supply-side leg of the 'Stakeholder Concentration' risk and the main channel through which 'Cyclicality', the one HIGH RISK, hits the ₹26-per-₹100 operating margin.
