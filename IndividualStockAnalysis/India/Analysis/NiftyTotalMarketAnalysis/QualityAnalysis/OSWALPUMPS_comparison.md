### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 2 weakened, 7 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Every FY26 call frames the model as winning lowest-price government tenders while defending returns through full backward integration (castings, motors, winding wire, cables, modules, BOS) and value engineering — Q2 quantified 2.85% of a 6.5% margin hit mitigated, and Q3 delivered a 164bp sequential margin recovery while still posting 45.9% ROCE. (The long view had said: A genuine cost culture is evident and repeated across all four calls — 100% backward integration, value engineering, 'frugal operating mindset' — and it is what lets them survive tender price cuts ('Oswal will be the last sufferer'). But the economics are high-margin/low-turn (24.9% EBITDA, 172-day cash cycle), not the low-margin, high-asset-turn scale model the pattern describes.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Cumulative installations rose from ~48,900 (Jun 2025) to 80,000 (Oct 2025) to 90,000 (Jan 2026) to 106,122 across 15 states, with a stated 31% live-to-date PM-KUSUM share and FY26 revenue up 44.3% while a competitor was flagged as slowing — though the share is being won in L1 price tenders, so part of it is bought with margin (EBITDA margin down from 27.4% to 23.2% over the year). (The long view had said: Cumulative installs rose from 48,900 (Jun-25) to 90,000 (Jan-26) to 1,06,122 (May-26) across 15 states on a stated 31% PM-KUSUM share, which is genuine leadership — but the share figure is quoted once and not tracked upward thereafter, the order book shrank each quarter (29,961 → 24,500 → 19,912) and incremental share is being defended through falling tender prices and margins, which is exactly the caveat the pattern warns about.)

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Management uses 'trusted leader' language, but there is no A&P spend, distributor leverage or price premium — the buyer is a state agency awarding on lowest bid, and management concedes prices are set by the tender, not by the name. (The long view had said: There is real heritage — a 55-year-old Oswal name in agriculture with a pan-India dealer and 500-person service network — but the brand delivers no premium pricing since ~90% of revenue comes from lowest-price government tenders, and there is no A&P spend, consistency promise or branded adjacency discussed.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No R&D spend, pipeline or named new products with revenue impact are disclosed in any of the four calls; the only engineering discussed is cost-side value engineering to offset falling tender prices, and gross-margin direction is down rather than innovation-led up. (The long view had said: Value engineering is real and repeatedly credited with defending margins, and a new-product pipeline (helical rotor, PCP, boiler feed, chemical, industrial pumps) is described — but no R&D spend is ever disclosed, and the helical pump promised for 'end of third quarter' launch in Aug-25 was still unlaunched in Feb-26 on performance grounds, so innovation is not yet a delivered, revenue-generating cadence.)

*Unchanged: Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 2 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Revenue is tied to the award and execution timing of discrete government tender programmes rather than to ongoing operational demand, and management guided FY27 growth down to 20-25% with an outright revenue decline in H1 pending PM-KUSUM 2.0 rollout; Q4 FY26 also saw input-cost pressure from geopolitical uncertainty. The 120-150 day execution windows and lumpy order book (29,961 pumps in Jul-25 to 18,800 in Oct-25 to 24,500 in Jan-26 to 19,912 in May-26) show award-cycle swings, though underlying irrigation demand is structural. (The long view had said: Demand is a lumpy, one-time capital installation funded by a subsidy programme rather than a flow/consumable, so revenue and margins swing with scheme timing — growth guidance fell from 50-60% to 20-25% with an outright H1 FY27 decline, EBITDA margin from 27.4% to 23.2%, while the company is simultaneously scaling capacity from 2 lakh to 5 lakh pumps and 600 MW to 2.1 GW of modules with ~INR350 crores of FY27 capex into a scheme pause — the classic over-investment-at-peak pattern. Metal and input costs at 'all-time high' add a second cyclical lever.)
- **New Entrants**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Competitive bidding intensity is repeatedly cited as the direct cause of margin compression — L1 tender prices fell ~7.5% and management has flagged 'competitive tender pricing' in each of the last three calls — with cost cuts framed as the response. No structurally different new-format entrant is named, so the exposure is real but bounded on the evidence given. (The long view had said: Across Q3 and Q4 FY26 management describes a widening field of tender bidders resetting price: tender realisations fell 13-14%, EBITDA margin guidance was cut from 27-29% (Aug 2025) to 22-23% for FY27, and management frames new bidders as taking 'desperate' price calls it cannot control.)

**That worsened:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: The product is sold into L1 (lowest-price) government tenders where the intermediary — the state agency — decides on price, the end user (the farmer) has no brand pull, and the pump system is not technically complex; margin has slid from 27.4% (Q1) to 23.7%, 25.4% and 23.2% (Q4) as tender prices reset downward. Defence is entirely cost-side value engineering, not pricing power. (The long view had said: The product is sold through lowest-price government tenders where the nodal agency, not the farmer, chooses the supplier and brand awareness at the end user counts for little — cheaper bidders cut realisations 13-14% in two consecutive tenders and management defends the premium on cost structure rather than product differentiation. Bounded because backward integration gives a genuine cost floor and the new helical/PCP products were held back specifically to be better than incumbent alternatives.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Technological Innovation (WATCH); Fashion Risk (QUANT FLAG).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (HIGH RISK).*

*Not comparable: Shifting Consumer Preferences (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
