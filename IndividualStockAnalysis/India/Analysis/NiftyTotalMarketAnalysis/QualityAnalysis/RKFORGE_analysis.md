# Ramkrishna Forgings Ltd. (RKFORGE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: INDUSTRIAL MANUFACTURING*

Ramkrishna Forgings is an Indian manufacturer that makes forged and cast metal parts — mostly steel components such as axle and drivetrain parts, differential housing cases and similar critical pieces — which it sells to vehicle and equipment makers rather than to consumers. Its core customers are commercial-truck manufacturers, in India (M&HCV), North America (Class 8 trucks) and Europe, and it wins multi-year supply programs (typically four-year 'program life' orders) that it then produces and ships over the life of the contract; revenue therefore depends on volumes (tonnes forged), steel prices passed through into realisation per tonne, and how fully its presses are utilised. Beyond trucks it has deliberately diversified into railways, tractors/farm equipment, passenger vehicles, two-wheelers, EVs, energy, mining and off-highway, and it also does castings and assemblies through acquired subsidiaries. It is headquartered in Kolkata with manufacturing in India (including Jamshedpur) plus a subsidiary in Mexico serving North America; consolidated revenue was about Rs 4,238 crore in FY26.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Automotive / commercial vehicle forgings (core business)** — Forged components for medium and heavy commercial trucks sold to OEMs in India, North America and Europe — management repeatedly calls commercial vehicles 'our core business', and it still made up the majority of order wins (e.g. Rs 323 crore of Rs 594 crore in Q4 FY26).
- **Exports / international business (North America and Europe)** — The same forged parts sold to overseas truck and vehicle makers, a very large share of new order wins — in Q2 FY25, Rs 1,475 crore of Rs 1,522 crore of orders came from North America alone.
- **Railways** — Forged railway components plus fabrication work for coaches and wagons, described as 'a strong pillar of growth', with railways rising to 7.5% of revenue in FY26 from 4.6% a year earlier.
- **Rail wheel joint venture** — A separate JV plant (capacity around 2 lakh wheels) to make forged railway wheels, starting production around Q1 FY27 with a confirmed 80,000-wheel order from Indian Railways for passenger trains, locomotives and Vande Bharat, plus ~25,000 wheels from the JV partner, and roughly 40,000 wheels targeted in the first year.
- **Castings and assemblies (subsidiaries)** — Acquired businesses — Multitech Auto, JMT Auto (renamed Ramkrishna Castings) and ACIL — that add cast parts and an assembly vertical; the calls describe FY25–FY27 as a period of ramping up and integrating these, with integration 'substantially completed' by Q1 FY27.
- **Ramkrishna Forgings Mexico** — A Mexican company acquired in FY25 that acts as an operational hub for North American business; it was still loss-making (a Rs 4.5 crore loss in Q4 FY26).
- **Non-automotive / industrial (energy, mining, off-highway, farm equipment)** — Forged parts supplied to industrial end-users as orders come in — a growing slice of the order book, with Rs 258 crore of the Rs 594 crore Q4 FY26 orders coming from the energy segment alone.
- **New-age segments: EV, passenger vehicles, two-wheelers and aluminium forging** — A newer push including a Rs 57.5 crore, ~3,000 tonne-per-year aluminium forging line at Jamshedpur aimed at EVs; in Q1 FY27, Rs 278 crore of auto orders were ~82% passenger vehicle and 18% two-wheeler, segments the company was previously not in.
- **Advanced / non-ferrous materials venture (stated as a future direction)** — A newly flagged plan to forge and cast aluminium, titanium, Inconel and nimonic grades for aerospace, robotics and semiconductors — announced in the Q1 FY27 call as an ambition, with no revenue attached yet.

### The story in depth

Ramkrishna Forgings is a maker of forged components that has genuinely been winning more of its customers' business. The quality framework's strongest single mark goes to market share gains, scoring 2 out of 2: the company outgrew a shrinking domestic medium- and heavy-truck market by taking a bigger share of business, lifted railways from 4.6% to 7.5% of revenue by FY26, and is pushing trailer axles from a 4-5% share toward 10%. Alongside that sits a credible export build-out — large North American order wins, traction in Europe, and a Mexico hub now in production, aiming for 35% of consolidated revenue from exports — and a steady stream of new products: differential housing cases, cold forging, assemblies, aluminium forgings for electric vehicles, and now titanium, Inconel and nimonic parts for aerospace, robotics and semiconductors. The multibagger screen picks up the same two threads, rating Innovation Dominance a likely fit and Market Share Gainers a partial fit, and the risk review finds the business fundamentally sound, with fashion risk low and no signal of new entrants or shifting end-customer tastes, because demand rests on functional, engineered parts sold into multi-year programmes rather than on trends.

The trouble is that the very thing driving the wins also creates the biggest exposures. The company grows by adding capacity and buying businesses, not by charging more. Steel is a contractual pass-through with a quarter's lag, and energy and freight inflation cannot be passed on at all — so pricing contributes nothing to growth, which is why the screen rates Pricing Power, Toll Roads, Brand Strength and Cost to Replicate all as no fit, and why the risk review flags Good Enough Goods as elevated. That same capacity-led model makes the business heavy on assets: every ₹1 of assets produces only ₹0.59 of sales, forging utilisation sits at 70%, and FY26 revenue of ₹4,238 crore follows years of heavy press and casting spending. The acquisitions (Multitech, JMT/Ramkrishna Castings, ACIL, Mexico) were turnarounds rather than already-strong assets, and consolidated operating margins fell from about 22% to 17% afterwards, with the Mexico unit still loss-making. Most damaging to the management score, the 2022 promise to be net-debt-free within three years was reversed into a debt-funded buying and building spree — debt rose ₹282 crore in the first half of FY25 alone, and deleveraging by FY27 depends partly on promoter funding. That single fact pulls stewardship down to -2, drags the culture pattern to only partial despite a stable, long-tenured family leadership team present on every call from 2022 to 2026, and is why capital allocation, return on capital, competitive advantage and industry structure all score zero.

Put together, this is a capable operator in a tough industry rather than a compounding franchise, and the arithmetic reflects that: 45% weight on business quality (53), 30% on multibagger fit (18) and 25% on risk safety (68) gives 46 out of 100 — a Mixed, two-star rating. The multibagger score is the weakest leg because seven of eleven patterns are outright no-fit: there is no repeat or subscription income (revenue is one-time component shipments against monthly schedules, with no order book kept in the early years), no network effect that strengthens with scale (-2 on that check), no middleman channel, and no low-cost culture. Cyclicality is rated elevated for the same reason the share gains matter — the company rides the truck cycle, and after 'highest ever' quarters and roughly 22% margins in FY23-25, FY26 revenue grew just 5%, fourth-quarter FY25 operating profit fell to ₹98.5 crore, and FY26 pre-tax profit dropped 24%. The diversification into railways, passenger vehicles, two-wheelers, energy and castings is the deliberate answer to that cyclicality, but it introduces its own watch-list items: dependence on Indian Railways approvals for the 80,000-wheel joint-venture obligation, and capacity built for named individual customers such as the aluminium line. Meanwhile the shift to electric powertrains remains a live question for a steel-forging asset base, answered so far only by a small ₹57.5 crore, 3,000-tonne aluminium line described as testing the waters. Margins are steady year to year (about ₹15 of operating profit per ₹100 of sales, moving roughly ±2.6 points), and product development keeps converting into order wins — but until capital discipline and returns improve, the strengths and the risks remain two sides of the same capacity-led coin.
