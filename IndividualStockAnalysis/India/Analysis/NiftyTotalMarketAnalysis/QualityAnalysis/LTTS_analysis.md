# L&T Technology Services Ltd. (LTTS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: IT*

L&T Technology Services (LTTS), a listed subsidiary of Larsen & Toubro, sells outsourced engineering and R&D services — it does design, development, testing and digital/AI work on other companies' products, plants and software, rather than selling products of its own. Its customers are large global manufacturers and technology firms: carmakers and their Tier-1 suppliers, aerospace and rail companies, oil & gas, chemicals and consumer-goods plant owners, industrial equipment and electrical makers, semiconductor, telecom and media companies, and medical-device and pharma firms. It earns money from services contracts won as deals with a stated total contract value (management repeatedly flags large deals of $10M–$100M+; FY26 large-deal wins totalled $855 million), staffing them largely with engineers working from low-cost India delivery and 'high value engineering' centres, so revenue growth plus utilisation drives an EBIT margin that was 15.7% in the quarter to June 2026. Revenue is reported in US dollars (about $1,321 million in FY26 including a divested unit, $1,233 million from continuing operations; roughly $310 million a quarter) and is spread across North America — its largest market — Europe, Japan, India and the rest of the world, with delivery and design centres in India and abroad including Toulouse and an Engineering Intelligence Center in Munich.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Mobility (earlier called Transportation)** — Engineering for anything that moves — cars, trucks and off-highway vehicles, aerospace and rail — covering electric vehicles, software-defined vehicles, vehicle architecture, avionics and rail signalling; management called it a $400 million-a-year run-rate segment in 2024 and it contributed over 40% of large-deal wins in the March 2026 quarter, though its revenue has been roughly flat to slowly recovering lately.
- **Sustainability — Plant Engineering sub-segment** — Designing, modernising and digitising process plants for oil & gas, LNG, chemicals and consumer-goods customers, including new capacity projects, digital twins, engineering data management and site construction supervision; it has been the company's most consistent grower and is described as part of its most profitable segment.
- **Sustainability — Industrial Products sub-segment** — Product design and digital engineering for industrial and electrical equipment makers — machinery, motors, drives and controls, building automation, robotics, factory/digital-manufacturing and asset-management systems — currently riding demand from data centres, energy transition and US reindustrialisation after a weak patch in 2024 caused by customer inventory gluts.
- **Tech / Hi-Tech (earlier Telecom & Hitech plus Medical)** — Work for technology and healthcare clients, spanning semiconductor and chip design ('Semcon'), telecom and media networks, software and platform companies (hyperscalers and ISVs), and MedTech — medical devices, drug-delivery devices, regulatory/quality (QARA) work and medical imaging AI; it is the segment being deliberately shrunk and repositioned, with non-strategic business exited and revenue subdued, even as it grew 19.7% in FY26 helped by the Intelliswift acquisition.
- **SWC business (discontinued)** — A seasonal business that regularly dented first-quarter revenue and margins; it was classified as discontinued and divested in FY26 as part of the portfolio clean-up, and accounted for roughly $88 million of FY26 revenue based on the gap between total and continuing-operations figures.
- **Engineering Intelligence / AI (cross-cutting offering, not a reported segment)** — The company's AI and generative-AI push — platforms such as AgenticIQ, PlxAI and Ainfonix, partnerships with Anthropic, NVIDIA, AWS, Google and Databricks, dedicated Engineering Intelligence centres, and 244 AI patents out of 1,757 total — sold into all three segments and described by management as the pivot the whole strategy ('Lakshya 31', 'Six Technology Bets') now rests on.

### The story in depth

L&T Technology Services earns its living by renting out engineering brainpower, and the financial record shows that model working cleanly. Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.05 of sales, the business returns about 21% of the capital employed as operating cash each year, return on equity is 20.4%, and it converts 100–153% of reported profit into free cash while collections have tightened sharply (day sales outstanding falling from 125 days in Q1FY25 to 77 days). Sales have compounded at roughly 14% a year, right inside the steady 10–15% band the quality framework prizes, and ₹18 of every ₹100 of sales is operating profit with only about ±2.2 points of year-to-year wobble. Capital spending is genuinely for growth rather than upkeep — 5G, sensor and device test labs, a Toulouse design centre, the Munich Engineering Intelligence Centre, digital manufacturing labs — and clients say they would rather use LTTS' facilities than rebuild their own. The balance sheet is net cash at ₹3,555 crore and the dividend is rising (₹58 a share in FY26, a 48% payout), though there is no buyback and so no evidence that management times repurchases to price. The single strongest item in the whole quality review is people: attrition cut from 23.2% to a stable 14.7%, Great Place to Work recognition, fresher intake honoured, and the entire FY26 leadership refresh filled from inside. In a business where the product is the workforce, that is the asset that matters most. The end market is expanding too — engineering R&D spend moving from $1.6 trillion in 2021 toward $2.0–2.3 trillion in 2025, helped by AI data-centre building, software-defined vehicles, electrification and US reindustrialisation.

The pattern screen finds credentials and share gains but no moat, and it is the same set of facts that drives both. Brand Strength and Market Share Gainers are the two best fits: L&T parentage, leader rankings from ISG in FY23 and Everest in FY25, empanelments with aerospace majors and hyperscalers, partnerships with MIT Media Lab, NVIDIA, AWS, Google and Anthropic, and a claimed 12.4% five-year growth rate against an 8% industry rate with named consolidation wins. But the same screen records that this trajectory weakened materially, with guidance falling from the 13.5–15.5% range set in FY23. Beneath that, the way LTTS actually wins is cost: five straight years of India delivery, an offshore mix of 56% in FY23 rising toward a 60% aspiration, pyramid optimisation, overhead discipline and AI-led non-linearity, with clients buying explicitly on cost. That is why Low Price Plus registers as a partial fit — and it is precisely why Pricing Power is a clear no fit. In four years of management calls there is not a single instance of raising prices; every margin lever named is a cost lever. The quality framework scores the same trait from the other side, marking intangible benefits at minus one: this is a business-to-business engineering vendor with no image- or reputation-driven pricing, and operating margin fell from 18.3% in FY23 to 14.1% in FY26 under customer cost pressure. Innovation and hard-to-copy assets are real but only partial — 1,757 cumulative patents including 244 in AI and 1,033 co-authored with clients, named product launches like Ainfonix, PharmaWiseAI and AgenticIQ, plus embedded engineering hubs and labs clients say they will not rebuild — yet all of it sits in a crowded services market. Revenue is project and time-and-materials work (73% in Q1FY23, still 64.9% in Q1FY27) with no licences, maintenance contracts or renewal rates, so the Recurring Revenue pattern does not apply, and engineering spend is a large negotiated line in the customer's budget rather than a small unavoidable one, so Toll Roads does not either. The two apparent fits for Friendly Middleman and Forward Integrators come from numbers only, with nothing in the calls to support them — a screening hint, not a finding. That is the arithmetic behind the pattern score of 47 out of 100, and behind the two zero scores in the quality review for competitive advantage and industry structure.

The risk review lands on the other face of the same coin. Three risks are elevated, and each one is the shadow of a strength described above. Because the business is cost-led and substitutable, Good Enough Goods is elevated — clients frame their spend as cost optimisation, cheaper-faster India delivery and vendor consolidation, and the 400-basis-point margin slide is the evidence. Because the model bills for people's time, Technological Innovation is elevated: management concedes automation has broken the link between headcount and revenue, and headcount has been roughly flat at 23,600 to 23,845 since FY25 while the whole strategy has been rebuilt around engineering intelligence. The counterweight is the same patent and platform push the innovation pattern credits, which is why the record treats it as a genuine early move rather than a defence after the fact. And because demand sits inside customers' capital and R&D budgets, Cyclicality is elevated — plant engineering rides new capex projects, industrial products swing on customer inventory, automotive swings on EV programme spending — which is the direct explanation for why the otherwise steady 14% growth is lumpy year to year. Four further risks sit on watch. Growth now leans on a few very large deals ($855 million of large-deal contract value in FY26, roughly $200 million a quarter for six quarters, and a first $50-million account), so ramp timing at a handful of clients visibly moves a quarter, offset by a steadily widening base of $1-million-plus through $20-million-plus accounts and falling top-5, top-10 and top-20 concentration. Customers' own in-house and captive engineering teams are the structurally different competitor. Government exposure has largely been dealt with by divesting the Smart World & Communication business, which carried the state cyber-security work, election-delayed collections and half-year seasonality. And the company misjudged one technology shift itself: it built around electric vehicles, and management admits on the July 2026 call that the EV strategy did not play out as expected as demand moved toward hybrids — though the labs proved transferable and mobility returned to sequential growth in Q1FY27. Only fashion risk is low, because the demand is functional and contracted. Put together, the combined rating is 56 out of 100 — 63 for business quality, 47 for pattern fit, 54 for risk safety — a Decent, three-star verdict with resilience judged sound. The honest reading is a well-run, cash-generative, people-strong operator in a genuinely growing market that has not yet demonstrated it can charge more rather than cost less.
