# Zydus Wellness Ltd. (ZYDUSWELL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSUMER GOODS*

Zydus Wellness Limited is an Indian consumer-goods company that makes and sells branded packaged foods, nutrition products, sweeteners and personal-care items — brands like Glucon-D glucose powder, Complan nutrition drink, Sugar Free sweeteners, Nutralite spreads/mayonnaise, Everyuth face scrubs and washes, Nycil prickly-heat powder and RiteBite Max Protein snacks. It earns money by selling these everyday-use products to households through a very wide retail network (management cited availability across more than 2.5 million stores, split roughly evenly between urban and rural), plus modern trade, e-commerce and quick commerce, and it also sells to restaurants and food-service kitchens through Nutralite Professional. The business is reported in two segments, Food & Nutrition and Personal Care, with quarterly consolidated net sales of roughly ₹700–840 crore in the earlier years shown (₹6,930 million in Q1 FY23, ₹8,391 million in Q1 FY25). Historically an India-focused company, it became meaningfully international after acquiring the UK/Europe online supplements business Comfort Click, which lifted FY26 net sales 46.4% and made overseas sales a large growth driver while domestic sales grew only 2.4%.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Food & Nutrition** — The larger reported segment, covering Glucon-D glucose powder, Complan nutrition drink, the Sugar Free sweetener range and Nutralite; it grew 15% in Q1 FY25 and, boosted by acquisitions, 134% in Q3 FY26.
- **Personal Care** — The second reported segment, made up of Everyuth skincare and Nycil powder; it grew 41.8% in Q1 FY25 on a strong summer but declined 1.4% in Q3 FY26 as seasonal demand weakened.
- **Glucon-D (glucose powder)** — Described as the company's largest brand and a summer-heavy seasonal product, holding the No. 1 spot in glucose powder with roughly 59.7%–60.4% market share, now extended into performance hydration with the Recharge launch.
- **Complan (nutrition / health food drink)** — A malted nutrition drink for children that has been a persistent weak spot — market share of about 4.3%–4.8% and a 4th-rank position — which management plans to relaunch and reframe for newer needs.
- **Sugar Free (sweeteners)** — Sugar substitutes (Sugar Free Green, Gold Plus, I'mlite) plus the Sugar Free D'lite cookies and spreads range; a dominant brand with about 95.5%–95.9% share of the sugar-substitute market and 20 straight quarters of double-digit growth in Sugar Free Green.
- **Nutralite (dairy and spreads)** — Table spreads, mayonnaise, sandwich spreads and DoodhShakti ghee sold both to shoppers (B2C) and to restaurants and food service (B2B Nutralite Professional); management says it has delivered consistent double-digit growth on a 6-year CAGR.
- **Everyuth (skincare)** — Face scrubs, washes, peel-offs and tan-removal products; No. 1 in facial scrubs (about 46% share) and peel-offs (about 78%), though only 5th with 6.6% of the wider facial-cleansing market, and a consistent double-digit grower.
- **Nycil (prickly heat powder)** — A summer-season powder that is No. 1 in its category at roughly 34%–35% share, now also sold as a soap in international markets.
- **RiteBite Max Protein** — An acquired protein-snacking business (bars, chips, ready-to-drink) that management calls the leader in protein snacking; in its first year it roughly doubled its pre-acquisition performance and moved from breakeven to near double-digit EBITDA margins, and now sells in 9 countries.
- **Comfort Click (international online wellness)** — A recently acquired digital-first UK/Europe supplements business — brands WeightWorld, Animigo and MaxMedix, plus gummies and shilajit — that runs at much higher gross margins than the core, was funded by a low-cost bridge loan later refinanced in Euros, and is expanding into Poland, Finland, Portugal, Boots.com and Amazon UAE.
- **Cuticolor (hair care)** — A newly added hair-colouring brand described as the No. 1 doctor-prescribed product in its space, which Zydus Wellness began marketing and distributing in organized retail to build out functional skin and hair care.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Zydus Wellness earns its keep on brand ownership. Several of its products sit in a near-monopoly position in the shopper's mind — Sugar Free at 95.9% of sugar substitutes, Everyuth peel-off at 78.2%, Glucon-D at around 60%, Nycil the leader in its category, and Cuticolor the number-one doctor-prescribed hair colour — and the company keeps about ₹60 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs, with consolidated gross margins guided at 66–67%. That pricing strength is being actively fed rather than harvested: sustained advertising, celebrity campaigns and 360-degree media, funded partly by reinvesting gross-margin gains, have bought measurable share (Everyuth scrub up 373 basis points to 46.2%, peel-off up 200 basis points, sweeteners up 80 basis points), and a steady yearly stream of launches — Sugar Free Green and Gold Plus, Immuno-Gro, D'lite, Recharge, ready-to-drink, wafer bars, Nutralite Professional — carries an explicit ambition that 6–8% of revenue come from products launched in the prior 36 months. This is why the screen reads Brand Strength as a strong fit and Market Share Gainers and Innovation Dominance as likely fits, and why Customer Benefits is the single strongest area of the quality framework.

The same traits that create those patterns also create the sharpest risks. Dominance is close to its ceiling — at 95.9% in sweeteners there is very little runway left — so the share-gain pattern is running out of room in exactly the brands where the moat is deepest. Management's deliberate refusal to compete on price is what makes premiumisation work, and it is also why the screen marks Low Price Plus as no fit and Pricing Power only partial: in health food drinks, cheaper sachets and pouches have reset category economics into something close to a price war, Zydus stayed a reluctant participant to protect margin, and Complan has slid while remaining a roughly 5% player the company has chosen to protect rather than win. That is the Good Enough Goods risk and the New Entrants risk in one story — and it is the clearest limit on the advertising strength, since the same spend that won scrubs and peel-offs has not arrested Complan's decline. Growth has likewise come by purchase rather than by building: capex is maintenance-only at ₹30–35 crore with no growth programme, so expansion runs through acquisitions. RiteBite has worked, moving from breakeven to near double-digit margins and doubling its earlier growth, and Comfort Click adds the owned online channel behind the partial Forward Integrators reading — but Comfort Click is a large, debt-funded, cross-border deal concentrated in five European markets and leaning on the Amazon marketplace, which is the stakeholder-concentration worry sitting directly on top of the acquired growth engine.

What the balance sheet returns on all of this is where the story tightens. For every ₹100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings in the business, the company generated ₹3 of operating cash this year, and roughly 106 days of cash stays trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers — so the gross margin that funds the brand-building is not yet arriving as cash return. Sales have averaged around 22% a year but arrive lumpily, and weather adds real swing on top: seasonal brands fell 18.8% in FY26. Set against that, the risks that could break a business outright are all low — no installed base to be made obsolete, no regulator setting prices, and demand resting on functional repeat purchases rather than a trend. The result is a company with genuinely strong customer-facing assets, a flat competitive-advantage score, a negative return-on-capital score, and a resilience read of strained, which is how the combined rating lands at 52 out of 100 — decent, three stars — from business quality of 55, multibagger fit of 41 and risk safety of 60.
