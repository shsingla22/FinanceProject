# Hindustan Unilever Ltd. (HINDUNILVR) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSUMER GOODS*

Hindustan Unilever Limited (HUL) is an Indian consumer-goods company that makes and sells everyday branded household and personal products — soaps and skin cleansing, shampoo and hair care, skin care, toothpaste, laundry and dishwashing products, tea, and packaged foods — under brands such as Dove, Vaseline, TRESemmé, Clinic Plus, Closeup, Vim, Kissan, Bru, Minimalist and OZiva. It sells to ordinary Indian households, reaching them through millions of small general-trade shops (direct coverage was expanded by about 2 lakh outlets in FY26), modern-trade supermarkets, specialty retail such as chemists and cosmetic stores, fast-growing quick-commerce apps (about 3% of business and doubling every quarter), and its own direct-to-consumer online brands. It makes money on the volume and price of goods sold, with results driven by underlying volume growth, price changes it passes on as commodity costs (palm oil, tea, crude derivatives, soda ash) move, and cost savings; FY26 turnover was Rs. 63,763 crores with EBITDA margin around 23-24% and profit after tax before exceptional items of Rs. 2,711 crores in the March 2026 quarter alone. It operates essentially entirely in India, is majority-linked to the global Unilever group, is listed on the BSE and NSE, and pays large dividends (a Rs. 19 interim plus Rs. 10 special dividend in October 2024 totalled Rs. 6,814 crores).

**The parts of the business:**

- **Beauty & Wellbeing** — Hair care, skin care and premium beauty brands such as Dove, TRESemmé (Lamellar Gloss, Hydramatrix ranges) and Clinic Plus, which management calls out as a reported segment and a focus of premiumisation — HUL has committed Rs. 2,000 crores of capex to premium formats in Beauty and Home Care.
- **Personal Care** — Everyday personal products including skin cleansing (soaps), bodywash — described as "a core strategic priority" — Vaseline (which crossed Rs. 1,000 crores in FY26 with double-digit growth) and Closeup; the segment is important enough to have its own finance head.
- **Home Care** — Laundry and household cleaning plus dishwashing, led by Vim (over 30 years old, with Vim Liquid delivering double-digit growth on its RhamnoTech bio-surfactant technology); it is the portfolio most exposed to chemical input costs like soda ash and sulfuric acid, where HUL has repeatedly taken price increases.
- **Foods (and Refreshment)** — Packaged food and beverages including Kissan (being extended from ketchup into chutneys, a space management says has roughly four times the consumption occasions), tea brands and Bru coffee; tea prices are a recurring cost swing factor discussed on the calls.
- **Masstige / D2C (Minimalist and OZiva)** — Acquired and incubated digital-first premium brands sold mainly online and now being pushed into offline stores, described as a "fewer, bigger bets" priority and together running at about Rs. 1,100 crores of annual revenue.
- **Premium Beauty Business Unit / Agile Innovation Hub** — An in-house incubator that launches digital-native brands (for example Acne Squad and Find Your Happy Place) using small "nano factories", 14 bespoke D2C websites and influencer marketing; it is small in revenue terms but positioned as the innovation engine for new products.
- **Ice Cream (demerged)** — A former business line that was separated out of HUL in the December 2025 quarter, so recent reported numbers exclude it; the demerger produced large one-off accounting gains and was framed as improving the growth quality of the remaining portfolio.
- **Water business (sold)** — A business HUL exited by sale in December 2024, referenced only as a one-off gain in the prior-year comparison base.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Hindustan Unilever comes out of all three reviews as a genuinely strong business whose strengths are concentrated in one place: getting well-known products into the hands of ordinary households, everywhere, cheaply. The quality framework gives its best marks to distribution (more than 95% value-weighted reach, 2 lakh outlets of direct coverage added in a year, 1.37 million kirana stores on the Shikhar retailer app, and a 1,400 basis point service improvement in quick commerce), to the customer-facing convenience that reach creates, to brands that hold monopoly-like positions in the shopper's mind, and to advertising spend held at roughly 10% of turnover with absolute increases even through a weak demand cycle. The money side supports it: after direct input costs the company keeps about ₹55 of every ₹100 of sales, and cash comes back from customers 89 days before suppliers have to be paid, so the business is funded by its own customers rather than by borrowing. Every ₹100 of capital in the business produced ₹22 of operating cash. Management also scores full marks for candour — stripping out one-offs, flagging tax and gratuity adjustments unasked, and admitting share loss rather than claiming victory. Overall the combined rating is 72 out of 100, or Strong, built from 69 on business quality, 67 on multibagger fit and 84 on risk safety.

The multibagger screen reads as a direct consequence of those same strengths, and its gaps come from the same place too. Brand Strength and Innovation Dominance are the two strong fits, and both are paid for by the same wide gross margin: heritage names like Vim (30 years), Clinic Plus (three of four households) and Dove sit alongside a steady stream of launches with named revenue impact — Lakme lip oil at ₹80 crore of sales, TRESemme lamellar gloss, Vim Ultra Pro with RhamnoTech, Horlicks Nutrimax — and an extra ₹270 crore of advertising in FY26. Market Share Gainers is a likely fit for the same reason, with a 200 basis point gain held since March 2021 and record shares in Home Care and powders. But the screen is honest about where the story stops. Pricing Power is only a likely fit because prices largely move with input costs in both directions, including three straight quarters of falling prices into FY25 — so the wide margin reflects brand strength and mix, not the ability to simply raise prices at will. Forward Integrators and Cost to Replicate are only partial: there are no owned stores or royalty streams, and the company had to buy Minimalist and OZiva to get into digital-native beauty, which is the clearest evidence that a well-funded newcomer can build something HUL's distribution machine could not simply out-reach. Low Price Plus is partial because there is a real cost culture (₹99 Surf excel packs, 85g Hellmann's) sitting inside a premium-margin model, and the middleman, toll-road and recurring-revenue patterns do not apply at all — this is a business that must re-win the shopper on every purchase.

The risk review is where the strengths turn around and become the exposures, and the connection is direct. Selling to the mass Indian consumer is what makes the distribution asset valuable, and it is also what puts four risks on Watch: Good Enough Goods, where mass-end value pressure recurs across the whole four-year window from Glow & Lovely and talc in 2022 to soft mass skin in 2026; Shifting Consumer Preferences, where tea downgradation and muted nutrition drinks show legacy franchises migrating under the company's feet; New Entrants, where quick commerce is described as doubling every quarter and reshaping how people shop; and Stakeholder Concentration, which is not about customers at all — the customer base is about as spread out as one can get — but about the fact that the brand trademarks, research platforms and central buying are licensed from a single parent. Each has a stated offset: an internal superiority framework covering more than 80% of turnover, a portfolio pivot that returned Lifestyle Nutrition to four quarters of volume growth, a dedicated quick commerce organisation with e-commerce up 25%, and an India research organisation now being built. The remaining four risks are Low, and they are low precisely because of the everyday-consumption nature of the products: demand is not tied to anyone's capital spending, innovation is continuous rather than industry-resetting, revenue comes from shoppers rather than governments or subsidies, and fashion-linked bets are small pieces inside a very wide portfolio. That is why risk safety scores 84 and resilience reads Sound. The honest weak spots are elsewhere: industry structure scores zero, and the quality framework marks the company down hard on obscurity — this is the most covered, most owned consumer bellwether in India, the opposite of a neglected idea, so the operating story is well known to everyone before it reaches you.
