**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: No discount war is mentioned and margin guidance is being held, but the excerpts contain no discussion of new entrants or competitive intensity to confirm durable entry barriers. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing rationality has been breached repeatedly — competitive intensity compressed retail wire margins and lighting saw outright 5-10% price erosion — indicating limited entry barriers in the consumer-facing categories.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Consistent ability to outgrow the industry for ten straight quarters in FMEG and to hold an 11–13% W&C margin band through a commodity spike implies a consolidated structure with limited end-customer bargaining pressure. | The long view had said: The market is consolidating toward a handful of organized players, but competition remains active with rivals chasing volume and end customers buying through distributors who can switch brands.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Weak. Now: The international business declined year-on-year in Q1FY27 on geopolitical disruption, so geographic expansion is currently detracting rather than compounding. | The long view had said: Global footprint expanded from 48 to 94 countries with US distribution re-established and a healthy multi-region order book, but exports are only 4.4% of revenue and fell year-on-year in Q1FY27, well short of the >10% FY2030 goal.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: Only generic commitments to innovation and a 'richer product portfolio' are cited, with no R&D spend, new-product revenue share or efficiency evidence. | The long view had said: No formal R&D intensity disclosed, but new-product development is measurably productive — recent launches now supply about half of retail wire sales and dozens of new SKUs drive FMEG volume.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: Distribution reach and channel-partner intimacy under Project Spring are explicitly credited as the execution enabler behind outperformance and share gains. | The long view had said: Distribution intimacy is the core capability — ~90% of sales via channel, continuous district/distributor/retailer additions, dedicated key account managers and influencer programs — and management says this is where competition is weakest.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: Wires and cables carry no switching costs — realizations move with copper and growth is broad-based across all four regions and both channel and institutional buyers, with no lock-in cited. | The long view had said: No switching-cost or captive-aftermarket economics are evidenced; wires and cables are re-priced monthly to LME and sold through a contestable dealer channel.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: Repeated share capture and industry-beating growth in both segments imply weaker players are ceding ground, though the calls never name them. | The long view had said: A large unorganized segment is structurally donating share, and management frames the organized shift as permanent rather than cyclical, which is exactly where Polycab's gains have come from.

Now: Project Spring is a multi-year FY30 framework whose capex and margin guidance management has held to across all four quarters, attributing current results to earlier deliberate investments. | The long view had said: Project Leap's ₹200bn revenue goal set in FY22 was delivered a full year early, and management immediately rolled into a successor five-year plan (Project Spring to FY2030) that is tracking ahead of guidance.

Now: Polycab is the leader with brand pull in wires, but nothing in the calls indicates a monopoly-like hold in the customer's mind or a captive aftermarket. | The long view had said: Polycab holds a leading position in the electrician/dealer mind with 30-31% organized share and four straight years as the industry's most profitable player, but this is leadership rather than a monopoly-like lock.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 4 weakened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Price/realization does lift revenue, but it reads as commodity pass-through rather than pricing above cost inflation: Q4 FY26 EBITDA grew only 13% on 27% revenue growth, and in Q1 FY27 domestic W&C revenue rose 43% while volumes grew only low-to-mid single digits, with margin guidance held at an unchanged 11–13% band. (The long view had said: Management explicitly describes wires and cables as a pass-through, cost-plus business with monthly repricing to LME and USD-INR, and reports outright price deflation in lighting (bulbs/battens stagnant, downlights down 5-10% in Q2FY25, 'deflationary pricing environment' still in FY26); premiumisation exists but true above-inflation price increases without volume loss are not evidenced.)

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Capex has landed inside the Project Spring guidance (INR 14.8 billion in FY26 against INR 12–16 billion annually) alongside a consistently net-cash balance sheet and promoter-family leadership formalised as Joint MDs, but the calls contain no owning of mistakes, and the working-capital normalization target was quietly reset from 50–55 days to 45–50 days between quarters while the CFO/MD seat has changed hands more than once over the five-year record. (The long view had said: Promises made on early calls were delivered and beaten: the Rs 20,000 crore Project Leap target set for FY26 was reaffirmed in Jul 2022 despite commodity swings and hit a year early in FY25, followed by Project Spring FY30 milestones (capex Rs 14.8bn, payout 27.2% en route to 30%) tracked quarterly; the balance sheet stayed net cash throughout and weak spots (FMEG losses, margin compression, EPC revenue dips) are explained candidly — though CFO/MD turnover over the period is a mild offset.)
- **Low Price Plus**: LIKELY FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: No cost-culture language, itemised savings, or no-frills positioning appears in the last four calls; margin discussion is framed as a guidance band, not as a low-cost advantage. (The long view had said: Scale, backward integration and manufacturing depth are repeatedly cited as the source of competitive positioning and of being 'the most profitable company in the electrical industry for the fourth consecutive year', but the calls show no itemised cost-obsession culture or no-frills positioning — the company competes on quality and premiumisation, not on price.)
- **Brand Strength**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management cites market leadership, brand strengthening and a widening FMEG portfolio reaching more customers, but the calls disclose no A&P spend, heritage narrative, or premium-price evidence to substantiate brand-driven pricing. (The long view had said: Sustained A&P investment (TV/digital campaigns from FY23, rising A&P ahead of festive season in FY25), the brand stretched from cables into a full FMEG adjacency (fans, lights, switches, solar) now growing 47-71% year-on-year, and share gain plus premiumisation together — premium fans ~25% and premium lighting 35% of segment revenue by FY26.)
- **Toll Roads**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Realizations are explicitly commodity-linked and revenue growth is partly copper-price inflation, with channel partners pre-stocking on price moves — the opposite of a small-share, price-insensitive critical input; no certification or standards anchoring is claimed. (The long view had said: Wire/cable is a small but critical line item in a building or plant, and management cites approvals and a customer 'preference for quality and compliance' as share drivers (e.g. data-centre approvals), but pricing is explicitly commodity-linked cost-plus rather than the price-indifferent, standards-anchored economics of a true toll road.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 1 worsened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Across all four calls management frames demand in terms of commodity prices and capex cycles: Q3 FY26 W&C growth of 59% was attributed to 'sustained commodity price inflation' plus channel pre-stocking on elevated copper, and by Q1 FY27 the same business grew 43% in value on only 'low to mid-single digit' volume growth — revenue is being carried by copper realisations, not underlying units. Demand is repeatedly anchored to government capex (28% year-on-year), the private capex revival, real estate launches and institutional/project-led orders, and margins swing with input costs (EBITDA growth decelerated to 13% year-on-year in Q4 FY26 against 27% revenue growth), while the company itself is expanding into the upcycle with ₹12–16bn of annual Project Spring capex through FY30. (The long view had said: Across all 18 calls realizations are set off copper/aluminium LME and USD-INR with monthly price revisions, dealers destock when metal prices fall (Jun-2022) and margins compress when copper rallies 14% (Q2FY25); cable demand is explicitly tied to government capex, real-estate launches and the 'next leg of the infrastructure cycle' of private capex, and the entire window is an expansionary phase in which FY26 margins ran 13.9% ('far above the guided range') while the company committed to record capex of INR 60-80 bn over five years — the classic elevated-margin, heavy-investment cycle-peak setup, with FY27 Q1 volume growth already down to low-to-mid single digits.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
