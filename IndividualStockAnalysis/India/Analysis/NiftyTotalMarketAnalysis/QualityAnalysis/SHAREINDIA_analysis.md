# Share India (SHAREINDIA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Share India Securities is an Indian stockbroking and financial-services group that buys and sells shares, futures and options, commodities and currency on the country's exchanges — it is a member of NSE, BSE, MCX, NCDEX and MSEI, a depository participant with CDSL and NSDL, an AMFI-registered mutual fund distributor and a SEBI-registered research analyst and portfolio manager. It serves retail investors, high-net-worth individuals, foreign portfolio investors, other brokers and institutions (around 115 institutional clients as of Q3 FY25), and it also trades heavily with its own money using technology, artificial intelligence and machine-learning-driven strategies plus market-making. Money comes from brokerage and trading/market-making gains, interest on lending (an NBFC arm and margin funding for clients), fees from merchant banking and SME IPOs, and newer wealth-management fees; consolidated revenue was about INR 1,470 crore in FY26 with profit of about INR 324 crore, and net worth of roughly INR 2,760 crore by June 2026. Operations are in India — corporate office in Noida, registered office in GIFT City, Gandhinagar, with a growing branch network in tier-3 cities — plus international/GIFT City activity and stated overseas expansion.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Broking (equity, derivatives, commodity, currency)** — The core business of executing trades for clients across cash equity, futures and options, commodities and currency; management describes it as the largest engine, with daily turnover figures quoted in thousands of crores (average daily turnover fell from about INR 10,400 crore to INR 8,200 crore between Q2 and Q3 FY25 after SEBI's derivatives rule changes).
- **Proprietary / strategy-wise trading and market-making** — The firm trades its own capital using algorithmic, AI and machine-learning strategies and acts as a market maker; historically a key strength, with claimed market shares of over 6% in options, 5%-7% in commodities, 8%-10% in currency and 20% as an ISFC market maker, though management notes RBI's tighter funding norms for proprietary 'top-desk' trading are now a constraint.
- **Retail business and branches** — A push to sign up ordinary investors through digital platforms and physical offline branches in smaller tier-3 cities — seven of a targeted 30 new branches were open by Q1 FY27 (Banaras, Indore, Bhopal, Raipur, Agra, Nagpur, Hyderabad) — and management repeatedly calls retail expansion its main strategic focus.
- **Margin Trading Facility (MTF)** — Lending money to clients so they can buy more shares than their own cash allows; the book grew from about INR 361 crore (9M FY25) to roughly INR 470 crore by Q1 FY27, with a two-year target of INR 1,000 crore, and management calls it a sustainable, sticky revenue source.
- **NBFC lending – Share India Fincap** — A wholly owned non-banking finance company doing lending out of 75 branches, with a loan book of about INR 253 crore and net worth over INR 120 crore as of 9M FY25; it has raised money through its first non-convertible debenture issue.
- **Merchant banking – Share India Capital Services** — A wholly owned subsidiary that helps small and mid-sized companies raise equity and list via SME IPOs — 16 SME IPOs across about 11 sectors completed by early 2025 — supported by an MoU with a Centre of Excellence for aspiring SMEs; management says it is 'doing great'.
- **Wealth management (PMS, AIF, distribution)** — A newer vertical built through Share India Wealth Multiplier: portfolio management services launched in Q1 FY27 with about INR 150 crore of PMS and advisory assets (target roughly INR 250 crore by year end), an Alternative Investment Fund in the works, and plans to distribute third-party investment products to retail clients.
- **Institutional desk** — A desk servicing large institutional clients — about 115 of them as of Q3 FY25 — which management says is doing extremely well and is part of the shift from proprietary trading toward customer-facing revenue.
- **Technology products (UTrade Algos, Silverleaf, IDT/algo platforms)** — In-house and acquired trading technology, including UTrade Algos (described as the 'dream project' to bring algorithmic trading to retail users) and Silverleaf for high-frequency trading aimed at attracting FPIs and HNIs; technology is presented throughout the calls as the company's central differentiator.
- **International / GIFT City operations** — Trading in international markets run out of GIFT City, including an SGX connector that started operating in FY23, with overseas expansion listed as an ongoing priority to diversify revenue.
- **Treasury and strategic investments** — Deployment of the company's own capital, including a roughly 5% stake bought in the Metropolitan Stock Exchange of India (MSEI) at INR 2 per share as a strategic bet on a third exchange; fair-value swings on group investments were blamed for weaker consolidated profits in FY26.
- **Funding programme (CPs, NCDs, bank borrowing)** — Not a customer business but a stated pillar: commercial paper rated CRISIL A1+, listed non-convertible debentures, bank borrowings and retained profits, used to work around RBI's restrictions on proprietary-desk funding and to finance MTF and lending growth.

### The story in depth

Share India comes across as a busy, fast-moving broker that is genuinely building things rather than standing still. Sales have grown about 35% a year on average, and the record shows a steady cadence of named launches — the IBD platform, uTrade Algos, margin trading funding, an institutional desk, PMS, AIF, Share India Cred and GIFT City — which is why the Innovation Dominance pattern is judged a likely fit. Money is going into growth rather than just upkeep: company-owned branches in smaller Tier-3 cities with a stated 8-month payback and a hard 12-month stop-loss rule per branch, alongside the build-out of the margin funding and lending books. That effort shows up in the adjacencies rather than the core: institutional empanelments rose from 137 to 186 to 212 and PMS assets reached INR 150 crore within a quarter of launch, which supports the Market Share Gainers pattern, while the main broking share only held flat through the derivatives rule change. Day-to-day cash management is tidy — only about nine days of cash sits between paying suppliers and collecting from customers — and the checks on growth investment, geographic expansion and market share capture are all positive.

The problem is that the very things driving those patterns also drive the biggest risks, and often it is the same fact appearing twice. Growth is volume-driven: the record says turnover and exchange activity, not price or mix, do the work, and average daily turnover fell from INR 10,400 crore to INR 8,200 crore in a single quarter. That single trait explains why Pricing Power and Toll Roads are outright no-fits — charges are set by exchanges and regulators, not by the company — and why Cyclicality and Government Dependency are both rated high risk. The same quarter's regulatory package (two weekly expiries, higher margins, standardised exchange charges, a higher securities transaction tax) shows up as a 24% sequential fall in revenue and a 34% fall in profit. Likewise, the push toward the end customer through owned branches and own apps is what earns the partial score on Forward Integrators, but it puts the company head-on against discount brokers who have already reset industry pricing, which is exactly the New Entrants and Good Enough Goods concern. Even the constructive answer to cyclicality — the margin funding book growing from INR 239 crore to INR 424 crore to INR 470 crore, the lending book, and prop capital — is the reason the business is becoming more balance-sheet heavy, with every rupee of assets producing only 32 paise of sales. The record is explicit that entry barriers are low, competition is cost-based and margins swing by ten percentage points, which is why Cost to Replicate and Brand Strength find no support at all.

Put together, this is a company doing sensible things inside an industry that keeps taking the reward away, and the combined rating of 35 out of 100 — Mixed, two stars — reflects that split, with a resilience reading of strained. Business quality scores 48, multibagger fit 26 and risk safety 22. The weakest single item is the return on capital: for every INR 100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings in the business, operating cash was negative INR 5 this year, a minus 5% cash return, and the competitive advantage and return-on-capital areas score zero and minus one respectively. Meanwhile consolidated profit was flat at INR 324 crore versus INR 328 crore on a net worth of INR 2,655 crore, and shareholders have seen only a token INR 0.20 dividend, no buyback and no stated policy on distributions. Management has a decent record of doing what it said — the algo product promised for early 2023 became uTrade with 5,231 clients, PMS was approved and launched as promised, branches were opened — which is what keeps the Corporate Culture and Recurring Revenue patterns at partial rather than no-fit. But only 7 of the 30 planned branches are open, the recurring streams are still small next to the volume-driven core, and by the most recent calls management itself describes market share as merely stable and cannot explain why the stock is unloved. The strengths are real but early; the risks are already showing up in the reported numbers.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Earnings are a direct function of market turnover and volatility: PAT swung from +68% year-on-year growth and record 47% EBITDA margins (Q2 FY23) to a 24% revenue / 34% PAT sequential decline in Q3 FY25 when volumes fell, and consolidated FY26 PAT was flat at INR 324 crore versus 328 crore despite standalone growth, with two consecutive 'rough' Q4s attributed to volatility. > "ADTO has dropped from Rs. 10,400 crores from Quarter 2 to Rs. 8,200 crores per day in Quarter 3" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate build-out of operations-linked, recurring streams — MTF book (INR 239cr to INR 470cr), NBFC loan book, PMS AUM, institutional empanelments 137 to 212 — reduces reliance on the trading cycle*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 10 years with swings of ±34 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from 16% to 45% (swinging ±10.0 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 2% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 3%).
