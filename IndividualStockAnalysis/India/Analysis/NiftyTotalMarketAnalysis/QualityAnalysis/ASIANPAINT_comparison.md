## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 28 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 4 improved, 4 regressed, 20 unchanged. 6 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them).

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Allocation | Good (+0.67) | Good (+0.83) | +0.17 |
| Return on Capital | Good (+1.33) | Good (+1.33) | +0.00 |
| Growth | Neutral (+0.17) | Neutral (-0.17) | -0.33 |
| Management | Neutral (+0.50) | Neutral (+0.33) | -0.17 |
| Industry Structure | Neutral (+0.17) | Neutral (+0.33) | +0.17 |
| Customer Benefits | Good (+1.25) | Excellent (+1.75) | +0.50 |
| Competitive Advantage | Good (+1.00) | Good (+0.67) | -0.33 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Advertisement and promotion** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: A&P was deliberately stepped up all year and aimed at durable equity — BCCI colour partnership, Har Ghar, KBC, Big Boss, Spotify, digital shift — and management explicitly links it to consumer mind-share. | The long view had said: Sustained, escalating A&P that visibly builds brand equity (Virat Kohli for the bottom-of-pyramid latex launch in FY24, BCCI colour partnership, KBC, Spotify, digital shift by FY26), though the payoff shows up in volume rather than value growth.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: The proposition is explicitly built on certainty — 10-year warranties, supervision-led Total Assure and Smart Assure, and Metacare asset protection where trust, not price, is the purchase driver. | The long view had said: A deliberate build-out of certainty-based propositions — Total Assure, Smart Assure, Metacare and warranty-backed B2B solutions with measured NPS of 77 — where customers buy supervision and reliability rather than price.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: Benefits are correctly matched by customer type: emotional/aspirational branding for retail, and technical solutions, warranty and supervised service platforms for corporate and government buyers. | The long view had said: The benefit types are correctly matched by customer — image, colour authority and celebrity-led intangibles for retail consumers, and technical solutions, warranties and supervision-led service platforms for corporate/B2B accounts.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Execution-based barriers exist (regional pack logistics claimed hard to replicate), but negative value growth alongside positive volume and affordable-price-point pushes point to a competitive pricing environment. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing rationality deteriorated over the five years — a 3.7% price decrease in FY24, four consecutive deflationary quarters in FY26, and value growth persistently 4-5 points below volume, indicating discounting rather than disciplined pricing.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Neutral → Weak. Now: Asian Paints is already the dominant incumbent so runway to gain share is limited, and this year's decorative print — 3.9% volume with -1.2% value — shows defence rather than capture. | The long view had said: The company claims growth above market and cites 3-4x competitor reach, but it is already the dominant incumbent with limited runway, and value growth of 4-5% amid price cuts suggests share is being defended rather than captured.
- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Q1 openly flagged the value shortfall, monsoon impact and a luxury miss, though later quarters shift toward initiative narrative with less numeric self-criticism. | The long view had said: Management repeatedly names failures unprompted and tracks itself against prior commitments — flagging Kitchen/Bath losses, White Teak's weak bottom line, decor's 'limiting' progress, and explicitly referencing the promise made in the previous quarter.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Excellent → Good. Now: A real multi-product technology edge is claimed and evidenced (Lotus effect, graphene exteriors, world-first PU Gold, VAM-VAE emulsion), but the claims are self-reported and have not yet shown up in premium realisation. | The long view had said: A deep, multi-product technology edge sustained across the period — 160+ patents including process patents that are hard to copy, 17% of revenue from new products, and world-first launches plus VAM-VAE capability few players globally possess.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Expansion talk is intra-India regionalisation rather than new geographies; the only overseas item is a Dubai white cement plant, which is an input asset, not market entry. | The long view had said: International operations improved from a loss-making, currency-hit position in FY23 to 9% FY26 growth with PBT margin up 370 bps in Q4, led by Sri Lanka, Egypt and UAE — credible execution, though the footprint is emerging-market rather than premium-brand travel.

*Unchanged: Profit margin; Disciplined stewards; Out of limelight; Cyclical market growth; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Halo effect (caution); Mini-monopolies; Distribution advantage; Asset turn; Convenience benefits; Cost of working capital; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Structural end-market growth; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Intangible benefits; Security by obscurity; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Return on capital (headline); Research and development.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 6 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 5 weakened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Friendly Middleman**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Architects, designers and contractors are repeatedly named as the audience for Nilaya Arc, wood finishes and the 23-year ColourNext property, and retailer margins are used as a lever — but the calls are dominated by mass consumer brand-building (BCCI, KBC, Bigg Boss, Spotify), so the professional channel is one route rather than the core mechanism. (The long view had said: Across all four calls the sale is routed through painters, contractors, architects and designers — 23 colour academies, 6.8 lakh painters/plumbers/carpenters trained in FY24 with 8-9 lakh targeted, and the two-decade ColourNext property built explicitly to win architect-designer recommendation at the premium/luxury end.)
- **Forward Integrators**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The company moves toward the customer through the Beautiful Homes Painting Service across 650+ towns, direct-to-consumer service models, and the AP Assure digital B2B platform — but no own-store count, franchise royalties or D2C revenue share is disclosed in these calls. (The long view had said: Consistent move toward the end customer with disclosed store counts rising 31 (Jul 2022) → 60 (May 2024) → 74 across 20 states (Jun 2026), company-owned White Teak stores, beautifulhomes.com as the digital engine, and the world's largest own painting service growing 70-80% a year; management is candid that decor is still only ~4% of decorative revenue.)
- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Leadership is stable (Syngle as MD since FY2022, same CFO across all four calls) and long-horizon capex into backward integration and brand is consistent; management did admit luxury emulsions missed expectations in Q1, but the calls are heavily promotional and give little candid discussion of returns on capital or of what went wrong in the weak quarters. (The long view had said: Bad news is owned promptly and repeatedly ("Kitchen and Bath have not done well, to be upfront with you" in May 2024; White Teak bottom line and Bath weakness in Feb 2026; decor "struggling" in Jun 2026), promises are tracked and delivered (the double-digit volume promise made in Q3 FY24 and met in Q4), leadership is stable across all 17 calls, and capital goes to organic capex/backward integration with a consistent 60% dividend payout.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The calls point to genuinely hard-to-copy assets — a 1.7 lakh outlet network, the logistics of regional SKUs, the 23-year ColourNext colour research franchise, 160+ patents and the largest painting-service footprint — though management asserts rather than demonstrates the barrier, and the FY26 volume/value weakness suggests competitors are contesting the space. (The long view had said: A concrete, articulable barrier exists: a 1.6 lakh-plus retail network built since 1942 and 3-4x the next player, 160+ patents on products and manufacturing processes, 23 colour academies training ~8 lakh painters a year, architect relationships from a 23-year ColourNext programme, and now upstream VAM-VAE/white-cement capacity that "very limited players" worldwide possess — none of which a funded entrant rebuilds quickly.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Over the last 12 months value growth lagged volume growth — Q1 FY26 showed 3.9% decorative volume growth against -1.2% value — with down-trading in luxury emulsions and pushes into 'affordable price point' and 'very good VFM price point' products; no price increases without volume loss are described. (The long view had said: Price increases were taken and held in FY23, but the later record is the opposite — a 3.7% price cut in FY24 and four straight quarters of deflation in FY26 with value growth (4.3%) running far below volume (9%); the offset is genuine premiumisation and mix, with margins expanding on deflation rather than on price rises.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 1 worsened, 7 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The pattern across all four calls is textbook competitive-response spending: a sustained 'upsurge' in brand investment (BCCI colour partnership, KBC, Bigg Boss, Spotify), value growth running below volume growth (Q1 volume +3.9% against value -1.2%), new variants pitched explicitly against competitors' quality, and sweeteners routed to the trade — margin being invested to defend share rather than harvested. (The long view had said: From FY24 the calls show the classic incumbent response to a structurally new competitor: an outright 3.7% price cut, value growth collapsing to ~3-4% while volumes stayed high (a persistent 4-5% volume-value gap through FY26), a price-matched bottom-of-pyramid launch with a marquee ambassador, and margin given up in industrial to 'competitive intensity'.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
