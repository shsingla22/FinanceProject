**Checks that improved:**

- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Good. Now: Margin expansion of ~47bps YoY to 5.9% has come mainly from falling cost of funds and mix normalisation (unsecured recovering, CASA improving) rather than pricing power on the asset side, but it does show favourable mix alongside volume. | The long view had said: Growth is overwhelmingly volume/balance-sheet led with yields broadly flat; margin gains came from falling cost of funds and seasonality, not pricing power.
- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Neutral. Now: The only M&A reference in the window is a backward-looking mention of the Fincare bank acquisition strengthening South India presence, with no post-deal OPM evidence in these calls and no new deals; management explicitly frames the next phase as organic. | The long view had said: The Fincare deal was a large, transformational merger (not a small bolt-on) that imported an MFI book which promptly drove elevated credit costs, and integration took until April 2026.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Good. Now: Sustained 8-10% of OPEX into tech over 8 years with genuine shipped AI capability (agentic LOS, unified data lake, 11-language voice bots) is above peer norm for an SFB, but none of it is proprietary or hard for larger banks to replicate. | The long view had said: Serious and broad AI/data investment, but built largely on third-party platforms (Salesforce, Darwinbox) that larger banks can match or exceed, so no durable tech edge yet.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Regulatory licensing is a genuine barrier — the universal bank licence took years and RBI approval — but pricing rationality is weak, evidenced by rate-led deposit competition the bank is trying to step away from. | The long view had said: RBI licensing is a genuine entry barrier, but pricing discipline in the industry is imperfect, with competitive rate-setting on both deposits and mortgages.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Management explicitly called the bottom of the credit cycle in Oct 2025 and the subsequent three quarters delivered it — credit cost fell from 1.4% to 0.8%, GNPA from 2.30% to 2.03%, and unsecured swung from decline to 11% YoY growth. | The long view had said: The MFI/unsecured credit cycle bottomed in FY25 and the bank is now on the visible upturn with credit costs nearly halving year-on-year.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Indian retail/MSME credit penetration is a secular growth market and the bank cites supportive structural drivers, though banking growth remains materially cyclical as the 10% system growth in the first call showed. | The long view had said: Retail and small-business credit penetration in India is in secular expansion, and management explicitly anchors growth at a multiple of nominal GDP.
- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Disclosure is specific and quantified with bad news reported plainly (unsecured book down 1% YoY, one-time INR23 crore provision, cost-to-assets rising to 4%), and management admits past errors, though in general terms. | The long view had said: Management repeatedly disclosed missed guidance and problem areas unprompted, including admitting credit cost exceeded its own promise and that credit cards are loss-making.
- **Advertisement and promotion** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: The bank is spending on brand — celebrity ambassadors Ranbir Kapoor and Rashmika Mandanna and a new campaign — but the calls show no evidence linking this A&P to durable pricing power or measurable brand equity, and celebrity-led campaigns in banking are the least differentiated form of the spend. | The long view had said: Consistent, deliberate brand investment ('Badlaav Humse Hai') alongside record CASA acquisition growth, but brand equity is still being built rather than proven pricing power.

*Unchanged: Security by obscurity; Asset turn; Geographical expansion; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Intangible benefits; Mini-monopolies; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Convenience benefits; Distribution advantage; Assurance benefits; Research and development; Halo effect (caution); Customer types (retail vs corporate); Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Disciplined stewards; Out of limelight; Gain in market share; People matter (talent development); Share donators.*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
