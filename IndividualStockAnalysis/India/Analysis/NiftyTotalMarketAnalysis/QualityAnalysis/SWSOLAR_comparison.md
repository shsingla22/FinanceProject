Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±3.1 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Management explicitly flags new smaller EPC entrants bidding aggressively on PSU tenders, indicating low entry barriers and irrational pricing, only partly offset by gigawatt-scale/BESS complexity. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±21.7 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. ### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 4 strengthened, 1 weakened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Explicit and rising share numbers across FY26: ~15% of India's utility-scale solar commissioned, order inflows up 43% to INR10,062 crores against 15% guidance, record UOV of INR13,000 crores, and an O&M book taken from 8.7 GW to 18.3 GWp — and management says the gains were made while walking away from aggressively-priced bids. (The long view had said: Explicit and rising share numbers across the trajectory: order inflow went INR6,023cr (FY24) to INR8,000cr guided (FY25) to INR10,062cr (FY26, +43%), UOV from INR2,098cr (Jun 2022) to a record INR13,000cr (Jun 2026), and roughly 15% of all Indian utility-scale solar commissioned in FY26 was theirs, with international adaptation via a 50-50 Egypt JV with Hassan Allam. Management ties the gain to selectivity rather than price-cutting, claiming post-COVID international projects came in at margins above domestic.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Reputation with marquee counterparties is a genuine asset — the multiyear Adani Green framework agreement, the Coal India L1 win and the Egypt JV with Hassan Allam — but there is no A&P spend, premium pricing or consumer-brand heritage story on these calls. (The long view had said: Execution reputation does win repeat mandates — 'preferred EPC of choice' for Serentica, repeat AMEA Power work, and a 1GW Egypt JV attributed to developer confidence — but the name carries no pricing premium and was materially damaged in the period (credit rating fell to D, bank guarantees encashed by four customers, INR300cr indemnity provision for past project failures).)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: No R&D spend, pipeline or named-product economics are disclosed on any of the four calls; the BESS move is capability extension into an adjacent EPC scope, not disclosed innovation investment. (The long view had said: Some technical differentiation is claimed in O&M (drone thermography, IV curve tracers, analytics) and the Reliance Jamnagar pilot was framed as testing multiple technologies for BESS rollout, but R&D spend is never disclosed in any call and no named product launches with revenue impact are tracked year to year — this is an execution contractor, not an R&D-led business.)
- **Corporate Culture**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Good marks on prompt bad-news disclosure (the INR580 crore Conti write-off, SBLC invocation and OEG settlement all itemised in Q2 FY26), on beating a stated order-inflow guidance twice, and on a conservative negative-working-capital stance; against that sit the legacy US losses themselves and turnover at Chairman/CFO level over FY2022–FY2025. (The long view had said: The delivery-versus-promise record across the 17 calls is poor: the Nigeria order was 'in the last leg of approval' in Jul 2024 and by Apr 2026 had slipped indefinitely; Reliance mega-orders have been imminent since 2022 with still nothing booked by Jul 2026; FY25 revenue landed near INR6,290cr against INR8,000cr+ guidance; and FY27 guidance was cut from 15% to 10-15% within one quarter. Leadership is unstable rather than long-tenured (Chairman and CFO exited, CEO changed from Amit Jain to C.K. Thakur), and the balance sheet was not conservative through the cycle — rating D, encashed guarantees, and INR611cr of FY26 exceptional litigation charges.)

**That weakened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Management explicitly declines to compete on price — 'patient', 'only pursuing margin-accretive projects' — and no itemised cost-culture savings are described; thin 8-10% EPC gross margins come from industry structure, not a hard-to-copy cost system. (The long view had said: Some cost discipline is visible — recurring overheads held flat at INR349cr through FY26 while revenue grew 20%, producing genuine operating leverage, and they win competitively on price (L1 awards) at 8-10% gross margin. But there is no articulated, hard-to-copy cost culture with itemised savings, and management frames itself as selective on margin rather than as the low-cost champion.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Cost to Replicate (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Stressed on the long view, Stressed on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Solar EPC is awarded on lowest-bid L1 auctions where the buyer's procurement process, not brand, decides — and cheaper new entrants are already bidding aggressively, with company gross margins guided at just 8-10% on EPC. Management's only defence is walking away from work, which shows up as the deferred revenue and muted quarters rather than as protected pricing. (The long view had said: EPC contracting is awarded on lowest-price L1 tenders with no end-customer brand premium to defend — management guides gross margins of just 8-10% (EBITDA ~5-6%) and repeatedly says it must be 'patient' and 'selective' to find margin-accretive work, which is the signature of a commoditised service where a cheaper challenger can take the job. The differentiators cited (drone thermography, IV curve tracers) are modest.)
- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Management explicitly flagged aggressive bidding from smaller new EPC entrants on PSU tenders over two quarters, and responds by declining work rather than matching price — a real but bounded pressure so far, concentrated in the lower-complexity PSU/BoS segment. (The long view had said: Solar EPC has low structural barriers and management repeatedly frames the market as consolidating toward stronger balance sheets rather than protected — they lost a floating-solar tender to a smaller player (KPI), and the discipline talk about 'exiting projects without negative surprises' is defensive positioning against less disciplined bidders. No structurally different new-format competitor is described, so exposure is real but bounded.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The forward story leans repeatedly on a handful of named counterparties: a multiyear strategic framework with Adani Green plus a INR1,381 crore gigawatt order, and Reliance discussed in every one of the four calls as the swing factor for future inflows without a single order yet booked. The US legacy disputes also show how much damage two or three individual customer relationships can do (INR580 crore write-off, SBLC invocation). (The long view had said: Results hinge on a handful of named relationships — Reliance (also its promoter) is discussed on essentially every call as the awaited mega-order that is still not booked four years on, alongside Serentica, AMEA, Plenitude and Coal India; a single client transferring 2GW away collapsed O&M revenue in FY23, and four customers encashed Rs588 crore of guarantees. Module supply is likewise narrowed to a few approved manufacturers.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (QUANT FLAG); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
