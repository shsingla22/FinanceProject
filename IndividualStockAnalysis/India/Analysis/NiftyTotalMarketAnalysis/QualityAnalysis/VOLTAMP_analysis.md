### The story in depth

Voltamp comes out as a decent but not outstanding business, scoring 53 out of 100 (3 stars). That score is built from three uneven parts: business quality scored 50 and carries the largest weight (45%), fit with multibagger patterns scored only 19 (30% weight), and risk safety scored a full 100 (25% weight). In plain terms, the company looks financially safe but only moderately special — the clean risk review is doing a lot of the work in lifting the overall grade, while the pattern screen is pulling it down.

The strengths and the weaknesses largely come from the same few traits. The company keeps ₹47 of every ₹100 of sales after paying for direct inputs, which the quality framework reads as real pricing power customers are willing to pay for, and that same trait is what triggers the Pricing Power quant signal in the multibagger screen. But the pricing power does not hold steady: operating margins move by about ±4.8 points a year, which is why the framework scores barriers to entry and industry structure at zero and profit margin at zero too — the ₹16 of operating profit per ₹100 of sales is decent, but key costs appear to sit outside management's control. Growth tells the same story twice: sales have compounded at about 14% a year, right in the 10–15% band the framework prizes, yet the growth is lumpy year to year. The good number and the uncomfortable number are two sides of one business.

The clearest tension is between how the business is built and how much cash it actually returns. Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.07 of sales, an asset-light setup that should throw off strong returns — but about 169 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, the single worst check in the review at -2, and capital allocation as a whole scores -2. The result is that the cash return on capital is only 8%, scored -1. In other words, the asset-light advantage is being absorbed by the working capital cycle before it reaches shareholders. On the pattern side, no pattern is a confirmed fit: three (Pricing Power, Innovation Dominance, Market Share Gainers) rest on numbers alone, four (Recurring Revenue, Friendly Middleman, Brand Strength, Forward Integrators) are partial, and two (Toll Roads, Low Price Plus) do not fit at all. Importantly, every single pattern verdict carries the note that no conference-call judgement was available, and both Competitive Advantage and Management are scored null — so the qualitative half of the case is unverified rather than weak. Meanwhile the 8-channel risk review flagged nothing and rates resilience SOUND. The honest reading: a financially sound, reasonably profitable, steadily-growing company whose capital efficiency and competitive durability remain unproven.
