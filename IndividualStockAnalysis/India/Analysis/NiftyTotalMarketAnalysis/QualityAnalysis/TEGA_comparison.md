### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

**Checks that improved:**

- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Good. Now: Gross margin expansion of 200–300bps is explicitly attributed to region/product mix and high-margin order execution rather than volume, though headline growth was only 5–6% with consumables volumes flat. | The long view had said: Growth has been volume-led with pricing acting only as a lagged cost pass-through rather than pricing power; the pass-through is explicitly framed as cost recovery with a quarter's lag.
- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: After direct input costs, the company keeps ₹60 of every ₹100 of sales — pricing power customers are happy to pay for. And from the calls: Gross margins of ~60% held or improved through raw-material volatility, indicating real pricing power, though it derives from engineering criticality rather than image or taste. | The long view had said: After direct input costs, the company keeps ₹60 of every ₹100 of sales — pricing power customers are happy to pay for. And from the calls: Gross margin near 60% is high, but the pull is engineering performance for industrial buyers, not image or taste, and pricing follows input costs rather than brand power.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: Manufacturing is deliberately sited close to mines across regions and supported by a dedicated global product/sales organisation, giving accessibility rivals must replicate plant-by-plant. | The long view had said: Localised manufacturing beside mining regions (Chile, South Africa, India), on-site service income and a dedicated business-development function give proximity and customer intimacy rivals must ship in from afar.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Good → Excellent. Now: Combined reach of 26 manufacturing sites serving 400+ mines across 40 countries, with proximity explicitly cited as the distribution advantage, is a service footprint rivals cannot easily match in an industry where on-site service is vital. | The long view had said: Multi-continent manufacturing and service reach into mine sites — where liner installation and service income matter — is a real advantage, now materially widened by Molycop's global footprint.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: Greenfield Chile capacity for growth (not maintenance) is funded and on schedule for commercial production in Q2 FY27, with interim alternate plants avoiding sales loss, though no incremental-ROIC disclosure is given. | The long view had said: Growth capex is real and sizeable (~$30mn consumables programme, Chile greenfield plus INR21cr extra land) but commissioning slipped from June 2025 to early Q3 FY27 while consumables revenue went flat, so incremental ROIC is unproven.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: The cited copper demand trajectory (+40% by 2040, ~80 new mines needed) is a genuine secular driver, tempered by mining's inherent cyclicality and the company's own 5% FY26 growth. | The long view had said: End market is in secular expansion on independent data: copper demand +40% by 2040 needing ~80 new mines and >$250bn investment, gold demand at record levels with ~1% supply growth, and falling ore grades mean more tonnes milled per unit metal.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Advanced trials in new geographies imply displacing incumbents, but no weak competitor is identified and the only named share loss is Molycop's own decline from closed mines. | The long view had said: Share is taken by converting mills off legacy metallic liners, and the acquisition of the weak McNally Sayaji equipment franchise (revenue 183→206) is share bought from a donator, but the main rival Metso is not weak.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Neutral → Weak. Now: Talent retention is asserted as an integration priority, but the finance function shows repeated instability — a third CFO change since FY2022, with an Interim CFO presenting the FY26 results. | The long view had said: Positive signals on engagement, a dedicated CEO for Tega McNally and a 'strong talent base', but the finance seat has churned three times in five years and the company entered its largest-ever acquisition with an interim CFO.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: The mining-consumables niche is unglamorous, but obscurity is diminishing — the asset was private-equity owned by American Industrial Partners and Apollo has now taken a significant minority stake, so capital is clearly attentive to the space. | The long view had said: Mining consumables is an unglamorous, capital-light-of-attention niche that Tega has quietly compounded in, though the Molycop deal and analyst coverage now put it firmly on the radar.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 0 weakened, 7 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Toll Roads**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Management repeatedly frames the products as 'critical-to-operate' consumables for mineral processing — mill liners and grinding media that are a small line item versus a mine's cost base but essential to running the mill — and sustains ~59-60% gross margins through raw-material volatility, tariffs and freight shocks across Q2, Q3 and Q4 FY26. (The long view had said: Mill liners are a small but operationally critical input to miners, the market is effectively a duopoly with Metso, and once a mill is 'converted' after trials the business repeats — but management never quantifies the product as <1-2% of customer cost and there is no certification/standards or regulatory anchoring language.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Heritage and reputation language appears mainly for the acquired asset (Molycop's 100-year history, 400 mines in 40 countries) and for 'deeper customer relationships' post-deal; there is no A&P spend, distributor leverage or adjacency-launch evidence in the last-12-months calls. (The long view had said: The calls are silent on heritage, A&P spend, distributor leverage or brand-led adjacency — DynaPrime is discussed as a product/technology, not as brand equity.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Tega has just staked $1.48 bn on grinding media — a physically simple, largely steel-based consumable whose economics look materially weaker than its own: Molycop runs at ~11.5% EBITDA margin versus Tega's ~20–22%, and an analyst on the September 2025 call opened by challenging that Molycop's revenues have been declining. The margin plan rests on SG&A, headquarters relocation and procurement synergies rather than on any demonstrated pricing power in the acquired product. (The long view had said: This is a premium-priced wear part sold at ~57-60% gross margin and 22-23% consumable EBITDA margins against a scale competitor, and analysts have pressed directly on whether Metso can replicate the product and whether the flat FY26 consumables year meant lost share — management defended the premium rather than pointing to a structural moat. Bounded because the buyer is the sophisticated end-user itself, converting only after documented mill trials, so no intermediary pockets the price difference as in the Nobel Biocare case.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No single customer, licensor or supplier is cited as driving results in any of the four calls; the combined platform spans 26 manufacturing sites and Molycop alone serves 400+ mines across 40 countries. The only concentrated item disclosed is the $120 mn deferred contingent payout tied to a handful of previously closed mines, and that is structured to trigger only on the associated EBITDA. (The long view had said: Individual relationships visibly move the P&L — one European contract generating ~INR10 crores a month over six years, the single NMDC order for equipment, and equipment revenue missing a quarter because of 'delay in receipt of payments, inspection reports and certain dispatch clearance certificates from few customers' — and the ultimate customer set is the small, consolidated universe of global miners. Bounded by breadth of accounts and geographies.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (LOW); New Entrants (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
