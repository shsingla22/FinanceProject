### The story in depth

Jamna Auto earns a "Decent" 3-star rating with an overall score of 60 out of 100, and the arithmetic behind it is worth spelling out: 45% weight on business quality (61), 30% on multibagger fit (25), and 25% on risk safety (100). The business quality half rests on one clear engine. Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.58 of sales, an asset-light setup that scores full marks, and that same light asset base is what turns a ₹15-per-₹100 operating margin into ₹44 of operating cash for every ₹100 of capital employed — a 44% cash return. These are not two separate strengths; the high asset turn is the reason the return on capital is high, which is why Return on Capital is the standout area at 1.67 while Capital Allocation and Customer Benefits sit flat at 0.0. Alongside this, margins have moved only about ±2.0 points over the years, which the framework reads two ways at once: as evidence that costs are firmly under control, and as a sign of a rational industry where few new competitors have shown up to spoil pricing (Industry Structure scores 1.0).

The weak spots are the mirror image of that same profile. After paying for direct inputs, the company keeps only ₹38 of every ₹100 of sales — thin pricing power, with customers likely choosing on price. That single trait explains why the stable margins read as cost discipline rather than as the ability to charge more, and it lines up with the multibagger screen finding no fit for Pricing Power, Brand Strength, Low Price Plus or Innovation Dominance. An important caveat: the screen's low score of 25 comes almost entirely from absence of evidence, not from a tested failure — every one of the nine patterns, including the sole PARTIAL fit on Recurring Revenue, carries the same reason, "no conference-call judgement available." The screen was effectively flying blind, and Competitive Advantage and Management both come back as null in the quality areas for the same reason. Growth is the one area scored outright negative at -1.0: sales have compounded at about 8% a year, but lumpily from year to year, which sits uneasily beside the price-taking position — a company that cannot set its own prices has little cushion when volumes swing.

The risk review is the most important thing to read carefully, because it is doing a quarter of the work in the final score. All eight channels came back clean with an empty risk list and a resilience reading of SOUND, and that perfect 100 for risk safety contributes 25 of the 60 points. Nothing in the record contradicts it, but it is a score earned by nothing being found rather than by a strength being demonstrated. Meanwhile the quality framework does flag a live capital drag the risk channels did not pick up: roughly 37 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, a long cycle that ties up money in a business whose whole case rests on being asset-light and cash-generative. Put together, the story is a well-run, capital-efficient supplier in a calm and rational industry, throwing off strong cash returns, but with little pricing power, uneven growth, working capital that leaks cash, and — critically — no conference-call evidence yet to confirm or deny whether any durable competitive advantage exists behind the numbers.
