# Exide Industries Ltd. (EXIDEIND) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: AUTOMOBILE*

Exide Industries is an Indian battery maker — it has been in the lead-acid battery market for about 75 years and calls itself a leading player in that space. It sells batteries for vehicles (both to auto manufacturers as original equipment and, in larger and more profitable volume, to vehicle owners replacing worn-out batteries) and for power back-up and industrial uses such as home inverters, UPS systems, solar installations, telecom towers, railways and traction. Money is made by manufacturing and selling these batteries through a pan-India distribution network and a strong brand, with some sales exported; quarterly revenue crossed Rs. 4,000 crores in Q3 FY26 at an EBITDA margin of about 11.7%, and profitability swings with lead and other raw-material prices. Alongside this core business, it is spending heavily to build a lithium-ion cell factory near Bengaluru airport through a wholly owned subsidiary, Exide Energy Solutions, into which Rs. 4,802 crores of equity had been put by March 2026.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Automotive – replacement / aftermarket** — Sells 2-wheeler and 4-wheeler batteries to existing vehicle owners through the trade channel; management calls this a consistently strong, higher-margin part of the business, growing in the mid-teens quarter after quarter and hitting record quarterly revenue in Q3 FY26.
- **Automotive OEM** — Supplies batteries fitted into new vehicles at the factory (for example, being 100% supplier on the Tata Sierra petrol and new Kia Seltos models); it is described as lower-margin, moves with car and two-wheeler production, and grew about 25% year-on-year in both Q3 and Q4 FY26 after a weak FY25.
- **Home UPS / inverter batteries** — Batteries for household power back-up, sold through distributors and highly seasonal — hurt by early or extended monsoons, helped by early summer, and it posted its highest-ever Q4 sales in FY26.
- **Industrial UPS (trade and OEM)** — Batteries for commercial and industrial back-up power systems, growing steadily at roughly 13%–15% year-on-year in recent quarters.
- **Solar** — Batteries and related products for solar installations, a vertical incubated over the last few years that grew 25%–30% at times and crossed Rs. 1,000 crores of revenue in FY26.
- **Industrial infrastructure (Railways, Traction, Motive Power)** — Large B2B orders tied to public and private capex in power, railways, traction and motive power; order inflow and execution have kept it growing at double digits year-on-year.
- **Telecom** — Batteries for telecom towers — once around 10% of revenue during the 5G rollout, now a structurally declining business as operators shift from lead-acid to lithium-ion; together with exports it was about 7%–8% of turnover, with telecom (including some e-rickshaw sales) roughly 3%.
- **Exports** — Sales to overseas markets including Central Asia, Western Europe and the US; it has fallen from about 8% of revenue a couple of years ago to roughly 5%–6%, held back by tariff barriers and geopolitical tension, and management expects recovery once tariffs settle.
- **Exide Energy Solutions (lithium-ion cell manufacturing)** — A wholly owned subsidiary building a lithium-ion cell plant on about 80 acres near Bengaluru airport — two 6 GWh phases, roughly Rs. 6,000 crores of total capex, Rs. 4,802 crores of equity invested by March 2026 — with cylindrical lines starting customer samples around May 2026 and prismatic lines in trial, targeting 2-wheeler, 3-wheeler, 4-wheeler and stationary energy customers; it also already supplies lithium-ion battery packs to telecom tower operators.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Exide comes out of these three reviews as a solid, well-run business in a stable industry that has not yet shown it can turn that stability into fast growth. The quality framework's best marks are for industry structure (0.83) and the things customers get (0.75): operating margins have barely moved, about a point either way in a year, which the framework reads as a sign of rational pricing and no meaningful new competition, and the pan-India distribution and service network is the single most consistently cited strength across four years of calls. The business earns Rs.16 of operating cash for every Rs.100 of capital employed and keeps Rs.10 of operating profit out of every Rs.100 of sales, with a documented cost culture behind it — fixed expenses cut from 20.2% to 19.4% of sales, and cost excellence projects worth 175 basis points of gross margin. But the growth score is the weakest area at 0.17, and the multibagger screen agrees: of eleven patterns only one, Brand Strength, is a likely fit, seven are partial and three do not apply at all. That is why the combined rating lands at 48 out of 100 — Mixed — built from 64 on business quality, 56 on risk safety, and just 18 on multibagger fit.

The same traits that create the strengths also create the biggest risks, and this is the heart of the story. Brand and distribution are what earn the one clear pattern fit and what let the company launch new lines like solar (Rs.1,000+ crore) and win 100% supplier status on new Tata Sierra and Kia Seltos models — but the risk review flags 'Good Enough Goods' as elevated precisely because batteries are a low-awareness purchase where the mechanic or dealer often picks the product, so the brand is doing the work of holding a position that the buyer is not consciously defending. The steady margins that signal a rational industry are also why the Pricing Power pattern is only partial: Exide did push through roughly 5-6% of increases between January and April of FY26 without losing volume, and posted its highest-ever revenue quarter, but that was recovering costs with a lag rather than pricing above inflation. Distribution and a 75-year brand give the lead-acid business a real replication barrier, yet the Cost to Replicate pattern is still only partial because the new lithium cell business rests on licensed technology and imported Chinese raw materials rather than anything Exide built itself.

The lithium bet is where every thread meets. Lead-acid is being displaced segment by segment — telecom fell from about 10% of sales to about 3%, e-rickshaw is heading the same way, and grid storage will be lithium only — which is why Technological Innovation is the second elevated risk. The company's answer is the same thing that costs it points elsewhere: roughly Rs.4,802 crore of equity into its own 6 GWh cell plant by FY26 on top of Rs.400-500 crore a year of core capex, funded independently with no private equity or strategic partner, which the framework credits as genuine tenacity but scores only 1 because start of production keeps slipping and the return on that money is entirely unproven. That single project simultaneously drives three watch-level risks: reliance on one technology partner and imported inputs (stakeholder concentration), Chinese overcapacity and price dumping setting the price the new plant must beat (new entrants), and government policy shaping the outcome. Meanwhile the promised second growth leg went backwards — exports were meant to be a major driver from November 2024 but shrank from 8% to 5-6% of revenue, the one check scored negative. The recovery now visible in several verticals is real but partly policy-made: auto OEM up 25% for two straight quarters after the GST 2.0 rate cuts, and railway order flow that runs on tender cadence — so the upturn that helps is the same dependency the risk review flags, and with about a third of revenue riding on customer capex the overall resilience reads as strained.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Technological Innovation, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

### Technological Innovation — ELEVATED
*"Out-invented by the industry"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 2 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** The core lead-acid installed base is being displaced by lithium-ion in segment after segment across the nine calls — telecom fell from ~10% of sales to ~3%, e-rickshaw is going the same way, BESS will be lithium-only, and analysts ask defensively whether data centres and other industrial segments follow. Exide's answer is a Rs. 4,802 crore captive cell plant that as of May 2026 has still not shipped a commercial cell, so the hedge is enormous, unproven and cash-consuming.

> "Telecom and E-Rickshaw continue to see shifts towards lithium-ion technology" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Growth is holding up: 2% a year earlier, 12% recently — no structural stall.

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Batteries are a low-brand-awareness purchase where the mechanic or dealer often picks the product, and Exide competes with unorganized players and a close organized rival; the price-hike discussion in FY26 is framed around 'competitive dynamics' and staged 5-6% corrections rather than free pricing power. > "our target will be meeting the landed cost of the imported cell" — *management, on an earnings call*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.
