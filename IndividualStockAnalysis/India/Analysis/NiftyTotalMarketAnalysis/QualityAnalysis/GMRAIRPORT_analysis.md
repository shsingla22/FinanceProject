# GMR Airports (GMRAIRPORT) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

GMR Airports Limited (GAL) is an Indian company that builds, upgrades and runs airports, and then earns money from everything that happens inside and around them. Its income comes in two main streams: regulated 'aero' charges paid by airlines and passengers for using the runway and terminal (tariffs set by the regulator AERA, with a big Delhi tariff revision from mid-April 2025 lifting Delhi aero revenue 161% in FY26), and 'non-aero' income from duty-free shops, food and beverage, retail, cargo handling, advertising and aircraft maintenance — which management says is now more than 50% of total income versus about a third from aero. It also monetises the land it controls around its airports through hotels, offices, retail and warehousing. Its main assets are Delhi (its biggest, ~20-21 million passengers a quarter), Hyderabad (74%-owned, ~8 million a quarter), Goa's Mopa airport, plus Nagpur and the under-construction Bhogapuram airport in India and an airport at Crete in Greece; total traffic was a record 121.6 million passengers in FY26 on total income of INR152 billion and EBITDA of INR61.5 billion.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Aeronautical (aero) business** — The regulated core: charging airlines and passengers for landing, parking and terminal use at Delhi, Hyderabad and Mopa, with prices fixed by the regulator in multi-year 'control periods' — combined aero yield per passenger was about INR430-434 in FY26, and it makes up roughly a third of total income.
- **Non-aeronautical (non-aero) business — described as the 'second platform'** — Retail, duty-free, food and beverage, lounges, advertising and other passenger spending inside the terminals; management says it now contributes over half of total income, with non-aero income per passenger of about INR640-666 and duty-free spend per passenger around INR1,033 at Delhi and INR769 at Hyderabad.
- **Airport adjacency businesses (duty-free, F&B, cargo operations taken in-house)** — Rather than leasing these out to third parties, GAL has taken over running them itself — Delhi duty-free from July 2025, Hyderabad duty-free from Q2FY26, and the Delhi cargo terminal (whose concession it formally won in FY26) — which management calls turning GAL into a 'scaled consumer platform'.
- **Commercial property development (CPD) / real estate — the 'third platform'** — Developing the land around its airports: Delhi Aerocity offices (first self-developed building handed over in FY27), hotels under Hilton/Waldorf and Taj Vivanta brands, four hotels at Goa, retail at Hyderabad, and warehousing via the wholly owned ESR GMR Logistics Park; still early but flagged as the next big growth leg.
- **Cargo and logistics** — Freight terminals and warehouses — the Delhi cargo terminal (record monthly tonnage in December 2025) plus a separate 'Cargo City' being built at Delhi through GMR Cargo and Logistics with a INR7.5 billion loan, and a new 50,000-tonne Hyderabad cargo terminal two with a temperature-controlled pharma zone.
- **MRO (aircraft maintenance, repair and overhaul)** — Aircraft servicing at Hyderabad, including a three-year base-maintenance deal with Akasa Air for its ~30 Boeing 737 MAX fleet, a Boeing Defence India contract for Indian Navy P-8I aircraft, and a half-million-square-foot facility built for Safran.
- **Airports under construction / new assets** — Greenfield and upgrade projects that are spending money now and will earn later — Bhogapuram in Andhra Pradesh (98.7% complete as of March 2026, opening targeted Q2FY27), Nagpur airport upgrade, and Crete in Greece (69% complete); these are the main reason consolidated net debt sits around INR340 billion.

### The story in depth

GMR Airports scores 56 out of 100 — "Decent," or three stars — built from 62 for business quality, 48 for multibagger fit and 56 for risk safety, with overall resilience judged sound. The core of the story is a genuinely protected position: each airport concession is a de facto monopoly over its catchment ("Mini-monopolies" scores a full 2), long-dated concessions plus slot-based switching costs make the position hard to attack (another 2), and the "Cost to Replicate" pattern is a strong fit because nobody can build a second city-centre Delhi airport no matter how much capital they have. Sitting on top of that is a strong end-market: Indian aviation is expanding structurally, with passport penetration at only 6.5% and the air-versus-rail price gap collapsing from ₹4.7 to ₹1.5 per km. Customer-facing quality is the highest-rated area at 1.0 — after direct input costs the company keeps ₹93 of every ₹100 of sales, and cash comes back from customers 278 days before suppliers need paying, so customers effectively fund the working capital. Four of the eight risk channels — technology, fashion, shifting consumer preferences and good-enough goods — come back low, precisely because demand here is functional, repeat-purchase travel infrastructure layered on physical assets no software format threatens.

The most striking connection is that the same two traits produce both the best patterns and the worst risks. First, forward integration: pulling duty-free at Delhi and Hyderabad in-house from joint ventures in July 2025, taking back the Delhi cargo terminal concession and running its own F&B makes "Forward Integrators" the clearest pattern across all seven calls, and by the fourth quarter of FY26 the non-aero platform earned more than the whole. That same build-out is also the stated cushion against the top risk, Government Dependency, rated high — because aeronautical pricing is set by the regulator AERA, and the single biggest swing factor in the period was a tariff notification that lifted Delhi aero revenue 127% and then 173% year-on-year with no volume loss. That is why "Pricing Power" rates only a likely fit rather than a strong one: the increase is a regulator-granted true-up of past under-recovery, not a price the company set itself, and it comes bundled with the CP4 order, the HRAB recomputation now at the Supreme Court and the Hyderabad reset still pending. Second, asset intensity: large growth capex — Bhogapuram delivered to 98.7% ahead of its December 2026 target, plus Crete, Nagpur, Delhi Cargo City and Aerocity — earns a positive mark and drives the concession-adding growth story, but the same building programme is why asset turn scores a full negative 2 (every ₹1 of assets produces ₹0.27 of sales) against ₹340bn of net debt at 5.5x net debt to EBITDA on ₹152bn of FY26 income, and why Return on Capital is the weakest quality area at negative 0.67.

The remaining risks all trace back to how narrow the earnings base still is. Delhi alone contributes roughly half of total income and its EBITDA swing drove the entire FY26 result, while system traffic is repeatedly explained by the fleet decisions of just two or three airlines — Air India, IndiGo and Akasa — which is why Stakeholder Concentration is elevated. Cyclicality is elevated for a related reason: traffic swung from 10% year-on-year growth to 2.5% and then 1% on aircraft-supply constraints and the Air India crash. That near-flat organic traffic is exactly why "Market Share Gainers" rates only partial — portfolio share is being added by winning new concessions rather than growing existing ones — and it echoes the negative mark on persistence of growth, where sales have averaged about 3% a year and lumpily. The Noida/Jewar airport entering Delhi's catchment is on watch rather than high risk, held down by the same locational scarcity that powers "Cost to Replicate," though management's answers are described as confident but defensive and it concedes possible cargo share loss. Six patterns land at partial and two — "Friendly Middleman" and "Low Price Plus" — do not fit at all, which is what holds multibagger fit to 48. Worth flagging: Management is the lowest-scoring quality area at 0.17 even though the "Corporate Culture" pattern notes guidance has largely been delivered, including the Hyderabad dividend after a five-year gap and Delhi turning profitable the quarter after management said it would.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Forward Integrators — STRONG FIT
*"Owns the shop, not just the product"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence and the numbers agree (1 of 2 checks computable supporting).

**What the calls show:** The clearest pattern across all seven calls: GAL pulled duty-free at Delhi and Hyderabad in-house from JV structures (July 2025), took the Delhi cargo terminal concession, runs its own F&B outlets, and by Q4FY26 the non-aero platform earned more than the whole Hyderabad airport, with real estate named as the 'third platform'.

> "our strategy of creating a platform for non -aero is now reflecting in numbers. Non-aero platform numbers are now higher than Hyderabad airport numbers" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Operating margin moved from about 21% (early years) to 36% (recent years) — the widening-profitability signature of pricing power / lock-in.
- ❌ Operating margin of 39% but swinging ±11.4 points a year — too volatile for a protected franchise.

### Cost to Replicate — STRONG FIT
*"Could a newcomer copy this?"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** Finite, un-replicable locations under multi-decade OMDA concessions: a new entrant with capital literally cannot build a second city-centre Delhi airport, and management's response to Noida — that it will generate its own demand rather than cannibalise — has held up across the calls.

> "It is a city centre airport. It has certain locational advantages , which Noida does not have." — *management, on an earnings call*

### Pricing Power — LIKELY FIT
*"Raises prices, keeps the customers"*

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**What the calls show:** Aeronautical pricing — the single biggest swing factor in the period, with Delhi aero revenue up 127% then 173% year-on-year purely on a tariff notification — is set by AERA, and the CP4 order, the HRAB recomputation (TDSAT, then Supreme Court), the Hyderabad MYTP filing, the 46% OMDA revenue share to AAI, government-awarded concessions (Nagpur/MIHAN, Bhogapuram), Union Budget duty-free allowances, VAT-on-ATF cuts and a 25% cut in landing/parking charges all sit with government or regulator.
