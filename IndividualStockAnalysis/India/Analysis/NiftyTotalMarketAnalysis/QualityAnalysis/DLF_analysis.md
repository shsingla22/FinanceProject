# DLF Ltd. (DLF) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSTRUCTION*

DLF Limited is an Indian real estate company that builds and sells homes and also owns commercial property it rents out. On the development side ("DevCo", largely through DLF Home Developers), it builds and sells apartments, floors and luxury/super-luxury residences to individual home buyers, mainly in Gurugram (NCR), plus Mumbai, Goa and other cities — booking sales up front and collecting cash in instalments as construction milestones are hit (record collections of about INR 13,500 crores in FY26 and new sales bookings of INR 20,143 crores). On the rental or "annuity" side (mostly held through DLF Cyber City Developers Ltd, DCCDL), it owns roughly 50 million square feet of offices, shopping malls and data centres that it leases to corporate tenants and retailers for recurring rent, at about 95% occupancy. Money therefore comes from two streams: one-off profits on homes sold (gross margins in the 40-50%+ range) and steady rental income (DCCDL revenue near INR 7,400 crores in FY26), with operations concentrated in Gurugram/Delhi NCR and extending to Chennai, Mumbai, Goa and Noida.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Development business ("DevCo" / DLF Home Developers)** — Builds and sells residential projects — plotted floors, apartments and townships such as DLF City Floors, Privana in Gurugram and West Park in Mumbai — and is the group's main sales engine, delivering INR 20,143 crores of new sales bookings in FY26 and now run with zero gross debt.
- **Super-luxury residential (The Camellias, The Dahlias)** — A premium slice of the housing business selling very large, very expensive homes (The Dahlias filed at roughly INR 26,000 crores of revenue with 70%+ margins, minimum unit size 10,300 sq ft) to wealthy buyers from across India and abroad; management treats it as a separate, slow-burn sales process and it drove most of Q4 FY26 sales.
- **Rental / annuity business (DCCDL and DLF's own RentCo)** — Owns and leases income-producing property — about 50 million sq ft at ~95% occupancy generating roughly INR 7,400 crores of DCCDL revenue in FY26 — with management guiding to mid-teens growth in net operating income over the next four to five years; most of it sits in DCCDL, though DLF Limited's own smaller rental pool (under 10% of the total in 2024) is being grown.
- **Offices** — The largest part of the rental business — campuses like DLF Cyber City Gurugram, Downtown Gurgaon, Downtown Chennai and the Atrium Place joint venture with Hines — leased to global capability centres and BFSI/technology companies, running at 98-99% occupancy in non-SEZ space and 88-89% in SEZs.
- **Retail (shopping malls)** — Owns and operates malls at 97-98% occupancy earning rent from retailers, with income from existing malls growing about 10-11% a year; three new malls — Midtown Plaza, Summit Plaza and Promenade Goa — are being opened, and management has long aimed to roughly double the retail footprint, including a Mall of India in Gurugram.
- **Data centres** — A newer rental asset class in Noida, where a second data centre building began earning rent in Q3 FY26 and a third is under construction; small relative to offices and retail but a growing use of rental capital.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

DLF's strengths all trace back to one thing: land it already owns in places nobody else can copy. The quality framework gives top marks to its mini-monopoly in super-luxury housing (Camellias set the benchmark, Dahlias sells at Rs.100cr-plus tickets, and no rival reaches double digits at that price point), to a regional monopoly on the DLF City/Golf Course micro-market where it holds the last developable premium parcels, and to image-driven pricing power that sustains 50%-plus gross margins with a 70%-plus margin filed on Dahlias. The multibagger screen reads the same facts and calls Cost to Replicate a strong fit — decades-old Gurugram land with golf course, Aravalli and lake-park frontage, described as the last plotted parcel that will ever exist there. Pricing Power and Brand Strength come through as likely fits on the evidence of prices rising without volume support: Dahlias GDV moved from Rs.26-29,000cr at filing to Rs.42,000cr on the same inventory, unit prices from Rs.60cr to Rs.90cr, while the buyer base widened from a 3% NRI share to about 28% and out to Tier-2 cities. Corporate Culture is the other strong fit, and it lines up with the management checks: a stable team across all sixteen calls, a public five-year Rs.1,14,000cr launch roadmap tracked quarter by quarter, and candour that repeatedly volunteers bad news — telling analysts to discount a Rs.600cr one-off tax gain and not to annualise 35% NOI growth.

The same traits that create the strengths also create most of the risks, and the record is unusually direct about it. Growth being price- and mix-led rather than volume-led is what earns the pricing marks, but it also means a quarter can hinge on 32 apartments: Q4 FY26 sales were primarily led by Dahlias, which is exactly why Stakeholder Concentration sits on watch and why value creation now rests increasingly on aspiration and status, the basis of the elevated Fashion Risk. Owning irreplaceable land is the moat and simultaneously the weakest number in the whole file — every Rs.1 of assets produces only Rs.0.11 of sales, with roughly Rs.900-1,000cr of construction spend a quarter against a four-year build, which is why the return-on-capital area scores negative even as the competitive-advantage story looks strong. Cyclicality and Government Dependency are both flagged high risk and they compound each other: presales swung from Rs.2,040cr in Q1 FY23 to Rs.690cr in Q1 FY25 to Rs.419cr in Q3 FY26, and the two weak quarters trace to approval delays and a RERA design re-approval that froze Dahlias bookings for about two and a half months. In other words, the launch roadmap that demonstrates the company's discipline is gated by a regulator it does not control, and the single project that carries the quarter is the one that can be frozen.

The offsets are real but partial. Management delivered a multi-year deleveraging promise ahead of schedule — zero gross development debt and Rs.14,155cr of net cash, with rating upgrades — and has been pushing growth capex into rental assets (Downtowns, Atrium Place, three malls, data centres) that converted into 34-35% NOI growth and rental income guided from about Rs.5,300cr toward Rs.7,400-8,200cr by FY27. That annuity engine, a 50 msf portfolio at 95% occupancy with nine-year renewal cycles and contracted escalations, is the main buffer against the cyclical development half, but the screen only rates Recurring Revenue a partial fit, and the annuity book carries its own elevated technology risk: those offices lean on GCC, IT and BFSI tenants, management was fielding AI questions by May 2026 and conceding tenants are re-reviewing decisions, and SEZ occupancy already lags at 88-89% against 98-99% for non-SEZ space. Two patterns are outright no-fits — Toll Roads, because the product is the single largest item on a buyer's balance sheet rather than a trivial input, and Low Price Plus, because this is a deliberately premium business celebrating price points 40% above local markets. That mix produces the final grade of Decent at 53 out of 100: business quality 66 and multibagger fit 56 are both respectable, but risk safety at 26 pulls the score down, and resilience is judged sound rather than strong. The honest read is a genuinely advantaged franchise whose advantages are concentrated in a few assets, a few buyers, one region and one regulator.

### Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) — PARTIAL
*"Sells once, earns forever"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (2 of 3 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** DLF runs a genuine annuity engine alongside the upfront development sale — a 50 msf rental portfolio at 95% occupancy with nine-year renewal cycles, contracted escalations and rental income guided from ~Rs.5,300cr (FY25) to Rs.7,400-8,200cr (FY27) — and the development side is funded by customers in advance (Rs.11,200cr of RERA escrow, construction-linked collections of Rs.13,500cr in FY26). ## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Government Dependency, Technological Innovation, New Entrants, Fashion Risk. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.0× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 249% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ✅ Returns on capital held near 6% (was about 7%) — no slide in what the business earns on its capital.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 2 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Residential development is a classic cycle business and management itself framed the strategy as riding an upcycle (Aug 2022), while quarterly presales swung violently across the window — Rs 2,040 cr (Q1 FY23), Rs 690 cr (Q1 FY25), Rs 419 cr (Q3 FY26) and Rs 3,967 cr (Q4 FY26) — with gross margin moving from 53% to 39% for FY26; land-bank monetisation and a 4-year build cycle mean revenue and margin are hostage to price and demand cycles in NCR.

> "provide us a unique opportunity to leverage this upcycle" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: A growing flow-type annuity book (rental/NOI ~Rs 6,400 cr FY26 heading to ~Rs 8,200 cr) plus zero gross debt in DevCo and Rs 14,155 cr net cash lets it invest counter-cyclically*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 4 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 18% and 42% (±7.8 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 51% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 3% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Across the whole window launches, sales and construction are gated by the state: approval cycles repeatedly slipped launches (Q1 FY25 sales fell to Rs 690 cr), a RERA design re-approval froze Dahlias bookings for ~2.5 months in Q3 FY26 (sales Rs 419 cr), GRAP pollution bans halt construction 30-45 days every Q3, a PIL is holding back the Goa launch, and SEZ policy leaves that sub-portfolio at 88-89% occupancy.

> "sometimes you can't micromanage the approval cycles" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Demand and pricing are private-market, not subsidy- or tender-driven; no reliance on government orders or regulated tariffs*

### Technological Innovation — ELEVATED
*"Out-invented by the industry"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** The office annuity portfolio — the stable half of the business — is levered to GCC/IT/BFSI tenants, and by May 2026 management was fielding AI questions and conceding tenants are re-reviewing decisions; SEZ occupancy already lags at 88-89% versus 98-99% non-SEZ, showing formats can become legacy.

> "The recent issues related to AI and then the war between Iran and U.S. has not had any impact on the portfolio per se. However, there are large tenants who are reviewing their internal processes" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Residential and retail are technologically insulated; new assets are 99-100% leased and management's innovation is incremental (design codes, ESG/WiredScore ratings, data centres) rather than platform-level*

- ✅ Growth is holding up: -6% a year earlier, 9% recently — no structural stall.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has collapsed from a peak of 42% to 18% — the Nobel-Biocare-style fingerprint of a premium being competed away.

### New Entrants — ELEVATED
*"A new player changes the rules"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Analysts flagged peers raising equity and aggressively buying NCR land (Oct 2024) and management answered defensively rather than dismissively; capital is flowing into the same micro-markets, though no structurally different low-cost format has appeared in the calls.

> "peers will come and go, but I think we need to work on our areas" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Legacy low-cost land bank, DLF 5 community/infrastructure moat and a dedicated 4-year-old re-trade team servicing existing owners — repeat customers generate much of new demand*

- ⚠️ Operating margin drifted down from about 40% to 26% — steady profit erosion, the mark of rising competitive pressure.

### Fashion Risk — ELEVATED
*"Cool today, uncool tomorrow"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** An increasing share of value creation rests on aspiration and status rather than function — Dahlias pricing is explicitly dynamic and taste-driven, management credits demand to lifestyle aspiration, NRI and Tier-2 elite buzz, and even owner dinners comparing price points; a cooling of the super-luxury vogue would hit both volume and the assumed price escalation.

> "today, people are looking for the best lifestyle that money can buy, and Dahlias is today that option for the country" — *management, on an earnings call*

**What the calls show:** The two channel shifts that could erode the demand base — work-from-home for offices and e-commerce for malls — were affirmatively worked through over the window: Cyber City attendance was only 50% of pre-COVID in Aug 2022 but non-SEZ occupancy reached 98-99% by FY26 with portfolio value-vacancy at 3.5%, and retail consumption and NOI kept growing 10-11%.

### Good Enough Goods — LOW
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the judge affirmatively found the risk absent or mitigated in the calls — that grounded reading overrides the 2 numeric flags, kept visible below.

**What the calls show:** The calls affirmatively show the premium is holding rather than being competed away: Dahlias prices rose ~25% in a year and again from Rs 60 cr to Rs 90 cr per unit while selling, Camellias exit value went from Rs 7,000 cr to Rs 12,500 cr, and gross margins stayed 50%+ on new products with a filed 70%+ margin on Dahlias — the opposite of the Nobel Biocare pattern.

> "from the past year alone, there has already been a 25% increase in Dahlia's pricing and we are selling" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Genuine niche/scale protection — irreplaceable DLF 5 land, direct high-awareness brand relationship with buyers and no intermediary steering the choice, so cheaper substitutes cannot replicate the location or community*

- ⬜ No input-cost data.

## What to watch

- Concentration in a single project — if Dahlias-type sales stop carrying the quarter (Stakeholder Concentration, on watch: Q4 FY26 sales primarily led by 32 apartments), the Pricing Power and Market Share Gainers evidence thins out fast.
- Approval and RERA timing — another launch freeze like the roughly 2.5-month Dahlias stoppage that took a quarter to Rs.419cr would confirm Government Dependency as the binding constraint on the Rs.1,14,000cr roadmap.
- Whether price rises keep holding without volume — the Rs.60cr to Rs.90cr per-unit escalation on flat volumes is the core of the Pricing Power and Intangible Benefits case; a stall there converts Fashion Risk from elevated to live.
- Office annuity health — SEZ occupancy at 88-89% versus 98-99% non-SEZ, plus tenants re-reviewing decisions on AI, is the crack in the Recurring Revenue pattern that is supposed to offset high Cyclicality risk.
- Capital discipline under peer pressure — competitors raising equity and buying NCR land (New Entrants, elevated) tests the stated refusal to accelerate land buying; abandoning it would put the zero-gross-development-debt and Rs.14,155cr net cash position, and the asset-turn score of Rs.0.11 per Rs.1 of assets, under further strain.
