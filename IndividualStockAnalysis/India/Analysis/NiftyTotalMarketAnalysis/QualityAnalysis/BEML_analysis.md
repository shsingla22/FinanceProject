# BEML Ltd. (BEML) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: INDUSTRIAL MANUFACTURING*

BEML Limited is an Indian government-controlled engineering company (incorporated in 1964, with the Ministry of Defence holding about 54% of the shares) that designs, builds and services heavy machinery — mining and earthmoving equipment, rail coaches and metro trains, and military vehicles. Its customers are almost entirely large institutional buyers: Coal India and its subsidiaries for mining equipment, Indian Railways and city metro corporations (Delhi, Mumbai, Bangalore, Chennai, Kolkata, Jaipur) for rolling stock, and the Indian Army and Ministry of Defence for defence platforms. It makes money mainly by winning tenders and contracts to build and deliver equipment, plus a growing stream from spare parts, overhauling and annual maintenance contracts (management says about 20% of revenue is service revenue, which carries higher margins). It manufactures from complexes at Bengaluru, Kolar Gold Fields, Mysore and Palakkad (with a new rolling-stock plant at Bhopal), sells across India through regional and district offices, and exports to 73 countries — Africa is its biggest export market, followed by the Middle East — with an all-time-high export order booking of $107 million in FY26.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Mining & Construction (M&C)** — Builds excavators, dump trucks, rope shovels, motor graders and other earthmoving equipment sold mostly to Coal India — it was 41% of FY26 revenue and the historic core of the company, but only 5-7% of the order book because these are quick-turnaround products, and management expects only 3-5% annual growth as Coal India shifts to contractor (MDO) mining.
- **Rail & Metro (R&M)** — Makes metro cars, EMU and LHB rail coaches, the Vande Bharat sleeper train and track machines for Indian Railways and metro authorities — it is the biggest part of the order book (65-68%, of about Rs 16,000 crore) but only 24% of FY26 revenue because deliveries on orders won in 2022-24 are just starting, so management expects a large revenue jump as Bangalore, Chennai and Mumbai metro projects run in parallel.
- **Defence & Aerospace (D&A)** — Supplies high-mobility trucks that carry missile, radar and command systems for the Indian Army (over 9,000 delivered since 1986), armoured recovery vehicles, BMP transmissions and aggregates, and newer items like mine-laying equipment and a light armoured vehicle — 35% of FY26 revenue, roughly 25-30% of the order book, and the fastest-growing vertical (revenue up from about Rs 1,000 crore to Rs 1,500 crore, with management guiding to 70-80% growth in FY26).
- **Strategic Systems (new SBU)** — A newly created business unit reflecting BEML's shift from supplying just the truck to supplying complete defence systems — minefield marking equipment, self-propelled mine barriers and command post vehicles — several of which enter bulk production from FY27.
- **Spares, service, overhaul and AMCs** — Sells replacement parts and maintenance on its large installed fleet (33,100 mining machines, 18,000 rail coaches, 9,000 defence vehicles delivered to date), described by management as about 20% of revenue with high margin and high growth, now being expanded through a hub-and-spoke warehouse model.
- **Exports** — Sells equipment overseas to 73 countries — mining equipment to West Asia, rolling stock to Africa, and business developing in CIS, SAARC and Asia-Pacific — with a record $107 million of export orders in FY26 and a pipeline including Tel Aviv Metro (~$250 million) and Dublin Metrolink (~$90 million).
- **New diversification: maritime cranes and tunnel boring machines** — Two businesses BEML is entering from scratch using its existing fabrication and hydraulics skills — port and shipbuilding cranes, which management believes could eventually be worth Rs 4,000-5,000 crore of annual revenue, and 6.5-metre tunnel boring machines for metro projects, against an India requirement management pegs at about $5 billion over ten years; neither contributes meaningful revenue yet.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

BEML sells into end markets that are genuinely growing: more than 15,000 rolling-stock cars needed over five years across metro, RRTS, high-speed and LHB lines, a $5bn tunnel-boring-machine requirement, defence indigenisation and the Sagarmala port build-out. That is the single strongest thing in the file, scoring the maximum on structural end-market growth, and the company is spending to meet it — INR350cr of capex planned in FY24, a INR1,500cr greenfield rolling-stock plant at Bhopal that adds 300-800 cars a year, and an all-time-high FY26 capex and R&D budget that has already produced a run of indigenous products (12x12 HMV, LAMV, 21 cu.m rope shovel, 550HP grader, 35T electric dumper, Vande Bharat sleeper). Management also scores top marks for candour, owning the INR80cr and INR250cr one-time corrections, admitting it missed its INR20,000cr order-book target by INR4,000cr, and declining to guess at quarterly numbers. But the returns underneath are poor: every INR100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings generated only INR4 of operating cash this year, and about 401 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. Growth is coming from volume and order book, not price — revenue rose about 24% in Q3FY26 while FY26 EBITDA fell 38%. Return on capital scores -0.67 as an area and capital allocation 0, which is why business quality lands at 56 out of 100 despite the strong demand picture.

The multibagger screen scores just 10 out of 100, and the reason is that the same traits that make BEML durable also cap what it can earn. Its state-owned, Mini Ratna standing since 1964 opens doors — sole-bidder status, nomination orders, a DMRC export partnership — so Brand Strength is present, yet it converts into neither premium pricing nor consumer-style marketing power, and it lands only PARTIAL. Defence gives a real Cost to Replicate barrier that money alone cannot shortcut: five-year tender-to-production cycles, two years of field trials, DRDO/VRDE qualification and a 40-year installed base of HMVs under every major missile system. But the very customers who confer that protection also set the price: tenders and cost-plus nomination formulas at 2.5-7.5%, gross margins of 2.5-10%, metro bids won on razor-thin differentials against Chinese competitors, and not one price increase across six calls — so Pricing Power is a clear NO FIT, as is Low Price Plus, since management concedes its own cost base runs above industry norm. Toll Roads is a NO FIT for a related reason: BEML sells large capital equipment that is a major line item for the buyer, so customers defer purchases, move to opex models or buy second-hand rather than stay locked in. Market Share Gainers is the most encouraging of the eight PARTIALs — exports went from INR62 crore in FY20 to INR830 crore in FY23 to a record $107mn order book in FY26, including a first-ever overseas metro rolling-stock win of $60mn in Africa, with a 73-country fleet and Tel Aviv and Dublin bids live. Innovation Dominance and Recurring Revenue are similar: a large installed base (9,000+ HMVs, 33,100 mining machines, 18,000 rail coaches) does throw off high-margin spares and overhaul work, and new products do appear every year, but neither yet shows up as durable pricing or profit.

The risk review reads as the mirror image of the strengths rather than a separate list. Government Dependency is the one HIGH RISK, and it is the same fact that produced the Brand Strength and Cost to Replicate patterns: a 54%-government-owned Schedule A company under the Ministry of Defence, selling to MoD, Coal India, Indian Railways and state metro corporations, with nomination pricing literally set by the buyer. Stakeholder Concentration is ELEVATED for the same reason and is management's own disclosure — mining rests on Coal India, defence on a single customer, rail on a handful of metro corporations. Cyclicality is ELEVATED because that revenue base is customer capex, which also explains the check noting mining sits at a cyclical low on delayed, monsoon-hit Coal India tendering while rail and metro are only at the start of their ramp. Good Enough Goods is ELEVATED for precisely the reason Toll Roads failed: under the MDO model, operators pick opex over capex and buy lower-end machines BEML does not make, or second-hand ones. Technological Innovation is ELEVATED despite record R&D, because mining is shifting from drill-and-blast to surface and underground continuous mining — a move from 25 MTPA toward a projected 200 MTPA — in segments where BEML admits it has no product and is still hunting partners. New Entrants and Shifting Consumer Preferences sit at WATCH, with private bidders now contesting work once done on nomination and 65-70% of sales already competitive. The silver linings all point the same way — exports, new verticals, electric dump trucks, a hub-and-spoke spares model — which is to say the diversification effort is real but unfinished. Resilience is judged SOUND, so this is not a question of survival; it is that a 4% cash return, a 401-day cash cycle and buyer-set pricing hold the rating to 36 out of 100, a Mixed two-star verdict built from 56 on business quality, 10 on multibagger fit and 32 on risk safety.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, Cyclicality, Technological Innovation, Stakeholder Concentration, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.1× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 61% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 5% to 13% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** BEML is a 54%-government-owned Schedule A company under the Ministry of Defence whose customers are MoD, Coal India, Indian Railways and state metro corporations, and whose pricing on nomination orders is literally set by the buyer — management states margins are 2.5% on spares and 2.5-7.5% on many orders. Fortunes turn on policy: Coal India's shift from departmental purchase to the MDO model, awaited long-term MDO policy, delayed Coal India tendering, and delayed MoD collections that hit FY26 working capital.

> "if I get order on nomination basis, from Government of India, there is no mechanism to have a cost advisor's assessment before the pricing itself. So, margin can be 2.5% and 7.5%" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate diversification away from policy-set revenue — record $107mn export order book, 73-country fleet, Tel Aviv/Dublin/Malaysia metro pursuits, maritime cranes and TBMs as new non-Coal India lines*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across all three calls the revenue base is customer-capex equipment (Coal India overburden machines, metro corporations' rolling-stock programmes, MoD platforms) and management repeatedly describes a hard-wired intra-year cycle plus a lumpy multi-year one — FY25 rail & metro collapsed from 38% to 19% of revenue on execution gaps, FY26 mining orders slipped on monsoon, and Q4 alone carried 40-45% of sales, dragging working capital and debtors. Margins swing violently with it (FY26 PBT -51%, EBITDA -38%).

> "generally first quarter 10%, second quarter 20%, third quarter 30%, fourth quarter 40%, even 45% in some cases... year after year, that is the cycle" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: ~20% service/spares revenue with fast turnaround, hub-and-spoke spares model, and an explicit push to build a day-one executable order book (Rs 5,500 cr opening FY27) to level the quarters*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 3 of the last 11 years with swings of ±15 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 3% and 13% (±3.2 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 2% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 9%).
