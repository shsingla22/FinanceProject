# Ather Energy (ATHERENERG) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Ather Energy makes and sells electric two-wheelers (scooters) in India, under two product lines described on the calls: the original 450 performance range and Rizta, a lower-priced family/convenience scooter launched in FY25 that by Q4 FY26 was about three-quarters of sales. It sells to retail consumers through a dealer-run store network that management doubled from 351 stores in March 2025 to 700 by March 2026, backed by service centres and a company-installed fast-charging network (3,611 chargers as of FY25). Most revenue comes from selling the scooters themselves — 155,000 units and INR 2,305 crores of total income in FY25, rising to roughly INR 940-1,000 crores of income per quarter and 83,000 units in Q4 FY26 — with government EV subsidies forming part of that realisation; a growing slice (14% of revenue in Q3 FY26) is non-vehicle income from software packs, accessories and servicing, which carries higher margins. Operations are almost entirely Indian, split by management into South India (its historic stronghold, ~23-25% market share), 'Middle India' (five states it targeted in FY26, taking share from ~4% to ~17%) and 'Rest of India', plus a small international presence with Sri Lanka added as a second export market; the company is still loss-making but narrowed EBITDA losses from -36% in FY24 to about -3% in Q3 FY26.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Rizta (family scooter line)** — A cheaper, practical family scooter launched in Q2 FY25 that dropped the entry price from about INR 130,000 to INR 109,000; management calls it the single biggest driver of growth, crossing 2 lakh cumulative units by Q3 FY26 and accounting for nearly three-quarters of sales.
- **Ather 450 (performance scooter line)** — The original, higher-priced performance scooter range that built the brand — especially in South India — and remains the second of the company's two vehicle platforms alongside Rizta.
- **Software / ProPacks (AtherStack)** — Optional software feature packs sold on top of the scooter — safety, navigation, convenience and ride-assist features — bought by 88% of FY25 customers, contributing over 600 basis points of revenue and described as the largest single component of non-vehicle revenue and a key reason for the company's premium pricing and margins.
- **Non-vehicle revenue (accessories, spares and service)** — Everything sold besides the scooter itself, including software, the Halo and Halo Bit smart helmets and servicing; it reached 14% of revenue in Q3 FY26 — its highest ever, close to the 15-17% typical of legacy players — and management expects it to compound as the installed fleet ages and service income builds from a still-early base.
- **Distribution network (stores and service centres)** — The dealer-operated retail footprint that management calls its main growth lever, doubling from 351 to 700 stores in FY26 at roughly 80-90 new stores a quarter with 75% opened by existing dealers, alongside service centres that more than doubled in the year, including higher-standard 'Ather Gold' centres.
- **Charging infrastructure** — A company-built fast-charging network that added 1,128 chargers in FY25 to reach 3,611 nationally, supporting the scooters rather than being described as a separate revenue stream.
- **Manufacturing and R&D** — In-house engineering that management credits with a 31% cost reduction over three-and-a-half years and 643 patents filed to date (283 in FY26), plus 'Factory 3.0' being built at AURIC as the company's largest capacity, intended to scale the upcoming EL platform later in FY27.
- **International (Sri Lanka)** — A second overseas market opened in FY25; the calls mention it only in passing, with no volume or revenue figures given, implying it is very small relative to India.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Ather's record reads as one story about a company that has bought share and reach with cash it does not yet earn back. The clearest strengths are commercial: market share more than doubled from about 8% to 18.6% in FY26, with pan-India moving 7.4% to 13.3% to 17.4% to 18.8% to 18.6% quarter by quarter, Middle India from 4% to 17.3%, Rest of India from about 4% to 12%, and the South from 13% to 23% and number one in every southern state. That expansion came through the company's own front end — dealer-funded experience centres doubling from 351 to 700, service centres more than doubling, and India's largest owned fast-charging network at over 6,000 points now earning money rather than costing it. This is why the screen calls Ather a strong fit for both Forward Integrators and Market Share Gainers, and why the Corporate Culture pattern also scores strongly: the May 2026 call audited the promises made at IPO — Rizta unlocking share, stores opening only when independently viable, EBITDA improving — and each was delivered. The quality checks agree, giving top marks for disciplined stewardship, market-share gain, candour and structural end-market growth, with electric two-wheelers still only about 17-18% of scooters sold.

The interesting part is how often the same trait produces a pattern and a risk. Ather sits at the premium end — highest average selling prices, 93% ProPack attach, prices held within a few hundred rupees even as it pushed into lower-income interior markets — which is exactly what earns the Brand Strength and Pricing Power readings; but that same premium stance is the reason the review flags Good Enough Goods, because Ather runs a self-described inverted pyramid with six premium variants and nothing in the INR1–1.25 lakh mass segment it calls the largest chunk of the market, where rivals field 10–12 cheaper variants. The same premium positioning meets New Entrants, where legacy petrol two-wheeler makers arrive with 700–800 dealer outlets against Ather's 351 at the start of FY26. Innovation cuts the same way: 283 patents filed in FY26 out of 643 to date, AtherStack 6 and 7, the Ather Charge Drive Controller, AEBS, and the LECCS charging protocol adopted as an industry standard by a 20-plus member consortium underpin the Innovation Dominance and Cost to Replicate patterns — yet the risk review notes Ather is mid-transition from NMC to LFP chemistry and that its own 450/Rizta aluminium-frame architecture is being superseded by its own new EL platform. And the itemised cost engineering behind Low Price Plus — COGS per unit down 19% in FY25 and another 9% in FY26, a 31% research-led reduction over three and a half years — exists in the same world as elevated commodity cyclicality, where rare-earth magnets, a global memory-cost spike and lithium inflation all hit in FY26 and management openly says it cannot forecast where prices go.

The financial picture explains why the overall grade is only Decent at 63 out of 100 — 45% weight on business quality at 62, 30% on multibagger fit at 71, and 25% on risk safety at 54. Sales have compounded at roughly 117% a year, but lumpily, and the returns are not there yet: out of every INR100 of sales, INR11 is an operating loss, and every INR100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings produced just INR1 of operating cash this year. Margins have swung about 186.5 points a year, which the framework reads as a sign that key costs sit outside management's hands — the same conclusion the cyclicality and supplier-concentration risks reach from the other direction, where one rare-earth supply switch cost INR20–25 crore of profit and blocked subsidy filing on most units in a quarter. Two things keep the structure sound. First, customers effectively fund the business: cash arrives 68 days before suppliers must be paid, a genuinely rare strength that lets a loss-making company expand without leaning on outside money, and growth has been purely organic. Second, government support is being deliberately weaned away — FAME/EMPS has already stepped down from INR20,000 to INR10,000 to INR5,000 per unit, and management says pricing is increasingly independent of subsidy — though this remains the single highest-rated risk, and it is the same dependence on lower entry prices (INR130,000 to INR109,000 to INR76,000 via battery-as-a-service) that makes the Toll Roads pattern a clear non-fit: this is a large discretionary purchase, not an unavoidable small cost. The bet, in plain terms, is that share gains, a doubling store base and a growing non-vehicle revenue stream (14%, with ProPack attach at 93%) eventually turn scale into profit before commodity and subsidy shocks force a retreat.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, Cyclicality. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 2 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.3× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ⬜ No profitable years to compare.
- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about -63% to -54% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Subsidy is a recurring determinant of revenue and margin across all five calls — FAME/EMPS stepped down from INR20,000 to INR10,000 to INR5,000 per unit, a subsidy-filing failure during the rare-earth supply switch cost ~INR20 crores of revenue/AGM in Q2 FY26, Q3 FY26 flagged 'potential risks to subsidies and potential pullbacks', and 5 of the ~24 percentage points of AGM in FY26 are incentive-derived (24% with subsidy vs 21% without). The company also employs a dedicated Head of Government Relations on the call.

> "we believe that this year, there are some headwinds ahead for the industry, auto in general and EV specifically, in terms of commodities and potential risks to subsidies and potential pullbacks there" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Management has deliberately reduced subsidy dependence — 'increasingly, pricing is becoming independent of subsidy' — and the without-subsidy AGM improved from 12% to 21% in FY26, showing the P&L absorbing each step-down; non-vehicle revenue (software, charging, accessories, insurance) is now ~14% of revenue.*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 3 numeric fingerprints reinforce it.

**What the calls show:** Demand is retail-consumer, operations-linked and non-capex, but the input side is openly commodity-cyclical: FY26 was hit by rare-earth magnet prices, a global memory-cost spike and lithium-ion inflation, and management concedes it cannot forecast the cycle and expects near-term margin compression it cannot fully price away.

> "I would love to tell you, this is cyclical, and there is a very smart analyst with a great report who knows what is happening. I do not think anybody knows what is happening... commodities are in a crazy space right now" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Revenue is retail two-wheeler demand, not customer capex; management is investing counter-cyclically through the cost spike (Factory 3.0, 700→2,000 stores, EL platform) and used the prior FY23 subsidy squeeze to build the ProPack model, plus strategic pre-buying, LFP re-engineering and VAVE to push out cost hits.*

- ⚠️ Year-to-year sales growth swings by ±172 percentage points — boom-and-bust revenue, not a steady compounder.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from -523% to -11% (swinging ±186.5 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 331% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 15% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Technological Innovation — WATCH

## What to watch

- Whether the pan-India share line resumes climbing or stalls — it slipped from 18.8% to 18.6% in the latest quarter, and the Market Share Gainers pattern is the single biggest support for the rating.
- Whether the two loss-making checks improve: the -11% operating margin and the 1% cash return on capital are what hold business quality at 62 and drag the Return on Capital area to -1.3.
- Whether Ather enters the INR1–1.25 lakh mass segment it currently skips — the Good Enough Goods risk and the partial Pricing Power reading both hinge on whether premium positioning holds as cheaper rivals scale.
- Whether the EL platform transition and the NMC-to-LFP chemistry switch land cleanly — the Technological Innovation risk is that Ather's own new architecture obsoletes the 450/Rizta base it just grew on.
- Whether the negative working capital position holds — the 68-day cash advantage under Cost of Working Capital is what funds a 700-to-2,000 store expansion without external money, and losing it would change the whole funding story.
- Whether another supplier shock repeats the rare-earth episode — Stakeholder Concentration is demonstrated, not hypothetical, and it cost INR20–25 crore plus a blocked subsidy filing in a single quarter.
