# Data Pattern (DATAPATTNS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Data Patterns (India) Limited is a Chennai-based defence and aerospace electronics company that designs, builds and supplies mission-critical electronic systems — radars, electronic warfare equipment, avionics (such as cockpit displays and mission systems for the LCA fighter), communication systems, test equipment and naval systems. Its customers are almost entirely Indian government defence buyers — DRDO, HAL, ECIL, the Ministry of Defence and the Army, Air Force and Navy — with a small but growing export business (repeat work in the UK and a precision approach radar delivered to a European country; export order book about INR53 crore as of May 2026). It earns money from contracts booked into an order book that is worked off over time: first paid development/design contracts, which then convert into higher-volume production orders and follow-on service contracts. Because it designs almost everything in-house rather than assembling imported parts, margins are unusually high — FY26 revenue of INR925 crore with 63% gross margin, 40% EBITDA margin, an order book of roughly INR2,062 crore, and a net-cash (debt-free) balance sheet.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Development contracts** — Paid design-and-build work where the customer (usually DRDO or the MoD) funds Data Patterns to develop a new system or prototype; management calls these the seed of future revenue because they later convert into production orders — roughly 31–53% of revenue depending on the quarter (37% in Q3 FY26).
- **Production contracts** — Serial manufacture and supply of systems already developed and approved, which is the larger and more repeatable revenue stream — around 50% of revenue in Q1 FY25 and the biggest contributor at 57% in Q3 FY26.
- **Service contracts** — Ongoing support, maintenance and related services on delivered equipment; the smallest of the three contract types at roughly 11% of revenue in Q1 FY25.
- **Radars** — The company's flagship area — ground, airborne, maritime, fire-control, surveillance and precision approach radars, including a first-of-its-kind Indian deep-space surveillance radar (initial prototype contracts of about INR189 crore and INR173 crore in FY23, with the full-scale versions described as potentially worth thousands of crores).
- **Electronic warfare (EW), including COMINT and ELINT** — Systems that detect, intercept and jam enemy communications and radar signals, plus self-protection jammer pods for Indian fighter aircraft; management calls it a core strength built over more than a decade with DRDO, and large EW orders from ECIL and the MoD were the headline wins of FY26.
- **Avionics** — On-board aircraft electronics such as cockpit displays and mission systems, notably for the LCA Mk1A and the smart cockpit for LCA Mark 2 (expected to carry over to the AMCA fighter); early orders were in the INR60–183 crore range and it is described as a repeat-order, high-margin area.
- **Communication systems** — Military radios, radio relays and related equipment for the armed forces, developed in-house to replace imported kit; listed as a focus area alongside radars and EW but no separate revenue figure is given.
- **Automatic test equipment (ATE), naval systems and other strategic electronics** — Smaller lines covering ground test/support equipment, naval electronics, fire control systems (e.g. BrahMos), seekers and an autonomous battle-tank autoloader; individually modest orders (single-digit to ~INR31–60 crore) that round out the portfolio.
- **Exports** — Direct sales to overseas defence customers — regular repeat business in the UK and transportable precision approach radars delivered and site-accepted in a European country — currently small at about INR53–63 crore of order book but flagged as a strategic long-term growth pillar.
- **In-house / internally funded new product development** — Self-funded R&D (INR54 crore spent in Q1 FY25 alone out of QIP proceeds) to build products India currently imports, including drone detection/spoofing/jamming and airborne radars — no revenue yet, but management positions it as what moves the company from subsystem supplier to full-system provider and drives future growth.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

> "Strategically, Data Patterns is steadily transferring from a subsystem supplier to full systems and solution provider for the defence sector" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Operating margin moved from about 19% (early years) to 41% (recent years) — the widening-profitability signature of pricing power / lock-in.
- ❌ Operating margin of 40% but swinging ±11.6 points a year — too volatile for a protected franchise.

### Low Price Plus — NO FIT
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls contradict the pattern — that grounded reading overrides any supportive numbers.

**What the calls show:** The model is the opposite of low-price/high-turn: gross margins of 63% and EBITDA of 40% in FY26 with a 365-day cash conversion cycle. Cost advantage is cited only relative to European development cost, not as a scale-and-thrift culture passing savings to price.

> "cash conversion cycle improved meaningfully to 365 days in FY26 from 428 days in FY25" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹63 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 0.5× a year — not the high-turnover, low-margin machine this pattern needs.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, Cyclicality, Stakeholder Concentration, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.0× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ⚠️ Only about 15% of recent operating profit became cash (was 59%) — profits are getting stuck in paper, an early stress signal.
- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 5% to 21% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Almost the entire business is set by government: DRDO/MoD/HAL/ECIL/ISRO orders, L1 competitive tenders, Union Budget defence allocations and Atmanirbhar/indigenous-content policy are cited as the demand driver in every call from Aug 2022 to May 2026, and management openly concedes it controls neither order timing nor outcome. Exports remain only ~10% of revenue (order book INR53–63cr), too small to offset policy dependence.

> "this is government-driven orders. So I don't have absolute control on the timing nor on the contract" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Nascent export diversification (UK repeat business, PAR exports to Europe, US inquiries) and partnerships with Indian corporates such as Bharat Forge, though still a small share of revenue*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 2 numeric fingerprints reinforce it.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is tied to government defence procurement/capex programmes rather than customer operations, and the calls repeatedly show lumpy, order-timing-driven results — a historically Q4-heavy profile (>80% pre-IPO), Q4 FY26 revenue down 13% year-on-year yet up 99% quarter-on-quarter, and EBITDA margin swinging from a low-margin strategic contract quarter to 56% in Q4 FY26. Offsetting this, defence spending is described across FY23–FY26 as a structural multi-decade indigenisation cycle with a growing order book (INR664cr → INR2,062cr) giving 2–3 years of visibility.

> "revenue was lower by minus 13% year-on-year due to timing of execution of certain programs, revenue nearly doubled sequentially with a growth of 99 percentage" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Multi-year order book (~3 years of revenue targeted), repeat/single-vendor production orders on already-developed products, and reduced Q4 concentration (37% in FY26 vs 55% in FY25)*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 11 years with swings of ±36 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from 16% to 45% (swinging ±11.6 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 17% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 4% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Stakeholder Concentration — ELEVATED
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but the only computed fingerprint does not confirm it — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** The customer base is effectively one buyer — the Indian MoD and its agencies (DRDO, HAL, ECIL, BrahMos) — with results driven by a handful of named programmes (LCA Mk1A, AMCA, BrahMos seeker, space surveillance radar), and analysts question single relationships directly (e.g. whether the BrahMos seeker contract is split with a PSU or winner-takes-all). Diversification across radars, EW, avionics and communications broadens the programme mix but not the ultimate paymaster.

> "we are two vendors, so both the vendors should be considered is what we expect. But, you know, I can't talk for the government" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Order inflows described as well diversified across radar, avionics, EW, services and strategic electronics, plus early export and global-OEM customers*

- ✅ Customers pay in about 291 days (was 389) — no sign of weakening bargaining power in collection terms.

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Much of the business is won on lowest-price government tendering (L1), and management admits taking contracts at deliberately lower margin and that system-integration/bought-out content structurally compresses gross margin — the classic route by which a cheaper-but-adequate bid erodes a premium. Protection comes from genuine niche depth: in-house-designed building blocks and IP rather than imported-and-integrated content, sustaining ~63% gross and ~40% EBITDA margins.

> "we take some contracts with lower margin consciously to ensure that we build our capability" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: In-house IP across the full stack ('we don't import anything and integrate'), certified/qualified defence products with high switching cost, and single-vendor repeat orders (~INR1,900cr pipeline) that bypass price competition*

- ⚠️ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs fell from about 74% to 64% — pricing is being competed away at the gross level.
- ✅ Operating margin (40%) remains near its historical peak (45%) — no collapse in the premium.
- ✅ Operating margin held (about 19% then 41%) — no drift down under competition.
