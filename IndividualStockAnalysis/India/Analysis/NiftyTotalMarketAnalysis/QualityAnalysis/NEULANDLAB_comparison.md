## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 28 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 4 improved, 5 regressed, 19 unchanged. 1 check carries the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them).

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |

| Return on Capital | Good (+0.67) | Good (+0.67) | +0.00 |
| Growth | Good (+0.83) | Good (+1.17) | +0.33 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Customer engagement is broadening across geographies and the company has begun working with big pharma, but this is export selling from Indian sites rather than a demonstrated geographic footprint expansion. | The long view had said: Revenue is already largely US/EU export-led from a single Hyderabad manufacturing base, and beyond generic references to customers 'across geographies' and tariff/trade-deal commentary there is no distinct geographic expansion programme.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: FY25 was an admitted trough ('year of consolidation', slight degrowth) with Q1FY26 revenue down 32% year-on-year, and the upturn was identifiable and has now delivered — but the trough is behind rather than ahead, limiting the remaining cyclical upside. | The long view had said: The FY25 trough and FY26 rebound were driven by company-specific product lifecycles and capacity phasing rather than an identifiable industry cycle bottom.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: FY26 capex cash outflow of INR397 crores went into genuinely new capacity (Unit 3 block, large-scale peptide commercial facility, new R&D campus) rather than maintenance, funded from negative net debt and accompanied by 37% revenue growth and EBITDA margin expansion to 29.4%. | The long view had said: Growth capex has scaled steeply and is clearly expansionary (Unit-3 blocks, land adjacent to Unit-1, large-scale peptide commercial facility, new Genome Valley R&D campus) — Rs 15cr/quarter in FY23 to Rs 397cr in FY26 — but management concedes returns will dip during the deployment cycle and has never disclosed ROA/ROCE on new assets.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: The record FY26 is separable from skill because management itself attributes much of it to lumpy shipment timing and favourable exchange rates, so the assessment does not rest on a halo from one blockbuster quarter — but it also means results overstate underlying execution. | The long view had said: The headline FY26 result is partly flattered by FX and shipment timing, and cash conversion has not followed profits (negative FCF, rising inventory prompting a pointed analyst question about inventory-driven profits), so results should not be read as pure skill.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Team capability is referenced repeatedly but only in generic terms — no succession plan, internal pipeline, retention or development programme is described in any of the four calls. | The long view had said: There is deliberate capability and organisational building — key account management structure, dedicated resources for large projects, in-house peptide process chemistry teams — but no explicit evidence of a systematic internal leadership pipeline, and the CFO seat changed hands during the period.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Commentary is plain and process-focused, guidance is explicitly refused, and even the record Q4 was talked down rather than promoted — though the company is actively covered by sell-side analysts and hosts a full IR programme. | The long view had said: Management consistently declines guidance and actively downplays its own best quarter rather than promoting it, and refused to over-claim on a publicly announced peptide contract.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: Once a molecule is commercialised the validated process and DMF filing create real switching costs — evidenced by growing share of business from existing customers and multi-year visibility on commercial molecules — but customer concentration means the lock-in cuts both ways, as the Paliperidone shipment delay showed. | The long view had said: Once a process is validated and filed with the regulator, replacing Neuland requires re-development and re-filing, producing very high switching costs — evidenced by the multi-year, hard-to-displace commercial relationships and continuity of customer engagement even after clients are acquired by Big Pharma.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Neuland is a credible 'approver of choice' in complex APIs but describes itself as one of several viable players in a competitive canvas, with no monopoly-like hold on any customer or aftermarket. | The long view had said: Neuland is the named drug-substance supplier inside customers' NDA filings for commercial NCEs, giving it a de facto sole-source position on those molecules, though only for a handful of products.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: The customer base is entirely corporate (innovators, big pharma, generic formulators) and the benefits offered — reliability, regulatory track record and process lock-in — are correctly matched to that customer type rather than to brand intangibles. | The long view had said: Customers are corporate (innovators, biotech, big pharma, generic formulators) and the benefit set is correctly matched — reliability/regulatory assurance plus NDA-filing switching-cost lock-in rather than brand appeal.

*Unchanged: Candor; Return on capital (headline); Disciplined stewards; Gain in market share; Asset turn; Profit margin; Barriers to entry and rationality; Security by obscurity; Cost of working capital; Intangible benefits; Share donators; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Convenience benefits; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Structural end-market growth; Assurance benefits; Technology advantage; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Research and development.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 3 weakened, 6 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is a genuine four-decade B2B reputation asset that management says is pulling in customers and larger shares of existing business, but this is track-record credibility with pharma buyers rather than consumer brand with A&P spend, distributor leverage or adjacency launches. (The long view had said: A four-decade B2B reputation for quality and regulatory reliability is repeatedly invoked as the reason innovators come to them, and the peptide push is being sold off that same name — but there is no consumer brand, no A&P spend, no pricing premium claimed.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls, margin movement is attributed exclusively to product mix, operating leverage and exchange rates — no price increase, premiumisation or elasticity discussion appears, so there is no basis to judge pricing power either way. (The long view had said: Across 16 calls management never cites a price increase; gross margin swings (46% Aug-22, 66% Aug-24, 52% Feb-26, 62% May-26) are attributed to product mix, shipment timing and FX, and prime APIs face open competition from Indian and European players.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management repeatedly claims wallet-share gains with existing customers and first-time orders from new ones, and FY26 revenue grew 37.1% to INR2,053 crores without compromising margins (EBITDA margin up to 29.4% from 22.9%), but no explicit market-share numbers or named-competitor comparisons are given in any call. (The long view had said: Management talks about winning share of wallet with existing customers and market share in key APIs, and the CMS pipeline moves steadily from Phase 1/2 to commercial across the years — but no explicit share percentages or named-competitor comparisons are ever given.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Neuland sells directly to the innovators and generic formulators who both choose and pay; growth is credited to its own business development team, RFPs and customer visits, with no prescriber, installer or trade-channel intermediary anywhere in the FY26 calls. (The long view had said: No evidence across the 16 calls of sales driven through prescribers, installers or any professional intermediary; Neuland sells directly to pharma innovators and generic formulators.)
- **Forward Integrators**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Neuland remains a pure upstream API/CDMO supplier with no owned stores, franchising, licensing or D2C; the entire capex programme in FY26 (INR397.1 crores) goes into production blocks, peptide facilities and R&D, not distribution toward the end customer. (The long view had said: No discussion anywhere in the calls of own stores, franchising, licensing or direct-to-customer channels; Neuland remains a pure-play API/CDMO supplier.)

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The last four calls show extreme swings — revenue -32.4% year-on-year in Q1FY26, +63.7% in Q2, +11.4% in Q3, +134.9% in Q4, with EBITDA margin moving 14.4% → 30.4% → 19% → 40.5% — and this volatility is arriving alongside a heavy build-out (INR397 crores capex in FY26, peptide plant, Unit 3 block, new R&D campus) funded ahead of the orders. The swings are order-lumpiness in a CDMO/GDS book rather than a commodity price cycle, and end demand is patient-driven medicine supply, which bounds the risk. (The long view had said: Demand is flow-type (APIs consumed as customers' drugs sell, not customer capex), yet reported results swing violently on project/shipment timing — Q3FY26 EBITDA margin 19% versus 40.5% in Q4FY26, and FY25 was a 'year of consolidation'/slight degrowth between two growth years; management itself says judge over 10-12 quarters. The company also kept investing through the soft FY25 (Unit-3, peptides, new R&D campus), which is the countercyclical behaviour of a strong operator rather than peak-cycle overbuild.)
- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Across all four calls, revenue is described as coming from private innovators, big pharma and generic formulators in regulated markets — no government orders, PSU tenders, subsidies or regulated pricing appear anywhere. The only state-linked item is a cost, not a revenue dependency: a INR10 crores labour-code charge in Q3FY26. (The long view had said: No revenue is priced by a regulator or subsidy, and management says pharma has been tariff-exempt and the India-EU/US deals bring 'no material change'; but policy repeatedly frames the demand narrative (Biosecure Act discussed across FY25 calls as the swing factor for China+1 inflows) and management flags US intent to onshore API manufacturing as an unresolved overhang, alongside 'changing regulations' and a INR10 crore labour-code hit in Q3FY26.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Results this year are explicitly driven by a handful of molecules and customers: Q2's record quarter came from 'our top two commercial CMS molecules', Q3 missed because one high-margin CMS product did not ship and Paliperidone was delayed by problems at a single customer's end, and CMS was over two-thirds of Q4 revenue. Management itself flags that individual shipments are now large enough that one logistical slip moves the quarter. (The long view had said: Results are visibly hostage to a handful of molecules and customers: CMS commercial went from >50% of Q3FY26 revenue to over two-thirds in Q4FY26, growth 'has been driven by one particular molecule' (Aug 2022), and a single missing high-margin CMS shipment plus one customer's operational problem on Paliperidone dragged Q3FY26 gross margin down. Individual shipments are now INR50-100 crore, so one customer's scheduling decision moves the quarter.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL); Fashion Risk (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Technological Innovation (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
