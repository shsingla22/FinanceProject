# India Glycols (INDIAGLYCO) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

India Glycols is an Indian manufacturer that turns alcohol (ethanol) made from crops — grain, rice, corn and molasses — into three main kinds of products: industrial chemicals, drinking liquor, and fuel-grade ethanol that oil companies blend into petrol. It sells bulk and specialty chemicals to industrial customers in India and abroad (including surfactant ingredients used by companies like Unilever and oilfield chemicals shipped to the Middle East), bottled Indian-made foreign liquor to consumers through state markets and the army canteen (CSD) channel, ethanol to the government's fuel-blending programme, and plant-based extracts and nutraceutical ingredients to pharma customers. Money comes mostly from selling these products at a margin over its main input costs — ethanol/grain, coal and energy — helped by making its own ethanol in-house instead of importing it, plus a steady stream of profit-share income from a chemicals joint venture with Clariant to which it supplies ethylene oxide. It is headquartered near Delhi (Noida) with plants at Kashipur in Uttarakhand and Gorakhpur, and reported FY26 gross revenue of about ₹9,827 crore, net revenue of ₹4,211 crore, EBITDA of ₹654 crore (15.5% margin) and profit of ₹293 crore.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Bio-based Specialties and Performance Chemicals (BSPC / Chemicals)** — Makes ethanol-derived industrial and specialty chemicals — including green/bio-based surfactant ingredients, oilfield chemicals, specialty amines, plasticizers and green solvents — and at ₹1,203 crore of FY26 net revenue with ₹141 crore EBIT it is one of the three big segments; sales dipped about 10% in FY26 as management deliberately dropped low-margin business, but margins rose to 11.7%.
- **Potable Spirits (Liquor / IMFL)** — Makes and sells Indian-made foreign liquor, including its own brands (Bunty Bubbly is described as the largest selling brand in the country) plus tie-ups with Bacardi and Amrut; at ₹1,331 crore FY26 revenue, 14.4% growth, ₹285 crore EBIT and roughly 23.7 million cases sold in nine months, it is the most profitable of the main segments and a key growth driver.
- **Bio-Fuels** — Produces grain-based ethanol sold into India's petrol-blending programme; carved out as its own reported segment only from mid-2024 because it had grown too big to sit inside Chemicals, it was the fastest grower in FY26 at ₹1,470 crore revenue (up 40.9%) with EBIT nearly doubling to ₹115 crore as national blending reached 20%.
- **Ennature Biopharma** — Sells plant-derived pharma and nutraceutical ingredients — management highlights leadership in thiocolchicoside plus a growing nicotine business and newly launched branded nutraceuticals — and is the smallest of the four segments, with FY26 revenue roughly flat and margins under pressure.
- **Clariant joint venture (ethylene oxide derivatives, the former EOD business)** — The ethylene oxide derivatives business was transferred into a joint venture with Clariant, so instead of showing up as sales it appears mainly as 'other income'; India Glycols supplies EO to the JV, and management says this income is large enough that including it lifts the reported quarterly EBITDA margin from about 17.1% to roughly 20.8%.
- **New value-added / performance chemicals pipeline** — A newer effort built from scratch through R&D, a pilot plant and a dedicated new plant, targeting around 20 products across six segments (specialty amines, oilfield products, plasticizers, green solvents and more); still a small part of Chemicals revenue but management calls the pipeline strong and expects it to drive future growth.

### The story in depth

India Glycols comes out of the quality work as a well-run company in a hard business. The three areas that score full marks are management, competitive advantage and customer benefit: a strategy laid out in 2022 — grain-ethanol self-sufficiency, a '3x3' multi-feedstock model and a specialty chemicals arm built up from research — was carried through a severe cost cycle and delivered, and management talks about its own problems in unusual detail (indexed coal costs, freight up five to six times per kilo, 'prolonged commissioning', chemicals sales down 10.4%). The economics are respectable rather than spectacular: after direct inputs the company keeps ₹45 of every ₹100 of sales, ₹15 of every ₹100 is operating profit, margins move only about ±2.9 points a year, and ₹100 of capital in the business threw off ₹17 of operating cash. Recent growth is the good kind — Potable Spirits revenue up 17% on just 5% more cases, and chemicals profit up 68% even as sales fell because low-margin lines were dropped. The one clear weakness inside the quality work is capital allocation (scoring 0.2 of 1): instead of buybacks or a stated payout policy, the company sold ₹467 crores of fresh shares to promoters and friends and relatives and used the money to pay down debt.

On the multibagger screen only one of eleven patterns is a strong fit — Corporate Culture — and it is a fit for exactly the reason the quality work liked management: things promised on early calls (the Kashipur and Gorakhpur grain plants, a specialties plant built 'from almost scratch') showed up as delivered results on later ones. Pricing power is rated only a likely fit because it lives in one segment: spirits, where EBIT margins reached 21–22% and premiumisation has held, while management itself describes soft pricing in chemicals and biofuels. Everything else — brands, innovation, market share, cost leadership, barriers to entry — lands at partial, and the reasons overlap with the risk review rather than sitting apart from it. The same regional liquor franchise that earns the brand and pricing marks (Bunty & Bubbly the largest-selling brand in the country, Amazing Vodka from 18% to 21% share in Uttarakhand) is also what carries Fashion Risk, because a meaningful slice of that growth comes from novelty launches and flavour editions whose appeal rests on taste of the moment. The same feedstock flexibility that management uses as a cost programme is judged 'feedstock arbitrage rather than a hard-to-copy low-cost culture' — a strength in a good year, not a moat. And barriers to entry score partial for the same fact that drives one of the risks: management concedes India now has surplus ethanol capacity.

The risk review is where the score is lost — two high risks and two elevated ones. Cyclicality is high because performance is framed through commodity price decks and margins swung from 8.4% to 17.1% on feedstock spreads. Government dependency is high for the segment doing the most work: biofuels, at ₹1,470 crore and up 40.9% in FY26, sells into oil-company allocations at a price the regulator sets, and management says plainly that profitability is whatever the regulator chooses to leave. So the fastest-growing part of the business is the least controlled part, and the part with genuine pricing power (spirits) is the part most exposed to taste. Concentration is elevated for a related reason — the oil-company tender channel is the only route for biofuels, and the Clariant joint venture supplies the largest part of other income, worth roughly 370 basis points of Q4FY26 margin. The four watch-level risks all point the same way: bio-based positioning did not protect the company when ethanol stayed expensive against crude, and pricing effectively tracks a cheaper petrochemical substitute. The offsets are real — ethanol capacity can be redirected into potable spirits or chemicals feedstock, spirits is repeat consumption, and the customer base is being deliberately broadened — which is why resilience is judged sound even with the risks high. The combined rating of 46 out of 100, or Mixed, is an honest reflection: 67 for business quality, 33 for multibagger fit and 24 for risk safety. Good operators, a narrow moat, and an earnings base that other people partly control.

*"A culture that compounds"*

**What the calls show:** The clearest pattern: promises made in early calls were delivered in later ones — the Kashipur/Gorakhpur grain plants under trial in Aug 2022 became the biofuels segment driving FY26 growth, and the new specialties plant promised 'from almost scratch' was commissioned and commercialised — with management openly conceding the bad years, disclosing a five-year EBITDA and ROCE ladder, and cutting debt by INR582 crores under a CEO/CFO stable since FY22.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Government Dependency, Stakeholder Concentration, Fashion Risk. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.6× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 100% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 6% to 12% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 2 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across all 16 calls management frames performance through commodity price decks — coal up 2x, ethanol, freight up 5-6x in FY23, crude/MEG, grain, DDGS prices — and margins swung from 8.4% EBITDA in Q1FY23 to 17.1% in Q4FY26 largely on feedstock spreads plus a war-driven crude move; the chemicals segment also sits in a globally over-supplied petrochemical chain. Mitigants are real but partial: the potable spirits and biofuels segments are operations/consumption-linked flow demand, and the 3x3 feedstock strategy plus countercyclical grain-distillery capex during the 2022 cost shock has lifted EBITDA margin steadily from 9.6% (FY22) to 15.5% (FY26).

> "in some of the areas, we've been dealing with structural overcapacity in the world" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow-product potable spirits (21% EBIT margin, repeat consumption) and multi-feedstock '3x3' ethanol optionality; countercyclical investment in grain-based distilleries during the 2022 cost spike*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 4 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 5% and 15% (±2.9 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 20% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 6% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Biofuels — the fastest-growing segment (INR1,470 crore, +40.9% in FY26) — sells into OMC allocations at a government-fixed procurement price under a government blending mandate, and management openly says profitability is whatever the regulator chooses to leave; potable spirits is equally policy-bound through state excise regimes (Delhi excise volatility, UP/Uttarakhand policy, mandated 100ml SKU, CSD/paramilitary channels). Growth beyond 20% blending is explicitly a policy decision, not a market one.

> "because the government is significantly involved in regulating it, regulating it and controlling it in some way, they will keep it profitable, but it is not a business where suddenly if the demand is high and the supply is low, you can fetch a huge amount of price" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Ethanol capacity is fungible into potable spirits and chemicals feedstock, so policy-driven volume can be redirected; chemicals and Ennature Biopharma are non-policy revenue*

### Stakeholder Concentration — ELEVATED
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but the only computed fingerprint does not confirm it — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Repeatedly named single relationships drive results: OMC tender allocations are the sole channel for biofuels, the Clariant JV supplies 'the most significant part of our other income' (worth ~370bps of Q4FY26 margin), the Amrut licence and Bacardi packaging tie-up underpin premium spirits growth, and a single oilfield customer going quiet cut exports. Spirits volume is also geographically concentrated in UP and Uttarakhand.

> "for big customers like New Park, where we send our oilfield chemicals largely to the Middle East, that business is largely on hold" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate broadening — new chemicals customers (BASF, Dow, L'Oreal), Ennature 'acquiring new customers', spirits expansion into Delhi, Kerala, CSD and paramilitary channels and 5 new UP districts*

- ✅ Customers pay in about 36 days (was 59) — no sign of weakening bargaining power in collection terms.

### Fashion Risk — ELEVATED
*"Cool today, uncool tomorrow"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** A meaningful part of spirits growth is credited to trending launches and novelty SKUs — Amazing Vodka, Zumba Lemoni, Bunty Vodka in Jeera/Cranberry/Jamun flavours, Dhurandhar Whisky, city- and state-specific commemorative editions — demand that rests on taste and can move without warning. Risk is bounded by breadth rather than one big bet, and by a large repeat-purchase regular/country-liquor base.

> "We've introduced seven new brands in the market to the new stations." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Many small brands across price points rather than one hero brand, anchored by functional repeat-purchase IMIL leadership in UP/Uttarakhand and the millionaire-case Bunty Bubbly / Soulmate franchises*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 4 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ✅ Growth is holding up: -2% a year earlier, 13% recently — no structural stall.

**What the calls show:** The ethanol-blending demand base is exposed to vehicle-technology shifts — management itself flags the global move to EVs over blending, and the next leg of demand depends on flexi-fuel vehicles — while the chemicals franchise is a bio-route challenger to entrenched petrochemical EO/MEG capacity rather than the incumbent. ### Shifting Consumer Preferences — WATCH
*"The customer is changing"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** The spirits demand base is migrating — consumers moving from blended whisky to malts, and toward premium/luxury — and the industrial side depends on a sustainability preference for bio-based over petro inputs; management is pivoting rather than defending, with the Amrut single-malt tie-up, city/state special editions and a full price-ladder portfolio. ### Good Enough Goods — WATCH
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** The bio-based premium is not reliably paid: management admits the green positioning did not protect competitiveness when ethanol stayed dear versus crude, and pricing effectively tracks Reliance's petrochemical EO — a cheaper good-enough substitute for most volume. Protection comes from genuine niche positions rather than brand pull, and management has responded by exiting low-margin commodity lines toward higher value-added chemicals.

*Silver lining: Niche protection — 'the niche customers have continued to buy glycols from us and have continued to grow' — plus deliberate discontinuation of low-margin businesses and a shift into customised specialty/performance chemicals developed with partners*

- ✅ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs held near 45% (was 38%) — no gross-level price erosion.
- ✅ Operating margin (15%) remains near its historical peak (15%) — no collapse in the premium.
- ✅ Operating margin held (about 7% then 14%) — no drift down under competition.

## What to watch

- Whether Potable Spirits holds its 21–22% margin and keeps gaining share (Amazing Vodka's 18%-to-21% move) rather than fading with the novelty launches flagged under Fashion Risk — this is the only segment carrying the Pricing Power pattern.
- The government-fixed ethanol procurement price and blending allocations behind the ₹1,470 crore biofuels segment — the Government Dependency risk means a change here hits the fastest-growing business directly.
- How much of the surplus national ethanol capacity management now admits to gets absorbed in-house versus dumped into the market — this drives both the New Entrants risk and the partial score on Cost to Replicate.
- Capital allocation after the ₹467 crore promoter share issue: whether a stated distribution policy or buyback appears, since this check scores -1 and the area scores 0.2, the lowest of the seven quality areas.
- The Clariant joint venture income (about 370 basis points of Q4FY26 margin) and progress on broadening beyond it via BASF, Dow, L'Oreal and the new spirits geographies — the Stakeholder Concentration risk turns on whether that broadening actually shows up in results.
