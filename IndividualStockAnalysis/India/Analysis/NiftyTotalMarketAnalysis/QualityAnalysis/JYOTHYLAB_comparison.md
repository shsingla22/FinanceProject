### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: A steady cadence of named launches with disclosed traction — Dr. Wool, Jovia, Ujala Young & Fresh, Maxo Aerosol and rackets, bio-enzyme Exo, and liquid detergents nearly doubling in FY26 — but no R&D spend is disclosed and gross margins fell 320bps, so the innovation is not funding pricing power. (The long view had said: A regular cadence of named launches with revenue impact runs through the calls — Ujala/Henko liquids (2022), Morelight liquid 5L (2024), Dr. Wool, Jovia, Maxo Aerosol and bio-enzyme Exo Liquid (FY26), with liquid detergents nearly doubling in FY26 — but there is no R&D spend disclosure, no share-of-industry-R&D claim, and gross margins are falling rather than funding the innovation.)

**That weakened:**

- **Toll Roads**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The opposite of toll-road economics: the products are visible, substitutable household purchases where rivals cut MRPs and add grammage and Jyothy must follow, and management repeatedly cites 'fixed price points' in low unit packs as a constraint. (The long view had said: the calls are silent and the numbers are mixed (1 of 2 checks supporting).)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Prices are falling but returns are falling with them — gross margin down 320bps and EBITDA down 230bps for FY26 — so this is reactive discounting under competitive pressure, not a hard-to-copy cost culture converting low price into high turns and strong returns. (The long view had said: Across FY25-FY26 the company deliberately plays the value end — Morelight liquid launched at a disruptive INR70/litre, 5-litre bulk packs, grammage increases and MRP cuts to defend volume — and it does run high asset turns with a debt-free balance sheet and 15-day working capital. But there is no evidence of an un-copyable cost culture: the price cuts are reactive to competition and have destroyed margin (gross margin down 320bps in FY26), which is the opposite of passing savings on while holding returns.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (QUANT SIGNAL); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Cost to Replicate (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 5 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Management discusses quick commerce defensively as a structurally different, price-led format that substitutes rather than adds demand, and notes urban demand 'remains value-driven and highly competitive, particularly in online channels'. Exposure is bounded because Jyothy is growing strongly in those same channels rather than being locked out of them. (The long view had said: Repeatedly and increasingly through FY25-FY26, management describes structurally different entrants — local/unorganised liquid detergent players and multiple MRP-cutting competitors in dishwash — and responds with price cuts, extra grammage and promotions, producing a persistent 2-3% negative price/volume gap and a Dishwash value decline on 6% volume growth.)

**That worsened:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The clearest and most repeated risk in the window: Dishwash grew 5-7% in volume every quarter while value declined (FY26 value -1.3%), because competitors cut MRPs and pushed grammage — an analyst noted segment margins fell from 18% to ~10%. The same realisation erosion spread to liquid detergents, and management admits it cannot pass on crude-driven cost inflation in low unit packs where price points are fixed, with gross margin down 400bps in Q4 FY26. (The long view had said: The Nobel Biocare pattern is visible: cheaper local and organised challengers force Jyothy to cut MRPs and add grammage rather than defend a premium, so Dishwash and Liquid Detergent post volume growth with value decline and gross margin fell from ~50% to 45.2% by Q4FY26; management concedes pricing is only 'at parity to competition' and exited Bangladesh explicitly because of cheap local product.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Two genuine demand-base migrations are visible: coils structurally declining within household insecticides (coil share cut from ~50% to one-third of HI revenue) and detergent powder giving way to liquids, plus a channel migration to online. Each is being met with a portfolio pivot rather than absorbed as a loss, but the legacy formats are eroding on their own. (The long view had said: The shifts visible in the calls — powder to liquid, GT to quick commerce/e-commerce, and natural/neem and bio-enzyme formulations — are ones the company has pivoted into successfully rather than a demand base eroding; the one genuine decline (coils in HI) is a deliberate mix exit, offset by liquid vaporisers.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Government Dependency (LOW); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
