# Amber Enterp. (AMBER) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Amber Enterprises is an Indian business-to-business manufacturer that builds products and components for other companies' brands rather than selling under its own name — it is an OEM/ODM, meaning it both makes to customers' designs and designs products itself for them to badge. Its biggest business is making finished room air conditioners and AC components for appliance brands; alongside that it makes electronics (printed circuit board assemblies and bare printed circuit boards) for appliance, telecom, smart-meter, wearable, automotive and defence customers, and air-conditioning and other sub-systems for railway coaches and metro trains. It earns money by manufacturing on order and selling the units and components to those customers, with volumes tied to India's AC season, to electronics demand, and to government railway/metro spending. Operations are in India — plants include Rajpura (Punjab), Sri City in the south, Hosur, Noida, Pune and Faridabad — with the customer base mainly domestic brands and multinationals, plus some exports; FY26 consolidated revenue was about INR 12,186 crores with operating EBITDA of INR 970 crores and adjusted profit of INR 338 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Consumer Durable division** — Makes complete room air conditioners plus AC components, non-AC components (telecom, smart meter, IT server, refrigerator, microwave, washing machine, water purifier and auto parts) and, through the Resojet JV, fully automatic washing machines — by far the largest division at INR 8,383 crores of FY26 revenue (about two-thirds of the group), growing 14% in a flat AC year.
- **Electronics / EMS division (IL JIN, Ever, Ascent Circuits, Shogini, Power-One, Unitronics)** — Contract electronics manufacturing — printed circuit board assemblies for appliances, consumer electronics, hearables/wearables, telecom, smart meters, autos and defence, plus bare PCBs (single-layer up to HDI) — the fastest-growing part at INR 3,268 crores in FY26, up 49%, with management guiding to roughly 40% more growth in FY27.
- **Railway Sub-system and Defense division (Sidwal, Yujin India JV)** — Supplies air-conditioning and other sub-systems for trains and metros — including pantry units, doors, gangways, couplers, gears and pantographs — a smaller but higher-margin division that grew 19% in FY26 and sits on an order book of over INR 2,600 crores, with 30-35% growth guided for FY27 and FY28.
- **Mobile phone manufacturing (Oppo collaboration)** — A brand-new venture signed in June 2026 to assemble phones for Oppo, OnePlus and Realme at a subleased Oppo facility — asset-light and very low margin (industry EBITDA of roughly 1.5-2%) but high volume, starting around 8 million units in year one and targeting 13-15 million in year two, with trial production only from Q4 FY27.

### The story in depth

Amber is a behind-the-scenes manufacturer: it builds air conditioners, components, circuit boards, railway and metro air-conditioning units, and now assembles mobile phones, all under someone else's brand name. Its clearest strength is that it keeps taking work away from rivals. Its share of the value of air conditioners made for brand owners rose from 21.2% in FY18 to 26.6% in FY22, its room-AC business grew 50% against an industry at 35% in FY25, and it grew 14% in FY26 while the industry was flat — which is why the 'Market Share Gainers' pattern is the one clean fit in the whole screen, and why 'Gain in market share' scores at the top of the quality checks. The end markets behind it are genuinely expanding: air conditioning is spreading into smaller towns and rural India, and the bare circuit-board market is set to go from about ₹37,000cr to ₹80,000cr with 85% of it still imported today. Backing this is 25 years of steadily bringing parts in-house — roughly 70% of an air conditioner's bill of materials is now made internally, across 23 locations, alongside its own inverter circuit-board designs. The combined verdict, though, is Mixed: 41 out of 100, built from 52 on business quality, only 18 on multibagger fit, and 48 on risk safety.

The trouble is that the very thing that wins the share is the thing that caps the reward. Amber competes by being cheap and high-volume, not by being wanted: after paying for direct inputs it keeps only ₹18 of every ₹100 of sales, consolidated operating margins sit at 7-8%, and the new Oppo phone-assembly work was deliberately taken on at 1.5-2% margins. That single trait shows up three times over. It makes 'Low Price Plus' a partial fit — cheapness plus deep in-house manufacturing is a real strategy — while simultaneously making 'Pricing Power' and 'Brand Strength' outright non-fits, and it drives the 'Good Enough Goods' risk to elevated, because the customers who choose the brand have no reason to pay Amber a premium. Prices are simply passed through with a one-to-two-quarter lag; Chinese suppliers have already dropped prices in circuit boards; railway work is fixed-price tender. Growth is therefore almost entirely volume, not price or better mix, which is why those checks score at the bottom. And the money keeps going out: spending runs at ₹350-375cr a year with more than ₹4,500cr of approvals for advanced circuit boards, yet the return so far is ₹3 of operating cash for every ₹100 of capital in the business, and the 19-21% return on capital promised back in FY23 has not arrived. Fast-growing, hard-working, low-return — that is the shape of it.

The risks mostly extend the same story rather than sitting apart from it. Government support is a first-order part of the economics, not a bonus: production-linked incentive receipts of ₹49.5cr in FY25 rising to an expected ₹78cr in FY26, ₹80cr of capital subsidies, ₹4,500cr of electronics-component approvals, and an anti-dumping duty that directly enabled the circuit-board push — which is why government dependency is the one high risk, and it is inseparable from the same expansion that produces the market-share wins. Cyclicality is elevated for a related reason: results still swing with the weather and with commodity prices, with volumes down 30% in the first half of FY26, though the record also places the company near a low point with a recovery visible for FY27 at roughly 20% in the first quarter and 12-13% for the year, against a guided 50-100bps margin squeeze at the start of that year. Diversification into non-seasonal electronics, railways and phones is the deliberate answer, but it is being bought rather than built — five-plus acquisitions and joint ventures in FY26 alone, one joint venture written off, and a brand-new phone vertical — which is exactly why the stewardship check scores negative even though borrowing has stayed sensible. Set against that, the leadership team has been unchanged since 2022 and tells bad news early, from railway delays to the coming margin hit, and the balance sheet is judged sound. Concentration on a handful of brand-owner customers, new entrants copying the design-and-build model, and repeated technology re-basing in both air conditioning and circuit boards are all live but second-order watch items rather than present dangers.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Policy is a first-order driver of Amber's economics throughout the record: PLI receipts (INR49.5cr FY25, INR78cr expected FY26), INR80cr of central/state capex subsidies, INR4,500cr of ECMS investment approvals, anti-dumping duty on PCBs that directly 'enabled us to onboard four new customers', compressor QCOs and import quotas, Press Note 3 structuring, and a railway division whose revenue went flat because 'the railway ministry shifted focus to non-AC coaches' — on fixed-price tender contracts with no pass-through.

*Silver lining: Three-division structure means no single policy line dominates; metro contracts carry a pass-on mechanism and defence/telecom/data-centre contracts have escalation clauses.*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.
