# Amber Enterp. (AMBER) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Amber Enterprises India Limited is an Indian contract manufacturer that builds finished products and components for other companies' brands rather than selling under its own name — management calls itself a "one stop solution provider" and an ODM/OEM player, and its customers are appliance brands, electronics and telecom companies, automotive and defence firms, and railway/metro rolling-stock makers. It started as a maker of room air conditioners and AC components for India's AC brands, and has since diversified into electronics manufacturing services (assembled circuit boards and bare printed circuit boards), railway and metro sub-systems, and most recently mobile phone assembly for Oppo/OnePlus/Realme. It earns money by manufacturing to customer order, passing raw-material cost changes through to customers (with a roughly one-quarter lag), and it grows both by winning more of each customer's spend and by buying or partnering into new product areas (IL JIN and Ever, Ascent Circuits, Sidwal, Resojet, Yujin, Power-One, Unitronics, Shogini). Operations are almost entirely in India — plants in Punjab, Haryana (Faridabad), Noida, Pune, Hosur and Sri City in the south — with consolidated revenue of INR12,186 crores in FY26, up 22%, and operating EBITDA of INR970 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Consumer Durable division** — Makes complete room air conditioners for brands plus AC components, non-AC components (telecom, smart meter, IT server, refrigerator, microwave, washing machine, water purifier and auto parts) and, through the Resojet JV, fully automatic washing machines; it is by far the largest part at INR8,383 crores of FY26 revenue (about two-thirds of the group), growing 14% in FY26 with roughly 6-8% operating margins.
- **Electronics / EMS division (IL JIN, Ever, Ascent Circuits, Shogini, Ascent-K, Power-One, Unitronics)** — Assembles printed circuit boards (PCBA) for appliances, consumer electronics, hearables/wearables, telecom, smart meters, automotive and defence, and now also manufactures the bare PCBs themselves; it is the fastest-growing part at INR3,268 crores in FY26 (up 49%, with EBITDA up 89%) and management guides to about 40% further growth in FY27.
- **Railway Sub-system and Defense division (Sidwal, Yujin JV)** — Supplies equipment that goes inside trains and metro coaches — air conditioning/HVAC, pantry units, doors and gangways, plus couplers, gears and pantographs — to railway and metro projects and defence customers; smallest of the three divisions, it grew 19% in FY26 and sits on an order book of over INR2,600 crores with 30-35% growth guided for FY27 and FY28.
- **Mobile phone manufacturing (Oppo collaboration)** — A brand-new, asset-light arrangement signed June 2026 to assemble phones for Oppo, OnePlus and Realme in a facility subleased from Oppo India, starting with roughly 8 million units in year one and 13-15 million in year two, at thin industry-standard margins of about 1.5-2% EBITDA; trial production is only due in Q4 FY27, so it contributes nothing yet.

### The story in depth

Amber builds air conditioners, circuit boards, motors and railway cooling units that carry other companies' brand names, and on the record it is winning share in every pool it plays in: its value share of the OEM air-conditioner market rose from 21.2% in FY18 to 26.6% in FY22, it now makes roughly 22-23% of India's inverter circuit boards, and its consumer durables division grew 14% in FY26 against an industry that was flat. Sales have compounded at about 23% a year. The markets underneath are genuinely widening — air conditioning is spreading into Tier 3 and Tier 4 cities and villages, and the bare circuit board market is expected to move from INR37,000cr to INR80,000cr by 2030 with 85% of it still imported today. The quality framework gives its top mark to both market share gain and structural end-market growth, the pattern screen rates Market Share Gainers a likely fit, and Corporate Culture is the strongest single finding anywhere in the review: the same three leaders appear on all 17 earnings calls from FY21 onward, and bad news has been volunteered rather than extracted — railway and Vande Bharat delays in August 2024, and a INR112cr write-down on the Shivalik joint venture in FY26.

The trouble is that the same engine driving those wins is what holds the returns down, and the three analyses agree on this from different directions. Amber competes by being the cheap, high-volume, deeply backward-integrated supplier — roughly 70% of the bill of materials made in-house after 25 years of stacking one component layer on another. That is a real barrier a copycat must rebuild plant by plant, and it is why Cost to Replicate and Low Price Plus both register as partial fits. But it is also why, after paying for direct inputs, only ₹18 of every ₹100 of sales is left, and why this year the business generated just ₹3 of operating cash for every ₹100 of capital employed — a 3% cash return, the framework's worst score. Building that integration takes continuous heavy spending (Sri City and Sidwal greenfields, over INR4,500cr of approved circuit-board investment, INR350-375cr of guided annual capex), and the promised 19-21% return on capital has been pushed back repeatedly. Management's defence is capital efficiency rather than margin — the June 2026 Oppo phone-assembly deal is targeted at just 1.5-2% operating margin yet a claimed 'more than 30%, 35%' return on capital standalone, on 4-10 days of working capital — but that claim has not yet shown up in the headline number, and the same deal drags the overall product mix down-market. Consistent with all of this, four patterns that depend on charging more — Pricing Power, Brand Strength, Toll Roads and Friendly Middleman — are all flat 'no fit'. Amber passes commodity costs through with a one-to-two quarter lag; it does not set prices.

The risk review then shows those same traits as exposures. Being the low-cost invisible supplier makes Amber vulnerable exactly where it has been attacked: Chinese players cut inverter circuit-board prices hard enough to force a change of strategy, and one customer used volume data Amber had disclosed to reopen a price negotiation — hence 'Good Enough Goods' at elevated risk and the framework's bottom score on intangible benefits. The policy support that helped it win — PLI receipts of INR49.5cr in FY25 with INR78cr expected in FY26, ECMS approvals behind the circuit-board program, and an anti-dumping duty that management said directly 'enabled us to onboard four new customers' — is the single highest-rated risk, because the same rules can move the other way. The air-conditioner cycle cuts both ways too: volumes fell about 30% in the first two quarters of FY26 on bad weather before an expected industry rebound of 20% in Q1 FY27, and the diversification into circuit boards, railways and phone assembly that is meant to smooth this out concentrates the new mobile division on a single Oppo/BBK relationship in a subleased facility. Growth is also strategically bought as much as built — a near-continuous run of deals and joint ventures funded with debt, which is why the stewardship check is marked down even though net debt has come down from INR965cr to INR511cr. Netting it out, the combined rating is 45 out of 100 — 'Mixed', two stars — from a business-quality score of 52, a much weaker multibagger fit of 33, and risk safety of 48, with overall resilience judged sound. This is a credible, honestly run, fast-growing manufacturer that has not yet shown it can turn its scale into a return worth the capital it consumes.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** Stable founder-led leadership (Jasbir Singh, Daljit Singh, Sudhir Goyal present across all 17 calls, FY21 to date), bad news disclosed promptly and unprompted (railway/Vande Bharat delays in Aug 2024, the INR112cr Shivalik JV impairment in FY26, and a pre-announced 50-100 bps margin hit), promises delivered over years (Sri City, Resojet washing machines, Sidwal greenfield commissioning, EMS EBITDA 3% in 2018 to 7.7% by Q1FY25), and a deleveraging balance sheet (net debt INR965cr to INR511cr). The one blemish is the ROCE promise of 17-20% made in Aug 2022 that was still being re-guided in FY25.

> "it's our duty to inform everybody that next 1 or 2 quarters should be -- you should see this hit coming in" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Market Share Gainers — LIKELY FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern, but the numbers push back (0 of 1 check supporting) — held one step below a full confirmation.

**What the calls show:** Explicit, sustained and quantified share gain: OEM value share 21.2% (FY18) to 26.6% (FY22); ~22-23% of India's inverter PCB boards by FY25; RAC business +50% vs a ~35% industry in Q1FY25 and Consumer Durable +14% against a flat FY26 industry; and with Ascent/Shogini/Ascent-K, 'India's largest and most comprehensive PCB manufacturer'. > "Our market share in value terms at OEM manufacturing level has increased from 21.2% in FY18... to 26.6% in FY22." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Sales compounding at 23% but margins fell (9% → 7%) — growth may be bought with profitability, this pattern's red flag.

> "Our AMC business has also grown significantly in this quarter." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Operating cash was positive in 11 of 12 years — the reliable cash engine recurring-revenue businesses show.

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 1 check supporting).

**What the calls show:** Management explicitly frames the model as low-margin/high-volume/high-turn with capital efficiency — the Oppo deal (Jun 2026) targets 1.5-2% EBITDA yet 'more than 30%, 35%' standalone ROCE, alongside 4-10 day working capital — and scale plus 70% BOM backward integration is the cost lever. But the calls show little itemised cost-saving culture, and consolidated ROCE guidance (17-20% promised in Aug 2022) was still only ~15% by FY25.

> "This is a low-margin business, but very high-volume business... the ROCEs will be more than 30%, 35%." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹18 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 0.9× a year — not the high-turnover, low-margin machine this pattern needs.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, Cyclicality, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Fortunes are repeatedly tied to policy across all five years: PLI receipts (INR49.5cr for FY25, INR78cr expected FY26), anti-dumping duty on PCBs that directly 'enabled us to onboard four new customers', ECMS approvals, compressor QCO and import quotas, and a Railway division made flattish in FY25 purely by government priority shifts (Vande Bharat coach reconfiguration, shift to non-AC coaches) with Indian Railways contracts being fixed-price tenders that cannot pass on cost.

> "The recent announcement of government imposing antidumping duty on PCBs up to 6 layers has given the much needed boost to local PCB industry." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Some diversification away from policy-linked revenue — private OEM consumer durables and the Oppo mobile collaboration are commercial contracts, and metro contracts carry pass-through unlike Indian Railways tenders.*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** RAC revenue swings violently with weather and season — Q1 FY23 RAC grew 138% and industry +35% in FY25, then FY26 saw Q1/Q2 volumes 'down by 30%' and a flattish year; margins ride commodity pass-through with a 1-2 quarter lag (copper clad laminate and gold both +60% in a year, guided 50-100bps consol margin hit), and the Railway division is pure customer-capex exposure to metro/Vande Bharat programme timing.

> "the quarter 1 and quarter 2 were very, very flattish or they were like down by 30%" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate diversification into non-seasonal Electronics EMS/PCB, railways-defence and now mobile assembly; management explicitly targets a ~50-50 AC vs non-AC split, and capex/capacity expansion (Sri City, Sidwal greenfield) continued through the weak FY26 season.*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 3 of the last 11 years with swings of ±28 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
