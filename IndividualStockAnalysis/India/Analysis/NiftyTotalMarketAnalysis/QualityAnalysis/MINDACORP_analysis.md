# Minda Corporation Ltd. (MINDACORP) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: AUTOMOBILE*

Minda Corporation (brand name Spark Minda) is an Indian auto-parts maker that manufactures and sells components and systems that go into vehicles — things like locks, keys and other vehicle-access hardware, wiring harnesses and electrical distribution systems, instrument clusters and driver-information displays, plastic and light-weighting parts, die-cast parts, and EV-specific electricals. Its customers are vehicle manufacturers (OEMs) — leading two-wheeler, three-wheeler, commercial-vehicle, tractor and passenger-vehicle makers in India plus newer EV makers and some overseas OEMs — with an additional slice of sales going into the replacement/aftermarket channel. It makes money by winning long-run supply contracts ('lifetime order book', e.g. INR 10,000 crores of new wins in FY26) and then supplying parts on every vehicle built, so revenue tracks auto-industry volumes plus 'content per vehicle' — how much of its product goes into each vehicle. It is mostly an India business (domestic sales were about 85% of revenue in FY23, exports/overseas ~15%), operating 42 plants with about 23,000 people as of FY26; consolidated revenue was INR 6,185 crores in FY26 with an 11.9% EBITDA margin, and much of its technology comes via joint ventures and licence agreements with foreign partners.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Mechatronics (vehicle access / security systems), Aftermarket and other businesses** — Makes locks, keys, smart keys and other vehicle-access and security hardware, plus die-casting and interior/plastic light-weighting parts, and also sells spares through the replacement market; reported together with aftermarket, this vertical was INR 710 crores of the INR 1,560 crores quarterly revenue in Q3 FY26 — roughly 45% of sales, and historically the larger of the two verticals (58% in Q1 FY23).
- **Information & Connected Systems** — Makes wiring harnesses and electrical distribution systems plus instrument clusters and driver-information/TFT display units, including high-voltage harnesses and power distribution parts for EVs; it grew to INR 850 crores in Q3 FY26 (up 32% year-on-year) and is now the slightly bigger of the two verticals at about 55% of sales.
- **EV products** — A named product domain covering EV-specific parts such as high-voltage wiring harness, power distribution units, integrated power modules and junction boxes; management said EV made up over 20% of new orders won as early as Q1 FY23 and calls EV/premium products a main driver of recent growth.
- **Flash Electronics (associate company)** — A separately reported associate that supplies EV power electronics, traction motors, motor controllers and vehicle control units; it did INR 1,803 crores of revenue in FY26 at a 17.2% EBITDA margin — noticeably higher-margin than the parent — and is described as key to the EV growth roadmap.
- **Sunroof and closure systems (HCMF joint venture)** — A 50-50 JV signed with HCMF of Taiwan in FY25 to build automotive sunroofs and closure systems, with a plant in Pune targeted for commissioning in Q1 FY26; still an early-stage, small addition being pitched to customers.
- **Switches (Toyodenso joint venture)** — A JV with Toyodenso of Japan for the switch business across vehicle segments; it had already secured significant orders from leading Japanese OEMs by FY26, with production expected to start in Q4 FY27 — so revenue is still ahead of it.
- **EV powertrain (Turntide Technologies joint venture)** — A newer JV with UK-based Turntide to locally manufacture cost-competitive electric powertrain solutions, combining Turntide's technology with Minda's manufacturing and localisation; announced in FY26 and not yet a revenue contributor in the calls.
- **Minda VAST (associate being consolidated)** — An associate company that Minda Corporation said it will consolidate into its own accounts from FY27 after a change in the shareholder agreement, intended to strengthen its four-wheeler system-solutions and platform-based offerings.
- **Spark Minda Technical Centre (R&D)** — The in-house R&D arm that develops products across the divisions and generates the patent portfolio — 220+ patents filed in FY23, 280 by FY25 and 330 filed by FY26 (147 granted), supported by around 950 engineers.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Minda Corporation comes out of these three reviews as a well-run, steadily improving auto-parts maker rather than an exceptional franchise, and the combined rating of 62 out of 100 — a "Decent" three-star grade built from 62 on business quality, 48 on multibagger fit and 80 on risk safety — reflects exactly that split. The strongest evidence sits with the people and the money: management scored 0.83 and capital allocation 0.8 in the quality framework, and the multibagger screen's only outright STRONG FIT is Corporate Culture, resting on the same facts — a promoter family in charge throughout the period, INR 104 crore put in by the promoter through warrants and used to repay debt, an employee share plan in 2025, and a track record of doing what was promised on the calls. The operating numbers back this up: about INR 12 of operating profit on every INR 100 of sales with margins moving only about a point a year, INR 16 of operating cash generated per INR 100 of capital employed, only about 18 days of cash tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and roughly 11% average annual sales growth. Growth spending looks genuine rather than defensive — a stated INR 1,500-2,000 crore capex programme running to FY30 for die-casting, sunroof and switch plants, with return on capital guided up from around 22% to 25%.

The clearest link across all three reviews is market share. The quality framework gives its highest single mark (a 2) to share gains, and the screen rates Market Share Gainers a LIKELY FIT, both citing the same run of numbers: revenue up 25% against industry growth of 17% in the third quarter of FY26, up 29% against 20% in the fourth, and 22.3% for FY26 as a whole. This is reinforced by rising content per vehicle — from INR 4,000-5,000 on a petrol two-wheeler to INR 22,000-27,000 — and by R&D rising from 1.4% of sales in FY21 to about 3.0% in FY24, patents filed going from 220 to 330 with 147 granted, and 950 engineers across nine engineering centres. But the same facts that produce the wins also cap the score. Because the company competes on cost and engineering rather than on price, four patterns land only PARTIAL (Recurring Revenue, Low Price Plus, Innovation Dominance, Cost to Replicate) and five land NO FIT. Pricing Power fails because management says plainly that metal cost increases — copper, aluminium and zinc up 30-40% in FY26 — are passed through with no profit element and a quarter's delay; margin improvement came from product mix and efficiency instead. Toll Roads fails because the parts are a meaningful share of vehicle cost, not a trivial ingredient, and contracts move both ways. Brand Strength and Friendly Middleman fail because this is a business-to-business supplier designed into vehicles, with no consumer pull and no marketing spend. That is why the two weakest quality areas by far are competitive advantage at 0.0 and industry structure at 0.17, and why multibagger fit scores only 48.

The risk review scores highest of the three (80, described as SOUND) but its five WATCH items trace back to the very same cost-led, design-in model. Good Enough Goods is flagged precisely because the customer setting the price is the vehicle maker and the value proposition is stated as cost leadership in manufacturing — the mirror image of the Pricing Power NO FIT — though the offset is that margins have widened, from high single digits to 11.9%, helped by bringing 18-19% of connector needs in-house. Cyclicality is flagged because sales follow Indian vehicle production and margins follow the one-quarter commodity lag: exports were subdued for four to five quarters through FY26 and commercial vehicles were flat in FY25 before FY26's 20-29% surge — the same swing that produced the celebrated share-gain numbers. Stakeholder Concentration is flagged because a handful of Indian two- and three-wheeler makers account for 45-48% of revenue and individual awards move the story (INR 250 crore for commercial-vehicle harnesses, INR 1,000 crore for switches from leading Japanese makers, INR 350 crore for sunroofs) — the same order wins that evidence the share gains. Government Dependency is flagged not because any revenue is set by a regulator but because management attributes recent demand upturns to tax and emission policy changes. Against these, the technology shift from petrol to electric is judged only a WATCH and consumer-preference and fashion risks are LOW, because the company has so far responded by raising content per vehicle rather than losing it, and its parts are functional items designed into four-to-five-year platforms. Read together: the culture, capital discipline and share gains are real and mutually reinforcing, but they operate inside an industry where the company does not set price — which is what keeps this a solid compounder story rather than a high-conviction one.

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Revenue tracks Indian auto production volumes and commodity pass-through, so both sales and margins swing with the cycle: exports were subdued for 'four to five quarters' through FY26, CV was flat/subdued in FY25, then FY26 delivered 20-29% industry-led growth; the CFO himself frames commodity as a cycle ('after every alternative 3 years, commodity cycle moves on an uptrend'). Exposure is bounded because the products are consumed in OEM production runs and the ~10-12% aftermarket, not in customers' capex.

> "commodity cycle has been extremely volatile... we saw escalation anywhere between 30% to 40% and most of our contracts with our customers are on pass-through basis" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/operations-linked demand (parts fitted per vehicle produced, not customer capex), ~10-12% aftermarket base, indexed pass-through with a one-quarter lag on both vendor and customer side, and countercyclical capex (INR 413 crores in FY26, land parcels acquired) through the FY25 export slump*
