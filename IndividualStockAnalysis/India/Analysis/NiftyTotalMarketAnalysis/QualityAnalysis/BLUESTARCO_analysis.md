# Blue Star Ltd. (BLUESTARCO) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSUMER GOODS*

Blue Star Limited is an Indian air-conditioning and refrigeration company that both makes cooling products and installs cooling and electrical systems inside buildings. On the consumer side it sells room air conditioners, water coolers/purifiers and commercial refrigeration products (like deep freezers) through a dealer network; on the business side it sells commercial air-conditioning equipment — ducted systems, VRF systems, scroll and screw chillers — and takes on contracted electro-mechanical projects for offices, factories, data centres, hospitals, malls, metro rail, railways, water and power infrastructure. Money comes partly from selling equipment it manufactures at plants such as Wada and Sri City, and partly from executing project orders booked into a carried-forward order book (Rs 6,923 cr as of March 31, 2026). It is primarily an India business but also exports and operates internationally — the GCC/Middle East, Qatar projects, a Malaysia joint venture, newer markets like Nigeria, Bangladesh and Nepal, and recent entries into the U.S. and Europe. FY26 consolidated revenue was Rs 12,402 cr with net profit of Rs 527.3 cr.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Segment I: Electro-Mechanical Projects & Commercial Air Conditioning Systems** — The business-to-business half of the company, roughly Rs 6,763 cr of revenue in FY26 (about 55% of the total) at ~7.4% segment margin, combining contracted building/infrastructure projects with sales of commercial cooling equipment.
- **Electro-Mechanical Projects business** — Wins and executes contracts to install air-conditioning, electrical and related systems in commercial buildings, factories, data centres, hospitals, malls, metro rail, railway electrification, water and power projects; it holds the bulk of the order book (Rs 4,664.5 cr as of March 31, 2026) and management calls infrastructure work less profitable than buildings/factories/data centres.
- **Commercial Air-Conditioning Systems** — Sells and services larger cooling equipment — conventional and inverter ducted systems, VRF systems, scroll and screw chillers — to customers such as retail, manufacturing, healthcare, education, government and entertainment; management claims No. 1 share in ducted systems and scroll chillers and No. 2 in VRF and screw chillers.
- **International Business** — Exports and overseas operations reported inside Segment I, covering the Middle East/GCC (including a projects business in Qatar), a Malaysia joint venture, Nigeria, Bangladesh and Nepal, plus a newer push into the U.S. and Europe whose outlook management says depends heavily on the India–U.S. trade deal and tariffs.
- **Segment II: Unitary Products** — The consumer- and shop-facing products half, Rs 5,332.4 cr of revenue in FY26 (about 43% of the total) at ~8.2% segment margin, sold through dealers and highly dependent on how hot the summer is.
- **Room Air Conditioners (within Unitary Products)** — The single biggest swing factor in the company's results — household and small-office ACs sold through an expanding dealer network, where regulatory energy-label changes, GST changes and summer weather visibly drive quarterly sales up or down.
- **Commercial Refrigeration / Cooling and Purification Products** — Products like deep freezers, storage water coolers and other refrigeration and purification lines sold to shops and institutions (and exported to the Middle East); in FY26 most of these lines shrank apart from storage water coolers.
- **Segment III: Professional Electronics and Industrial Systems** — A small niche arm — about Rs 75 cr of revenue in the December 2025 quarter, under 3% of company revenue — that distributes and supports Med-Tech Solutions (slowed by unresolved regulation), Industrial Solutions (growing on automotive and steel demand) and Data Security Solutions.

### The story in depth

Blue Star runs an asset-light business inside a genuinely expanding market, and it is run by people the records rate unusually highly. Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.45 of sales, and FY26 revenue of ₹12,402 cr sat on capital employed of ₹3,258 cr — roughly 3.8 times — on a balance sheet that was largely net cash. The management checks are the strongest in the whole file: candour scores a full 2 for pre-empting hard questions, quantifying about ₹250 cr of self-inflicted lost revenue, and warning analysts off over-optimistic margin models, while stewardship scores 2 for staying organic, prepaying NCDs in 2022, refusing low-margin infrastructure work and cutting costs early in the FY26 downturn. That same behaviour is the one pattern rated STRONG FIT — Corporate Culture — where the same MD/CFO pair guided conservatively, pre-announced a bad quarter in November and landed on it in January. Underneath sits a structural end-market (score 2): very low Indian air-conditioning penetration, commercial refrigeration compounding at 15%, and a runway management calls a golden period. Because demand rests on weather, penetration and functional replacement rather than taste, both Shifting Consumer Preferences and Fashion Risk come out LOW.

The awkward part is that several of these strengths are also what caps the returns. The company is investing hard for growth — new Wada and Sri City plants, continued capacity, R&D and digitalisation — and capital employed rose from about ₹1,018 cr in June 2022 to ₹3,258 cr in March 2026, but the records note incremental returns are modest, and the headline return on capital scores -2: about ₹4 of operating cash per ₹100 of capital, a 4% cash return. Those same plants, the deep component localisation and the applied engineering for chillers and data centres are exactly what supports the Cost to Replicate and Innovation Dominance patterns; the spending that builds the moat is the spending that dilutes the return. Pricing shows the same double edge. Management passes costs through rather than pricing above inflation — 13% of price increase described as warranted but only 8% realised by May 2026, with price framed as market-determined — so Pricing Power is only a LIKELY FIT, and that restraint is part of the same discipline scored so well elsewhere. Share gains follow the pattern: real but slow, from 13.25% in FY23 to 13.75% in FY25 and only decimal-point gains in FY26, with the ranking slipping from #2 to top-three, which is why Market Share Gainers is LIKELY rather than STRONG.

The risks cluster around the two things the business cannot control: policy and the weather. Government Dependency is the one HIGH RISK, and it has repeatedly moved quarterly results — BIS rules stalling water-cooler sales in Q1 FY25, the August–September 2025 GST announcement freezing secondary sales, and the 1 January 2026 energy-label change driving channel stocking and then destocking. Cyclicality is ELEVATED for the same structural reason the growth story is attractive: Segment-I orders track the customer construction and capex cycle while room air-conditioner sales swing with the summer. Below that sit three WATCH items that all point at the consumer end — New Entrants, Good Enough Goods and Stakeholder Concentration — in a price-led market where 3-star is the best-selling product, consumers down-trade to cheaper brands, dealers hold real influence through shared consumer-finance costs, and inputs like copper, compressors and electronics are still imported. This is also why Toll Roads is the single NO FIT: air conditioning is a big-ticket, discretionary, tendered purchase with no certification or standards anchoring. The offsets are genuine — the Weatherproofing programme, variable ad spend, cost rationalisation that lifted Segment-II margins to 10.4% on flat revenue, and share gained through the downturn without discounting — but the overall picture is a well-run, honest, growing business earning a low cash return in a hard industry. The blended score of 53 out of 100 (58 business quality, 49 multibagger fit, 50 risk safety) lands at Decent, three stars, with resilience marked STRAINED.
