# Sudarshan Chemicals (SUDARSCHEM) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Sudarshan Chemical Industries is a Pune, India-based maker of colour pigments — the powders that give paint, printing ink and plastics their colour. It sells these pigments in bulk to industrial customers such as coatings, printing-ink and plastics makers (and, after the Heubach acquisition, into newer end-markets like cell phones, digital inks and personal care), earning money on the gap between raw-material/energy costs and the prices it can charge, so results swing with input prices, freight and customer destocking. Historically it was roughly half domestic Indian sales and half exports (about Rs. 287 cr domestic vs Rs. 302 cr exports in Q1 FY25); after acquiring Heubach's global business (which included Clariant's pigment business) in March 2025, it describes itself as a global pigment leader with 19 manufacturing sites in 11 countries across 5 continents, more than 4,000 customers in 100 countries and about 1,600 products. Alongside pigments it runs a much smaller engineering/projects business called RIECO.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Pigment business** — The core business — making and selling colour pigments to paint/coatings, printing ink and plastics customers in India and abroad; it is almost the whole company (e.g. Rs. 589 cr of Rs. 634 cr consolidated revenue in Q1 FY25).
- **Specialty pigments** — The higher-value, higher-margin part of the pigment range, including high-performance pigments; the larger slice of pigment sales (Rs. 402 cr of Rs. 589 cr in Q1 FY25) and the main driver of gross-margin improvement.
- **Non-specialty pigments** — The more commodity-like, lower-margin pigments, the smaller portion of pigment sales (Rs. 187 cr in Q1 FY25) and more exposed to raw-material cost swings.
- **Legacy Sudarshan (India-rooted pigment business)** — The original Sudarshan pigment operation, described as the fastest-growing and most profitable pigment company globally before the deal; management now reports it separately and says its revenue was flattish through FY26.
- **Acquired group (Heubach + Clariant pigments)** — The global pigment business bought in March 2025, being turned around through cost cuts, customer-trust rebuilding, inventory reduction and merging four SAP systems into one — it swung from a Rs. 78 cr quarterly profit to a Rs. 38 cr EBITDA loss in Q3 FY26 on destocking, then back to positive (Euro 11 mn business EBITDA) in Q4 FY26.
- **RIECO** — A separate, much smaller engineering/project-execution business (revenue Rs. 268 cr in FY26 vs Rs. 228 cr the year before) that was loss-making and is being turned around, reaching about Rs. 4.2 cr EBITDA over nine months of FY26.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Sudarshan's clearest strengths are about supply and reach rather than price. Customers buy certainty: supply disruption was their biggest complaint, the company deliberately carried costly excess inventory to rebuild trust, and it won Best Supplier awards for it — the "Assurance benefits" check scores a full +2. The "Distribution advantage" check also scores +2 on a footprint rivals cannot match: 19 sites in 11 countries with over half the asset base in Asia, against under 1% for global competitors. Behind that sits real product work — a ₹750 crore growth capex programme, half of it for new products, which after early yield and teething trouble ramped ahead of plan and lifted gross margin from 42.9% to 47.2% on high-performance pigments. Those same facts are what make "Innovation Dominance" the one pattern rated a likely fit, and what give "Cost to Replicate" and "Toll Roads" partial credit, since pigments are a small but critical input that customers must re-approve through long sample-and-qualification cycles. Consistent with that, the risk review sees little threat from technology or new entrants, and none from fashion or government dependence.

The trouble is that several of these same traits cut both ways. The Asian low-cost base and the ₹40 crore "value capture" cost programme support the "Low Price Plus" pattern, but low cost is not the same as the ability to charge more — "Pricing Power" is a clear no-fit, because management admitted it could pass on raw material but not coal and freight inflation, watching EBITDA margin fall from about 13% to 7.5%, and it openly calibrates price to protect volume. That is also why "Good Enough Goods" is an elevated risk: about a third of pigment sales sit in commoditised grades under severe price pressure, and why "Cyclicality" is the highest risk of all, with margins swinging from 13.1% down to 7.5% and back to 15.3% on input and inventory cycles. The rivals' collapse is a second double-edged fact. Two pigment players filing for insolvency earns a +2 on "Share donators" and helps explain why new-entrant risk is low — but Sudarshan then bought one of them, and that same deal is the framework's worst mark, a −2 on "Merger and acquisition": debt-funded, larger than itself, insolvent, unintegrated and loss-making, with EBITDA swinging from +₹78 crore to −₹38 crore. It is also why the "Market Share Gainers" pattern is judged bought rather than earned, with legacy revenue flattish in FY26.

What is left is a company with genuine competitive advantage but weak returns on the capital it uses. Competitive Advantage scores 1.5 and Customer Benefits 1.0 across the quality areas, while Return on Capital sits at −0.33 and Capital Allocation at −0.2: every ₹100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings produced only ₹6 of operating cash this year, and roughly 129 days of cash stays stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. Growth has averaged about 21% a year but is lumpy enough that "Persistence of growth" scores −2. Management's own conduct is the steadier part of the picture — the "Corporate Culture" pattern notes plainly disclosed bad news, an owned slippage ("I would have loved that we did this much faster"), net debt to equity of 0.3x, a ratings upgrade from AA− to AA, and a beat on its own Q4 FY26 guidance. The route to market runs through third-party distributors rather than the company's own channels, which rules out "Forward Integrators" and contributed to flat FY26 revenue through distributor rationalisation in Europe and Latin America; quarterly results also still hinge on a few global key accounts, hence the watch rating on stakeholder concentration. Netted out, the combined rating is 44 out of 100 — 45% weight on business quality at 61, 30% on multibagger fit at just 18, and 25% on risk safety at 46 — a Mixed, two-star verdict with resilience described as stressed.
