# Apollo Tyres Ltd. (APOLLOTYRE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: AUTOMOBILE*

Apollo Tyres is an Indian company, headquartered in Kochi, that manufactures and sells tyres — mainly truck and bus radial tyres (TBR), passenger car radial tyres (PCR) and farm tyres. It sells them three ways: to vehicle makers fitting new vehicles (OEM customers like Mahindra, Maruti Suzuki, Toyota, VW, Skoda, BMW, MINI, Genesis and KIA), to the much larger replacement market of existing vehicle owners buying through dealers, and as exports to overseas markets. It makes money on the spread between tyre selling prices and raw-material costs (natural rubber being the biggest swing factor), so management talks constantly about passing on cost increases through price hikes and about shifting mix toward premium, higher-priced products. It runs two reported businesses — India Operations and Europe Operations — with plants in India (including Chennai and an Andhra Pradesh plant) and in Europe (Hungary, plus a plant at Enschede being shut), and consolidated revenue of roughly INR 73–77 billion per quarter by FY26 with EBITDA margins around 14–15%.

**The parts of the business:**

- **India Operations (standalone)** — The core business — making and selling Apollo-brand truck/bus, passenger car and farm tyres in India plus exports — and by far the bigger piece, at INR 52.4 billion of the INR 73.4 billion consolidated revenue in the March 2026 quarter, at about 14.6% EBITDA margin.
- **Europe Operations** — Tyres sold in European markets, led by the premium Vredestein brand and weighted toward high-performance passenger car tyres (UHP mix 52% of the quarter), running at roughly EUR 170–180 million of revenue a quarter with margins in the mid-teens to ~18%; demand there has been flat to muted while India grows.
- **Replacement channel** — Selling tyres to owners of vehicles already on the road, through dealers — described by management as the core, most profitable channel, with the TBR and PCR replacement segments repeatedly called out as the growth engine in India.
- **OEM channel** — Supplying tyres as original fitment to vehicle manufacturers; smaller and more volatile than replacement (it dragged growth down in FY25 when truck OEM demand fell), but strategically important because premium approvals — a 'marquee German passenger car manufacturer', BMW, MINI, Genesis, KIA — validate product quality and help premiumise the brand.
- **Exports** — Tyres shipped from India to overseas markets; a swing factor that grew 20%+ year-on-year in mid-2024 but was knocked back to mid-single-digit growth by FY26 due to freight rates, shipping disruptions and West Asia geopolitics.
- **Product categories: TBR, PCR and farm** — The three product lines management reports on — truck and bus radials, passenger car radials, and agricultural/farm tyres (the 'Virat' farm range was launched in India) — with the current INR 5,800 crore Andhra Pradesh plant expansion aimed at adding PCR and TBR capacity over FY27–29.
- **Brands (Apollo and Vredestein)** — Apollo is the mainstream brand, Vredestein the premium European brand (backed by a Formula-race-winner ambassador) that hit its highest-ever volumes in the December 2025 quarter; brand spend is significant, including sponsorship of the Indian cricket team jersey, which temporarily squeezed India margins.
- **Adjacencies (e.g. Trumigo)** — Occasional side ventures meant to support the core tyre business — Trumigo was a doorstep car-servicing business intended to connect with car tyre customers, but it was shut down in 2024 after financial performance fell well short of expectations.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Every check, area by area

**Capital Allocation — Neutral (+0.40)** · 5 of 6 checks had evidence

- **Capex for growth (not maintenance) — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: Five years of deliberately low growth capex and asset-sweating (utilisation now high-80s/90%) has been followed by a credible, demand-backed INR 5,800 crore AP expansion with FY27 capex of INR 3,500 crore (~80% growth), though ROCE of 13.4% is still below the 15% target.
  > "We've had low levels of growth capex over this period and substantial improvement in our underlying operating metrics." — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Advertisement and promotion — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: A&P stepped up from ~2% to ~2.5% of sales with the BCCI jersey sponsorship and 'Har Safar Mein Dum Hai' campaign, and management links it directly to record revenue, dealer morale and share gains rather than treating it as a cost.
  > "brand visibility through our strategic sponsorship has also led to the strong performance, especially in the consumer tyres category" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Research and development — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: R&D has consistently converted into new customers — EV tyre ranges (FY23), a marquee German OEM in India (FY25), and BMW, MINI, Genesis, KIA, Mahindra approvals plus European test podiums (FY26).
  > "we secured fresh approval from leading OEMs, including BMW, MINI, Genesis, KIA and Mahindra" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Merger and acquisition — Neutral** *(from the conference calls)*: No acquisitions over the period; the one adjacency venture (Trumigo doorstep car servicing) underperformed and was written off, so there is neither a track record of value-adding bolt-ons nor of value destruction at scale.
  > "the financial performance of the venture was significantly lower than expectations and hence we took the decision to shut this right off" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Cost of working capital — Weak** *(from the financial statements)*: About 93 days of cash stays stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers — a long cycle that ties up capital.
- *Not assessed (the evidence was silent — never guessed): Distribution to shareholders.*

**Return on Capital — Good (+0.67)** · 3 of 3 checks had evidence

- **Return on capital (headline) — Good** *(from the financial statements)*: For every ₹100 of capital in the business (shareholders' funds + borrowings), it generated ₹18 of operating cash this year — a 18% cash return on capital.
- **Profit margin — Good** *(from the financial statements)*: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹15 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±2.3 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control.
- **Asset turn — Neutral** *(from the numbers and the calls together)*: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹0.98 of sales — a moderate asset intensity. And from the calls: Tyre manufacturing is structurally asset-heavy — capacity additions cost ~INR 17 crore per tonne and the entire strategy has been to 'sweat' a large fixed asset base to lift ROCE only to 13.4%.
  > "It is about a 350 tonnes capacity addition leading to about INR 17 crore per metric tonne of capex." — *management, on an earnings call*

**Industry Structure — Neutral (-0.33)** · 6 of 6 checks had evidence

- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly) — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: The Indian tyre market is a concentrated oligopoly of a handful of scale players where the company can push through 6–8% replacement price increases in coordinated rounds, indicating limited end-customer negotiating power.
  > "given how the industry implements price increases in small quantums, we would need two rounds of price increases" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Barriers to entry and rationality — Neutral** *(from the numbers and the calls together)*: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.3 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Capital intensity is a real barrier (INR 5,800 crore for one plant expansion) and stable promoter ownership persists, but pricing rationality is imperfect — price increases are only taken in step with competitors and peers have run share-grab strategies.
  > "to be able to take price increases beyond a point and go out of sync with what the pricing accepted in the market is a difficult thing" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Share donators — Neutral** *(from the conference calls)*: Some donators exist (Russian imports exiting Europe, weak bus-segment players), but over FY24–25 Apollo was itself the donator to more aggressive peers before recovering share in FY26.
  > "There were close to 8 million to 10 million passenger car tyres coming in from Russia into main Central Europe. That, because of the war, has stopped" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Mini-monopolies — Weak** *(from the conference calls)*: No monopoly-like hold in the customer's mind or in aftermarket/services; the company competes as one of several branded tyre makers and must exit unprofitable sizes rather than dictate terms.
  > "we have gone out of the 12-inch sizes, which were not profitable" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs — Weak** *(from the conference calls)*: Replacement tyre buyers face minimal switching costs — management concedes share moves quickly with competitors' pricing strategy, and Apollo itself lost share when it priced ahead of the market.
  > "a section of the industry is focusing more on price increases, while one or two players are focusing more on market share" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Security by obscurity — Weak** *(from the conference calls)*: This is a widely covered large-cap with quarterly calls hosted by JM, Axis, ICICI and Elara plus a national cricket sponsorship — the opposite of an ignored niche.
  > "Our sponsorship of the official Indian Cricket Team jersey has garnered us extensive media attention and coverage" — *management, on an earnings call*

**Competitive Advantage — Neutral (-0.33)** · 3 of 3 checks had evidence
