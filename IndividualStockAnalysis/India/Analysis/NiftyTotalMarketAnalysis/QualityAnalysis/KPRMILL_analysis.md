# K.P.R. Mill Ltd. (KPRMILL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: TEXTILES*

K.P.R. Mill is an Indian textile manufacturer based around Coimbatore/Tirupur in Tamil Nadu that runs a vertically integrated chain: it spins cotton (and viscose) yarn, knits and processes fabric, and stitches finished garments — mainly basic cotton knitwear — which it sells largely to overseas retail buyers, with roughly 50% of garment sales going to Europe, about 20% to the US and about 15% to Australia, plus a small share in Asia. It makes money by selling yarn and grey fabric to outside customers while using most of its own yarn and processed fabric internally to make garments, where margins are highest (garments around 21–27% versus yarn at about 12–14%); management says value additions like full-body printing, fabric and garment printing and embroidery have lifted EBITDA per piece from about INR30 to INR40. Alongside textiles it runs a sugar business (KPR Sugar Mill) that crushes sugarcane and increasingly converts sugar syrup and molasses into ethanol, which management describes as the higher-margin end of that chain and which supported group profits when textile demand was weak. It also generates its own power — sugar co-generation plus wind and rooftop solar — consuming most of it captively and selling the surplus co-gen power. Blended group EBITDA margin ran around 23% and management repeatedly calls textiles the core business.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Garments** — Stitches and exports mainly basic cotton knitwear to buyers in Europe, the US and Australia, running at roughly 40 million pieces a quarter (about 150 million a year) at around INR185 per piece with a standing order book of about INR1,000 crore — described by management as the segment that supports overall performance, earning about 21–27% margins.
- **Yarn / spinning (textile)** — Spins cotton yarn (about 14,600 tons and INR391 crore of sales in one quarter) plus a new INR100 crore vortex spinning mill for viscose yarn; margins here swing with cotton prices, running about 12% against a stated sustainable 17–18%, and much of the output feeds the company's own garment units.
- **Fabric (knitting, processing and printing)** — Knits fabric and runs processing/printing operations, selling only grey fabric outside (about 2,285 tons for roughly INR66 crore in a quarter) while processed and printed fabric is kept for in-house garment use; a INR50 crore debottlenecking added about 20% processing capacity, described as value addition for own consumption.
- **Sugar** — Crushes sugarcane and produces sugar and molasses through KPR Sugar Mill; sugar and ethanol together brought in about INR619 crore over nine months of FY23 (of which sugar sales were about INR115 crore in the quarter discussed), at a blended sugar-segment margin of around 25%.
- **Ethanol** — Converts sugar syrup and molasses into ethanol, the higher-margin use of the sugar stream; capacity was being expanded from 130 KLPD to 250 KLPD for INR150 crore (taking both units together to roughly 5 lakh litres a day), expected to add about INR200 crore of revenue gross, or about INR100 crore net of the sugar and molasses it displaces.
- **Power (co-generation, wind and solar)** — Produces about 32 crore units of co-generation power, of which around 12 crore units are used in sugar production and the balance sold in the market, plus about 11 crore units of wind and solar (including a 12 MW / INR50 crore rooftop solar plant) that is entirely consumed captively and saves roughly INR12 crore a year.
- **Ethiopia garment operation** — An overseas garment manufacturing facility set up in Ethiopia that management says is no longer feasible after Europe and the US withdrew the tax concession, and the company is now focused on Indian expansion only.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

  > "All these growth initiatives at a total outlay of INR500 crores will result in improving our performance further." — *management, on an earnings call*

  > "In the marketing side, we are taking the next level of advertisement and the sales promotion activities in South India." — *management, on an earnings call*

**Return on Capital — Good (+0.67)** · 3 of 3 checks had evidence

- **Return on capital (headline) — Good** *(from the financial statements)*: For every ₹100 of capital in the business (shareholders' funds + borrowings), it generated ₹18 of operating cash this year — a 18% cash return on capital.
- **Profit margin — Good** *(from the financial statements)*: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹19 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±2.4 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Government Dependency. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.1× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 85% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ✅ Returns on capital held near 20% (was about 19%) — no slide in what the business earns on its capital.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across all four calls management frames performance in terms of commodity prices — cotton swinging from Rs.1,05,000 to below Rs.60,000 a candy, yarn spreads collapsing from a 'sustainable' 17-18% to 12%, and sugar/ethanol earnings alternating between crushing season and off-season. Garment margins are explicitly attributed to inventory cost rather than franchise strength, and management itself deferred greenfield capex 'waiting for the market to improve' across FY24, confirming the cycle drives the capital plan.

> "the cotton price where the highest in the last year reached more than 1 lakh and suddenly within a couple of months it has fell down below 60,000" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Basic/essential garments are operations-linked repeat demand rather than customer capex, and vertical integration plus low 2-3 month cotton inventory dampens (not removes) the swing*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 11 years with swings of ±12 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 17% and 25% (±2.4 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 18% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 6% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** The sugar/ethanol arm — the profit cushion that carried FY24 — is almost entirely policy-determined: ethanol capex is 'eligible for interest subsidy under the ethanol augmentation scheme', sugar exports run on a government quota, domestic sugar sales wait for government release orders, and in FY24 the government directed a shift of feedstock from ethanol back to sugar. The textile arm is likewise hostage to the 11% cotton import duty, the new cotton QCO, FTAs (UK, Australia) and the Mudra scheme, all of which management says it is waiting on before committing capex.

> "because of this drought situation in Karnataka and Maharashtra, government asked us to produce more sugar during this season... So ethanol production will be less" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Two-segment structure means textiles are not subsidy-dependent, but the segment carrying incremental margin is policy-set*
