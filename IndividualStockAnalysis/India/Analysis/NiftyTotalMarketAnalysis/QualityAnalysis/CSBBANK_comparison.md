The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 0 weakened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 3 could not be compared.

*Foundation test: FAIL on the long view, FAIL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Every one of the four calls shows growth at roughly double system rates — deposits 25%/21%/20%/26% vs industry ~10-13.5%, advances 29%/29%/27%/24% vs 11.4-18.6% — and the gains came alongside improving asset quality (GNPA down to 1.66% and NNPA 0.40% in Q4 FY26) and a deliberate pullback from unsecured and repledge lending, so the share was not bought with credit standards. (The long view had said: Share gain is explicit and repeated — deposits +20% vs industry 13.5% and advances +27% vs 16% in FY26, +26%/+24% vs 13.4%/18.6% in Q1FY27, four consecutive quarters of outperformance — but it is funded with ~52% bulk deposits and accepted NIM compression on wholesale lending, which is the 'share bought with profitability' caveat rather than clean share capture.)

Why now: Pralay Mondal has led since FY2022 with a consistent SBS 2030 roadmap; management disclosed the Q3 FY26 slippage deterioration promptly with a stated corrective plan and then delivered the promised improvement in Q4, while holding ~INR200 crore of contingency buffers, accelerated provisioning ahead of ECL and CRAR near 20% through the build-out. (The long view had said: Consistent conservatism across all 17 calls (accelerated NPA provisioning above RBI norms, ~Rs.200 Crore contingency buffer held intact from 2022 to 2026, CRAR 20-25%), stable leadership (Mondal/Divakara/Gundewar throughout) and public ownership of mistakes; but delivery against promises is mixed — the FY25 guidance of 4.5-4.8% NIM and 1.5-1.8% ROA was missed (FY26: 3.76% NIM, 1.29% ROA) and the retail franchise promised in 2022 for 'after 2024' has slipped to FY2028.)

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 2 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Unknown on the long view, Unknown on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Funding is concentrated in wholesale money — bulk is ~52% of term deposits and ~40% of total deposits, which an analyst directly challenged as inherently volatile and margin-damaging — and within wholesale advances roughly 35-40% is still financial markets/NBFC exposure. Asset-side concentration in one product (gold, 54%) compounds this. (The long view had said: Both sides of the balance sheet are concentrated: gold is 54% of advances (management concedes 'not just a gold loan bank' is still an aspiration for 2030), and funding leans on bulk/wholesale deposits at ~50-52% of term deposits with CASA down from 35% in FY23 to 19.4% in Q1 FY27, supplemented by FCY borrowings and CDs. Analysts pressed on this in successive calls; the retail deposit franchise is only expected to build from FY2028.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: The book has become progressively concentrated in a commodity-price-linked product — gold loans went from 47% of advances (Nov 2025) to 54% (Jul 2026) — and management concedes the growth is price-driven rather than volume-driven, while SME/BLG and unsecured lending have been deliberately paused until the operating environment turns, an explicitly cyclical posture. (The long view had said: Earnings swing with the rate/liquidity cycle (NIM fell from >5% in FY23 to 3.66% in Q1 FY27 on deposit-cost inflation) and loan growth is now visibly gold-price-driven rather than volume-driven — accounts fell from ~6-7 lakh to ~4 lakh with flat tonnage — while slippages have been erratic (Rs.140cr Q1, Rs.200cr Q3, upgrades in Q4) and SME underwriting was tightened on tariff/supply-chain uncertainty. Mitigating it: lending is an operations-linked flow business, gold LTVs carry a large cushion, and the bank kept investing (branches 603→868, full core-system rebuild) through the down-cycle rather than retrenching.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

*Not comparable: Shifting Consumer Preferences (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view); Fashion Risk (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
