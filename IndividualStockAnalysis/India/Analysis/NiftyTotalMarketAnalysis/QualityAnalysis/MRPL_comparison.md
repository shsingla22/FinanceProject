**Checks that improved:**

- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Poor → Neutral. Now: Real mix improvement (83% distillate yield, ATF +31%, benzene +65%) offsets the fact that price realisation is entirely set by external cracks and crude, not by the company. | The long view had said: Every earnings swing across the five calls is explained by throughput volume and externally-set crack spreads/crude prices, never by pricing or mix — management explicitly attributes the Q2 FY26 jump to +1 MMT throughput and better cracks, and domestic pricing is RTP-linked to international benchmarks.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±3.3 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Refining capital intensity keeps new entrants out — global capacity is closing rather than being added — and ownership is stable under ONGC. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±5.1 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Refining carries genuine capital and licensing barriers with global capacity closing rather than being added, but there is zero pricing rationality — margins are set by international cracks and crude, entirely outside the industry's control.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Weak → Neutral. Now: Real operating capability — three crude trains, six new crude grades processed, best-ever MBN of 67 — but this is process optimisation, not a proprietary multi-product technology edge. | The long view had said: Configuration flexibility (three crude trains, gas/reformate/PX mode switching) is real but is offset by structurally high fuel and loss of 10-11% that the company is still trying to bring under 10% via a grid-power project; R&D intensity is negligible and the PX complex sits idled on economics.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Indian refining is a concentrated few-player market, but MRPL is a marginal participant whose realisations are dictated by benchmark cracks and government pricing policy, not by pricing power over customers. | The long view had said: MRPL is a price taker at both ends — crude bought on global markets under shifting sanctions, ~40% of turnover exported into volatile crack spreads — so it captures none of the pricing power an oligopoly position would confer.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Tiny retail base gives genuine runway and outlets grew 167 to 200 with 250 targeted, though the original 150-addition target for FY26 was not met. | The long view had said: Real runway exists in retail (only ~1.5-2% of volumes sold through own outlets), and outlets grew 100→200 across the period, but delivery badly lags the promise: the May 2024 target of 1,000 outlets by FY27 has been reset to 250 by FY26 and 1,000 in five years.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: Modest but repeatedly delivering innovation pipeline — IBB pilot plant, first Indian Bio-ATF plant, new toluene production, and four consecutive Energy Technology Meet innovation awards. | The long view had said: There is genuine, repeated innovation output (Isobutyl Benzene pilot from patented technology, MTO, toluene extraction, indigenous EIL/IIP SAF, four consecutive innovation awards), but the commercial scale is trivial relative to a 15 MMTPA refinery and the IBB plant has only just reached mechanical completion after two years of mentions.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Weak. Now: Reliability investments (desalination plant, three crude trains, grid power) serve MRPL's own uptime rather than a customer paying a premium for trust; fuel buyers still buy on price. | The long view had said: In aviation fuel the company does sell certified reliability rather than price — first AS9100D-certified refinery in India for ATF and first Indian refiner building a Bio-ATF plant for CORSIA compliance, underpinning a Shell tie-up.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: Direct sales, the new Bengaluru marketing terminal and a growing retail chain build genuine accessibility, but the network remains negligible next to national OMC reach. | The long view had said: The retail build-out is deliberately about owning direct customer access at better-than-transfer-price margins, and throughput per outlet runs above the industry average, supported by depots and pipelines being added behind it.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Weak. Now: The mix spans retail outlets, direct corporate diesel and polypropylene buyers, but neither intangibles for retail nor switching-cost lock-in for corporate customers exists — all are price-led commodity relationships. | The long view had said: The benefit mix is only loosely matched to customers — corporate/institutional ATF sales are correctly built on certification and reliability, but the bulk of volume is undifferentiated RTP-linked and export sales with no lock-in, and the retail consumer offer carries no intangible appeal.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: MRPL's competitors are larger, better-capitalised OMCs and private refiners; no weak share donators are identified, and MRPL itself is the sub-scale marketer with 200 outlets. | The long view had said: Global refinery closures do remove weak capacity, but MRPL captures that only indirectly through wider cracks, and in retail it is the sub-scale challenger facing entrenched OMCs rather than the beneficiary of weak rivals.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Weak → Poor. Now: Output is undifferentiated commodity fuel and reformate sold as feedstock, with no monopoly position in the customer's mind or in aftermarket/services. | The long view had said: Fuels are fungible commodities with no ownership of the customer's mind; the only monopoly-flavoured claim is a regional ATF position, asserted rather than evidenced.

*Unchanged: Geographical expansion; Structural end-market growth; Distribution advantage; Cyclical market growth; Intangible benefits; Asset turn; Candor; Network effects; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Return on capital (headline); Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; People matter (talent development); Independent, long-term and tenacious; Halo effect (caution); Disciplined stewards; Profit margin; Cost of working capital; Out of limelight; Security by obscurity.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: FAIL on the long view, FAIL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is a genuine and repeated unit-cost obsession — fuel & loss driven from 11.41% to 10.06%, best-ever MBN of 67, grid-power project targeted to take F&L to 9.5–10%, opex of Rs.601 crores, explicit cost-leadership mandates and debt cut from Rs.13,608 to Rs.9,290 crores — but there is no evidence of low price plus differentiation, since MRPL is a price-taker realising benchmark cracks rather than passing savings to customers. (The long view had said: Genuine cost-efficiency obsession is repeatedly itemised across the calls — fuel & loss cut from 11.13% to 10.06%, best-ever MBN of 67, grid-power project targeted to take F&L below 10%, desalination removing summer water constraints, and an explicit cost-leadership mandate — but there is no low-price-plus-differentiation consumer proposition, only commodity cost control.)

**That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across all four calls revenue is discussed purely as commodity throughput, cracks and GRM (Q1 FY26 GRM $3.88/bbl vs $6.23 in Q4 FY25) — no license fees, AMC, subscriptions, attach rates, renewals or deferred-revenue funding is mentioned anywhere; the company funds itself with Rs.9,290–13,608 crores of debt, not customer advances. (The long view had said: the calls are silent and the numbers are mixed (1 of 3 checks supporting).)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Nothing in the calls describes a barrier a funded newcomer could not rebuild: crude sourcing is opportunistic and interchangeable (Russian barrels dropped entirely on sanctions with management saying it barely mattered), and earnings swing with cracks the company admits it cannot forecast. (The long view had said: A 15 MMTPA coastal refinery with three separate crude trains, its own desalination capacity, aromatics and polypropylene units and AS9100D/CORSIA certifications is genuinely capital- and time-expensive to replicate — management itself invites the replacement-cost question — but the moat is capacity, not advantage: competitors are adding refining capacity and MRPL's earnings still swing with cracks and inventory.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
