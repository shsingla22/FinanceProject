# Aditya Birla Capital Ltd. (ABCAPITAL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

Aditya Birla Capital is an Indian financial services group that sells lending, savings/investment and insurance products to retail customers, small and mid-sized businesses (MSMEs/SMEs) and corporates across India. It makes money mainly from interest earned on its loan books (an NBFC lending arm and a housing finance arm), from fees on managing mutual fund and other investment assets, and from premiums on life and health insurance policies; a listed holding company sits on top and also collects dividends from subsidiaries such as the asset management company. It reaches customers through an "omnichannel" network of about 1,712 branches (September 2025), virtual relationship managers, bank and other partner tie-ups, and its own digital apps — the consumer ABCD app and the MSME platform Udyog Plus. Consolidated revenue was about 14,181 crore rupees and profit after tax about 983 crore rupees in the December 2025 quarter, and the business operates in India only.

**The parts of the business:**

- **NBFC business (Aditya Birla Finance)** — The largest part: a non-bank lender making secured and unsecured business loans to MSMEs/SMEs, personal and consumer loans, and corporate/mid-market loans, with a loan book of about 1.48 lakh crore rupees (Dec 2025) that has grown from ~86,000 crore in mid-2023, roughly 73% secured, earning a ~6.1% net interest margin and ~2.25% return on assets.
- **Housing finance business (Aditya Birla Housing Finance / ABHFL)** — A home loan lender serving both prime and affordable home buyers plus construction finance, and the group's designated growth engine — the book grew about 48% a year over three years to 42,204 crore rupees with very low bad loans (gross stage 3 of 0.54%), and in Feb 2026 Advent International agreed to put in 2,750 crore rupees for about 14.3%, valuing it at 19,250 crore rupees.
- **Asset management business (Aditya Birla Sun Life AMC)** — Runs mutual funds and passive/alternate investment products for savers, with average assets under management of over 4.25 trillion rupees (Sep 2025 quarter), of which equity was 1.92 trillion, earning roughly 240-270 crore rupees of quarterly profit and paying dividends up to the parent.
- **Life insurance business** — Sells individual and group life insurance, distributed heavily through bank partners (Axis Bank, Bank of Maharashtra, IDFC First Bank); management calls it among the fastest-growing private life insurers, with individual first-year premium up 19% year-on-year and a value-of-new-business margin of 14.2% in Q3 FY26.
- **Health insurance business (ABHI)** — A standalone health insurer using a "Health First" model, described as the fastest-growing standalone health insurer with gross written premium up 39% year-on-year in Q3 FY26 and an 11.9% share among standalone health insurers; it is still loss-making at the underwriting level with a combined ratio of about 111% (above 100% means claims plus costs exceed premiums).
- **ABCD app (direct-to-consumer digital platform)** — A consumer app launched April 2024 offering 26-plus products — payments, loans, insurance, investments — used mainly as a customer acquisition engine, with more than 7.6 million customers signed up by late 2025 against a stated goal of 30 million in three years.
- **Udyog Plus (B2B MSME platform)** — A digital lending and services platform for small businesses, fully paperless, which had reached about 4,400 crore rupees of loans and roughly 32% of the unsecured business loan book, with about 38% of its disbursements sourced from the wider Aditya Birla Group's dealers and vendors.
- **Omnichannel distribution / "One ABC" branch network** — The shared physical footprint that sells all the above products together — 1,712 branches as of September 2025, including 1,028 co-located branches across more than 260 "One ABC" locations where one office serves lending, insurance and investment customers.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Aditya Birla Capital scores 54 out of 100 — 'Decent', three stars — and the number comes from a company that is genuinely good at reaching customers and genuinely mediocre at earning a return on the money it employs. The strongest checks all cluster on the front end: distribution (top mark) with 1,712 branches and 12 bancassurance tie-ups including Axis, IDFC First, Bank of Maharashtra and PSU/India Post partnerships; convenience (top mark) through an omnichannel build of co-located One ABC branches, virtual relationship managers and the ABCD and Udyog Plus apps, with 7.6 million app acquisitions; and market-share gain (top mark) in every vertical from a small base — health share from 11.6% to 11.9%, life share up 50 basis points to 4.9% while growing 19% against private-industry 13%, and a top-three position on incremental housing-finance book. Those same facts are what make three multibagger patterns a strong fit: Forward Integrators (branches from 1,331 in June 2023 to 1,712, co-located branches 621 to 1,028, the direct-to-consumer app live from April 2024), Market Share Gainers, and Corporate Culture — the last because things promised in early calls actually arrived later, with housing-finance return on assets moving from 1.53% in Q2 FY25 to 1.96% in Q3 FY26 and credit cost held inside the guided 1.2–1.3% band. Growth (1.2) and competitive advantage (1.0) are the best-scoring areas of the whole framework.

The trouble is that the engine driving those wins is a balance sheet, not a light-touch platform, and the same trait shows up as the weakest scores and the flattest patterns. Asset turn and return on capital both score at the bottom: the bulk of earnings sits in a Rs 1.48 trillion lending book and a Rs 42,204 crore housing book earning roughly 2.2% and 1.96% return on assets, which is the structural opposite of asset-light. Growth of that kind has to be paid for, and it has been — a Rs 3,000 crore institutional placement plus a promoter preferential issue in FY24, and Rs 2,750 crore of fresh Advent money into the housing arm in FY26, with no buyback or dividend policy discussed, which is why capital allocation only reaches 0.5. It is also why the cost patterns land as partial rather than strong: the cost discipline is real and itemised (housing operating expenses down 51 basis points to 2.37% of loan book, cost-to-income down 442 basis points quarter-on-quarter, lending cost-income around 31%, borrowing cost improving to 7.41%), but it buys operating leverage, not a moat. Pricing Power is the one outright no-fit: margins compressed from 6.98% to 6.06–6.12%, commissions were realigned downward, and what margin expansion there was in life came from product mix and rider attachment rather than from charging more. Innovation is a partial fit for the same reason — Udyog Plus at Rs 4,400 crore of assets and 32% of unsecured business-loan book, plus the app and 52 passive products, are real and quantified, but they sit on top of a lending franchise rather than replacing it, which is exactly why the technology risk channel is only a 'watch'.

The risks then read as the flip side of the strengths rather than a separate list. Government dependency is the single high risk, and it is the mirror of the weakest structural check — barriers to entry score negative because licensing is effectively the only real barrier, so the regulator sets the economics: surrender guidelines cut life value-of-new-business margin, expense and commission rules forced channel choices, multi-year health rules suppressed reported premium, and the GST waiver removed input tax credit even as it was framed as demand-expanding. Industry structure is the worst-scoring area at -0.67 for precisely this reason, compounded by the FY25 unsecured cycle where the industry underwrote badly and ABCL had to tighten filters — the same episode that keeps cyclicality on watch, alongside a Rs 500 crore stressed-asset sale in Q2 FY26, cushioned by a 73%-secured book and 18% renewal-premium growth. The prized distribution asset carries its own shadow: because growth runs through intermediaries, the Friendly Middleman pattern is only partial and stakeholder concentration is a watch item, with alternates assets dominated by one government mandate (roughly Rs 25,800–28,000 crore of a Rs 30,250–32,663 crore book) and life growth resting on mindshare at a handful of bank counters. Meanwhile the asset-management arm faces the cheap-substitute problem — passives at Rs 38,600 crore average assets, up 28%, against a core equity franchise whose share had been slipping, now partly recovered with 70–75% of equity assets in the top two quartiles. Fashion risk is the only low one, because persistency of 84.4%, renewal premium and 40 lakh contributing systematic-investment accounts make demand functional and repeat. Resilience is recorded as unknown, so nothing here has been tested against a genuine stress scenario.

## What to watch

- Regulatory resets (Government Dependency, HIGH RISK): another IRDAI or tax change of the surrender-guideline or GST-waiver kind would again reprice life margins and commissions — this is the one channel that can undo the market-share gains outright.

- Concentration in the intermediated channels (Stakeholder Concentration, WATCH): loss of the ESIC-scale mandate or reduced shelf space at Axis, IDFC First or Bank of Maharashtra would hit the Market Share Gainers pattern at its source.
