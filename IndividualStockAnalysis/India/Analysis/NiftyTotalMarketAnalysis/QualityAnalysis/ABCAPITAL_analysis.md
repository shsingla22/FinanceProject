# Aditya Birla Capital Ltd. (ABCAPITAL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

Aditya Birla Capital is an Indian financial services group that sells a broad range of money products to Indian customers — loans to individuals, small businesses (MSMEs) and larger corporates, home loans, mutual funds, life insurance and health insurance — all under one roof through what management calls a "One ABC, One P&L" approach. Its customers are retail consumers, SMEs/MSMEs and corporate/mid-market borrowers, reached through an "omnichannel" network of 1,712 branches (as of September 2025), virtual relationship managers, bank partnerships, and its own digital platforms. It makes money mainly from interest spreads on its lending books (NBFC net interest margin about 6.1%, housing finance similar), from fees on managing mutual fund assets, and from insurance premiums; the parent also collects dividends from subsidiaries such as the asset management company. It operates only in India, backed by the Aditya Birla Group (ABG) parentage, and by Q3 FY26 was earning roughly 983 crore rupees of quarterly consolidated profit on about 14,181 crore rupees of quarterly revenue.

**The parts of the business:**

- **NBFC business (Aditya Birla Finance / lending)** — The largest business — a non-bank lender making secured and unsecured business loans to SMEs/MSMEs, personal and consumer loans, and corporate & mid-market loans, with a loan book that grew from about 86,000 crore rupees in June 2023 to about 1.48 lakh crore rupees by December 2025 and delivered 772 crore rupees of quarterly profit; roughly 73% of the book is secured and business loans to MSMEs are about 56% of it.
- **HFC business (Aditya Birla Housing Finance / ABHFL)** — The home-loan arm, described as a "full-stack" lender across prime, affordable housing and construction finance, growing fastest in the group — the book grew about 48% a year over three years to 42,204 crore rupees with best-in-class bad loans (gross stage 3 of 0.54%), and in Feb 2026 Advent International agreed to put in 2,750 crore rupees for about 14.3% of it, valuing it at 19,250 crore rupees.
- **Asset management business (Aditya Birla Sun Life AMC)** — Runs mutual funds — equity, debt, passive/index and alternate funds — for investors, with average assets under management growing from about 2.97 trillion rupees in mid-2023 to over 4.25 trillion rupees by September 2025 and quarterly profit of roughly 241–242 crore rupees; it is also the source of the parent's dividend income (311 crore rupees in Q2 FY26).
- **Life insurance business** — Sells individual and group life insurance policies, distributed heavily through bank partnerships (Bank of Maharashtra, IDFC First Bank, Axis Bank), described as among the fastest-growing private life insurers with individual first-year premium up 19% year-on-year in FY26 and a new-business (VNB) margin of 14.2% in Q3 FY26.
- **Health insurance business (ABHI)** — A standalone health insurer using a "Health First" model, repeatedly called the fastest-growing standalone health insurer in India, with premiums up 31–39% year-on-year and a combined ratio (claims plus costs versus premiums) of about 111–112%, meaning the underwriting itself still runs at a loss.
- **ABCD (D2C digital platform)** — A consumer app launched April 2024 offering 26+ products — payments, loans, insurance, investments — that acts as the group's customer-acquisition engine, with more than 7.6 million customers acquired by late 2025 against a stated goal of 30 million in three years.
- **Udyog Plus (B2B MSME platform)** — A paperless digital platform for small businesses offering loans plus business services and supply-chain finance, tied into the wider Aditya Birla Group ecosystem; it reached about 4,400 crore rupees of loans and now sources roughly 32% of the unsecured business-loan book.
- **Omnichannel branch network / One ABC locations** — The physical distribution layer — 1,712 branches across all businesses as of September 2025, including 1,028 "co-located" branches at 260 One ABC locations where a customer can access several of the group's products in one place, with a push into tier 3 and tier 4 towns.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Aditya Birla Capital is a group of lending, housing finance, asset management and insurance businesses that scores 48 out of 100 — a Mixed, two-star verdict built from a business-quality score of 54, a multibagger-pattern fit of 30 and a risk-safety score of 60. The single most positive fact in the whole record is where it operates: retail credit, small-business lending, housing finance, mutual funds and insurance in India are all under-penetrated and growing, and this is the only check to earn full marks. The company is genuinely taking share in those markets — life insurance share up 50 basis points to 4.9% as the fastest-growing private life insurer, health share up from 11.6% to 11.9%, and the housing finance arm among the top three on new book growth — and the multibagger screen confirms this as a strong fit, noting the share is being won without letting loan quality slip (housing bad loans down to 0.54%). But the same records show that growth is not translating into good returns on the money invested: the return-on-capital area scores the worst possible mark, because this is a balance-sheet-heavy, borrowing-driven business earning a spread of roughly 1.8% to 2.25% on assets with, in the framework's words, no protecting moat. Strong markets and rising share sit on top of a structure that does not convert either into high returns, and that gap is what pins the overall grade at Mixed.

The clearest thread linking the strengths, the patterns and the risks is who owns the customer. The company's best-evidenced pattern is forward integration: branches up from 1,331 to 1,712, co-located One ABC branches up from 621 to 1,028, a direct-to-customer app that went from launch in April 2024 to 7.6 million customers, and a deliberate move away from digital sourcing partners. That build-out is also why the growth-investment check and the geographic-expansion check both score positively — the spending on branches, platforms and housing finance capacity is visibly converting into returns through better cost ratios (housing operating costs down 51 basis points to 2.37% of the loan book, group cost-to-income down 442 basis points quarter-on-quarter, borrowing cost down to 7.41%). But the very same fact is a risk finding: the retreat from digital partners happened because the industry's digital sourcing was behaving irrationally, which is why the barriers-to-entry check scores negative and why new digital-native entrants are flagged as a live concern. Meanwhile the company still depends heavily on other people's shelf space — 12 bank distribution tie-ups, partner-sourced insurance premium growing 26% against 8% for its own channels, with named banks driving the difference and one of them limited by zone access. That intermediated growth is the 'friendly middleman' pattern and the stakeholder-concentration risk at once, and it is also why commissions had to be realigned in line with the rest of the industry to keep bank shelf space, which the risk review treats as evidence the products are largely undifferentiated.

Pricing, regulation and capital tell the same double-edged story. The pricing-power pattern is only a partial fit and the pricing check is outright negative, and both say the same thing: growth is overwhelmingly volume-led, lending margins actually compressed from 6.98% to around 6.06–6.12%, and the real margin win — life insurance value-of-new-business margin rising from 7.4% to 14.2% — came from product mix, riders and interest rates rather than from charging customers more. That insurance margin is exactly what government dependency, the only high-severity risk, keeps threatening: expense and commission rules, the October 2024 surrender guidelines that cut margins and forced product and commission redesign, and health rules that knocked around ten percentage points off. Policy is simultaneously counted as a tailwind in the end-market check, so the same regulator that opens the markets also sets the economics. On capital, the picture is likewise two-sided: the Aditya Birla name is rated the one thing a competitor cannot simply copy, giving cheap and dependable access to funding — a Rs 3,000 crore raise with the promoter participating, Advent putting Rs 2,750 crore into the housing arm at a Rs 19,250 crore valuation, plus captive origination worth 17.6% of housing disbursements — yet that same repeated fundraising is why the shareholder-distribution check scores negative, with dilution, continuous cash infusions into subsidiaries, no buybacks and no stated payout policy. Two mitigating notes: management's credibility scores well because it flagged the unsecured-lending problem early, tightened, and showed it working while credit costs moved between 1.43% and 1.16%; and technology risk is rated low because the steady, self-funded build (Finverse, Udyog Plus, the direct app, AI assistants) adds value without any competing platform threatening the model. Overall resilience, however, is recorded as unknown.

## What to watch

- Whether lending margins stop compressing — the pricing check is negative because net interest margin fell from 6.98% to about 6.06–6.12%, so a further slide would undercut the volume-led growth story entirely.
- Any new insurance rule on commissions, expenses, surrender values or health products — government dependency is the only high risk, and past rounds cut value-of-new-business margins and forced product redesign.
- The split between partner-sourced and own-channel insurance growth (26% versus 8%) — if partners keep widening the gap, the friendly-middleman pattern turns into the stakeholder-concentration risk, especially given zone-access limits at one named bank.
- Whether the asset management share stops slipping — the market-share check flags that this was only 'narrowing the dip', and the shift to passive funds and ETFs is the named consumer-preference risk.
- Any further equity raising or fresh cash infusion into subsidiaries — the shareholder-distribution check is already negative on dilution, and more of it worsens the bottom-scoring return-on-capital area.
- Credit costs breaking out of the 1.43%–1.16% range or the stressed book growing — cyclicality is a watch risk, and management's credibility score rests on having called and fixed the last unsecured-lending problem.
