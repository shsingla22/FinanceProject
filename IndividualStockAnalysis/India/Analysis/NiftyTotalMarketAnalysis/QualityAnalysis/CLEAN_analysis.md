# Clean Science (CLEAN) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Clean Science and Technology is an India-based specialty chemicals manufacturer (registered in Pune, with plants at units including Unit-3 and a subsidiary Clean Fino-Chem) that makes small-volume, high-value chemical molecules used as ingredients by other manufacturers — things like MEHQ, Guaiacol, para-benzoquinone (PBQ), TBHQ, BHA, 4-MAP, Veratrole and a range of Hindered Amine Light Stabilizers (HALS). It does not sell to consumers; its customers are industrial buyers in polymers/plastics, coatings, agrochemicals, pharmaceuticals, food and cosmetics, who blend these molecules into their own products. It makes money by producing these chemicals in-house using processes it develops itself (management stresses 'differentiated technology', better yields and backward integration into its own raw materials such as hydroquinone and catechol) and selling at high margins — standalone EBITDA margins in the 40-46% range, with a debt-free balance sheet. It is heavily export-oriented — roughly two-thirds of sales overseas versus a third domestic in FY23 — shipping to Europe, the US, China and India, with consolidated revenue of about INR 945 crore in FY26.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Performance Chemicals** — The core and largest business, at roughly 66-72% of revenue across the period, built on flagship products MEHQ (a polymerisation inhibitor) and Guaiacol plus BHA, with two new 'Performance Chemical 1 and 2' plants (about INR 150 crore capex each) added — one targeting ~15% of global capacity for its product with expected revenue of roughly INR 260-350 crore.
- **Pharma and Agro Intermediates** — Building-block chemicals sold to drugmakers and crop-chemical makers, about 18-23% of revenue, including para-benzoquinone (PBQ) — an agrochemical input previously made only in China where Clean expanded capacity from 500 to 1,000 tonnes — and a pharma intermediate called DHDT still in customer testing as of early 2026.
- **FMCG Chemicals** — The smallest segment at roughly 5-13% of revenue, covering food and personal-care ingredients such as TBHQ (a food antioxidant) and 4-MAP for cosmetics; it shrank sharply in FY26 after losing a key 4-MAP customer in China.
- **HALS (via subsidiary Clean Fino-Chem Limited)** — The newest growth engine — Hindered Amine Light Stabilizers (grades 770, 622, 944, 783, 119, 701, plus higher-value NOR-HALS) that stop plastics, masterbatches, agricultural films and coatings from degrading in sunlight; started commercial production in FY25, scaled from ~80-125 tonnes a month to about 2,000 tonnes over nine months of FY26, grew 55% year-on-year in Q3 FY26 at a blended realisation of INR 425/kg, and reached its first positive EBITDA (INR 7 crore) in Q4 FY26 after cumulative investment of about INR 750 crore.
- **Backward integration / captive raw materials** — Not a sales segment but an important cost lever — a hydroquinone and catechol plant commercialised in December 2025 that now fully replaces imports and feeds its own TBHQ and Veratrole production, plus planned in-house production of key HALS starting materials, both aimed at lowering raw-material cost and lifting margins.

### The story in depth

Clean Science makes specialty chemical additives — polymerisation inhibitors, antioxidants and light stabilisers — that customers use in tiny doses but cannot easily do without. The quality work finds a genuinely well-run business: no debt, a stated cash cushion, all growth funded internally, no acquisitions, and promoters who voluntarily cut their own bonus from an entitled 4% of pre-tax profit to under 1%. Management scored highest of all on candor, plainly owning a permanently lost customer, an abandoned pharma project, lower revenue expectations and a margin goal it would not reaffirm. The economics are still good — ₹37 of operating profit per ₹100 of sales, and ₹17 of operating cash per ₹100 of capital employed — and the research spend keeps turning into actual sold products, from four commercialised HALS grades to an in-house process and a HALS intermediate validated in Europe. HALS itself grew 55% in a weak year, with exports in select products rising from about 20% to 50% of sales. That combination is why 'Corporate Culture' scores a strong fit and 'Innovation Dominance' a likely one.

The awkward part is that the very trait powering the good patterns also creates the biggest risks. The company's edge is a cheaper, self-invented process route — which is what earns partial credit on 'Low Price Plus' and 'Cost to Replicate', helped by solar power covering about half its electricity and backward integration into its own raw materials. But an advantage built on being the low-cost producer is not the same as being able to charge more, and the record is explicit that the company cannot: management refused price increases even in the good 2022 window, said realisations were not growing, and cut MEHQ prices in FY26 simply to hold volume. 'Pricing Power' is therefore a clear no-fit, and the check on price, mix and volume is negative — growth has been volume-led with flat-to-falling prices throughout. The same story shows up on the risk side twice over: Chinese producers using the older conventional route made it cheaper than the differentiated one, so the moat was eroded by process economics rather than by scale, which is flagged both as a technology risk and as the 'good enough goods' pattern where a cheaper substitute forces price cuts and drags margins from roughly 45% down to 33%. Market share tells the same split story — real gains in the new HALS business, where monthly volumes rose from about 125 tonnes to over 1,000, sit alongside share being conceded in the legacy base.

Concentration ties the remaining threads together and is rated the highest risk of all: four products account for 75–80% of standalone revenue, and the loss of a single 4-MAP customer in China who chose to make the product itself was big enough to be named a main driver of the FY26 revenue decline. Government trade policy compounds it, with US tariffs of 55% on Indian avobenzone collapsing 4-MAP demand and tariff uncertainty pushing customers to delay orders. Set against that, the business is genuinely defensive in character — repeat, functional, contract-based industrial demand with no fashion exposure, and end-markets in polymer stabilisers, water treatment and China+1 substitution that are still expanding — which is why the resilience read comes out sound and cyclicality, while elevated, is judged to run through consumables rather than customer capital spending. The weakest scores are on growth and capital allocation, and about 118 days of cash sits tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. The combined rating of 55 out of 100, or 'Decent', is an honest reflection of this: a high-integrity, well-financed operator with a proven ability to invent and commercialise new products, currently being repriced by competitors in its established franchise, whose case rests on the new HALS business growing faster than the legacy one shrinks.

## What to watch

- Whether HALS volume growth and the export mix shift (the 'Market Share Gainers' pattern) keep outrunning the share being conceded in the legacy base — if HALS stalls, the growth case goes with it.
- Whether MEHQ price cuts stop or deepen — continued cuts to hold volume would confirm the 'Good Enough Goods' risk and the 'Pricing Power' no-fit as permanent rather than cyclical.
- Any further decline in the four products that make up 75–80% of standalone revenue, or another customer backward-integrating as the 4-MAP customer did — the 'Stakeholder Concentration' high risk.
- Whether the higher-value niche grades (NOR-HALS and more derivatised polymers at $13–15/kg and above) actually earn better realisations, which is the only route in the record to reversing the negative 'Pricing, Mix and Volume' check.
- Whether operating margins recover from the 33% level and the ±5.9 point annual swings narrow, and whether the roughly 118-day working capital cycle shortens — the two checks that most drag on the return-on-capital picture.
- Whether backward integration into HQ/catechol and HALS intermediates restores the cost edge that Chinese conventional-route producers eroded — the core of the 'Technological Innovation' risk and the 'Cost to Replicate' pattern.
