### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 5 eased, 0 worsened, 2 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Technological Innovation**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: The whole franchise rests on a single legacy format — a local-search directory monetised by paid listings — and over the window management explicitly parked its newer transaction initiatives rather than building an alternative platform, saying there is no merit in spending to scale them and capitalising nothing towards new products all year. Traffic is still compounding (191.3 million quarterly uniques, +11.8%; mobile 87% of traffic, +15%), so no disruption is visible yet in the numbers, but the company is running a one-format business with no second engine and the excerpts contain no discussion of AI or platform-shift defence. (The long view had said: The company sits in a high-innovation-rate industry and is repeatedly questioned defensively about platform shifts — Google as default Android/Chrome search, ONDC, and vertical apps (Zomato, Practo) — and its traffic engine depends on SEO and content enrichment inside someone else's distribution stack; over the 11 calls the answers are framed as why incumbency still works rather than as its own platform advance, while the transactional layer built to modernise the platform was shelved.)
- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The calls never name a competitor, but the monetisation numbers show pressure consistent with alternative merchant-acquisition channels: paid-campaign growth decelerated from 8.6% to 5.1% year-on-year and collections growth fell to 5-7% for three quarters despite management's own claim of an 80-85 million SME universe against only ~490,000 paying customers — penetration is under 1% and volume growth is slowing, and the fix offered is more advertising and re-engineered telesales rather than a product answer. (The long view had said: Structurally different formats — vertical transaction platforms, ONDC, quick commerce-style delivery — were raised across the calls, and management's own assessment is that matching them means negative unit economics plus cannibalising existing subscription revenue, so JD Shopping/JD Experts were built and then parked; top-line growth decelerated to 7-9.5% in FY25 even as margins rose, so the response has been retreat rather than price war.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: The relevant behavioural shift — search moving to mobile — is one the company has captured rather than lost to: mobile is 87% of traffic and grew 15% year-on-year, total uniques grew to 191.3 million and listings to 48.8 million. Nothing in the window attributes any volume weakness to changing user habits. (The long view had said: Analysts pressed on users migrating from horizontal directories to vertical, task-specific apps, and management could only assert a horizontal-platform thesis rather than show the shift reversing; the demand base itself (how Indians find local businesses) is what is moving, though traffic of 191 million quarterly users still grew 11.8% with mobile at 87% of the mix.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Only ~613,000 of 48.8 million listings pay anything — the free listing is itself the good-enough substitute — and management concedes its Tier 2/3 pricing is 'very cheap' at ~INR1,100 per campaign per month, still only 48-49% of Tier 1 after several years of trying to close the gap, with realisation growth decelerating through the window (10.2% to 7.2% to 5.7% year-on-year) and ARPU stuck around INR18,500. Price realisation is repeatedly framed as a future lever in 'underpriced categories' rather than a premium being achieved today. (The long view had said: The service is replicable and faces free or near-free substitutes (Google local listings, organic SEO, social channels), and management repeatedly defends the price by arguing it is cheap rather than differentiated — INR1,580 blended per campaign per month, INR1,100 in Tier 2-3 — with blended realisation growth of only 5.7% Y-o-Y in Q1 FY25; the protection is a low absolute price point, not a moat.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The base is granular and broadening: 613,290 active paid campaigns against 48.8 million listings, with the top 11 cities' revenue share drifting down from 59% to 56.5% over the window as Tier 2/3 grows faster. Even the one named large relationship (Reliance Retail/Jio, MyJio integration) contributes nothing financially, so no single stakeholder drives results. (The long view had said: Customer concentration is genuinely negligible (613,000 campaigns, no category over 3-4% of revenue), but management openly concedes that user acquisition runs through a single gatekeeper — Google/Chrome default search on a 90-95% Android market — and capital allocation is now gated on one shareholder, with the dividend policy repeatedly deferred pending 'discussion with our parent'.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (LOW); Government Dependency (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 8 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
