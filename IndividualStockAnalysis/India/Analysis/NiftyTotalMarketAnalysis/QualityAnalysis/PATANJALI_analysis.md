# Patanjali Foods (PATANJALI) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Patanjali Foods (formerly Ruchi Soya) is an India-listed consumer goods and edible oil company that buys, refines and sells cooking oils and sells packaged food and household products under the Patanjali and its own oil brands. It makes most of its revenue from edible oils — importing and processing palm, soya and sunflower oil and selling branded packs like Ruchi Gold, Mahakosh, Sunrich and Nutrela — while most of its profit comes from a smaller but far more profitable FMCG business of biscuits, staples, ghee, soya foods, nutraceuticals and home and personal care items. It sells almost entirely to Indian consumers through a very wide retail network — over 2 million retail outlets by early 2026 — plus modern trade and e-commerce/quick-commerce platforms such as Zepto, Big Basket, Amazon and JioMart. It also grows its own oil palm on plantations across 12 Indian states. Revenue crossed Rs. 40,169 crores in FY26 with EBITDA of about Rs. 1,931 crores (4.79% margin).

**The parts of the business:**

- **Edible Oil (oil and vanaspati)** — Imports, refines and sells cooking oils in branded consumer packs (Ruchi Gold, Mahakosh, Sunrich, Nutrela) — by far the biggest business at Rs. 29,313 crores in FY26 (about 72% of sales) but thin on profit, with segment EBITDA margins of only 2-3% that management aims to keep in a 2-4% band; branded oils are ~75-85% of segment sales and profits swing with global palm/soy/sun oil prices.
- **FMCG** — The packaged consumer products arm — biscuits, staples, ghee, soya foods, nutraceuticals, beverages and home and personal care — at Rs. 11,188 crores in FY26 (about 28% of revenue) but around 61% of the company's annual EBITDA at roughly 10-11% margins; most of it was bought in stages from group company Patanjali Ayurved (biscuits ~2021, foods 2023, home and personal care for Rs. 2,800 crores in 2024).
- **Biscuits (within FMCG)** — Sells biscuits led by the Doodh brand (about 70% of biscuit sales, over Rs. 1,300 crores in FY26) and the newer Nariyal biscuit, totalling a record Rs. 1,907 crores in FY26, up about 16%; management says distribution reach, especially in south India, is the main lever.
- **Staples and ghee (within FMCG)** — Everyday kitchen items such as atta, honey and other staples at Rs. 3,658 crores in FY26 — fast-growing but lower-margin, which management says drags the segment's margin — plus cow ghee at Rs. 1,423 crores in FY26 after a 'revised ghee strategy'.
- **Textured soya products (within FMCG)** — The legacy soya protein food business (Nutrela-style textured soya), a smaller line at Rs. 526 crores in FY26.
- **Nutraceuticals / health and wellness (within FMCG)** — Vitamins, supplements and general-nutrition products; once a strong performer (Rs. 400 crores of revenue at ~35% margins in one quarter of FY23), it was deliberately shrunk and rationalised down to just Rs. 58 crores in FY26, with new launches like Slim Choice and Sea Buckthorn capsules and a 'Vaidya enrolment' programme meant to rebuild it.
- **Home and Personal Care (HPC)** — Toothpaste (Dant Kanti), skin care (aloe vera gel, face wash, creams), hair care (Kesh Kanti oils and shampoos), soaps, detergents and home care — acquired from Patanjali Ayurved in 2024 for Rs. 2,800 crores; it ran at roughly Rs. 627-840 crores per quarter in FY26 and grew about 35% year on year, with dental care the largest piece at around Rs. 339 crores a quarter.
- **Oil palm plantation** — Grows oil palm in India to feed its own refining and cut import dependence — about 1,10,722 hectares under cultivation across 12 states as of March 2026 (out of 6.63 lakh hectares allotted), roughly 38% of it in the prime 7-25 year yielding age; small in revenue (Rs. 1,792 crores in FY26) but the most profitable segment at over 21% EBITDA margin.
- **Other businesses (oil extraction, wind power)** — Legacy activities including seed extraction and wind energy that management mentions only in passing as minor contributors alongside the main segments.

### The story in depth

Patanjali Foods' strongest cards are all on the front end of the business — the brand and the way it reaches people. The framework's best marks go to convenience (a top score of +2, on more than 2 million direct retail outlets, a roughly 1 lakh strong direct yog-karyakarta network and a full presence on quick-commerce apps) and to customer benefits as a whole (+0.75). That same reach is what makes the two clearest multibagger patterns fit: Brand Strength, where one swadeshi/Ayurveda name has been stretched across foods, biscuits, dental, skin, hair and home care and branded oils now make up roughly 75-85% of edible oil sales; and Market Share Gainers, where home and personal care grew 38.9% and 35.4% year-on-year in the third and fourth quarters of FY26 against a category growing only 3-5%, and 0.2-0.25 million outlets were added in a single calendar year. Advertising is genuinely converting into sales too (+1): dental care added about Rs.116 crores year-on-year on new ambassadors and variants, reaching Rs.1,413 crores in FY26. The high-margin part of the business is now doing the heavy lifting — home and personal care and other FMCG account for about 61% of FY26 operating profit on roughly 28% of revenue.

The trouble is that several of these strengths are the same facts that generate the risks. The brand is the engine of the Brand Strength pattern, but the brand itself and Swami Ramdev's endorsement sit outside the listed company, and the growth story has been assembled through successive slump-sale transfers from promoter Patanjali Ayurved — including the Rs.2,800 crore home and personal care business — which is exactly what the Stakeholder Concentration risk flags. The celebrity machinery that makes advertising work also earns the company two of its worst quality marks, -2 for 'out of limelight' and -2 for 'security by obscurity', because a nationally famous chairman-figure, film and cricket ambassadors and openly stated ambitions to dethrone Unilever are the opposite of operating quietly. The same tension runs through the product line. Edible oil is roughly 70% of revenue: it is what gives the company its scale and shelf presence, and it is also why Pricing Power is scored a clear NO FIT (the segment is deliberately run to a 2-4% profit band, GST cuts were passed straight to consumers, and fourth-quarter FY26 input costs rose 294 basis points while the segment earned 2.58%), why Cyclicality is HIGH RISK (Rs.200 crores of realised plus Rs.250 crores of mark-to-market losses when palm fell from $1,350 to $840 a tonne), and why the Good Enough Goods risk sits on WATCH, since cheaper unorganised and loose alternatives compete for the same buyer. Government Dependency is the other HIGH RISK, and it works the same way: an import-duty hike in September 2024 produced a roughly 22% one-off gain, GST 2.0 forced repricing and trade disruption in late 2025, and wheat prices are capped by intervention schemes. Innovation shows the pattern in miniature — launches are frequent and named, but no research spend is ever disclosed and the innovation-heavy nutraceuticals franchise collapsed from about Rs.400 crores of quarterly sales, which is why Innovation Dominance is only PARTIAL and the research check scores -1.

Put together, the numbers say the front end is working and the economics are not. Return on capital is the weakest area by a distance (-1.33): for every Rs.100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings in the business, the company generated Rs.2 of negative operating cash this year. Out of every Rs.100 of sales only Rs.4 is operating profit, and margins have swung by about 12.9 points a year — swings large enough that the framework reads them as a sign that key costs sit outside management's control, which is also the basis for the -2 on barriers to entry and the -0.67 on industry structure. Sales have grown about 10% a year but lumpily, so persistence of growth scores -2 as well. Capital allocation is one of the few positives (+0.4), driven by real growth spending on the oil palm estate, where planted area rose 23.65% to about 1.10 lakh hectares at roughly 21.6% segment operating margin — and this is also the most genuinely hard-to-replicate asset behind the PARTIAL Cost to Replicate pattern. But only about 38% of that area is in prime yield years, so the cash is committed now and the return arrives later, which is precisely what a -2% cash return on capital looks like while it is happening. Six of the eleven patterns land at PARTIAL and two at NO FIT, no risk channel is rated better than WATCH except fashion risk, and resilience is judged STRAINED. The composite score is 32 out of 100 — 42 for business quality, 26 for multibagger fit, 20 for risk safety — a Weak, one-star verdict: a company with a real consumer franchise bolted onto a commodity balance sheet that is not yet earning its keep.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Government Dependency, New Entrants, Shifting Consumer Preferences, Technological Innovation. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.2× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 2% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ⚠️ Returns on capital slid from about 12% to -31% — the business is earning less and less on the money invested in it.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 2 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Edible oil is ~70% of revenue and management repeatedly explains results through global commodity prices — the Nov 2022 call detailed Rs.200 crore realised plus Rs.250 crore mark-to-market losses when palm collapsed from $1,350 to $840/tonne, and by Feb/Jun 2026 the same mechanism recurs (quarter-end mark-downs, palm down 12.6% y/y then up ~20% in March '26), with segment EBITDA guided to a narrow 2-4% band and swinging from ~5.5% in Q3FY25 to 2.4% in Q3FY26.

> "the vegetable oil business, edible oil business by its very nature, our targeted EBITDA stream is between 2% and 4%" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Products are consumption staples (flow demand, not customer capex), and the deliberate shift toward FMCG/HPC — now ~61% of FY26 EBITDA on ~28% of revenue — reduces commodity dependence over time.*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 4 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from -42% to 6% (swinging ±12.9 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 1% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 3%).

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Government action repeatedly drives earnings: the September 2024 import-duty hike produced a ~22% one-off gain, GST 2.0 forced repricing and caused trade disruption in Sep-Oct 2025, wheat prices are capped by 'government's intervention schemes', rural demand rests on 'government welfare schemes', and the oil palm business depends on 6.63 lakh hectares of allocated land under the Atmanirbhar Bharat policy — with management flagging policy 'overreach' as a risk to staples in Jun 2026.

> "the government policies that we might witness. There might be a bit of overreach there potentially" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: The growing HPC/FMCG portfolio is consumer-demand driven rather than policy-priced, diversifying away from duty- and subsidy-linked revenue.*

### New Entrants — ELEVATED
*"A new player changes the rules"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Management describes intense competitive entry pressure in its FMCG categories — toothpaste 'competition is fairly intense' in a category growing only 3-5%, and regional biscuit players holding Rs.4.5/9 price points against the leaders' Rs.5/10 — while quick commerce has reshaped the channel.

> "competition is fairly intense, that is straightforward and it's pretty much since the growth is very limited, anywhere between 3% and 5%" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: The company is building the new-format channel itself — scaling presence on Zepto, Big Basket, Amazon and JioMart — and its own scale is framed as an advantage as volatility squeezes 'smaller and unorganized players'.*

- ✅ Operating margin held (about 3% then 4%) — no drift down under competition.
