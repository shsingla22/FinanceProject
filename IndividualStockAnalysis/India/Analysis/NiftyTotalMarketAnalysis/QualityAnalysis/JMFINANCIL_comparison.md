**Checks that improved:**

- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: The #1 IPO ranking is a league-table position in a contested market, not a monopoly in the customer's mind, and no aftermarket or captive-services position is described. | The long view had said: No monopoly-like position anywhere — league-table share is 15–20% in capital markets and deals are openly won and lost each quarter.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: Growth is driven by deal volume and headcount rather than pricing, and revenue is explicitly hostage to transaction throughput and broking volumes, though the mix shift toward recurring AUM (29% of total) is a mild positive. | The long view had said: Growth is volume/headcount-led (more RMs, branches, franchisees, deals) while pricing has compressed — wholesale yields fell 100–125bps and broking mix shifted to lower-yielding derivatives.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Good → Excellent. Now: The clearest structural asset in the window: ~1,050 RMs, 72 branches, 874 franchisees and 151 housing branches give placement reach that supports a INR140,000 crores IPO pipeline — a combination few mid-sized rivals can match. | The long view had said: Distribution reach across institutional, private wealth, elite wealth, retail broking and an IFA/franchisee network is a genuine and growing asset that underwrites the syndication model, though it is not a reach rivals cannot match — hence the continuing need to buy scale through hiring.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Talent is the explicit growth engine — sales/RM strength up 30-43% year-on-year past 1,000 people, asset management headcount up 15-18% — and business heads (Sonia, Chirag, Manish, Amitabh) answer questions directly, evidencing a real leadership bench. | The long view had said: Systematic and counter-cyclical talent build-out with a stable, consistently fronted senior team, but the pipeline is built largely by external hiring from disrupted competitors rather than demonstrated internal development.
- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: The end markets — Indian capital markets, domestic fund flows, wealth/AIF and affordable housing — are in secular expansion, evidenced by the INR140,000 crores IPO filing pipeline, SIP book up 59% year-on-year and management guiding 25%+ housing growth for three years. | The long view had said: Genuine secular tailwind from Indian financialisation — domestic ownership rising from 8% to ~18%, strong SIP flows, growing wealth and affordable-housing penetration — but the core capital-markets revenue pool remains highly cyclical rather than permanently expanding.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: The core investment thesis of the last year is distribution intimacy — 1,046+ RMs, 72 branches, 874 franchisees, 151 housing branches and 33,000 customers — deliberately built ahead of revenue. | The long view had said: Deliberate, funded build-out of accessibility and client intimacy — 72 branches, 874 franchisees, 1,046 wealth RMs plus a 7,400-strong IFA network — created specifically to widen client touch points before new products launch.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Excellent → Weak. Now: The business is highly cyclical and management describes being past the peak of the issuance cycle, with FPI selling and geopolitical volatility stalling execution in Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 — nearer a downturn than a bottom. | The long view had said: Explicitly positioned at a cyclical trough with an identifiable upturn — FPI selling has suppressed issuance while a record INR140,000 crore IPO pipeline has built up, and management calls FY26 the bottom for Private Markets income.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Expansion is domestic branch/franchisee build-out (72 branches, 874 franchisees, 151 housing branches) with no international or premium-brand-travels evidence discussed. | The long view had said: Credible, well-executed domestic geographic expansion in affordable housing — branches from 75 (2022) to 151 (FY26) with AUM +22% and GNPA at 0.5% — though there is no international expansion or a brand that travels.
- **Network effects** (Competitive Advantage): Good → Neutral. Now: There is a plausible issuer-to-distribution flywheel (IPO mandates feeding wealth/retail placement) but no call quantifies or claims a self-reinforcing two-sided network. | The long view had said: A real, self-reinforcing two-sided loop exists — issuer/borrower origination on one side feeds institutional, wealth and AIF distribution on the other, letting JM syndicate a INR3,300 crore transaction rather than balance-sheet it — but it strengthens with scale only modestly and is replicable by larger peers.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: Indian investment banking and broking are fragmented and price-competitive, with the calls showing revenue swinging on industry-wide broking volume declines rather than any pricing stability. | The long view had said: Indian investment banking is a concentrated but genuinely contested field where JM sits among several strong domestic and global banks and issuers hold pricing power; there is no stable oligopoly with a captive end customer.

*Unchanged: Customer types (retail vs corporate); Independent, long-term and tenacious; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Intangible benefits; Distribution to shareholders; Halo effect (caution); Out of limelight; Return on capital (headline); Candor; Profit margin; Disciplined stewards; Cost of working capital; Barriers to entry and rationality; Merger and acquisition; Assurance benefits; Asset turn; Security by obscurity; Gain in market share.*

**That strengthened:**

Why now: The clearest pattern in the window: the FY26 Q4 call scores the May-2024 pivot against its own targets and confirms the INR250-300cr annual recovery guidance was met (INR270cr in FY26), while bad news — the affordable-housing NPA spike, three quarters of wealth margin compression, asset-management losses, slow IPO execution — is disclosed unprompted, alongside borrowings cut INR4,300cr, net worth above INR10,000cr and a rising dividend. **That weakened:**

- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No barrier is articulated that a well-funded entrant could not rebuild: the assets are people (1,046 RMs hired at cost), branches and relationships, and management itself concedes execution depends on external market receptivity rather than any structural lock. *Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 9 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
