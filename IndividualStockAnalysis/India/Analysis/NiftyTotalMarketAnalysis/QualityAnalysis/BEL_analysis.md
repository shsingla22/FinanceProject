# Bharat Electronics Ltd. (BEL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: INDUSTRIAL MANUFACTURING*

Bharat Electronics Limited (BEL) is an Indian government-owned company under the Ministry of Defence that designs, builds and supplies electronic systems used by the armed forces — radars, missile-system electronics, electronic warfare systems, military communication networks, naval fire-control and command-and-control systems, and land-navigation equipment. Almost all of its customers are Indian government buyers: the Army, Navy and Air Force via the Ministry of Defence, plus sister defence bodies such as DRDO (it calls itself DRDO's largest development-cum-production partner on Project Kusha) and Bharat Dynamics (Akash Prime), Indian shipbuilders that nominate BEL kit onto new warships, and a smaller stream of civilian government work such as an IT project for the Central Board of Indirect Taxes. It makes money by winning large, multi-year contracts, holding them as an order book (about INR 72,258 crores as on 1 July 2026) and converting that backlog into revenue over several years — FY26 revenue was INR 27,480 crores with profit after tax of INR 6,048 crores and EBITDA around 30%, and roughly 58-62% of sales goes on bought-in materials, including imported semiconductors. It operates entirely from India through some 29 Strategic Business Units and plants including Bangalore (registered office), Chennai, Ghaziabad, Palasamudram and Hyderabad-area facilities, with capability centres for research, software, unmanned systems and cyber security.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Missile and weapon systems (LRSAM, Akash, QRSAM, Project Kusha)** — Supplies the electronics and subsystems for surface-to-air and other missile programmes — LRSAM alone contributed about INR 842 crores of execution in a single quarter, an Akash Prime order from BDL was flagged at around INR 4,000 crores, and management expects a QRSAM order plus a Kusha programme it sizes at INR 40,000-plus crores over time, making this the company's biggest single growth driver.
- **Radars** — Builds ground, air-force and test-range radars such as Rudra (~INR 3,000 crores), Weapon Locating Radars (12 more approved, ~INR 1,000 crores) and an MPR radar for the ITR Chandipur test range; repeatedly named among the largest expected order categories each year.
- **Electronic warfare (EW) systems** — Makes jamming and signal-intercept systems for land, air and naval use — the Himshakti land-based EW system (~INR 3,200 crores), a Mi-17 helicopter EW mid-life upgrade (~INR 1,500 crores) and Shakti naval EW deliveries (~INR 127 crores in one quarter); described as one of the recurring large-order lines.
- **Communication and network systems** — Supplies military communication equipment and networks including AFNET for the Air Force, the CSS programme and shipborne communication sets for P-15B destroyers; management treats these as steady 'regular' orders that come in every year alongside the big projects.
- **Command, control and network-centric systems (IACCS, CMS)** — Delivers large integration projects that tie sensors and weapons together, such as the Air Force's IACCS — a multi-year project still contributing about INR 132 crores of revenue in one quarter years after starting — and naval Combat Management Systems.
- **Naval systems and shipborne equipment** — Acts as a 'buyer nominated' equipment supplier on new ship programmes (NGMV, NGOPV, MPV, CTS), each averaging roughly INR 2,500 crores of BEL content, plus standalone fire-control systems like Lynx-U2 (bid value around INR 2,500 crores, INR 95 crores executed in one quarter).
- **Land systems and armoured-vehicle electronics** — Upgrades and supplies electronics for army vehicles — BMP-2 upgrade orders, T-90 stabiliser spares and a tank upgrade programme management pegged at about INR 2,000 crores.
- **Advanced Land Navigation System (ALNS)** — A wholly in-house navigation product made at the Chennai unit, already delivered in Mark-I to the armed forces, with a Mark-II adding support for India's own NavIC/IRNSS satellite signals; order size not yet disclosed.
- **Optronics and other standard defence products** — Smaller-ticket sighting and optical devices sold alongside communication gear; described as part of the routine, smaller-size order flow rather than the headline programmes.
- **New-age technologies (drone electronics, AI, quantum, cyber security)** — An emerging area built with DRDO, start-ups, academia and in-house teams covering drone electronics, quantum key distribution and quantum-safe communication, backed by over INR 100 crores of computing capex in two years and another INR 100-200 crores in approval; still pre-revenue with proof-of-concepts given to defence users, but management expects higher margins from it.
- **Services and annual maintenance contracts (AMC)** — Ongoing maintenance and support on installed systems, such as the Akash AMC, which management counts as part of the dependable base order flow every year.
- **Civil / non-defence business** — Non-military government projects such as CBIT for the Central Board of Indirect Taxes (about INR 300 crores executed in one quarter); management guides to a minimum of around INR 500 crores of civil orders a year, i.e. a small add-on to the defence base.
- **Component and third-party supply** — Occasional build-to-print work for outside customers, for example TR Modules supplied to Thales, mentioned as part of a quarter's order intake.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Bharat Electronics scores 62 out of 100 — "Decent", three stars — from three separate lenses that all point at the same underlying fact: BEL is the default electronics supplier to a single, permanently expanding customer. The quality framework rates its competitive position and customer benefits at the top of the scale: it is the de facto leader on complex Indian defence programmes, capturing 50-60% of electronics content on major platforms, retained as lead maintenance agency on AEW&CS, and protected by deep switching costs — buyer-nominated equipment on shipbuilding programmes, multi-year maintenance contracts, and spares and upgrade contracts running seven years. Sales have compounded about 13% a year with unusually steady year-to-year growth, and ₹29 of every ₹100 of sales is operating profit, with margins moving only about ±3.6 points a year. The end market is in permanent expansion — mandated indigenisation, replacement of Russian and Ukrainian supply, and exponentially growing drone and counter-drone demand.

The multibagger screen picks up the same traits and turns them into patterns, and the risk review picks up the same traits and turns them into exposures. Innovation Dominance is a strong fit — roughly 82% of turnover comes from indigenous technology, about 40% from BEL's own designs, with a stated target of 75%, funded through in-house research labs and converting into named products every year. Corporate Culture is also a strong fit, and it rests on the same evidence as the framework's top mark for candor: guidance promised and met over four years, with bad news such as the ₹2,600 crore chip-shortage revenue slippage quantified and disclosed promptly rather than buried. But being the government's default supplier is exactly what makes Government Dependency a high risk and Stakeholder Concentration elevated — roughly 85-90% of revenue comes from one paymaster, the Ministry of Defence, and around 40-42% of turnover has rested on technology transferred from a single source, DRDO. Order timing, approvals and pricing all sit inside a government process BEL does not control, and the flagship QRSAM order has slipped. The push to raise own-design content to 75% is therefore not just a growth story; it is the direct remedy for the concentration risk. The same doubling-up appears in the Friendly Middleman, Brand Strength and Market Share Gainers patterns, all rated only "likely" — the standing is real, but it is conferred by the customer and its agencies rather than earned in an open market.

Where the story turns cautionary is pricing and cash. Pricing Power is a clear no-fit: across all 17 calls there is not one discussion of raising prices, gross margin is guided flat at 40-42%, and input cost increases are absorbed and offset through indigenisation rather than passed on, because contracts are awarded by lowest-price L1 tendering. Toll Roads is also a no-fit, since BEL's content sits at the centre of programme cost — electronics is 25-30% of a submarine's value — not at the edge of it. That same L1 mechanism is what makes the Good Enough Goods and New Entrants risks live rather than theoretical: BEL has already lost the system-integrator role on Netra 2 to Adani Defence on price, and private mid-caps and startups are taking the lower-complexity counter-drone work. Cash tells a related story — about 342 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, the framework's worst mark, and capital allocation, return on capital and industry structure all score weakly at 0.33. The lumpy, customer-capex-driven order flow has flattened, from ₹76,705 crore in July 2024 to ₹72,258 crore in July 2026, and on the technology side BEL is described as playing catch-up in quantum computing, drone electronics and AI with only proof-of-concept deliverables so far. Recurring revenue from maintenance and spares, long seven-year execution tails, roughly 15% civil work and a small export book of about USD 96 million cushion this — but not enough to change the overall resilience reading, which is strained.

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds"*

**What the calls show:** Guidance made and met repeatedly over four years — 15% revenue growth promised in Jul 2022 and delivered, 16% in FY26 with 'all the guidance parameters' met — alongside prompt disclosure of bad news (chip-shortage revenue slippage quantified at INR2,600 crores, LD and doubtful-debt provisions rising to INR132 crores, losing the Netra 2 bid), a net-cash balance sheet and rising receivable discipline (176 to 140 days). > "there are umpteen buyer nominated equipments in these" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Operating margin of 29% moving only ±3.6 points a year — the steady, protected profitability these patterns produce.

## What to watch

- Order book direction — it has already fallen from ₹76,705 crore (Jul 2024) to ₹72,258 crore (Jul 2026); continued flattening would confirm the Cyclicality risk rather than leave it a watch item.

- Progress on lifting own-design content from ~40% toward the stated 75% target — this is the single lever that reduces the Stakeholder Concentration risk of DRDO-sourced technology while sustaining the Innovation Dominance pattern.
- The 342-day cash cycle (Cost of working capital, scored -2) — whether it shortens or lengthens, given the business runs on customer advances and weak return-on-capital and capital-allocation scores.

- Movement in the quantum/drone/AI programmes beyond proof-of-concept stage — the Technological Innovation risk is currently only a watch because incremental indigenisation is still delivering.
