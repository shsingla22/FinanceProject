## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 31 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 6 improved, 2 regressed, 23 unchanged. 2 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them).

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Allocation | Weak (-0.60) | Neutral (-0.40) | +0.20 |
| Return on Capital | Weak (-0.67) | Neutral (+0.00) | +0.67 |
| Growth | Neutral (+0.00) | Neutral (+0.17) | +0.17 |
| Management | Neutral (-0.50) | Weak (-0.67) | -0.17 |
| Industry Structure | Weak (-1.17) | Weak (-1.00) | +0.17 |
| Customer Benefits | Neutral (-0.25) | Neutral (-0.25) | +0.00 |
| Competitive Advantage | Poor (-2.00) | Weak (-1.33) | +0.67 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Entry barriers are extreme (spectrum costs, 500,000+ site networks) with no new entrants and increasingly rational pricing evidenced by 19 consecutive quarters of ARPU growth, though management still argues current pricing is below sustainable levels. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±9.5 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Barriers are formidable — spectrum auctions costing Rs 188bn plus multi-hundred-billion capex deter entrants and pricing has turned rational since 2021 — but management itself concedes returns still fall short of the cost of capital.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹42 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹42 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±9.5 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Advertisement and promotion** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Neutral. Now: A&P shows creative traction (Cannes Lions, London International and Clio awards for Number Rakshak, CSK IPL partnership) and brand awareness is claimed to hold, but it took until Q4FY26 for the subscriber base merely to stop shrinking, so the spend is defending rather than compounding brand equity. | The long view had said: Heavy award-winning A&P (Cannes Lion, Spikes Asia, CSK/IPL partnership, 'Be Someone's We') has not translated into sales — the subscriber base fell from 240.4mn in Q1FY23 to 192.8mn by Q4FY26.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Poor → Weak. Now: No proprietary technology edge — the network is vendor-supplied (Nokia, Ericsson, who were paid in equity), 5G arrived roughly three years after competitors, and the AI features described are commodity operator capabilities. | The long view had said: Structurally behind on technology — 5G launched only in March 2025, years after peers, legacy Chinese equipment blocked from 5G upgrades, and no proprietary technology edge.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Poor → Weak. Now: Vi has been the share donor, not the gainer: losses of ~5 million subscribers per quarter as recently as FY25, narrowing to 0.5mn in Q1FY26 and only reaching flat at 192.8mn in Q4FY26 — stabilisation, not capture. | The long view had said: Sustained share donation rather than capture — subscribers fell from 240.4mn (Q1FY23) to 192.8mn (Q4FY26), with management celebrating mere stabilisation as a post-merger first.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Poor → Weak. Now: Coverage — the distribution equivalent in telecom — is a deficit not an advantage, with 4G population coverage still only ~86% against the ~99% industry level management itself cites, and the 90% target still unmet. | The long view had said: Distribution/coverage reach is inferior to rivals rather than unmatched — 4G population coverage only crossed 86% in FY26 and 5G reached just 80 cities, well behind competitors' national footprints.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Candor** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Operating disclosure is specific and includes bad news (subscriber losses quantified, capex and ARPU given precisely), but the calls open with lengthy promotional macro narrative and the excerpts contain no ownership of past strategic mistakes or discussion of the residual funding gap. | The long view had said: Communication is unusually transparent about weakness — they publish cash EBITDA alongside reported, strip out IndAS 116 distortions, openly state industry tariffs do not cover cost of capital, and acknowledge subscriber losses rather than spinning them.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: The market is a three-private-player oligopoly with high barriers and tariff repair underway, but pricing power is not shared equally — management states ARPU remains among the lowest globally and that incremental data is priced at an unsustainable level. | The long view had said: Indian wireless is a stable three-private-player oligopoly where coordinated tariff hikes of 20-25% flowed straight through to ARPU with minimal consumer pushback, though a state-backed BSNL is now re-emerging.

*Unchanged: Out of limelight; People matter (talent development); Disciplined stewards; Share donators; Distribution to shareholders; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Cost of working capital; Cyclical market growth; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Intangible benefits; Security by obscurity; Geographical expansion; Network effects; Halo effect (caution); Asset turn; Convenience benefits; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Mini-monopolies; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Assurance benefits; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Structural end-market growth; Return on capital (headline).*

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these — identical by construction): Persistence of growth (Neutral); Research and development (Neutral).*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 1 weakened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: The Vi brand does show real assets — sustained A&P, award-winning campaigns (Cannes Lions, Clio, London International Awards), CSK partnership and differentiated propositions — but brand strength is not converting into the pattern's two tests, premium pricing and share gain, since ARPU remains among the lowest globally and the base only stopped shrinking in Q4FY26. (The long view had said: There is genuine heritage (Vodafone + Idea), sustained A&P and award-winning campaigns (Cannes Lion, CSK/IPL partnership, 'Be Someone's We'), but brand strength never converted into the required proof — share was lost every quarter until Q4FY26 and pricing sits at a discount to peers rather than a premium.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Named products recur each quarter with disclosed traction (Non-Stop Hero at >25% sequential growth for three quarters, REDX Family, Easy+, AI recharge assistant, CCaaS winning Asian Telecom Awards 2026), but there is no R&D disclosure, no share-of-industry-R&D claim, and the spending is network capex funded by dilution rather than high gross margins funding innovation. (The long view had said: Named propositions do recur each year with disclosed traction (Vi Guarantee, Non-Stop Hero growing >25% sequentially for three quarters, REDX Family, Vi App/Vi Finance, CCaaS, IoT lab), but there is no R&D disclosure, no share-of-industry-R&D claim, and innovation is funded by rescue equity and government relief rather than by high gross margins.)
- **Corporate Culture**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management set 7 public KPIs and claims delivery against all of them, and the 5G rollout promised in Jun 2025 (all 17 circles by Aug/Sep 2025) was in fact delivered by the Nov 2025 call — genuine promise-keeping. Against that: CEO and CFO both changed mid-window, the 5-year record is repeated dilution and government rescue (GoI now 49%), and the balance sheet is the antithesis of the conservative, through-cycle posture the pattern requires. (The long view had said: Delivery against promises is poor and leadership unstable: the INR250bn debt facility "in active discussions" in Aug 2024 was still unclosed in May 2026, the Rs 500–550bn three-year capex plan produced only Rs 8,742cr of investment in FY26, and CEO/CFO/Chairman all turned over twice in the period against a balance sheet that is the antithesis of conservative.)

**That weakened:**

- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across all four calls the sale is direct-to-consumer via brand marketing, the Vi app, plans and an IPL sponsorship — no prescriber, installer or trade professional chooses the product, and no channel training programme is described. (The long view had said: No discussion across any of the calls of professional intermediaries, prescribers, installers or trade training programmes driving the sale; distribution and marketing are aimed directly at end consumers.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Market Share Gainers (NO FIT); Cost to Replicate (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 1 worsened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The last 12 months are dominated by a forced generational re-platforming: 5G launched only in March 2025 (well after peers) and scaled from Mumbai to 80+ cities by Q4FY26, on top of a still-incomplete 4G build (population coverage only ~86%), plus AI-driven traffic needs and a satellite tie-up with AST SpaceMobile. Each format shift requires the installed base to be rebuilt with capital the company does not comfortably have. (The long view had said: The installed base repeatedly becomes legacy: 3G refarmed and shut down across circles (20,200 3G sites shut in one quarter), 2G handset customers incentivised to upgrade, and a Rs.500–550bn / Rs.45,000 Cr three-year capex programme simply to move to 4G/5G. Management also flags circles with Chinese equipment 'where 5G is not allowed', forcing vendor re-platforming — the classic incumbent's dilemma of infrastructure built around a superseded method.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 2 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 2 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
