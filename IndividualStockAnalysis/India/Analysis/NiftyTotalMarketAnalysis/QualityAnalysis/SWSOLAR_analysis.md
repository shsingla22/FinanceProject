# Sterling (SWSOLAR) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Sterling and Wilson Renewable Energy (SWSOLAR) is an Indian contractor that builds large solar power plants for other people — it does the engineering, buys the panels and equipment, and constructs the plant on a project basis, handing over a working facility. Its customers are power producers and developers who own the plants: private IPPs and renewable developers such as Serentica and AMEA Power, government-owned buyers like Coal India, and the Reliance Group, which is also a promoter (shareholder) of the company. It makes money mainly by earning a margin on construction contracts — management says gross margins run about 8%–10% on EPC work — plus a smaller, steadier annuity fee stream from maintaining solar plants after they are built, at roughly 20% gross margin. India is the core market (about 78% of the pending order book and INR 5,836 crores of FY26 revenue), with a smaller international business in Africa, the Middle East and parts of Europe; total FY26 revenue was INR 7,548 crores against a record pending order value of over INR 13,000 crores as of June 2026.

**The parts of the business:**

- **India (domestic) solar EPC** — Builds utility-scale solar PV plants across India under engineering-procurement-construction contracts and is described as the company's core business and 'pillar of strength' — about 78% of the pending order book (~INR 9,250 crores) and INR 5,836 crores of FY26 revenue, with roughly 10% gross margins.
- **International EPC** — The same solar plant construction work outside India, focused on Africa, the Middle East and select European countries (recent projects in South Africa, Spain, Italy and a 1,000 MW Egypt project via a 50-50 joint venture with Hassan Allam Construction) — much smaller at INR 1,444 crores of FY26 revenue, but management says post-COVID it has been more profitable than domestic work, with 13.2% gross margin in FY26.
- **Turnkey vs BOS orders** — Two ways the EPC contracts are structured — 'turnkey', where Sterling supplies everything including the solar modules, and 'BOS' (balance of system), where it does everything except supply the modules; turnkey has grown to about 70% of new FY26 orders and roughly two-thirds of the pending order book, and the mix drives whether margins sit at the low or high end of the 8%–10% range.
- **Operations and maintenance (O&M)** — Runs and services solar plants after construction — for its own completed projects and as a third-party contractor for others — a recurring, low-overhead fee business that management calls near an 'inflection point'; the portfolio grew from 5.8 GW in 2022 to 8.2 GW in 2024 to a record 18.3 GW by mid-2026, making it, in management's words, probably one of the largest third-party O&M players globally, though it is still a small slice of total revenue (about 3.7% back in Q1 FY23).
- **Battery energy storage systems (BESS)** — Building battery storage, either bolted onto solar plants or as stand-alone projects — started as a pilot for Reliance at Jamnagar, and by FY26 included a pure 790 MWh battery order and a 600 MWh storage component in the Egypt project; management says the ordering opportunity here could eventually be about as large in value as the solar PV market.
- **Reliance Group relationship** — Not a separate segment but repeatedly flagged as a key driver — Reliance came in as an additional promoter in 2022, strengthening the balance sheet and lender/customer confidence, and management says it is working closely with Reliance on its planned multi-gigawatt renewable hub in Kutch, Gujarat and expects to win a large share of that rollout, though no firm orders are described yet.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

> "Our employees headcount have now touched about 3,500 compared to 2,500 a year ago." — *management, on an earnings call*

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Government Dependency, Good Enough Goods, Stakeholder Concentration, New Entrants, Fashion Risk. The balance sheet and cash engine shows multiple financial stress signals — 2 of the 3 stress checks are flagged — multiple warning lights at once.

- ⚠️ Borrowings are now 1.8× shareholders' funds (up from 0.9×) — a leveraged balance sheet leaves little room when any of the risks above bites.
- ⚠️ Only about -97% of recent operating profit became cash (was -12%) — profits are getting stuck in paper, an early stress signal.
- ✅ Returns on capital moderated from about 20% to 16% — lower, but still well clear of the deep slide this check looks for.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 2 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is pure customer-capex EPC — IPP/PSU solar build-out — so order inflow and execution swing with module/commodity prices and developer sentiment; gross margins went negative in FY23 (labour, weather, module price spikes), recovered only to 8-11%, and Q4 FY26/Q1 FY27 both saw ordering and revenue drop on commodity volatility. Management itself frames the business in cycle language, and O&M annuity is still a small offset.

> "The unexpected geopolitical tensions, coupled with high commodity price fluctuations meant that EPC ordering activities by developers in the fourth quarter was largely muted." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Growing multi-year O&M annuity portfolio (5.8GW in 2022 to 18.3GW by 2026) at ~20-25% gross margin is operations-linked, not capex-linked; back-to-back supplier pricing and negative working capital model dampen commodity pass-through*

- ⚠️ Year-to-year sales growth swings by ±56 percentage points — boom-and-bust revenue, not a steady compounder.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from -56% to 8% (swinging ±21.7 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 0% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 0%).

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Demand exists only because of policy: the 500GW target, COP26 commitments, SECI/PSU auctions, Coal India and NTPC-DVC tenders, and pricing is directly exposed to policy instruments — US anti-circumvention duty exemptions in 2022, ALMM/DCR rules from Sept 2026, Chinese export tax-credit removal, BESS localisation duties. The Nigeria order has slipped for years on elections. This is textbook fixed-infrastructure, subsidy-anchored revenue.

> "as per the policy guidelines, any project which were bidded after 1st September 2026, they are all under DCR category" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Geographic diversification across India, South Africa, Egypt, Europe and MENA spreads single-policy-regime risk, and c.78% domestic UOV includes private IPPs alongside PSU tenders*

### Good Enough Goods — HIGH RISK
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** EPC contracting is awarded on lowest-price L1 tenders with no end-customer brand premium to defend — management guides gross margins of just 8-10% (EBITDA ~5-6%) and repeatedly says it must be 'patient' and 'selective' to find margin-accretive work, which is the signature of a commoditised service where a cheaper challenger can take the job. The differentiators cited (drone thermography, IV curve tracers) are modest.

> "We reiterate that we anticipate EPC gross margins to range between 8% to 10%" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Some scale protection at the gigawatt end — 15% of all Indian utility-scale commissioning in FY26, and complex storage-integrated and floating projects where execution track record, bonding capacity and credit lines genuinely screen out smaller bidders; O&M earns a defensible 20-25%*

- ✅ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs held near 100% (was 100%) — no gross-level price erosion.
- ✅ Operating margin (4%) remains near its historical peak (8%) — no collapse in the premium.
- ⚠️ Operating margin drifted down from about 7% to -17% — steady profit erosion, the mark of rising competitive pressure.
