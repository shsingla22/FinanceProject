Now: Demand-side cyclicals are favourable — record wedding dates, rising hotel occupancy and air traffic — while company margins sit near a trough with recovery guided. | The long view had said: Travel macros are at a cyclical high (record weddings, strong air traffic and hotel occupancy) rather than a bottom, while the company's own cycle is depressed by competition rather than by a turning end-market.

Now: Management stayed organic and deleveraged meaningfully — Rs.118 crore debt reduction and Rs.292 crore positive operating cash flow — though the inventory bloat was self-inflicted. | The long view had said: No large M&A and a credible FY25 clean-up (inventory down Rs.200cr, debt down Rs.118cr) offset the fact that management created the working-capital and leverage problem in the first place and cut investment pro-cyclically.

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Stressed on the long view, Stressed on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: Demand is consumer replacement/travel-usage led, not customer capex or commodity prices; across all four calls management points to structural travel indicators (air traffic, hotel occupancy, wedding dates) as positive and attributes the revenue and margin damage to competitive pricing, not to a demand cycle. (The long view had said: Demand is tied to a discretionary travel/wedding cycle (post-COVID boom then de-growth) and to volatile petrochemical input costs — FY23 saw ~24% raw-material inflation, while FY25-26 saw price wars compress realisation; but it is a consumer flow product, not customer-capex equipment, and management repeatedly points to repeat-purchase categories.)

Why now: Demand is functional and repeat-purchase — hard luggage, backpacks and travel accessories — and growth is credited to distribution, pricing and new launches rather than a trending product or celebrity moment; management leans on long-standing brand reliability, and the portfolio spans several brands (VIP, Skybags, Carlton, Aristocrat, Caprese, Alfa) rather than one taste-dependent bet. (The long view had said: Growth is repeatedly credited to design attractiveness, celebrity campaigns and 'buzz', and the FY24 collapse was diagnosed by management itself as ranges being unattractive and the brand not being seen as contemporary — taste risk is real, though the underlying product is functional and repeat-purchase.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
