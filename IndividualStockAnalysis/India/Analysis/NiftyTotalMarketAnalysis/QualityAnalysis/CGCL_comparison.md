### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 1 worsened, 1 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Unknown on the long view, Unknown on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: As an RBI-regulated NBFC, key economics — gold-loan LTV caps, the co-lending framework (now ~23.5% of AUM and the fastest-growing line, up 93% year-on-year) and housing demand — sit inside policy; management explicitly credits a government subsidy scheme and 'supportive' government schemes for growth, so a policy tightening in co-lending or PMAY would hit the growth engine directly. (The long view had said: As a regulated NBFC, key economics are set by RBI: the gold-loan cash-disbursement circular forced operational change (Q4 FY24 call), and the new co-lending guidelines effective Jan 2026 hit a channel that is ~21-23% of AUM and roughly half of fee income, with management conceding volumes could slow for a couple of quarters; bank appetite for the book itself rests on PSL policy.)
- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Across all four calls technology appears as incremental, margin-accretive adoption — GenAI/data science in underwriting, a fully digitised gold-loan journey with sub-30-minute TAT, AI-enabled branch security — with cost-to-income falling from 65% to 46% rather than any defensive discussion of a competing platform or a legacy installed base. (The long view had said: Analysts raised fintech disruption of housing and gold loans as early as Aug 2022 and management answered defensively ('Fintechs do not have the balance sheet'), while the company's 1,429-branch brick-and-mortar model is capital-heavy if origination shifts digital; the offsetting evidence is a sustained, incremental in-house tech cadence rather than a bet-the-company platform change.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Despite 60% AUM growth and heavy entry into competitive southern gold-loan markets, blended yields held at 16.3–16.9% and spreads at ~7% with management describing yield expansion as broad-based across MSME, gold and housing — no evidence in these calls of price-led erosion or of a cheaper substitute forcing rate concessions. (The long view had said: Gold and LAP lending is close to a commodity where the borrower shops on rate and brand awareness is low, and management effectively admits it needs co-lending lines to match cheaper bank pricing — the classic cheaper-challenger squeeze on a 16.3% yield, 7.1% spread book; protection comes from serving segments banks underwrite poorly rather than from product complexity.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Roughly a quarter of AUM is originated through co-lending arrangements with partner banks (INR7,138cr, 93% year-on-year) and INR73cr of quarterly income comes from that channel, so growth and fee income depend on a handful of bank relationships continuing to take the book; funding is likewise bank/debt-market dependent, addressed this year via a maiden INR400cr public NCD and global ratings. (The long view had said: No single customer, partner or funder is described as driving results across the 17 calls; the book is retail and granular (6.9 lakh customers, CF outstanding ticket size INR20 crore across 282 projects) and both the funding base and distribution partnerships have been steadily broadened.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Unknown on the long view (none of the stress checks could be computed.) vs Unknown on the one-year view (none of the stress checks could be computed.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
