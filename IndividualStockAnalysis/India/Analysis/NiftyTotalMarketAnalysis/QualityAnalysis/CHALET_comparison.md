Now: A maiden interim dividend was declared in Q2 FY26 alongside heavy reinvestment, which is a coherent start, but there is no buyback record or articulated long-run distribution policy to judge. | The long view had said: No dividend or buyback policy is ever discussed; capital flows the other way via the April '24 QIP and a minority equity dilution at the DIAL subsidiary, with all cash reinvested.

Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Entry is genuinely slow and capital-gated — land, environmental clearances and ~36-month builds — and the industry is competing on rate rather than discounting, though signings pipelines are healthy so entry is not closed. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±13.3 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. Now: Indian hospitality is fragmented rather than an oligopoly, and while a supply-demand gap gives pricing latitude today, Mumbai's flat ADRs in Q4 show customers do have alternatives. | The long view had said: Indian hospitality is fragmented and contestable — a supply surge in Navi Mumbai directly cut Vashi RevPAR, and corporate/crew customers negotiate rates annually.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 3 weakened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Chalet launched its own brand Athiva in Nov 2025 with 5 further properties (900 keys) identified, but most of the portfolio still trades under licensed third-party brands (Marriott, Westin, JW, Taj, Ritz-Carlton, Hyatt), so brand equity and premium pricing largely accrue to the operators, not Chalet; the owned brand has one full quarter of history. (The long view had said: The consumer brands Chalet trades under are licensed from others — Marriott, Westin, JW, Novotel, Taj, Hyatt, Ritz-Carlton — so heritage and A&P scale sit with the operators, not Chalet; the company is only now building its own brand (Athiva at Khandala, with Vashi being converted from Four Points), which is too new to judge.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The only genuinely recurring stream is the leased commercial/annuity portfolio (contracted rentals, committed leasing rising from 77% to 83%+, Powai at 90%, March '26 rental run-rate INR280mn), which management calls a stable annuity hedge — but there is no attach-rate, AMC, renewal or customer-advance-funding discussion, and the hotel business itself is transactional. (The long view had said: Chalet is an asset-heavy hotel owner: no attach rates, AMC/licence/subscription streams or renewal metrics anywhere across the 16 calls; growth needs enormous capital (INR30 billion capex planned FY27-29) rather than customer-funded float. The only recurring stream is office rent, which is an annuity on separately built real estate, not a stream attached to a front-end product sale.)

**That weakened:**

- **Cost to Replicate**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The owned prime-location assets are genuinely slow to replicate — finite sites (JW Sahar/Mumbai airport, Powai, the 8.2-acre Udaipur land), long approval chains (Airoli only just cleared environmentally) and ~36-month builds — but this is a capital-and-time barrier available to any well-funded developer rather than an un-replicable advantage, and the brand and operating layers are licensed in. (The long view had said: The advantage is finite locations that a funded copycat cannot rebuild: a bid-won hotel directly across the arrivals of Delhi T3 physically connected to the terminal, scarce South Goa beachfront with approvals already in place, prime Udaipur land, and 2.4 million sq ft of Powai/Whitefield offices adjacent to metro infrastructure — precisely the port/airport-style barrier the test looks for.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Inventory is growing faster than the market (3,389 operating keys plus a 1,655-key pipeline across 7 assets, Udaipur acquired for INR1,710mn, Hyderabad Ritz-Carlton added) and MMR RevPAR beat the wider market in Q3, but Q4 saw Mumbai underperform and no explicit, repeated market-share figures are given. (The long view had said: Management repeatedly claims outperformance versus its micro-markets (MMR high-single-digit RevPAR vs market early-single-digit in Q3 FY26) and keys are scaling from 3,389 operating to over 5,000 with the pipeline, but no explicit market-share numbers are tracked over time and Q4 FY26 showed the portfolio losing occupancy faster than the market in its largest region.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The stated model is the opposite of low-price-plus: premium/upper-upscale and ultra-luxury positioning (Ritz-Carlton Hyderabad, Athiva at ADR north of INR15,000), with growth led by rate rather than volume or asset turns. (The long view had said: There is a genuine, itemised cost culture running through all four years — payroll at 13% of revenue vs 15% pre-COVID, utilities 6% vs 7%, room-to-employee ratio 0.87, and repeated 'cost-efficient structures' language producing sector-leading 46-49% EBITDA margins — but the positioning is the opposite of low price (5-star deluxe Goa, ultra-luxury Ritz-Carlton Hyderabad), and savings are retained as margin rather than passed to price.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Innovation Dominance (QUANT SIGNAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: There is no sign in these calls of the premium being competed away by cheaper substitutes — pricing is the main growth engine, with ADRs up 17%, 16% and 16% year-on-year in the first three quarters and Athiva Khandala sustaining ADR north of INR15,000, and management is moving further up-market (first ultra-luxury Ritz-Carlton, upper-upscale Udaipur) rather than defending price. (The long view had said: Premium price points show real resistance while cheaper tiers do not — in Goa management flagged softening confined to the INR25,000-40,000 band with 'no resistance' at INR15,000-20,000 — and the Vashi asset was undercut by new similar-category supply, prompting a shift off Four Points by Sheraton to the in-house Athiva brand. The company defends the premium via location scarcity rather than product complexity.)

**That worsened:**

- **Fashion Risk**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Part of demand rests on taste and one-off events: management explicitly blamed a weak January on the absence of the prior year's Coldplay concerts and MICE, credits growth to event- and festival-linked travel, and the new Athiva brand is an untested lifestyle proposition whose success depends on staying desirable ('multiple marketing activities... to create market awareness'). (The long view had said: Some demand is event- and trend-linked — management attributes a weak January 2026 to the absence of the prior year's Coldplay concert and MICE, and earlier quarters leaned on IPL — but the core demand base is functional and repeat: business travel, crew, MICE and weddings, which management calls constant and evergreen.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Technological Innovation (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
