# KEI Industries Ltd. (KEI) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: INDUSTRIAL MANUFACTURING*

KEI Industries is an Indian manufacturer of electrical wires and cables — everything from house wiring sold to retail consumers to low-tension, high-tension and extra-high-voltage (up to 400 kV) power cables used by industry and utilities. It makes money three ways: selling to institutional/B2B customers (power transmission and distribution utilities, solar and thermal power projects, metros and railways, real estate and construction, oil and gas, data centres), selling through a dealer/distribution network of about 2,125 active dealers to the retail market, and exporting cables to markets including the Middle East, UAE, Australia, Spain, the UK, the Caribbean and the United States. It also takes on turnkey EPC (engineering, procurement, construction) contracts and runs a small stainless steel wire business. Revenue was roughly INR11,746 crores in FY26 (up about 21%) with profit after tax of about INR918 crores; manufacturing plants are in Rajasthan (Bhiwadi, Chopanki, Pathredi), Silvassa/Chinchpada and a large new plant at Sanand in Gujarat.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Sales through distribution network (B2C / dealer sales)** — House wires and cables sold through an all-India dealer network (about 2,125 active dealers as of March 2026) — the largest and fastest-growing part of the business at INR6,349 crores in FY26, roughly 54-56% of total sales and growing about 25-29% a year.
- **Domestic institutional cable sales (B2B, wires and cables)** — Direct sales of low-tension and high-tension power cables to Indian industrial and infrastructure customers — INR2,688 crores in FY26; together with EHV and exports this institutional bucket is about 40-42% of sales.
- **Extra high-voltage (EHV) cables** — High-end cables for power transmission at very high voltages (management cites 220 kV to 400 kV, first Indian supplier of 330 kV cable to Australia and the only Indian firm qualified in the UK National Grid framework) — a small but fast-growing and prestigious line at INR559 crores domestically in FY26, up 82%.
- **Export sales** — Cables shipped to overseas customers in the Middle East, Australia, Spain, the UK, the Caribbean/West Indies and the US — INR1,833 crores in FY26, up 45%, which management wants to take to roughly 20% of total sales.
- **EPC (engineering, procurement, construction) other than cable** — Turnkey project contracts, including donor-funded overseas work (an ADB-funded Nepal project and a World Bank-funded Gambia project were in the order book) and EHV cable-laying projects — a small line at INR311 crores in FY26, and a shrinking share of the company.
- **Stainless steel wire division** — A separate small manufacturing line making stainless steel wire, essentially flat at around INR212 crores a year — under 2% of sales.
- **House wire division / manufacturing capacity** — The plants themselves (Bhiwadi, Chopanki, Pathredi in Rajasthan; Silvassa/Chinchpada; and the new Sanand plant in Gujarat) are the engine of growth — Sanand cost about INR2,000 crores, started phase-1 production in December 2025, and is expected to add roughly INR5,000-6,000 crores of annual sales once fully ramped by around FY29.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

KEI is a cable and wire maker whose strengths sit in two places: how it sells and how it pays for growth. Distribution is the standout — active dealers grew from 1,800 to 2,125, backed by about 26 stocking depots and a pan-India sales team, and the retail-facing share of sales rose from 38% in the first quarter of FY23 to 56% by the fourth quarter of FY26, with that business growing 25-29% a year. Alongside it, exports built patiently on country-by-country certifications went from roughly ₹700cr of guidance in FY23 to ₹1,833cr in FY26, including first-time entries such as 330 kV in Australia, 132 kV in the UAE, Spain, the US, the Caribbean and qualification with National Grid in the UK. Net sales rose from ₹8,104cr in FY24 to ₹11,746cr in FY26, faster than the roughly 15% a year management sizes the industry at, which is why the Market Share Gainers pattern is a strong fit.

The financing side reinforces this and is why Corporate Culture also scores as a strong fit. Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.31 of sales, return on capital employed is 27.4%, and the whole expansion has been organic and paid for out of operating cash — net debt of just ₹32cr in the first quarter of FY23, and ₹700cr of cash against ₹134cr of borrowings in FY24. Growth spending stepped up from ₹397cr in FY24 to about ₹1,000cr in FY25 and ₹928cr in the first nine months of FY26, mainly the ₹2,000cr Sanand plant, aimed at 3-3.5 times asset turns and ₹5,000-6,000cr of extra revenue. The same leadership has been in place throughout with the same finance head, guidance given in 2022 was delivered, and management openly disclosed the six-month Sanand delay, the lost March exports, and the fact that a capacity shortage let Havells grow faster than they did.

The problem is that the very traits behind those strengths also create the biggest risks. Selling through dealers is what powers growth, but roughly 100 of the 2,125 dealers are said to drive 70-80% of sales, and house wire and ordinary low-tension cable are standard-specification products where dealer margin and stock availability decide the sale — which is why Good Enough Goods is rated elevated and Brand Strength and Friendly Middleman only partially fit. That same wire segment, now more than half of sales, is exactly where a wave of well-funded new entrants (Torrent, Surya Roshni, Luker) has arrived. Prices are a straight pass-through of copper and aluminium, reset fortnightly and written into tender clauses, so Pricing Power is no fit and cyclicality is elevated — part of the 20.66% FY26 sales growth is simply metal inflation. Add policy-created demand (grid reform schemes, rural and railway electrification, public-sector tenders) and the weakest quality area is industry structure. Resilience is still judged sound, but the combined score of 61 out of 100 — 70 on business quality, 43 on multibagger fit, 68 on risk safety — lands at Decent rather than exceptional, because there is no pricing power, no repeat-revenue stream and no direct route to the end customer to protect the returns.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Across all 15 calls demand is framed in terms of customer capex programmes (solar developers, T&D utilities, RDSS, thermal/pumped storage, real estate, data centres) and headline growth is substantially metal-price inflation — FY26 net sales grew 20.66% while net metal volume grew only 6.21% and copper averaged +16.85%; KEI is also committing ~INR2,000 crores of greenfield capex (Sanand) plus new Bhiwadi/Baroda land at a visible cycle peak. Bounded, however, because margins have not swung with the cycle — EBITDA margin rose steadily from ~10.4% (FY23) to 11.81% (FY26) through both copper up- and down-moves — and B2C distribution is now 54-56% of sales.

> "the net volume increase was 6.21%" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Price-variation clauses and natural hedge (3-4 month order book vs 2.5 month inventory) pass metal moves through; B2C distribution/retail flow sales now 54-56% of revenue vs 42% in FY23; export and geographic diversification 'to insulate... from any risk in the demand cycle in any particular geography'; countercyclical capacity build funded from operating cash with near-nil net debt*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 11 years with swings of ±13 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 10% and 11% (±0.3 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 11% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 2% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** House wire and LT cable are BIS-standard, relatively simple products sold through an intermediary channel where dealer margin and stocking drive the choice — management, when asked what makes a dealer stick with KEI, fell back on approvals, brand and depot stocking rather than any product moat, and is simultaneously facing cheaper new wire entrants and 'exchanging from the weak hands to the stronger hands' in the channel. Protection exists at the top end but not across the retail book.

> "it is the approvals with the various end users and also our brand strategy" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Genuine niche/scale protection in EHV — first Indian supplier of 330 kV to Australia, 220 kV to UAE and Spain, only Indian company qualified in the National Grid UK framework to 400 kV — where type tests, plant-specific customer approvals and BIS licensing (which delayed KEI's own Sanand ramp) block good-enough challengers; EHV grew 82% in FY26*

- ⚠️ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs fell from about 32% to 26% — pricing is being competed away at the gross level.
- ✅ Operating margin (10%) remains near its historical peak (11%) — no collapse in the premium.
- ✅ Operating margin held (about 10% then 10%) — no drift down under competition.

## What to watch

- Dealer concentration (Stakeholder Concentration risk): whether the top ~100 dealers' 70-80% share of sales falls and churn stays near 10-12% as the base widens beyond 2,125.
- Wire-segment competition (New Entrants risk): if the 25-29% retail growth or the margin gains slow once Torrent, Surya Roshni and Luker scale up in the segment that is over half of sales.
- Sanand execution (Capex for growth check): whether the ₹2,000cr plant actually delivers 3-3.5 times asset turns and the ₹5,000-6,000cr of extra revenue, after the six-month delay management disclosed.
- Volume versus metal prices (Cyclicality risk): how much of headline growth is real tonnage rather than copper and aluminium inflation passed through in fortnightly resets.
- High-voltage and export franchise (Cost to Replicate pattern, Good Enough Goods silver lining): whether EHV growth of 82-85% and exports at about 20% of sales keep building on approvals rivals cannot quickly obtain.
- Balance-sheet discipline (Disciplined stewards check): any move away from organic, cash-funded growth toward acquisitions or meaningful borrowing.
