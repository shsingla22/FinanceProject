## The verdict: Mixed — 42 out of 100 ★★☆☆☆

In one breath: the quality framework finds an average business today (59/100); no winning pattern is strongly confirmed yet; the risk review found 2 high risks (Cyclicality, Government Dependency). Weighing those together gives 42 out of 100 — mixed.

**The exact arithmetic:** Overall = 45% × 59 (business quality) + 30% × 10 (multibagger fit) + 25% × 48 (risk safety) = 42 out of 100 → Mixed (2 stars).

- **Business quality (59/100):** The 34-check quality framework scored the business +0.35 on its −2 (poor) to +2 (excellent) scale, with 91% of checks backed by evidence; mapped onto 0–100 that is 59 points.
- **Multibagger fit (10/100):** the foundation test partly passed (+10); no pattern found meaningful support (+0) → 10 of 100.
- **Risk safety (48/100):** started from a clean 100; 2 high risks (Cyclicality, Government Dependency) (−40); 3 worth watching (−12) → 48 of 100.

### The story in depth

Thermax's strongest suit is the people running it and the way the business is funded. Management scores highest of any area in the quality framework, and the reasons are concrete: across four calls they own bad news rather than bury it — losses and reserves on FGD, a chemicals business that 'went through hell', a cost overrun at First Energy caused by a failed contractor, and a build-up in working capital in FY26 admitted once an analyst pointed at it. They grow organically, explicitly avoid acquisitions, hold hard return floors, and have walked away from underpriced work including the largest project in the company's history. The balance sheet reflects the same discipline: cash comes back from customers one day before suppliers have to be paid, so customers effectively fund the business. On top of that, customers pay a small premium for engineering certainty and reliable execution — the 'last look' worth about 2-3 paise in solar — which is the clearest single strength in the whole record. Operating profit runs at about Rs.10 on every Rs.100 of sales and has moved only about a point either way each year, so costs are visibly under control.

The trouble is that the same trait that produces the strengths also caps them, and this shows up as a screen where not one of the eleven patterns is a clean fit — six come out partial, five don't fit at all, and multibagger fit scores 10 out of 100. The trust-and-execution advantage supports partial marks on brand strength and market share gains, backed by roughly 40% share in biomass and multifuel and over 90% in on-site energy, plus new wins in data-centre cooling, a Rs.450 crore Middle East order and a Dangote order. But management itself says there is no proprietary technology a competitor cannot access, which is exactly why the cost-to-replicate pattern is ruled out and why 'good enough goods' sits on the risk watch list: a 2-3% premium, project margins structurally capped at 5-8% and government tenders decided purely on lowest price mean a cheaper rival can take work. Pricing power is ruled out for the same underlying reason — margins are set by commodity swings rather than by the company, with steel, styrene and nickel taking 6% off the bottom line in one quarter on fixed-price contracts and styrene doing it again years later. Meanwhile the willingness to walk away from thin-margin work, which earns full marks under disciplined stewardship, is precisely why the low-price-high-volume pattern doesn't fit either. The move downstream into owning and operating assets — TOESL steam sold per use, First Energy solar and wind, about Rs.400 crore invested and roughly 250 MW on the ground — is the most interesting bridge in the record: it partially delivers the forward-integration and recurring-revenue patterns and is the main defence against cyclicality, yet it is also where capital gets tied up, and incremental returns have disappointed. Headline return on capital scores negatively at Rs.7 of operating cash per Rs.100 of capital, and the return-on-capital area scores zero.

The risk review reinforces rather than contradicts this picture. Two channels are rated high risk and both trace back to what the company sells: equipment bought out of other people's capital budgets in steel, cement, refining, petrochemicals and power, so orders and margins ride those capex waves; and policy that decides the fate of whole product lines, with a Rs.1,800 crore FGD backlog stalling and bleeding margin after ministry correspondence let customers slow down, steel export duty deferring large orders, and coal gasification hinging on policy. Three more sit on watch: the technology shift around a fossil-fired installed base, dependence on a handful of lumpy named contracts whose absence management says 'will hurt our ability to show good growth', and the good-enough-goods problem above. Two are low and one shows no signal, and each high or watch item comes with a real offset drawn from the same strengths — a growing flow-based mix of chemicals consumables and services, spares and annual maintenance compounding at 20-30%, the annuity assets, a preference for private-sector customers, and a deliberate base of Rs.10-200 crore orders spread across sectors and geographies. Overall resilience is judged sound. The combined rating lands at 42 out of 100 — 45% weight on business quality at 59, 30% on multibagger fit at 10, and 25% on risk safety at 48 — a Mixed, two-star verdict. In short: a well-run, honestly managed, customer-funded engineering business with genuine niche positions, held back by a competitive advantage its own management describes as reputation rather than technology, and by returns on capital that have not yet followed the capital being deployed.

*"Sells once, earns forever"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (2 of 3 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Management has consistently pushed services/AMC/spares off an installed base — services grew 30% in FY22 and ~20%+ thereafter, and the H/H-live digital platform was built specifically to reconnect with an installed base of which two-thirds had been lost; the projects business is also described as running on negative working capital. But service revenue is never quantified as a share, no attach or renewal rates are given, and by Q4 FY26 working capital had built up on collection delays rather than being customer-funded.

> "focus really deeply on the services part of our portfolio - bring digital in... have AMC capability, rapid spare parts... services have been growing for us at above 20%. Last year it grew 30%" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Customers effectively fund the business — cash arrives 1 day before suppliers are paid.
- ❌ Capital spending is about 9% of sales — growth is capital-hungry.
- ✅ Operating cash was positive in 11 of 12 years — the reliable cash engine recurring-revenue businesses show.

## What to watch
