### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 7 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 6. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 2 weakened, 4 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: QUANT SIGNAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Capex is going into surgical robots, MRIs and cath labs to lift case-mix complexity toward tertiary/quaternary work, which is the ARPOB driver at Indore — but this is bought equipment, not disclosed or growing R&D, and no named innovation pipeline is discussed. (The long view had said: all 2 computable checks point this way but the calls offer no evidence — numbers alone are only ever a screening hint, never a confirmed fit.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The four FY26 calls discuss occupancy, ARPOB, ALOS and payer mix only; there is no discussion of subscriptions, AMC/service contracts, attach rates, renewals or customer advances as a funding source. (The long view had said: Across all 11 calls revenue is described as episodic, per-encounter hospital care with no licence, subscription, AMC or renewal stream; growth is funded by capex and internal accruals, not customer prepayments, and no deferred revenue or attach-rate metric is ever discussed.)

**That weakened:**

- **Friendly Middleman**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Doctors are the effective channel — Jupiter courts them ahead of each launch and pays them on minimum-guarantee/variable models rather than fixed wages, and 55% of revenue is paid by insurers rather than the patient — but the calls never describe trade training programmes or channel-relationship investment. (The long view had said: Doctors are the de-facto referrers and insurers pay ~55% of revenue, and management's site-selection logic is explicitly about attracting affiliated consultants with better infrastructure — but there is no trade-training programme or channel-investment language, and insurers negotiate rates hard (biennial rate revisions).)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Management explicitly positions the opposite: an asset-heavy, owned-real-estate, top-end-technology model targeting quality/tertiary care with rising ARPOB, not scale-driven low price. (The long view had said: There is genuine capital-cost discipline — Dombivli delivered 500 beds in ~24 months at INR425 crores with no escalation, Indore beds at ~INR30 lakh/bed, and the whole model is built around the insured population to solve affordability — but no consumer-facing low-price positioning, no itemised opex savings culture, and margins are mid-20s rather than thin-margin/high-turn.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Market Share Gainers (LIKELY FIT); Forward Integrators (QUANT SIGNAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
