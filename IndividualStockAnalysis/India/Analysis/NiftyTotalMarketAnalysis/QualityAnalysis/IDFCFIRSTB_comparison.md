**Checks that improved:**

- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Good. Now: A 1,000-branch national footprint plus digital reach built from a standing start in seven years is real distribution scale, though it does not yet rival the top private banks' networks. | The long view had said: 961 branches plus a strong digital channel give respectable reach, but it is a fraction of large private-bank networks and branch additions have slowed sharply (only 17 opened in a half-year), so rivals can match it.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: Convenience is the actual moat being built — 1,000+ branches, a strong mobile app, hyper-personalization and relationship managers producing 50.8% CASA, the clearest evidence of customer primacy in daily banking. | The long view had said: Accessibility is a genuine strength — 961 branches, a well-regarded app, digital onboarding and call-centre stack, with fee income up 22.9% on 25%+ disbursement growth indicating an effective distribution engine.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: Growth is overwhelmingly volume-led (20%+ book growth) with margin gains coming from falling cost of funds rather than pricing power — NIM rose only 5bps adjusted, and the mix is deliberately shifting toward lower-yield mortgages and LAP. | The long view had said: Growth is overwhelmingly volume-led: NIM has compressed from 6.18% (Q2FY25) to 5.96%, savings rates were cut to 3%, and mix has shifted toward lower-yield secured/mortgage/corporate lending.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Neutral → Weak. Now: The only substantive people evidence in the window is negative — branch employees colluding in a INR590 crore fraud — with management offering culture assertions rather than any talent-pipeline system. | The long view had said: Management repeatedly credits employees and stresses hiring quality technology people, but no systematic talent-development programme or internal succession pipeline is described across the four calls.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: Management stakes its positioning on trust and customer-first products and cites employee pride in service levels, but the Chandigarh fraud directly undercut the reliability proposition in the same window. | The long view had said: Depositors are buying safety over rate — CRISIL upgraded the FD programme to AAA and retail deposits compounded 37% even after the savings rate was cut, showing trust dominating price.
- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Weak. Now: Pricing power runs the wrong way — the bank had to overpay depositors and is now migrating loan mix toward 8%-9% mortgages, indicating commoditized, rate-driven demand rather than image-based pricing power. | The long view had said: The customer-first, no-asterisks, no-hidden-fees positioning builds goodwill but management explicitly declines to monetise it, and NIM is drifting down rather than showing pricing power.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: No regional monopoly or captive aftermarket; deposits are rate-shopped nationally and the bank has had to reprice buckets explicitly to control who stays. | The long view had said: There is genuine deposit stickiness — retail deposits still grew 37% after the savings rate was cut to 3% — but no regional monopoly or contractual switching-cost lock-in.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Weak → Poor. Now: A listed, heavily analyst-covered bank running a high-profile brand campaign of customer-first positioning and holding ad-hoc media and investor calls is the opposite of an ignored niche. | The long view had said: A listed, heavily covered bank with brokerage-hosted quarterly calls, rating-agency scrutiny and constant media attention on its legacy exposures — nothing under the radar.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Organic focus and prudent provisioning (a voluntary INR515 crores contingency buffer) are offsets to a business that has repeatedly needed external capital and has just absorbed a INR590 crore fraud loss revealing control gaps. | The long view had said: Organic-only growth, deliberate exit from infrastructure lending, repayment of high-cost legacy borrowings and voluntary counter-cyclical provisioning of INR515 crore show prudence, though repeated dilutive capital raising tempers the score.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Branch build-out is domestic and largely complete at ~1,050 branches with NRI banking mentioned only in passing; no credible new geographic expansion narrative in the window. | The long view had said: Credible, well-executed domestic footprint expansion (651→961 branches) plus an NRI deposit franchise of INR25,000 crore, but this is national branch build-out, not a premium brand travelling into new markets.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Weak → Poor. Now: Indian banking is intensely contested and the bank itself concedes it competes on price-parity with mid-tier peers, having had to pay 7% on savings to attract deposits — the opposite of a monopoly position in the customer's mind. | The long view had said: Retail banking products here — mortgages, vehicle loans, cards, deposits — are commoditised with no aftermarket or mind-share monopoly; the bank competes as a challenger on service, not position.

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
