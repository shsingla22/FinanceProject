### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 3 worsened, 2 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The customer base is explicitly broadening rather than narrowing: million-dollar customers rose from 212 (FY21) to 290, the $10m+ cohort doubled from 14 to 30, and FY26 wins are spread across geographies and verticals (German manufacturer, UK wine major, APAC banks, Indian fund house); no single named customer or licensor is described as driving results. (The long view had said: Strategy is deliberately to concentrate — 'deeper with fewer' is repeated every year, growth leans on large marquee deals whose closure timing swings order booking, and single-relationship events move segments (JLR/MOVE SOTA campaigns, 'customer specific churn' in cloud, 'customer specific issues in layer 1'). This is bounded by an unusually broad underlying base of 300+ Fortune 500 customers across many geographies.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Management itself catalogues platform shifts hitting the installed base: UCaaS/cloud communications halved GSIP revenue (~30% of digital revenues), and the MPLS/VPN legacy estate is being self-cannibalised into cheaper internet-based hybrid WAN; the whole FY26 narrative (Digital Fabric, AI Cloud, agentic Voice AI) is a defensive-plus-offensive re-architecture against AI-era platform change. (The long view had said: The record repeatedly shows the installed base being reset by platform shifts: management concedes 'structural pricing decline seen in the legacy businesses', CPaaS growth is capped by SMS being displaced by newer channels, media customers are migrating off linear formats, and by Apr 2026 the incoming CEO is rebuilding the pitch around AI-era infra while admitting readiness gaps in sales enablement and the Commotion AI OS. Years of digital investment still ran EBITDA-negative, showing how costly keeping pace is.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Management concedes persistent 10-15% annual price erosion and churn on core connectivity — 'the bulk of the revenue' — and that customers accept cheaper internet in place of premium-priced MPLS, the classic good-enough substitution; core connectivity consequently grew only ~0.9% year-on-year in Q2 FY26 despite volume, and IoT pricing was revised down even as SIMs grew 21%. (The long view had said: Core connectivity faces a textbook good-enough attack — public internet, broadband and 4G/5G substituting premium private circuits — and management openly acknowledges ongoing 'structural pricing decline' in legacy businesses while citing 'price competitiveness' as a growth lever; core connectivity growth has stayed at 2-3% while digital carries the top line. Exposure is bounded because the buyer is a sophisticated enterprise CIO with high brand awareness, not an uninformed end-customer steered by an intermediary.)
- **New Entrants**: NO SIGNAL → WATCH. Why now: Structurally different competitors — cloud/UCaaS communications platforms and hyperscaler-adjacent providers — have already reset the economics of the voice/GSIP business, and Tata must now compete in AI cloud and CPaaS against far larger platform players; it is bounded because Tata is itself the displacer in core network deals. (The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and all 2 computed fingerprints are clean.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
