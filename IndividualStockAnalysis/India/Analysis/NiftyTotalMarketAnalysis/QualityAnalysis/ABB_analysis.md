# ABB India Ltd. (ABB) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: INDUSTRIAL MANUFACTURING*

ABB India makes and sells the electrical and automation equipment that powers and runs factories, buildings and infrastructure — everything from 'substation to socket' power gear (switchgear, compact substations, low- and medium-voltage distribution products, UPS), to electric motors, drives and generators, to process control systems, robots and factory automation. Customers are industrial and infrastructure buyers across 23 market segments — data centres, railways and metros, renewables, buildings and infrastructure, metals and mining, energy, petrochemicals, autos, electronics, food and beverage — served by 16-20 distinct product divisions (the number has been consolidated over the years). It earns money mainly by booking orders and converting a backlog into product and project revenue, plus a recurring services stream; roughly 41% of business comes through 750+ channel partners and integrators and 59% direct. It manufactures in India at 5 locations (Nashik, Faridabad, Baroda and two Bengaluru campuses) with 25 shop floors, sells through 28 sales offices nationwide, and exports about 10% of revenue to 30-plus countries; CY2025 revenue was about INR 13,203 crores with orders of INR 14,115 crores, PAT of INR 1,669 crores and a backlog of INR 10,471 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Electrification** — Sells the full chain of power distribution products — 'substation to socket' — including low- and medium-voltage switchgear, compact substations, smart power and installation products for cities, buildings, data centres and infrastructure; management calls it part of the core portfolio that makes up a major share of revenue, and it is the segment most tied to the fast-growing data centre and building/infrastructure demand.
- **Motion** — The energy-efficiency portfolio — electric motors, generators, variable-speed drives, mechanical power transmission products and related services, used in pumps, HVAC and cooling, metals plants, and railway/metro propulsion; described alongside Electrification as a major part of the revenue stream, and the business behind new lines like energy-efficient drives and the first locally made wind power converter.
- **Process Automation** — Supplies process control systems, SCADA, remote terminal units, leak detection and integrated electrification-plus-digital solutions for process and hybrid industries such as oil and gas, city gas distribution, petrochemicals, energy, and metals — typically larger, longer-cycle projects rather than short-cycle products.
- **Robotics & Discrete Automation** — Sells industrial robots and factory/machine automation — palletising and end-of-line packaging for FMCG and food and beverage, electronics and mobile-phone assembly, and automotive paint shops and new auto plants; a smaller, value-added solutions business that management describes as expanding beyond its automotive base.
- **Automation (combined)** — In the more recent calls (2025-2026) Process Automation and Robotics & Discrete Automation are presented together under a single President for 'Automation', reflecting management's framing of the whole company as 'electrification and automation'.
- **Services** — Aftermarket service and support attached to the installed base, sold close to the customer through the sales offices and channel partners; flagged as a distinct growth line (services revenue grew 14% in Q2 CY2022) though no size is disclosed in the calls.
- **Exports** — Sales from Indian manufacturing to 30-plus countries, growing fast (up 45% in Q2 CY2022 and 23% in Q1 CY2024) but still around 10% of the portfolio because domestic growth has been stronger.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

ABB India comes out of these three reviews as a well-run, cash-rich franchise whose best qualities are about how it serves customers rather than how cheaply it can produce. The quality framework gives its highest marks to customer benefits and management: customers buy certainty rather than the lowest price, and management deliberately absorbed higher imported-material cost to guarantee reliable QCO-compliant supply, winning base-order growth because of it. Reach is unusually dense for the category — 5 plants, 25 shop floors, 28 customer care offices and 750-plus partners who handle about 41% of sales alongside 59% sold direct, with deliberate push into smaller cities. The financial housekeeping matches: about ₹42 of every ₹100 of sales is kept after direct input costs, only about 21 days of cash sits between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and the balance sheet carries no debt and a net cash pile of roughly ₹5,700-7,600 crore. Management's candour is documented rather than assumed — the 2022 admission of sub-10% profit before tax, the reconciliation of group versus India orders, and the Q1 2026 margin miss broken out into competition, commodity and mix. That combination is exactly why the multibagger screen rates Corporate Culture a strong fit: the same leadership team has fronted all 17 calls, promised a 10% profit floor in 2022 and delivered 16.9% profit before tax and 21% return on capital employed by CY2025.

The patterns and the risks are largely two readings of the same handful of traits. The premium brand — 140 years as a group, 75 years manufacturing in India, best-in-class supplier status with hyperscalers, railways and metals customers — is what makes Brand Strength a likely fit and gives the roughly 40% gross margin its support. But the risk review reads the same fact from the other side: management has to keep explicitly defending that premium against cheaper alternatives, which confirms those alternatives are pressing, and the pandemic-era price premiumisation has now stabilised away. That is why Pricing Power scores only a partial fit and why both Good Enough Goods and New Entrants sit on watch, with competition quantified as roughly 1 percentage point of the 3.5-point material-cost deterioration in Q1 2026. The same doubling applies elsewhere. The partner network that earns full marks for convenience and distribution is what supports the Friendly Middleman pattern and also keeps stakeholder concentration risk low, because no single customer, licensor or supplier drives results across 23 market segments. The steady drumbeat of product launches — the ACH180 compact drive, the next-generation machinery drive, the ARTU Formula LV platform, the first locally made wind-power converter, plus a fresh $75mn manufacturing and R&D commitment — is incremental rather than transformative, so Innovation Dominance is only a partial fit while technology disruption risk is judged low. And the QCO import rules that let ABB turn supply reliability into an order win are also part of why government dependency stays on watch.

The main tension is growth: the end markets are genuinely attractive but the demand is lumpy. The portfolio sits on electrification and automation, data centres, railways and metros, and renewables, with a 5-year order growth rate of 16% a year and backlog growth of 21% — a full-marks structural growth score. Yet the framework marks down persistence of growth: sales have averaged about 21% a year but arrive unevenly. That is the same fact the risk review calls elevated cyclicality, since revenue depends on customers' own capital spending programmes and management concedes the market took a breather. The cushions are real — 16 to 20 divisions across 23 segments, an explicit refusal to chase hot segments at the expense of slower ones, net cash, and a stated willingness to keep investing in capacity through the soft patch — which is why overall resilience is judged sound and risk safety scores 78. The gaps are that returns on capital score lowest of the seven quality areas, capex is only partially credited as growth spending because it looks modest against the ₹6,000-7,000 crore revenue base, three of the eleven patterns are outright no-fits (toll roads, low price plus, forward integrators) and seven more are only partial, leaving multibagger fit at 48. The blended rating of 64 — 45% weight on business quality at 68, 30% on multibagger fit at 48, 25% on risk safety at 78 — lands at Decent, three stars: a durable, honestly managed business whose case rests on customer trust and reach rather than on any single unbreakable moat.
