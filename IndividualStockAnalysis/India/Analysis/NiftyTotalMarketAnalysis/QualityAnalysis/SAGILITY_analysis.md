# Sagility (SAGILITY) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Sagility is an outsourcing company that runs back-office and clinical support work for the US healthcare industry, and does nothing else — 100% of its business is US healthcare. Its customers are health insurance companies ("payers", including five of the top 10 US payers and three of the top six pharmacy benefit managers) and healthcare providers (large hospital systems, labs, radiology and medical-equipment companies), 109 client groups as of Q1 FY27, with the top five having been clients for an average of about 17 years. It makes money by being paid to process work for these clients — handling claims reviews and appeals, recovering overpayments, nurse-led clinical reviews, enrolment and member calls — with pricing increasingly shifting toward managed-service deals where Sagility takes ownership of a whole process and commits to outcomes at lower cost. Work is delivered from five countries: onshore in the US, nearshore in Jamaica and Colombia, and offshore in India and the Philippines, where about 94% of its ~47,000 staff sit, which is why margins run near 24–26% EBITDA on FY26 revenue of $814 million.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Payer segment** — Services sold to US health insurers — by far the main business at roughly 89–90% of revenue (89.7% in FY26), growing about 24% year-on-year, and with a seasonal bulge in Q3/Q4 from the annual enrolment/open enrolment period (about 6% of FY26 revenue).
- **Claims** — Handling the roughly 15–25% of insurance claims that do not clear the payer's automatic system — reviews, rework, and member/provider grievances and appeals — described by management as a critical, core part of what it does.
- **Payment Integrity** — Finding and clawing back money the insurer overpaid on claims; capability was boosted by the acquisition of a company called DCI, and it is called out as unique to healthcare.
- **Clinical services** — Nurses and other clinicians doing prior authorisation, concurrent and post-claim medical review to control current spend, plus chronic/complex case management and population health to head off future high-cost patients — flagged as a fast-growing area because of the US clinician shortage; the group employs around 2,000 clinicians.
- **Other administrative services** — The remaining back-office work for insurers — member enrolment, plan building, premium collection and managing the provider network.
- **Provider segment** — Similar operations work for hospital systems (IDNs), labs, radiology and durable-medical-equipment firms, including revenue cycle work; the smaller side at about 10–11.5% of revenue, growing fast (33.4% year-on-year in Q2 FY26) though its share fell in FY26 because acquisitions skewed payer-heavy.
- **BroadPath (acquisition)** — An acquired business that adds seasonal and member-facing operations, especially during peak enrolment; it drove much of the reported (vs organic) growth in FY26 and is a source of cross-sell wins into its existing payer clients.
- **CareSeed (acquisition, closed June 2026)** — A small Kansas City company doing HEDIS quality reporting and medical-record review through two platforms (Forecast and Harvest) — about $5.1 million of 2025 revenue at a 31.4% margin, bringing 26 small/mid-sized payer clients each under $1 million of revenue, and strengthening Sagility's quality/star-ratings offering for Medicare Advantage plans.
- **Sagility Synchrony / technology and AI** — An AI-led platform and suite of outcome-based solutions that ties together fragmented claims and adjustment workflows; management presents it as the vehicle for larger managed-service contracts and says GenAI-led efficiency helps both client costs and Sagility's own margins.

### The story in depth

The strongest thing about Sagility is that it is taking business away from others in a market that is itself getting bigger, and doing it while keeping money on the table. Client groups went from 45 in November 2024 to 82 in September 2025 to 109 by June 2026, clients billing over $20 million a year rose from four in FY23 to nine in FY26, and revenue grew 29% in FY26 (20% of that from existing operations rather than purchases). That sits inside a US healthcare market of $4.7 trillion moving from 17% to 18% of the economy, where only about a fifth of the $200 billion of core back-office work is outsourced at all — and Sagility's roughly $814 million of revenue is a small slice of even the $45 billion already outsourced. This is why the Market Share Gainers pattern is a strong fit and the industry checks on end-market growth and share gain both score at the top. It is backed by a management team the record treats as unusually straight: guidance was raised mid-year from 20% to 21%-plus and then beaten at 23.6% in constant currency with 25.3% adjusted profit margins, the debt plan has been followed since FY25 with borrowings now negligible at 0.06 times, and management volunteers bad news — including that its own AI will eat its own revenue. That record is what makes Corporate Culture a strong fit and gives the Management area a perfect score.

The trouble is that the single trait powering the growth is also the source of the biggest risks. Sagility wins by taking cost out of a client's administrative base — 94% of its people sit offshore or nearshore, and to win work it now contracts to hand clients 20% to 40% of savings and bills AI-handled transactions at a fraction of human rates. That structurally low cost with high returns (adjusted profit margins above 25%, return on capital consistently above 50%) is what makes Low Price Plus a partial fit. But the very same fact is why Pricing Power and Toll Roads are marked no fit at all — prices here move down, not up — and why the check on intangible, image-driven pricing power scores negative. It is also why Technological Innovation is a high risk: the work is labour-arbitrage transaction processing sitting directly in the path of generative AI, and management concedes the cannibalisation rather than denying it. The same logic feeds the Good Enough Goods and Shifting Consumer Preferences watch items, where buyers are moving from paying per person or per transaction toward paying for outcomes with guaranteed savings attached. Sagility's own AI build — Sagi360, Synchrony, SmarTec Nurse Assist, 25 use cases across nine clients, plus the BirchAI and CareSeed purchases — is simultaneously the defence against this and the instrument of it; the record notes the innovation is defensive and that research spend is never actually quantified, which is why Innovation Dominance only partly fits.

The second doubling-up is in the client base. Very long relationships — an average of 17 years with the top five clients, CareSeed bought with 95% repeat revenue, seasonal enrolment volumes described as recurring — are what make the revenue annuity-like and give the Cost to Replicate and Recurring Revenue patterns their partial credit, alongside 26 years of healthcare-only knowledge, 2,000 clinical staff, accreditations and 47,000 people across five countries that a newcomer cannot simply buy. But depth in a handful of accounts is also the Stakeholder Concentration high risk: the top three clients have only recently dropped below 60% of revenue, and the loss or in-housing of one would hurt badly — the broadening in client count is real but not yet finished. Set against that, the business is genuinely durable in the ordinary sense: it is tied to clients' day-to-day operations rather than their spending plans, cyclicality is rated low, it needs little fixed investment (about ₹745 million of capital spending in a quarter against ₹19,635 million of revenue), and margins have barely wobbled at around ₹23 of operating profit per ₹100 of sales. The weak spots in quality are narrower — about 93 days of cash tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and growth that has averaged very high but arrives in lumps rather than steadily, which scores worst of all the checks. Netting it out, resilience is judged sound and the combined rating is 57 out of 100, or Decent: business quality 65, pattern fit 54, and safety from risk the weakest leg at 48.
