**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: The India-based full value chain (design, pattern making, metallurgy, service) is hard to replicate, and pricing discipline was externally validated when Brazil terminated the antidumping duty and cut CVD from 6.5% to 2.9%. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±3.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Barriers are genuine — metallurgy, design, pattern-making and a full Indian value chain, with no new entrants named in four years and pricing deliberately kept off the table — but antidumping/countervailing duties and protectionism are an escalating structural threat.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Weak. Now: FY26 capex is essentially maintenance plus renewables (INR100–150cr) while the two growth projects in Ghana and China have sat in 'paperwork approval' for four straight quarters and capacity was actually cut by closing Welcast Steels. | The long view had said: Sustained, self-funded growth capex across the period — 50k t mill lining plant, 80k t grinding media (~Rs 250cr), Rs 65cr composite/rubber brownfield, plus Ghana/China plants — but by FY26 capacity utilisation is only ~55% and capex has fallen back to maintenance-plus-renewables, so incremental ROIC on the recent build is unproven.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: Forged-media incumbents across an 800k–1mn ton gold/copper pool are the identified donators, with the Chile mine being the first ever to switch from forged to hi-chrome. | The long view had said: The entire growth thesis is displacing an inferior incumbent product class — local forged grinding media producers — who cannot match throughput, power and recovery benefits.

Now: Global service and metallurgy reach is a genuine asset, but with all production in India, shipping volatility and country-by-country duties are actively impeding delivery and the offshore plants meant to fix this are stalled. | The long view had said: Global reach across ~140 countries with local operations in Brazil, Canada and South Africa and warehousing near customers, but the India-centric manufacturing base leaves it structurally exposed to freight, containers and duties.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: The hi-chrome niche is concentrated, but the addressable 800k–1mn ton pool is still served by fragmented forged-media suppliers and customers have shown they can simply stay with the incumbent, as four quarters of flat volume show. | The long view had said: High-chrome grinding media is effectively a two-player global market (AIA and Magotteaux) supplying large, sophisticated miners, but those customers are concentrated global majors with real negotiating power and slow decision cycles.

Now: Customers are corporate mining operators and the pitch is correctly built on TCO and reliability — throughput, power cost, metal recovery — rather than brand intangibles. | The long view had said: Correctly matched to a corporate/industrial customer base: the pitch is total cost of ownership — throughput, power, recovery, reagent cost — rather than product price or brand.

Now: The end market expands secularly because worsening ore grades force more tonnage to be milled per unit of metal, on top of decades of copper demand ahead. | The long view had said: The core driver is permanent and secular: falling ore grades force miners to process more tonnage per unit of metal, mechanically raising grinding media consumption, on top of the forged-to-chrome conversion runway.

Now: Positions itself as effectively the sole supplier solving the grade-decline/throughput bottleneck for gold and copper miners via engineered hi-chrome solutions. | The long view had said: Management claims, consistently across years, to be the only supplier able to offer the design-and-solution package for ball mills, competing on throughput and power savings rather than product price.

Now: Customers buy certainty of throughput, power reduction and recovery rather than price, and are conservative enough to demand multi-quarter trials before switching. | The long view had said: Customers buy proven performance and certainty of supply — management states conversion is 'surely not a price' discussion, and the multi-year trial-to-order cycle shows trust, not price, gates the sale.

Now: Organic focus with zero acquisitions, INR4,300cr net cash and no leverage, and willingness to shut a subpar subsidiary plant rather than chase volume. | The long view had said: Purely organic growth, zero acquisitions, net cash of ~Rs 4,300cr with no leverage, capacity built two years ahead of demand and capex tapped down when conversion slowed — textbook counter-cyclical prudence.

- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. ### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 3 weakened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Management names specific engineered products each year with claimed customer benefit — the 75,000-ton mid-liner line, the 'new generation discharge system' win in South America, integrated liner-plus-media packages — but no R&D spend, R&D-to-sales ratio or share of industry R&D is disclosed in any of the four calls, and the innovation has not yet converted into volume growth. (The long view had said: There is a steady, self-funded cadence of named product development — 50,000t mill-lining plant (2022), in-house composite/rubber liners (Rs.65 cr, Aug 2024), and the "new generation discharge system" whose 2-year customer trial succeeded in May 2026 — with claimed global uniqueness. But R&D spend is never disclosed or quantified, and the innovation has not yet translated into volume growth, which was flat across FY25-FY26.)
- **Low Price Plus**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: There is a visible cost culture — captive renewables taken to ~60-65% of power explicitly for cost saving, closure of the sub-scale Welcast Steels plant, and India's ecosystem cited as a structural cost/value-chain edge — but AIA runs 28% operating (40% reported) EBITDA margins on a differentiated product, not the low-margin/high-turns model, and never describes passing savings into price. (The long view had said: The opposite model: AIA sells a higher-cost chrome product against cheaper forged media and asks the customer to pay a delta (including ~$300/ton freight vs $100 pre-COVID) in exchange for process benefits. Margins are high (25-28% operating, 40% reported in Q3 FY26) and asset turns low — capacity utilisation just 55% in FY26 — the inverse of the low-margin/high-turns profile.)

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Stable long-tenured leadership (same Majmudar/Shah duo all four calls), a very conservative balance sheet (INR4,083cr rising to INR4,300cr net cash, capex restricted to maintenance and renewables), candid refusal to give guidance it cannot back, and willingness to shut a sub-scale plant (Welcast) — but the growth promised in Aug 2025 ('return back to a decent level of volume growth from the next fiscal') and the 5,000-15,000 ton uplift hinted in Nov 2025 were not delivered, with FY26 ending flat. (The long view had said: The same two managers have run every call since 2022 and repeatedly volunteer bad news rather than spin it — admitting the logistics problem was "far more worrisome than what it appeared", withdrawing guidance instead of repeating an unmeetable number, shutting the loss-making Welcast plant, and refusing to extrapolate the FY26 win. The balance sheet stayed conservative throughout (net cash ~Rs.2,065 cr in 2022 to ~Rs.4,300 cr in FY26) with capex funded organically and a Rs.500 cr buyback.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: A concrete replication barrier is articulated — the full India-based chain of metallurgy, design, pattern making, production and on-site service, 436,000 tons of capacity, and conversions that take two years of in-mill iteration per customer. It falls short of strong because the same calls show the advantage is location-exposed: 50% US Section 232 duty, antidumping cases and shipping fragility are pushing AIA itself to build plants in Ghana and China to stay reachable. (The long view had said: The articulable barrier is mine-specific solution engineering that cannot be bought: design, pattern-making and metallurgy built over decades, plus per-customer trial cycles that take years (the May 2026 discharge-system win came after "over 2 years, we work with that customer... doing multiple rounds of supply"), on top of 436,000 tons of high-chrome capacity. A funded copycat would need both the India-based ecosystem and the accumulated per-mill process data; the caveat is that freight and antidumping duties, not competitor capability, are what actually cap the advantage.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across the four calls realization moved INR170 → INR163 → ~INR165 for the full year with no price increase ever discussed; the Q4 jump to INR178 was explicitly attributed to rupee depreciation and casting mix and flagged as a one-off, while antidumping/CVD proceedings in Brazil and the US show price is contested, not commanded. (The long view had said: Realisation rose from ~Rs.120 to Rs.150+ (FY23) to Rs.165 (FY26) and the company demonstrably passed through raw material and freight inflation while holding margins — but management itself attributes the moves to cost pass-through, product mix and rupee depreciation, not to real price increases, and volumes were flat (255,000 tons FY25 to 258,000 tons FY26) rather than growing through the price rises.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (QUANT SIGNAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: AIA's premium high-chrome media competes against cheaper forged media that is 'good enough' for many mills, and the last twelve months show how hard the premium is to sell: volumes were flat for a second year, conversions repeatedly slipped ('this process is taking a lot of time'), management cited 'general overall conservatism' among customers, and antidumping cases keep the argument on price. Management is deliberately re-basing the pitch from product price to a liner-plus-media solution measured in throughput and power savings precisely because price-led selling is exposed. (The long view had said: The competing proposition is cheaper forged media from local producers, and AIA's premium must survive a freight delta it cannot control — 'if a freight cost was $100 per ton before COVID... that cost is $300 per ton... that additional cost becomes my delta over a local producer'. Management has spent the last two years deliberately moving the conversation off price, an implicit admission that on price alone the good-enough product wins; in composite/rubber liners it concedes 'there are many people who can offer composite and rubber, but not the design and not the solution'.)

**That worsened:**

- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Access to AIA's key markets is set by government trade policy, and duties dominate the commentary in all four calls: a 50% US Section 232 tariff stacked on ~10% antidumping, a Brazil sunset review that cut CVD from 6.5% to 2.9%, and management repeatedly framing the world as protectionist. Management concedes it cannot forecast the US business under this regime. (The long view had said: No revenue is regulator-priced, but trade policy repeatedly shapes market access and economics: US antidumping/countervailing sunset reviews, Brazil dumping proceedings, and by FY26 an explicitly protectionist world ('duty measures... antidumping measures... Trump and U.S. bringing measures to protect local industry'). Roughly Rs.15-18 crore per quarter of other income is government export benefits (RoDTEP/drawback), and the Ghana plant sits waiting on government clearances.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (QUANT FLAG); New Entrants (NO SIGNAL); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
