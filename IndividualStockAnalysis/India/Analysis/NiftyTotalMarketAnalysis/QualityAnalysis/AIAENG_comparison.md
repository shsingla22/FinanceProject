### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

**Checks that improved:**

- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Weak → Good. Now: The record Q4 PAT is decomposable — INR65cr FX plus INR67cr treasury income on flat volumes — and management's own disclosure makes it possible to separate operating skill (40% reported vs ~28% operating EBITDA) from windfalls. | The long view had said: Record FY26 EBITDA and PAT are flattered by factors outside management skill — Rs 65cr of FX sitting inside EBITDA (4-5% of margin), Rs 67-83cr quarterly treasury income on the cash pile, and a one-off Rs 25-30cr tax benefit — while the core operating metric, tonnage, has not grown in four years.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Entry requires an integrated metallurgy-design-pattern-production ecosystem, and pricing discipline was externally validated when Brazil's sunset review terminated antidumping and cut CVD from 6.5% to 2.9%. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±3.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Barriers are broad (design, pattern-making, metallurgy, service ecosystem — not just casting capacity), no new entrants are cited, ownership is stable, and management deliberately refuses to compete on price.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: AIA's own volumes are at a flat trough while its end market shows an identifiable upturn — surging precious metals and a described structural copper shortage — with a conversion pipeline in advanced trials ahead. | The long view had said: Mining end-markets are described as buoyant with metals surging rather than near a cyclical bottom, and management explicitly says its fortunes are not tied to the size of the pie.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Calls are run by two directors with plain, unpromotional delivery, they withhold a marquee customer's name at its request, and they actively discourage quarter-by-quarter attention-seeking. | The long view had said: Management is low-key and substance-oriented — refusing to hype the first chrome win, declining volume guidance rather than promising numbers, and repeatedly asking analysts not to extrapolate.
- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Worsening ore grades permanently increase tonnes milled per unit of metal produced, which is a secular volume driver for grinding consumables, and management ties its solution directly to that bottleneck. | The long view had said: The structural driver is credible and permanent — falling ore grades mean more ore must be ground per unit of metal, and copper supply must roughly double over 20 years — though four years of flat tonnage show AIA has not yet monetised it.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Enormous stated runway to convert forged grinding media users (800k-1m ton gold/copper market) with a genuine first capture — one Chilean mine at ~15,000 tons/yr — but conversion is slow and volumes stayed flat all year. | The long view had said: The runway is genuine and large (an 800k-1m ton gold/copper market versus ~250k tons of total sales) but actual share capture has stalled — FY26 sales of 258k tons versus 255k in FY25, well short of the 25-35k tons/year conversion promised in 2022.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Weak. Now: Capex in the window is almost entirely maintenance and renewable power (INR100-150cr), the Ghana/China growth plants remain stuck in paperwork with no spend or estimate, and capacity was actually reduced by closing Welcast Steels. | The long view had said: Sustained growth capex (50k t mill-lining plant, 80k t grinding media at ~Rs 250cr, Rs 65cr composite-rubber liner line, Ghana/China plants) was funded and built, but incremental ROIC has been poor — volumes are flat at ~258k tons against 436k capacity and utilisation is only ~55%, with 24k tons of Welcast capacity actually shut.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Realization rose to INR178 in Q4 but management explicitly flagged this as a currency and casting-mix one-off (full-year INR165), so neither pricing nor volume drove real growth — volumes were flat at 258,000 vs 255,000 tons. | The long view had said: With volumes flat, essentially all revenue and record-profit growth came from realisation moving Rs 150 → Rs 165-178/kg on richer casting mix and rupee depreciation, plus a demonstrated ability to pass through raw material and freight inflation.
- **Distribution to shareholders** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Weak. Now: Net cash grew from INR4,083cr to INR4,300cr against only INR130cr of capex with no buyback, dividend or distribution policy articulated in any call — capital is simply accumulating in treasury. | The long view had said: A Rs 500cr buyback plus dividends were executed in FY25, but net cash still climbed from Rs 2,065cr (2022) to ~Rs 4,300cr (2026) with capex needs of only Rs 100-150cr, indicating no coherent policy for deploying or returning the accumulating idle cash.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: Customers buy a proven operational outcome — throughput and power reduction under abusive mill conditions — earned through multi-year trials rather than price, though the long trial cycle shows trust is slow to build. | The long view had said: Customers explicitly buy certainty of process outcome — throughput, power savings and recovery — and management states the sale cycle turns on confidence and willingness to trust a new solution, not on price.

*Unchanged: Share donators; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Profit margin; Geographical expansion; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Return on capital (headline); Distribution advantage; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Technology advantage; Mini-monopolies; Disciplined stewards; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Convenience benefits; Intangible benefits; Cost of working capital; Candor; Research and development; Security by obscurity; Asset turn.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 4 weakened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: The Nov-25 and May-26 calls name specific new solutions (mill/mid-liner package, 'new generation discharge system') with quantified customer benefits and a first South American high-chrome conversion, but no R&D spend, R&D-share or pipeline economics are disclosed in any of the four calls. (The long view had said: There is a genuine, sustained in-house development culture — composite/rubber mill liners developed and pilot-trialled internally (Aug-24), then the 'new generation discharge system' proof-of-concept win in May-26 — but R&D spend is never disclosed or quantified, no share-of-industry-R&D framing exists, and the innovation had not yet converted into volume growth by FY26.)
- **Low Price Plus**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: There is visible cost discipline — 60-65% of power shifting to captive renewables explicitly for cost saving, closure of the Welcast unit, balancing/cost-saving capex — but AIA runs 28% operating (40% reported) EBITDA on a value-priced product, not the low-margin/high-turns economics this pattern describes. (The long view had said: The strategy is the inverse of low price plus: AIA's chrome product carries a cost premium over local forged suppliers plus freight, and the stated aim is to move customers off price discussion entirely onto operating-benefit economics; cost work (65% renewable power, brownfield capex) is margin defence, not a price-led scale model.)

**That weakened:**

- **Toll Roads**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Grinding media/liners are a small slice of a mine's cost base but sit at the throughput bottleneck, and management frames the offer as solving the customer's top operating constraints (worsening ore grade, power, recovery); but the calls also show customers are conservative, trials run 12-24 months and duties/price scrutiny (anti-dumping, CVD) are live, so it is not a frictionless toll. (The long view had said: Across all four sampled calls the pitch is that high-chrome media/liners are a small consumable line item that unlocks throughput, power, recovery and ore-grade problems for the mine, so the discussion is explicitly not about price; management repeatedly frames itself as the niche solution provider in a market where the alternative is commodity forged media.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management articulates a concrete replication barrier — the full Indian value chain of design, pattern-making, metallurgy and on-site service built around high-chrome, plus multi-year co-development with single customers — but the last-12-months evidence is assertion rather than demonstrated exclusion of competitors. (The long view had said: There is a concrete replication barrier repeatedly articulated: not the castings themselves (many can make metal, rubber or composite) but the design/metallurgy/pattern-making/service value chain built on the Indian ecosystem, plus 2-year mine-level trial cycles that a funded newcomer cannot compress; the May-26 discharge-system claim of being the only ball-mill solution of its kind is consistent with a rational two-to-three-player global niche.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Over the four calls realisation fell from INR170/kg (Q1) to INR163 (Q2) and the FY26 average was INR165; the higher Q4 INR178 was explicitly attributed to rupee depreciation and casting mix, not price. No price increase is discussed anywhere, and duties are absorbed in the customer conversation rather than passed through. (The long view had said: Realisation rose from ~120 to 150+ (FY23) to INR165–178 (FY26) and reported EBITDA expanded from ~25% to ~40% (Q3 FY26), but management itself attributes this to cost-plus pass-through of raw material and freight, product mix and rupee depreciation rather than real price increases — and volumes were flat (255,000t FY25 to 258,000t FY26), so there is no price-plus-volume flywheel.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Volumes were flat all year — 258,000 tons in FY26 vs 255,000 in FY25, with mining tonnage described as flat quarter after quarter — and the conversion pipeline that would drive share gain remained at trial stage through May-26 with only one named 15,000tpa Chile win. (The long view had said: Share gain from a large forged-to-chrome pie is the entire stated thesis and management guided 25,000–35,000 incremental tonnes every year from Aug-22, but delivery has not followed: guidance was withdrawn in Aug-24 on logistics, volumes were 255,000t FY25 vs 258,000t FY26, and utilisation is only ~55%; the ambition is credible, the persistent share gain is not yet evidenced.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (QUANT SIGNAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: AIA sells a premium high-chrome solution against cheaper forged media, and three years of flat volumes (255k to 258k tons) despite an intensive conversion push is itself evidence that customers find the cheaper incumbent product good enough; management explicitly cites customer conservatism about switching and has re-strategised the pitch around quantified throughput and power savings rather than price. (The long view had said: Cheaper local forged media is a persistent good-enough substitute: management has spent four years explaining that the freight delta on top of the chrome premium is what stalls conversion, and by FY26 the strategy is explicitly to move clients off price altogether — meanwhile volumes went nowhere (255,000 tons FY25 to 258,000 tons FY26 at ~55% utilisation). Bounded, though, because unlike Nobel Biocare the premium is holding: realisation ~INR165/kg, operating EBITDA ~28% and record PAT in Q4FY26.)

**That worsened:**

- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Trade policy is a first-order determinant of the economics in every call: US Section 232 duty raised to 50% on top of ~10% antidumping, the Brazil sunset review cutting CVD from 6.5% to 2.9%, and repeated commentary that 'every country is going back to bringing borders on.' Government export benefits also run through the P&L at INR15-18 crores a quarter, and management concedes it cannot guide on US volumes until policy settles. (The long view had said: Pricing and market access are repeatedly shaped by trade policy rather than by a regulator setting tariffs: US antidumping/countervailing sunset reviews, Brazil dumping case, and by FY26 an explicit protectionism theme, plus the Ghana plant stalled 'awaiting certain clearances from the government'. Bounded because end demand comes from private mines in 140 countries, not government contracts, with only ~INR15-18 crore/quarter of export benefits (RoDTEP/drawback) as policy-derived income.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
