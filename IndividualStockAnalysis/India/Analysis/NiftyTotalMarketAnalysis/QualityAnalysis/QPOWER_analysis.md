# Quality Power Elect. (QPOWER) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Quality Power Electrical Equipments (QPOWER) makes heavy electrical equipment that helps electricity move around a grid safely and efficiently — things like reactors, line traps, instrument transformers and special-purpose transformers rated up to 765 kV, plus 'power quality' gear such as harmonic filters, capacitor banks, SVC and STATCOM systems. Its customers are power utilities and transmission companies, renewable-energy developers, data centres and industrial users, and it wins work through tendering and purchase orders, so revenue comes from executing a booked order book (INR 830 crores in Nov 2025, over INR 1,400 crores entering FY27) rather than from repeat off-the-shelf sales. It manufactures at plants in India (Sangli, Cochin, and Bhiwadi near Delhi through Mehru) and in Turkey (Endoks), and management says it has supplied over 100 countries across two decades, with current demand from India, Europe, the US, the Middle East and Australia. In FY26 it crossed INR 1,000 crores of total income with EBITDA of INR 236 crores (about 23.8% margin), and it is spending heavily on new capacity, in-house R&D and acquisitions to ride the global grid-modernisation and energy-transition build-out.

**The parts of the business:**

- **High-voltage power products (Sangli and Cochin, India)** — The original core business — reactors and line traps up to 765 kV, instrument transformers up to 500 kV and small special-purpose transformers for data centres, traction and power electronics; management calls it the part that 'holds the margin line for the group', and it had about INR 180 crores of booked orders plus roughly INR 450 crores near-final in early 2025, with a new 10-acre Sangli coil factory targeted for completion by June 2026 and the Cochin expansion already complete.
- **Power quality solutions / FACTS (Endoks, Turkey)** — Harmonic filters, capacitor banks, SVC and STATCOM systems that stabilise voltage and clean up grid disturbances, delivered mainly from the Turkish operation; it won large FACTS orders in Sweden and multiple global STATCOM orders, and large deliveries out of Turkey lifted Q3 FY26 revenue, though a INR 25.7 crore non-cash hyperinflation accounting adjustment on this subsidiary hit reported FY26 numbers.
- **HVDC products** — Reactors for high-voltage direct-current transmission links — the calls flag 'historic' wins including smoothing reactors for India's Rihand–Dadri HVDC corridor and converter reactors for a VSC HVDC project in a G20 country; management describes HVDC as one of the fastest-expanding parts of its pipeline, constrained mainly by scarce inputs like HVDC magnet wire.
- **Mehru Electrical and Mechanical Engineers (51% subsidiary, Bhiwadi/NCR)** — An instrument-transformer maker bought for about INR 120 crores just after the IPO; it is described as on track to be the largest and most cost-competitive producer in its category, and its EBITDA margin climbed from about 12% (Q2 FY26) to 16.4% (Q3) to around 20% (Q4 FY26), with a new 765 kV product line under development.
- **GIS (gas-insulated) instrument transformers** — A newer product line built around a technology partnership with Hyosung to bring gas-insulated instrument transformers to India through Mehru, aimed at utilities shifting from AIS to GIS substations; the first trial product was targeted for market readiness by June–July 2026, so it is an investment for the future rather than a current earner.
- **Power electronics and PCS for battery storage** — An in-house R&D-developed power conversion system for grid-scale battery energy storage, with a dedicated PCS factory being set up and a 1,725 kW inverter design in progress; management says it deliberately chased growth here in FY26 while accepting lower margins, to build scale and customer references before entering the Indian storage market.
- **Acquisitions and stakes (Sukrut, STATCON Energiaa)** — A rolling M&A effort run through a dedicated board committee — a completed 50% stake in Sukrut Electric (electrical components, a joint venture alongside Yash Highvoltage, which turned operationally positive in its first month under new management) and an evaluated majority stake in Noida-based STATCON Energiaa (around 300 people, INR 170 crores revenue, power electronics); these bolt on capabilities across the transformer value chain rather than forming a separate reporting segment.

### The story in depth

Quality Power makes specialised high-voltage electrical equipment — reactors, line traps, instrument transformers and FACTS/HVDC gear — and the records score it Strong (65 out of 100), built from 74 on business quality, 66 on pattern fit and 48 on risk safety, with overall resilience judged Sound. The strongest part of the story is how hard the business is to copy. Getting qualified to supply this equipment means passing 40-50 separate customer audits from buyers like Power Grid, Adani, Hitachi, Siemens and GE, running accredited high-voltage test labs, and carrying decades of references across more than 100 countries. That single trait shows up three times over: it earns a top mark on the barriers-to-entry check, it makes 'Cost to Replicate' a strong pattern fit, and it is exactly why the new-entrant risk is rated Low. Management's record supports it — FY26 guidance of INR 800 crore at 20% margins was raised to INR 900 crore and closed at INR 1,007 crore at 23.5%, and a promise to lift the Mehru unit to 15% margins was met (16.4% in the third quarter, about 20% in the fourth). That delivered-what-it-promised habit is what makes 'Corporate Culture' a strong fit and gives credibility to a very large building programme: a new 10-acre coil plant at Sangli, expansion at Cochin, extra Mehru lines, and new magnet wire, CTC and battery-converter factories taking capacity to roughly nine times current levels.

The awkward part is that the same feature driving the growth also creates the two biggest risks. This equipment is sold into other people's capital spending plans — utility transmission build-outs, HVDC corridors, renewable connections — and much of that spending is decided by governments and state utilities: Power Grid, NTPC, the Rihand-Dadri HVDC link, the UK's INR 59,000 crore HVDC commitment. That backdrop is what makes the end-market-growth check a clear positive and supports the 'Market Share Gainers' pattern, with revenue up 157% to INR 1,007 crore and wins in Sweden, Denmark, Israel, Korea, Abu Dhabi and Australia. But it is also why both Cyclicality and Government Dependency are rated High Risk, with policy touching the business at several points including BIS licensing that slows magnet-wire imports. The visible symptom is in the quality framework itself: growth persistence is the one badly scored check, because sales have averaged roughly 51% a year but arrive in lumps — one quarter showed revenue of INR 726 million after a sequential drop of about half. Revenue is entirely order-book and project driven, which is why the recurring-revenue pattern only partly fits: there is no mention of service contracts, renewals or installed-base income to smooth the gaps. For now the order book at about 1.4 times revenue, near-zero net debt and exports at roughly 60% across 100-plus countries are the cushions, not a steady revenue stream.

The remaining risks are best read as questions about where inside the group the quality actually sits. Margins differ sharply by product: the niche coil and HVDC factory earns about 34% at the operating level and the Turkish Endoks business about 30%, while Mehru's more standard instrument transformers began near 8% and only reached 16-20% after operational fixes. That spread is the whole 'Good Enough Goods' watch item and the reason pricing power is judged only a partial fit — management can pass aluminium costs into price and cites an absence of competition above 220 kV, but that protection is concentrated at the top end rather than spread across everything it sells. Brand is similarly rated as an ambition still being built rather than an established asset. Two other watch items have the company's own strengths as their answer: the tightness in insulators, bushings and HVDC magnet wire — Indian capacity described as booked out for the next year — is being met by backward integration into magnet wire and CTC through the Sukrut venture, and the threat that grid-scale batteries reduce demand for HVDC and FACTS is being met by building the battery converters and energy management systems in-house, which is also what earns the top R&D mark. The one number that does not yet match the story is return on capital, the weakest of the seven quality areas, which is consistent with a company mid-way through spending heavily on plants that have not yet produced revenue.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is capital-equipment sold into customers' capex programmes — utility transmission expansion, HVDC corridors, renewable integration — and the calls show violent swings (Q3 FY25 revenue INR 726mn with a ~50% sequential dip, versus Q3 FY26 consolidated INR 2,843mn, +250% year-on-year) plus explicit metal-price sensitivity in copper and aluminium that management says drives the margin guidance. Management itself frames the current 22-24% margins as demand-supply driven, i.e. cycle-elevated, and admits order-taking is deliberately capped at 12-15 months so 'the metal prices give us a very rude shock' does not hit them.

> "I believe the demand and supply is what is driving margins in most of the high voltage product segments... we do not see any drastic difference unless there is a dollar or a metal disruption" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Multi-year structural energy-transition/grid-modernisation demand across many geographies, order book at ~1.4x revenue, near-zero net debt and countercyclical capacity investment ahead of demand*

## What to watch

- Order book versus revenue — it is the only thing offsetting the High Risk on Cyclicality and the failed growth-persistence check; if the 1.4x cover shrinks, the lumpy revenue has nothing behind it.
- Mehru's margin holding at 16-20% and the group staying above its stated 22% floor — the direct test of the 'Good Enough Goods' risk and of whether pricing power is broader than the above-220 kV niche.
