**Checks that improved:**

- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Exports rose ~500bps of mix year-on-year with concrete wins — US data-centre transformers and offshore GIS exported to the Middle East from Sambhajinagar — and export mix is measurably lifting margins. | The long view had said: Exports rose ~300bps in FY25 and another 500bps in H1FY26 with US data-centre transformers and Middle East offshore GIS, but management concedes it cannot control this channel since sales rights outside South Asia sit with the parent.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Good → Excellent. Now: Deep multi-product technology edge across HVDC leadership, statcom grid stabilisation, PEM electrolyzers, SF6-free clean-air switchgear and waste heat recovery, backed by 1,500 engineers in the global R&D network. | The long view had said: Access to a genuinely differentiated multi-product portfolio — HVDC leadership, PEM electrolysers, SF6-free clean-air switchgear, waste-heat recovery — but the technology is licensed from and developed largely by the global parent rather than owned locally.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: It owns the largest installed turbine fleet in India and monetises it through OEM-only modernisation, upgrades and efficiency services — a classic captive aftermarket position even in coal, where it sells no new equipment. | The long view had said: Owns the country's largest installed thermal/industrial turbine fleet plus 2,000+ Vadodara turbines, giving OEM-only rights to modernisation, upgrades and aftermarket work on that base.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: HVDC, large power transformers and statcoms are a technically gated few-player market, but the end customers are large state utilities that tender aggressively and retain negotiating power. | The long view had said: Supply in HVDC/STATCOM/large transformers is concentrated among a few qualified global players, but the end customers are utilities buying through TBCB auctions, which retain real negotiating power.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Good → Excellent. Now: Eight factories, four service centres and four engineering/R&D centres attached to the country's largest installed turbine fleet give a service-and-supply reach rivals described as niche players cannot replicate in a market where uptime is critical. | The long view had said: Eight factories, four service centres and four engineering/R&D centres give pan-India manufacturing and service reach in a business where proximity for servicing installed equipment is decisive.

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
