### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 8 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Relaxo owns recognised mass brands (Sparx, Flite, Bahamas, Hawai), spends a sustained 3-4% of revenue on advertising, and stretched into licensed Disney/Marvel collections — but the brand did not deliver premium pricing or share defence, with consumers down-trading to local players through FY25 and the Disney/Marvel contribution described as negligible. (The long view had said: Relaxo has multi-decade brands (Sparx ~40%, Flite, Bahamas), 'strong brand recall' and ~4% of revenue on A&P, but the brand failed the durability test — consumers downtraded to unbranded product, share was lost, and management cut ad spend to 3% in FY25 precisely when defending the brand mattered most.)

**That weakened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No R&D spend, pipeline or named innovation with revenue impact is disclosed across the four calls; the premiumisation/closed-footwear mix shift management has been promising is explicitly stuck at 20% of sales, unchanged year on year despite capacity already added. (The long view had said: Incremental product innovation is regular and named (Sparx designs added yearly, Flite 'Urban Basic', athleisure/sneakers, price ladder extended to INR 2,800) but no R&D spend, pipeline economics or share-of-industry-R&D is ever disclosed, and capex is mostly moulds and civil work rather than innovation capability.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Market Share Gainers (NO FIT); Cost to Replicate (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 5 unchanged. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Demand is consumption-linked repeat purchase rather than customer capex, and margins held (EBITDA 12.9%-16.5%) through three years of falling revenue (FY25 INR 2,790cr to FY26 INR 2,702cr); but the May 2026 call shows severe input-commodity swings forcing 15-18% blended price hikes, so earnings are not free of cycle-driven volatility. (The long view had said: Demand is flow-type (mass-market footwear, repeat purchase, no customer capex linkage), but polymer input cycles swing margins violently — EBITDA margin collapsed to 8.9% in Q2FY23 on EVA/PVC costs and the same script repeated in FY26 with 15-18% price hikes; a 6-month imported raw-material supply chain amplifies each swing.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Consumers are down-trading and demand keeps shifting toward closed footwear and sneakers while Relaxo remains stuck at an 80/20 open-to-closed mix with capacity added but no change in mix across three calls; the pivot to premium (Sparx, sneakers, e-commerce) is stated as intent more than delivered result. (The long view had said: Consumers shifted from indoor Hawai/EVA slippers to out-of-home sports/athleisure — Hawai degrew ~20% while shoes grew 30% — yet closed footwear has stayed stuck at 20% of sales for years despite repeated stated intent, and management admits being slow on e-commerce.)

**That worsened:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: This is the central risk on display: cheaper unorganized substitutes drew consumers down-trading away from the brand, the mass Hawai segment lost share, and management admits it could take no price increase in FY25 for competitive reasons — a branded premium under sustained attack from adequate low-price alternatives in a category where mass buyers show little brand attachment. (The long view had said: The defining risk in this record: mass consumers repeatedly traded down to cheaper look-alikes 'at the cost of quality', the product is not complex and unbranded rivals undercut freely, and management's answer was to defend the quality premium rhetorically while conceding market share and eight straight quarters of volume decline.)

*Unchanged: New Entrants (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
