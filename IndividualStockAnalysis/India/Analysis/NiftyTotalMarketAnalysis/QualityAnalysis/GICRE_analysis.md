# General Insurance Corporation of India (GICRE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

GIC Re is India's government-owned reinsurance company: it does not sell policies to the public but instead insures the insurers, taking a share of the risk that general (non-life) insurance companies have written on motor, property, crop, health, marine and similar covers, in exchange for a share of their premium. Part of that business arrives automatically because Indian regulation requires every domestic insurer to cede a fixed slice of each policy to GIC Re (the 'obligatory' or mandatory cession, cut over the years from 30% down to 4%), and the rest is won competitively against cross-border reinsurers, foreign branches and new GIFT City players. It earns money two ways — underwriting (premium collected minus claims and expenses, tracked as the 'combined ratio', which has run above 100% and was 106.02% for FY26, meaning underwriting alone lost money) and investment income on the large float it holds (about INR3,059 crore in the March 2026 quarter alone), which is what makes the overall result profitable. Gross premium was INR44,007 crore in FY26 with profit after tax of INR8,392 crore, split roughly 75% from India and 25% from international business.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Domestic business** — Reinsurance written for Indian insurance companies — the core of the company at INR32,979 crore of FY26 premium, about 75% of the book; management says it will keep running above a 100% combined ratio by design because the investment income earned on Indian business makes up the difference.
- **International / foreign book** — Reinsurance written outside India, INR11,028 crore or about 25% of FY26 premium; it shrank sharply in the early years shown (down to 16% of the book by mid-2024) after old loss-making contracts, notably a US marine contract stopped in 2021 whose claims kept arriving for years, and management wants this book to get below a 100% combined ratio.
- **Obligatory / mandatory cession** — The regulator-mandated 4% slice of every Indian non-life policy that must be passed to GIC Re — a proportional book an investor sized at roughly 38% of revenue; management says it performs reasonably but worse than the rest, and that if the cession were reduced or abolished it would not all be lost because much of it would come back through negotiated deals.
- **Non-obligatory business** — Everything won on merit rather than by rule — proportional treaties, non-proportional treaties, facultative (case-by-case) covers and miscellaneous covers; management describes it as a diverse mix that should perform better than the obligatory book.
- **Crop / agriculture (PMFBY)** — Reinsurance of the government crop insurance scheme; it was once a very heavy share of the book with a bad combined ratio, was deliberately pruned to about 25% of premium by mid-2022, and swung back up strongly in FY25 (around INR10,000 crore written the prior year).
- **Health** — A line management called out as growing meaningfully alongside agriculture in the June 2024 quarter.
- **Marine (cargo and hull) and motor** — Older lines whose overseas premium collapsed after the US contract was stopped in 2021 while claims kept coming, pushing the overseas marine combined ratio into the hundreds of percent; provisions have since been made.
- **Investments** — The pool of premium held before claims are paid, generating INR2,000-3,900 crore of income per quarter across the period — described by management as an important earnings contributor and the reason the adjusted combined ratio (about 84.79% for FY26) is far better than the headline one.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Reinsurance pricing is explicitly a cycle: management narrates hardening (2023-24) turning to softening at Jan-26 and Apr-26 renewals with 'plenty of capacity, plenty of capital', signing-down of shares, and admits growth will fall to single digits; combined ratio swung from ~123% (Q1FY22) to ~103-106% (FY26) and PAT from a loss of INR771cr to INR8,392cr across the period, alongside CAT-event volatility (Taiwan EQ, UAE/Brazil/German floods, nine CAT events in FY24).

> "the global reinsurance market is shifting towards a competitive phase with continued increase in reinsurance capital, requiring us to navigate softening trends across both property and long-tail casualty lines" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Demand is flow-linked — reinsurance is bought out of insurers' operating premium flow, not customer capex — plus deliberate diversification across classes, investment income as a stable second earnings leg, and refusal to chase premium in the soft cycle ('we did forgo' contracts beyond threshold)*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 2 of the last 11 years with swings of ±24 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from -12% to 19% (swinging ±11.1 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 0% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 0%).

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** GIC Re is a Government of India company whose fortunes turn on regulator-set terms: the obligatory cession has been cut from 30% to 20% to 15% to 10% to 5% to 4%, still ~38% of the book, and management concedes 'this policy decision is in the realm of the regulator'; crop (PMFBY/Beed, cup-and-cap vs burn-cost) is a state-tender-driven line whose design and adoption dictate premium and results, and the chairman's own post is a government appointment (repeated CMD churn, ED holding additional charge of CMD).

> "If obligatory goes away or gets reduced, it is not a straight loss of business. A substantial part, something like probably 25% to 50%, will get readily converted into voluntary business" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Partial substitution of obligatory into voluntary business, deliberate pruning of the policy-driven crop book from a heavy share to ~25%, and diversification into retail health and a 60/40 international target*

### New Entrants — HIGH RISK
*"A new player changes the rules"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 2 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Structurally new competitors keep arriving in a market GIC once monopolised: cross-border reinsurers, foreign reinsurance branches over 8-9 years, multiple IIOs in GIFT City, and two new domestic reinsurers set up just before 1 April 2026 — with analysts directly asking whether this threatens the obligatory privilege, and management conceding pricing pressure from the added capacity.

> "there are 2 reinsurers that have been set up just prior to 1st of April. So the competition is at our doorstep." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Incumbency advantages management leans on — decades of relationships, lead-reinsurer status ('capacity availability... only when there was a credible lead'), and a regulatory hurdle requiring new players to build track record before obligatory share*

- ✅ Operating margin held (about -8% then 18%) — no drift down under competition.
- ⚠️ Growth has stalled: sales compounded at 24% a year in the first half of the record but only 2% recently — demand may be structurally fading.

### Stakeholder Concentration — WATCH
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Single contracts and single relationships have visibly driven results: one US motor/marine contract stopped in 2021 still dominated the overseas combined ratio years later ('the premium which used to come from that one particular contract... has completely stopped'), foreign motor/cargo losses concentrate in Israel, Turkey and China, and a single rating relationship with AM Best gated access to international business for years.

> "Motor constitutes about 11% of our foreign book... the peak regions will be Israel and Turkey" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Broad cedent base across the Indian market plus explicit derisking by diversifying across classes; management notes the Jan-26 book 'became a little more broad-based than what it was before'*

- ✅ Customers pay in about 0 days (was 0) — no sign of weakening bargaining power in collection terms.

### Good Enough Goods — WATCH
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Reinsurance capacity is largely undifferentiated, so cheaper capital competes directly on price: management repeatedly reports 'heavy competition' and pricing pressure in small and mid sum-insured property, shares being signed down at Jan-26 renewals despite wanting bigger lines, and forgoing contracts where cedents' price expectations exceeded its threshold — the classic commodity squeeze rather than a defended premium.

> "We wanted to write good shares, but we could not. Eventually, our shares got reduced. It is called signing down." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Some protection from complexity and scale — lead-reinsurer role, expertise-driven segments like refineries/energy where 'pricing is under pressure, it is not as much', and contractual 'handles'/levers that shift deteriorating loss ratios back to the cedent*

- ⬜ No input-cost data.
- ✅ Operating margin (19%) remains near its historical peak (19%) — no collapse in the premium.
- ✅ Operating margin held (about -8% then 18%) — no drift down under competition.
