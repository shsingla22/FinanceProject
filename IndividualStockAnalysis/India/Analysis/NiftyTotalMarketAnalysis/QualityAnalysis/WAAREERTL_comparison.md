Now: Growth is volume-led (MWp executed) with realization swings driven purely by whether modules sit in scope, not by pricing power. | The long view had said: Growth is purely megawatt-volume and module pass-through driven with tender prices falling; management concedes continuous cost/price erosion and per-MW realisation swings only on module scope, not on pricing power.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: There is bid selectivity and margin discipline — guidance of >15% was raised in practice to 18-20% across FY26 — but no price increases are ever discussed; realisation swings (INR 1.3-2.0 crore/MW) are purely module-in-scope mix, and GST cuts on modules are passed through as lower customer capex. (The long view had said: This is a fixed-price, tender-priced business with structural deflation, not pricing power: EBITDA guidance was cut from the FY23 15-25% range to 'near 15%' by Nov-2024, and by Jan-2026 management concedes bid rates keep falling and that cost optimisation from competition is 'a regular phenomenon'.)

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: the calls are silent and no computable check supports the pattern. (The long view had said: The company leans on the Waaree Energies parentage for module supply on customers' approved lists and for pre-qualification credibility, but there is no A&P spend, no heritage narrative, no premium pricing and no adjacency brand stretch discussed in any call.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Nothing articulated would stop a well-funded EPC entrant: the stated edge is operational efficiency, timely delivery and end-to-end capability, all of which are replicable, and the one structural asset — turnkey module supply — comes from parent Waaree Energies and is subject to the customer's approved vendor list rather than being proprietary. (The long view had said: The only articulated barrier is tender pre-qualification and GW-scale track record ('newer players will always find it difficult to qualify'), which a funded entrant can accumulate in a few years — and management itself admits the market is open ('the market is sufficiently available for all the people'), with the durable advantages (module manufacturing, brand) sitting in the parent, not in this entity.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Innovation Dominance (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
