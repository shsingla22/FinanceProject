# Archean Chem Inds (ACI) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Archean Chemical Industries (ACI) is an Indian "marine chemicals" producer: it pumps up natural brine from its own reserves in the Great Rann of Kutch, Gujarat, and separates it at its Hajipir plant into three basic products — elemental bromine, A-grade industrial salt and sulphate of potash (a speciality fertiliser). It sells these in bulk to industrial buyers rather than consumers: industrial salt goes almost entirely to overseas chemical makers who turn it into caustic soda, soda ash, chlorine and PVC (Japanese trading house Sojitz is a major customer), while bromine goes to pharma, agrochemical, flame-retardant, water-treatment and oil-and-gas users in India and abroad. Money is made on volume and price of these commodities — historically the bulk of revenue was exports to China, Japan, South Korea, the Middle East and Europe, with the plant sited near Jakhau Jetty and Mundra Port because trucking and shipping costs are a big part of the economics. More recently the company has been pushing downstream into higher-value bromine derivatives through its subsidiary Acume, into oilfield drilling chemicals, and into two unrelated new bets — silicon-carbide semiconductors and zinc-bromide batteries — which are still years from meaningful revenue.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Industrial Salt (A-grade)** — Bulk salt that comes out of the same brine process as bromine and is shipped almost entirely to overseas chemical plants; it is the volume engine of the company, running at about 1.1 million tonnes a quarter (4.2 million tonnes in FY26) and contributing roughly 67-70% of standalone revenue, though prices have been under pressure as new capacity is added in Australia and the Middle East.
- **Elemental Bromine** — Extracted from the brine and sold to pharma, agrochemical, flame-retardant, water-treatment and oil-and-gas customers; ACI calls itself India's leader in merchant bromine sales and its largest exporter, having taken capacity from 10,000 to 28,500 tonnes and then toward 42,000+ tonnes — it was about half of revenue in the early years and around 30% of sales by FY26, with a growing share now consumed captively by its own derivatives plant.
- **Sulphate of Potash (SOP)** — A speciality fertiliser for chloride-sensitive crops that ACI makes by a low-cost, chemical-free route from natural sea brine (only ~15% of world SOP is made this way) and can export to Europe on a REACH certification; it has been dogged by feedstock and technical problems for years and remains tiny — 644 tonnes and about ₹3.5 crore of revenue in FY26 — with management still expecting meaningful contribution only from FY27.
- **Bromine derivatives — Acume Chemicals (Jhagadia plant)** — A wholly owned downstream subsidiary, originally a ~₹250 crore greenfield project, that converts ACI's own bromine into higher-value products — flame retardants, clear brine fluids, bromine/PTA-synthesis catalysts, calcium bromide, NPBR, PBR-3 and zinc bromide — and is the main growth story: FY26 revenue up nearly 300% year-on-year, but still only ~45% capacity utilisation across its ~40 reactors, so margins remain thin during ramp-up.
- **Oilfield chemicals — Idealis Mudchemie (formerly Oren Hydrocarbons)** — An acquired business taken over via an NCLT order in July 2024, being refurbished plant by plant to sell speciality drilling-mud chemicals and clear brine fluids to oil-and-gas drilling customers; three plants have been commissioned over the past year and it is still at the customer-trial and approvals stage.
- **Semiconductors — SiCSem and Clas-SIC Wafer Fab (UK)** — A diversification into silicon-carbide wafers and MOSFET devices for EVs, renewables and data centres, via a GBP 15 million investment for 21.33% of UK foundry Clas-SIC plus an Indian project through subsidiary SiCSem — a plant in Odisha that broke ground in November 2025 and signed a fiscal support agreement with the Government of India in May 2026; management says commercialisation takes 2-3 years, so it earns nothing today.
- **Energy storage — Offgrid Energy Labs (ZincGel batteries)** — About USD 12 million for a 21% stake in a US-registered, IP-led zinc-bromide battery company, pitched as a natural fit with ACI's bromine business, starting with a pilot plant in the UK and a possible Indian gigafactory later; also roughly a two-year road map to commercialisation with no current revenue.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Archean Chem Inds comes out of all three reviews as one business wearing two hats, and the combined score of 36 out of 100 (Mixed, two stars) reflects the tension rather than a settled verdict. The core is a marine-chemicals producer sitting on captive brine reserves in the Great Rann of Kutch, feeding one of the largest integrated plants near the Jakhau and Mundra ports. That position is real and hard to copy, which is why the screen marks 'Cost to Replicate' and 'Toll Roads' as partial fits: bromine and A-grade salt are small-cost but critical inputs to agrochemical, flame-retardant and caustic/soda-ash customers, sold from globally limited sources with REACH certification and customer approval requirements attached. But the very thing that creates the moat is also what the quality framework penalises. Brine fields, evaporation ponds that need a full season to fill, washeries, a truck fleet and now a semiconductor fab produce only about INR1,100cr of revenue on that base (Asset turn, -1), and roughly 666 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers (Cost of working capital, -2). The same physical, location-bound asset base that keeps rivals out keeps capital tied up inside.

The second thread is that what the company sells is, in the buyer's eyes, ordinary. The screen returns outright 'no fit' on Pricing Power and Brand Strength, and the risk review flags 'Good Enough Goods' as elevated for the same reason: contracts are awarded on price, roughly 75% of sales go overseas to trading conglomerates and large industrials, and management has admitted it could not pass bromine cost increases through to derivative customers, withdrawing volumes from products where the cost impact was too heavy. This shows up everywhere at once. Growth is genuine but volume-led, not price-led — salt volumes up 22% to 4.2mn tons in FY26 (Gain in market share, +1; Market Share Gainers, partial) against salt realisation down about 10% year-on-year (Pricing, Mix and Volume, -1). The upturn the framework credits as a positive, bromine prices bottoming around FY25 and realisation rising 14% by FY26 (Cyclical market growth, +1), is the same event that squeezed the derivatives mix, because higher input costs could not be recovered downstream. Meanwhile new bromine capacity from other Indian players and new salt capacity in Australia and the Middle East (New Entrants, elevated; Barriers to entry, -1, with margins swinging by plus or minus 8.9 points) is eroding the moat in practice, and the cycle itself is doing visible damage: EBITDA margin fell from 45% in Q3FY23 to 27% in Q3FY26 and 21.8% in Q4FY26, with FY26 EBITDA down 17% (Cyclicality, high risk). The claimed leadership in India's merchant bromine sales is never backed by a share percentage, which is why the pattern stops at partial.

The third thread is what management has chosen to do about all this, and it is where the strongest positive and the sharpest concerns share a single root. Innovation Dominance is the only 'likely fit' in the whole screen: R&D is repeatedly described as a cornerstone, with a downstream pipeline covering CBF, PTA synthesis, calcium bromide, NPBR and PBR3 launched in Q4FY26, 15 products in trials, and intellectual-property-led stakes in Clas-SiC and Offgrid carrying more than 50 patents — though R&D spend is never disclosed. That same push outward, however, produces the worst marks in the file. Management pursued three unrelated diversifications at once — a UK wafer fab stake, a US battery intellectual-property stake and a distressed oilfield-chemicals acquisition — while the core bromine and SOP operations were still unfinished (Disciplined stewards, -1), and the deals are the opposite of small complementary purchases of strong businesses: a distressed NCLT asset needing refurbishment plus minority stakes in pre-commercial ventures two to three years from commercialisation (Merger and acquisition, -2). Those same ventures carry the technology risk (elevated: silicon-carbide wafers and zinc-bromide batteries against entrenched competitors) and much of the policy risk, since the semiconductor venture's economics are explicitly undetermined until the Indian Semiconductor Mission decides, on top of brine land renewal still pending with the Gujarat government (Government Dependency, high risk). Delivery history is the reason the screen rejects Corporate Culture outright: derivatives promised to be in commercial production and billing before FY2024 ended only reached 30–45% utilisation by FY26, and SOP, described as making meaningful contributions 'next year' since Feb 2023, produced 644 tons, roughly INR3.5 crore. Balance-sheet resilience is judged sound and there are genuine cushions — 52 clients across 27 global and 25 domestic accounts, the ability to divert delayed China bromine volumes into the domestic market, and one of very few natural sea-brine SOP routes globally — but 100% of industrial salt, about 70% of standalone revenue, is exported, with Sojitz Corporation named as a major customer and 'retaining our key customers in bromine' repeatedly named as the year's priority (Stakeholder Concentration, watch).
