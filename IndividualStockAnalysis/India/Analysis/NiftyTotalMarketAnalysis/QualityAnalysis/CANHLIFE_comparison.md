## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 28 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 2 improved, 7 regressed, 19 unchanged. 1 check carries the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them). 2 were newly assessable in the recent view.

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |

| Return on Capital | Neutral (-0.33) | Neutral (-0.33) | +0.00 |
| Growth | Good (+0.83) | Good (+0.83) | +0.00 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Weak → Good. Now: Quasi-captive access to Canara Bank and HSBC customer bases gives a privileged position inside those branch networks, but no monopoly-like standing in the customer's mind as a brand. | The long view had said: No monopoly-like position anywhere — 9th among private players with 2.6% private-sector share, and even its own parent's counter is shared with LIC.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Long-duration policies create real stickiness — 13-month persistency at 86.3% and 61-month at ~55.5% — plus effective channel exclusivity inside the promoter bank, though customers face no true switching cost beyond surrender economics. | The long view had said: The parent-bank bancassurance counter functions as a quasi-captive channel with real distribution lock-in, but it is already open architecture and the DFS is signalling further opening, so the protection is partial at best.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Purely organic growth with no M&A, comfortable 190% solvency, and a small INR250cr subordinate-debt raise explicitly to fund new-business and channel growth rather than financial engineering. | The long view had said: Consistently organic and prudent — no M&A, phased agency ramp explicitly subordinated to margins, modest INR250cr sub-debt rather than dilution, solvency at ~190%, and a deliberately conservative Ind AS forbearance request.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Small and previously unlisted, but the FY26 IPO and Motilal Oswal-hosted analyst coverage have moved it onto the radar of capital rather than away from it. | The long view had said: A small, newly listed niche player (2.6% private share, 9th rank) with limited analyst coverage until its FY26 IPO, though the Canara/HSBC parentage means it is not truly invisible.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: Accessibility through the Canara Bank branch network with data-analytics-driven targeting of high-propensity customers is a genuine convenience edge, but the sales force is the bank's rather than its own and the captive agency force is only six months old. | The long view had said: Distribution intimacy is the central advantage — embedded in Canara Bank and HSBC branches with analytics identifying high-propensity customers, giving accessibility a standalone insurer cannot replicate.
- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Quantified the GST drag (2.25% annualised, 185bps realised) and the INR9cr labour-code one-off up front with ex-item reconciliation, gave a numeric 22-23% VNB margin guide, and corrected its own rider disclosure live on the call. | The long view had said: Consistently specific and self-correcting — GST drag quantified and revised (225bps→185bps→delivered), labour-code one-off disclosed separately, the rider-attachment figure corrected mid-call from premium to policy-count basis, and guidance withheld rather than fabricated.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.1 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Barriers are being actively lowered — the FDI limit rising from 74% to 100% is explicitly expected to attract new long-term capital and deeper participation into an already crowded field. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.4 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Regulatory capital and solvency requirements are real barriers and management calls distributor payouts 'rationalized', but 100% FDI plus pending regulation on agency commissions point to more capital and competition entering, not less.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Strong FY26 results are materially attributable to non-skill factors management itself names — GST-led demand tailwinds, a favourable yield-curve move, and captive Canara Bank distribution — so the outperformance cannot yet be cleanly separated from circumstance. | The long view had said: Results are partly environment-driven (GST tailwind, buoyant ULIP demand, yield curve), but management itself strips these out and the durable items — 18.7% expense ratio and 13-month persistency rising 82.5%→86.3% — are attributable to execution.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: Reliability metrics are strong and improving — claim settlement ratio 99.6% and NPS up from 76 to 80 — which is precisely the certainty an insurance buyer purchases. | The long view had said: The core proposition is certainty and it is being delivered and measured — claim settlement ratio 99.4%→99.6%, NPS 76→80, and persistency improving across every cohort and line of business.

*Unchanged: Return on capital (headline); Cyclical market growth; Structural end-market growth; Distribution advantage; Technology advantage; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Gain in market share; Profit margin; Asset turn; Intangible benefits; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Share donators; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Geographical expansion; Research and development; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Independent, long-term and tenacious; Out of limelight; Cost of working capital.*

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these — identical by construction): Persistence of growth (Poor).*

**Newly assessable in the recent view:**

- **People matter (talent development)** — Good: Some systematic evidence — five consecutive years of Great Place to Work participation with a ranking improvement into the BFSI top 25 — but no discussion of an internal leadership pipeline (the Chief Agency Officer appears to be a new external build-out).
- **Network effects** — Weak: Nothing in the business model is self-reinforcing with scale — growth comes from branch activation and distributor addition, not from a two-sided network.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 8 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Margins expanded through a cost shock — VNB margin 19.1%→22.4% despite a ~2.25% annualised GST drag, and guidance of 22-23% for FY27 — but the drivers cited are product mix, rider attachment, longer premium terms and yield curve, plus a repricing of renewal commissions, not price increases taken on customers without volume loss. No premiumisation or scarcity language. (The long view had said: Margin gains came from mix (protection, riders, PT-PPT) and yield curve, not price; management explicitly said the GST hit permanently re-bases margins and cannot be priced back, and expects to hand yield-curve gains to customers.)

**That weakened:**

- **Friendly Middleman**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Sales run overwhelmingly through bank staff and now agents rather than direct end-customer marketing — Canara Bank branch activation, HSBC's niche customer base, and a newly launched agency channel with ~500 onboarded distributors. But the calls never discuss trade training, channel reputation or low end-customer price sensitivity; in fact GST affordability is framed as a demand driver, cutting against the low-price-sensitivity leg. (The long view had said: Essentially all distribution runs through intermediaries who recommend the product while the customer pays: ~70% Canara Bank branches, 14-15% HSBC, plus a newly built agency force of 500 distributors; management invests in the channel through branch activation, customer segmentation analytics shared with distributors, and negotiated commission agreements rather than end-customer marketing.)
- **Low Price Plus**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: There is a real, itemised cost culture — expense ratio improved 20.5%→19% (H1) →18.7% (FY26, 17.5% ex-GST/labour code), management claims top-quartile cost ratio and cites OpEx saves, higher fixed-cost absorption and AI initiatives. What is missing is the other half of the pattern: no evidence savings are passed to customers as lower price to win volume. (The long view had said: Cost obsession is itemised on every call — expense ratio 20.5% → 19% → 18.7% (17.5% ex-GST/labour code), self-described top-quartile cost ratio, commissions below industry, vendor renegotiation, and opex efficiency showing up as a Rs 130 crore positive operating variance in EV — combined with a high-quality book (86.3% persistency, 99.6% claims ratio), the insurance analogue of the bank variant.)

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (STRONG FIT); Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Half the book is unit-linked, so earnings and EV move with markets rather than with a commodity cycle: FY26 showed a negative economic variance of ~Rs.82 crore driven by equity falls hitting future fund-management charges, while a favourable yield-curve move was cited as a margin tailwind; the underlying premium flow is recurring and operations-linked, not customer-capex-driven. (The long view had said: Roughly half to 60% of APE is unit-linked, so sales and embedded value swing with equity markets — FY26 saw a negative economic variance of ~INR820mn from falling equity hitting future fund management charges, and management flagged that FY26's yield-curve margin benefit may not repeat in FY27. Offsetting this, the book is a flow/recurring business: renewal premium grew 25-34% and 13-month persistency rose to 86.3%.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The business is structurally a two-bank bancassurance franchise (Canara Bank plus HSBC), with growth attributed to branch activation and segmentation within those partners; alternate channels are still only ~9% of WPI and the agency channel, launched Oct-2025, contributed just Rs.14 crore APE with a 5% three-year ambition, so the partner relationships remain the single dominant driver. (The long view had said: About 70% of APE comes from Canara Bank and 14-15% from HSBC — the two promoter-banks — leaving under 15% elsewhere; analysts questioned both a possible HSBC stake change and whether LIC's counter share inside Canara Bank could rise under open architecture. Diversification is real but small and slow: agency did only INR14 crores APE in six months and is guided to ~5% of business over three years.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Product demand swings hard on customer preference rather than management plan — ULIP held ~50% of mix through H1 FY26 on metro demand, then traditional jumped to 81% of Q4 FY26 mix versus 39% to December — showing the mix is set by prevailing saver sentiment; the underlying savings/protection need itself is not eroding. (The long view had said: Preference movement discussed is between ULIP, traditional, protection and annuity within the same demand pool — not erosion of the demand base; management pivoted the mix on demand (traditional to 81% of Q4 APE) and cites structural under-penetration, GST-driven first-time buyers and longevity-driven retirement demand as expanding, not shrinking, the base.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); New Entrants (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 2 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 2 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
