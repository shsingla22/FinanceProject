# Hyundai Motor I (HYUNDAI) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Hyundai Motor India Limited (HMIL) makes and sells passenger cars and SUVs in India under the Hyundai brand, with models like Creta, Venue, Exter, Alcazar, Verna and Grand i10 Nios sold to retail buyers through a dealer network that it keeps expanding, including into rural markets (rural penetration reached about 24% of sales). It earns money by manufacturing vehicles at its own plants in Chennai and at a newer Pune/Talegaon facility (engine production from mid-2025, vehicle production from 1 October 2025) and selling them for a profit, with revenue of roughly ₹172-175 billion per quarter and EBITDA margins of about 12-14%. Roughly three-quarters of its volumes go to Indian customers and the rest is exported — management calls this a 'two-pronged' or 'dual engine' strategy, and describes India as parent Hyundai Motor Company's manufacturing hub for emerging markets ('Make in India, Made for the World'). Profitability is driven by selling a richer mix (more SUVs, higher trims with sunroof and ADAS), cost control, localisation of parts, and calibrated price increases.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Domestic vehicle sales (India)** — Selling cars and SUVs to Indian retail customers — about 132,000-149,000 units a quarter, the larger share of total volumes; demand was weak through FY25 and H1 FY26 and then recovered strongly after the September 2025 GST rate cuts, with Q4 FY26 the highest-ever quarterly domestic sales.
- **Exports** — Shipping India-built vehicles to emerging markets such as the Middle East, Africa, Mexico and South Africa; exports rose to about 27% of the sales mix, grew 16.4% for full-year FY26, and management guides to 8-10% growth in FY27 — it is repeatedly described as the cushion that offsets weak domestic demand.
- **SUV portfolio** — By far the most important product group, at roughly 68-71% of volumes, led by Creta (the mid-size segment leader for ten years running), plus Venue and Exter in the compact/entry SUV space and Alcazar higher up; the all-new Venue launched November 2025 is called a key growth driver.
- **Hatchbacks** — Smaller cars such as Grand i10 Nios; a declining part of the mix (about 30,000 units in Q2 FY25) as customers shift to SUVs, though brand i10 has cumulatively sold over 3 million units in India and export markets.
- **Sedans** — Cars like Verna, contributing roughly 12% of volumes and broadly flat — the smallest of the three body-type groups.
- **Powertrain / fuel mix** — A deliberately 'technology-agnostic' spread across petrol, diesel (about 23% of volumes), CNG including its dual-cylinder technology (a record ~15-16%) and electric (only about 1.4%), with a first locally built dedicated compact-SUV EV planned for FY27 and a hydrogen R&D centre (HTWO) set up with IIT Madras.
- **Manufacturing operations** — Two manufacturing bases — the long-standing Chennai plant and the new Pune/Talegaon plant — which together add capacity for the planned 26 product launches by FY2030 and are backed by record capex of about ₹7,500 crore in FY27; the new plant's start-up costs weigh on near-term margins.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Hyundai Motor India earns its money well and runs itself with unusual honesty. Every ₹100 of capital in the business threw off ₹35 of operating cash this year, and customers pay in roughly 15 days before suppliers have to be paid — so buyers effectively fund the working capital instead of shareholders or lenders. Management scores top marks for candor: it publishes margin bridges broken down by driver, discloses its own discount levels, separates one-offs, and volunteers bad news such as losing domestic market share. It also does what it says: the dividend policy promised in November 2024 turned into ₹21 a share and a 31.4% payout, and the Pune plant promised for Q3 FY2026 started building vehicles on 1 October 2025. That track record is the single strongest finding in the whole file — 'Corporate Culture' is the only one of eleven multibagger patterns rated a strong fit, and it rests on the same evidence as the candor and stewardship checks.

The rest of the pattern screen comes back mostly 'partial', and the reason is that the same choice which produces the strengths also caps them. With volumes flat to down, nearly all the growth and margin came from price and mix rather than more cars sold: SUVs to a record 71% of the mix, CNG from 13% to 18%, sunroof at 53% and driver-assistance features from 3.3% to 14.4%, plus calibrated price increases in January, March and May. Management deliberately sat out the industry price war. That discipline is what earns the 'Pricing Power' and 'Low Price Plus' credit — the cost work is real and itemised, with localisation from 78% to 82% and material costs down 70 to 110 basis points — but the savings are kept as margin rather than handed to buyers, so volume did not hold. Domestic volumes fell in FY25 and the first half of FY26, the company itself acknowledged the share dip in registration data, and 'Market Share Gainers' is scored a clear no fit. The offset is exports, up 16.4% in FY26 against 7–8% guidance, spread into Africa, Mexico and Latin America. The quality framework tells the same story from the other side: management and capital allocation score well, but competitive advantage scores zero and industry structure is negative, and this is not an overlooked stock — a recent large IPO, an Investor Day with the global CEO, a cricket sponsorship and coverage by five global brokers make 'security by obscurity' the one deeply negative check.

The risk review reads as the mirror image of the strengths rather than a separate list. Cyclicality is the one elevated risk, and it is inseparable from the stewardship the framework praises: committing record FY27 capex of ₹7,500 crore and commissioning Pune during the industry's worst demand phase is disciplined, counter-cyclical behaviour, and it is also what puts fixed costs into a business whose margin already swung from 14.1% in Q4 FY25 to 13.9% and then 10.4%. The premium, feature-rich positioning that drives the pricing and mix strength is also the 'good enough goods' exposure — discounts running at 3.2% of average selling price were the largest single drag on first-quarter FY26 margins, in a market where rivals attack on price. The export engine that cushions the domestic cycle is also the concentration risk, since the company is a captive manufacturing arm of its Korean parent for platforms, models and export mandates. And the barriers that look real — thirty years of dealer and rural reach, deep local sourcing, three plants scaling toward 1.1 million units — are rated only partial on 'cost to replicate' precisely because share still slipped, which is the proof they are contestable. Six further risks sit at watch rather than danger, resilience is judged sound, and the blended rating comes out at 60 out of 100 — decent: a well-run, honestly reported, cash-generative business whose moat and industry are the weak links, not its management or its balance sheet.

## What to watch

- Domestic market share: management pinned FY27 as the year it stops losing share — if registration data keeps slipping, the 'Market Share Gainers' no-fit hardens and the partial 'Cost to Replicate' barrier looks thinner.
- Discounting versus margin: discounts at 3.2% of selling price were the top margin drag in Q1 FY26 — sustained discounting would break the 'Pricing Power' case and confirm the 'Good Enough Goods' risk.
- Absorbing the new capacity: record ₹7,500 crore FY27 capex and the ramp toward 1.1 million units are the 'Capex for growth' strength and the 'Cyclicality' risk at once — watch whether volumes fill the plants or fixed costs compress margins further below the 11–14% guided band.
- Export momentum and parent dependence: exports grew 16.4% and carry the 'Geographical expansion' check, but the mandates come from the Korean parent — any pullback hits both the cyclical hedge and the 'Stakeholder Concentration' risk.
- Electric transition: EV contribution was only 1.4% in Q1 FY26 against a 26-product roadmap to 2030, with R&D spend never disclosed — the reason 'Innovation Dominance' is only partial and 'Technological Innovation' stays on watch.
