**Checks that improved:**

- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Weak → Good. Now: Exports grew 21% in 9M FY26 and a Saudi Arabia subsidiary is being incorporated, though the Saudi entity is still at law-firm/VAT-registration stage and Q3 exports fell year-on-year on Middle East disruption. | The long view had said: Four years of promised export recovery has not been delivered — the Saudi distributor lost in 2021 was still not replaced by Feb 2026, Iran remains payment-blocked, and exports fell from a promised Rs 1,400–1,600 crore uplift to just Rs 1,555 crore in FY26.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Domestic 9M growth of 6% was explicitly value-led with volumes merely stable and margins expanding, indicating price/mix rather than volume drove growth. | The long view had said: Growth is a roughly balanced mix rather than pricing-led — FY26 Q4 was 16% volume vs 5% realization and FY24 domestic was 10% volume vs 6% realization — with mix helped by premiumisation but realization often set by the paddy cycle.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Good → Poor. Now: Management is diversifying into real estate with ~INR1,400 crore of land and project outlay plus a memorandum object-clause change that drew a recorded board dissent — the opposite of organic focus and no-big-diversification discipline. | The long view had said: Organic-only growth, net cash balance sheet and explicit refusal to chase risky Iran/Iraq receivables show prudence, though the equity portfolio, real-estate monetisation of Panipat land and the unresolved ED matter dilute the focus.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Good → Weak. Now: The stated long-term plan is land monetization and relocation of the Ghaziabad plant rather than deepening the core franchise, and an independent director resigned over board-process concerns within the window. | The long view had said: The same promoter family has run a consistent multi-year domestic playbook (the same three-to-four pillars from 2022 to 2026) and executed it tenaciously in distribution, even if the export rebuild has dragged for four years.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: The branded retail side is correctly built on intangibles and reach, but a large bulk export book to institutional buyers carries no switching-cost lock-in and swung sharply (Q3 exports INR357cr vs INR563cr). | The long view had said: The benefit type is well matched — retail households are sold intangible brand and aging cues — though the export book leans on bulk and private-label buyers with no lock-in, which is exactly where revenue proved fragile.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: Management says the core agri-food business needs minimal future capex and is instead deploying surplus into a ~INR403 crore Panipat land bid and ~INR1,000 crore of real estate projects — capital deployment is rising but into an unrelated, unproven vertical rather than high-ROIC core growth. | The long view had said: Growth capex is real but modest — new plants in Karnataka and MP for the non-basmati/regional rice push (which grew to Rs 271 cr, +38%) plus a long-term Panipat consolidation, though returns on it are not yet quantified and part of the Panipat land is being treated as a real-estate/treasury asset.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: The basmati market is fragmented with loose/unbranded and regional competitors plus Pakistani exporters, and large global buyers hold real negotiating leverage in bulk export. | The long view had said: Branded basmati is concentrated among a few large players, but a very large unorganised loose-rice fringe supplies the same need and export bulk buyers hold real negotiating power.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: Distribution reach of 3.2 lakh outlets, 1.2 crore urban households, 16 C&Fs and 8 super stockists is being deepened into underpenetrated towns with tighter channel governance. | The long view had said: Availability is the standout asset — 3.4 lakh retail outlets, 16 C&Fs and 8 super stockists, direct feet-on-street coverage and the leading e-commerce share in the category.
- **Candor** (Management): Neutral → Weak. Now: Management did lay out the INR58 crore Africa write-off chronology in detail and commissioned a third-party review, but framed the governance concerns defensively as having 'no apparent lapses' and refused business questions on that call. | The long view had said: Disclosure of bad news is quantified and specific (export shortfalls bridged rupee by rupee, Q3 flatness admitted), but a pattern of large unmet guidance — Rs 700 crore Saudi, Rs 1,400–1,600 crore export uplift — is never explicitly owned as a miss, and volume data is withheld.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: KRBL is already the clear domestic leader (37.8% general trade, 39.3% modern trade, 41.2% e-commerce) leaving limited runway, and Q3 domestic revenue was flat with basmati share under pressure from loose and regional players. | The long view had said: Share was captured strongly (GT +440bps in FY24) and leadership held across all channels, but KRBL is already the dominant branded player and modern-trade share has since slipped from 55.4% to 38.7%, limiting further runway inside the branded pool.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Excellent → Good. Now: The combination of 3.2 lakh outlets domestically, leading e-commerce share and a deep multi-decade paddy sourcing/supplier network is difficult for rivals to replicate, though it is not decisive against loose-rice competition. | The long view had said: Distribution reach is the company's hardest-to-replicate asset — leading share in general trade, modern trade and e-commerce simultaneously, built on 150+ new distributors, 44,000 added outlets and a rebuilt C&F/super-stockist supply chain.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Pakistani exporters lost volume as their prices traded at a premium, offering some share to take, but domestically the weak players are actively gaining rather than donating share to KRBL. | The long view had said: A very large unbranded/loose segment functions as a persistent share donator, with a million new households converted to the India Gate umbrella in one year.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 4 weakened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Nothing in the four calls describes licences, AMC/service contracts, subscriptions, renewal or attach rates, or customer advances as a funding source; revenue is one-off product sale of rice. (The long view had said: Across all 15 calls there is no license, AMC, subscription or installed-base revenue — sales are one-off FMCG shipments through distributors, and working capital runs the wrong way: inventory of Rs 4,451 crore (Mar-24) funded by bank debt rising from Rs 138 crore to Rs 460 crore, i.e. the company funds customers, not vice versa.)

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The India Gate franchise delivers clear channel leadership (GT 37.8%, MT 39.3%, e-commerce 41.2%) and management calls basmati branding core to margins, plus adjacency stretch into branded non-basmati (+35% in 9M FY26); but the calls show no sustained A&P disclosure and admit share of voice is being contested by regional and loose players. (The long view had said: India Gate is repeatedly evidenced as the world's No.1 branded basmati, commands a 20-25% premium over loose rice, carries sustained heavy A&P (60%+ share of voice, 4,200 GRP festive campaign, 200bps brand-equity gain) and has been stretched into Biryani Masala, meal mixes, regional rice and Uplife health rice.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: KRBL states leading share in all three channels and grew branded non-basmati 35% in 9M FY26, but the share figures are given as a single-point snapshot with domestic revenue flat year-on-year in Q3 — there is no evidence in this 12-month window of share persistently rising. (The long view had said: Explicit, repeatedly disclosed share numbers rise across the period — general trade 32% (Q1 FY23) to 36.9% (Q4 FY24, +440bps year-on-year) to 37.8% (Q3 FY26), e-commerce to 40-41% — and leadership is held across every channel, though modern-trade share has come off its Q4 FY24 peak of 55.4% to 38.7% in FY26 and recent gains are more defence than advance.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across a full domestic-strategy segment with four named pillars, there is no R&D spend, no named new products with revenue impact and no pipeline language; the only productivity gains cited come from publicly bred Pusa seed varieties available to the whole industry. (The long view had said: There is a steady, disclosed cadence of launches with revenue attached — regional rice Rs 212 crore (FY24) to branded non-basmati Rs 271 crore (+38% FY26), Biryani Masala at 10%+ modern-trade share within three months, Uplife health rice, edible oil Rs 12 crore — but no R&D spend is disclosed and the key seed varieties (Pusa 1885/1886) come from IARI, a public institute, not KRBL.)
- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The Sep 2025 call was convened because an Independent Director resigned citing board-process concerns, disclosing a INR58 crore export write-off, INR37 crore of unspent CSR and a recorded director dissent; the board is 4 promoter-family to 3 independents and surplus cash is being redirected from the core business into a ~INR1,000 crore real-estate venture. (The long view had said: Stable family leadership (Mittal/Gupta since before FY21) and a conservative balance sheet that swung to net cash of minus Rs 388 crore by Dec-25 sit against a weak promise-delivery record: the Rs 700 crore Saudi uplift and HoReCa distributor promised for Sept-Oct 2022 were still unfinalised in Feb 2026, the Rs 1,400-1,600 crore export jump guided in May 2024 never arrived (exports Rs 1,345 crore FY24 to Rs 1,555 crore FY26), and the ED matter is declined on every call.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 6 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Exports are concentrated in the Middle East, where March 2026 Iran/US/Israel tensions stranded cargo and forced routing through a handful of transshipment ports, and Q3 export revenue dropped ~37% year-on-year because bulk export volume was restricted; single-counterparty credit risk was demonstrated by the INR58cr write-off on one African buyer for FY23 shipments. No single customer is described as driving recurring results, and domestic distribution is broad. (The long view had said: The loss of a single 40-year Saudi distributor was called 'one of the biggest setbacks' and depressed exports for years; Iran and Iraq alone are ~40% of India's basmati exports and their payment/geopolitical problems cost KRBL an estimated Rs 600cr of revenue in FY24; by Feb 2026 Saudi was still being run through wholesalers with no long-term distributor finalised.)
- **New Entrants**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No structurally different new-format competitor is discussed defensively; the competitive pressure named is from existing loose and regional rice players, and in the newest channel — e-commerce — KRBL reports the leading share, indicating it has built rather than been displaced by the new format. (The long view had said: Q3 FY26 explicitly flags structurally cheaper loose and regional players compressing demand and margin in general and modern trade, and modern-trade share has drifted down (41.7% FY24 to 38.7% FY26); quick commerce is being embraced rather than resisted.)

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Cyclicality (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
