**Checks that improved:**

- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: The company entered the year with entry-segment demand recovering off a weak base and rate cuts/GST reduction triggering a visible upturn (January retail +21% y-o-y), i.e. positioned into a cyclical recovery rather than at its peak. | The long view had said: The company is well into an upcycle rather than near its bottom — post-GST-cut retails +21% in Jan'26 with management itself guiding to moderation on a high base.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Weak → Neutral. Now: There are genuine technology investments (connected vehicles with 3 lakh units on cloud, flex fuel, Gen AI in development cycle time), but hiring a CTO to 'accelerate its R&D transformation' signals the tech edge is aspirational rather than established. | The long view had said: Technology is a stated gap being bought or hired rather than an edge — premium relies on a Harley-Davidson licence, EV capability is supplemented by an Ather stake, and a new external CTO is being brought in to 'accelerate' R&D.
- **Advertisement and promotion** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: A&P spend was raised 22% in FY26 alongside 6 high-impact campaigns, and the spend visibly converted into share gains (entry segment +800bps, October share +370bps) while EBITDA margin still expanded. | The long view had said: A&P was raised 22% in FY26 with six high-impact campaigns and a traceable sales response (Glamour X campaign drove 70% year-on-year retail growth), though the earlier IPL-backed premium push (Mavrick/Karizma) did not scale as promised.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: Nine launches in FY26 filled white spaces and drove share gains across 100cc, 110cc, scooters and EV, and a dedicated CTO was hired for R&D transformation, though the framing of 'accelerate its R&D transformation' implies capability is still being built. | The long view had said: Consistent product cadence (9 launches in FY26, connected vehicles, flex-fuel, EV) but low historical R&D efficiency — the 440-platform premium bikes never reached the 10,000/month capacity built for them, and Hero is now importing an external CTO to 'accelerate its R&D transformation'.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Hero captured 800bps of entry-segment share year-on-year and grew ICE registrations 16.2% against 14.7% industry, evidence that weaker rivals are ceding ground. | The long view had said: Hero is gaining in scooters and 125cc, but the segments it targets are led by strong incumbents (Honda in scooters, established premium players) while Hero itself has been the historical share donator in scooters and premium.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Global dispatches grew 27%, then 77% (3x industry), then 41% year-on-year, with entry into Europe and the UK taking presence to 52 countries — credible and well executed. | The long view had said: Exports moved from a stated weak spot in FY23 to 41% volume growth and 7.5% share (+100bps) with premium at 40% of global mix, a multi-year improvement in execution.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: Tone is generally understated ('with pride and humility') and the CFO/CBO team does most of the substantive talking, but the promoter Executive Chairman is repeatedly thanked and the firm actively publicises its 'world's largest' status. | The long view had said: The culture reads promoter-centric rather than substance-first — both the Executive Director and the incoming CEO open their scripted remarks with personal tributes to the Executive Chairman before discussing the business.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: ASP rose 6%, then 4.2%, then 3% sequentially on mix and price and ICE margin expanded 90bps on pricing and LEAP savings, but a large part of FY26 growth was GST-driven volume rather than price/mix. | The long view had said: ASP is rising (+4.2% year-on-year, +3% quarter-on-quarter) on premiumisation and Xtec/125cc mix, but pricing power is demonstrably capped — a 2% price increase against high-single-digit BOM inflation, echoing the same 'price increases have lagged cost inflation' admission from 2022.
- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Neutral. Now: Capital deployment is via minority stakes in associates (Ather, Euler Motors) rather than complementary bolt-ons that improve OPM; the only P&L effect cited was a one-time ₹722 crore dilution gain, not operating improvement. | The long view had said: Capital deployed into minority stakes in loss-making EV startups (Ather, Euler) and repeated funding rounds into Hero FinCorp despite ~7% credit costs — not simple bolt-ons of already-strong businesses with post-deal OPM improvement.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Share rose from 30.9% (Q1) to 31.6% in October with gains across every key segment, and management explicitly identifies large under-indexed categories (scooters, premium, EV, global, parts) as remaining runway. | The long view had said: Genuine runway where it is under-indexed (scooters ~7%, EV, premium, global) and evidence of actual capture — four consecutive quarters of entry-segment share gain, scooters +48%, VIDA to 10.8% — though share in premium was promised in FY24 and delivered only slowly.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: The largest installed vehicle park in India gives a captive aftermarket position — parts, accessories and merchandise run ~₹1,500 crore a quarter and a ₹700 crore parts centre is being built to exploit it — though it is a strong position, not an absolute monopoly. | The long view had said: Near-monopoly in the customer's mind in Deluxe 100cc (91% share, highest since FY18) plus a captive aftermarket from the largest vehicle park in India feeding a record Rs1,673cr quarterly parts business.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Aftermarket captivity via the installed park gives some lock-in, but new-vehicle switching costs are effectively nil — Hero itself is taking share from rivals segment by segment, which cuts both ways. | The long view had said: A structurally captive aftermarket — the largest parked fleet on Indian roads served through ~6,000 touchpoints, now being expanded with a Rs700cr second parts centre — though two-wheeler buyers face low switching costs on the new-vehicle purchase itself.

*Unchanged: Distribution advantage; Convenience benefits; Profit margin; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Return on capital (headline); Structural end-market growth; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); People matter (talent development); Barriers to entry and rationality; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Candor; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Halo effect (caution); Intangible benefits; Disciplined stewards; Cost of working capital; Asset turn; Assurance benefits.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 8 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Share gains are explicit, quantified and repeated across all four calls: Vahan share 30.9% in Q1 to 31.6% in October (+370bps year-on-year), entry segment +800bps year-on-year, EV share 7% to 11.7%, and global dispatches +77% then +41% across 52 countries — and it was accompanied by record margins and the lowest inventory and receivables in recent years, so the share was not bought with profitability. (The long view had said: Recent share numbers are explicit and rising (four consecutive quarters of entry-segment gains, 91% in Deluxe 100, scooters to ~7%, VIDA to 10.8%, global to 7.5% up 100bps), but this follows years of erosion that analysts pressed on in FY24, so the record is recovery rather than persistent gain across the 5-year window.)

**That weakened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: The LEAP cost programme is cited as a margin driver in Q1, Q3 and Q4 FY26 alongside scale leadership in the no-frills entry 100cc segment (HF Deluxe gained 800bps year-on-year to an 11-quarter high), but management frames savings as flowing to margin expansion rather than back into price, and the stated strategy is premiumisation. (The long view had said: Hero's franchise is mass-commuter scale (world's largest two-wheeler maker 25 consecutive years, 91% Splendor share in Deluxe 100cc) combined with an explicit, repeatedly-cited cost programme (LEAP savings, BOM/value engineering, discretionary spend cuts) that is named as a margin driver in every recent quarter alongside 70%+ dividend payouts and strong cash generation.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Demand is created by direct consumer marketing — the 'Hathi Wala' emotional campaign, six high-impact brand campaigns in FY26, A&P up 22% — not by a professional prescriber choosing on the buyer's behalf; dealers are distribution, and financing penetration (65%) drives affordability, the opposite of low price sensitivity. (The long view had said: Sales run through a ~1,000 primary dealer / 6,000 touch-point network with heavy investment in dealer capability-building and trade relationships, and the new CEO frames value-chain trust as the core advantage — but the end customer, not a prescribing professional, chooses the bike and pays.)

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (LIKELY FIT); Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Pricing Power (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 1 worsened, 7 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The powertrain shift is a large-scale platform change against which Hero's ICE installed base and plant infrastructure are the legacy asset: every quarter of the last year discloses an EV investment drag (₹189cr, ₹252cr, ₹220cr) that pulls group EBITDA margin from ~17% ICE to ~14.5%, and management is simultaneously funding EV, flex-fuel, connectivity and Gen AI while hiring a new CTO to 'accelerate its R&D transformation'. (The long view had said: The ICE-to-EV powertrain shift is a large-scale platform change for an incumbent whose entire manufacturing and dealer infrastructure was built around commuter ICE motorcycles; Hero has been in a multi-year loss-making build-out (INR208-252 crore of EBITDA drag per quarter through FY26), took equity stakes in Ather and Euler, and analysts repeatedly probe why it is 'under-indexed' in electric two-wheelers versus peers.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW); Good Enough Goods (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
