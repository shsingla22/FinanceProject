# Bajaj Housing (BAJAJHFL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Bajaj Housing Finance is an Indian housing finance company (an HFC regulated by the Reserve Bank of India) that lends money against property. Its main product is home loans to individual borrowers, alongside loans against property, lease rental discounting to owners of commercial real estate, and construction/developer finance to builders — with home loans the dominant piece at roughly 54-57% of the loan book. It makes money on the gap between what it charges borrowers and what it pays to raise funds — portfolio yield around 9.1-9.9% against a cost of funds of 7.3-7.9%, leaving a gross spread of about 1.7-1.9% and a net interest margin near 3.8-4.1% — topped up by fee income and income from assigning (selling down) loans; it borrows from banks, the money market (NCDs and commercial paper) and National Housing Bank refinance rather than taking deposits. It operates only in India, through 226 branches across 182 locations as of March 2026, with assets under management of over Rs. 1,40,000 crore, growing about 23% a year, and very low bad loans (gross NPA 27 bps, net NPA 11 bps).

**The parts of the business:**

- **Home Loans (HL)** — Loans to individuals to buy homes — the company's largest and defining business at about 54-57% of AUM (management calls prime housing a 'scale builder'), growing roughly 18-24% year on year.
- **Loan Against Property (LAP)** — Loans given to borrowers who pledge property they already own as security — a smaller but steady slice at roughly 10-11% of AUM, growing 24-32% in recent quarters.
- **Lease Rental Discounting (LRD)** — Lending against the rental income streams of commercial properties, including large 'marquee' and REIT-linked transactions — the second-biggest product at about 20-22% of AUM and the fastest grower recently (up 44% in Q4 FY26).
- **Developer Finance / Construction Finance (DF)** — Construction funding to real estate developers for their projects, at roughly 11-12% of AUM; growth slowed to 13-18% lately because funded projects were repaying cash back faster.
- **Sambhav Loans SBU (Near Prime and Affordable)** — A separate business unit set up about 18 months before Feb 2026 to reach lower-ticket, higher-yield customers — 'Near Prime' at Rs. 40-60 lakh ticket sizes with 9-11% yields in the top 36 markets, and 'Affordable' at Rs. 15-35 lakh with 11-13% yields in outskirts, tier-4 and rural areas — currently disbursing Rs. 325-350 crore a month with a target of Rs. 600 crore-plus monthly in 12-15 months.
- **Other loans** — A small residual category of about 1.7% of the portfolio that management does not break out further.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Bajaj Housing is a very well-run company operating in a business that does not reward being well-run. The management-related checks are the standout: candor scores +2 because the company published an explicit FY26 assessment, cut it openly with reasons when competition bit, and then came back in the Q4 FY26 call with a panel comparing what actually happened against what it had promised — with AUM, operating costs, bad loans and returns all in line or better. Stewardship also scores +2 for an explicitly organic strategy, conservative leverage (an 8x threshold against capital adequacy of 22-29% versus the 15% required) and for setting capital aside ahead of regulatory clarity. That behaviour is what earns the Corporate Culture pattern a strong fit. But the surrounding industry checks are uniformly negative: barriers to entry, mini-monopolies, oligopoly structure, switching costs, intangible pricing power and obscurity all score -2, which pulls Industry Structure to -1.67 and Return on Capital to -1.0. The combined rating of 45 out of 100 ('Mixed', two stars) is essentially this split — 38 on business quality, 45 on multibagger fit, 56 on risk safety.

The two patterns that fit strongly, Low Price Plus and Market Share Gainers, come from the same engine — and so does the biggest risk. The company is deliberately built as a thin-spread, high-volume, low-loss lender: operating costs fell from about 22.1% of net income in Q2 FY25 to 19.7% for FY26 with a stated 14-15% target in three to four years, gross spread is only 1.7-1.9%, bad loans are 27 basis points and funding is AAA-rated. That cost position is what lets it undercut and grow AUM 23-26% a year — from Rs.1.02 lakh crore in Q2 FY25 to over Rs.1.40 lakh crore in Q4 FY26 — against industry individual-home-loan growth of about 9.5%, and importantly the share was won without loosening credit, since bad loans actually improved from 29 to 27 basis points. The catch is that a low price is not a moat. The same commodity pricing that lets Bajaj Housing take share lets everyone else take it back: portfolio yield fell from 9.9% to 9.1% and gross spread from 2.3% to 1.7%, all growth is volume with price a persistent drag, roughly 20% of the book runs off each year with about 40% of balance-transfer applications leaving within 90 days, and 10-15% of that goes largely to public sector banks. Hence Pricing Power, Toll Roads and Cost to Replicate are all no-fit, and Good Enough Goods is flagged high risk. The strength and the vulnerability are one and the same trait.

The remaining risks sort cleanly into one that is genuinely outside the company's control and several that are not. Government Dependency is the other high risk: the RBI's November 2025 consolidated circular quietly removed an illustration and forced capital to be held against all undisbursed home-loan and construction-finance tranches, taking roughly 300 basis points off Tier-1 capital in a single quarter with no clarity on what comes next — a reminder that in this business the rulebook can move the numbers more than the operator can. Cyclicality is rated watch because spreads compress mechanically as rates fall (net interest margin down from 4.4% to 3.8%), though the book is granular retail home loans across about 3.3 lakh customers rather than lumpy corporate exposure. Technology and stakeholder concentration are both low risk — the tech story is incremental efficiency (93% e-agreement penetration, 88% digital onboarding) rather than a platform bet, and funding spans 17-18 banks across money markets (49-53%), banks (37-41%) and the National Housing Bank (9-11%), with parent-sourced leads a stable, disclosed 12-15% of home-loan disbursals. The most interesting thing in the file is the escape route management is already building: the Sambhav business, started from scratch and now doing Rs.410-425 crore of monthly disbursals at 9-11% near-prime and 11-13% affordable yields in tier-4 and rural markets, is explicitly cited as the silver lining to the commodity-pricing risk. That partly-formed move sits behind the partial-fit ratings on Innovation Dominance, Forward Integrators, Brand Strength and Friendly Middleman — capabilities that are real but not yet decisive. Overall resilience is marked unknown, which is the honest reading: an excellent management team is executing well inside a structure that gives it very little to defend.
