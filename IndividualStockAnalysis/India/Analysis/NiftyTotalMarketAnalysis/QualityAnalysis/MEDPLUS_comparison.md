Now: The FY26 margin story looks less like durable skill once Q1 FY27 consolidated EBITDA fell to 3.5% from 5.8% and mature-store EBITDA dropped from 13.1% to 10.4% as the private-label incentive lever was eased — results here are lever-driven and reversible, not a stable capability. | The long view had said: Granular cohort disclosure (store EBITDA by age bracket, breakeven rates, allocated vs unallocated costs) lets skill be separated from headline growth, and the modest 3.5-5.8% consolidated EBITDA margin leaves little room for a halo.

Now: Management candidly owns errors — admitting staff over-pushed private label and issuing a corrigendum on a misstated operating cash flow — yet repeatedly declines to quantify franchisee economics and refused to explain the rationale behind a shelved board-approved capex. | The long view had said: Management repeatedly owns errors plainly — admitting employees overpushed private label and annoyed customers, publishing corrigenda on cash-flow numbers, and saying outright when it has not tracked data (GLP-1).

Now: Reliability of supply is a real part of the offer — management credits new warehouses with better on-shelf availability driving same-store growth — but the calls give no evidence that customers pay a premium for that trust. | The long view had said: The founding proposition is certainty of genuine, non-spurious medicine, which management says resonates most in smaller towns, though price remains a co-equal part of the pitch rather than secondary to trust.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 9 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Forward Integrators**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: MedPlus owns its entire route to the customer — 5,476 stores and 2.9mn sq ft as of Jun 2026, its own warehouses, its own labels — and is now layering franchising so third parties fund growth: ~500+ franchise outlets, ~310 of FY26's 618 additions franchised, company-owned stores being converted to franchise, and FY27 guidance of 800 net adds including franchisees. (The long view had said: MedPlus owns its shelf (5,476 stores, 2.9m sq ft), runs a profitable owned omnichannel with two-hour delivery, and from FY26 added franchising (~500 franchise outlets, 800 net planned for FY27, COCO conversions offered to 3-year staff) — but franchising is not third-party-funded growth as the pattern requires, since MedPlus puts in the ~INR10 lakh capex itself, and the Jul 2026 backward-integration plan (food park, oil extraction) was shelved.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Growth is clearly ahead of market — pharmacy revenue +15.6% (Q3), +23.4% (Q4), +21.8% (Q1 FY27) with 10%+ SSSG and network up from 4,552 to 5,476 stores — but management never cites a market-share number or names competitors, and closures are rising (28→46→77→52 per quarter, incl. 27 franchisee withdrawals in Q1 FY27), so share gain is inferred, not evidenced. (The long view had said: Network grew from 3,557 stores in 7 states (Feb 2023) to 5,476 in 13 states (Jul 2026) with pharmacy revenue up 21-23% Y-o-Y, implying share gain against fragmented mom-and-pop trade; however management never quotes a market-share number or names competitors, and admits same-store growth is only 'inflation and slightly above that' with cannibalisation from densification.)

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Innovation Dominance (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Revenue is entirely retail-consumer funded across ~5,500 stores with no regulator-set pricing, tenders or subsidies discussed in any of the four calls; the only policy touchpoint is a one-off cost item — a INR70.59mn non-recurring charge from the new Labour Code in Q3 FY26 — which affected costs, not the revenue model. (The long view had said: No revenue comes from government tenders, subsidies or fixed infrastructure — it is private retail across 13 states — but the cost and pricing base is policy-exposed: state statutory wage revisions hit employee costs hard in Q1 FY27, and management explicitly counts government promotion of generics as a driver of its private-label thesis.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Demand is functional and repeat-purchase (medicines, FMCG); no volume decline is attributed to changing habits in any call. Management is actively extending into preventive health — diagnostics revenue and margin rose every quarter (EBITDA 15.3% in Q2 FY26 to INR65.9mn in Q1 FY27) — though the wellness/concierge extension was put on hold in Q1 FY27. (The long view had said: Two live preference migrations run through the calls — toward generics/affordability and toward online ordering — and MedPlus has pivoted hard into the first, taking private-label pharma from 7.9% of GMV in Q1 FY24 to 17.7% by Q3 FY25. The pivot has limits: share stalled and then slipped from 22% (Q4 FY26) to 20% (Q1 FY27) after the push irritated customers, forcing a rebalance back toward branded.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Technological Innovation (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 11 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
