# Indian Oil Corporation Ltd. (IOC) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: ENERGY*

Indian Oil Corporation (IOC) is India's largest oil refining and marketing company — a government-owned firm that buys crude oil (mostly imported), refines it into fuels like petrol (MS), diesel (HSD), LPG cooking gas, aviation fuel and lubricants, and then sells them across India through a network of over 42,000 retail outlets and a large pipeline and distribution system. It makes money on the gap between what crude costs and what refined products fetch (the refining margin, or GRM, reported at around $8-11 per barrel on a normalized basis in recent quarters) plus marketing volumes, selling roughly 105 million tonnes of product in FY26 on revenue of about Rs. 8.86 lakh crore and profit after tax of Rs. 36,802 crore. It also sells petrochemicals, natural gas/LNG and biofuels, and takes government compensation when it is forced to sell domestic LPG below cost (IOC's share of the Rs. 30,000 crore package was Rs. 14,486 crore). It operates almost entirely within India — refineries at Panipat, Paradip, Gujarat, Digboi and elsewhere — with a small overseas footprint through an Abu Dhabi exploration joint venture and LNG exports to Nepal.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Refineries** — Turns crude oil into fuels at its refineries; the biggest engine of the business, running at over 100% of nameplate capacity with a record 75.5 million tonnes of crude processed in FY26, and group capacity is being expanded from about 80.8 to 98 million tonnes a year with roughly Rs. 72,000 crore of investment.
- **Pipeline** — Moves crude and finished products around the country through the company's pipeline network, carrying a record 105.6 million tonnes in FY26 at about 74% utilisation — the backbone that gets fuel from refineries to selling points cheaply.
- **Marketing** — Sells the fuel to end customers through petrol pumps, LPG cylinders, bulk and aviation channels; total sales hit a record 105.117 MMT in FY26 (up about 5%), with 42,818 retail outlets after 2,597 were added during the year, and management calls this the source of its national market share.
- **Lubricants (lube business)** — Sells engine oils and industrial lubricants — a smaller but high-growth piece, with record sales of 905 thousand tonnes in FY26, up about 16%.
- **Petrochemicals** — Converts refinery streams into plastics and chemical products such as polymers, PTA, MEG, acrylics and oxo-alcohols; sales were a record 3.396 MMT in FY26, and management wants to lift petrochemical integration from about 6% of throughput to 15% because global margins are currently weak but Indian demand is under-served.
- **Gas** — Buys, imports and sells natural gas and LNG, including city gas distribution (CGD) to homes and vehicles; FY26 sales were 7,276 thousand tonnes including 188 TMT of CGD, with a mid-term Henry Hub-linked LNG supply deal signed with Trafigura and India's first LNG-by-road exports to Nepal.
- **Biofuels and renewable energy** — Blends ethanol into petrol (19.97% achieved), sells compressed biogas under the IndiGreen brand, and is building green hydrogen, sustainable aviation fuel, EV charging and a 31 gigawatt renewable portfolio by 2030 through subsidiary Terra Clean Limited — currently small in revenue but where a growing share of future capex is being directed, with a net-zero target of 2046.
- **Exploration joint venture (Urja Bharat Pte Ltd)** — A 50:50 overseas joint venture that announced an oil discovery in an onshore block in Abu Dhabi in January 2026 — mentioned as a milestone rather than a material earnings contributor.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Low Price Plus — LIKELY FIT
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and the only computable check supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** Project Sprint, launched Q1 FY26 with 'cost leadership' as an explicit pillar and a 20% cost-reduction target, delivered an itemised Rs 2,200 crore of savings in FY26 (R&M, energy efficiency, supply-chain) with Rs 2,500 crore targeted for FY27 — a real, quantified cost culture on enormous scale. > "we have achieved measurable savings of approximately INR2,200 crores during financial year 25-26 on account of Project SPRINT" — *management, on an earnings call*

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across all five calls earnings are driven by crude price, crack spreads and inventory swings rather than volumes: PAT fell from ~INR34,781cr in 9M FY24 to INR5,697cr in 9M FY25, GRM collapsed from $13.26/bbl to $3.69/bbl, a single quarter carried an INR6,500cr inventory loss versus an INR3,500cr gain the prior quarter, and Q4 FY26 saw crude jump ~30% q-o-q on the US-Iran conflict — all while INR72,000cr+ of refinery expansion capex is being spent into the cycle.

> "GRM in 9 months '24 -'25 was $3.69 per barrel vis -a-vis the 9 months for '23 -'24, which was $13.26 per barrel." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Underlying demand is flow/consumption-linked and grows steadily (PPAC ~4.65% growth, record sales volumes of 105.1 MMT in FY26), and the balance sheet absorbs the cycle (net debt/equity 0.32) allowing continued investment through the downturn.*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 5 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 2% and 11% (±3.1 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 2% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 3%).

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Retail fuel and LPG pricing is not free — the company absorbed LPG under-recoveries funded by borrowings and then depended on a Union Cabinet grant (IOC share INR14,486cr, paid in 12 monthly instalments) booked as revenue; ethanol blending targets, excise duty timing, and the state-mandated 'energy security at affordable cost' role all sit above commercial pricing, and by May 2026 management declined to say when market-linked daily pricing would return.

> "In August 2025, the Union Cabinet has approved compensation amounting to INR30,000 crores to the 3 public sector oil marketing companies, Indian Oil, BPC and HPC, for the under -recoveries incurred on sale of domestic LPG." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Some diversification away from policy-set fuel pricing into petrochemicals (6%→15% integration target), gas, lubes and renewables, though these remain a minority of earnings.*
