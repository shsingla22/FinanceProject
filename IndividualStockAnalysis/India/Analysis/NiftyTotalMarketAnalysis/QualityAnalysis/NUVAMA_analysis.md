# Nuvama Wealth (NUVAMA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Nuvama is an India-listed financial services firm (NSE: NUVAMA, demerged from Edelweiss and listed in 2023, with PAG as majority shareholder from 2021) whose main business is wealth management — advising well-off individuals on where to put their money and selling them investment products. It serves two client tiers: 'affluent and HNI' clients (roughly INR 1-25 crore) through the Nuvama Wealth brand, and 'ultra-HNI' clients (INR 25 crore plus) through Nuvama Private, using around 900 and 115 relationship managers respectively at the time of listing, across 65-70 locations in India plus an offshore office in DIFC. It earns money from commissions and annuity/trail fees on managed products (AIF, PMS, mutual funds) and other investment products (fixed income, MLDs, insurance), from interest on loans made against clients' shares and ESOPs, from fees for managing its own funds, and from fees for servicing institutional and capital-markets clients. In FY26 total revenue was about INR 3,100 crore with operating profit after tax of about INR 1,050 crore, with the wealth cluster historically contributing around 70% of both revenue and profit.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Nuvama Wealth (affluent and HNI segment)** — Sells a full basket of investments, lending, exchange-traded products and estate-planning services (e.g. the 'Virasat' legacy-planning service) to clients below roughly INR 25 crore, through about 900 in-house relationship managers plus external wealth managers who bring in around a quarter of the business; its core growth engine, 'managed products and investment solutions' (MPIS), grew revenue ~38% in FY26 and now makes up about 60% of this segment's revenue.
- **Nuvama Private (ultra-HNI segment)** — A more bespoke service for clients above INR 25 crore, covering not just investments (said to be only 25-30% of their needs) but wealth structuring, estate planning and cross-border business advice, served by roughly 115 relationship managers; net flows into annuity products reached about INR 8,000 crore in the first nine months of FY25, up 88% year on year.
- **Asset Management** — The company's own fund-management arm, started around 2021, running alternative funds (AIFs) and some PMS — including a market-leading pre-IPO/'Crossover' private equity strategy, a venture debt fund, a long-short hedge fund, an absolute-return product and a commercial real estate fund whose first close was about INR 1,700 crore; AUM was about INR 6,200 crore two years in, and the firm has in-principle approval for a mutual fund licence and plans to launch SIF products.
- **Lending / loan book (inside the wealth businesses)** — Loans given to wealth clients against their investments and ESOPs, deliberately shrunk for a while to improve returns on capital and then grown again — the book closed FY26 at about INR 4,900 crore, up 27% for the year, with net interest income contributing roughly 20-22% of Nuvama Wealth's revenue.
- **Asset Services** — Custody, clearing and back-office servicing for institutional and fund clients, including foreign high-frequency trading firms and domestic PMS and AIF managers, with RTA services and trustee solutions being added to become a 'one-stop shop'; management calls it a key growth and monitorable segment, and its profit grew about 14% in FY26 despite a disruption at the end of Q1.
- **Capital Markets (Investment Banking and Institutional Equities)** — Deal-making and institutional broking/research — the investment bank also supports ultra-HNI promoters with their business needs — and it was the one segment management flagged as declining, with revenue moderating in FY26 while other segments grew.
- **One Nuvama** — Not a separate business but a cross-selling and synergy programme launched in 2023-24 to make the wealth, asset management, asset services and capital markets arms feed each other, which management frames as the basis of its 'full stack, multiproduct platform' pitch.

### The story in depth

Nuvama Wealth comes out of the three reviews as a well-run company in a genuinely growing market, rated Decent at 58 out of 100 (45% × 67 on business quality, 30% × 56 on multibagger fit, 25% × 44 on risk safety). The strongest scores sit with the people running it: management scores highest of any quality area at 1.5, with full marks for candor (volunteering unflattering detail such as splitting an interest-income shortfall into its INR4 crore and INR2-3 crore causes, an INR11 crore one-time labour-code hit, and the loss of a large asset servicing client), for capital discipline (shrinking the loan book to repair returns before regrowing it, and refusing to overpay for relationship-manager talent when rivals were bidding it up), and for a decade-long independent thesis executed through the demerger from Edelweiss into the One Nuvama programme. This is the same evidence that makes Corporate Culture a strong pattern fit, alongside the same MD/CEO throughout. The end market backs them: client assets grew from INR2.9 lakh crore to INR4.5-4.6 lakh crore over the period, and share gains are quantified rather than asserted — institutional equities from 5.4% to over 6.2%, investment banking from 18% to 19% of value, and 22% of incremental AIF/PMS registrations — which is why Market Share Gainers is also a strong fit.

The interesting part is that the traits producing the patterns are, in several cases, the same traits producing the risks. The one-stop platform — wealth, lending, asset management, investment banking and custody, delivered through roughly 1,200 relationship managers and 65-70 locations — earns full marks for convenience and underpins both the Forward Integrators and Cost to Replicate patterns, yet the very same calls concede that private-equity-backed entrants can fund a comparable build, which is why New Entrants is rated elevated and the industry-structure check scores negative on low barriers and irrational behaviour at the margin. The push into recurring revenue (roughly 60% of both wealth and private revenues now annuity-based, on about INR54,000 crore of assets) is what cushions the cyclicality risk down to elevated rather than high, but part of that shift was forced rather than chosen: the SEBI circular capping upfront AIF commissions cut reported wealth revenue growth to 16% from a like-for-like 30%, which is the single clearest item behind the high government-dependency rating. Distribution reach — management's stated competitive currency and the basis of the Friendly Middleman pattern, with about 25% of wealth business arriving through external wealth managers — is simultaneously a reliance on intermediaries the company does not own.

The weak spots are concentrated in economics rather than in execution. Return on capital scores worst of anything in the framework: for every INR100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings, the business generated negative INR19 of operating cash this year, and the return-on-capital area score is negative overall. Pricing is the other soft point — Pricing Power is a clear no-fit, with no price increases anywhere in the calls and yields instead drifting down and being defended: private annuity retention guided to 90-95 basis points with quarters at 0.85%, and 50 basis points of yield compression in asset services. That fee pressure connects straight to the good-enough-goods risk, where cheaper, more tax-efficient vehicles — specifically SIF, which appears again under new entrants — are pulling flows away from the company's AIF strategies and pushed it to apply for a mutual fund licence. Concentration is a live rather than theoretical worry: one asset-services client leaving in July 2025 dented that segment's revenue and float balances for about three quarters. Group revenue growth swinging from roughly 45% to roughly 8% across comparable nine-month periods, alongside the negative cash return, is why the overall resilience reads as strained despite the quality of the management team.

### Toll Roads — PARTIAL
*"Small cost, can't skip it"*
