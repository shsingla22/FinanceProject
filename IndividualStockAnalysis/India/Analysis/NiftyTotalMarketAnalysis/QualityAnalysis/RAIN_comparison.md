- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: The customer base is entirely corporate/industrial and the pitch is correctly matched to it — reliability, technical service and supply continuity rather than brand — though switching-cost lock-in is not demonstrated. | The long view had said: The customer base is correctly corporate-oriented with reliability and TCO positioning, but switching-cost lock-in is weak given customers push back hard on price each cycle.

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: RAIN's demand and cost position are repeatedly shaped by policy rather than by its own actions: Chinese government caps on national smelting capacity are cited as the structural driver of where future CPC demand comes from, Indian import relaxations were the direct reason Indian CPC plants ran at optimum capacity in Q1 2026, and trade measures/tariffs recur in every call. Pricing is not regulator-set and US tariffs are explicitly immaterial, which bounds the exposure. (The long view had said: Indian calcination capacity was throttled for six years by pet coke import restrictions, with earnings turning on CAQM relief in Feb 2024, Supreme Court-ordered emission rules, annual GPC import allocations and a still-unresolved SEZ exemption; Cement demand is anchored on government infrastructure spend and the Amaravati capital project, and the battery/ESM R&D programme is funded by Canada, Ontario, the EU and Flanders.)
- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Structurally lower-cost entrants are pressing on both ends: Chinese carbon producers already supply the Indonesian market RAIN wants to grow into, Asian resin imports have compressed Advanced Materials volumes and realised prices, and management notes commodity volatility 'has drawn new entrants into certain raw material streams', i.e. new bidders for its feedstock. Management's answer is cost and logistics optimisation rather than price-matching. (The long view had said: Chinese exporters function as a structurally lower-cost entrant that repeatedly reset global CPC and carbon pricing (analysts explicitly ask about 'Chinese dumping'), management notes volatility 'has drawn new entrants into certain raw material streams', and in Cement pan-India acquirers entering South India forced volume loss and competitive pricing.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: In Advanced Materials, RAIN's European-made products are priced against Asian imports and management concedes competing on a low-cost basis against structurally cheaper regions is inherently difficult, with intensified Asian pricing pressure already hurting both volumes and realised prices — the classic good-enough attack on a value-added position. The Carbon segment is better protected, where customers pay for consistent quality, technical support and supply reliability. (The long view had said: Management describes itself as a converter earning a processing spread rather than a premium franchise, and has faced sustained price pushback — cheaper Chinese CPC eroding margins from 2023, customers pushing back aggressively on CTP prices while tar costs rose, and repeated 'margin challenges driven by market competition'. Specification stringency and reliability provide only partial protection.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Two technology shifts are reshaping RAIN's input base: battery anode material producers diverting green petroleum coke and needle coke to synthetic graphite, and the blast-furnace to electric-arc-furnace transition shrinking the coal tar pool that pitch is distilled from. These are squeezing feedstock rather than obsoleting RAIN's product, and management has responded with alternative binder and raw-material substitution work. (The long view had said: Management repeatedly states there is no credible substitute for CPC/CTP in carbon anodes, and the innovation cadence described is incremental and value-adding (ACP pellets, HHCR, resins) rather than platform-resetting. The battery/lithium-ion theme is treated as adjacent demand and a feedstock-competition question, not as displacement of the core.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
