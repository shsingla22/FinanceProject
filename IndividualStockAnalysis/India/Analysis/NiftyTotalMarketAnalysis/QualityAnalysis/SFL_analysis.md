# Sheela Foam (SFL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Sheela Foam (stock symbol SFL) is an India-based maker of polyurethane foam and finished mattresses, selling to households under its Sleepwell and Kurlon brands and selling plain and technical foam to other manufacturers such as auto, furniture and Indian Railways suppliers. It makes money by converting bought-in chemicals (mainly TDI and polyol, its biggest cost swing) into foam and mattresses, then selling them through a dealer and exclusive-showroom network — roughly 600 net new showrooms added in FY26 — plus its own websites, online marketplaces, and small-town distributors. India is the core business; overseas it owns operating companies in Australia (Joyce, INR422 crores revenue in FY26) and Spain (INR391 crores), and is building a retail and outsourced-manufacturing presence in Dubai and the wider GCC. Consolidated India core EBITDA margins ran about 10.7% in FY26 on 11% revenue growth, and the company paid its first-ever dividend that year after integrating Kurlon and cutting debt.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Mattress business (Sleepwell and Kurlon brands)** — Sells branded finished mattresses to retail consumers through dealers and exclusive showrooms; the largest and most-discussed part of the company, with volumes up 12% and value up 10% in FY26, and Kurlon (acquired October 2023, ~INR890 crores annualised at the time) now fully merged and lifted from mid-single-digit to roughly SFL-level margins.
- **Foam business** — Sells foam as an input to other industries — auto, furniture, and Indian Railways among the customers named — with FY26 volume growth of 18% and value growth of 14%, and management calling out the highest-ever annual foam production; its value growth lags volumes when raw-material prices fall because selling prices track input costs.
- **U2O / "unorganized to organized" (earlier called STI or Small Town India, brand Tarang)** — A low-price mattress push into small towns sold through existing FMCG distributors rather than the regular mattress dealers, now reaching 8,400 dealers in over 5,000 towns across 24 states; still small in rupees — about INR75 crores by December 2025 at a INR120 crore run rate — but growing fastest, with FY26 value growth of 111%.
- **E-commerce (brand.com plus marketplaces)** — Direct online selling through the company's own websites and platforms like Amazon; roughly INR180 crores in the first nine months of FY26, with own-website sales up 136% and platform sales up 39% in FY26.
- **Australia — Joyce** — The Australian foam and bedding subsidiary, INR422 crores of revenue in FY26 with EBITDA margin improved to about 10% on waste reduction, price renegotiation and supply-chain restructuring.
- **Spain** — The European foam operation, INR391 crores of revenue in FY26 at a 10.4% EBITDA margin (up from 8.4%), helped by better pricing discipline after several years of weak, uncertain European demand.
- **Middle East / GCC (Dubai)** — A newer market entered through exclusive retail stores in each Emirate, tie-ups with large local retail chains, online sales via Amazon and noon.com, and locally outsourced manufacturing of Sheela Foam and Kurlon products with a UAE partner; described as an early-stage but growing footprint, with no separate revenue figure given.
- **Furlenco** — A furniture business acquired in August 2023 that started as an online model and is now being folded into Sleepwell and Kurlon stores (40-plus integrated stores, targeting 100+); INR370 crores of revenue in FY26, up over 60%, with PAT of about INR60 crores, and it raised INR125 crores of fresh equity including INR30 crores from Sheela Foam.
- **Staqo (IT business)** — A technology subsidiary providing enterprise workflow, analytics and AI-related digital solutions; the smallest piece named, at INR70 crores of revenue in FY26, positioned as a capability for the group rather than a standalone growth engine.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

*"The name itself sells"*

**What the calls show:** Sleepwell plus Kurlon give the group the No.1 and No.2 branded positions nationally, backed by sustained A&P (4.5–8.6% of sales, World Cup campaign), consistent brand-led growth (Sleepwell mattress +26% in Q3FY24, broad-based Sleepwell+Kurlon growth in FY26), stretch into adjacent categories (pillows, home comfort) and export of the brands into GCC exclusive stores.

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 1 check supporting).

**What the calls show:** There is a genuine cost-and-scale thread — Tarang/U2O priced at ~INR2,000 against cotton mattresses and deliberately piggy-backed on existing FMCG distributors to avoid a dedicated cost structure, plus Kurlon synergies (10% better foam yield, lower freight, waste reduction in Australia) — but this is one segment, not a company-wide low-cost culture, and margins are built on brand premium elsewhere.

### Pricing Power — PARTIAL
*"Raises prices, keeps the customers"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (1 of 2 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Price increases are taken and passed through without volume loss (FY26: mattress volume +12%, value +10%; ASPs rising in offline, e-com and U2O with explicit premiumisation), and management says a 5% rise 'is not a determining factor' for a mattress buyer — but the increases are inflation/raw-material recovery of 4–5%, and in FY23–FY24 pricing was flat-to-negative with ASP pressure and margin compression, so this is cost pass-through more than pricing power above inflation.

> "I generally take the inflation, which is a low number, and some raw material changes that come. But it is of the order of between 4-5% here." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹46 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ❌ Operating margin moved from about 10% (early years) to 9% (recent years) — no widening trend.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.3× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Earnings swing with polyol/TDI commodity prices — management repeatedly says direction is unclear and by FY26 describes INR40-50/kg moves multiple times a month, and consolidated EBITDA margins have ranged from ~7.5% to ~11.5% across the period; the industrial/B2B foam book also tracks auto and furniture customers. Mitigated because the core is replacement-driven consumer mattresses, not customer capex.

> "the pricing of TDI and polyol is like a crystal ball gazing at the moment... Now you suddenly have INR40 and INR50 at a time happening multiple times in a month" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/consumer demand base (mattresses as repeat consumer purchase) rather than customer capex; countercyclical capacity and acquisition investment (Kurlon, Furlenco, Jamalpur) while margins were depressed*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 0 of the last 11 years with swings of ±6 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 7% and 15% (±2.3 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 79% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 5% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Management itself says ~85% of the Indian market is unorganized/traditional, with cotton mattresses at INR800-1,200 and local operators covering loose foam undercutting branded product — Tarang at ~INR2,000 and FitRest exist precisely to fight that. The MBO/dealer channel compounds it: Kurlon prices had to be corrected to stay competitive and dealer margin and incentive schemes are an explicit lever, meaning the intermediary strongly influences the sale.

> "around 85% of its market currently occupied by unorganized and traditional mattress segment" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Partial only — brand-led premiumisation with rising ASPs online and offline, and scale/distribution advantages after the Kurlon merger that a small local maker cannot replicate*

- ✅ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs held near 44% (was 45%) — no gross-level price erosion.
- ✅ Operating margin (10%) remains near its historical peak (15%) — no collapse in the premium.
- ✅ Operating margin held (about 10% then 9%) — no drift down under competition.
