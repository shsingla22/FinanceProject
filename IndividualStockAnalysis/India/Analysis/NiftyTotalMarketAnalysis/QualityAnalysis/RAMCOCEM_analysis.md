# The Ramco Cements Ltd. (RAMCOCEM) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CEMENT & CEMENT PRODUCTS*

The Ramco Cements Limited (RAMCOCEM) makes and sells cement in India, mainly across the southern and eastern states — its plants, grinding units and markets covered in the calls include Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh (Kolimigundla/Kurnool), Karnataka and Odisha, with headquarters in Chennai. It digs limestone from its own mines, burns it into clinker at integrated plants and grinds that into cement, which it sells in bags and bulk to individual house builders through its dealer/trade channel and to infrastructure and construction projects through the non-trade/builder channel. Money is made on the spread between the selling price per tonne and costs — chiefly fuel (pet coke and coal), power and freight — so results swing with cement prices, fuel prices and volumes; management tracks the business in millions of tonnes sold and EBITDA per tonne (about Rs. 894–1,007 per tonne in FY24 quarters, on quarterly revenue of roughly Rs. 2,000–2,350 crores). By Feb 2024 it had about 16 MTPA of clinker and 22 MTPA of cement capacity, was heading for roughly 17.5 million tonnes of sales in FY24, and carried net debt of about Rs. 4,993 crores from a long capacity-building programme.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Cement (the core business)** — Manufacturing and selling grey cement from integrated plants and grinding units in the south and east — essentially the whole company: 4.61 million tonnes sold in Q2 FY24 at 82% utilisation, with FY25 volumes guided at 19–20 million tonnes.
- **Premium / specialised products ("right products for right applications")** — Higher-priced branded cement variants developed in-house through the company's R&D centre and customised to particular customer and application needs, sold in both trade and non-trade channels; they carry a price premium of roughly Rs. 5–10 per bag and had grown to 27–28% of volumes by FY24, with management targeting a much larger share over the next few years.
- **Dry mix / dry mortar products** — A newer line of ready-to-use dry construction mixes, with plants being built at RR Nagar and Salem in Tamil Nadu (started Dec 2022–Feb 2023) and further units in Andhra Pradesh and Odisha commissioned by March 2024 — a small add-on to cement, described only in terms of plant commissioning rather than sales.
- **Captive green power (wind + Waste Heat Recovery Systems)** — Wind farms and waste-heat recovery units at the cement plants that supply the factories' own electricity rather than being sold outside — green power rose to 38% of usage in Q2 FY24 (targeted 40% in FY24 and 45% in FY25), and switching wind power from sale to captive use was a main reason power and fuel cost per tonne fell from Rs. 1,989 to Rs. 1,358.
- **Limestone mining and land** — Owning and buying limestone-bearing mining leases and land next to its plants — for example an Andhra Pradesh/Karnataka purchase adding about 140 million tonnes of reserves — which secures the key raw material and is also the gateway to entering new regions, since management says it cannot expand north or west without mining leases there.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Ramco Cements is winning in the market. Volumes grew 38% year-on-year in the second quarter of FY24 and 33% in the first half, with plant utilisation climbing from 66% to 82% — growth well ahead of the market that management itself attributes to share gains. That earns a LIKELY FIT on the Market Share Gainers pattern. Behind it sits a genuinely differentiated product effort: an in-house research centre produced a 'right product for right application' range whose premium share rose from 25% to 28%, and the company is the exclusive supplier on certain infrastructure projects — enough for a LIKELY FIT on Innovation Dominance. The economics of the day-to-day business are attractive too. After direct input costs the company keeps ₹79 of every ₹100 of sales, and cash comes back from customers before suppliers need to be paid, so customers effectively fund the working capital. The end market is expanding, with a ₹14 lakh crore infrastructure and housing budget allocation and a Tamil Nadu market that has compounded 4-5% a year over a decade. Fuel costs are also coming off a bad patch — pet coke down from around $200 — with profit per ton recovering from ₹575 to ₹894 to ₹1,007.

The trouble is that the very thing producing the share gains is also the biggest problem. Growth here is bought with capacity, and the capacity is expensive: ₹9,800 crore spent between FY19 and FY24 for 10 million tons, with capex per ton among the highest in the industry, an analyst putting it at roughly $200 a ton against peers at $70-80, and returns of only 10-12%. Every ₹1 of assets produces just ₹0.54 of sales. So the same spending that drives the Market Share Gainers pattern is exactly what scores negative on the asset-turn and growth-capex checks, sinks Return on Capital into negative territory, and pushes Low Price Plus down to only PARTIAL — the cost work itself is real and itemised (waste heat recovery meeting about half of one plant's power, green power going from 22% to 38% and on to 45%, savings of ₹50-100 a ton), but the scale-and-returns half of the test fails. The same pattern repeats on the product side. The premium range that supports Innovation Dominance earns only ₹5-10 a bag after higher packing and production costs, part of which goes to the dealer, so Brand Strength stays PARTIAL and the Good Enough Goods risk is ELEVATED. And the share gains come from volume, not price: prices were rolled back in May and June of FY23 and described as 'under constant pressure' in the two following quarters, which is why Pricing Power is a flat NO FIT and the pricing-and-mix check scores negative. The limestone position cuts both ways in the same manner — leases to 2050 and 140 million tons of reserves next to the plant are the one thing rivals cannot copy, but the dependence on government leases and permissions is precisely what makes Government Dependency the single HIGH RISK channel, with the Karnataka project stalled for years, the Tamil Nadu auction still un-notified and Kurnool timing slipping.

Putting it together, this is a good operating business inside a hard industry, run with a spending habit that the record does not justify. The quality framework's strongest marks are for competitive advantage and customer benefits, while return on capital scores below zero and management and industry structure score close to it — the same split the patterns show. Management credibility is the thread that ties the weak scores together: FY24 capex guidance was raised from ₹900 crore to ₹1,600 crore to ₹2,000 crore, the third consecutive year of overrun, net debt kept rising towards ₹5,000 crore despite repeated talk of paying it down, and management declined to state any return hurdle for the money being spent. That combination is why Corporate Culture is a NO FIT rather than a partial pass. Around it, three further risks are rated ELEVATED and all stem from the same commodity position — cyclicality of prices and fuel, new capacity arriving in the core southern market from Shree, Dalmia and UltraTech, and the thin premium over ordinary cement. The three genuinely low risks — technology, customer or supplier concentration, and fashion — are low simply because cement is a functional repeat purchase sold to a broad base, not because of anything the company has built. The combined rating of 45 out of 100, a Mixed two-star grade, with resilience judged STRAINED, is a fair reading: the demand recovery and the new low-cost capacity could lift earnings from here, but the returns on what has been spent to get there remain unproven and unexplained.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** The business is anchored in mining leases and state permissions: the Karnataka/Bommanahalli project has been stalled for years awaiting government approval for land acquisition, the Tamil Nadu limestone auction remains un-notified, Kurnool line-2 timing slipped on environmental clearance and elections, captive wind conversion needed TNEB approval, railways re-imposed a 15% busy-season surcharge costing ~Rs.50/ton, and management frames demand off Budget allocations (PMAY, INR 14 lakh crore infra).

> "We are prepared for land acquisition, but government approval is awaited." — *management, on an earnings call*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Cement is a regional commodity and the calls are dominated by price volatility and input-cost swings: prices rose in April 2022 then rolled back in May/June, slipped through FY23-24 with margins swinging on pet coke ($200 to $104 to $138), and management itself refuses IRR targets on capex because of volatility — all while spending Rs.9,800 crores over FY19-24 with net debt/EBITDA at 3.22-3.5x, the classic expansionary over-investment pattern.

> "it is a capital intensive and long gestation period of construction and very volatile price conditions and very volatile commodity prices" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Cement is an operations/consumption-linked flow product rather than customer-capex equipment, and capex is increasingly funded from free cash flow (Rs.7,800 crores of the Rs.9,800 crores)*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 11 years with swings of ±12 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 14% and 30% (±5.8 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 21% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 36%).

## What to watch

- Whether profit per ton keeps climbing past ₹1,007 or stalls — the Cyclicality risk means the recovery so far rests on pet coke falling from $200, not on better pricing.
- Cement prices actually holding instead of being rolled back — the Pricing Power NO FIT and the negative pricing-and-mix check are the clearest single thing that would have to change.
- Capex guidance being met rather than raised a fourth year running, and management finally naming a return hurdle — the Corporate Allocation and Corporate Culture failures both hinge on this.
- Net debt turning down from around ₹5,000 crore, which would test whether the deleveraging talk flagged under Corporate Culture is real.
- Government approvals moving — Karnataka land acquisition, the un-notified Tamil Nadu limestone auction, Kurnool line 2 — since these are the substance of the single HIGH RISK channel and gate the Cost to Replicate advantage.
- Premium mix breaking clearly above the 27-28% it has hovered at, and the ₹5-10 per bag premium widening — the difference between Innovation Dominance holding and the Good Enough Goods risk winning.
