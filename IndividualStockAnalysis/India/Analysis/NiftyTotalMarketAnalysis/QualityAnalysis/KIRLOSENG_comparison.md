### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 3 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Promoter-family leadership talks in multiyear terms, tracks a stated $2bn-by-FY30 roadmap, discloses negatives promptly (Fluid Dynamics quarter-on-quarter degrowth, slow construction & mining, Arka's investment drag on PAT) and holds a net-cash balance sheet; against that, the CFO and CEO seats have turned over repeatedly across FY22-FY26. (The long view had said: Family-owned with stable Kirloskar leadership through the period; the 2X-3Y ambition set in FY23 was substantially delivered and rolled into a stated 2B2B FY30 goal, bad news is disclosed (LGM margin failure and 'that is our learning', FMS degrowth, construction/mining softness, flat Arka PAT), and the balance sheet stayed net cash (INR552 crores) with capex/R&D funded organically.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Aftermarket is repeatedly cited as a growing, installed-base-linked stream — service requests at an all-time high, D&A growing 13-15% every quarter, and an explicit push to raise the AMC-customer share — but no attach rates, renewal rates or deferred-revenue/advance-billing funding are quantified in the last four calls. (The long view had said: Across the years management builds an annuity layer on top of the genset sale — Aug 2024 describes maintenance/annuity contracts on a ~15-year genset life, and by FY26 Distribution & Aftermarket is the highest-ever quarter at INR238-281 crores growing 14-20% with AMC penetration explicitly rising. No deferred-revenue/customer-advance funding is claimed, so the negative-working-capital leg is absent (receivables ~41 days, payables ~60 days).)
- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The FY26 calls show deliberate movement toward the customer — restructuring B2C into LGM, building own international offices with local hiring and evaluating local assembly rather than agent-based exports, and a new systems-integration subsidiary — but there is no own-store count, franchise royalty or D2C revenue share disclosed. (The long view had said: Movement toward the customer is via owned service/aftermarket ecosystem and 'being international' with local offices and hiring rather than exports through agents; there are no own stores, franchising or D2C economics.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (LIKELY FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Stressed on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Demand is visibly cycle- and capex-linked: the Q1 FY26 call had to normalise for the CPCB IV+ 'pre-buy' distortion of the base year, Q3 flagged construction & mining as 'a bit slow', High Horsepower swung +235% in a single quarter, and the MD herself framed FY26 in cycle language — all while the company posts record sales (FY26 standalone +25%) and expanding margins, i.e. plausibly near a peak. Bounded by a genuinely operations-linked base: Distribution & Aftermarket (INR238cr in Q3, +14/15%), ~92,000 service requests, AMC conversion and over 1 million pumps sold. (The long view had said: Demand is repeatedly explained by external cycles and customer capex: the FY23 windfall came from a coal/power-deficit crisis and real-estate/infra spend, industrial revenue rides construction & mining OEM capex (38% growth in Q1 FY25, then a subdued Q3 FY26 as 'OEMs were correcting their inventory'), and the FY26 surge (35% quarterly growth, HHP +235%) is infrastructure- and data-centre-capex driven — classic peak conditions where we cannot know how much of the growth or the 13.1% margin is cyclical. Management itself frames the business in cycle terms across the four years.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Across the year management never defends a price premium against cheaper alternatives and reports no channel own-label pressure; instead margins expanded (Q3 EBITDA 12.2% vs 10.3%) alongside share gains, and the Q4 call describes an 18-month-plus technical qualification cycle for construction OEMs that protects the position from a purely price-led challenger. (The long view had said: The low-to-medium horsepower genset and pump businesses are price-fought commodity categories — management flagged in FY23 that the industry has 'aggressive and dynamic pricing' and that water management margins were 'immensely under pressure' when input costs could not be passed on, and consolidated gross margin sits flat at ~35% with EBITDA in the 12–13% band, i.e. no Nobel-Biocare-style premium to lose but persistent pressure from cheaper alternatives.)

**That worsened:**

- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Repeatedly across all four calls, growth is anchored in government-determined demand and government-determined product rules: NPCIL and Indian Navy 'Make 1' projects, defence emergency procurement, railways, nuclear and marine driving the Industrial segment (INR1,444cr FY26, +22%), while regulator-set emission norms (CPCB IV+, CEV BS-IV to BS-V) directly created the pre-buy boom and the subsequent product reset. Diversification exists (retail Powergen, exports, pumps, Arka) but the policy-linked share is large and rising. (The long view had said: Policy sets the product spec and the timing of demand — the CPCB 4+ deadline change created a prebuy that distorted FY24/FY25 comparisons, and CEV BS-V reset the industrial segment; growth tailwinds are repeatedly attributed to government infrastructure spend, PLI and 'Make in India'. Government customers are now being institutionalised (Defence, Nuclear, Railways; the NPCIL 10x6.3 MW order; a new Kirloskar Advanced Systems entity for defence contracting), which adds tender/margin-discipline exposure — but pricing in the core retail genset and pump business remains market-determined, not regulated.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); New Entrants (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Technological Innovation (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Stressed on the long view (2 of the 3 stress checks are flagged — multiple warning lights at once.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
