### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 0 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Toll Roads**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: The integrated pipeline network is a literal toll road: a PNGRB-set tariff per MMBTU levied on shippers whose alternative is no gas at all, with the regulator lifting the toll 12.1% from 1 Jan 2026 (~Rs.1,200 crore p.a.) and a separate LPG pipeline tariff escalating 3.4% every year; the toll is a small fraction of a refinery's or fertiliser plant's cost base and is anchored entirely in the regulatory framework. (The long view had said: GAIL's core economics are a literal toll: a regulated per-MMBtu tariff on an 18,000 km integrated grid that customers book through an open-access portal (15,000+ capacity transactions in CY2025), with the JLPL LPG pipeline tariff escalating 3.4% a year and capacity expansion adding ~Rs.700 crore of revenue. The anchor is regulatory (PNGRB) rather than commercial, which both guarantees the toll and caps it — the Rs.58.61 tariff was raised to Rs.65.69 from 1 Jan 2026 only after a two-year process.)
- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: GAIL is deliberately moving downstream from transmission into retail: the Board approved transferring its six CGD geographical areas into GAIL Gas explicitly for a retail-focused approach, and the calls disclose own-network counts each quarter (215 CNG stations and 4.64 lakh DPNG at GAIL; 674 CNG stations and 12.46 lakh DPNG at GAIL Gas) plus a 29-station retail LNG rollout and 13 commissioned LNG/LCNG stations. (The long view had said: GAIL has consistently moved down the chain toward the end customer across the full period — direct CGD in six authorised areas (154 CNG stations in Jul 2023 to 215 by Feb 2026), the wholly-owned GAIL Gas retail arm now at 7.8 MMSCMD, a planned 29-station retail LNG network, and 13 LNG/LCNG stations already commissioned — and is now preparing a GAIL Gas listing with consultants appointed, plus two fertiliser plants (Rs.21,000 crore) to anchor its own pipeline load.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Price increases come from the regulator, not the company — GAIL had to file a review petition seeking a further Rs.15/MMBTU after being awarded less than it asked — and the merchant segments show the opposite of pricing power, with polymer prices falling ~Rs.1,000/MT and high HH/spot gas costs pushing refineries and industry to alternative fuels, i.e. volume lost on price. (The long view had said: GAIL is a price-taker on both ends: its transmission tariff is set by PNGRB (it asked Rs.78, was granted Rs.65.69, and is still appealing for Rs.15 more), marketing margin swings with the Henry Hub/Brent arbitrage rather than any pricing decision, and the petrochemical and LHC segments show outright price deflation — Q3 FY26 polymer loss of Rs.483 crore and a 45% LHC PBT drop on a ~Rs.4,600/tonne price fall. This is the opposite of raising prices above cost inflation without volume loss.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Cost to Replicate (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (NO FIT); Innovation Dominance (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: GAIL sells an undifferentiated molecule with no brand protection, and when spot gas priced above substitutes customers simply left — management attributes lost transmission volume to industrial switching, and is reduced to tax engineering (state-wise CST procurement from ONGC Gujarat) to stay price-competitive. (The long view had said: GAIL sells undifferentiated molecules into a market management itself calls price-sensitive, so cheaper substitutes take volume immediately: refineries and industries 'switched to alternative fuel' when spot LNG rose, materially cutting FY26 transmission volumes, and sourcing decisions are made on price alone with no geography or brand loyalty. Polymers and LPG are likewise commodity grades with no premium to defend.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Technological Innovation (ELEVATED); New Entrants (WATCH); Fashion Risk (QUANT FLAG).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
