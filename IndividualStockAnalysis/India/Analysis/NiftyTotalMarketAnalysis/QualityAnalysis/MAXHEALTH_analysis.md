# Max Healthcare (MAXHEALTH) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Max Healthcare runs a network of large multi-speciality hospitals in India, treating in-patients and out-patients across areas like oncology, orthopaedics and renal sciences, and it earns almost all of its money by charging for those hospital stays, surgeries and procedures. Patients pay in four broad ways the company tracks closely — cash/self-pay, private insurance, institutional/government schemes such as CGHS (lower-priced), and international/medical-tourism patients — so profitability depends heavily on this 'payor mix' plus how full the beds are (occupancy ~74-77%) and the average revenue per occupied bed (about INR 77,900-80,100 a quarter recently). Its hospitals are concentrated in and around Delhi NCR (Saket, Shalimar Bagh, Dwarka, Patparganj, Vaishali, Gurgaon), with units in Mohali, Lucknow, Nagpur, Mumbai (Nanavati) and, from FY26, Bhubaneswar via the Kalinga acquisition, plus planned entry into Pune. Alongside the hospitals it runs two smaller 'strategic business units' — Max@Home for home healthcare and Max Lab for outside-the-hospital pathology testing. Full-year FY26 network gross revenue was INR 10,538 crore with operating EBITDA of INR 2,638 crore (26.2% margin) and PAT of INR 1,631 crore.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Hospital Network (in-patient and out-patient care)** — The core business: running multi-speciality hospitals where patients are admitted for surgery and treatment, and this is essentially the whole company — of roughly INR 2,664 crore of quarterly gross revenue in Q4 FY26, the two named side businesses (Max@Home and Max Lab) together contributed only about INR 125 crore.
- **Oncology (and other clinical specialities)** — Cancer care is the single biggest speciality management calls out, at 21-27% of in-patient revenue over the period, with orthopaedics, renal sciences and robotic surgery named as the other drivers of higher revenue per bed.
- **Institutional / CGHS business** — Beds sold to government schemes and other institutional payors at lower tariffs — management deliberately manages this share down (from 33% to 30% of beds in FY23) because it drags on revenue per bed, and CGHS pricing rules directly hit results in FY26 when the company stopped supplying some high-value chemotherapy drugs.
- **International patients / medical tourism** — Treating patients who fly in from abroad, served through 20-plus overseas offices; it ran at INR 227-230 crore a quarter in FY26, about 9% of hospital revenue, growing 12-14% year-on-year.
- **Digital channel** — Not a separate business but a source of bookings — online marketing, web appointments and digital lead management drove INR 838 crore, roughly 31% of total revenue, in Q4 FY26, up from 16% of revenue in Q1 FY23, on 90 lakh website sessions a quarter.
- **Max@Home** — A strategic business unit delivering healthcare at the patient's home across 16 service lines in 15 cities with over 56% repeat customers; small but fast-growing, at INR 73 crore of revenue in Q4 FY26, up 30% year-on-year (versus INR 32 crore back in Q1 FY23).
- **Max Lab** — The 'non-captive' pathology arm — lab tests sold outside its own hospitals through collection centres and channel partners in over 60 cities, serving nearly 6 lakh patients in Q4 FY26 for INR 52 crore of revenue, up 14% year-on-year.
- **Capacity expansion (brownfield, greenfield and acquisitions)** — A continuous build-out programme that management treats as the main growth engine — roughly 20% of extra brownfield beds commissioned during FY26 at Mohali, Nanavati and Max Smart, greenfield projects at Gurgaon Sector-56 (500 beds), Lucknow (700 beds, INR 1,400 crore), Zirakpur, Patparganj, Vaishali, Nagpur, Dwarka and Pune, plus acquisitions such as Lucknow, Nagpur and Kalinga Hospital in Bhubaneswar; funded largely from operating cash flow, with net debt of INR 1,908 crore and net debt-to-EBITDA under 1.
- **Free treatment for economically weaker sections** — A non-revenue obligation the company reports every quarter — around 42,000 patients treated free in Q4 FY26, worth INR 59 crore at hospital tariff.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** Same leadership trio (Soi, Sareen, Gupta) through all 16 calls; promises made in early calls were delivered (Dwarka opened Jul 2024, broke even in 6 months vs 9 guided; Lucknow/Nagpur turnaround, +58% EBITDA in one quarter), net debt/EBITDA held below 1 while self-funding capex, and bad news is disclosed promptly and owned — GRAP delays, insurance stalemate, oncology drug loss, Vikrant litigation.

> "we did have GRAP-related delays but we did build close to 2.5 million square feet over the last 3 to 3.5 years ... And we, of course, continue to ask more of ourselves and there has been a lot of learnings in this." — *management, on an earnings call*
