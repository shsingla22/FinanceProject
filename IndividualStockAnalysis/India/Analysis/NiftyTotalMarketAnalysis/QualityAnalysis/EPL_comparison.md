**Checks that improved:**

- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Growth is materially mix-led — shift to higher-value Beauty & Cosmetics (now 53% of portfolio, growing 30%) and premium formats at 8x standard tube pricing — with pricing used mainly as cost pass-through rather than as margin expansion. | The long view had said: Pricing is largely cost pass-through (with negative price corrections in FY25 when commodities softened) so growth is volume- and mix-led rather than priced; the B&C mix shift is favourable but has yielded only ~50bps annual margin expansion.
- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: End markets are structurally expanding — global beauty & cosmetics packaging, sustainable packaging adoption (38% of sales and rising as a customer purchase criterion), and emerging-market consumption which will be 75% of combined revenue. | The long view had said: Two durable tailwinds are evidenced — mandated conversion to sustainable/laminated tubes (including aluminium-to-laminate in pharma) and emerging-market consumption at 75% of combined revenue — though the underlying tube market itself grows only modestly.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Laminated tubes for oral care and beauty is a genuinely unglamorous, low-attention niche, and EPL operated it quietly for years — though the $1bn merger and IVL/Blackstone promoter presence reduce the obscurity going forward. | The long view had said: Tube converting is an unglamorous niche, but EPL is a widely covered listed company with global PE/strategic sponsors and is now building a $1bn platform — no longer capital-ignored.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: Clear growth (not maintenance) capex — a greenfield Thailand plant built in 9 months and now supplying, Brazil plant outperforming, plus capacity ahead of demand for B&C — with ROCE rising to 19% and a 25%+ FY29 target showing incremental returns are improving. | The long view had said: FY26 capex of ~INR480cr ran well above depreciation of INR385cr and was explicitly directed at growth (Brazil greenfield, Thailand plant, B&C capacity) with ROCE rising to 19%, though returns are good rather than exceptional.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Systematic people investment is evident — Great Place to Work certification across seven countries, a planned internal-facing CEO handover with a two-month overlap, and explicit reference to talent quality after a global plant tour. | The long view had said: There is systematic capability investment and Great Place to Work certification in seven countries, but the internal pipeline looks thin given repeated CFO/Chairman churn and an externally recruited CEO succession.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Headline results are genuinely strong (seven quarters of 20%+ margins, ROCE +96bps to 19%) but PAT was flat-to-+1% in two of the four quarters and the merger will make future results hard to attribute to operating skill, so the record does not yet cleanly separate skill from favourable mix. | The long view had said: The improvement looks skill-driven rather than lucky — margins rose region by region (Europe +230bps, Americas from single-digits to high-teens) against, not with, the commodity cycle — but ROCE of 19% and an untested doubling merger temper the conclusion.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Structure is consolidated enough that ~50% of business runs on contractual pass-through and costs are 'fully passed on', but end customers are large global FMCG buyers with real negotiating power, and no oligopoly structure is described. | The long view had said: The structure is a genuine four-player global oligopoly, but the end customers are Colgate/Unilever-scale buyers who extract price corrections when commodities fall, capping the benefit.
- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Management names specific shortfalls rather than burying them — Europe below expectation, the China plant closure and severance cost, GST-driven Oral destocking, and flat PAT explained by a base one-off — although guidance framing remains consistently upbeat. | The long view had said: Management consistently names its own shortfalls without prompting — calling Europe margins 'not acceptable' in 2022 and admitting the India B&C miss in 2024 — and refuses to give timing commitments it cannot back.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Four consecutive quarters of double-digit revenue growth with management explicitly claiming share gains against market growth (Americas +27.4% then +24.1%, EAP +25%, B&C +26% to +30%), achieved alongside rising margins (7 straight quarters 20%+) and rising ROCE to 19% — i.e. share bought with capability, not price or profitability. (The long view had said: Wallet-share gains are named repeatedly and by geography from FY25 onward (two new customers in Europe, India, Americas) on the back of sustainable-tube conversion, alongside new-market entry (Brazil FY23–25, Thailand in nine months) and the FY26 Indovida merger doubling revenue to ~$1bn — with share won on differentiation, not price.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Genuine premiumisation exists in innovation formats (8x premium tube-in-tube; NEOSeam/extruded with global beauty brands) and mix-led margin expansion to seven straight 20%+ quarters, but the Q4 FY26 discussion frames pricing largely as cost pass-through under contract rather than price rises above cost inflation. (The long view had said: EPL demonstrates cost-recovery pricing (monthly increases in FY23, landed-cost-plus-power pass-through in the FY26 Middle East crisis) but admits negative price/mix and 'price corrections' when commodities softened — pass-through, not pricing above cost inflation.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Named formats recur across all four calls with revenue and pricing impact (NEOSeam, extruded, tube-in-tube at 8x premium, 38% sustainable tube mix) and external innovation awards, and the incoming CEO singles out the B&C pipeline — but R&D spend is never disclosed or benchmarked against industry, so innovation dominance cannot be established. (The long view had said: Innovation is repeatedly named and monetised — Platina, Neo-seam tubes, sustainable tube mix rising 21%→29%→38% and directly credited with oral-care wallet-share gains, plus EcoVadis Platinum/SBTi firsts — but R&D spend is never disclosed or quantified, so the 'outsized R&D funded by gross margin' test is unproven.)

**That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The calls discuss contractual pass-through arrangements on raw-material cost but never license fees, AMC/service revenue, attach rates, renewals or customer advances as a funding source. No evidence either way on a recurring-revenue-after-product model. (The long view had said: Across all 21 calls the revenue discussion is tube volumes, price/mix and pass-through contracts; there is no mention of licence fees, AMC/service revenue, attach rates, renewals or customer advances funding the business.)
- **Forward Integrators**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across all four calls EPL moves backward/sideways in the value chain — new plants (Thailand, Brazil), and a merger with rigid-packaging maker Indovida — with no own stores, franchising, licensing or D2C. Its integration story is manufacturing footprint and format breadth, not proximity to the end consumer. (The long view had said: No discussion of own stores, franchising, licensing or D2C anywhere in the excerpts; EPL remains a B2B manufacturer selling into brand owners.)

*Unchanged: Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Demand is a flow/consumable product — tubes consumed in customers' day-to-day FMCG production (oral care, beauty), not tied to any customer capex programme — and the last four calls show unbroken double-digit revenue growth with seven consecutive quarters of 20%+ EBITDA margin. The only cyclical element is input cost (Middle East-driven resin/laminate cost and availability), which is being passed through rather than showing up as demand volatility. (The long view had said: Demand is flow-type (tubes consumed in customers' FMCG production, not their capex), but the calls repeatedly show polymer/aluminium/energy cost cycles swinging margins — EBITDA fell to ~15% in FY23 with single-digit Europe margins, recovered to 20%+ by FY26, and the May 2026 call flags a fresh Middle East raw-material shock. Volume demand is stable; input-price cycles, not end-demand cycles, are the exposure.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Individual large customers repeatedly drove reported outcomes across the window: Europe's Q2 FY26 growth of 2.8% was attributed to "temporary softness from a few large customers," and in Q3 FY26 a single customer relocating its filling facility forced EPL to close a China plant and book severance. Supplier-side concentration also surfaced in Q4 FY26, when the Middle East crisis hit availability and cost of key raw materials. (The long view had said: Management concedes the core oral-care end market has only four global buyers, growth is repeatedly explained as 'wallet share gain with a few key customers', and the Brazil plant investment was underwritten by the commitment of a single anchor customer — analysts asked directly about that dependency. The five-year trajectory is one of deliberate de-concentration, which bounds the risk rather than removing it.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Not comparable: Government Dependency (NOT ASSESSED → WATCH — could not be assessed on the long view).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
