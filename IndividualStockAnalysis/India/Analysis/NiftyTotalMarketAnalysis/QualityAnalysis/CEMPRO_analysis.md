# Cemindia Project (CEMPRO) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Cemindia Projects Limited (NSE code CEMPRO, formerly ITD Cementation India Limited) is an Indian heavy civil-engineering and construction contractor that builds large infrastructure to order for others — it does not own or operate the assets it builds. Its customers are mainly government bodies and state agencies (metro rail corporations, the Navy, state governments, port authorities like Vadhvan and JNPT) plus large private and group clients such as Adani, JSW and Petronet. It makes money by winning tenders and executing contracts — some fixed-price, some 'star price' where key input costs are passed through — recognising revenue as work progresses and earning a thin contractor's margin, roughly 10-12% EBITDA and 5-6% net profit, on FY26 revenue of about INR 10,061 crores. It works across India (Chennai, Bangalore, Mumbai, Kolkata, Delhi and Pune metros, Ganga Expressway in UP, ports in Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Odisha, a Bihar road) and has been pushing into overseas markets — Colombo, Bangladesh and Abu Dhabi. As of April 2026 it was sitting on an order book of about INR 29,000 crores, roughly three years of revenue.

**The parts of the business:**

- **MRTS / Metro (underground and elevated)** — Builds metro rail civil works — tunnels bored with TBMs, underground stations, and elevated viaducts — across Chennai, Bangalore, Mumbai, Kolkata, Delhi and Pune; described repeatedly as the company's core technical strength and a steady source of large orders, with several signature metro jobs completed in FY26.
- **Marine / Maritime structures** — Constructs ports, jetties and breakwaters — Udangudi (a breakwater 9 km out to sea, called a first in Asia), Vizhinjam deep-sea port, Sea Bird, JNPT/BMCT, Navy's Project Varsha and the awaited Vadhvan Port; a major segment (around INR 6,400 crores of order-book exposure in 2024) though management calls marine 'a limited market'.
- **Roads and highways** — Executes greenfield road and elevated-corridor projects, notably the Ganga Expressway in UP (won for about INR 4,850 crores, contributing over INR 600 crores of revenue in a single quarter at peak) and a newer road-cum-bridge job in Bihar; management notes road margins are structurally lower than its other work.
- **Buildings and general civil works** — Puts up institutional and commercial buildings — Sikkim University, a Guwahati riverside development, the new High Court/Circuit Bench building handed to the Government of West Bengal — a smaller, supporting line rather than a headline driver.
- **Industrial and group projects (including data centres)** — Newer work for industrial and private-group clients, such as the Petronet job at Dahej and prospective data-centre construction for Adani group entities; small today but flagged on recent calls as a growing order source since the promoter change.
- **Overseas / international** — Contracts outside India — West Container Terminal in Colombo, a project in Bangladesh (worth about INR 1,500 crores, delayed by political unrest), and Ruwais in Abu Dhabi; presence was 'nil' two years before 2024 and management repeatedly calls it the key focus area because overseas contract terms are more equitable.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is 100% linked to customers' capex programmes — metros, ports, expressways, data centres — not to any flow/consumable demand, and the calls repeatedly show order timing swinging results (Q3 FY26 revenue grew only 5.6% because FY25 inflows were weak and Vadhavan stalled, while Q4 FY26 EBITDA margin jumped to 15.1% on completions and claim realisations before management guided back to 10.5%). Management's own framing — one big-ticket win changing 'the whole scenario' — is the definition of lumpy capex-driven earnings.

> "If we are lucky, if we get one or two jobs in that big-ticket jobs, the whole scenario will change." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Diversified across marine, underground metro, buildings, roads and now data centres; large multi-year order book (INR29,000 crores) and conservative balance sheet (net D/E 0.18-0.3x) smooth the cycle somewhat*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 3 of the last 10 years with swings of ±33 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 6% and 13% (±2.2 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 11% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 4% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**What the calls show:** The core business is government-awarded fixed infrastructure — Chennai/Bangalore/Kolkata/Mumbai/Pune/Delhi metros, Ganga Expressway, Navy's Project Varsha, SAIL, Vadhavan and Seabird ports — with inflow timing set entirely by when authorities open tenders, budget allocations discussed as the addressable pool, a GST change altering contract economics, and Vadhavan revenue lost to local/administrative issues 'not in our control'.
