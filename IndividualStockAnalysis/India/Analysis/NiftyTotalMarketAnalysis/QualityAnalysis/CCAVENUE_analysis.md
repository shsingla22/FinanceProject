# AvenuesAI (CCAVENUE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

AvenuesAI (formerly Infibeam Avenues, listed as CCAVENUE) runs CCAvenue, a payment gateway that lets businesses accept digital payments — credit and debit cards, net banking, prepaid instruments, NEFT/RTGS, UPI — online and, increasingly, at physical stores; alongside this it sells software platforms to governments and enterprises, the biggest being the technology behind India's Government e-Marketplace (GeM). It sells mainly to merchants and banks, from very large enterprises in utilities, telecom, travel and hospitality down to SMEs and cooperative banks, adding roughly 2,550 new merchants a day in Q1 FY25 (230,022 in the quarter). It earns money by keeping a thin slice of every rupee processed: gross revenue is huge because it includes money passed through to banks and networks, while the 'net revenue' it actually keeps is a take rate of a few basis points — 6.9 bps in Q1 FY23 rising to 11.2 bps in Q1 FY25 — so Q1 FY25 gross revenue of INR753 crores translated into net revenue of INR119 crores, EBITDA of INR70 crores and profit of INR50 crores. It is primarily an India business (total transaction processing volume of INR87,218 crores in Q1 FY23 alone), with a growing international arm in the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Oman plus a GIFT City base for cross-border and dollar settlement, targeted to reach 12–15% of net revenue.

**The parts of the business:**

- **CCAvenue (India payments / payment gateway)** — The core business and, in management's words, 'the economic engine' — it processes card, net-banking and other digital payments for merchants and holds an RBI payment aggregator licence, an in-principle licence to issue prepaid wallets and RBI authorisation for offline (POS) payment aggregation; it drives essentially all of the transaction volume and take-rate income.
- **Platform business (including GeM)** — Software platforms sold to government and enterprises, most notably the Government e-Marketplace, whose volumes are counted inside reported transaction processing volume and which had crossed INR50,000 crores by 1 August 2022 at double the prior year's pace; it is reported together with the India payments business as the 'standalone' core.
- **International payments** — Merchant acquiring and payment infrastructure work in the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Oman (where it works with several leading banks as a technology partner), plus IFSCA approval at GIFT City for cross-border payments, escrow and international acquiring — currently a single-digit share of net revenue that management wants to lift to 12–15%, with the US named as the next focus.
- **Rediff / RediffOne / RediffPay (consumer and business platform)** — A majority stake in Rediff.com, bought in 2024, bringing cloud e-mail, content and commerce services with over 55 million monthly visitors; management is turning it into a consumer engagement and payments front-end (RediffPay, UPI) and an 'AI-native back office' for SMEs and enterprises (RediffOne), described as a customer-acquisition and lock-in layer rather than a major revenue line today.
- **Phronetic AI (AI vertical)** — The in-house artificial-intelligence arm — set up partly as a new subsidiary for AI-based fraud detection and prevention and holder of a US patent filing — which management calls 'the invisible orchestrator', automating transaction routing, risk, reconciliation and compliance across the group; it is framed as a margin and efficiency driver rather than a separately sized revenue business.
- **Embedded finance and lending** — A stated priority for FY27 rather than a running business: instead of lending off its own balance sheet, the company plans to distribute and underwrite merchant, checkout and consumer credit using its payments data, via a proposed investment in Online PSB Loans and a stake in NBFC Ratnaafin.
- **Infibeam Digital Entertainment** — A subsidiary in digital media technology and live events, taken to 100% ownership in 2024; mentioned only in passing and clearly small relative to payments.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

AvenuesAI's strongest features are the shape of its business and the market it sits in. Every rupee of assets produces ₹1.11 of sales, and only about five days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, so the company can grow without heavy investment or borrowing — ₹8,116 crore of FY26 revenue on a payments and software stack with low leverage and no lending on the balance sheet. It operates in the one part of the record that scores at the top: Indian digital payments is in durable long-term expansion with regulatory tailwinds, a point management sustained across all four years of calls. Against that backdrop the company is clearly taking volume from a non-dominant starting position — payment volumes and gross revenue more than doubled in FY26 on enterprise, utility, government and business-to-business merchant wins — and three of the eleven growth patterns are judged likely fits: repeat per-transaction revenue from an installed base of five million merchants adding roughly 2,550 a day, a steady cadence of named product launches from TapPay in 2022 through PayCentral.ai in 2026, and market-share gains running well above the industry, including a claimed number-two position in the UAE and expansion into Oman, Saudi Arabia and GIFT City.

The problem is that the very trait producing those wins is also what generates the biggest risks, and the two cannot be separated. Share is being bought with price. Gross revenue rose 103% in FY26 while net revenue rose only 15%, and in the third quarter gross revenue rose 122% against just 6% for net revenue, with management explicitly choosing volume-led growth and absolute profitability over headline take rates — in other words, abandoning price as an objective. That single choice is why the low-price posture only partially fits as a pattern, why pricing power and the toll-road pattern are judged no fit outright, and why the risk review flags 'good enough goods' as high risk: gateway processing is a service where many rivals quote similar rates, savings have been passed to merchants since 2022, and by FY25–FY26 the company stopped defending price at all. The same dynamic feeds the elevated new-entrant risk, since the competition includes burn-funded consumer players and the zero-commission UPI rail that earns nothing at the gateway. UPI is also the reason technology risk is rated high and shifting customer preference is on watch: volume is migrating to a rail that is excluded from reported payment volumes because it carries no commission, so the part of demand that can actually be charged for shrinks in the mix even as total digital payments grow. Government exposure cuts the same way — the regulator and government that supply the structural tailwind also set both the volume and the price, and the flagship GeM marketplace relationship stopped producing recorded revenue and went to arbitration while GeM continued running on the company's platform, which is the single event behind both the high government-dependency rating and the watch flag on customer concentration.

Management is where the record is weakest, scoring at the bottom of every quality area at -1.0, and this compounds rather than offsets the above. The company renamed itself AvenuesAI at the peak of the artificial-intelligence cycle and speaks in grandiose flywheel and world-stage terms — the opposite of the substance-over-celebrity trait the framework looks for, and a move that also weakens the brand pattern, since the two-decade-old CCAvenue merchant name has now been rebranded twice. Acquisitions are diversifying rather than adding to the core: a majority stake in loss-making Rediff on roughly ₹36 crore of revenue, a media and live-events subsidiary, an AI subsidiary and minority stakes in non-bank lenders. The culture pattern is judged no fit for goalpost-shifting: the FY23 promise of a ₹7.5 lakh crore volume run-rate with steadily rising take rates was replaced by a focus on absolute profitability once take rates compressed. Reported growth of roughly 35% a year is also lumpy rather than steady. The combined rating lands at 38 out of 100 — 45% weight on business quality at 50, 30% on growth-pattern fit at 34, and 25% on risk safety at 22 — a 'Mixed' two-star verdict. Underlying resilience is still judged sound, because demand is functional and repeat rather than taste-driven or tied to customers' capital spending, with only festive seasonality showing through and volumes, earnings and profit holding up across FY23 to FY26. The honest reading is a durable, cheap-to-run business winning real share in a growing market, but doing so at a falling price, on a rail it does not control, under a regulator that can remove revenue at will, and run by a management team the record does not credit with discipline.
