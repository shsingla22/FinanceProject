### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 4 strengthened, 1 weakened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Repeated evidence of branded share leadership plus premium positioning: Candid Powder held 56%+ share while growing 17%, 11 brands in the IPM top 300, and RYALTRIS grew 50%+ globally across 52 markets while gaining share in every launched country; the name is also being stretched into consumer care (Scalpe +50%, Bontress, La Shield) via a dedicated subsidiary. (The long view had said: Real brand-building evidence — 9 rising to 11 brands in the IPM top 300, Candid Powder at 56% market share, Ascoril, and stretch into a consumer-care/OTC franchise grown fivefold to Rs 3bn — but there is no disclosed A&P spend, no heritage-based premium pricing claim, and much of the branded pipeline is in-licensed (Winlevi, Tevimbra, Brukinsa, Aumolertinib) rather than owned heritage.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: FY26 delivered a landmark out-licensing of the in-house BEAT-platform asset ISB 2001 to AbbVie (USD 700mn upfront, USD 1.925bn potential plus tiered double-digit royalties), the world's first fixed-dose nebulised triple therapy for COPD (NEBZMART), first-to-market LIRAFIT biosimilar and GLIPIQ semaglutide, plus a first US complex-respiratory approval with CGT designation and 180-day exclusivity — named products with revenue impact in every single quarter of the window. (The long view had said: Named launches every year with revenue impact (Ryaltris to $100m, Indamet, Lirafit, world-first triple neb), R&D disclosed at ~10.7% of sales, and the IGI/BEAT platform delivered the $700m-upfront AbbVie deal for ISB-2001 in FY26 — but management itself admitted the innovation spend was unaffordable and cut it from ~$120m to ~$50m, so the innovation has not been consistently profitable or self-funding.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Share gain is quantified and persistent through all four calls: India secondary sales grew 15.1%/10.8%/15.8%/12.3% against IPM at 8.5%/6.4%/10.9%/10.1%, making Glenmark the second-fastest-growing of the top 15 in FY26, with share edging 2.30%→2.34%, a rank move to second in respiratory and ninth→eighth in Russian dermatology, and it was achieved partly by deliberately discontinuing low-margin tail-end brands rather than buying volume. (The long view had said: Share gain is documented with numbers across the whole four-year span: India rank 14th/2.17% share (Aug 2022) improving to 13th/2.34% (Jun 2026), growing 1.5x the IPM and second-fastest among the top 15 in FY26, plus Europe +50% in two years and Ryaltris expanding from a handful of markets to 55 — and it is accompanied by debt reduction rather than bought with leverage.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Management shows premiumisation intent — shifting Europe and emerging markets toward branded respiratory/dermatology, in-licensing innovative oncology (TEVIMBRA, BRUKINSA, Trastuzumab Rezetecan) and taking RYALTRIS direct in the US — but across all four calls there is not one explicit price increase taken without volume loss, and the US generic base still grew only 4.1%–7.8% core. (The long view had said: The explicit opposite is present and recurring: management concedes continuing price erosion in US generics from Aug 2022 through FY26, and India growth is volume/new-launch driven (4-5% of growth from launches) rather than price-led; no price increase without volume loss is ever cited.)

**That weakened:**

- **Friendly Middleman**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The India and branded international businesses run on the classic prescriber model — doctors choose, patients pay — with management repeatedly citing dermatologist/physician reception and a patient-support programme, but the calls never describe trade training programmes or channel-relationship investment that would create switching costs, and the large US generics book is payer/PBM-driven rather than prescriber-driven. (The long view had said: Every call describes demand created through prescribers rather than end-customers — rankings by therapy (#2 derma, #2/#3 respiratory), 500 additional India medical reps added in FY25, products judged by doctor reception, and LatAm growth explicitly attributed to prescription generation; the patient pays but the physician chooses.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: No revenue is set by a regulator or subsidy, but government action repeatedly moved the P&L within these four calls: the August 2025 GST regime change collapsed reported India formulation sales 87% in Q2 FY26, and US FDA status gated the Monroe plant until the VAI/EIR classification allowed manufacturing to restart. Exposure is process/regulatory rather than a policy-anchored revenue base. (The long view had said: Regulators, not customers, have repeatedly determined Glenmark's fortunes across the five years: adverse USFDA actions kept Monroe out of commercial production from before Aug 2022 until the VAI/EIR in Nov 2025, Goa remediation ran for years, and quarterly revenue and margin still hinge on approval timing — Q4 FY26 US margins missed purely because Flovent/nasal spray approvals slipped. Europe and US institutional business is also explicitly tender-driven, and India pricing sits under CDSCO/NLEM oversight.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: The bulk of the business is generics and branded generics, where cheaper equivalents are the competitive default and the prescriber — not the patient — controls the choice; the pressure is visible in tail-end brand discontinuations and in US core growth of only 4.1-7.8% despite 13 launches in FY26. Glenmark's defence is to move up the complexity curve into products that are harder to commoditise. (The long view had said: A large part of the business is itself the good-enough good: US generics, where pharmacists/PBMs — not patients — choose, brand awareness is nil, and management concedes continuous price erosion that no manufacturer escapes; Glenmark's US revenue was flat-to-declining for years and core growth was still only ~4-8% in FY26 ex the AbbVie licensing income. The mitigation is exactly the Nobel Biocare lesson applied deliberately — retreat into niches challengers cannot cheaply copy (inhalation devices, injectables, in-licensed novel molecules, branded India/EM franchises).)

**That worsened:**

- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: NO SIGNAL → LOW. Why now: The consumer-facing part of the business is riding the shift rather than being eroded by it — Consumer Care grew 20-21.5% on self-care adoption with Candid at 56%+ share — and the prescription base is clinical need, not habit. The one preference-like move (diabetes shifting to GLP-1s) was met with first-mover launches of LIRAFIT and GLIPIQ. (The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and the only computed fingerprint is clean.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Technological Innovation (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
