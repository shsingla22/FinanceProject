- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. - **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Weak → Neutral. Now: The brand carries weight with large enterprises but does not confer pricing power — margins hold at 21% only via automation and currency while peers discount. | The long view had said: No image-driven pricing power; margins sit in a 20-22% band defended by cost levers (pyramid, offshoring, utilization) rather than price, with clients pressing for discounts and productivity pass-through.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Deep embedding in large accounts and consolidation wins suggest real switching costs, but the same quarter shows a client able to walk, so lock-in is partial at best. | The long view had said: Embedded multi-year delivery creates real switching costs — deep incumbency inside client estates, renewals and consolidation wins — even if contracts remain contestable.

Now: Growth is not price-led: peers flag irrational competitive pricing, management cites volume weakness and a one-time client loss, and margin gains came from currency and internal automation rather than realisation. | The long view had said: Growth is volume/large-deal led rather than price led; pricing is described as merely stable and used as a margin lever (value-based selling) while peers flag irrational competitive pricing.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 2 weakened, 7 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Orderly internal succession after a 10-year CEO tenure to a 31-year insider, promises kept in-year (20,000 fresher hiring commitment, ~18,000 onboarded by Q3), candid disclosure of the one-time client loss and downward guidance revision, and consistent framing of guidance bands against macro scenarios; one year of evidence is too short to judge long-run capital allocation or return-on-capital discipline. (The long view had said: Across the calls management owns bad news on the record (McCamish cyber incident and regulator notifications in Jul 2024; the Jul 2026 guidance cut attributed to a one-time client decision rather than excuses), delivers what it promised (the FY23 margin-lever plan resurfaces as Project Maximus results in FY25), and shows stable long-tenured leadership with a planned internal succession — a 31-year insider given a six-month mentored handover.)
- **Brand Strength**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Enterprise reputation clearly wins work — 'AI partner of choice' with 90% of the top 200 clients, the NHS mega-deal, and being the beneficiary of five vendor-consolidation deals in Q1 FY27 — but there is no heritage/A&P/premium-pricing evidence, and share gain is not accompanied by price premium. (The long view had said: Real institutional trust and stretch into named sub-brands (Cobalt, Topaz, Topaz Fabric) and client-quoted confidence at the Feb 2026 AI Day, but the brand does not deliver premium pricing — guidance fell from 14–16% growth (FY23) to 1.5–3% (FY27) with no pricing benefit cited.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 2 worsened, 3 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Journalists put peer commentary on 'irrational competitive pricing' directly to Parekh in Jul 2026, and Nilekani's build-vs-buy thesis implies AI-native builders and enterprises' own in-house teams can now attack work that previously required a large systems integrator. Management did not describe losing work to a structurally different entrant, so the evidence is of pressure rather than displacement. (The long view had said: Two structurally different competitors are raised by analysts and handled somewhat defensively: client-owned Global Capability Centres insourcing work (Vanguard, Daimler named) and peers pricing irrationally, with Salil deflecting the pricing question entirely ('maybe they are seeing that, you should check with them'). AI-native delivery models (frontier/forward-deployed engineers) are a further entrant format Infosys is having to build toward from scratch.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Core IT services are increasingly commoditised: the productivity tools underpinning delivery are third-party and available to everyone ('Claude Code or Codex or several others'), peers are pricing irrationally, and margin defence now depends on Project Maximus value-based selling and automation rather than an unassailable price premium. Enterprise buyers are sophisticated and procurement-led, so a cheaper-but-adequate vendor is a live threat. (The long view had said: Commoditisation pressure is a running theme: pricing/discount negotiations with clients in 2022, pricing only 'stable' in 2024 despite a dedicated value-based-selling programme, and peers openly complaining of irrational competitive pricing by 2026 — a question Salil declines to engage with. Nandan himself names the mechanism, citing Christensen's overshoot: newcomers win with products that are merely good enough, and AI coding tools make greenfield output near-free ('I can take a tool and give it to a kid and he will generate a million lines of code'). The exposure is bounded because the defended niche is real.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Management itself frames AI as a discontinuity rather than incremental innovation — Nilekani's Investor AI Day says this transition is 'much more fundamental' than cloud or mobile, that legacy modernisation 'cannot be deferred anymore', and Parekh concedes 'productivity-led benefits that compress some legacy areas', i.e. AI deflates the effort-priced base the current model rests on. Analysts pressed on AI revenue quantification for several quarters; disclosed AI revenue is still only 8.2% of the total, so the compressing 92% is the larger exposure today. (The long view had said: The entire Feb 2026 Investor AI Day is devoted to a platform shift the chairman himself calls unlike prior transitions — 'root and branch surgery' — and the linear people-based delivery model is directly threatened, with journalists asking when AI revenue will offset 'productivity-led compression'. AI is only 8.2% of revenue in Jul 2026 while headcount-based work is the other ~92%, and management concedes AI revenue is 'a bit up and down' quarter to quarter; the whole 2022-2026 arc shows revenue growth decelerating as the transition bites.)
- **Government Dependency**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The delivery model depends on US immigration policy — the Oct 2025 call opened with H-1B questions and management had to reassure that client delivery would continue 'without any disruption'. Government is also becoming a direct customer: the largest deal of the year was the $1.6bn NHS contract. Pricing and revenue are not regulator-set, and the business is not fixed-infrastructure, which caps the exposure. (The long view had said: Revenue is set by commercial contracts with private enterprises across BFSI, manufacturing, retail, hi-tech and CMT in the US, Europe and Australia — no regulator, tariff or subsidy sets pricing anywhere in the four years of calls. The only policy touchpoints are labour-side (Karnataka's local reservation bill, localization/visa programmes), which management treats as compliance rather than a revenue driver; India itself is called 'a small part of our business'.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
