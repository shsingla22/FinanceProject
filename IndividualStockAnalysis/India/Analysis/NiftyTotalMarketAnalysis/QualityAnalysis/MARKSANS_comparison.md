### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 2 worsened, 3 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Marksans supplies largely undifferentiated OTC/store-brand and generic Rx product where the retailer or intermediary controls the choice and end-consumer brand awareness is minimal, so price is the battleground: high single-digit UK price erosion in Q1 FY26, continuing high single-digit Rx erosion in Q3 FY26, UK/EU revenue flat year-on-year in Q3 on persistent pricing pressure, and management repeatedly framing new launches as a defence against pricing rather than as pricing power. (The long view had said: Marksans is a contract supplier of unbranded product where the retailer owns the brand and the end consumer has zero awareness of the manufacturer, so the intermediary controls the choice entirely — and management states plainly that buyers will switch if 'cheaper cost of goods from somewhere else' appears. The consequence is visible across every call: high-single-digit US price erosion persisting from FY23 to FY26 and UK/EU revenue going flat in FY26 on pricing pressure, meaning growth must be bought with volume and new SKUs.)
- **Cyclicality**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Demand is flow/consumable — repeat-purchase OTC and Rx in pain, allergy, GI and cough-cold, not customer capex — but the last 12 months show real supply-demand and input-cost swings: UK Rx suffered oversupply-driven price erosion in Q1 FY26, and Q4 FY26 flagged 20-30% petroleum-linked raw-material inflation, with EBITDA margin ranging 16.1% to 22.8% within the year. (The long view had said: Across all 16 calls demand is described as operations-linked repeat-purchase medicine (pain, cough-cold, allergy, GI) with only mild seasonality — Q3 being the strongest quarter — rather than tied to any customer's capex or a commodity price cycle; revenue compounded steadily from ~INR 434cr/quarter in Q1FY23 to INR 856cr in Q4FY26. The volatility that exists is input-cost driven (freight, petroleum-linked intermediates), and management invested countercyclically by acquiring and scaling the Teva Goa plant while remaining debt-free with ~INR 990cr cash.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Stakeholder Concentration (ELEVATED); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
