# Sudeep Pharma (SUDEEPPHRM) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Sudeep Pharma, founded in 1989 and headquartered in Vadodara, Gujarat, makes high-purity mineral-based ingredients — calcium, zinc, iron, potassium, magnesium and sodium compounds — plus more engineered 'specialty' versions of them, selling roughly 100 products to pharmaceutical, food and nutrition companies in close to 100 countries. Its ingredients go into tablets, dietary supplements, infant formula, medical/clinical nutrition, fortified foods, beverages and dairy, so it earns money by selling these inputs in bulk to manufacturers, including 14 Fortune 500 and 40 blue-chip customers, with the US historically its largest market alongside India, Europe, Asia-Pacific and South America. Its edge is regulatory: it says it is the first and only Indian company with US FDA approval for mineral-based ingredients and the only Indian holder (one of nine globally) of a European CEP certificate to sell calcium carbonate as an API, and the only Indian supplier approved by the three largest global infant-nutrition companies — approvals that take five to seven years to win and keep customers sticky. It manufactures at five plants, four in Gujarat and one in Ireland (acquired in May 2025), and is building both a large greenfield plant at Nandesari and a new battery-materials plant at Dahej.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Pharmaceutical, Food and Nutrition** — The original and largest business, making high-purity mineral excipients and ingredients (calcium, iron, magnesium, zinc, etc.) for drug tablets, supplements and fortified foods; it was about 60% of revenue in H1 FY26 and roughly 56% in FY26, growing 10% over FY25, with calcium the core volume and customer-entry product.
- **Specialty Ingredients** — The faster-growing, higher-margin side started in 2021, where the same minerals are engineered through encapsulation, spray drying, granulation and premixing so they absorb better, last longer or taste right in infant, medical, sports and supplement products; it rose from 34% of revenue in FY25 to about 44% in FY26, with revenue up 62% to INR 280 crore from INR 172 crore, having compounded at 32% since inception.
- **Sudeep Advanced Materials (SAM)** — A new, pre-revenue-scale battery-materials venture making battery-grade iron phosphate for LFP batteries — an extension of the food-grade iron phosphate it has made for 10 years — positioned as one of very few non-China sources; a 25,000 tonne-per-year Phase 1 plant at Dahej is due by April 2027, 42 customers are in qualification (22 in lab validation, 14 in pilot, 6 commercially validated and in off-take talks), and first commercial orders of about 700 tonnes have started, served from 5,000 tonnes of capacity added at existing sites.
- **NSS (Nutrition Supplies and Services), Ireland** — An 85%-owned Irish subsidiary bought in May 2025 through Sudeep Pharma BV, a 40-year-old specialist in infant and medical nutrition vitamin/mineral premixes, which gives Sudeep immediate European access without the usual four-to-five-year approval wait and is being expanded under a newly appointed business head.
- **Nandesari greenfield facility** — The company's fifth and largest plant in Gujarat, with 51,200 tonnes of capacity, built to serve tighter pharma and infant-nutrition regulatory standards and to add next-generation, higher-value molecules such as bisglycinates (sold under the AbsorBis brand), gluconates, glycinates and citrates; commissioning slipped on LPG and energy shortages and it is now in customer qualification expected to take six to 12 months.
- **R&D platform** — Three dedicated R&D centres — two in India, one in Ireland — with 41 scientists and about 2% of revenue spent annually, providing the encapsulation, spray drying, liposomal delivery and precision blending know-how the specialty business is built on.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Sudeep Pharma comes out of these three reviews as one story told three ways: a small Indian maker of mineral ingredients that has spent 36 years buying itself permission to sell to the world's most demanding buyers, and is now spending heavily to widen that position. The quality framework gives top marks to the things that took decades to build — sole Indian holder of US FDA approval for mineral ingredients and European CEP for calcium carbonate, the only Indian supplier approved by all three of the largest global infant-nutrition companies, R&D at about 2% of revenue across three facilities and 41 scientists. The multibagger screen picks up the same facts and calls them a Cost to Replicate barrier (STRONG FIT) and a Market Share Gainers story (STRONG FIT): 51 new customer approvals in FY26, specialty ingredients rising from 34% of revenue in FY25 to 44% in FY26, and the displacement of a sole American supplier at a global tortilla maker. The risk review looks at the identical evidence from the other side and rates New Entrants LOW for exactly the reason the pattern screen rates the barrier high — five-to-seven-year customer approval cycles and site-specific audits keep newcomers out. Business quality scores 73, pattern fit 64 and risk safety 68, blending to an overall 69 out of 100, or Strong.

The trait that drives most of the upside also generates most of the risk. Because the ingredient is critical to the customer but a small part of their cost, buyers absorbed US tariffs rather than switch suppliers and FY26 phosphoric-acid inflation was passed through without margin compression — that is the Toll Roads pattern (LIKELY FIT) and it is what keeps the Good Enough Goods risk at WATCH rather than worse. But the same reliance on a handful of blue-chip accounts named only by superlative — the three largest infant-nutrition companies, the world's largest bakery company, the largest tortilla maker — is why Stakeholder Concentration is rated ELEVATED, with the greenfield plant sized to top-10 customers. Likewise, the deep approvals that make the position hard to copy are the reason Pricing Power reads only LIKELY FIT rather than strong: FY26 gains were input-cost recovery and a mix shift into higher-realisation molecules, not pricing above inflation, and consolidated EBITDA margin has drifted down from 40.9% in H1 FY25. The push into direct sales teams in the US, Europe and LatAm scores well as Geographical expansion and shows up as Forward Integrators (PARTIAL), but the record notes it has so far cost margin rather than added it — the same move read as strength in one framework and as a cost in another.

The clearest tension in the file is between what the balance sheet says and what the working capital says. Management is scored full marks as Disciplined stewards — organic-first, capex funded from internal accruals, net debt of just INR33.6cr or 0.04x equity, investing through a volatile FY26 — yet Cost of working capital is the framework's worst check at -2, with about 372 days of cash tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and the cycle worsening from 184 to 213 days in FY26 against guidance of 140-150 days. That is why Return on Capital is the only negative quality area at -0.33 while Competitive Advantage and Customer Benefits both sit at 1.5, and why Corporate Culture rates only PARTIAL despite candid disclosure of bad news. The second tension is the battery-materials bet: roughly INR600cr at Dahej on top of a INR150cr greenfield, sitting alongside a stable, consumption-linked ingredients business. That single decision is what pushes Cyclicality to ELEVATED, keeps Technological Innovation on WATCH because the capex concentrates on one cathode chemistry in an industry where chemistry shifts happen, and creates the Government Dependency WATCH because the case for it rests on policy forcing sourcing away from China — while management concedes it will not compete with China on price. Overall resilience is judged SOUND, but the verdict rests on the core ingredients business carrying the company while the new platform proves itself.
