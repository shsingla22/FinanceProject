### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 3 weakened, 6 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Management leans on brand — own-channel share rising to 74% of Q4 sales, A&P guided to 7-8%, adjacency stretch into decor/plants under one name, and confidence to push through price rises — but the brand is young (founded 2016), holds well under 1% share, and competitors are 'matching of prices similar to Wakefit' with rising ad spend, so heritage and durability are unproven in this window. (The long view had said: A 10-year-old D2C brand with A&P guided to 7-8% of sales, cross-category stretch (mattress → furniture → furnishings → decor) and enough standing to push through 15% price rises, but no heritage, competitive intensity is rising from smaller labels matching prices, and the aspiration 'when they think of home, they should think of Wakefit' is still stated as a goal rather than an achieved position.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: The two calls describe one-time product sales (mattresses, furniture, furnishings) with no licence fees, AMC/service contracts, subscriptions, deferred revenue or customer advances discussed; a 35% repeat-customer ratio is repeat purchasing, not a contracted recurring stream, so the evidence is silent on this pattern. (The long view had said: No licence, AMC, subscription or deferred-revenue stream exists; the model is one-off product sales in what management itself calls an infrequent purchase category, with repeat purchase (35% repeat ratio, cross-category flywheel) offered as the substitute — repeat buying, not contracted recurring revenue.)

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: LIKELY FIT → NO FIT. Why now: The March/April FY26 price actions (7-8% + 7-8%) were reactive pass-throughs of 30-160% raw-material inflation taken industry-wide, still leaving margin expansion constrained, and management expects possible volume slowdown — price increases below cost inflation with volume risk, not pricing power. (The long view had said: Two 7-8% price hikes in March and April 2026 were passed through and premiumisation is lifting ASP (COCO ASP ~80% above online), but these were defensive cost pass-throughs against 30% COGS inflation that still left margin compression, were matched industry-wide, and management concedes likely volume loss — the opposite of pricing above cost inflation without volume loss.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Growth is ahead of industry on management's telling (mattress +20% in Q4 'outpacing the industry growth trends', furniture +24% FY26, company +17%) from a sub-1% base, and they say they are 'very protective' of share — but no explicit share percentages over time versus named competitors are given, and they concede they may sacrifice 1-2 points of EBITDA to hold share. (The long view had said: Management repeatedly frames the story as share capture — 17% FY26 revenue growth, mattress +20% in Q4 'outpacing the industry', estimated early-double-digit organized share and >25% online, and growth roughly double legacy players — but the numbers are self-estimates in the absence of industry data, share targets are explicitly not set, and only two quarters of listed history exist to verify persistence.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Management's own commentary describes the advantage as replicable: INR100-300 crore rivals with lower cost structures are already matching Wakefit's prices and ad spend, and consumer price outcomes across major brands now differ by only 1-3% — no time-, capital- or data-based barrier a funded newcomer could not rebuild is articulated. (The long view had said: Management is candid that the advantage is scale, not entry barrier — in-house manufacturing, supplier preferential allotment of polyol/TDI, 100-day trial returns processing and 10-20 year warranty balance-sheet strength, plus a multi-category flywheel — while explicitly admitting a well-funded copycat can enter cheaply, which is why the test is only partly passed.)

*Unchanged: Forward Integrators (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 2 worsened, 3 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The Q4 FY26 call is dominated by an input-commodity cycle — crude-linked polyol/TDI, foam, coils and boards spiking 30–160% — forcing two price hikes in two months and threatening 1–2pp of EBITDA, alongside discretionary demand moderation in H2 FY26; the demand side is replacement/consumer-operations driven rather than customer capex, which bounds the risk. (The long view had said: Demand is consumer replacement/flow demand rather than customer capex, but the cost base is squarely commodity-cyclical: management says PU foam, spring coils and fabric are 'subject to the global commodity cycles, especially the crude', with polyol/TDI up 30-150% in Q4 FY26, forcing two 7-8% price hikes and guiding EBITDA down 1-2ppt if crude stays at $100; demand also 'moderated' on softer discretionary spending in H2 FY26.)
- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Management confirmed competitive intensity has stepped up over the last six to nine months from smaller INR100–300cr D2C/marketplace players, and is responding with margin investment — A&P rising from 5.7% in FY26 to a guided 7–8% of sales — but these are same-format rivals rather than a structurally new retail format resetting industry economics. (The long view had said: Management repeatedly and defensively flags a wave of structurally cheaper D2C entrants: competitive intensity 'definitely gone up over the last six to nine months' from INR100-300 crore players with lower cost structures matching Wakefit's prices and outspending on ads, prompting A&P to be guided up from 5.7% of FY26 sales to 7-9%. An analyst laid out that post-COVID entrants like Sleep Company are already half Wakefit's size and that the industry gross-profit and EBITDA pool has deteriorated sharply over 10 years — and management conceded 'barrier to entry to this industry might be low'.)

**That worsened:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Management explicitly described lower-cost challengers without recognised brands — marketplace labels and regional players at INR100–300cr — matching Wakefit's prices while running a much lower cost structure, exactly the good-enough attack pattern on a product with ~56% gross margin; the ability to hold a 15% price increase now rests on brand strength that management concedes it must spend 7–8% of sales to build. (The long view had said: Management openly concedes 'barrier to entry to this industry might be low' given abundant contract foam capacity, and spends much of the Q4 call defending its price premium against cheaper alternatives — unorganised players using limestone adulterants and structural cash/GST/PF cost advantages, plus unbranded marketplace labels. It also conceded the price gap widens further after Wakefit's 15% hikes, and an analyst documented the industry's gross-profit and EBITDA pool deteriorating sharply over a decade — the classic good-enough squeeze on a not-very-complex product.)
- **Government Dependency**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: No government customers, tenders or regulated pricing, but management explicitly flagged that the GST reform left their categories at 18% while cutting other discretionary categories, shifting consumer wallets away from them and depressing Q3 demand — a demonstration that a single tax-policy decision moves their volumes. (The long view had said: Revenue is consumer-funded through own website, COCO stores, marketplaces and MBOs with no government orders, tenders, tariffs or subsidised pricing anywhere in either call; the only policy touchpoints are an indirect, self-described temporary GST-driven shift in consumer wallets (their categories stayed at 18%) and a one-time INR39.3 million Labour Code charge.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Technological Innovation (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
