# KRN Heat Exch Refrig (KRN) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

KRN Heat Exchanger and Refrigeration is an Indian manufacturer, founded in 2017 and based at Neemrana in Rajasthan, that makes heat exchangers — the aluminium and copper tube coils that move heat inside cooling equipment, including condenser coils, evaporator coils and water coils. It sells almost entirely business-to-business to OEMs (original equipment makers) who build air conditioning and refrigeration equipment, with named customers including Daikin and Schneider Electric; management said on the Aug-2025 call that the business is 100% B2B and that it deliberately stays out of the low-margin aftermarket for its established products. It makes money by manufacturing to customer specification and repricing quarter-on-quarter against copper LME, aluminium LME and the USD-INR rate, so it says it passes on close to 100% of raw material moves, and it typically carries 2 to 2.5 months of metal inventory. It sells mainly in India (about 81% of revenue in Q2 FY25) with the rest exported to markets including the UAE, US, Canada, Italy, Germany and France; consolidated total income for FY26 was Rs.609.81 crores, up 38.06%, with net profit of Rs.76.47 crores, and it listed on the NSE and BSE in October 2024.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Heat exchangers and coils (fin and tube) — the core business** — The original product line of aluminium and copper tube heat exchangers, condenser coils, evaporator coils and water coils sold to HVAC and refrigeration OEMs; this is the bulk of revenue and management describes the company as having started from 'four heat exchanger products', with the old plant running at 100% capacity by late 2024.
- **KRN HVAC Products Private Limited (the new Neemrana facility)** — A wholly-owned subsidiary whose new plant began commercial operations on 31 May 2025 and adds roughly six times the old facility's capacity; it won a PLI (production-linked incentive) approval for white goods worth Rs.141.72 crores, added 40-plus new customers in a single quarter, and management expects only about 50% utilisation in FY27, so it is the main growth engine but still ramping.
- **New refrigeration products** — Wire and tube condensers, roll bond evaporators, frost-free evaporators and skin condensers — components for refrigerators, made at the new facility; these are new enough that KRN is selling some of them into the aftermarket while it settles machines, manpower and quality systems.
- **Bar and plate heat exchangers** — A different construction type of heat exchanger added as a new product with the new capacity, aimed at bringing in customers the existing fin-and-tube range did not serve.
- **Complete (finished) products — dry air coolers and ammonia evaporators** — A step up from selling components to selling whole units used in industrial cooling and refrigeration; described as part of the move toward a 'more integrated offering' rather than sized separately in the calls.
- **Bus air conditioning** — Entered during FY26 as a strategic move from component supplier to full system supplier, helped by in-house heat exchangers, tubing and FRP; it contributed about Rs.10 crores in FY26 and management is targeting at least a 15% market share, so it is small today but flagged as a meaningful future avenue.
- **Data centre cooling** — Currently supplied with the same fin-and-tube coils used in chillers and dry coolers, this was about 18.7% of Q4 FY26 revenue, up roughly 2.7 percentage points quarter-on-quarter, with long lead-time orders booked six to seven months out and two new export customers — described as the fastest-growing end market.
- **Exports** — Sales outside India, roughly 19% of revenue in Q2 FY25 and about Rs.100 crores in the latest year, driven by the UAE, US, Canada and Italy with early traction in Europe; the company reported an export order book of over Rs.120 crores including data centre orders.
- **Thermotech Research Laboratory** — A separately identified testing lab held under a subsidiary, set up under an MoU with AHRI and described as the first AHRI-approved lab in India; it earns no meaningful revenue yet and exists to certify products for Indian, European, American and UAE customers and eventually support patents.
- **Products under development** — Micro-channel heat exchangers (MCHX), expected in the portfolio by end of 2026 or early 2027, and plate heat exchangers for liquid-cooled data centre CDUs, still at the study stage — neither contributes revenue today.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

KRN is a small heat exchanger maker riding genuinely expanding end markets, and the quality work reflects that: structural end-market growth, market share gain and profit margin all score at the top of the scale. Commercial air conditioning is growing over 20% and data centre cooling over 50%, against 8% for room air conditioners, and KRN is capturing a visible slice of it — roughly two new customers a month, more than 40 new customers billed from the new plant in a single quarter, and data centre work rising from 7% to 15% to 18.7% of revenue with a 15% target ahead. Out of every Rs.100 of sales, Rs.19 is operating profit, and that figure has barely moved (about plus or minus 1.7 points a year), which the framework reads as an industry where pricing is rational and few new competitors are crowding in. The multibagger screen finds the same growth but almost no durable moat behind it: only one of eleven patterns is a likely fit, Forward Integrators, based on the small Spear bus air conditioner purchase that moves KRN from selling a component to selling a finished system and carries better-than-company gross margin above 20%, supported by in-house plastic, tubing and sheet metal work and the Thermotech research lab, whose 7mm-to-5mm tube already delivers the same capacity at about 10% lower cost.

The uncomfortable part is that the same aggressive build-out that produces the growth is what drags down the returns. The 6x greenfield expansion — a 600,000 square foot plant plus a Rs.1,000 crore state agreement — was funded and commissioned, but it slipped about two months and ran at only 15% utilisation in FY26 against the 25% management had promised, so the capex check scores just 1. That unfinished ramp shows up directly in the two worst checks in the whole review: the business generated minus Rs.15 of operating cash for every Rs.100 of capital employed this year, and about 208 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. Because the plant does not yet pay for itself, the money has come from shareholders rather than from operations — a Rs.342 crore listing followed by approval to raise up to Rs.500 crore more, with no stated use of proceeds at announcement and no dividend or buyback policy, which is why the shareholder distribution check is negative. The cyclicality risk sits on exactly this point: day-to-day demand is tied to customers' production rather than their own investment plans, and raw material costs pass through quarterly, but the company has stacked a 6x capacity build, incentive and equity fundraising and a tripling of inventory on top of that. The culture pattern captures both sides — management openly flagged the delay and the Rs.4 crore of startup costs that hit early FY26 profit, yet the ramp has repeatedly come in below its own guidance.

On durability the three reviews say the same thing from different angles, which is why the combined score lands at 43 out of 100, or Mixed. Four patterns are outright non-fits — pricing power, brand, recurring revenue and a friendly middleman channel — and each has a matching risk. Prices are a mechanical quarterly pass-through of copper, aluminium and currency in both directions rather than increases above cost inflation, and KRN deliberately sits 15-20% below Western suppliers and walked away from the higher-margin spare parts business; that same trait is what earns the rational-pricing point on the quality side but also feeds the 'good enough goods' risk, since a fin-and-tube coil is not complex and the buyer is an equipment maker that already builds the same part in-house. The low-price position is likewise propped up by policy — a sanctioned Rs.141.72 crore incentive worth roughly twice FY26 net profit, state incentives, a 15% concessional tax entity and the Rs.1,000 crore state agreement — which is the single HIGH RISK finding, government dependency, softened only by the fact that the actual buyers are private companies spread across the UAE, US, Europe and Canada. The new-entrant risk points at cheaper Chinese and Vietnamese supply and what happens if anti-dumping duties are relaxed, and management's answer is an assertion of non-overlap rather than demonstrated protection. Most pointedly, the fastest-growing end market is the one drifting away from KRN's core format: management conceded that the indoor side of data centres is moving toward liquid cooling units and plate heat exchangers, and that micro-channel is not yet in the portfolio. Customer concentration compounds it, with results anchored on a few named equipment makers and lumpy orders such as a single Rs.120 crore UAE order against Rs.610 crore of FY26 revenue. The one steadying note is that the business is judged structurally SOUND, with fashion risk low — this is functional, repeat-purchase industrial supply designed into customer platforms, protected by two-to-twelve-month qualification cycles and factory audits.
