Now: No R&D spend is disclosed, but the CDMO development pipeline is converting into commercialised products and a visible launch queue. | The long view had said: R&D is modest and lifecycle-oriented (new products only ~3% of growth, 70-80% within existing therapies), and the FY26 call flags execution delays converting CDMO development contracts to revenue.

Now: Q4 management named and quantified the self-inflicted reset, disclosed the trade-generics drag size and duration, and refused to guide where visibility was poor. | The long view had said: Management openly quantified negative items — Azmarda's outsized gross-margin drag, the deliberate South Africa tender reduction, acute gastro slowdown — though it turned evasive on dividends and merger timing in FY26.

Now: Strong headline margins are traceable to identifiable levers (price hikes, mix, cost optimisation, portfolio pruning) rather than unexplained excellence, and Q4 showed the results can break. | The long view had said: The Q4 FY26 'operational reset' by the acquirer — resetting credit periods, field incentives, sales cut-off policies and in-market inventory — suggests part of the celebrated growth record reflected channel practices rather than pure operating skill.

Now: Reach and execution consistently convert into above-market growth across chronic, acute and ophthalmology, and CDMO adds entrenched global partner channels. | The long view had said: Distribution is solid but not unmatchable — the network required optimisation post-acquisition and JB's reach is concentrated in select states with an acknowledged coverage gap versus Torrent.

Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. ### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 7 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 5. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The four calls discuss product revenue, order books and CDMO dispatches only — no attach rates, AMC/service revenue, renewals, deferred revenue or customer advances are mentioned. (The long view had said: Across 17 calls there is no license fee, AMC, subscription or renewal stream — revenue is unit sales of formulations and CDMO order-book manufacturing; and working capital is positive and funded by the company, not customers (87 days net working capital in FY24, improved from 98).)

**That weakened:**

- **Friendly Middleman**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The business is explicitly a prescription-branded model — management repeatedly separates the 'branded business' from trade generics and rationalises the latter, and discusses field force incentives and distribution, implying doctor-led demand where the patient pays; but no call in the window discusses prescriber training or channel-relationship investment directly. (The long view had said: The entire India franchise is prescription-led: 2,500 MRs detailing cardiologists, nephrologists, consulting physicians, paediatricians and ophthalmologists, with prescription counts and MR productivity as the headline KPIs (2.6cr to 4.09cr scripts in Q1FY23; productivity doubling to INR7 lakh/MR/month). The doctor chooses, the patient pays.)
- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Execution and conservatism are evident — gross debt of INR7cr against INR939cr net cash, margin guidance of 27-29% reiterated and met, and Q4's disruptions disclosed plainly as an 'operational reset' with itemised causes — but the domestic 12-14% growth guidance was quietly re-anchored to 'market plus 200-300bps' in Q3, and the entire leadership (MD, CFO) turned over after Torrent's January 2026 change of control, so long-tenure continuity cannot be judged from this window. (The long view had said: Promises made in early calls were delivered later — EBITDA guidance walked up 24-26% (FY23) to 26-28% (FY24) to 27-29% (FY26) and met, debt of INR548cr repaid to net cash of ~INR1,200cr, ROCE 21% to 27%, operating cash flow more than doubled; bad news is disclosed plainly (South Africa tender exit, Q4FY26 'operational reset', CDMO 'delays in execution of these contracts').)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (QUANT SIGNAL); Toll Roads (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Pricing Power (STRONG FIT); Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
