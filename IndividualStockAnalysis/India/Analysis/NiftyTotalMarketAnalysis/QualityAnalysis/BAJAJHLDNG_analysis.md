### The story in depth

Bajaj Holdings & Investment scores 51 out of 100 — "Decent", three stars — and that number is built from three very different pieces: 55 for business quality (45% weight), just 10 for multibagger fit (30% weight), and 92 for risk safety (25% weight). The picture underneath is of a company whose money-making mechanics are excellent but whose engine is small relative to its size. On the strong side, the "Intangible benefits" check scores the maximum: the product has essentially no direct input costs, so nearly all of every ₹100 of sales is left over for people, marketing and profit. That flows straight into a profit margin of ₹86 of operating profit per ₹100 of sales. The "Cost of working capital" check also scores the maximum — cash comes back before suppliers need to be paid, so the business funds itself rather than tying up money. Capital Allocation and Customer Benefits both score a full 2.0.

The catch is that the same structure that produces those strengths also produces the weaknesses, and this is the central tension in the record. Every ₹1 of assets generates only ₹0.01 of sales — the "Asset turn" check scores -2 for being fairly asset-heavy. So an ₹86-per-₹100 margin sitting on a very thin stream of sales still only produces ₹7 of operating cash for every ₹100 of capital employed, a 7% cash return that scores -1 and drags the Return on Capital area to -0.67. High margins and low returns are not a contradiction here; they are two readings of one fact. The second linked pair is volatility: margins swing by about ±7.9 points a year, which the "Profit margin" check reads as key costs sitting outside management's control, the "Barriers to entry and rationality" check reads as a hint of price wars or new entrants (Industry Structure scores 0.0), and the risk review picks up as the numeric fingerprints behind both flagged risks — Cyclicality and Fashion Risk. Growth is the weakest area at -2.0: sales have grown about 7% a year but lumpily, and lumpy growth is what you would expect from the same swinging economics.

The third thread is how much of this verdict rests on missing evidence rather than proven facts. Every one of the eleven multibagger patterns was judged without any conference-call input: Recurring Revenue, Pricing Power, Brand Strength and Innovation Dominance came back PARTIAL, and Friendly Middleman, Toll Roads, Low Price Plus, Forward Integrators and Market Share Gainers came back NO FIT — all for the same stated reason, that no call judgement was available. That is why the multibagger score is only 10 out of 100 and why Competitive Advantage and Management have no score at all. The same silence cuts the other way on risk: both Cyclicality and Fashion Risk are labelled QUANT FLAG, meaning a number showed up but the calls said nothing either way, and the framework is explicit that numbers alone are a flag to investigate, never a verdict. The 92 risk-safety score is therefore closer to "nothing was proven against it" than "it was proven safe", and resilience is rated SOUND on that basis. The honest read is a company with genuinely attractive per-rupee economics, weak growth and weak returns on a heavy asset base, and a large evidence gap in the middle that management commentary would need to fill before the score could move meaningfully in either direction.
