# Tenneco Clean (TENNIND) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Tenneco Clean Air India makes auto parts that it sells to vehicle manufacturers (OEMs) rather than to car buyers — emissions/exhaust after-treatment systems and powertrain components on one side, and shock absorbers, struts and other suspension parts on the other. Its customers are passenger-vehicle, commercial-truck, off-highway, tractor and industrial OEMs in India, with whom it has relationships averaging about 20 years; it earns money by winning multi-year vehicle programs and then supplying parts for the life of each platform, and it tracks its results on 'value added revenue' (VAR), which strips out pass-through costs of substrates and precious metals. It operates 12 locations in India including plants and two R&D centres sited near customer factories, and exports to 20 countries, with exports over 20% of the order book. FY26 value added revenue was INR49,180 million (up 12.3%), EBITDA INR9,255 million at an 18.8% margin, PAT INR6,044 million, with no debt, a negative 23-day cash conversion cycle and a 94% return on capital employed; it listed on the NSE and BSE in November 2025 after an IPO subscribed 61.8 times.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Clean Air and Powertrain Solutions** — Makes exhaust after-treatment and emissions systems (plus powertrain parts such as bearings) that help engines meet tighter standards like BS6 and Euro 7 — roughly half the business at INR24,296 million of FY26 value added revenue, growing more slowly at 5.5%, and where management says it leads the Indian market for commercial-truck and off-highway OEMs; recent wins include a first-time entry at a large Japanese passenger-vehicle OEM in India and a modular after-treatment program with a global commercial-vehicle OEM.
- **Advanced Ride Technologies** — Makes suspension and ride-comfort parts — shock absorbers, struts and dampers — where the company says it is number one in India for passenger vehicles; it is the faster-growing half at INR24,885 million of FY26 value added revenue, up 19.7%, helped by the shift to SUVs and premium models and by its patented DaVinci DCx mechanical suspension, adopted by a leading Indian OEM for a flagship SUV (about INR2,200 million of annual revenue potential from that one program) and now spreading to other OEMs.
- **Order book and exports (spans both segments)** — Awarded but not-yet-started programs, quoted as lifetime value, stood at INR124,000 million as of March 2026 — enough, management says, to cover 100% of its FY28 revenue target and support double-digit growth, with exports over 20% of it and helped by lower US and EU tariffs; two new greenfield plants (Clean Air in Kharkhoda, Haryana and Advanced Ride Technologies in West India) totalling about INR1,400 million of capex are being built to serve it.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Tenneco Clean scores 68 out of 100 ("Strong", four stars), and the way that number is built tells the story: 83 for business quality, 76 for risk safety, but only 39 for multibagger pattern fit. The quality half is genuinely unusual. The business turns every ₹1 of assets into ₹2.13 of sales, runs fixed asset turns of 9.6x, earns a 94% return on capital and generated ₹114 of operating cash for every ₹100 of capital employed. Customers pay it about 30 days before its own suppliers need paying, so working capital is negative and the business partly funds itself. On top of that it carries no debt (net debt-to-equity of negative 0.4), has done no acquisitions, and paid for two new greenfield plants of roughly INR 1,400mn out of internal cash while utilisation climbed from 80% to above 90%. Capital Allocation scores a full 2.0 and Return on Capital 1.67 — the strongest areas in the framework. The weak spots are Growth and Industry Structure at 1.0 and Management at 0.67, the last of which is simply a record-length problem: only three earnings calls exist since the November 2025 listing, which is also why the Corporate Culture pattern lands at PARTIAL rather than higher despite promises being kept inside that short window.

The low pattern score is not a contradiction of the high quality score — it reflects what kind of company this is. Only one of eleven patterns is a STRONG FIT: Market Share Gainers, backed by CRISIL-sourced shock absorber share moving from 48% to 52%, value-added revenue growing 8-17% a quarter against served sub-markets growing 4-5%, and 'white space' entries into a leading Japanese passenger-vehicle OEM's clean air and bearings panels. Three more are LIKELY: Innovation Dominance (the patented DaVinci DCx won a flagship SUV platform worth about INR 2,200mn a year, plus a Euro 7 proof-of-concept), Low Price Plus (the P3 operating system, modular capex, capacity moved from Europe because India is cheaper), and Recurring Revenue, which fits only in a funding sense — there is no subscription or aftermarket service line, but a lifetime order book of INR 124,000mn with 5-7 year programme lock-ins gives 100% revenue coverage through FY28. Five patterns are PARTIAL and two — Friendly Middleman and Forward Integrators — do not fit at all, because this is a direct tier-1 supplier with no trade channel, no stores, no consumer brand and no aftermarket. So the pattern score is low mostly because the company's shape excludes several patterns outright, not because the business is failing tests it should pass.

The most important thing to see is that several traits cut both ways. Deep OEM entanglement — multi-year validation cycles, resident engineers, 12 plants sited next to customer bases, roughly 20-year average relationships — produces the switching-cost strength and the Cost to Replicate pattern, and it is the same trait that creates the Stakeholder Concentration risk, since results hinge on a handful of named relationships (Mahindra, Tata, Toyota, the Japanese OEM described as the strategic swing factor) plus captive exports back to Tenneco Europe. It also creates the Good Enough Goods risk: the OEM both specifies and pays, so it can push relentlessly on price — management describes being asked 'Can you make it more affordable?', won a Clean Air programme by cutting overall system cost, and continuously strips out platinum, palladium and rhodium. That same dynamic is why Pricing Power is only PARTIAL: EBITDA margin rising from 14.3% in FY24 to a record 18.8% in FY26 reads as cost recovery and better mix rather than the ability to simply charge more. Likewise, emissions rules (BS6.2, BS7, CAFE 3, TREM 5, Euro 7) are simultaneously the structural growth engine, the basis of the Toll Roads pattern where a tiny slice of vehicle cost is legally indispensable, and the Government Dependency risk — demand exists because the law says so. The EU-India FTA is the sharpest two-sided item: management calls tariff outcomes a 'game changer' for exports, while an analyst pointed out the same deal lets global component peers into India tariff-free, and management conceded 'all of the above' before pivoting. Six of eight risk channels sit at WATCH and none at high; two are LOW, and overall resilience is judged SOUND, cushioned throughout by the debt-free, negative-working-capital balance sheet.
