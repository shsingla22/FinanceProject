**Checks that improved:**

- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹20 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹20 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±4.1 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Deep engineering, regulatory and safety-critical requirements plus full-stack ownership raise entry barriers, and there is no sign of discount-led competition — but management itself flags AI-led disruption narrowing the window. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±4.1 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Deep IP and regulatory certification create real barriers in banking and transit, but the spaces are bid-based and the AI shift is drawing in new capital and potential frontier-model entrants that management is explicitly asked about.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: Strong multi-year growth is partly attributable to favourable AI/data-centre end-markets and acquired revenue, and the FY26 miss exposed structural stretch — so results cannot yet be cleanly attributed to managerial skill. | The long view had said: Stripping the narrative from the numbers shows repeated guidance slippage (30-35% guided vs 20.2% delivered in FY26), 9M PAT up only 9%, margin compression and negative interim cash flows, so results lag the rhetoric.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Q4 was depressed by a geopolitical shock management believes is temporary with intact spend appetite, and the data-centre business is described as reaching an inflection — consistent with being near a trough with an identifiable upturn. | The long view had said: The only cyclical framing is a temporary MEA geopolitical trough expected to recover, while the core end markets (AI data centres, transit capex) are already in an up-cycle rather than near a bottom.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Aurionpro operates in concentrated niches but its customers are large PSU banks, hyperscalers and metro authorities buying through tenders — parties with substantial negotiating power — and no stable oligopoly structure is evidenced. | The long view had said: Core markets are contested global tenders against many international players, and the customers are large banks, metros and government bodies with strong procurement power, not a stable oligopoly.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Predominantly organic with only small bolt-ons and counter-cyclical investment through a weak quarter, but discipline is strained by negative interim cash flow, rising capitalisation and a willingness to accept balance-sheet pressure. | The long view had said: Counter-cyclical, conviction-led investment and no large M&A are offset by continuous small acquisitions, equity dilution via QIP, rising working-capital borrowing and self-acknowledged balance-sheet strain.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Good. Now: A broadening global sales and delivery network plus single-window full-stack delivery in data centres is a real reach advantage in Asia, but the European, African and North Asian channels are newly built and unproven. | The long view had said: Global reach and delivery capability have been built out over five years, but management repeatedly frames distribution as the limiting factor to be expanded rather than an advantage rivals cannot match.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: The niches (AFC, transaction banking, data centres) are unglamorous, but management is actively courting visibility through PR, brand relaunch and AI positioning, so it is not flying under the radar. | The long view had said: The company is no longer under the radar — a QIP drew prominent global and Indian institutions, it runs regular IR events, and it operates in the two most capital-attracting themes (AI and data centres).
- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: The product has essentially no direct input costs — nearly all of every ₹100 of sales is available for people, marketing and profit (typical of software / internet platforms). And from the calls: Margins are respectable (EBITDA ~20%) but pricing derives from functional capability rather than brand image or taste, and there is no evidence of premium pricing power in the excerpts. | The long view had said: The product has essentially no direct input costs — nearly all of every ₹100 of sales is available for people, marketing and profit (typical of software / internet platforms). And from the calls: There is no image- or taste-driven pricing power; EBITDA sits at ~20% against management's own view that software businesses should earn 40-50%, with hardware and systems content diluting the mix.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 7. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 5 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Record logo additions are reported every quarter of the window (16 in Q1, 19 in Q2 for a record 35 in H1, 23 in Q3) alongside named displacement wins (UCO Bank, a Singapore-headquartered bank for Integro, Delhi Metro AFC, a hyperscaler data centre mandate) and 20-29% growth while, as one analyst noted on the Q4 call, the wider Indian IT sector struggles — though management never quotes an explicit market-share percentage. (The long view had said: Growth has persistently outrun the Indian IT industry (5-year CAGR of 32-33%, revenue roughly quadrupled, 23 new logos in Q3 FY26, expansion into US, LatAm, UK, Australia, Middle East and SE Asia), but the company never quotes explicit market-share numbers versus named competitors, and FY26 growth fell to 20.2% against a ~30% plan.)
- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: The Q4 FY26 call is unusually candid about a miss — management named the 20.2% outcome against a ~30% plan, took the Q4 shortfall as its own responsibility rather than blaming only the MEA war, and admitted five years of fast growth had 'left us stretched in places'; leadership is stable (Zaveri and Sheth as promoters through FY21-FY26, Vipul Parmar as CFO throughout) and capital is being pushed into organic R&D rather than payouts. (The long view had said: Leadership is stable (Rai, Parmar and the Sheth/Zaveri promoters throughout) and the Jun 2026 call owns the miss without spin — naming the MEA war, the slipped hyperscaler revenue and internal over-stretch — and capital is consistently allocated to R&D over near-term margin; against that, the working-capital/collections problem flagged in the very first call (Nov 2022) is still unresolved four years later, the 30% guidance was missed, and intangible capitalisation rose sharply just as growth slowed.)

**That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls the revenue conversation is order-book and project-execution driven (Rs.1,800 crore order book, go-lives, milestone completions) with no disclosure of AMC/licence share, attach rates or renewals; the working-capital signature is the opposite of customer-funded — DSOs of 100-110, negative H1 operating cash flow of ~Rs.80-86 crore, and management guiding to higher upfront working capital in FY27. (The long view had said: The licence-upfront-plus-lifetime-AMC mechanic is explicit and repeated (Jul 2024 call: AMCs deliberately excluded from the order book because they run 'for life'; software revenue described as very sticky in regulated banking), but the funding side of the pattern is contradicted — DSOs of 100-110 days, negative H1 operating cash flow in FY26 and rising working-capital needs since the Nov 2022 call mean customers do not fund the business.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (LIKELY FIT); Friendly Middleman (QUANT SIGNAL); Toll Roads (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (STRONG FIT); Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Pricing Power (QUANT SIGNAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
