**Checks that improved:**

- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Weak → Good. Now: Banking is inherently balance-sheet-heavy, but CUB earns an above-peer return on that asset base — ROA consistently 1.55-1.59% and NIM 3.78-3.89% — supported by a sticky MSME/gold niche rather than by leverage. | The long view had said: A deposit-funded lender is structurally asset-heavy — earnings scale with a Rs.78,000 crore balance sheet at ~1.55% ROA rather than on high asset turn — and the moat is a regional franchise, not a capital-light one.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Regional density in its Tamil Nadu MSME and agricultural gold-loan base gives real relationship stickiness — reflected in 22% average CASA growth and negligible third-party sourcing — but it is local strength, not a captive aftermarket. | The long view had said: Relationship-based MSME lending in a dense home region creates some switching friction, but the bank competes openly with banks and NBFCs and shows no captive pricing — utilisation on MSME limits even fell from 73% to 70%.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: A sub-1% share of Indian banking leaves ample runway, and the bank is visibly capturing share with 21-26% advance growth against an explicit 2-3% over-industry target delivered for eight straight quarters. | The long view had said: Small base (Rs.67,645 crore advances) with an explicit and delivered above-system growth policy — 26% advance growth in FY26, highest in 13 years, and eight consecutive double-digit quarters — though credibility is tempered by the lost CY2023 when growth fell to 2%.
- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Weak → Neutral. Now: The 122-year 'Swadeshi' legacy carries goodwill, but it produces no pricing power — advance yields are flat around 9.7-9.8% and margin improvement came entirely from the liability side. | The long view had said: No image- or taste-driven pricing power: yields on advances are flat around 9.8% across four years, NIM sits in a defended 3.6-3.9% band, and pricing is set by market competition rather than brand.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: MSME relationship lending in Tamil Nadu clusters creates familiarity, but nothing in the calls indicates a monopoly-like position in the customer's mind — the bank competes on being 2-3% faster-growing than a crowded industry. | The long view had said: A sticky, branch-walk-in customer base in its home MSME and agri-gold niches gives a local mind-share position management believes competition cannot easily dislodge.

*Unchanged: Independent, long-term and tenacious; Security by obscurity; Out of limelight; Barriers to entry and rationality; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Candor; Geographical expansion; Disciplined stewards; Cyclical market growth; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Halo effect (caution); Assurance benefits; Technology advantage; Share donators; Convenience benefits; People matter (talent development); Structural end-market growth; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Distribution advantage.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 1 weakened, 7 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The bank variant requires cheap deposits and a low operating cost base; CUB runs CASA at only 27% with cost of deposits of 5.56% and a cost-to-income ratio of 45-49%, and it earns high (not low) unit spreads on ~9.8% yield MSME and gold lending. Loan quality is excellent (GNPA 1.73%, net NPA 0.61%) but the funding and cost profile is not the low-cost model. (The long view had said: Classic bank variant traits are present in part — deposits raised without paying up (cost of deposits 5.56-5.60%), high yields (~9.8%) and very high-quality secured lending (GNPA 1.73%, credit cost guided 0.40%) — but cost discipline is only moderate: cost-to-income sat at 47-51% for years and is only now guided to 47-48%, and CASA is just 28%.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (QUANT SIGNAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
