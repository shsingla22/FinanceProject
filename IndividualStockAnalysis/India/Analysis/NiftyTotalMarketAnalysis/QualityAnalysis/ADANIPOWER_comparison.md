**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Entry barriers are high and visibly binding — land, multi-year BTG lead times, SHAKTI coal linkage and capital — evidenced by Adani taking 9 of 14.5 GW awarded, though pricing is still set by competitive bidding rather than by industry rationality. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±6.2 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Entry barriers are steep — capital, land banks, coal linkage under SHAKTI and multi-year BTG lead times that APL pre-empted — and awarded tariffs are rational two-part structures (INR 6.30/kWh Assam with INR 4.16 fixed) rather than discount wars.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Supply is concentrated among a few large private and state generators and Adani won a disproportionate share of bids, but capacity is allocated through DISCOM-run competitive reverse auctions, so the end customer retains real negotiating power. | The long view had said: Capacity is awarded through competitive state tenders where the DISCOM and its regulator hold the power — the Rajasthan regulator blocked a 3,200 MW PPA outright.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Good → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹37 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹37 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±6.2 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Consistent evidence of long-horizon, independent execution — land and BTG secured years ahead of need, in-house project management, a 10-year-dead plant restarted in two months, and USD 500 million of Bangladesh receivables recovered to normal levels. | The long view had said: A consistent multi-year plan articulated in 2023 and delivered against — BTG for the full 24 GW ordered in advance, land secured, and projects tracking to stated schedules (Mahan 80%, Raipur 62%, Raigarh 54%).
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: The company built through the FY26 demand trough (soft monsoon-hit demand, weak merchant tariffs, PLF down to ~62%) and the upturn has now identifiably arrived with record 271 GW peak demand and 8.4% consumption growth, with 24 GW of new capacity commissioning into it from FY27. | The long view had said: Company entered the window near the bottom of the thermal capex cycle after a decade of under-addition, and management identifies a continuing shortage-driven upturn in peak hours and state bidding.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Largely self-funded, brownfield-led expansion with leverage kept at AA-stable and finance costs controlled, but discipline is qualified by a steady stream of acquisitions and rising debt funded via bridge loans and INR 7,500 crore of NCDs. | The long view had said: Genuine discipline on deleveraging early on and self-funded capex, but offset by continuous large M&A, a related-party purchase from Adani Enterprises, and debt climbing from INR 32,214 cr to INR 58,381 cr.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: Growth is predominantly volume- and capacity-led (17% volume growth, PLF 67%→78%) with realisations swinging on the cycle — down through FY26, up 8%/13% in Q1 FY27 — though the mix shift to availability-based PPAs with higher capacity charges is a genuine positive. | The long view had said: Growth is overwhelmingly volume/capacity-led while realizations fell — merchant tariffs dropped from INR 6.86 to 4.54 and 9M FY26 revenue declined year-on-year despite higher volumes; only the FY27 quarter saw pricing help.

Now: There is no sales force or customer-intimacy story; the nearest analogue is plant siting and fuel logistics that improve delivered-cost access to load centres. | The long view had said: Deliberate locational siting near load centres and coal, plus adjacent captive mine acquisition, gives a fuel-logistics accessibility advantage rivals cannot easily replicate.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 8 unchanged; 1 could not be compared.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Explicit, repeated and quantified share gain across the window: >9 GW won of 14.5 GW of state bids awarded by Q2 FY26, capacity path from 18 GW to 42-45 GW, 56% of upcoming capacity already tied up by Q1 FY27, and Q1 FY27 volumes +17% while all-India demand rose 8.4%. Crucially the gain is not bought with credit standards — PPAs are long-term contracted with fuel pass-through and Bangladesh receivables were normalised. (The long view had said: Sustained, quantified capacity share gain across the whole period — 15.25GW (Q3 FY24) to 17.55GW to 18.15GW, via Godda, Mahan, Coastal Energen, Korba/Lanco, Dahanu, Vidarbha/Butibori and the Jaiprakash insolvency assets — with a 23.7-24GW build programme toward 42-45GW; importantly the growth is being converted into contracted PPAs (56% of upcoming capacity tied up) rather than bought with loose credit terms.)

**That weakened:**

- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Capacity is sold to state DISCOMs through competitive reverse-auction bids (Bihar, MP, Karnataka, Assam, UP), where price is the award criterion — the opposite of an expert recommender insulating the seller from price sensitivity. No trade channel, installer or training programme appears in any of the four calls. (The long view had said: No discussion anywhere in the 11 calls of professionals, installers, prescribers or trade channels; sales are direct to state DISCOMs and power exchanges.)

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Not comparable: Innovation Dominance (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 2 worsened, 3 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Across the four calls earnings swung on weather, merchant tariffs and imported coal indices — Q1–Q3 FY26 saw PLF fall to 62.6% and EBITDA/PAT decline, then Q1 FY27 heatwaves lifted PLF to 78%, merchant realisation +13% and PAT +47%; meanwhile management is committing a very large expansionary capex (18 GW to 42–45 GW, 24 GW of BTG ordered in advance) into the upswing, the classic over-investment setup. (The long view had said: Results visibly swing with weather, power demand and commodity indices: merchant realisation fell from INR 6.86/unit (Q3FY24) to INR 4.54 and INR 4.37, 9M FY26 EBITDA declined YoY, then Q1FY27 EBITDA jumped 22% on a record heatwave — and revenue on import-linked PPAs moves directly with the HBA coal index. Against this, the product is a flow/operations good (baseload electricity, not customer capex), and management has deliberately cut open capacity from 20% to 10% (targeting 3-4%) with fixed capacity charges and fuel pass-through, though the INR 2 lakh crore, 24 GW expansion is being committed at a demand peak.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The output is an undifferentiated commodity sold to DISCOMs through reverse tariff bidding, so there is no brand premium to defend and the only defence is being the cheapest — management frames its entire competitive case as cost and capital-cost leadership rather than product differentiation, leaving economics exposed to any rival that can bid a lower tariff. (The long view had said: APL is not the premium incumbent being undercut — it is itself the low-cost option, competing on lowest tariff in reverse auctions and citing fuel-logistics cost advantage, locational advantage and merit-order position as its edge; no call shows management defending a price premium or a channel partner pushing a cheaper own-label substitute.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Fashion Risk (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
