## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 30 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 11 improved, 3 regressed, 16 unchanged. 2 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them).

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Allocation | Neutral (+0.50) | Neutral (+0.25) | -0.25 |
| Return on Capital | Neutral (+0.00) | Good (+0.67) | +0.67 |
| Growth | Good (+0.83) | Good (+1.33) | +0.50 |
| Management | Neutral (+0.00) | Neutral (+0.17) | +0.17 |
| Industry Structure | Neutral (-0.17) | Neutral (+0.33) | +0.50 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Entry barriers are high and rising — multi-year BTG lead times now locked up, land banks, coal linkages under SHAKTI and balance-sheet capacity — but tariffs are still set by competitive reverse bidding rather than by pricing rationality. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±6.2 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Barriers are genuinely high — capital intensity, land, coal linkage under SHAKTI, and a constrained BTG supply chain that APL pre-empted by ordering 24 GW in advance — and few new thermal entrants exist, but tariffs are set by competitive reverse bidding rather than rational list pricing.
- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Poor → Weak. Now: There is no image- or taste-driven pricing power — realizations move with merchant clearing prices and imported coal indices, and revenue fell year-on-year in Q3 FY26 purely on rate weakness. | The long view had said: There is no image- or taste-driven pricing power in a commodity kilowatt-hour; realizations move entirely with market clearing prices and imported coal indices, with merchant tariffs falling from INR 6.86 to INR 4.54/unit purely on demand.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: FY26 growth was purely volume/acquisition-led against falling tariffs, and although Q1 FY27 added 8% PPA and 13% merchant realization gains, the 27% revenue jump was still dominated by 17% volume growth. | The long view had said: Growth is overwhelmingly volume- and capacity-led while realizations fell — merchant 9M realization dropped from INR 6.16 to INR 5.44/kWh and revenue declined YoY in FY26 despite higher volumes; the mix shift to PPAs is deliberately trading price upside for stability.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: The FY26 quarters were a demonstrable cyclical trough (early monsoon, PLF down to 62.6%, collapsed merchant prices) and the very next quarter delivered record 271 GW peak demand, 78% PLF and firmer merchant tariffs — a trough passed with the upturn visible. | The long view had said: FY26 was a demand trough (flat 392 BU, weak merchant prices) followed by a sharp Q1 FY27 upturn to record 271 GW peak demand and 8.4% consumption growth, with an identifiable thermal-shortage cycle ahead.
- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Poor → Weak. Now: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹0.38 of sales — a fairly asset-heavy business. And from the calls: Thermal generation is structurally asset-heavy — ~INR18,000 crore quarterly revenue against an 18 GW fleet plus a 24 GW build program and INR45,331 crore of debt — so despite contractual protection this is the opposite of asset-light. | The long view had said: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹0.38 of sales — a fairly asset-heavy business. And from the calls: This is an extremely asset-heavy business — ~INR 58,381 crore of debt and a >INR 2 lakh crore capex program to add 24 GW, with revenue of roughly INR 17,936 crore a quarter on a vast fixed-asset base.
- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: India's baseload requirement is in secular expansion with the national thermal addition target raised from 80 GW to 95 GW, and Q1 FY27 consumption grew 8.4% year-on-year. | The long view had said: India's baseload power demand is in secular expansion with states showing structural deficits over 4-6 years, though thermal specifically faces long-term renewable substitution which already compressed merchant prices in FY26.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Good → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹37 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹37 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±6.2 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Large-scale private thermal is concentrated among few credible players, but capacity is allocated through competitive state bidding where the DISCOM customer sets terms and retains real negotiating power. | The long view had said: The customer side is a monopsony of state DISCOMs procuring through competitive reverse bidding, and regulators can and do block signed awards, so the end customer holds substantial negotiating power against a fragmented supplier base including NTPC and state gencos.
- **Network effects** (Competitive Advantage): Poor → Weak. Now: No two-sided or self-reinforcing network exists in merchant/PPA thermal generation, and none is claimed anywhere in the calls. | The long view had said: Commodity power generation sold into a merit-order grid and bilateral PPAs has no two-sided network; nothing in any of the 11 calls suggests scale makes the offering more valuable to each customer.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Counter-cyclical thermal investment funded substantially from internal accruals with AA rating maintained and finance costs contained, but discipline is qualified by rising debt on bridge financing and a steady stream of acquisitions. | The long view had said: Mixed record: they deleveraged sharply in FY24 (INR 42,252 to 32,214 crore) and invest counter-cyclically in thermal when others will not, but have since re-levered to INR 58,381 crore on bridge financing, pursued near-continuous M&A including a related-party purchase, and added a QIP.
- **Candor** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Adverse facts are disclosed plainly — weather-driven demand weakness, PLF decline, lower realizations, acquisition-inflated opex, the Bangladesh receivable overhang and the size of prior-period items — though framed inside consistently promotional language and with no ownership of misjudgements. | The long view had said: They disclose granular data willingly when pressed (plant-level open capacity, Godda revenue/EBITDA, merchant tariffs, coal indices), but framing is promotional — an analyst had to challenge the 'robust' characterisation of a quarter where revenue, EBITDA and PBT all declined, and 'recurring/continuing' definitions shift around large one-time items.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: Deal flow is real and Vidarbha was turned around fast, but acquirees are distressed/insolvent rather than already-strong, the JPA deal adds complex minority stakes, and post-deal opex and depreciation rose rather than margins improving. | The long view had said: A long, repeatable record of buying distressed thermal assets cheaply and turning them around (Mahan, Coastal Energen/Moxie, Lanco Korba, Dahanu, Vidarbha/Butibori, Jaiprakash) with demonstrated post-deal improvement, but these are stressed rather than already-strong acquirees, are frequent and large, and include a related-party purchase from Adani Enterprises.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Good → Neutral. Now: The company generates rather than distributes — offtake reaches customers via the grid — though pan-India plant locations and fuel logistics give some reach advantage. | The long view had said: Not a distribution business, but there is a genuine hard-to-replicate supply chain — captive coal mines adjacent to plants, group port and fuel logistics, and in-house project execution — repeatedly cited as the source of cost advantage.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: In-house project management capability and an experienced execution team are repeatedly cited as an advantage, but no systematic talent development or succession pipeline is described. | The long view had said: Some evidence of systematic capability building — a dedicated in-house training institute, safety program, and repeated emphasis on in-house execution and O&M expertise — but no discussion of succession or a leadership pipeline, and the CFO changed mid-period without explanation.

*Unchanged: Mini-monopolies; Return on capital (headline); Convenience benefits; Geographical expansion; Technology advantage; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Gain in market share; Cost of working capital; Assurance benefits; Share donators; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Security by obscurity; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Halo effect (caution); Out of limelight.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

**That strengthened:**

Why now: Explicit, repeated and quantified share capture across all four calls: 9 GW won out of 14.5 GW of state bids awarded, capacity path 18 GW to 42/45 GW, 56% of upcoming capacity pre-contracted, and volumes up 17% in Q1 FY27 — and the share is being taken with capacity charges management calls better than legacy PPAs, not with underpriced bids. (The long view had said: Persistent, funded capacity gain across the whole 2023-2026 span: ~15 GW to 17.55 GW to 18.15 GW operating, plus turnarounds of acquired stressed assets (Mahan, Lanco/Korba, Coastal Energen, Dahanu, Butibori, Jaiprakash) and a 24 GW ordered pipeline toward 45 GW, at a time when little competing private thermal is being built — share gained on won PPAs rather than on loosened terms.)

**That weakened:**

- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Revenue comes from competitive reverse-auction bids to state DISCOMs and merchant/exchange sales, with no professional intermediary recommending the product and no channel training or relationship programme mentioned in any of the four calls. - **Forward Integrators**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Movement is upstream and lateral — coal linkages under SHAKTI, fuel logistics, in-house project execution, acquisitions of distressed generation assets — with no own-retail, franchising or direct end-customer channel; all sales route through DISCOMs and exchanges. (The long view had said: the calls are silent and the numbers are mixed (1 of 2 checks supporting).)

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The Bangladesh PDB relationship was material enough to be flagged as 'an issue of concern' with >US$500 mn of arrears cleared in Q1 FY26 and payment status re-reported in later calls; offsetting this, the customer base is broadening across many state DISCOMs and the BTG supply chain is pre-ordered rather than spot-dependent. (The long view had said: The Godda plant sells 100% of output to a single foreign sovereign buyer, BPDB, and contributed INR 1,092 crore of the INR 4,636 crore Q3FY26 continuing EBITDA (~24%); analysts probe the Bangladesh PPA and its receivables in essentially every call, with USD 400 million still outstanding as of June 2026. Supplier concentration is also visible in the single BTG order for the entire 24 GW expansion.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Across the four calls earnings swung with weather and commodity prices — PLF fell from 72.3% (H1 FY25) to 64.8% (H1 FY26) and PAT dropped to Rs.2,488 cr in Q3 FY26 on 'sharply' lower day-ahead prices and lower import coal-linked tariffs, then rebounded to a record Rs.4,867 cr (+47%) in Q1 FY27 on a heatwave; meanwhile the company is committing to a 18 GW → 45 GW build with 24 GW of BTG ordered in advance, the classic expansionary over-investment posture at a demand upswing. (The long view had said: Across FY24-FY27 the calls show classic cycle behaviour: merchant realisation fell from INR 6.86/unit (Q3FY24) to INR 4.54 and INR 4.37, PLF swung with weather (68.6% → 63.9% → 62.6% → 78%), and 9M FY26 revenue and EBITDA both declined year-on-year before Q1FY27 rebounded 27%/22% on a record heatwave — while the company commits over INR 2 lakh crore of capex for 24 GW at the top of a demand upcycle. Exposure is bounded because electricity is a flow/operations-linked product and management has deliberately shrunk merchant exposure from 20% to 10% of capacity, targeting 3-4%.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
