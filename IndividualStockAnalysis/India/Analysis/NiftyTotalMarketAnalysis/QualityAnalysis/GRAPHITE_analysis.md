### The story in depth

Graphite India comes out as Weak, scoring 28 out of 100 — built from 16 for business quality, 0 for multibagger fit, and 84 for risk safety. The one clear strength is what customers pay for the product: after direct input costs, the company keeps ₹53 of every ₹100 of sales, which the quality framework reads as real pricing power and scores as its only positive check (Customer Benefits, +1). Almost everything else in the framework scores negative. Capital Allocation, Growth and Industry Structure each sit at -2, and Return on Capital averages about -1.7. Two areas — Competitive Advantage and Management — could not be scored at all, so the quality picture is not just poor, it is also incomplete.

The same trait that produces the one strength also produces the biggest weakness, and this is the core of the story. That ₹53 margin after input costs is what makes the Pricing Power pattern come back as a PARTIAL rather than a flat no, and it sits behind the PARTIAL readings on Brand Strength and Innovation Dominance too. But operating profit is only ₹7 of every ₹100 of sales, and margins move by about ±21.1 points a year. That swing is what drags down both the Profit margin check and the Barriers to entry and rationality check to -2, and it is the same behaviour the risk review picked up as a Cyclicality flag, with sales growth of roughly 5% a year that arrives lumpily rather than steadily. In short, the company can charge well for what it sells, but it cannot hold onto that advantage year after year, so the pricing strength never converts into a repeatable pattern — the multibagger screen scored it 0, with six of the eleven patterns marked NO FIT outright.

The cash side compounds this. The business is asset-heavy — every ₹1 of assets produces only ₹0.38 of sales — and about 327 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. Together those tie up a large amount of capital, and the result shows up in the headline return: ₹100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings generated just ₹1 of operating cash this year, a 1% cash return. Against that, the risk score of 84 and the SOUND resilience reading look reassuring, but they should be read carefully. All four risks raised — Cyclicality, Technological Innovation, Fashion Risk and Good Enough Goods — are QUANT FLAGs, meaning the numbers show a fingerprint but management commentary offered nothing either way. The same missing commentary is why three patterns stalled at PARTIAL and why Competitive Advantage and Management went unscored. So the high risk-safety number reflects an absence of confirmed problems, not evidence of safety, and the 0 on multibagger fit reflects an absence of confirmed patterns, not a proven lack of them.
