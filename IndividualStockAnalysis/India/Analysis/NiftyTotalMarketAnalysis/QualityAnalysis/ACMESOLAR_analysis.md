# ACME Solar Hold. (ACMESOLAR) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

ACME Solar Holdings is an Indian renewable-power producer that builds and then owns large solar, wind, hybrid/FDRE (firm and dispatchable renewable energy) and battery-storage plants, and sells the electricity they generate. Almost all of its output goes to government-linked bulk buyers such as SECI, NHPC and REMC (which buys for Indian Railways) plus state utilities, under 25-year power purchase agreements at a fixed tariff per unit — about 86% of the portfolio was with central off-takers as of Q1 FY26, which management says makes revenue and cash flows highly predictable. It is 'fully integrated': it designs, engineers and constructs its own plants in-house (no third-party EPC work for others) and then runs them for 25 years, which management says lets it control cost and timelines and keep the whole value-chain margin. Its plants are concentrated in high-sunlight states, especially Rajasthan (2,250 MW of the operating fleet in Q1 FY26) plus Gujarat; FY26 revenue was INR 2,507 crores with EBITDA margin above 90% and PAT of INR 498 crores, with roughly 2,990 MW of generation capacity operating and a total portfolio of 8,071 MW.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Independent power producer (IPP) portfolio — long-term PPA generation** — The core business: owning and operating utility-scale plants and selling power under 25-year contracts, standing at 2,990 MW operational at end-FY26 and generating 6,464 million units in FY26, with a project-level EBITDA yield on capex of roughly 14–14.5%.
- **Solar** — Historically 100% of the fleet and still the bulk of it, including one of India's largest plants at 1,800 MW-peak energised in October 2024; solar is preferred because its output varies less than other renewables, which supports cheap long-term borrowing.
- **Wind** — A newer and much smaller addition used mainly inside hybrid projects — the first 50 MW wind project was commissioned in Q1 FY26 and a further 72 MW in Q3 FY26; management deliberately keeps the wind share low and the solar share high for revenue predictability.
- **FDRE / hybrid / round-the-clock (RTC) projects** — Combined solar-plus-wind-plus-battery plants that promise buyers firm, dispatchable power including at peak hours — the fastest-growing part of the order book (e.g. 250 MW FDRE with NHPC at Rs 4.33/unit, 301 MW peak-power FDRE with SECI, 130 MW RTC with REMC at Rs 4.35/unit requiring 75–85% annual availability), targeting mid-teens returns.
- **Battery energy storage (BESS) — contracted and merchant** — Batteries built both alongside generation projects and as standalone contracts (first standalone win: 550 MWh with NHPC); about 2.3 GWh was commissioned by May 2026 and is currently sold on merchant and short-term terms — buying cheap off-peak power and selling at peak — earning roughly INR 2.2 crores a day at 88–90% round-trip efficiency, with the total portfolio requiring around 17 GWh of batteries and about 10 GWh targeted to be operating.
- **In-house EPC and O&M (the 'integrated' model)** — Not a separate revenue line — the company does its own land acquisition, grid connectivity, design, construction and 25-year operation rather than hiring outside contractors or serving third parties, which management credits for its cost control, high plant/grid availability (over 99%) and CUF of roughly 24–28%.
- **Under-construction pipeline and project financing** — A 5.1 GW under-construction book (3,280 MW of it with PPAs already signed) with INR 12,475 crores of capex committed, full grid connectivity secured, and debt raised and repeatedly refinanced at falling rates — weighted average cost of operational debt down to about 8.4%, with net operational debt/EBITDA held at 4.2x.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*
