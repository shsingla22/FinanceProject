# CORONA Remedies (CORONA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

CORONA Remedies is an India-focused branded pharmaceutical company, set up in 2004 and headquartered in Ahmedabad, that develops, manufactures and sells finished medicines (formulations) — tablets, capsules, sachets, bottles, topical sprays, ointments and injections — under its own brand names such as B29, Myoril, Tricium, Cortel and Obimet. It earns money by getting specialist and super-specialist doctors to prescribe these brands and selling the products through a pan-India network of distributors and medical representatives, with a deliberate focus on the 'middle of the pyramid' in metro, semi-metro, urban and semi-urban markets; roughly 70-72% of sales come from chronic and semi-chronic (long-term treatment) therapies, which management says gives repeat, sticky volume. About 96% of revenue comes from India, with only a small export/B2B business; FY26 revenue was INR 1,403 crore (up 17.3%) with EBITDA of INR 293 crore (20.9% margin) and adjusted PAT of INR 199 crore. It runs two manufacturing plants (Gujarat and Solan, Himachal Pradesh) with in-house R&D, is net cash surplus, and grows both organically and by buying or in-licensing brands from large multinationals.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Women's healthcare** — Sells brands covering the full female life cycle — menarche to menopause, pregnancy, post-pregnancy and pre/post-menopause — and is described as one of the four key therapy areas, now reinforced by a dedicated IVF/infertility taskforce set up in Q4 FY26 (its 'fourth focus theme') to exploit the acquired Bayer-Zydus infertility brands.
- **Cardio-Diabeto (cardio-metabolic)** — Brands spanning insulin resistance and pre-diabetes through diabetes and its complications, plus cardiac conditions like hypertension, dyslipidemia and ischemic heart disease — a core chronic therapy area (Obimet is named among its prominent brands).
- **Pain management** — A diversified range across dosage forms — oral tablets and capsules, topical sprays, ointments and injections — for musculoskeletal spasm and neuropathic pain; one of the four key therapy areas, with Myoril among the flagship brands.
- **Urology** — Products for benign prostatic hyperplasia, overactive bladder, urinary tract infection and stone management, the fourth of the key therapy areas and one where the company also sells in-licensed brands from Ferring.
- **Other therapies (VMN, gastrointestinal, respiratory)** — A supporting basket of vitamins/minerals/nutrition, gastro and respiratory products that rounds out the portfolio; management notes CORONA today addresses only about one-third of the Indian pharma market and wants to add areas like infertility, nephrology, CNS, oncology and dermatology.
- **Acquired brands and in-licensing** — A deliberate growth engine — seven brand trademarks bought from Bayer Zydus in July 2025 (Noklot and Noklot CV in the 1,500-crore-plus anti-platelet market, infertility brands Fostine, Menodac and Ovidac, and women's-health brands), the Wokadine povidone-iodine brand (No. 2 in a 630-crore-plus market) acquired in FY26, and in-licensed brands from Swiss firm Ferring; management guides to 25% growth from acquired brands in FY27.
- **Biosimilars and biologics** — A newly entered area, with three biosimilar launches in FY26 (Fostine R, Tricium DnaB, Wyntide, Tyvenza named), positioned as a long-term diversification platform rather than a material earner today.
- **Manufacturing and backward integration** — Two WHO-GMP plants (the Gujarat one also EU-GMP and now EAEU-GMP certified) with total capacity of about 1.65 billion tablets/capsules, 20 million sachets and 10 million bottles, two DSIR-approved R&D centres, a new 600-kg line and a dedicated hormone plant due in Q1-Q2 FY27, plus an investment in La Chandra Pharma Lab which supplies hormonal APIs under a right-of-first-refusal arrangement.
- **International / export (B2B)** — The small remaining ~4% of revenue; EAEU GMP accreditation opens five Eurasian markets (Russia, Belarus, Kazakhstan, Armenia, Kyrgyzstan) worth roughly USD 25 billion on a B2B model, but management says dossier approvals take 1-2 years and the India:international mix will stay around 90:10 even after 3-5 years.

### The story in depth

CORONA Remedies comes out of the three reviews as a high-return, self-funded branded pharmaceutical business, rated Outstanding at 84 out of 100 (45% weight on business quality at 79, 30% on multibagger pattern fit at a full 100, and 25% on risk safety at 72), with overall resilience judged sound. The financial engine is the clearest strength: every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.16 of sales, return on capital employed runs 41-49% with return on equity around 29-31%, net working capital is just 23 days, and the balance sheet is net cash — the ₹143 crore that looks like borrowing is an overdraft drawn against the company's own fixed deposits. That surplus pays for growth directly: a 400 million tablet and capsule line commissioned in December 2025, a 600-kg line adding about 40% capacity, a dedicated hormone plant due in the first or second quarter of FY27 and another planned for FY28-29, all funded internally. Sales have compounded around 17% a year with unusual year-to-year consistency, and the company has delivered 15%-plus revenue and 20%-plus profit growth every year across a 21-year build, most recently beating its own January 2026 promise by May 2026 with 17.3% revenue growth.

The pattern screen scores a perfect fit largely because the same handful of traits show up again and again. The business is prescription-led — 2,600-plus medical representatives calling on specialists and super-specialists, key-opinion-leader work taking 10% of a 19% advertising and promotion budget, and a 46-person team built to cover 3,000 IVF centres — which is exactly the 'friendly middleman' setup where the doctor, not the patient, chooses. That same field force, running on a productivity curve that rises from ₹2.5 lakh per representative per month in the first three years to ₹8 lakh by years six to nine, is what powers the market-share pattern: rank in the Indian pharmaceutical market moved from 30 to 28 to 27 across three calls, growth ran at roughly twice the industry on volume and new launches rather than price alone, and covered-market share is still only about 1.9%. Pricing power and brand strength are two sides of one fact — 92-93% of the portfolio sits outside the government's essential-medicines price list and carries a 10% annual increase allowance, which holds gross margin at 81.4% across 70-plus brands, two above ₹100 crore and eight in the ₹50-100 crore range, many of them tired brands bought cheaply from GSK, Abbott, Sanofi, Bayer-Zydus and Wockhardt and then rebuilt. Where the fit thins is telling: innovation dominance is only a likely fit because research spending is capped below 2% of sales with around 100 scientists, an analyst flagged this as below peers, and 'cost to replicate' is only partial because the barriers described are time and relationships rather than anything structural — which lines up with the quality framework's weakest areas, competitive advantage at 0.5 and industry structure at 0.67 out of 2.

The risk review picks up those same traits from the other side. The single high risk, 'good enough goods', is the direct cost of the friendly-middleman model: the molecules are largely simple and off-patent — a vitamin-mineral in the largest brand B29, plus metformin, thiocolchicoside, povidone iodine and clopidogrel — so what is being sold is the prescriber relationship rather than a hard product edge, and the partial protection cited is differentiated line extensions, specialist positioning and the La Chandra hormonal-API backward integration rather than patent protection. The same is true of pricing: the 7.8% price growth that supports the pricing-power pattern rests entirely on the non-essential-list 10% ceiling, so any expansion of the government's price-controlled list would hit it directly — essential-list products are capped this year to a wholesale-price revision of under 1%, and marketing conduct is bound by the uniform code. The technology watch item sits against the innovation pattern: the oral anti-diabetic franchise including Obimet faces the GLP-1 shift as semaglutide comes off patent in March 2026, and the response is the company's own injectable Wyntide plus three biosimilar and biologic launches in FY26. The remaining five channels — cyclicality, stakeholder concentration, new entrants, shifting preferences and fashion — all rate low, resting on the same base: 70-72% of revenue from chronic and semi-chronic therapies with repeat-prescription demand, the largest brand at only about 11% of revenue, no government contracts or tenders, and a net cash position that funds capacity without leverage.

## What to watch

- Pricing power vs. government dependency: any expansion of the essential-medicines price list into the 92-93% non-controlled book would remove the 10% annual price lever that underpins the 7.8% price growth and the 81.4% gross margin.
- Technological innovation watch: whether Wyntide and the three biosimilar/biologic launches actually hold the cardio-metabolic franchise, including Obimet, once semaglutide comes off patent in March 2026.
- Good enough goods (the one high risk): erosion in a portfolio built on simple off-patent molecules would show up first as volume slipping back toward industry growth rather than the ~2x pace, or as the covered-market share stalling near 1.9%.
- Innovation dominance is only a likely fit while research spending stays capped below 2% of sales — an analyst already flagged this as below peers; new introductions contributing 6.4% of growth against the industry's 2.5% is the number that has to hold.
- Disciplined stewardship and capital allocation: the record rests on internally funded capacity and small brand bolt-ons; a large debt-funded acquisition, or slippage in the hormone plant due in the first or second quarter of FY27 and the further plant planned for FY28-29, would break that pattern.
