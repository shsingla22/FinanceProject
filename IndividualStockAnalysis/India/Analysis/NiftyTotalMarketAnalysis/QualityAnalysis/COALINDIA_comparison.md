### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 2 worsened, 2 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Roughly four-fifths of volume goes to a single customer group — power utilities (610 mt of a 780 mt target) — under government-mediated supply obligations, and evacuation depends on a single monopoly logistics supplier, Indian Railways, where management admits a two-year supply backlog in some areas. (The long view had said: Supplies have been 80-82% skewed to the power sector, largely state gencos and DISCOMs, with receivables jumping from Rs.11,000 crore to Rs.14,000 crore in a single quarter and management admitting it must keep supplying stressed SEBs to avoid power cuts. Dependence on Indian Railways for evacuation is a second concentration — rake availability, not demand, repeatedly caps loading.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: CIL is itself the low-cost option rather than the premium product under attack: the cheaper-substitute channel (imported coal used for 1–10% blending) has been displaced back to CIL, and demand exceeds supply at CCL and SECL even after a royalty-driven price increase. There is no evidence in the calls of management defending a price premium against a cheaper equivalent. (The long view had said: Coal India sells an undifferentiated, high-ash (up to 42%) commodity where buyers substitute freely on landed cost — imported coal, captive coal exempt from the compensation cess, and other domestic miners; management notes consumers 'are becoming choosy' as booking levels fell to 63%, and admits washed non-coking coal has no buyers willing to pay for the upgrade. There is no brand premium to defend, but equally no niche or scale protection for pricing.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Across the window, profitability visibly rides the coal price cycle: FY23 profits were boosted by 250–300% e-auction premiums when import prices spiked, and by May 2025 management concedes premiums have normalised to ~43% (Q4) from 55/50/46% in the preceding quarters, with management itself labelling this demand 'seasonal or cyclical'. Volumes also swing on rainfall, land and clearance issues (SECL, CCL), so both realisation and output are outside management's control. (The long view had said: Coal India is a pure commodity producer whose realisations swing violently with the coal cycle: e-auction premiums went from 250-300% in FY22-23 to ~117% in Q3FY24 to 43% by Q4FY25, and management explicitly links premiums to imported coal prices and to seasonal/cyclical demand. Volume and profit growth in FY23-24 came off an abnormal energy-shortage peak that management itself later described as exceptional.)
- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Commercial and captive mining — a structurally new competitor set created by policy — is scaling from ~198 mt to a projected 320 mt, and management states plainly this comes directly out of CIL's share; it is already scouting ~35 mt of additional long-term commitments to backfill. Imports of 190 mt+ remain a parallel alternative supply. (The long view had said: Captive and commercial miners are a structurally different entrant class — they pay no coal compensation cess on self-consumed coal and were auctioned explicitly to take share. Management states captive/commercial output reached 198 mt and is projected at 320 mt by FY30, conceding it will 'eat into Coal India's share', while e-auction booking rates fell from ~100% in FY22 to 63% as buyers gained alternatives.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Fashion Risk (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
