### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 3 weakened, 5 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Q4 FY26 cites integrating the Brunton Shaw brand with upgraded Ranchi capability to win global OEM work, and Q3 FY26 says the Ocean Fiber brand is now established after fast traction; but no A&P spend, heritage narrative or distributor leverage is quantified in these calls. (The long view had said: The Brunton Shaw UK brand is used as a premium wrapper for Ranchi-made ropes and management says this integrated model lifted share with premium OEMs, and 'Ocean Fibre' has been established as a new brand; but there is no meaningful A&P spend, heritage narrative or consumer-style distributor leverage discussed.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Named new platforms recur across FY26 — Oceanfibre synthetic slings, GALSTAR wires, plasticated LRPC — with R&D teams co-developing customised solutions with customers; however no R&D spend figure, share-of-industry-R&D or pipeline economics is disclosed in any of the four calls. (The long view had said: There is a steady cadence of named new products with revenue impact — GALSTAR coated wire, plasticated LRPC, Ocean Fibre synthetic slings (cash positive in year one) — plus a Global Development Centre R&D setup and R&D teams co-developing with customers; but R&D spend is never disclosed or quantified and gross margins fund capex more than research.)
- **Low Price Plus**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The cost culture is real and itemised across all four calls — 'One Usha Martin' cut fixed costs >10% vs FY25, employee cost from Rs.118.6cr to Rs.113.2cr per quarter, admin expenses -7% in FY26 — but the savings are retained as margin (19.1% FY26 vs 17.2%) rather than passed to price; management explicitly declined general-purpose volume to protect mix, which is the opposite of the low-price model. (The long view had said: There is real cost obsession under 'One Usha Martin' (fixed employee costs -3%, admin -7% in FY26), but it is deployed to widen margins on premium products, not to pass savings to price; management deliberately walks away from low-price general-purpose ropes rather than competing on cost.)

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Q4 FY26 shows cost pass-through and mix-led realisation gains (EBITDA/t from ~Rs.28,500 in Q1 to ~Rs.39,500 in Q4) rather than demonstrated above-inflation price rises; and LRPC — declining 3%, 26%, 13% and 20% year-on-year through the year — shows clear weakness at the commodity end. (The long view had said: Realisations rose even when steel prices fell (Nov 2023), and in FY26 rising steel, gas and logistics costs were passed through across wire, LRPC and rope while margins still expanded — EBITDA/ton went from ~INR 31,178 (Q2 FY24) to ~INR 39,500 (Q4 FY26) and margin guidance was raised from 18-19% to a 20% minimum.)
- **Toll Roads**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Q3 and Q4 FY26 describe value-added ropes for elevators, cranes, mining and oil/offshore as engineering-led rather than price-led, with long approval cycles and field-performance references acting as switching costs; but management never quantifies rope as a tiny share of customer cost, and no certification/standards or oligopoly language appears. (The long view had said: Across all 12 calls the value-added rope business is described as safety-critical, engineering-specified and approval-gated, with long qualification cycles and low price sensitivity once converted — plasticated LRPC needs approvals from 'two or three big players', mining ropes converted trials into a three-year contract, and Q3 FY26 explicitly calls the demand 'less price led'.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Sales are described as direct to OEMs and end users — direct European shipments, R&D co-development with end customers, own Saudi rigging operation — not through prescribing professionals or an installer channel, and no trade-training programmes are mentioned in any FY26 call. (The long view had said: Demand is largely specified through OEMs and channel partners rather than end-user marketing — OEM approvals in elevators and cranes, European OEMs generating orders for ropes used worldwide — but there is no professional training programme or trade-channel investment of the classic dentist/installer type.)

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Demand is tied to steel rod input prices and to customer project/capex cycles — LRPC fell 3.4%, 26%, 13% and 20.4% across the four quarters on infrastructure and monsoon-driven activity, and Middle East volumes fell on project delays — but the core rope business behaves like a replacement/operations consumable across elevators, cranes and mining, and FY26 margins actually expanded to 19.1% with net cash of INR 332 crore. (The long view had said: Demand is tied to steel input prices and to end-markets that swing with capex (mining, oil & offshore, ports, cranes, infrastructure/LRPC), and the calls repeatedly explain margins by steel/rod/gas price movement, LRPC mix and project timing (Middle East conflict cost ~900t of rope volume in Q4 FY26); however ropes are largely replacement/operations consumables, the group is diversified across products, end-industries and geographies (international 57%), and it has invested through the cycle from a net cash position.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The commodity end of the portfolio is visibly under price pressure — LRPC declined every quarter of FY26 and management chose to forgo general-purpose rope volumes rather than compete for them, an admission that the low-spec product is contestable; the defence is to migrate mix upward into engineering-qualified ropes. (The long view had said: The general-purpose rope and black LRPC lines are commodity products where cheaper substitutes compete directly and realisations are unattractive, and management has been steadily walking away from that tonnage; the protection is that the value-added ropes carry genuine engineering and approval barriers rather than brand alone, so the premium is defended by qualification cycles, not marketing.)
- **Fashion Risk**: QUANT FLAG → LOW. Why now: Demand is functional and repeat-purchase — engineering-qualified ropes for elevators, cranes, mining and offshore where customers run long qualification cycles and then reorder, with no growth attributed to any trend or brand moment. (The long view had said: 1 numeric fingerprint is present but the calls offer nothing either way — numbers alone are only ever a flag to investigate, never a verdict.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
