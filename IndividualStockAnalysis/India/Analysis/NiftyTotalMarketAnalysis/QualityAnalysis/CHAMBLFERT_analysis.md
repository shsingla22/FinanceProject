# Chambal Fertilizers & Chemicals Ltd. (CHAMBLFERT) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FERTILISERS & PESTICIDES*

Chambal Fertilisers and Chemicals is an Indian company that makes and sells farm inputs, chiefly fertilizer, to farmers across India through a dealer network. Its core business is urea, which it manufactures at its own plants at Gadepan in Kota district, Rajasthan (Gadepan-I, II and III); management calls itself India's largest private-sector urea producer, supplying close to 10% of the country's urea. Alongside its own urea it buys in and sells other bulk fertilizers — DAP, NPK and MOP (roughly 5%, 5% and 6% national share) — and a growing, higher-margin range of crop protection chemicals, specialty nutrients, biological products and, more recently, seeds. Because fertilizer prices to farmers are government-controlled, a large part of its revenue comes as subsidy from the Government of India (about INR 10,228 crore received in the first nine months of FY26), and delays in those payments swing its working capital and interest costs; it also earns from a phosphoric acid joint venture, IMACID, and is building a Technical Ammonium Nitrate plant.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Urea** — Manufactures urea at its Gadepan plants in Rajasthan using imported gas, running about 9.23 lakh tonnes a quarter and contributing close to 10% of India's urea supply; management describes urea as what 'anchors volumes and cash flows', and it is sold at a government-set price with the balance recovered as subsidy.
- **P&K / bulk fertilizers (DAP, NPK, MOP)** — Sources phosphatic and potassic fertilizers — largely imported — and sells them to Indian farmers under a 'portfolio approach' tuned to each region; it is a big revenue contributor and the more volatile one, since import prices, the rupee and NBS subsidy rates can produce one-time hits such as the loss taken in the September 2022 quarter.
- **Crop Protection Chemicals and Speciality Nutrients (CPC-SN)** — Sells pesticides, weedicides, fungicides, insecticides and specialty plant nutrients aimed at high-value crops like fruits and vegetables; smaller than fertilizer at about 3% of the national market, but the main growth and margin engine, with contribution up roughly 27-30% year-on-year in FY26 and 17 new products launched that year.
- **Biologicals** — A sub-set of the CPC-SN push selling bio-based inputs such as UTTAM PRANAAM (bio-nano phosphorus, developed with TERI) and UTTAM SUPERRHIZA; small but fast-growing — volumes up about 30% and revenue about 57% in FY26, roughly 6% of that market, with the two lead products covering close to 3 million acres since launch.
- **Seeds** — A newly entered line in hybrid and research-variety seeds — wheat, maize, mustard and bajra — described as completing the agri-input product profile; still at the early, portfolio-building stage with no size given.
- **Technical Ammonium Nitrate (TAN) project** — A new plant being built to convert the company's surplus ammonia into technical ammonium nitrate, with total estimated cost of INR 1,645 crore (INR 1,184 crore spent by December 2025) and completion scheduled for April 2026; management calls it a very important focus area and is already exploring further expansion.
- **Surplus ammonia** — Ammonia made in-house beyond what the urea plants consume — in the range of 90,000 tonnes a year as of 2022 — sold on or, going forward, fed into the TAN plant; management claims to be the country's most efficient ammonia complex.
- **IMACID (joint venture)** — A joint venture producing phosphoric acid and sulphuric acid, most of its output directed to the Indian market; it contributes to consolidated profit and is expanding P2O5 capacity from 5 to 7 lakh tonnes by December 2026.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Chambal comes out of these three reviews as a well-run company operating inside a business model it does not control. The management and customer-facing scores are the highest in the whole quality framework (management 0.83, customer benefits 0.75, capital allocation 0.6), and the individual checks explain why: the build-out has been entirely organic with no acquisitions, long-term loans were prepaid to leave the company debt-free in FY25 even while funding the ₹1,645 crore, 240,000 TPA technical ammonium nitrate plant, and investment continued right through the difficult FY23 subsidy squeeze. The farmer-facing effort is real and measurable — the Seed-to-Harvest programme with over 1,200 farmer meetings, 700 demonstrations and 43,000 soil samples, plus more than 100,000 downloads of the Krishak Mitra app — and it has visibly converted into growth in crop protection and biologicals. Research output is steady rather than showy: 38 new molecules over five years now contributing 25–30% of sales, 17 launches in FY26 and 12–14 more planned for FY27, alongside jointly owned intellectual property through the CFCL–TERI Centre of Excellence. Market position leaves room to grow (about 10% of urea, 5% of NPK/DAP, 3% of crop protection), and the newer verticals are actually taking share, with biologicals at roughly 6% of their market on volumes up 31% and revenue up 58%.

The trouble is that almost every one of these strengths stops short of becoming a durable advantage, which is why the multibagger screen returned eight 'partial' verdicts, three outright non-fits, and a fit score of only 10 out of 100. The same farmer-engagement machine that earns the advertising check its positive mark also sits behind the 'Friendly Middleman' and 'Brand Strength' patterns being only partial — the Uttam name is stretched across nano-phosphorus, SuperRhiza, seeds and crop protection, but nothing in fifteen calls shows it commanding a premium price, and the risk review notes that in a market where about 75% of crop-protection sales are generic, it is the dealer rather than the farmer who effectively picks the brand (the 'Good Enough Goods' watch item), with growth partly credited to placing stock early to occupy the shelf. Likewise, the genuine cost and energy discipline at Gadepan — described as the most efficient ammonia complex in India, with debottlenecking and energy schemes at each turnaround — supports the 'Low Price Plus' and 'Cost to Replicate' patterns, yet those savings accrue under a government formula rather than to shareholders as pricing power, and management itself points to well-funded newcomers rebuilding the same asset base under NIP-2012 ('Matix is there, HURL three plants are there'), which is exactly what makes 'New Entrants' a watch-level risk alongside record national output of 503 lakh MT and channel inventory at 51% of seasonal requirement against a normal 33%. The headline growth story runs into the same wall: complex fertiliser revenue rose 175% on volumes going from 5.64 to 12.31 lakh MT, but at roughly 4% operating margin with DAP explicitly a fixed-margin pass-through, so the pricing-mix-volume check scores negative, 'Pricing Power' and 'Toll Roads' register as no fit at all, and urea volumes were flat to lower (34.06 against 34.7 lakh MT).

Where all of this lands is the weakest number in the file: for every ₹100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings in the business, only about ₹1 of operating cash was generated this year — a 1% cash return that drags the return-on-capital area to −0.67 and, together with lumpy roughly 7% average sales growth, holds growth to 0.17 and industry structure to −0.17. The single risk rated HIGH is government dependency, and it is the common thread: urea prices and profitability are set under NIP-2012, NBS rates govern the rest, and the government is effectively the one paymaster behind the bulk of receivables (₹1,954 crore still outstanding at March 2026 against ₹12,276 crore received during FY26), with the FY23 DAP loss showing what happens when input and currency costs cannot be passed on. Cyclicality is rated elevated for the related reason that ammonia at $850–900 a tonne and sulphur at $900–950 a tonne in the fourth quarter of FY26 whipsaw margins the company cannot reprice. Against that, the company is on the right side of the slower-moving risks — shifting preferences, technology and fashion all score low, because demand is seasonal agronomic repeat buying across a 65-molecule portfolio, and the pivot toward biologicals and balanced nutrition is one Chambal is leading rather than resisting. The stated escape route — crop protection, biologicals, seeds at 23%-plus operating margins outside the subsidy system, plus the new TAN plant — is genuine but still small next to the fertiliser base. That combination of honest, disciplined management sitting on top of an administered, low-return business is what produces the overall 43 out of 100 and a Mixed, two-star grade, with resilience judged sound.

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, Cyclicality. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.1× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 67% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 10% to 24% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Nearly every call revolves around policy: urea MRP and profitability set under NIP-2012, NBS subsidy rates, gas allocation, and subsidy receivables (INR 1,954 crore outstanding at Mar-2026, INR 12,276 crore received in FY26). The FY23 one-time loss of ~INR 240 crore came directly from a government subsidy calculation, and management openly says it cannot forecast Gadepan-III economics after policy-period expiry because 'they have not yet started the exercise'.

> "we do not know what the government is actually going to do or what thought process they are going to follow" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Diversification into crop protection/biologicals/seeds (23%+ EBIT margins, non-subsidy) and the TAN project add non-policy revenue, but these remain small versus the subsidised fertiliser core.*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 2 numeric fingerprints reinforce it.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is operations-linked (farmers buy inputs every season; urea volumes flat at ~34 lakh MT), but input commodity swings repeatedly whipsaw margins — DAP down 20% from peak in FY23, ammonia to $850-900/t and sulphur $900-950/t in Q4 FY26, gas from $28 to $18.5/MMBtu, with Q4 FY26 EBITDA margin at 9.2% versus 13.9% a quarter earlier.

> "industry margins would remain under pressure due to high raw material costs and rupee depreciation" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow-product/consumable demand tied to sowing acreage, not customer capex; subsidy mechanism and fixed urea returns dampen the swing; countercyclical capex (TAN, IMACID capacity) continued through the volatile FY23-FY26 period.*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 4 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 7% and 19% (±3.7 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 32% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 4% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.
