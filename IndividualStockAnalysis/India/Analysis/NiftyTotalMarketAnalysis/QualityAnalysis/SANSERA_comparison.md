### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 3 weakened, 6 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Within the window management set a specific ADS target and delivered it (INR ~3,000 mn guided in Nov 2025, INR 3,155 mn product sales achieved in FY26), met the teens-growth guidance given in Feb 2026, disclosed bad news promptly (export declines, tariff margin hits, a INR 162 mn Labour Code charge, soft Q4 order booking, peak-revenue reset from INR 24.1 bn to INR 19.2 bn), stayed net debt free while funding INR 5,097 mn of capex, and handled leadership and board succession openly. (The long view had said: Leadership is stable (Preetham and CFO Vikas Goel through the whole period), bad news is disclosed promptly (the ~INR3,000cr North American contract withdrawal, the Sweden/Volvo lean patch, the deliberate pause in tech-agnostic orders), FY26 delivered the ADS guidance and improved ROCE to 18% with debt reduced; but the FY23 promise of 40% non-auto/tech-agnostic within three years was only ~30% by FY26 and the 20% EBITDA goal is still unmet.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Margin expansion through FY26 (17.2% to 19.3% EBITDA) is explicitly attributed to product mix and operating leverage, not to price increases; the only pricing gain cited is a negotiated improvement with the key Swedish customer, while tariffs and steel/aluminium/freight inflation are said to be pressuring margins. (The long view had said: Margin gains across FY23–FY26 come from product mix, operating leverage and capacity utilisation, never from price; commodity increases must be negotiated customer-by-customer and one export price rise was explicitly outside contract, and Q4 FY26 flags inflation in steel, aluminium, energy and freight as a pressure to absorb.)

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: QUANT SIGNAL → NO FIT. Why now: As a contract precision-engineering supplier there is no consumer brand, no A&P spend, no distributor leverage and no adjacency launches under a brand name anywhere in the FY26 calls; reputation is discussed only as an engineering track record with OEM buyers. (The long view had said: No consumer or heritage brand exists; beyond supplier awards from Toyota Industries there is no A&P spend, premium pricing or adjacency-under-one-name discussion in any call.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls there is no license, AMC, subscription or attach-rate revenue — the model is upfront sale of forged/machined components against open-ended OEM schedules, and Q4 FY26 shows working capital being funded by borrowings rather than by customer advances. (The long view had said: Across all four sampled calls (Aug 2022–May 2026) management discusses order books, peak annual revenues and lifetime backlogs — never licence fees, AMC/service revenue, attach rates, renewals or customer advances as a funding source.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Sales are direct B2B to OEMs (Collins Aerospace, a North American xEV maker, a US tractor OEM, Indian 2W/PV/CV OEMs) and to a semicon equipment customer; no prescriber, installer or trade channel intermediates the purchase, and no trade-training programmes are mentioned in any of the four calls. (The long view had said: Sansera sells direct to OEMs (Tata Motors, Force Motors, Volvo, Toyota Industries, Saab, Triumph); no intermediary professional, installer or prescriber channel is mentioned in any call.)

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Single relationships visibly swing quarters — the Swedish subsidiary's 80% jump was attributed to 'pricing and volume improvement with the key customer', the xEV segment stalled on 'lower offtake from one of our largest North American xEV consumers', and the fast-growing ADS backlog leans on named accounts such as Collins Aerospace and one semicon customer. (The long view had said: Single relationships repeatedly move the P&L: the Rs.3,000+ crore North American OEM contract was unilaterally withdrawn in 2022, Volvo's de-risking cut Sansera's share from 100% to 70% and drove a 20% degrowth in the Swedish subsidiary, a key 2-wheeler EV customer 'considerably reduced' business, and in FY26 the marquee North American EV customer was down 50% year-on-year and 60% below plan; semicon rests on 'one very active client'.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The only preference movement discussed is within the demand base — scooters down while entry-level and >200cc premium motorcycles thrive — and Sansera has pivoted its content to capture it rather than losing volume to it, with premium motorcycle components and higher content per vehicle cited as growth drivers. (The long view had said: The end-demand mix is visibly migrating — small-car demand weakening while SUVs grow, 2-wheeler premiumisation, and accelerating EV adoption in 2-wheelers — which changes which components are bought rather than eroding volumes outright, and management is actively repositioning content per vehicle toward premium motorcycles, SUVs and xEV parts.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Roughly two-thirds of Q4 FY26 revenue (INR 6,426mn of INR 9,987mn) is Auto ICE — forged/machined crankshafts and connecting rods — and management itself says EV adoption in 2-wheelers is accelerating, yet the FY26 capacity additions (new Pantnagar plant, ICE capability capex) deepen the legacy-powertrain installed base. (The long view had said: The core franchise is ICE powertrain forgings (connecting rods, crankshaft assemblies) that carry zero content in a battery vehicle, and the whole strategic narrative from FY23 to FY26 is an explicit race to de-risk into tech-agnostic, xEV and non-auto before the ICE base erodes; ICE was still ~68-70% of FY26 revenue and the 2022 cancellation of a Rs.3,000+ crore North American order was itself a 'new technology engine' programme being dropped.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW); Good Enough Goods (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
