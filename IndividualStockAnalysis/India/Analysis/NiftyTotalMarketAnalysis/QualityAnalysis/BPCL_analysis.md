# Bharat Petroleum Corporation Ltd. (BPCL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: ENERGY*

BPCL is an Indian state-run (PSU) oil company that buys crude oil from around the world, refines it at its own refineries (Mumbai, Kochi and Bina), and sells the resulting fuels — petrol (MS), diesel (HSD), jet fuel (ATF), cooking gas (LPG) and CNG — mainly to Indian motorists, households, airlines and industrial/commercial customers. It makes money two ways: a refining margin (the gap between what crude costs and what the products fetch, quoted as GRM per barrel, e.g. $11.74/bbl for FY26) and a marketing margin on the roughly 54 MMT of product it sold domestically in FY26 through a network of over 25,000 petrol pumps ('retail outlets'). Its economics swing with crude prices, the rupee, and government-influenced pump prices — notably on domestic LPG, where it has been selling below cost and carrying a large 'negative buffer' (about Rs 12,300 crore) pending government compensation. It operates almost entirely in India, with quarterly revenue in the range of Rs 1.2 lakh crore, plus a small overseas oil-and-gas exploration interest in Mozambique and Brazil.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Refining** — Three refineries process crude into fuels, running at 114–118% of nameplate capacity (41.15 MMT throughput in FY26, its highest ever) with a high distillate yield of about 84.5%, and management stresses flexible crude sourcing — Russian crude was 31% of the mix in Q4 FY26, alongside Brazil, US and West Africa grades.
- **Marketing (retail fuels)** — The petrol-pump business is the volume engine — 25,323 outlets at end-FY26, about 30% retail market share in both petrol and diesel, and the highest sales per outlet among PSU peers (143–153 kilolitres per month), with roughly 1,000–1,700 new outlets added a year.
- **LPG (cooking gas)** — Bottled domestic cooking gas sold to households at government-set prices below cost, which has been a major drag — losses of about Rs 2,100 crore in a single quarter in FY25 and a cumulative unrecovered 'negative buffer' of roughly Rs 12,300 crore, partly offset by government compensation received from November 2025.
- **Gas business (CGD / CNG, PNG, LNG)** — Piped and compressed natural gas sold in 25 licensed geographical areas won in bidding, with 2,650 CNG stations and FY26 gas sales of 2.29 MMT growing 26.5% year-on-year; management flagged a roughly Rs 48,000 crore investment commitment over the life of these licences.
- **Petrochemicals** — Plastics and chemical feedstock made from refinery streams — currently the PDPP unit at Kochi (about 80% utilisation, contributing around $0.79/bbl to margin in H1 FY25) — being scaled up substantially through the Rs 49,000 crore Bina petrochemical-plus-refinery-expansion project and a polypropylene unit at Kochi.
- **Pipelines and supply infrastructure** — Pipelines, depots, LPG bottling plants and aviation fuelling stations that move and store product — described as a distinct business alongside refining and marketing, with the 425-km Krishnapatnam–Hyderabad multiproduct pipeline commissioned in FY26.
- **Non-fuel retail** — Shops and services at petrol pumps — In & Out convenience stores, BeCafé cafés, Apna Ghar truckers' facilities and highway wayside amenities — a small but expanding add-on to the fuel network (from ~300 stores in 2023, with a target of 1,000 rural stores).
- **EV charging and future fuels** — Electric-vehicle charging points at outlets (6,823–7,402 stations), plus battery swapping and the first LNG fuelling stations, positioned as turning pumps into 'energy stations' rather than as a meaningful earnings contributor today.
- **Renewables, green hydrogen and biofuels** — Wind and solar projects (100 MW of wind awarded, 46 MW installed as of 2023), a green-hydrogen joint venture with Sembcorp called NeuEn Green Energy, compressed-biogas plants and ethanol plants — all tied to a net-zero-by-2040 Scope 1 and 2 pledge and to ethanol blending, which reached 19.6% of petrol in Q1 FY26.
- **Upstream (oil and gas exploration)** — Minority overseas exploration and production interests — a Mozambique LNG block, 42% complete with first cargo expected mid-2028, and the BM-SEAL-11 block in Brazil — a long-dated side business rather than a current earnings source.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Bharat Petroleum earns a Mixed rating of 44 out of 100 (two stars), built from 49 for business quality, 36 for multibagger fit and 44 for risk safety. The strengths are real and concentrated in how efficiently the company runs what it owns: every 100 rupees of capital in the business threw off 33 rupees of operating cash this year, and only about 14 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. It sells into a genuinely growing market — India's fuel demand is still expanding, and its gas and city-gas business is compounding quickly, with gas volumes up 26.5% and CNG up 62.1%. Return on capital is the one clearly positive area of the quality framework, and management scores mildly positive too. Almost every other area — competitive advantage, growth, customer benefits, capital allocation and industry structure — sits slightly below neutral.

The patterns explain where that cash return comes from, and why it may not compound. The one strong fit is forward integration: BPCL owns the chain from refinery to pump, growing from roughly 22,000 outlets in FY24 to 25,323 by FY26, plus 2,650 CNG stations, around 7,000 EV chargers and its own retail formats. A likely second fit is low-cost operating efficiency — refineries running at 114–118% of nameplate capacity, a distillate yield of about 84–85% described as among the highest in India, and best-in-class throughput per outlet. But the chain stops there. Pricing power and toll-road economics are explicit non-fits, because retail prices are set by crude movements and the government rather than by the company; after direct input costs only 17 rupees of every 100 of sales remains. Brand, innovation and market-share gain all land as partial: the assets and initiatives exist, but they never turn into a price the customer pays willingly.

The risks are largely the same traits viewed from the other side. The huge downstream retail network that makes forward integration a strong fit is exactly what makes government dependency the dominant risk — selling prices are effectively frozen, and LPG losses have piled up as a negative buffer of about 12,523 crore rupees in June 2025, still 12,319 crore in March 2026, awaiting a discretionary 30,000 crore payment. The scale efficiency behind the high return on capital also leaves earnings hostage to crude: refining margins fell from $12.64 a barrel to $4.41 and $4.88 before recovering to $11.74 for FY26 on inventory gains. The fast-growing CNG business is simultaneously a growth strength and the hedge against diesel demand quietly shifting away. Offsetting all this, resilience is sound: borrowings fell from 0.69 to 0.11 times equity to a net cash position, and disclosure is candid, including the 4,300 crore Brazil impairment. Capital allocation is the weak spot — a large dividend paid partly from borrowings and an 18,000 crore rights issue that dilutes rather than retires equity.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Government Dependency. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.5× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 100% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 22% to 25% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Earnings are driven almost entirely by crude prices, refining cracks and inventory swings: GRM collapsed from $12.64/bbl (Q1FY24) to $4.41 (Q2FY25) and $4.88 (Q1FY26) before recovering to $11.74 for FY26 on inventory gains, with quarterly PAT swinging from Rs 10,551 cr to Rs 2,397 cr to Rs 3,191 cr. Simultaneously the company is in a heavy expansionary capex cycle (Rs 1.4-1.5 lakh cr plan; capex rising Rs 16,000 cr → Rs 25,000 cr) with management conceding debt/equity will go to ~1 at peak in FY28-29, the classic over-investment-at-the-peak pattern.

> "Gasoline Singapore crack fell to $6.83 barrel in Q2 from $8.58 barrel in Q1... our refineries is recorded at GRM of $4.41 per barrel" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Marketing/retail is a flow business tied to daily fuel consumption (domestic sales +3.5% FY26, throughput/RO leadership at 143-153 KL/month), which partly buffers refining volatility; countercyclical build-out of Bina petchem and Andhra refinery continues through the downcycle.*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 4 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 2% and 10% (±2.4 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 4% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 5%).

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** This is the dominant risk: BPCL is a PSU whose retail selling prices are effectively frozen by the government, whose LPG losses accumulate as a 'negative buffer' (Rs 12,523 cr as of Jun-25, still Rs 12,319 cr at Mar-26) awaiting a discretionary Rs 30,000 cr budgetary grant, whose equity raise came from a government budgetary allocation, and whose CGD/GA business, ethanol blending and biofuel projects all exist because of policy mandates and bid rounds.

> "the Government has announced Rs. 30,000 crore compensation towards the under-recovery and sale of domestic LPG... We have not yet accounted anything against this particular compensation" — *management, on an earnings call*
