### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Regular named launches with client outcomes — ZenseAI agentic platform, Agentic Foundry, ZenseAI.Guidewire, FinOps for AI — plus Ignite AI Academy scaling to 85% of the workforce AI-certified. But gross margin is only ~31-34%, no R&D spend is disclosed, and 20% of order book being AI-influenced trails peers, so innovation is not yet funding dominance. (The long view had said: FY26 shows named offerings with revenue linkage (ZenseAI agentic platform, Agentic Foundry, FinOps for AI, ZenseAI.Guidewire), 85% of staff AI-certified and 20% of order book 'AI influenced' credited with the mega-deal win — but there is no disclosed R&D spend, no share-of-industry-R&D, and gross margin (33.7%) is services-level, not the high-margin engine the pattern requires.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Order book, book-to-bill (1.02 H1, 1.12 in Q3) and managed-services/AMS annuity work give some repeat revenue, and management notes lengthening contract tenure — but there is no licence/AMC attach-rate, renewal-rate or deferred-revenue-funding discussion, and Q4 FY26 flagged renewals as under pressure rather than as a strengthening annuity. (The long view had said: Across all 11 calls the model is project/staff-augmentation IT services with quarterly order-book renewals, not an upfront product plus contracted licence/AMC stream; there is no deferred revenue or customer-advance funding — working capital is a 71-80 day DSO receivable, and management itself describes the inherited platform as barely past staff augmentation.)

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The same four executives (Tandon, Bhandari, Vijayasimha, Ranjan) presented all four calls; attrition stayed below 10% for five straight quarters, the balance sheet holds $319.5m net cash with rising dividends, the July wage-hike promised in Q1 was confirmed absorbed in Q2, and management owned the Q4 miss (large deal slipped to February) and refused to inflate AI-influenced order book. Only one year of calls, so the multi-year record cannot be tested. (The long view had said: The clearest pattern in the record: the mid-teens margin promised by CFO Zute in Jan 2023 was delivered (17.8% FY24, 17.4% Q3FY26) and sustained, alongside attrition 22.8%→9.5%, CSAT 53→top-3, net cash $319-322m, rising dividends (450%→750% of face value), and a stable Tandon/Vijayasimha/Ranjan team since 2022; bad news is disclosed plainly — TMT decline budgeted to continue, accounts lost to consolidation, and the inherited platform's weakness owned rather than spun.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 2 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: TMT weakness was the single recurring drag every quarter (-9.9% Q-o-Q in Q2, -11.6% Y-o-Y in Q3, -9.7% for FY26) and analysts specifically pressed on the top client's contribution within it; the Q4 order book also jumped 122.9% to $401.8m on one large deal, so both the drag and the growth now hinge on a small number of relationships. (The long view had said: A single Hi-Tech/TMT client has driven negative vertical growth for 11-12 consecutive quarters, was explicitly flagged by analysts as 'significant concentration on one single customer', and management has now budgeted for continued decline as the client insources. Healthcare has separately been 'consolidated out of a couple of accounts', and FY27 performance is now openly stated to hinge on execution of one mega deal.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: AI is a platform-scale shift that directly attacks Zensar's effort-based delivery model: the CFO stated in Q4 FY26 that most AI spend is being repurposed out of existing app-dev and testing engagements, pressuring renewals and pricing, and Enterprise Application Services de-grew for three of four quarters. Analysts probed the AI gap versus peers ('When we look at peers, people indicate reasonably higher numbers there... Is it that we're sort of lagging?'), which management answered on classification discipline rather than delivery scale. (The long view had said: AI is a large-scale platform shift attacking the core linear-headcount delivery model: the CFO says AI spend is being repurposed out of existing application development and testing engagements, pressuring renewals and pricing, and the CEO says TMT clients are shifting spend from services to AI hardware capex. Analysts probe the AI-influenced order book share versus peers, and management is having to rebuild the delivery model around AI rather than extend it.)
- **Government Dependency**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: No revenue is regulator- or tariff-set — clients are private enterprises across BFSI, healthcare, TMT and manufacturing — but policy repeatedly hit the P&L and demand this year: trade tariffs, tighter immigration/visa rules, and an INR 25 crore PAT charge from India's new labour codes. (The long view had said: Across eleven calls the entire revenue base is private commercial verticals — BFSI, TMT, manufacturing/retail, healthcare — with no PSU tenders, regulated tariffs or subsidy-linked revenue discussed; government touches the business only through cost-side items such as immigration rules and the new Indian labour code (INR 25 crore PAT impact), not through pricing or demand.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
