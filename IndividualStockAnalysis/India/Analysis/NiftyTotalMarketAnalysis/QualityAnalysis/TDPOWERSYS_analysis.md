# TD Power Systems (TDPOWERSYS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

TD Power Systems (TDPS) is a Bengaluru-based manufacturer that builds electrical generators (alternators) — the machines that convert the spinning motion of a turbine or engine into electricity — plus a growing line of industrial electric motors. It does not sell to end-users directly; it mostly supplies the OEMs that build the 'prime movers' (steam turbines, gas turbines, hydro turbines, gas engines), who then package TDPS generators into power plants for captive/industrial power, biomass and waste-to-energy plants, hydro projects, grid-stabilization plants and, increasingly, AI data centres. Revenue comes from selling made-to-order machines against a project order book, with delivery typically ex-works; management reports order inflow, a pending order book and quarterly sales as the key metrics (FY26 standalone income INR 17.37 billion, up 35%, with ~18% EBITDA margin). It is heavily export-led — by FY26 roughly 76-80% of orders were exports or deemed exports, having sold 6,400-plus generators into 100 countries — with manufacturing at Dabaspet near Bengaluru (a third plant opened December 2025) plus a small subsidiary operation in Turkey.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Generators (steam turbine, gas turbine, hydro, gas engine)** — The core business: custom generators sold to turbine and engine makers worldwide, and by FY26 it is essentially the whole company — INR 16.77 billion of the INR 19.73 billion order book — with gas turbine and gas engine generators (data centres, grid stabilization, oil and gas) called the fastest-growing segment and hydro, including refurbishment work, at record levels.
- **Large generators / 2-pole machines** — A push into bigger machines (a 90 MW unit was the first with a rotor made fully in-house rather than imported from Germany), with management flagging in May 2026 heavy new investment in rotor manufacturing and machining up to about 200 MW, aimed mainly at export power generation and large AI data centres.
- **Motors (synchronous and induction)** — Industrial electric motors for oil and gas, water and lift irrigation, utilities and nuclear plants, sold in India and increasingly exported to the Middle East; still small — around INR 150 crore of revenue guided for FY26 — and deprioritized during the year in favour of generator volumes, but described as a key future focus area.
- **Railways (traction motors)** — Traction motors supplied to locomotive OEMs — originally through one international partner on Indian Railways' 12,000 HP locomotive programme (about INR 40 crore a quarter early on) — where the domestic business has stagnated, but by FY26 the company has export orders for the US, Europe and Russia; INR 2.47 billion of the FY26 order book.
- **Spares and aftermarket / wind repairs** — Servicing and repair work, including wind generator repairs, described as a strengthening outlet but tiny in the numbers — only about INR 0.2 billion of the FY26 order book.
- **TDPS Turkey** — A small overseas manufacturing subsidiary carrying its own order book (INR 0.29 billion at FY26), historically a drag from Turkish Lira translation losses and, in FY26, hit by shipping delays that triggered a heavy late-delivery penalty on one contract.
- **Projects business** — A separate, small engineering/projects line reported in the earlier calls (roughly INR 0.5 billion of guided revenue and a INR 459 million order book in FY23), which disappears from management's segment commentary in the later years.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**What the calls show:** TDPS sells generators that go into customers' capital projects — captive power plants, waste-heat recovery, hydro schemes, and now data-centre gensets — so revenue is a direct function of others' capex; management itself frames the domestic large-generator market as reviving 'due to CAPEX taking place in the metal sector' and conditions it on the cycle continuing, while the FY24–FY26 boom (order inflow +51% in FY26, sales from ~INR 840cr to ~INR 1,737cr, third plant plus a fresh large-generator capex round) is textbook expansionary over-investment at a visible peak, compounded by copper/special-steel input swings.

> "As long as this sector remains healthy and this CAPEX cycle continues" — *management, on an earnings call*

*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*
