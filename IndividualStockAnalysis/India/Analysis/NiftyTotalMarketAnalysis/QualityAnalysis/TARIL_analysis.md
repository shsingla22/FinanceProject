# T R I L (TARIL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

TARIL (Transformers and Rectifiers (India) Limited) designs and manufactures electrical transformers — the equipment that steps voltage up or down so power can travel across the grid and be used by industry. It sells to power utilities and state transmission companies (PGCIL, GETCO, TSTRANSCO, RRVPNL), renewable energy developers, railways and private industrial customers, mostly in India with a growing share of exports (including a US order), and it earns money by winning tenders and contracts, building the transformers at its plants near Ahmedabad in Gujarat (Changodar and Moraiya among them), and billing customers on dispatch. Revenue is order-book driven: unexecuted orders stood at about INR6,630 crore as of June 2026 against standalone FY26 revenue of INR2,395 crore, with capacity above 75,000 MVA said to support INR5,000-6,000 crore of annual revenue. Alongside the core business the company is spending INR900-1,000 crore building its own component plants so it can make roughly 80-85% of its raw materials in-house instead of buying them.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Power transformers** — Large high-voltage transformers rated 220KV, 400KV, 765KV and 1200KV for transmission grids and for evacuating power from solar parks — the biggest part of the business at about 58% of revenue as stated in the Aug 2023 call, and the segment where management says few players can compete.
- **Auxiliary transformers** — Smaller supporting transformers, described as about 16% of revenue in the Aug 2023 call.
- **Distribution transformers** — Transformers used further down the network to deliver power to end users, about 14% of revenue as of the Aug 2023 call.
- **Shunt reactors** — Grid equipment sold alongside transformers (recent orders from GETCO and RRVPNL cover transformers and reactors together), roughly 7% of revenue in the Aug 2023 call.
- **Furnace and specialty transformers** — Heavy-duty transformers for industrial furnaces and other special applications, the smallest slice at about 5% of revenue in the Aug 2023 call.
- **HVDC transformer repair** — A new line opened by a first-of-its-kind repair order from PGCIL won in FY26, which management calls TARIL's entry point into the HVDC segment rather than a material revenue contributor today.
- **Backward integration / in-house components** — A cluster of owned component facilities — a commissioned CRGO processing unit (CRGO is 30-35% of transformer raw material cost), plus CTC, pressboard and insulation, RIP bushings and fabrication plants being commissioned through FY27-FY28 — costing INR900-1,000 crore and intended to cover 80-85% of raw material needs and add 150-200 basis points to margins.

### The story in depth

The strongest parts of this business are its market and its growth. The end market is expanding for several independent reasons at once — grid modernisation, renewables, data centres, railway electrification and EV charging — with an inquiry pipeline of about INR23,000 crore behind it, and that is the single highest-scoring check in the framework (Structural end-market growth, +2). TARIL is spending into that demand rather than merely maintaining what it has: roughly INR150 crore on the Changodar expansion, INR900-1,000 crore on backward integration, and capacity taken past 75,000 MVA, while return on capital employed improved from 11.1% in FY21 to 19.1% in FY26 and return on equity from 2.1% to 15.7%. Sales have compounded at about 15% a year on the longer view and 27% over five years, and roughly INR15 of every INR100 of sales is operating profit, with margins moving only about three points either way. That combination is why Market Share Gainers is the one multibagger pattern rated a likely fit: second-largest capacity in India, a record 33,763 MVA produced, an order book up 26% to INR6,630 crore, an unprecedented single PGCIL order above INR1,000 crore, and first export wins in the US and Australia.

The trouble is that almost every trait producing those wins also produces a risk, so the same fact appears on both sides of the ledger. The margin improvement and the ability to refuse orders beyond 24 months come from a global two-to-three-year transformer shortage — which is why Pricing Power is only a partial fit (scarcity in the cycle, not structural pricing power) and why Cyclicality is rated high risk: this is equipment sold into other people's capital spending, and results have swung violently. The big utility orders that evidence share gain are the same orders behind an elevated stakeholder-concentration risk — the GETCO stop-deal and INR183 crore unpaid single-handedly caused the FY24 first-quarter collapse, and by the first quarter of FY27 one PGCIL order above INR1,000 crore again drives the picture. Because those domestic orders are awarded on lowest-price public tenders, the buyer is choosing on price, not reputation: that is why Brand Strength and Toll Roads do not carry, why Good Enough Goods sits on watch, and why Government Dependency is high risk. Backward integration is genuinely defensive — 80-85% of raw material to be made in-house, with 150-300 basis points of savings explicitly kept as margin rather than passed on — but it also confirms this is not a forward-integrating or low-price-plus business, and the savings are retained precisely because there is no channel to defend. Only one of eleven patterns fits cleanly; five are outright no-fits, and Competitive Advantage scores zero while Industry Structure scores negative.

Underneath the growth sits a cash problem that the framework flags hardest and that ties the whole story together. About 199 days of cash is stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers (receivables of 130 days in FY26), and on the headline measure every INR100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings produced minus INR5 of operating cash this year — a negative 5% cash return. Growth here consumes working capital rather than being funded by it, which is also why Recurring Revenue is a clear no-fit: there is no licence, maintenance contract or subscription anywhere, only one-off project sales billed against milestones. Capital is flowing towards shareholders' cost rather than back to them: no distribution policy is discussed at all, while an enabling INR750 crore share-sale approval and proceeds still being deployed mean dilution. Set against real positives — promoter-family continuity, low borrowing at 0.3 times equity, and candid disclosure of the Changodar monsoon delay and of missed guidance — the picture is a fast-growing, capability-building company in an excellent end market whose economics remain tender-priced, customer-concentrated and cash-hungry. That is what produces a strained resilience reading and an overall score of 38 out of 100, or Mixed.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Government Dependency, Stakeholder Concentration. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.3× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Demand is a pure customer-capex product: transformers sold into utility/grid transmission expansion, solar parks, railways and steel-industry furnace loads, with management explicitly framing the opportunity as a capex cycle, and results swinging violently (Q1FY24 revenue -48% year-on-year; FY26 revenue +23% with record MVA). Inputs are commodities (CRGO ~30-35% of RM, copper 'very fluctuating', copper disturbed by the Hormuz disruption in FY26), and the company is expanding capacity to 75,000 MVA plus INR900-1,000 crores of backward integration at what is visibly a boom in the transformer cycle — the classic expansionary over-investment pattern.

> "In the Indian market we are optimistic of the growth potential due to increase in demand on account of uptick in the capex cycle & government policies." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Some countercyclical/structural discipline: deliberate selectivity on orders ('we do not want to take any order which is beyond 24 months of delivery'), backward integration to reduce input-price dependency, and a widening demand base (data centres, EV charging, railway electrification, exports).*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 3 of the last 11 years with swings of ±25 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 6% and 16% (±3.4 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 12% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 1% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** The bulk of revenue comes from state and central utilities via price-only public tenders — PGCIL, GETCO, TSTRANSCO, RRVPNL — where pricing is set by L1 bidding, market access is gated by PGCIL empanelment/approvals, and demand is driven by government grid policy ('Viksit Bharat by 2047'). > "all the tenders that we quote in India are on L1 basis" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Ongoing diversification: Q1FY27 bidding mix described as '80% domestic and 20% export' with 'an equal mix between government, private customers and utilities, and industrial customers', plus a USD-denominated US export order and Americas/Australia pipeline.*
