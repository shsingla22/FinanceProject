**Checks that improved:**

- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: There is clear investment in talent — an ESOP grant of 94,500 options and a heavyweight COO hire for healthcare — but the key leadership addition came from outside (SHL Taiwan) rather than an internal pipeline. | The long view had said: There is genuine systematic effort — attrition cut from 14–15% to ~10% via performance-linked pay and long-term wealth creation — but the critical healthcare leadership role was filled externally and the CFO seat has changed four times in five years, indicating a thin internal pipeline.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Growth is fully organic with no acquisitions, leverage stayed low at 0.3x debt/equity through a heavy capex year, and the company kept investing in consumer/electronics programmes while that segment was contracting. | The long view had said: Growth has been entirely organic with prudent leverage (debt/equity 0.3x) and capacity largely pre-contracted, though the Rs.300–350cr Abu Dhabi plant is only 50–60% secured and the annual Rs.500cr enabling resolution signals appetite for large capital raises.
- **Advertisement and promotion** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: No consumer A&P line, but B2B promotion is evidently productive — trade-show presence at CPHI, PDA Europe and PODD generated 130+ customer meetings and converted into four new signed projects the same quarter. | The long view had said: As a B2B contract manufacturer the only promotional spend disclosed is trade exhibitions (CPHI, POD, PDA), which management flags as a one-off cost drag rather than durable brand equity.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: The GLP-1 angle has made this a well-covered story rather than an ignored niche — global brokers such as UBS are on the call and milestones are pushed out via press release. | The long view had said: It was a humble Vadodara niche moulder, but the business is now openly tracked by UBS, Investec, JM Financial, Ambit and 360 ONE and is expanding into the most capital-attracting themes (GLP-1, semiconductors), so obscurity no longer protects it.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: Shaily has built a preferred-supplier position in the customer's mind for generic GLP-1 pens — it supplies most of the generic filers and its pen was first to US tentative approval — but it is a chosen vendor, not a monopolist. | The long view had said: Its spring-driven pen platform is claimed to be the only globally marketable non-infringing device outside Novo's, translating into 65–75% of the early Canadian Semaglutide filers.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: The consumer/home-furnishings vertical is clearly in a downcycle (FY26 revenue down 9% on weak Europe/US demand) but management identified no concrete upturn trigger, only an intention to restore momentum. | The long view had said: The consumer/home-furnishing vertical is visibly near a cyclical trough (FY26 revenue down 9%, Q4 down 31%) with management pointing to eventual recovery plus tariff/FTA tailwinds, but the timing of the upturn remains unidentified.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 6. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 0 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Shaily now names its own device platforms (Axiom Max, Harmony, Neo) and repeatedly invokes customer trust and regulatory validation as reputational assets, but there is no heritage story, A&P spend or distributor leverage — this is B2B supplier reputation rather than brand power. (The long view had said: Shaily sells into other companies' brands (IKEA-type furnishings, customers' Semaglutide launches); apart from naming its own devices Shaily Neo and Harmony, there is no A&P spend, heritage story or distributor leverage discussed on any call.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Management claims it has onboarded most of the generic semaglutide field, healthcare grew 139% in FY26 to lift mix from 21% to 40%, and it is expanding internationally with a 75m-pen Abu Dhabi plant plus new entries into consumer electronics and semiconductor trays — share gained on capability, not on price or credit terms (ROCE 38.4%, D/E 0.3x). (The long view had said: Management claims 65–75% share of the first Semaglutide filers in Canada and first US tentative approval, with healthcare revenue up 139% in FY26 and industrial up 41%; but the largest segment, consumer, de-grew 9% in FY26 and 31% in Q4, and share numbers are given only once rather than tracked rising over time.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: No price increases, premiumisation or elasticity are discussed in any of the four calls; the 720-1030 bps margin expansion is explicitly attributed to healthcare mix shift, not pricing, and Q4 FY26 notes raw-material inflation absorbed without any mention of pass-through. (The long view had said: Roughly 80% of the business is on formula-based raw-material pass-through with quarterly resets, and Q1 FY23 margins collapsed to 12.6% on input inflation the company could not price ahead of; the later margin expansion to 29% EBITDA in FY26 is explicitly mix-driven (healthcare 21%→40% of revenue), not price increases.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Cost to Replicate (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
