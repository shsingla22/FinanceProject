# Shipping Corporation of India Ltd. (SCI) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: SERVICES*

The Shipping Corporation of India (SCI) is India's largest shipping company, a government-owned "Navratna" firm that owns and operates ocean-going ships and hires them out to carry other people's cargo. Its customers are mainly Indian oil and gas companies and government bodies (ONGC, Indian Oil, BPCL, HPCL, the Indian Navy, DRDO) plus commercial shippers of containers, coal and grain; management calls SCI a "national carrier" that keeps India's supply lines open. It earns money from freight and charter hire — either voyage-by-voyage at volatile spot market rates (tracked against benchmarks like the VLCC Worldscale, TD3/TD14 routes and the Baltic Dry Index) or under fixed time-charter contracts — and also earns fees for managing 40 vessels owned by government organisations. It operates on international routes, chiefly the Persian Gulf–India energy trade and India–Europe container services (rerouted via the Cape of Good Hope during Red Sea disruption), plus Indian coastal shipping; FY26 operating revenue was INR 5,778 crores with standalone net profit of INR 1,326 crores, from an owned fleet of 58 vessels averaging 15.5 years of age.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Tanker segment (crude, clean petroleum products and gas carriers)** — About 31 ships carrying crude oil, refined fuels and LPG/LNG — described as "the cornerstone of SCI's operations" and by far the biggest earner, with FY26 revenue of INR 3,942 crores (up from INR 3,609 crores) and segment profit jumping ~75% to INR 1,190 crores; it now includes two new very large gas carriers, Sahyadri and Shivalik (about 82,000 cubic metres each, built by Hyundai Heavy Industries), deployed on the Persian Gulf–India route.
- **Bulk carrier segment** — Ships carrying dry cargoes such as coal and grain, whose earnings swing with the Baltic Dry Index; it is the weakest part — revenue rose ~11% to INR 789 crores in FY26 and losses narrowed (from a INR 48 crore loss in Q1FY26 to a INR 2 crore profit in Q2), but the segment still ran at a loss for the full year.
- **Liner and Passenger Services (container shipping, including coastal)** — Scheduled container services on fixed routes, including the India–Europe trade using the 9,000 TEU SCI Delhi plus in-chartered ships, and a coastal business management calls "a key growth driver" (freight up 10% year-on-year, utilisation as high as 99%); FY26 revenue was INR 784 crores with profit of INR 75 crores, down from INR 1,036 crores and INR 166 crores the prior year on softer freight rates and lower volumes.
- **Offshore and Technical Services** — Ten offshore support vessels that service oil and gas exploration and defence work — seven are on long-term charters with ONGC, the Indian Navy and DRDO and the rest work the spot market — with recent contract renewals repriced at roughly 50% higher charter rates for three-year terms, giving a small but steady revenue base.
- **Ship management for government organisations** — SCI manages an extra 40 vessels across various categories on behalf of government bodies, on top of its own 58 ships, adding fee income without owning the assets.
- **Joint ventures and new strategic initiatives** — Planned tie-ups rather than current earnings: a proposed JV with oil PSUs (IOC, BPCL, HPCL) and Sagarmala Financial Corporation to jointly buy and run tankers for petroleum and petrochemical cargo — still awaiting Ministry approval with most tenders unfinalised — plus a February 2026 MoU with CONCOR and major ports to form "Bharat Container Shipping Line" to build an Indian container fleet (currently "as good as negligible"), an existing LNG joint venture (ILT), and a contract with Mazagon Dock for a methanol dual-fuel PSV pilot under the National Green Hydrogen Mission.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Shipping Corporation of India comes out of all three reviews as the same company seen from three angles: a state-owned, asset-heavy shipping line enjoying an unusually good year in a business it does not control. The quality framework scores it Weak overall (32 out of 100), and the reasons cluster. Every rupee of assets produces only about 43 paise of sales — the company carries roughly INR5,778 crores of revenue on INR8,489 crores of net worth plus INR2,409 crores of debt. Operating margin looks healthy at 38 paise per rupee of sales, but it has swung about six points a year, which is the framework's way of saying the main costs and prices are set outside the building. The multibagger screen agrees from the other side: Pricing Power, Toll Roads, Recurring Revenue and Innovation Dominance are all flat 'no fit'. Freight is priced off published global indices, VLCC rates moved from Worldscale 48 to over 100 to 460 on war news, the liner segment's revenue fell from INR1,036 crores to INR784 crores, and there is no research pipeline — fleet growth comes from buying second-hand tonnage averaging 15.5 years. The 40 government vessels SCI manages earn only cost-plus fees, and about 60% of the tanker fleet trades on the spot market. Nothing here repeats reliably.

The one genuinely distinctive asset — being a Navratna, government-owned 'national carrier' — is also the single largest source of risk, and this is the clearest link across the three reports. It is why the Brand Strength and Cost to Replicate patterns score partial rather than zero: cabotage rules plus sovereign ownership give SCI what management describes as a monopoly on coastal crude movement, privileged access to oil-company demand aggregation and to the Maritime Development Fund. It is also why the growth story exists at all — a 2047 business plan launched by the Prime Minister, a 59-vessel joint venture aggregated by the petroleum ministry, and a 51-vessel Bharat Container Shipping Line. But the risk review flags exactly the same facts as HIGH RISK under both Government Dependency and Stakeholder Concentration: the oil-and-gas joint venture sits unapproved with the ministry, and the named customers are a short list of state entities — IOC, BPCL, HPCL for the tankers, CONCOR and major ports for containers, ONGC, the Navy and DRDO for seven of ten offshore vessels. The 'Merger and acquisition' check scores negative for the same reason — these are two very large government-brokered ventures with no published return, timeline or approval, not small bolt-on deals. The Market Share Gainers pattern lands on the honest verdict: the opportunity is real (India's freight bill is around US$80 billion, the Indian container fleet is negligible), but capture so far is announced, not delivered — the owned fleet stood still at 58 vessels across both earnings calls.

The third connection is timing. FY26 profit before tax rose 67%, almost entirely from tankers during a war-driven rate spike that management itself calls temporary and abnormal, with the Baltic index moving from roughly 1,500 to 3,000. The quality framework therefore marks 'Cyclical market growth' at its worst score — the company is near a cyclical peak, not a trough — and the risk review rates Cyclicality HIGH RISK, with an analyst on the call raising the danger of buying ships at the top. That matters because growth capex is large and rising just as earnings peak: two very large gas carriers inducted, a dual-fuel platform supply vessel ordered, tenders out for Aframax, MR and container ships, and a 110-vessel joint venture pipeline stretching to 2047 — all against a stated return hurdle of only 10-12%. What holds the picture together is balance-sheet conservatism, which the Corporate Culture pattern credits and the resilience read calls SOUND: debt-to-equity of 0.29, INR2,676 crores of cash, debt-service cover of 4.61, ROE above 16%, a rising performance-linked dividend of INR7.5 per share for FY26, and project-by-project board approval. So this is not a fragile company; it is a durable one whose profits are borrowed from a rate cycle, whose competitive edge is granted by the state rather than earned from customers, and whose two 'watch' risks — ageing tonnage earning less than modern ships, and an undifferentiated service with no brand premium — sit quietly underneath a record year.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Government Dependency, Stakeholder Concentration, Fashion Risk. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 2 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Both calls describe demand and profit purely in terms of freight-cycle indices — BDI moving 1,500→1,800→~3,000, VLCC worldscale 48.76→100→TD3 at 460 — with FY26 PBT up 67% almost entirely on the tanker segment during a war-driven spike, and an analyst explicitly raising the last decade's mistake of buying ships at the cycle peak while SCI has open tenders for Aframaxes, MRs and container ships in exactly this abnormal market.

> "if we buy at this peak of the cycle, you know, then maybe our future revenues can, you know, actually suffer... Very well noted and definitely the Board, when we take the decision, will definitely take this point into account" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Stated 10-12% IRR hurdle per vessel evaluated as an independent project; intent to lock long-term OMC commitments with floor/ceiling formulas covering standing costs, interest and depreciation; net debt-light balance sheet (D/E 0.29) allowing countercyclical purchases*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 4 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 18% and 38% (±6.0 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 27% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 10% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** SCI is a Navratna PSU whose entire growth plan is state-directed: the 2047 business plan was launched by the Prime Minister, the 59-vessel demand aggregation was done by MOPNG, the oil-and-gas JV sits 'under active consideration of our Ministry', 10% of JV equity is expected from the government's Maritime Development Fund, offshore charters are with ONGC/Navy/DRDO, and an analyst asks directly about tax and GST incentives — a structure where policy, not the market, determines the fortune.
