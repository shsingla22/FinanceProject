### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 4 strengthened, 0 weakened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: The core model is forward integration: third-party manufacturing feeding 117 owned/franchised facilities (only 35 franchised, rest on payroll), exclusive in-house medicine supply capturing clinical and product margin, plus e-commerce, own OTC retail push, tele-calling/video consult and a health card — control of the customer from diagnosis through the medicine regimen. (The long view had said: Across all four calls the company moves steadily toward owning the customer relationship: 117 owned facilities against only 35 clinic franchises (franchise count deliberately shrunk over three years), doctors on payroll, exclusive medicine supply into its own network, D2C e-commerce, and now OTC pharmacy retail plus own diagnostics — capturing both clinical quality and product margin.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Management leans on trust, word-of-mouth referrals as a free marketing channel, a Swadeshi identity and research credibility, and is stretching the name into OTC retail and UAE — but there is no heritage/provenance moat of its own (the heritage claimed is Ayurveda's), no A&P spend disclosed, and no premium-pricing evidence. (The long view had said: Trust and word-of-mouth are the stated acquisition engine, sustained A&P across ABP/Zee/print has been backed by growing volumes (IPD +65%, OPD +69% in FY26), and the name is being stretched into OTC, supplements, beauty, diagnostics, a Swadeshi Health Card and UAE/Nepal/Kazakhstan. But the brand is barely five years old at scale, leans heavily on the founder ('Acharya ji') persona, and the borrowed heritage is Ayurveda's rather than the company's.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: Across all four calls management discusses volume, bed additions and mix, but never an explicit price increase, elasticity, premiumisation or waiting-list dynamic; margin expansion is attributed to operating leverage and product mix, not pricing. (The long view had said: Average revenue per occupied bed has been essentially flat for three years (INR7,900 FY24 → 8,280 Q1FY26 → 8,337 Q3FY26) and management says so explicitly; all earnings growth has come from occupancy, volume and cost leverage, plus 10% free treatment for the poor. Hoped-for realisation gains depend on insurer and Ayushman tariffs (INR8,800-9,400), i.e. third-party rate cards, not the company's own pricing power.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The assets a copycat would have to rebuild — ~600 employed Ayurvedic doctors, 49 NABH-accredited facilities, insurance/CGHS empanelment and ~180 published papers — are real but rebuildable in a few years; management's own disclosure that a 100-bed hospital costs only INR3-4 crore argues the physical network is cheap for a funded entrant to replicate. (The long view had said: The economics that make the model attractive — INR3-4 lakh per bed, third-party manufacturing, leased or college-tied hospitals, demand bought on TV and social media — are precisely what a funded entrant could replicate in a few years; management's defence is an assertion of uniqueness rather than a concrete barrier, and the durable assets (NABH accreditations, papers, founder persona) are not capital- or time-locked at a level a rival could not match.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Management explicitly retreated from government business in Q3 FY26 after payment delays forced roughly INR20-21 crore of receivable recovery, yet by Q4 FY26 was framing the UP government's UPSS cashless-Ayurveda scheme as a major growth driver and listing CGHS, CAPF, ECHS and four state-government panels plus AYUSH regulation as core assets — so policy still shapes a meaningful slice of the opportunity. (The long view had said: The growth story is repeatedly anchored to policy: AYUSH inclusion in cashless health insurance from 1 April 2024 was credited as a demand unlock in Call 1, and by Q4 FY26 the entire ARPB upside case rests on a Government of India Ayushman draft rate (INR8,800-9,400/day) and the UP government's UPSS cashless scheme, with empanelment across CGHS, CAPF, ECHS and four state governments. The company has already been burned by the mechanism — government receivables forced a INR20-21 crore recovery drive and a deliberate retreat from PSU business, with management conceding it cannot control the timeline.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The patient base is diffuse (6 lakh+ OPD, 40,450 IPD in FY26), but the product story rests on very few nodes: a single OTC SKU (Pet Yakrit Pleeha Shuddhi Kit) was the only launched product until Feb 2026 and had to cross INR10 crore monthly before a second was released, OTC scale-up hinges on one prospective national distributor, manufacturing is entirely third-party, and the whole franchise is built around one promoter-guru with a family-only board. (The long view had said: Revenue comes from a highly diffuse retail base — over 6 lakh OPD patients and 40,450 IPD admissions in FY26 across 117 facilities, 100 cities and 23 states — and contract manufacturing is deliberately split across four states. The one named single-relationship dependency, the exclusive Entero B2B distribution tie-up, simply failed to start ('Entero was asking us to bear the expenses') with no material revenue impact, which itself shows how little rests on any one counterparty.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Fashion Risk (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
