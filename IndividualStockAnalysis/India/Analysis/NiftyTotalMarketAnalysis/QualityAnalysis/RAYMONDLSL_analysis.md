# Raymond Lifestyle (RAYMONDLSL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Raymond Lifestyle Limited is an India-based men's clothing company, roughly 100 years old, that was demerged and separately listed on the BSE and NSE on 5 September 2024. It makes and sells fabric (suiting, shirting, linen, ceremonial and 'techno' functional fabrics) and finished apparel under brands such as Raymond Ready-to-Wear, Park Avenue, ColorPlus, Parx, Ethnix by Raymond and Sleepz by Raymond, selling to Indian consumers through about 1,600-1,700 of its own and franchise stores across roughly 600 towns, plus dealers/wholesale trade, multi-brand outlets and online. It also earns money business-to-business: stitching garments (mainly suits) on contract for overseas brands in the US, UK, Europe and Asia-Pacific, and selling cotton and linen shirting fabric in bulk to other Indian brands. Total income was INR 7,034 crores in FY26 (up 11% year-on-year) with EBITDA of INR 804 crores (11.4% margin), and the company describes itself as debt-free with net cash of INR 179 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Branded Textile** — Sells Raymond-branded suiting and shirting fabric (including premium wool, Italian, linen, ceremonial and wrinkle/stain-resistant 'techno' ranges) mainly through dealers and stores to men buying for weddings, work and occasions; it is the largest and most profitable segment at INR 831 crores of revenue in Q4 FY26 with segment EBITDA margins in the mid-teens or better.
- **Branded Apparel** — Sells ready-made clothing under four core brands — Park Avenue, ColorPlus, Raymond Ready-to-Wear and Parx — through exclusive stores, multi-brand outlets and online; it is the second-largest segment (INR 469 crores in Q4 FY26, INR 1,667 crores for the core four brands in FY26) but runs at much thinner margins (about 5-8%) because of store openings and marketing spend.
- **Emerging / new businesses (reported inside Branded Apparel)** — Newer categories being built out, chiefly Ethnix by Raymond ethnic and fusion wear (about 140 stores) and Sleepz by Raymond men's sleepwear priced at INR 500-999; management calls these a strategic investment phase that currently drags the apparel margin down, and said it will start reporting them separately.
- **Garmenting** — A contract manufacturing export business that stitches suits, jackets and trousers for overseas brands — the company says a completed capacity expansion would make it the world's third-largest suit maker; revenue was INR 342 crores in Q4 FY26 (up 38%), and it is the most volatile part, swinging to losses when US tariffs hit and recovering on trade deals, with US dependence cut from about 50% to 35%.
- **High Value Cotton Shirting (B2B)** — Makes and sells premium cotton and linen shirting fabric in bulk to other businesses rather than to consumers; the smallest segment at INR 197 crores in Q4 FY26 with single-digit to low-double-digit EBITDA margins (a year-ago quarter was flattered by a INR 53 crore one-time government subsidy for its Amravati plant).

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Raymond Lifestyle's case rests on two things it genuinely owns: a century-old brand and a route to the customer that would be expensive for anyone to rebuild. The distribution check is the strongest single mark in the framework (score 2) — a network of Raymond shops across more than 600 towns, layered with exclusive stores, multi-brand counters, large-format stores and online, and unusual in that a customer can buy fabric and have it stitched in the same place. The same asset shows up three more times in the pattern screen: it is why Brand Strength is a strong fit, why Forward Integrators (making the fabric and owning the shop that sells it) is a likely fit, and why Cost to Replicate is at least a partial fit. It also pays: after direct input costs the company keeps about ₹54 of every ₹100 of sales, and the framework reads that as pricing customers accept willingly. Growth checks are mostly positive for the same reason — share taken from imported fabrics, a push into smaller towns, and an end market slowly shifting from unbranded to branded — and the demand slump of FY25 (one quarter's operating profit down to ₹89 crore) has turned back up on tax relief and a heavy wedding calendar.

The awkward part is that several of these strengths are the direct source of the risks. Premium positioning is what earns the ₹54 margin and what makes Pricing Power a likely fit — average selling price up about ₹26 a metre while volumes grew, and margins held even as wool prices rose 25%. But that same premium is why 'Good Enough Goods' is flagged: roughly half the ethnic market is unbranded, management concedes smaller-town shoppers often prefer the local tailor's product and see Raymond as too expensive, and the answer has been cheaper entry-level ranges — which pulls against the premiumisation story. Reliance on occasion and wedding demand is what makes pricing look inelastic and what makes the cycle check positive, yet it is also the cyclicality risk: in the weak stretch revenue fell 6.2% and branded textile margins dropped from 22.2% to 18.9% on soft sentiment. The biggest flag, rated high risk, is one management names itself — casualization of how people dress, which erodes the formal suiting where it is winning share, and which it is now answering with casual, comfort-led and fusion ranges. Ethnic wear shows how contestable the moat really is: well-funded newcomers arrived, and Raymond's own Ethnix build stalled, with 18 of 140 stores shut in a single quarter. Exports tell a similar story — the geography check scores well precisely because the company is spreading beyond the US, but it only cut US dependence from about 50% to 35% after that concentration produced a quarter with ₹197 crore of revenue and an ₹8 crore operating loss, and the recovery is credited to a trade deal rather than anything the company controls.

Where the record is weakest is in what the business earns on the money tied up in it. For every ₹100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings, it produced about ₹5 of operating cash this year, and roughly 102 days of cash sit stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, in a model that is inventory-heavy and entirely one-time sales — which is exactly why the Recurring Revenue pattern is a clear non-fit. Capital spending is modest and mostly maintenance, with management openly saying it is not making a big growth investment; that same fact is what holds Innovation Dominance back to only a likely fit, since new products arrive every year with visible effect on selling prices and mix, but no research spend is ever disclosed and most of the capex is upkeep. Against that, the balance sheet has genuinely improved — from ₹396 crore of net debt to ₹179 crore of net cash — the board is voluntarily all-independent, and management discloses uncomfortable facts, such as ₹30 crore of losses on ₹140 crore of sales in newer businesses. There is also real cost discipline: operating costs up just 1.2% on income up 15%, factory utilisation lifted above 90%, and loss-making stores closed. Put together, the rating comes out at 50 out of 100 — 'Decent' — from a business-quality score of 55, a much weaker multibagger fit of 39, and risk safety of 56, with resilience judged sound. The short version: a real brand and a real shop network, a cycle currently turning in its favour, but thin returns on the capital employed and a core demand base its own management says is structurally shifting.

### Shifting Consumer Preferences — HIGH RISK
*"The customer is changing"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; the numeric fingerprints could not be computed, so the call evidence decides alone.

**What the calls show:** Management itself names the erosion of its core demand base: casualization of the dress code is called a structural trend, and by FY27 casualization is one of the two strategic pillars, pushing branded textiles toward comfort-first fabrics and knitwear and apparel toward polos, chinos, t-shirts and denim — a direct migration away from the worsted-suiting franchise the company is built on. > "We also recognize that casualization of dress code is a structural trend. Our response is not to retreat, but to adopt" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Active and so far successful portfolio pivot toward the new preference — ColorPlus positioned to spearhead casualization, premium casuals/fusion, linen and techno-series growth, with FY26 revenue at a record INR7,034 crores and ASP up INR26/metre*

## What to watch

- Pricing Power pattern: the average selling price gain of about ₹26 a metre and margins held through the 25% wool price rise must persist — a repeat of the earlier reversal (revenue down 6.2%, branded textile margin 22.2% to 18.9%) would break the pattern back to a non-fit.
- Return on capital check: the 5% cash return on capital and the ~102-day gap between paying suppliers and collecting from customers need to narrow; without that, the growth and distribution strengths never reach shareholders.
- Shifting Consumer Preferences (high risk): whether the pivot into ColorPlus-led casuals, comfort fabrics and knitwear actually replaces the formal suiting demand management says is structurally eroding.
- New Entrants risk and the Cost to Replicate pattern: the ethnic build-out, after 18 of 140 Ethnix stores were closed in one quarter — store count needs to keep rising from the 1,675 level and grow faster than the industry, not just hold.
- Government Dependency risk: the garmenting export book, now about 35% US after the tariff-hit quarter (₹197 crore revenue, ₹8 crore operating loss), and whether the 38% garmenting growth and the hoped-for UK/EU trade agreements make the diversification stick.
- Capex-for-growth check: management's explicit stance that it is not making major growth investment — any move to real growth capex, or continued maintenance-only spend, changes both the growth outlook and the Innovation Dominance verdict.
