# Kec International Ltd. (KEC) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSTRUCTION*

KEC International is an Indian engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) contractor: it wins tenders to design and build large infrastructure — power transmission lines and substations, buildings and factories, water pipelines, railway and metro systems, oil and gas pipelines and solar/wind projects — and also manufactures and sells hardware such as transmission towers, poles, cables and conductors from its own factories in India, Dubai, Mexico and Brazil. Its customers are government power utilities (PGCIL, state electricity boards), private transmission developers bidding under the TBCB route, Indian Railways and metro authorities, and private industrial and real-estate clients. It makes money by executing these turnkey projects against a large order book (about INR 36,000 crore at FY26 year-end, plus L1 positions, giving six to seven quarters of visibility), booking revenue as work progresses; margins are thin — roughly 7% EBITDA and 2-3% net on FY26 revenue of INR 23,506 crore — so profitability turns on commodity prices, labour availability and how fast clients pay. Roughly two-thirds of revenue comes from India, with the rest spread across the Middle East (about 27% of order book), Africa, the CIS, the Americas, SAARC and Australia.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Transmission & Distribution (T&D)** — Builds high-voltage power lines and substations (including 765 kV, GIS/AIS and HVDC) and supplies towers in India and overseas; by far the biggest and fastest-growing part, at INR 15,883 crore or 68% of FY26 revenue and about 70% of new orders, with an order book plus L1 of over INR 26,000 crore.
- **SAE Towers** — The overseas tower-manufacturing arm with plants in Mexico and Brazil that supplies towers, hardware, poles and monopoles across the U.S., Mexico and Brazil (and earlier did EPC work in Brazil); revenue of INR 1,800 crore in FY26, up 36%, with an order book plus L1 above INR 2,600 crore — a past problem area that management says is now profitable and much less indebted.
- **Civil** — Turnkey construction of buildings and factories — residential high-rises, industrial plants, hospitals, data centres, metals and mining facilities, defence and water pipeline projects; the second-largest business, described repeatedly as a key growth driver, with an order book plus L1 of over INR 10,000 crore, though revenue (around INR 900-1,000 crore a quarter) has been held back by labour shortages and slow payments on water projects.
- **Railways / Transportation** — Overhead electrification, gauge conversion, metro power supply, ballastless track and signalling work, including the KAVACH train collision avoidance system (611 route km done, 1,836 more under way) and automatic block signalling; a smaller unit of roughly INR 350-470 crore a quarter with an order book plus L1 above INR 3,000 crore, where management is deliberately bidding selectively and focusing on finishing existing jobs.
- **Cables and Conductors** — Manufactures and sells power and specialty cables plus transmission conductors and railway products (signalling cables, contact and catenary conductors) to T&D, railway, metro, solar and metals customers and increasingly for export; revenue of roughly INR 360-420 crore a quarter, doubling as a supplier to KEC's own railway and T&D projects.
- **Oil & Gas Pipelines** — Lays cross-country oil and gas pipelines for government and private clients, now including a first international project in Africa and approval to bid for Saudi Aramco; the smallest business at about INR 90-130 crore a quarter, still being built up by acquiring the pre-qualifications needed to bid for bigger jobs.
- **Renewables** — Builds solar PV plants on an EPC basis — including a 625 MWp project in Rajasthan and a 600 MWp project in Karnataka — and has begun moving into wind, with an order book of over INR 1,300 crore when first broken out; a newer, small but strategically emphasised growth engine tied to India's energy transition.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

KEC comes out of the three reviews as a genuinely growing business that has not yet been paid for its growth. The quality framework's strongest marks sit almost entirely on the demand side: end markets in secular expansion (non-fossil targets raised from 500GW by 2030 to 900GW by 2035, record peak power demand, grid congestion and data-centre demand in the US), sales compounding about 10% a year with unusual consistency, and steady growth capex across cables, conductors and tower capacity in Dubai, Jaipur, Jabalpur, Butibori and Brazil. That same demand story is what the multibagger screen picks up as its two best pattern reads — Market Share Gainers (T&D revenue up 24% in FY26 to INR15,883cr, segment mix rising from 59% to 68%, record order intake of INR25,280cr, SAE growth of 36%, first entries into Australia, Saudi 380kV GIS and Africa oil and gas) and Innovation Dominance, the only 'likely fit' of the eleven, built on a steady cadence of new capabilities from signalling cables and jumper dropper wire through aluminium conductors and UL export approvals to elastomeric and E-Beam cables, HVDC converter stations and KAVACH. But growth scores 0.83 while return on capital scores -0.67, and the headline check is blunt: for every ₹100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings, the business generated ₹-4 of operating cash this year, with group-level ROIC thin at about 2.6% PAT even as the cables business is claimed to earn very high returns. Growth is real; the cash return on the capital funding it is not there yet.

The reason the growth does not convert is the same single trait running through the patterns and the risks: every rupee of revenue is won project-by-project in open competitive tendering. That one fact produces the framework's worst scores (no mini-monopolies, no network effects, no switching costs — all at -2, and industry structure at -0.83), it is why six of the eleven patterns are outright 'no fit', and it is what turns three separate risk channels amber. Pricing Power fails because roughly half the order book is fixed-price, so margins are set by commodity and labour inflation rather than by KEC raising prices — the same mechanism that makes Cyclicality an elevated risk, and the same mechanism behind FY23's margin collapse on committed high-cost orders and the FY26 guidance cut mid-year from a promised 100 basis-point improvement down to around 7%. Toll Roads fails because EPC work is a large share of the client's project capex awarded on lowest price, which is precisely the Good Enough Goods risk: 41 bidders in civil tenders, competitive oil and gas pricing, cable margins management itself describes as below market. New Entrants is on watch for the same reason from a different direction — contractors displaced from a slowing NHAI road market have flooded adjacent tenders and reset bid economics far enough that KEC chose to withdraw and deliberately degrow transportation rather than match price. Brand Strength and Cost to Replicate land at 'partial' on this same boundary: PGCIL's award for maximum circuit-km added in FY24, Saudi Aramco approval, 765kV and GIS credentials and SAE's US, Mexico and Brazil tower plants are real and take years to build, but they buy pre-qualification and an invitation to bid, not a premium price.

The defences, the drags and the honesty all trace back to the same places too. The company's answer to commodity-price tendering is to climb out of it — five HVDC projects, 765kV lines and substations, the first 380kV GIS in Saudi, semiconductor EPC and KAVACH — which is simultaneously the Innovation Dominance pattern, the silver lining on the Good Enough Goods risk and the substance behind Cost to Replicate; whether that migration outruns the 41-bidder end of the market is the central open question in the file. Government Dependency is the one high risk, because spending, tariffs and payment cycles for PGCIL, state utilities, Indian Railways, metros and Jal Jeevan Mission water projects are all set by policy, and the offset is again a mix shift — private TBCB developers moving from 45% to a projected 75-80% of India T&D, with INR3,600 crores of private and state-utility orders in FY26. SAE Brazil cuts both ways in a single asset: the M&A check marks it down to -1 as a persistent drag needing loss funding and high-cost debt repayment over years, while the same plants supply the pre-qualifications behind Cost to Replicate and the 36% growth behind Market Share Gainers. Management scores well on candor — four straight years of admitting underinvestment and below-market cable margins, SAE losses, water-project payment stress and a downward hedging revision — yet Corporate Culture is only 'partial' because the delivery-against-promise record is weaker than the disclosure record. One genuine conflict inside the file deserves flagging rather than smoothing: the working-capital check credits cash coming back 201 days before suppliers must be paid, with customers effectively funding the business, while the Recurring Revenue pattern reads the same balance sheet as working capital absorbing cash with net debt including acceptances rising. That unresolved point matters, because it decides whether the growth is self-funding or debt-funded. The composite lands at 39 out of 100 — 46 on business quality, 18 on multibagger fit, 50 on risk safety — a Mixed, two-star verdict, with resilience marked strained.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** The business is anchored in fixed infrastructure whose spending, tariffs and payment cycles are set by government — PGCIL, state utilities, Indian Railways, metros and Jal Jeevan Mission water projects — and management repeatedly ties the outlook to policy (green energy corridors, the 500GW→900GW non-fossil target, the union budget). The mechanism has already bitten: state water-project payments stalled, forcing 'calibrated' execution, ~INR900 crores of receivables stuck in Odisha and MP, and a direct hit to FY26 revenue and margins.

> "We continue to adopt a calibrated approach to execution in Water projects given the current payment scenario in this segment." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Structural shift of India T&D to private TBCB developers (private share 45%→75-80%), INR3,600 crores of private/SEB orders in FY26, and ~half the T&D order book international (Middle East, Africa, CIS, Americas)*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** KEC's revenue is almost entirely tied to customers' capex programmes — utility/private TBCB transmission builds, metro and railway projects, real-estate and industrial construction — and to commodity input prices under ~50% fixed-price orders, which is exactly the customer-cyclicality plus commodity-pass-through combination. The FY23 calls show consolidated EBITDA collapsing to 5.1% on 'elevated commodity prices and logistic cost', and even in FY26 margins swing on water-project slowdowns, LPG/steel supplier shutdowns and claim settlements, with guidance cut mid-year from ~8% to '7% to 7.5%'.

> "The consolidated margins have been impacted primarily due to the elevated commodity prices and logistic cost and Brazil performance." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Diversification across T&D, civil, cables, railways, renewables and across India/Middle East/Americas/Africa; countercyclical capacity expansion (Butibori, Dubai, Jaipur, Jabalpur, Brazil) and strategic inventory buildup at benign commodity prices*
