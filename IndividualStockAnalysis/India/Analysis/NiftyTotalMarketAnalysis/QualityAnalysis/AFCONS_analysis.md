# Afcons Infrastr. (AFCONS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Afcons Infrastructure is a 65-year-old Indian construction contractor (founded 1959, majority-owned by the Shapoorji Pallonji group before its 2024 IPO) that builds large, technically difficult infrastructure — metros, bridges, tunnels, roads, railways, ports and jetties, dams and water projects, and oil-and-gas and industrial facilities. Its customers are mainly governments and government agencies in India (central and state) plus overseas clients, and it wins work by bidding for tenders; it gets paid as construction progresses on those contracts, so revenue depends on the size of its order book and how fast it can execute it. In FY26 it reported revenue of INR 12,322 crores with an EBITDA margin of 11.7%, and its pending order book was around INR 32,635 crores as of Feb 2026. It works across India and internationally — roughly 70 overseas projects since re-entering foreign markets in 2005, with a stated aim of taking overseas work to about 30% of the order book, including Africa (a EUR 100 million+ road job in Uganda) and a first entry into Europe via Croatia.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Urban infrastructure** — Builds elevated and underground metro lines and bridges in cities — recent examples include the Bangalore double-decker metro and flyover corridor, Agra and Kanpur metros, and the Delhi–Meerut RRTS corridor; management flags metros as a segment where bidding competition has become very aggressive.
- **Surface transport** — Roads and railways, including expressway packages like the Nagpur–Mumbai Samruddhi Mahamarg and railway work on the Udhampur–Srinagar–Baramulla link; this is also where its new international pushes sit (Uganda road project, Croatia road and rail tenders, two of which the client later cancelled).
- **Hydro and underground** — Tunnels (including NATM tunnels), dams, barrages and water projects — the technically hardest work and a showcase area, with the Chenab railway bridge stretch's 13 tunnels (36.3 km) and record tunnel-boring rates of 740–777 metres a month on the CIDCO water project.
- **Marine and industrial** — Ports, jetties, wharves, LNG tanks and similar industrial facilities; the calls treat this as a genuine global strength, citing an Engineering News-Record ranking of 8th worldwide among international marine and port contractors, plus two domestic marine contracts worth about INR 1,400 crores won in Q3 FY26.
- **Oil and gas** — Energy-related construction, described as a listed vertical alongside the other four; the calls cite the HRRL crude oil terminal at Mundra, commissioned in FY26, as evidence of growing capability in industrial and energy infrastructure.
- **Water supply (newer addition)** — A segment the company says it entered only the year before FY26, starting with a 3.35 km water tunnel in Raigad, Maharashtra, and the CIDCO water supply project; it also has Jal Jeevan Mission work in Uttar Pradesh that management says it remains cautious about.

### The story in depth

Afcons comes out of the three reviews as a well-run company having a genuinely bad year, which is why the combined rating lands at 42 out of 100 — Mixed, two stars — with resilience marked STRAINED. The management scores are the highest of any area (1.17), and the specifics back that up: the MD opened the FY26 call with an unprompted apology for the first loss since 2010, itemised every cause, and declined to give FY27 guidance rather than offer a comfortable number. Stewardship is graded a full 2 for purely organic growth, a formal go/no-go filter on which jobs to bid, a calibrated order book target of 2.5-3.5 times billings, net debt to equity around 0.49x, and an explicit decision to protect cash rather than chase revenue in the March quarter. Talent depth scores 2 as well, with senior management averaging 23 years of service, an employee-ownership heritage and a top-rated employer award drawn purely from voluntary staff reviews. The single best financial trait is that cash comes in 283 days before suppliers have to be paid, so customers effectively fund the business. Set against that, the return on capital check scores -2: for every ₹100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings, the business generated ₹-1 of operating cash this year, a negative 1% cash return, and the order book shrank from ₹35,311 crores in June 2025 to ₹32,635 crores while FY26 new orders came in at ₹4,125 crores against ₹20,000 crores of guidance.

The multibagger screen explains why that combination does not compound: five of the eleven patterns are only PARTIAL and six are NO FIT, giving a fit score of 10. The recurring reason is that the same discipline that earns high management marks also caps growth. The deliberate 18-22% hit rate and the willingness to walk away from aggressively bid metro work is why the New Entrants risk is only a WATCH and why the industry-leading 11.7% EBITDA margin survived — but it is also exactly why the market-share check scores -1 and why Low Price Plus is a NO FIT, since this is selectivity rather than a scale-driven cost model, and FY26 showed fixed costs not being covered. The technical strengths run the same way. Real barriers exist in tunnel boring and marine work, 16-plus tunnel boring machines and the largest marine equipment base cannot be assembled quickly, and the reputation is measured — 8th globally in marine, 12th in bridges, eight consecutive MIKE innovation awards, national tunnelling records of 740 then 777 metres a month. Yet Brand Strength, Innovation Dominance and Cost to Replicate all stop at PARTIAL because every contract is re-competed on lowest price, which is also the basis of the Good Enough Goods risk: the client picks on price and a cheaper qualified bidder wins regardless of Afcons' delivery record. Pricing Power is a flat NO FIT — prices are set by tender, revenue fell 5.4%, the fourth quarter was a loss, and all FY27 revenue and margin guidance was withdrawn. Innovation shows up as a defence rather than an engine: no R&D line is ever disclosed, which is also why the Technological Innovation risk reads LOW, since the calls show steady small improvements and no platform shift threatening the business.

The risk review points at the same root cause the growth numbers already showed. Government Dependency is the one HIGH RISK: roughly 80% of the order book is government clients, split evenly between centre and state, and nearly every operating problem across seven calls traces back to policy or public budgets — election-related certification delays, the UP Jal Jeevan Mission payment stoppage, Maharashtra packages sent back for rebid purely to rediscover price. This connects directly to the balance sheet strength: the customers who fund the business through advances are the same ones who can stop paying, and those advances have been turning from a free funding source into an interest-bearing cost, with the interest-free share falling from 75% to around 60% by FY26 — one of the stated reasons Recurring Revenue is a NO FIT. Cyclicality is ELEVATED because all the revenue is other people's capital spending with no consumable or repeat element, which is how the company went from record profits and 20-25% growth guidance in August 2025 to a full-year revenue decline. Stakeholder Concentration is a WATCH because single orders of ₹5,700 crores and an average order size above ₹1,500 crores mean one relationship can swing the book. The overseas push is the deliberate answer to all of this — roughly 70 overseas projects, about 30% of turnover, a target mix of 70:30, entry into Europe via Croatia and a EUR 100 million Uganda road win — and it is the strongest supporting evidence for the PARTIAL verdict on market-share gains. But it is only a partial answer, because the two Croatia road tenders were later cancelled on budget grounds, showing that overseas public clients run on the same budget mechanics as domestic ones.
