# Tata Steel Ltd. (TATASTEEL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: METALS*

Tata Steel makes and sells steel — it runs its own mines and blast furnaces, turning iron ore and coking coal into crude steel and then into finished products like hot- and cold-rolled coil, coated and galvanised sheet, rebar, tubes, wires and tinplate. Its customers are carmakers and other manufacturers, construction and infrastructure contractors, railways, engineering firms, and retail/small builders who buy branded products through dealers and its online portals. It makes money on the gap between what steel sells for per ton and what it costs to produce (mainly raw materials, energy and conversion cost), so profits swing with steel prices, coking coal and iron ore costs; management reports EBITDA per ton as the key measure and has been pushing a large cost-cutting programme (about Rs 10,868 crores of savings in FY2026). It operates in three main places: India, which is the anchor (about 23 million tons of crude steel in FY2026 and roughly a 24% EBITDA margin), the Netherlands (IJmuiden, ~6.7 million tons of liquid steel) and the UK (~2.2 million tons of deliveries), with FY2026 consolidated EBITDA of Rs 34,848 crores on a ~15% margin.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Tata Steel India (Standalone)** — The core Indian steelworks — including Jamshedpur and the expanded Kalinganagar plant with its new 5 MTPA blast furnace, 6 MTPA pellet plant and cold rolling mill — producing around 23 million tons a year and described by management as 'the anchor of our performance', delivering Rs 24,431 crores of EBITDA in the first nine months of FY2026 at a ~24% margin.
- **Automotive & Special Products** — Sells high-end, higher-margin steel grades (ultra-high-strength and coated steels) to vehicle makers, plus certified grades for oil & gas and shipbuilding; it delivered best-ever quarterly and annual volumes in FY2026 and auto downstream products are now more than half of its sales mix.
- **Branded Products & Retail** — Consumer- and dealer-facing branded steel — Tata Tiscon rebar for construction and Tata Steelium cold-rolled sheet for small businesses — sold through a retail network and the Aashiyana and DigECA online channels, which together did about Rs 2,380 crores of gross merchandise value in one quarter, up 68% year on year.
- **Industrial Products, Projects & Export** — Supplies steel and ready-to-use construction solutions to engineering firms, infrastructure projects and Indian Railways (including orders for the Amrit Bharat Express), with engineering volumes up 28% YoY in one quarter and 35 construction service centres across India.
- **Downstream businesses (Tubes, Wires, Colors, Tinplate)** — Value-added products made from the company's own steel — tubes (which added 0.3 million tons of capacity and hit record volumes), wires, colour-coated sheet, and tinplate for cans such as edible-oil packaging — all reported at best-ever sales levels in FY2026.
- **Neelachal Ispat Nigam Limited (NINL)** — A previously shut-down Indian steel plant acquired in July 2022, now running at its rated capacity of about 1 million tons a year with a 13% EBITDA margin, and slated for further expansion.
- **Tata Steel Netherlands (IJmuiden)** — The Dutch steelworks producing about 6.7 million tons of liquid steel with ~6.1 million tons of deliveries in FY2026, including a packaging-steel line; its EBITDA nearly tripled to €210 million over the first nine months of FY2026 on heavy cost savings, though ageing coke and gas plants face emission problems and possible future closure.
- **Tata Steel UK (Port Talbot)** — The UK operation, around 2.2 million tons of deliveries, which shut both its blast furnaces and is spending £1.25 billion (with a £500 million UK government grant) to switch to a scrap-based electric arc furnace; still loss-making but losses narrowed by £135 million to about -£170 million in the first nine months of FY2026.
- **Raw materials and mining** — Captive mining of iron ore, coal and chrome ore plus pellet-making (including the acquisition of a 50.01% stake in Thriveni Pellets) that feeds the steel plants and is a big lever on cost, since coking coal and iron ore consumption costs drive most of the swings in margin per ton.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

**Capital Allocation — Neutral (+0.50)** · 6 of 6 checks had evidence

  > "our total spend on capex was about Rs 14,000 crores on a consolidated basis and we intend to increase the same in FY2027 to around Rs 20,000 crores of which more than 60% will be spend in India" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Advertisement and promotion — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: Brand and consumer-connect spend is visibly converting into volume growth in retail brands Tiscon and Steelium, though A&P quantum is never disclosed.
  > "Our well-established retail brand Tata Tiscon witnessed 20% YoY growth aided by enhanced reach and scale-up of consumer connect programs" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Research and development — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: R&D consistently yields new grades and products — 25+ new auto grade approvals, oil & gas/shipbuilding certifications, InQuik bridges, mobile bore pile cages, trivalent chromium packaging line — but R&D intensity is modest for a commodity producer.
  > "secured over 25 new grade approvals across ultra-high strength steels and coated products" — *management, on an earnings call*

- **Merger and acquisition — Neutral** *(from the conference calls)*: Deals are a mix of a distressed, non-'already strong' turnaround (NINL, shut for two years, now at ~27% EBITDA margin) and genuinely complementary bolt-ons (Thriveni Pellets, colour-coated stake, ISWP merger), so execution has been decent but the acquiree-quality test is not met.
