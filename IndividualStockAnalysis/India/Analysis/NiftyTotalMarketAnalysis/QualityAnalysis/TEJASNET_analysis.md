# Tejas Networks (TEJASNET) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Tejas Networks is an Indian company that designs and builds telecom networking equipment — the hardware and software that carriers use to move data across their networks. It sells two broad families of products: wireline/optical gear (WDM optical transport, IP/MPLS routers, switching, GPON/FTTx broadband equipment) and wireless gear (4G/5G radio access network equipment, massive MIMO radios, and a 4G/5G core), plus satellite/SatCom IoT products through its Saankhya Labs subsidiary. Its customers are telecom operators and government network projects — most importantly BSNL's nationwide 4G/5G rollout and BharatNet — along with private Indian telcos, critical-infrastructure and data-centre buyers, and international operators; it makes money by selling this equipment on large, lumpy project orders and tenders (revenue is tracked against an order book), supplemented by government PLI manufacturing incentives (about INR 469 crores for FY25). It manufactures largely in India through contract manufacturers, invests heavily in in-house R&D and patents (676 filed cumulatively by Q4 FY26), and revenue is dominated by India — roughly 85-88% in FY26 — with the rest from international markets in Africa, Europe, Latin America, Asia and the Middle East.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Wireline (optical transport, routing/switching and broadband access)** — Optical WDM/DWDM transport systems, IP/MPLS and MPLS-TP routers, Ethernet switching and GPON/FTTx broadband access equipment sold to telcos, BharatNet, critical infrastructure and data-centre customers; this is the older, core business and in FY26 management said quarterly revenue was 'driven largely by the sale of wireline products' to India private and international customers.
- **Wireless (4G/5G RAN)** — Radio access network equipment — base stations, 5G massive MIMO radios and a cloud-native converged 4G/5G core — entered as a new segment around FY24 on the back of the BSNL 4G/5G contract (over 100,000 sites went live by FY26); it drove the record FY24 revenue but by FY26 was mostly trials, POCs and early international orders rather than large revenue.
- **BSNL 4G/5G programme (including the MAAN backhaul network)** — The single largest project in the company's history — supplying 4G/5G radio equipment plus roughly 15,000 indigenous IP/MPLS routers for BSNL's MAAN backhaul network — booked as 'India private' revenue because the immediate customer is TCS; it took the order book above INR 8,200 crores in FY24, and its wind-down (plus a delayed 18,000-site add-on order) is a main reason revenue fell to INR 1,103 crores with a INR 909 crore loss in FY26.
- **Saankhya Labs (satellite / SatCom IoT subsidiary)** — Acquired in mid-2022 (63.43% initially, later merged in), it adds chip and satellite capability — satellite transponders for the vessel tracking system project and what management calls the world's largest satellite IoT vehicle-tracking network — and qualified for semiconductor PLI incentives; small relative to the main equipment business.
- **International business** — Sales outside India — a GPON manufacturing and supply partnership with Telecom Egypt, WDM backbone wins in Africa, MPLS-TP for a Southeast Asian power utility, plus 4G/5G RAN trials in Europe, Latin America and the Americas routed partly through partnerships with NEC and Rakuten Symphony; it was about 10% of FY24 revenue and 12-15% in FY26, and is the segment management points to as the main growth and path-to-profitability driver.
- **Data centre connectivity** — A newer demand area management attributes to AI-driven traffic growth — data-centre interconnect products (including a hyperscalable platform launched at MWC), a nationwide multi-terabit DWDM network for a hyperscaler in India, and a first win for switching inside a sovereign data centre; described as an emerging driver rather than a reported segment.
- **PLI / government incentives** — Production-linked and design-led manufacturing incentives from India's Department of Telecom, which pay out against domestic manufacturing and R&D spend — INR 469 crores received for FY25 alone, making them a meaningful support to reported earnings rather than a product line.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 3 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is entirely tied to customer capex programmes (BSNL 4G/5G build, telco 5G rollouts, BharatNet), not to customer operations, and the swing is extreme: FY24 revenue of INR2,471cr and PAT +63cr, then FY26 revenue of INR1,103cr with a PAT loss of INR909cr after one project order slipped. Management itself calls FY26 a 'transition' year after the 'massive BSNL project in FY25', with inventory of INR2,438cr and net debt INR3,531cr built for an order that never arrived — the classic over-investment-at-the-peak damage.

> "this has been a year of transition for us after the execution of the massive BSNL project in FY25, which gave us a significant amount of revenues... several large customer projects... have got delayed, which resulted in significant revenue shortfall and the resulting financial loss" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Management did not cut R&D or capacity through the downturn ('we had not cut down on the investments during the year'), a countercyclical stance, and is deliberately shifting mix toward private telco and international wireline with shorter working-capital cycles.*

- ⚠️ Year-to-year sales growth swings by ±98 percentage points — boom-and-bust revenue, not a steady compounder.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from -62% to 22% (swinging ±24.8 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 80% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 16% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Technological Innovation — HIGH RISK
*"Out-invented by the industry"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 2 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** The company sits in a high-innovation-rate industry where the platform resets every few years — 4G to 5G to 5G-Advanced to 6G, Open RAN, and now an AI-driven 'network infrastructure build super-cycle' that management says will make current CSP networks insufficient; it must fund continuous redesign regardless of revenue, which is precisely why losses persist.
