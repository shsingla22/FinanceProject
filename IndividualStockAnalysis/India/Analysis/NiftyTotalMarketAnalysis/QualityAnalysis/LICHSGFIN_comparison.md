### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 2 worsened, 6 unchanged. Financial resilience: Unknown on the long view, Unknown on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The product is undifferentiated and the customer chooses purely on rate — management says the market is rate-conscious, watched Rs 4,014 crore of balance transfers leave in one quarter when banks offered 7.50%, and admits it cannot hold a premium, having been forced down to 7.15% and to accept 4.4% growth to defend a 2.68% NIM. There is no brand or niche protection in the prime salaried segment where it competes head-on with larger, cheaper-funded banks. (The long view had said: The product is an undifferentiated prime mortgage where the borrower chooses on headline rate alone, brand loyalty is minimal and intermediaries/aggregators direct the choice — evidenced by INR12,778 crores of BT-out in FY26 and management repeatedly cutting price (7.50% then 7.15%, a 25bp book-wide cut, discounted 'rewriting' at new-loan rate +50bp) purely to hold existing customers. Management concedes it is trapped in the segment banks price most aggressively and can only defend margin by conceding growth, the classic Nobel Biocare dynamic of premium erosion in a non-complex product.)
- **New Entrants**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: The repo-linked bank pricing model has structurally reset prime home-loan economics against a PLR-linked HFC: management discusses the rate war defensively in all four calls, Rs 4,014 crore of balance-transfer-out in Q2 alone stripped ~Rs 2,000 crore from the book, and it repeatedly framed rate cuts (7.50% then 7.15%) as a forced competitive response, ending FY26 with 4.4% growth against double-digit guidance while conceding growth is migrating to self-employed/affordable players. (The long view had said: The repo-linked pricing regime turned banks into structurally cheaper competitors (CASA-funded cost of ~5-5.5% versus LICHFL's 6.94% incremental), resetting economics in the prime salaried segment; management's response has been repeated price investment — rates cut to 7.50% then 7.15%, a 25bp across-the-book cut and discounted 'rewriting' — while affordable-focused HFC peers take the growth. These are incumbents operating under new pricing mechanics rather than genuinely new-format entrants, which bounds the risk.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
