# Five-Star Bus.Fi (FIVESTAR) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Five-Star Business Finance is an Indian non-bank lender (an NBFC, listed as FIVESTAR on the NSE and BSE, with its registered office in Chennai) that has been making small-ticket loans for about 20 years to small shopkeepers, self-employed people and cash-salaried workers — borrowers who have no formal documentary proof of income or credit history, which management describes as a niche, less-crowded segment. Every loan is fully secured (management repeatedly contrasts itself with unsecured lenders), and the company builds its own capability to assess these borrowers' cash flows and integrity and to collect on their earn-and-pay cycle. It makes money on the spread: it lends at high fixed rates (about 24% yield historically, cut by 200 bps from 1 November 2024 to roughly 22.5% on new loans) while borrowing from banks, mutual funds, NCDs, market-linked debentures, securitisation/PTC deals and ECBs at far lower cost (about 10.35% in FY23, down to roughly 8.8–9.1% by 2026), leaving a spread near 13.9%. It runs a branch-heavy, feet-on-the-street model that grew from 369 branches and a INR 6,242 crore loan book in December 2022 to about 856 branches, over 500,000 active loan customers and an INR 13,722 crore book by June 2026, with newer branch additions coming largely from states such as Maharashtra.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Small-ticket secured lending to small businesses / self-employed and cash-salaried borrowers** — The single core product — fully secured, fixed-rate loans averaging a small ticket size to shopkeepers, self-employed and cash-salaried customers with no formal income documents; the calls describe no other revenue line, so this is effectively 100% of the business, growing from INR 6,242 crore AUM (Dec 2022) to INR 13,722 crore (Jun 2026).
- **Branch network (including "split branches")** — The distribution engine: physical branches do the sourcing, underwriting and collection, and management deliberately splits busy branches into smaller ones to cut concentration risk in any one location — count went from 369 (Dec 2022) to 856 (Jun 2026), with 113 opened in the September 2024 quarter alone (32 new plus 77 splits).
- **Collections vertical** — A dedicated in-house collections organisation that management built out heavily after asset quality wobbled in FY26 — 678 business and collection officers added in the December 2025 quarter alone, taking collection officers from 1,329 (Dec 2024) to 2,452 (Dec 2025), plus a full collections hierarchy up to head-office level; it is treated as central to keeping collection efficiency in the 96–98% range and is the reason growth was deliberately slowed.
- **Borrowings / liability franchise (treasury)** — The funding side of the spread, run as a deliberate diversification effort away from banks — roughly 50 lenders in FY23 with banks at 56%, later spread across NCDs, market-linked debentures, securitisation/PTC deals subscribed by mutual funds (Nippon, Kotak, HDFC, HSBC) and ECBs; ratings improved to AA- (India Ratings) and cost of funds fell from about 10.35% to 8.80%, with liquidity buffers of roughly INR 1,000–2,276 crore held on the balance sheet.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## The verdict: Decent — 61 out of 100 ★★★☆☆

In one breath: the quality framework finds a good business today (79/100); it strongly fits 1 of the 11 patterns long-term winners share; the risk review found nothing severe. Weighing those together gives 61 out of 100 — decent.

**The exact arithmetic:** Overall = 45% × 79 (business quality) + 30% × 15 (multibagger fit) + 25% × 84 (risk safety) = 61 out of 100 → Decent (3 stars).

- **Business quality (79/100):** The 34-check quality framework scored the business +1.15 on its −2 (poor) to +2 (excellent) scale, with 71% of checks backed by evidence; mapped onto 0–100 that is 79 points.

### The story in depth

Five-Star's whole story runs through one asset: it does everything itself, on the ground, close to the borrower. The quality work scores Competitive Advantage at the top of the scale on the strength of 856 branches (up from 369 in December 2022), a collections force of 2,452 officers nearly doubled year-on-year and deliberately split out from the business side, and a split-branch model built to stay near borrower concentrations. That same fact is what the multibagger screen calls a partial fit on Forward Integrators — originating and collecting through its own network instead of through outside agents — and what it calls the Cost to Replicate barrier: 20-plus years of judging cash flows for borrowers who have no documentary credit history, a barrier the record says was validated when over-borrowed customers chose to repay Five-Star first during the FY25-26 stress. The economics that came out of this were exceptional for a lender: asset yields around 24%, return on assets of 8.6%, and a gross spread near 13.9%, all earned with very little borrowing against equity (INR 7,653 crore of net worth against INR 13,722 crore of loans outstanding).

The strongest and most consistent finding across all three reviews is the behaviour of the people running the company, and it is the only pattern rated a full fit. Across 14 calls management put out bad news before it had to — the September 2024 bureau check that showed 14% of borrowers were over-borrowed and 30% overlapped with microfinance lenders, growth running below its own guidance, and an admitted borrower behaviour crisis in Q3FY26. It reports credit costs before recoveries, refuses cosmetic clean-ups such as technical write-offs or selling bad loans to asset reconstruction companies, and declines to promise growth until collections genuinely recover. That candour is also why the risk review is comparatively calm: three of the eight risk channels come back low, and the reason is affirmative evidence rather than absence of information — half a million borrowers with falling average loan sizes, funding spread across banks, bonds, pass-through certificates, securitisation and a USD 100 million ADB line, and demand for this kind of credit described as never having been the constraint. The pause in growth was the company's own choice on the supply side, not customers walking away. Management even leaned into the downturn, adding 35 branches and 678 collection officers in a single quarter while cutting disbursements.

The tension is that the very thing that made the returns attractive is what is now attracting company and pressure, and this is where the low multibagger score of 15 out of 100 comes from. High yields, an 8.6% return on assets and near-14% spreads are exactly the setup the risk review flags under Good Enough Goods and New Entrants: the description shifted from a niche segment that is less crowded in February 2023 to management conceding by July 2026 that competitors are forcing it to pay up for staff, with costs as a share of assets rising from about 5% to about 6% and the benefit of scale explicitly given up. Pricing is going the wrong way — a deliberate 200 basis point rate cut in November 2024 and guided further compression of 10 to 20 basis points every quarter through FY27, toward yields of about 22.25% — so the screen scores Pricing Power as no fit and the quality check on intangible benefits only a point, since the margin came from scarcity rather than brand. Spreads are being held up by cheaper borrowing (cost of funds falling from 9.63% to 9.12% to 8.80%, fresh debt at 8.19%) rather than by any ability to charge more, which is why Low Price Plus is only half present. Meanwhile the credit cycle has taken a real bite: credit costs went from 27 basis points in Q3FY23 to 1.34%, then 1.44%, then 1.85%, and return on assets fell from 8.6% to roughly 7 to 8%. Add the regulator's hand on the economics — an RBI rule change that added 29 basis points of bad loans overnight, and a board that cut its own growth guidance citing regulators' views — and the growth score of 0.6 makes sense. The combined rating of 61 out of 100, or Decent, is the arithmetic of a genuinely good, honestly run business (quality 79, risk safety 84) whose ability to compound at the old rates is not evident in the record; resilience is marked unknown.
