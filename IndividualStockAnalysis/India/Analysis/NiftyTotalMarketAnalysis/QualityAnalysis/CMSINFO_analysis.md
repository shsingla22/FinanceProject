# CMS Info Systems (CMSINFO) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

CMS Info Systems is an India-only outsourcing company that banks, ATM operators and retailers hire to move, count and look after their physical cash, and to build and run the machines and technology around it. Its people and armoured-van network collect cash from shops, petrol pumps and NBFC branches, transport it between bank branches and vaults, and refill roughly 140,000 service touch points across more than 16,000 pin codes; alongside this it deploys and manages ATMs for banks, and sells camera/sensor-based remote monitoring, AI surveillance and payment-technology services. It earns money mainly as recurring annuity service fees — historically per-transaction or per-ATM charges, though management says it has now shifted deliberately to fixed-price contracts — plus some one-time revenue from selling hardware. FY2026 revenue was Rs 2,487 crore, of which Rs 2,312 crore was recurring service revenue, with the largest single customer at 18% of revenue.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Cash Management / Cash Logistics** — Physically handles currency for clients through three lines — ATM cash replenishment, retail cash pickup from shops and outlets, and currency-in-transit between bank branches and vaults; it is the older and larger half of the company (about 70% of revenue at IPO, roughly 55% by FY2026) and management says it gained 200 bps of market share in FY2026.
- **Managed Services & Technology Solutions** — Builds, deploys and runs ATMs and technology for banks — including brown-label ATMs (BLA) owned and operated by CMS, remote monitoring and AI/IoT surveillance systems, and software — grown internally from nothing to about Rs 800 crore by FY2024 and roughly 45% of revenue by FY2026, with CMS moving from the industry's number five to number three position.
- **ATM Management Solutions (platform, post-FY2026 restructure)** — The combined ATM-facing business — cash filling plus machine deployment and upkeep — now run as one platform focused on higher-yield, fixed-price contracts; its biggest single win is the SBI cash outsourcing deal for about 10,000 ATMs, worth Rs 1,000 crore over ten years (Rs 500 crore of it new revenue).
- **Retail and Currency Logistics (platform, post-FY2026 restructure)** — Direct-to-retail cash collection and reconciliation for organised retail, fuel outlets, airlines, NBFCs, travel and hospitality clients, now being expanded into tier-3 and tier-4 towns using a 'gig' partner model of 2,000-plus partners covering 20% of retail points.
- **Tech and Payment Solutions (platform, post-FY2026 restructure)** — The software, remote-monitoring SaaS and payments piece, described as growing from Rs 235 crore to Rs 330 crore of revenue in one year and targeted at Rs 400 crore in FY2027 — roughly a 30% annual growth rate, the fastest-growing part of the company.
- **Remote Monitoring / AIoT RMS** — A subscription service that watches ATM sites, bank branches, vaults and gold-loan vaults remotely using cameras, sensors and AI models; started during COVID as the smallest business (15,000 ATM sites live by mid-2022) and has since scaled to large mandates such as a 2,000-location integrated surveillance build-and-operate project for a leading bank.
- **Acquisitions (FY2026)** — Two deals were signed: Securens, to scale the vision-AI monitoring business (closed, integrated, expected to add to profit in FY2027), and FSS's managed services business, to consolidate the ATM managed-services market and open cross-selling into midsized private banks.
- **Bullion management (incubation)** — A very small new venture being incubated internally, repeatedly described by management as early-stage with no meaningful revenue yet.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

CMS Info Systems comes out of these three reviews as a well-run company in an awkward industry, and the combined rating says exactly that: 69 out of 100, or Strong, built from a solid 74 on business quality and a high 87 on multibagger fit, dragged down by a low 38 on risk safety. The quality work scores management and capital allocation highest - candour (management called an unscheduled analyst meeting mid-crisis and itemised each revenue miss: Rs25cr from weak consumption, Rs100cr from a delayed SBI decision, Rs30cr from AGS), discipline (no money raised at IPO, Rs650cr of net cash, Rs438cr of dividends over five years and a Rs168cr buyback at Rs340 approved only after the worst year in the company's history), and independence (walking away from a won Rs700cr contract worth about Rs75cr a year rather than accept the transaction-fee model, then pushing the whole industry to fixed fees). The same framework scores the business itself much lower - return on capital at 0.0, industry structure at 0.67, and competitive advantage and customer benefits at 1.0 each. Good stewards, difficult economics.

The patterns and the risks are largely the same facts read from two directions. Market Share Gainers is a strong fit - touch points from 115,000 to over 140,000, 200bps gained in cash logistics, a move from #5 to #3 in managed services, 36% of BFSI vision-AI monitoring - but the share is available because the industry is in distress: AGS exited outright, a key competitor wound down, and FSS sold its ATM management business to CMS. That same shakeout is why New Entrants is rated a Low risk. The physical network is genuinely hard to copy (20 years to build 70,000 ATMs and 65,000 retail points across 16,000+ pin codes), yet Cost to Replicate is only a partial fit and Good Enough Goods stays on the watch list for the same reason: the network keeps rivals out but does not protect price, and in FY24-25 competitors bid transaction-fee tenders below economic levels, forcing CMS to sit out and prune unprofitable retail points. Pricing Power is likewise downgraded to a likely fit - real in FY22-24 when compliance and inflation-linked increases drove roughly half of growth, working against the company in FY25-26. The Brand Strength that wins marquee long-tenure mandates from SBI, ICICI and HDFC is the same incumbency behind the Stakeholder Concentration high risk, where SBI alone was 22% of revenue and Rs750cr of order book sits with just two customers.

The FY26 setback shows how these threads pull on each other. Management's counter-cyclical instinct - dialling capex down to Rs100cr when tenders were unattractive and back up to Rs350cr 'in the middle of the storm' - is scored a strength under disciplined stewardship, and it is also precisely what produced the Cyclicality risk: routes were built ahead of an SBI contract that slipped, and services growth fell to 6% with EBITDA down 5% and profit down 20%. Regulation cuts both ways too: RBI rules drove about half of the pricing-led growth in FY23-24, and RBI interchange rates decide whether outsourcing deals clear at all, which is why Government Dependency is a high risk even with approved interchange hikes now in hand. The offsets are real. Roughly 93% of FY26 revenue (Rs2,312cr of Rs2,487cr) is annuity services under a Rs2,000cr order book that is now entirely fixed-fee, cash returns from customers 296 days before suppliers must be paid, and Rs40cr of tech spend (1.6% of revenue) sustained through a loss-making year has produced Hawkai from zero to Rs200cr and RMS from zero to 50,000 sites - the main answer to the technology and cash-usage risks, both of which sit at Watch rather than higher. The catch, flagged in the Recurring Revenue pattern, is that this annuity stream is capital-hungry, so the quality of the revenue depends on capex converting on schedule. Overall resilience is judged Sound; the case rests on execution through a cyclical trough, not on a protected franchise.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, Stakeholder Concentration, Cyclicality. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.1× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Unit economics are set by the RBI: the ATM interchange rate determines whether outsourcing deals clear ('discovered price was higher than interchange'), RBI cash-logistics compliance norms drove roughly half of pricing-led growth in FY23-24, PSU bank tenders are a rising 22% of revenue, and a new labour code forced an 11.1cr one-off provision. Management repeatedly describes waiting on policy decisions before growth can resume.

> "There also has been a fair amount of discussion on the ATM interchange hike... and we await a positive policy decision in the coming months... And if this interchange increase happens, I think that will give an impetus to ATM growth" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate diversification away from policy-driven ATM revenue — private banks plus direct-to-retail up 24%→30%, tech & payment solutions to 16% of revenue*

### Stakeholder Concentration — HIGH RISK
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 1 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** One customer (SBI) was 22% of revenue and 65-70% of the BLA estate, and the delay of a single SBI RFP cost Rs.100 Crores of FY26 revenue — the largest single driver of the year's miss; a further Rs.750 Crores of order book sits with just two customers (ICICI, IPPB), and the AGS collapse in the MSP channel forced provisions and service curtailment. Concentration is being reduced but remains material.

> "The SBI cash outsourcing delay caught us off guard with a significant investment in routes and infrastructure. This RFP closure delayed from February to April to December had a huge Rs. 100 Crores revenue impact for us in the year." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Broadening base: largest customer down from 22% to 18% of revenue, MSP-partner revenue down 35%→29%, retail direct logos expanded, customer mix shifted 58:42 to 48:52 in one year*

- ⚠️ Customers now take about 123 days to pay, up from 80 — lengthening collection terms are how weak bargaining power against concentrated customers shows up in the numbers.

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Revenue swings with bank ATM deployment cycles and consumption: FY26 services growth fell to 6% with EBITDA -5% and PAT -20% after management over-invested in routes ahead of an SBI contract that slipped, and capex swung 200cr→100cr→350cr across FY22-FY26. > "In hindsight, we invested ahead of this contract and our anticipation on things becoming normal was very aggressive given the subsequent delays to that RFP. This has been a big learning for us." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Annuity/flow revenue (services 2,312cr of 2,487cr; fungible routes serving daily operations); countercyclical capital allocation — cut capex to 100cr when RFP pricing was irrational, raised it to 350cr 'in the middle of the storm'*

## What to watch

- SBI and the two-customer order book (Stakeholder Concentration, high risk): another slipped tender or a step-down at renewal would repeat the Rs100cr FY26 hit and undo the diversification progress from 22% to 18% of revenue.
- RBI interchange and PSU tender flow (Government Dependency, high risk): the approved interchange hikes must actually translate into deals clearing at economic prices, or the cyclical-trough recovery thesis loses its main driver.
- Fixed-fee discipline holding across the industry (Pricing Power pattern and Good Enough Goods risk): if rivals resume bidding transaction-fee tenders below economic levels, CMS again chooses between share and margin.
- Conversion of the Rs350cr counter-cyclical capex into revenue (Cyclicality risk and the Cyclical market growth check): management called the Q3 FY26 margin bottom and beat its Q4 target - a stalled recovery would mean the routes were built ahead of demand a second time.
- Hawkai, RMS and tech and payments as a share of revenue (Innovation Dominance pattern, Technological Innovation watch): the Rs200cr line and the 7% to 16% revenue mix shift need to keep compounding to offset cassette-swap, offsite ATM contraction and digital payment migration.
