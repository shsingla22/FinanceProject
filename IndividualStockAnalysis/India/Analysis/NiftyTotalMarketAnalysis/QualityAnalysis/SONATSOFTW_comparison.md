### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 4 worsened, 1 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Sonata is a mid-tier services firm selling against larger vendors and, increasingly, against AI tooling that lets clients do more in-house; management repeatedly positions on differentiation ('one of the first mid-tier firms with enterprise-grade agentic AI workflows') while clients rationalise budgets and ramp down, and CSP-bundled deal costs diluted Q4 margins. The core offering — modernisation engineering — is not structurally hard for cheaper offshore or AI-native challengers to approximate. (The long view had said: The domestic licence-reselling business is a low-value-add intermediary position that the vendor can bypass — gross contribution degrew 3.5% in FY26 even as revenue grew 5.9%, after an OEM partner took large customers direct; the international side shows less commoditisation pressure, with price increases achieved rather than conceded.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The AI platform shift is reshaping the economics of the labour-based IT services model Sonata sells: AI moved from 8% to 18% of order book in four quarters, headcount fell to 6,283 from 6,404 while utilisation rose to 91.8%, and margin gains were explicitly attributed to 'sustainable productivity improvement... enabled by AI adoption' — i.e. the same work delivered with fewer people. The Board framed the CEO succession itself around the shift, appointing the architect of the AI strategy because 'the tech world is adopting itself to the new paradigm shift to the AI space'. (The long view had said: The AI platform shift is discussed in every recent call as both opportunity and threat to a headcount-linked services model — headcount fell from 6,649 to 6,283 while revenue stayed flat, margins rose on 'AI-led productivity', and the Board replaced the CEO explicitly to have an AI-strategy architect lead 'the new world of AI'; legacy modernisation demand cuts both ways when AI can also rewrite legacy code cheaply.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Microsoft is both the largest partner and an identified structural risk, named in the very first call as a headwind ('potentially Microsoft going direct for large clients') and confirmed as realised damage by Q4 FY26, where the domestic business lost a large client renewal to a direct Microsoft relationship and FY26 domestic gross contribution fell 3.5%. Client concentration is also meaningful with top 10 clients at 54% of international revenue, and the growth engine (Dynamics, Fabric, CSP AI co-sell) still runs through one vendor's channel. (The long view had said: Top 5 clients were 54% of revenue in FY23 and top 10 remain 54-55% through FY26, with three of the top 10 (largest BFSI, largest TMT, a large retail client) simultaneously ramping down and dictating the growth trajectory; separately the whole franchise leans on one partner, Microsoft, across Dynamics, Fabric and CSP, and the domestic business was hit when that OEM began billing large customers directly.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: NO SIGNAL → WATCH. Why now: Client buying preference is visibly migrating from headcount-based services to AI-consumption and outcome-led models, and Sonata has had to re-plumb its go-to-market around it — aligning with 'Microsoft's new AI consumption model, which went effective July 2025' and closing CSP-bundled AI deals whose cost partially offset Q4 margin gains. The demand base for traditional FTE-linked delivery is eroding even as execution holds, visible in falling headcount alongside flat-to-down dollar revenue. (The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and the only computed fingerprint is clean.)
- **Fashion Risk**: NO SIGNAL → LOW. Why now: Demand rests on multi-year enterprise modernisation and managed-services contracts — core lending platforms, digital wallets, mortgage systems, Java support — which are functional, contracted, repeat-purchase engagements rather than taste-driven. Nothing in the four calls attributes growth to a trending product or brand moment. (The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and all 2 computed fingerprints are clean.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (NO SIGNAL).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
