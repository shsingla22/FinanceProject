**Checks that improved:**

- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: The opposite of obscure — one of India's largest NBFCs, freshly IPO'd with brokerage-hosted analyst calls and separate media calls covered by ET, Bloomberg, Mint and Moneycontrol. | The long view had said: The opposite of under-the-radar — a headline IPO, one of India's best-known corporate brands, and coverage from IIFL, Citi, Kotak, Ambit and Emkay plus a dedicated national media call.
- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Neutral. Now: The Tata Motor Finance merger is a large related-party deal, not a small bolt-on, and the acquiree was weak — its book degrew 6% sequentially and it only reached break-even in Q3 FY26 with a modest INR 43 crore PAT in Q4, though integration and risk alignment appear on track. | The long view had said: The Tata Motors Finance merger was large (12–13% of book), an already-weak captive lender whose book shrank from INR 26,694 cr to ~INR 23,698 cr and which diluted consolidated RoA (1.9% vs 2.2% ex-MF) and raised GNPA to 2.2% — the opposite of a small bolt-on with an already-strong acquiree, though execution to break-even in Q3 was creditable.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Commentary is metric- and process-led with no personality promotion; the CEO speaks in terms of business-line delivery and even deflects AI hype by crediting underlying credit policy rather than the tool. | The long view had said: Communication is numbers-led and free of celebrity posturing, but the company just completed a marquee IPO and now runs separate media calls with ET, Bloomberg, Mint, PTI and Moneycontrol — a deliberately high-profile posture.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: Accessibility is the strongest customer benefit here — ~1,500 branches across 27 states skewed to Tier 2+, 8.4 million customers, and AI-driven turnaround times cutting SME credit memos from two days to 20 minutes. | The long view had said: Combining ~1,500 branches skewed to Tier 2 and beyond with near-total digital fulfilment gives genuinely superior accessibility for 8.4 million customers, though larger NBFCs and banks match this reach.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: They give explicit quantified guidance and track against it, separate out one-offs (labour code INR 44 crore, PE exit gain), added a new unsecured-retail disclosure slide voluntarily, and openly discussed the 18-month stress period — but stop short of explicitly owning any mistake. | The long view had said: Consistently specific and self-disclosing — a basis-point-level bridge of the RoA path, proactively added disclosure slides, flagged Motor Finance book decline, integration costs and the labour-code hit, and admitted Q2 momentum faded rather than spinning it.

*Unchanged: Asset turn; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Technology advantage; Structural end-market growth; Barriers to entry and rationality; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Intangible benefits; Mini-monopolies; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Assurance benefits; Distribution advantage; Halo effect (caution); Customer types (retail vs corporate); People matter (talent development); Gain in market share; Cyclical market growth; Share donators; Research and development; Network effects; Geographical expansion; Disciplined stewards.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 5 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared.

*Foundation test: FAIL on the long view, FAIL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Across all three FY26 calls the Tata name is cited as the core franchise asset — driving borrower acquisition (8.4m customers), lender confidence and the AAA/BBB ratings that keep funding costs low, and stretched into adjacencies (affordable housing, merged Tata Motor Finance). (The long view had said: The Tata name is cited as a genuine franchise asset with both lenders and borrowers and underpins the AAA/low funding cost, but there is no premium pricing, no A&P investment and management explicitly notes group-linked lending is under 3% of the book — so brand supports trust and funding, not price.)

**That weakened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The bank-variant traits are visible in FY26: AAA-backed low-cost funding (cost of funds down to 7.2% in Q3), cost-to-income improving ~335bps to 38.3% on tech-driven productivity, and high loan quality (credit cost 0.9%, net NPA 0.5%) — with rate-cut savings explicitly passed to borrowers. But there is no evidence of a deep, hard-to-copy cost culture or no-frills positioning, and funding is wholesale rather than cheap deposits. (The long view had said: Classic bank variant: cheap funding from the AAA rating (cost of funds 7.4%→7.2%, S&P upgrade to BBB), a falling cost base (cost-to-income 42%→38.3%, ~335bps in one year, guided to 33-34% by FY28), and high-quality loans (consolidated credit cost down to 0.9% in Q4, net NPA 0.5%) — with the savings explicitly passed to borrowers rather than kept.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
