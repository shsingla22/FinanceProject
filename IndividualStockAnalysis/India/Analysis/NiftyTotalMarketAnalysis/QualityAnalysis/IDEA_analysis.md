# Vodafone Idea Ltd. (IDEA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: TELECOM*

Vodafone Idea Limited (brand name "Vi") is an Indian mobile phone network operator — it sells wireless voice, SMS and mobile internet connectivity to consumers and businesses across India, using radio spectrum it buys at government auctions and a network of 4G and 5G sites it builds and runs. Most of its money comes from monthly recharges and plans bought by individual mobile subscribers, measured on the calls as ARPU (average revenue per user), which rose from Rs. 128 in the June 2022 quarter to Rs. 190 in the March 2026 quarter, across a subscriber base that fell from about 240 million in 2022 to 192.8 million by March 2026. It operates only in India, and concentrates its spending on 17 "priority circles" (licence regions) that management says generate about 98–99% of revenue. Full-year FY26 revenue was Rs. 44,873 crore with cash EBITDA of Rs. 9,217 crore; alongside the operating business, the calls devote heavy attention to raising equity and debt and to settling large government AGR dues, finalised at Rs. 64,046 crore.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Consumer mobile — prepaid** — Pay-as-you-go mobile plans sold to individuals (e.g. the "Non-Stop Hero" unlimited-data plan and the "Vi Guarantee" extra-validity offer), which the calls describe as the main engine of new customer acquisition, renewals and upgrades and therefore the bulk of the business.
- **Consumer mobile — postpaid** — Monthly-billed plans for higher-spending customers, such as Vi Max and the REDX / REDX Family plans bundling unlimited 4G/5G data, international roaming and lifestyle perks; smaller than prepaid but growing, with eight straight quarters of positive net additions by Q4 FY26.
- **4G / 5G network and spectrum** — The underlying asset base the company keeps buying and building — spectrum bought in auctions (Rs. 188 billion committed in 2022, Rs. 35.1 billion in 2024), around 527,000 broadband sites by September 2025, 5G live in 80+ cities across all 17 5G circles by May 2026, funded by capex of roughly Rs. 16,000 crore over six quarters against a stated three-year plan of Rs. 500–550 billion.
- **Enterprise / business services ("Vi Business")** — Selling connectivity plus IoT, cloud, mobility, business communications, cybersecurity and fixed-line services to companies in banking, manufacturing, utilities, logistics and government; management repeatedly calls it a long-standing "strength area" and is adding a "Dedicated Enterprise Corridor" with ~1.3 Tbps of capacity across data centres, though no revenue figure is given.
- **Digital services and advertising** — Apps and platforms layered on top of the phone business — the Vi app (now with an AI recharge assistant), Vi Movies & TV, music/gaming/jobs/education tie-ups, and the "Vi Ads" ad-tech platform that lets marketers reach its subscriber base — positioned as extra monetisation and customer stickiness rather than a large revenue line.
- **International roaming** — Packs letting Indian travellers use their phone abroad, expanded from 98 to 151 countries, with truly unlimited data and calls in 40+ countries; management flags it as a key differentiator and focus area but gives no size.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## The verdict: Weak — 29 out of 100 ★☆☆☆☆

In one breath: the quality framework finds a weak business today (30/100); no winning pattern is strongly confirmed yet; the risk review found 1 high risk (Government Dependency). Weighing those together gives 29 out of 100 — weak.

### The story in depth

Vodafone Idea comes out of all three reviews as the same company seen from three angles: a subscale third mobile operator that has no protection from competition and stays alive on outside help. In the 34-check quality framework, Competitive Advantage scores the worst possible -2.0 as an area, with individual checks for mini-monopolies, network effects, switching costs, technology advantage and market share all at -2. The specifics behind those marks fit together: switching costs are near zero under mobile number portability, and the company has been on the losing side of that, shedding 5.2mn subscribers in a single quarter; 5G launched only in March 2025, years after peers, with legacy Chinese equipment blocked from upgrade; and every ₹1 of assets produces just ₹0.23 of sales, so it carries the cost structure of a heavy network business without the pricing protection one would expect to pay for it. Return on Capital (-0.67), Industry Structure (-1.17) and Capital Allocation (-0.6) all sit below zero, and the combined rating lands at 29 out of 100 — Weak, one star — with resilience marked STRAINED.

The multibagger screen finds no pattern that fully fits: five come back PARTIAL and six come back NO FIT, and the reasons repeat the quality findings almost line for line. The most important connection is around price. ARPU has risen for 19 straight quarters, from ₹128 to ₹190, which on its own looks like pricing power — but the screen rejects it, because the increases came from industry-wide tariff moves rather than anything the company controls, and they came alongside the loss of roughly 48mn subscribers since August 2022 (240.4mn in Q1FY23 down to 192.8mn in Q4FY26). The same fact is therefore simultaneously the company's best operating number and the evidence behind the -2 checks for market share, share donation and broken competition. The same doubling-back shows up elsewhere: brand heritage from Vodafone and Idea, sustained advertising and award-winning campaigns are real, but the screen calls Brand Strength only PARTIAL because share was lost every quarter until Q4FY26; prepaid and postpaid billing is genuinely recurring and paid upfront, but the installed base it recurs on has been shrinking; and the low price versus peers is judged a symptom of weakness rather than an engineered cost advantage, with cash EBITDA essentially flat between FY25 (₹9,198cr) and FY26 (₹9,217cr).

The risk review explains what has kept the company going despite all of that, and it is the same thing that disqualifies it on the quality and pattern tests. Government Dependency is the only HIGH RISK channel: spectrum commitments of ₹188bn in 2022 and ₹35.1bn in 2024, the DoT reform package, moratoria, conversion of ₹161.3bn of interest into government equity, and a ₹55,622cr AGR write-back. That state support is precisely why the screen rules out Cost to Replicate — survival rests on infusions and write-backs, not on a barrier a funded rival could not cross — and why stewardship scores -2 against gross debt of ₹1,990.8 billion in FY23. The dilution used to bridge the gap is the same reason the distribution-to-shareholders check scores -2: a ₹180bn FPO, preferential issues of ₹20.8bn and ₹24.6bn, and OCD conversion, with no dividend or buyback anywhere in the calls. The ₹24.6bn equity issued to Nokia and Ericsson also sits inside the Stakeholder Concentration risk (WATCH), where suppliers had to be paid in shares to secure rollout. Technological Innovation is ELEVATED for the same reason the technology check scores -2 — the installed base keeps turning legacy, with 20,200 3G sites shut in one quarter — and the delivery gap that makes Corporate Culture a NO FIT is visible in the numbers: a ₹500–550bn three-year capex plan that produced only ₹8,742cr of investment in FY26, and a ₹250bn debt facility described as in active discussions in August 2024 that was still unclosed in May 2026. The four channels rated WATCH or LOW — cyclicality, shifting consumer preferences, fashion risk, new entrants — are calm mainly because demand for connectivity is functional, repeat-purchase and growing (data traffic up 21–30% year-on-year, usage per subscriber up 27.2%), not because the company is strong; risk safety still scores 50 out of 100, the highest of the three inputs, and it is doing most of the work holding the overall score at 29.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) — PARTIAL
*"Sells once, earns forever"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (1 of 3 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Prepaid recharges and postpaid plans are inherently recurring and billed in advance, and management cites renewal/retention mechanics (Vi Guarantee validity credits, Non-Stop Hero driving "higher renewals and upgrades"), but across the 16 calls the installed base shrank from 240.4mn (Q1FY23) to 192.8mn (Q4FY26), and deferred revenue/customer advances are never discussed as a funding source — the recurring stream weakened rather than compounded.

> "provides 2-day extra validity credited on every unlimited voice recharge @ Rs.199, which results in 24 days of extra validity" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ About 16 days of cash is tied up in the trade cycle — customers are not funding the business.
- ❌ Capital spending is about 24% of sales — growth is capital-hungry.
- ✅ Operating cash was positive in 12 of 12 years — the reliable cash engine recurring-revenue businesses show.

### Friendly Middleman — PARTIAL
*"The expert sells it for you"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls are silent and the numbers are mixed (1 of 2 checks supporting).

- ❌ Operating margin of 42% but swinging ±9.5 points a year — too volatile for a protected franchise.
- ✅ Operating margin moved from about 32% (early years) to 41% (recent years) — the widening-profitability signature of pricing power / lock-in.

### Brand Strength — PARTIAL
*"The name itself sells"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (1 of 2 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** There is genuine heritage (Vodafone + Idea), sustained A&P and award-winning campaigns (Cannes Lion, CSK/IPL partnership, 'Be Someone's We'), but brand strength never converted into the required proof — share was lost every quarter until Q4FY26 and pricing sits at a discount to peers rather than a premium.

> "Vi won the prestigious Cannes Lion award for its human network -testing telecom network campaign" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Essentially no direct input costs — typical of software and internet platforms — leaving ample room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ❌ Operating margin of 42% but swinging ±9.5 points a year — too volatile for a protected franchise.

### Innovation Dominance — PARTIAL
*"Out-inventing the competition"*
