# Piramal Pharma (PPLPHARMA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Piramal Pharma is an India-headquartered pharmaceutical company that became a separately listed company (BSE/NSE: PPLPHARMA) after being demerged from Piramal Enterprises in 2022. Most of its money comes from making medicines for other drug companies under contract — developing and manufacturing drug ingredients and finished products at its own plants for big pharma, smaller biotech firms and generic companies — and from selling its own hospital drugs such as inhaled anaesthetics and injectable pain products, mainly in the US and other overseas markets. A smaller third leg sells branded over-the-counter health and personal-care products directly to Indian consumers through shops, e-commerce and its own website. It runs a global network of manufacturing sites across India (Turbhe, Pithampur, Mahad, Dahej), the US (Riverview, Lexington, Sellersville, Aurora) and the UK (Grangemouth), with quarterly revenue of about Rs 1,720 crore in Q2 FY23 rising to about Rs 2,270 crore in Q1 FY27, and EBITDA margins in the roughly 10%-13% range.

**The parts of the business:**

- **CDMO (Contract Development and Manufacturing Organisation)** — Develops and manufactures drugs and drug ingredients on contract for other pharma and biotech companies — including specialised work like sterile injectables, high-potency APIs, antibody drug conjugates (ADCs) and peptides, plus a generic API business — and it is the largest business, having grown at about 13.5% a year over a decade and 19% year-on-year in Q1 FY27, with the top 10 clients making up about 40% of its revenue.
- **Complex Hospital Generics (CHG)** — Sells its own hard-to-make hospital medicines — inhaled anaesthesia (it is one of few players able to make all four generations, and holds about 48% US market share in Sevoflurane), intrathecal baclofen (its Gablofen brand held about 77% US share) and injectable pain products such as Fentanyl — contributing revenue of Rs 743 crore in Q1 FY27, roughly a third of company sales, with a pipeline of about 27-37 injectable products in development.
- **India Consumer Healthcare** — Sells branded over-the-counter health, wellness and personal-care products to Indian consumers through more than 200,000 general-trade outlets plus modern trade, e-commerce and its own Wellify.in site, growing in the low-to-mid teens with 'Power Brands' rising to about 53% of its sales and e-commerce to about 28% by Q1 FY27, while spending roughly 12%-15% of its sales on advertising and trade promotion.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Piramal Pharma comes out of the three reviews as a genuinely dependable manufacturer that has not yet turned that dependability into good returns on the money it employs. The strongest single trait is reliability: the quality framework scores 'Assurance benefits' at +2 on the back of an unbroken zero-OAI record across a global network of 15-17 sites, all five USFDA inspections in nine months closed successfully, and customers explicitly buying supply continuity and dual sourcing. The same trait is what the pattern screen credits under 'Toll Roads' (20 regulatory inspections and 100+ customer audits in a half-year creating switching costs) and under 'Cost to Replicate' (the ability to make all four generations of inhalation anesthesia with backward integration at Dahej, plus an integrated ADC platform). It also shows up in the pricing the company can hold: after direct input costs it keeps Rs.63 of every Rs.100 of sales, scored +2 on 'Intangible benefits'. Competitive advantage, customer benefits and growth all score around or above 1.0 in the quality areas. But the flip side sits in the risk review: because the whole franchise is gated by inspection outcomes, 'Government Dependency' is on WATCH even though pricing is not regulator-set and customers are private. The one thing that makes the business hard to replace is also the one thing that could take it away.

The same doubling-up runs through the growth story. The 'land and expand' approach with large customers is part of what earns the Toll Roads verdict, and it is exactly what puts 'Stakeholder Concentration' on WATCH, with the top 10 CDMO clients at about 40% of CDMO revenue and management stating it intends to make large customers larger. In complex hospital generics the company already holds 48% of the US sevoflurane market, 77% of the US baclofen prefilled-syringe market with Gablofen, and #1 fentanyl brands in Japan, South Africa and Indonesia — impressive positions, but the screen calls 'Market Share Gainers' only PARTIAL precisely because those positions are being defended rather than extended, and the quality check on market share notes that dominance in CHG leaves little room left to take. What is attacking those positions is the highest-rated risk in the review: 'Good Enough Goods' is ELEVATED, because cheaper Chinese entrants in inhalation anesthesia — a product chosen by hospitals and procurement rather than a brand-aware buyer — stalled CHG growth for five to six quarters. The related 'New Entrants' risk is on WATCH, with management confirming in Jul 2026 that the Chinese competitive situation remains even as countermeasures start to work and CHG grew 17% in Q1 FY27. Half the revenue sits in CDMO, which swings with customer R&D funding cycles — delayed decisions and a lagging order book in Nov 2022, soft generic API demand in FY23, and growth in Jul 2026 credited to continued recovery in US bio-pharma funding — hence 'Cyclicality' on WATCH.

The clearest brand economics sit in the smallest place. Consumer healthcare spends a consistent 12-15% of its sales on media and trade promotion, and Power Brands have risen from 42% of segment sales to 53%, growing 23% year-on-year by FY27; e-commerce has gone from about 15% of consumer sales in FY23 to 28% growing 40% year-on-year, with a Wellify.in D2C platform. That effort is why 'Brand Strength' and 'Forward Integrators' register at all, and why 'Shifting Consumer Preferences' and 'Fashion Risk' are both LOW — but the screen is explicit that only the smallest of the three segments shows these economics, with roughly 80% of company revenue being B2B CDMO and hospital products. That is the pattern of the whole file: nine of eleven multibagger patterns come back PARTIAL and two are NO FIT, giving a multibagger fit of just 10 out of 100. The financial side explains why the overall grade stops at Decent (50 out of 100, three stars, with resilience SOUND): return on capital scores 0.0 and capital allocation 0.17. About 155 days of cash stays stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers (-2), there have been no buybacks and instead Rs.1,050 crore was raised in a rights issue at Rs.81 largely to repay debt (-1), and while growth capex is real and rising — a $157mn brownfield program in FY23 and a fresh $90mn ADC program at Riverview and Lexington — the return on that spending remains unproven. Sales have compounded at about 7% a year, very consistently, against a Nov 2022 guidance of 15% revenue growth that the culture pattern flags as a promise-delivery gap. Reliable, hard to displace, openly managed — but so far a business that consumes capital faster than it visibly rewards it.
