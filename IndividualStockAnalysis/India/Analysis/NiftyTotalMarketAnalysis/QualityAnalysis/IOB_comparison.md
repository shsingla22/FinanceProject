### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 1 weakened, 4 unchanged; 4 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 2 could not be compared.

*Foundation test: FAIL on the long view, FAIL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management delivered rising profit for ten straight quarters, treats guidance as a floor rather than a ceiling, and provisions conservatively — an extra Rs.800 crore standard provision described as an unneeded buffer plus Rs.1,500 crore forward-looking ECL, with PCR at 97.67%. Against that: 92.44% government ownership, a short leadership tenure (MD from FY2024, third chairman/MD change since FY2022), and no instance in these calls of management openly owning a mistake. (The long view had said: The promise-delivery record across the nine calls holds up: NIM guidance of 'above 3%' given in Aug 2025 was met at 3.3-3.4% a year later, 13-14% credit guidance was repeatedly beaten at ~20-24%, MTNL's Rs 2,300 crore exposure was 100% provided upfront in the quarter it slipped, DTA was steadily run down from Rs 6,000 crore to ~Rs 3,400 crore, and the ECL buffer promised in May 2026 was built 1,500→1,750→2,150 crore refusing the four-year regulatory dispensation; the one persistent ding is that SMA data is left out of the presentation and must be asked for every quarter.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 2 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Unknown on the long view, Unknown on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The asset and liability books are granular rather than name-driven: 83% of domestic advances are RAM (retail/agri/MSME) with only 17% corporate, bulk deposits are just 6% of domestic deposits, and funding comes from 46mn customers across 3,522 branches and 12,000+ BC points. No single borrower, depositor or counterparty is discussed across the four calls; the only concentration is the sovereign shareholder, captured under government dependency. (The long view had said: Single relationships repeatedly move the numbers: the Rs 2,300 crore MTNL exposure (the bank's highest) drove a Rs 3,025 crore write-off quarter, and the exit of one ~Rs 10,000 crore corporate account shrank the corporate book 10% quarter-on-quarter; the shareholder base is also extreme (GoI 92.44%, LIC 3%), which constrains the Rs 5,000 crore capital raise. Offsetting this, the earning asset base itself is very granular.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Loans and deposits are undifferentiated products where a cheaper competitor can always undercut, but the calls affirmatively show IOB is not having to buy business on price — cost of deposits fell to 4.97% and cost of funds to 5.09% while NIM expanded from 3.21% to 3.37% and CASA held above 40% for four straight quarters, so the deposit franchise is funding growth without rate-led leakage. (The long view had said: Wholesale credit and bulk deposits are commodity propositions where the customer switches on price alone — IOB walked away from a ~Rs 10,000 crore corporate account 'since pricing was not matching', and management repeatedly frames strategy as pricing discipline rather than differentiation ('not chasing volume at the cost of margin'); the FY24 need to offer a special 444-day deposit rate shows the same price-taking dynamic on the liability side.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Technological Innovation (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Cyclicality (WATCH).*

*Not comparable: New Entrants (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view).*

**Financial resilience:** Unknown on the long view (none of the stress checks could be computed.) vs Unknown on the one-year view (none of the stress checks could be computed.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 11 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
