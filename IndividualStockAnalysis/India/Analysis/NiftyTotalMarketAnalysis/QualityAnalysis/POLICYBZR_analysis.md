# PB Fintech. (POLICYBZR) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

PB Fintech runs online marketplaces in India where ordinary consumers compare and buy financial products — chiefly insurance through Policybazaar (health, term life, savings/investment plans, motor and two-wheeler, travel) and loans and credit cards through Paisabazaar. It does not carry the risk itself: insurers and lenders sit behind the platform, and PB Fintech earns commission/fees on the premium it originates and on loans disbursed, plus a high-margin 'renewal/trail' stream when policies it sold earlier are renewed (management says roughly 85% of renewal revenue drops to the bottom line, with renewal ARR growing from ~₹270 Cr in FY23 to ~₹1,126 Cr by Q4 FY26). Alongside the direct online business it runs offline-assisted channels — home/office meetings in local languages, on-ground claims support, and an agent-aggregation platform (PB Partners) that supplies smaller advisors across the country. It is overwhelmingly an India business (management claims ~93% share of its online insurance market), with a growing consumer business in the UAE and stated ambitions to enter further international markets; FY26 operating revenue was ₹6,794 Cr with PAT of ₹670 Cr.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Policybazaar (Core online insurance marketplace)** — The main business and profit engine — consumers research and buy health, term life, savings, motor and travel insurance online with sales, onboarding and claims help from PB's own staff; core insurance premium and revenue grew ~40%+ a year through FY24–FY26 and health plus term are the biggest long-term value drivers.
- **Paisabazaar (Credit marketplace)** — A lending marketplace where consumers compare personal loans, credit cards and increasingly secured loans from banks/NBFCs, anchored by a free credit-score service used by ~46 million consumers as the entry product; it is much smaller than insurance (about ₹115 Cr revenue in Q3 FY26), was flat-to-declining through FY25 and returned to modest growth by FY26 while staying EBITDA-positive.
- **PB Partners (agent / POSP aggregation platform)** — A B2B2C platform that signs up small, independent insurance advisors — about 450,000 advisors covering 99% of India's pin codes — and lets them sell many insurers' products, skewed towards motor insurance and towns beyond the metros; management calls it the clear number-one player in its category.
- **UAE / international insurance business** — A direct-to-consumer insurance marketplace in the UAE that started in motor and has shifted so that more than half its premium is now health and life, differentiated by cross-border health cover and a claims-assurance programme; premiums grew 54–64% a year and it has been profitable for several quarters, and the company is raising money (QIP) to look at further overseas markets.
- **New initiatives (the group's newer bets, including corporate/B2B insurance)** — The bucket in which PB Partners, UAE and the corporate/group insurance business sit — a deliberate investment area (originally budgeted at about ₹200 Cr of annual burn) that grew 41–43% year on year recently while its losses narrowed from -31% to roughly -3% to -4% of revenue, with management saying it should now be at break-even or better.
- **Renewal / trail revenue** — Not a separate business but the part management highlights most: recurring commission on policies renewing year after year, earning about an 85% margin, which had reached a ₹1,126 Cr annualised run rate by Q4 FY26 and is described as a key driver of future profit.

### The story in depth

PB Fintech comes out of the three reviews as a Strong business, scoring 65 out of 100 — 63 on business quality, 79 on multibagger fit and 50 on risk safety — with an overall resilience read of SOUND. The single fact that explains most of the story is the roughly eighteen years of category-building advertising. That spend created a brand customers come to rather than one that has to chase them, which is why the quality framework gives full marks on advertising, convenience and owning the customer's mind (a claimed ~93% share of its channel), and why three patterns line up behind it: Brand Strength, Cost to Replicate and Market Share Gainers, the last supported by core new protection premium growing 35-68% a year against a much slower industry. Management's own explanation of why a well-funded newcomer cannot simply buy its way in is a chain: long advertising creates inbound, high-intent customers, who disclose more about themselves, which lets insurers price risk properly and share more of the economics back. The same long-horizon habit shows up in the claims-support build, three to four years of unpopular investment before it paid off, and in UAE, where four years of work produced market leadership, 54-64% growth and four consecutive profitable quarters.

The awkward part is that the very things that make the franchise hard to copy also make it expensive to run and cheap to squeeze. Delivering the promised certainty of claim settlement means on-ground claims teams in 114 cities, home and office meetings in local languages, 40 stores and 600-plus field staff — real fixed costs that sit behind the Forward Integrators pattern but also behind three of the weakest quality checks: only ₹7 of operating profit per ₹100 of sales, margins that swing by roughly 49 points a year, and just ₹1 of operating cash per ₹100 of capital employed, a 1% return. Growth has averaged about 56% a year but lumpy year to year, and Return on Capital is the one quality area that scores negative outright. On price, the record cuts both ways: take rates have never changed through seventeen years of commission controls, which is a form of defended pricing power, yet management openly welcomes take-rate reduction — so Pricing Power is only a likely fit, and Toll Roads is a clear no fit, because the 16-18% blended take rate sits inside a regulator-set 30-35% expenses-of-management cap that insurers and the press contest regularly.

That regulated envelope is also the highest-rated risk. Government Dependency is HIGH because outside decisions move the business directly: the IRDAI caps set the commission pool, a GST change on health insurance swung demand hard enough for that line to grow 80%, and an RBI advisory on unsecured lending knocked the Paisabazaar credit business. Cyclicality is ELEVATED for a related reason — the insurance renewal book is genuinely stable, with recurring revenue compounding from ₹270 crore in Aug 2022 to ₹1,126 crore by May 2026 at about 85% flow-through, but credit swings with lending supply and the savings and unit-linked line swings with markets, which is the same mix effect that produces the lumpy growth and unstable margins the quality checks flag. Shifting Consumer Preferences and Good Enough Goods are two more views of the same pressure: customers moving toward market-linked products has already pulled take rates down, and as a comparison intermediary the company is structurally exposed to its own commission being commoditised. Against that sit a founder-led team that discloses bad news unprompted (a ₹25 crore GST one-off, a deliberate $3 million overspend, credit performance it said needed repairing quickly), a granular distribution base of 450,000 advisors across 99% of pincodes, and a genuinely under-penetrated Indian protection market. Fashion Risk is LOW because the demand is functional and renews annually, with satisfaction consistently above 90%.

**What the calls show:** Economics are set inside a regulator-defined envelope: the IRDAI EoM caps (30%/35%) determine industry commission pools, GST changes on health insurance materially moved demand ('once GST came, it grew at 80%'), RBI's unsecured-lending advisory knocked Paisabazaar's growth negative, and commission-cap speculation is a recurring analyst topic across the last several calls; life insurance commissions were literally zero for them till 2018.

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED

**What the calls show:** Insurance premium is a flow/renewal product (renewal ARR ₹1,126 Cr, ~85% margin, growing 63% year-on-year) which is structurally stable, but Paisabazaar's credit business is openly cyclical on lending supply and the savings/ULIP line swings with markets — credit revenue fell ~8% year-on-year in FY25 on regulator-driven tightening and savings went through a 3-4 quarter 'low cycle' before recovering in Q4FY26.

> "at the unsecured, every few years, there is a big fluctuation in supply and that makes it a little challenging at times" — *management, on an earnings call*

## What to watch

- Any move by the regulator on the 30%/35% expenses-of-management caps or on commission structure — this is the HIGH-rated Government Dependency risk and the reason Toll Roads is a no fit; a cut lands straight on the 16-18% blended take rate.
- Whether the recurring renewal book keeps compounding at anything like the 63% year-on-year pace to ₹1,126 crore — it is the backbone of the Recurring Revenue pattern and the main offset to the ELEVATED Cyclicality risk.
- Whether the 1% cash return on capital and ₹7-per-₹100 operating margin start improving as the claims, store and field-staff build matures — sustained failure to convert the Forward Integrators investment into returns would confirm the negative Return on Capital and Profit Margin checks rather than explain them away.
- Take-rate direction in credit and in the shift toward market-linked savings products — the Good Enough Goods and Shifting Consumer Preferences risks, and the test of whether Pricing Power is real or merely tolerated so far.
- The insurer-side commission negotiation, including the move to a combined-ratio-based model and the fact that the largest-selling health product is also the lowest-commission one — the Stakeholder Concentration risk, which would erode the Market Share Gainers pattern's profitability even if volume share keeps rising.
