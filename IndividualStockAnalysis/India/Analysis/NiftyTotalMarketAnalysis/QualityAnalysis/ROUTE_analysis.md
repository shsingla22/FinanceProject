# Route Mobile (ROUTE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Route Mobile is an Indian company (listed in Mumbai as ROUTE) that acts as the plumbing businesses use to send automated messages to their customers' phones — order confirmations, one-time passwords, marketing offers and the like. Companies plug into Route Mobile's platform once, and it delivers the message over whichever channel is needed: plain SMS (its original and still largest business), or newer channels like WhatsApp, RCS, Viber, email and voice; it also sells fraud-blocking 'firewall' and network-API services to mobile phone operators themselves. It makes money mainly on volume — it bills per message, with average realization around Rs 0.30 per billable transaction and roughly 175 billion messages processed a year, keeping a gross margin of about 21%-23% after paying operators to carry the traffic. It works globally through 280+ direct connections to mobile operators built up over two decades, with India as its biggest and fastest-growing market plus operations in Latin America (a Colombian subsidiary, Masivian, and expansion in Chile, Mexico, Ecuador), the Middle East, UK/Europe; since 2024 it has been 83%-owned by Belgium's Proximus group and sits inside 'Proximus Global' alongside BICS and TeleSign, giving it access to 450+ carriers and a global enterprise base. FY26 revenue was over Rs 4,400 crore.

**The parts of the business:**

- **A2P SMS (enterprise messaging / CPaaS)** — The core business — bulk application-to-person text messages sent on behalf of banks, retailers and other enterprises; management calls it 'a significant part of the business today' and the foundation the company was built on, serving 3,100+ enterprise clients.
- **ILD (international long distance) SMS** — Cross-border message traffic that carries low margins; management deliberately let this volume exit the mix during the FY26 'reset', which is why revenue fell that year but gross margin improved, and they say it remains a growth challenge near term.
- **India domestic enterprise business** — Locally-sent messages within India — lower price per message but higher margin and growing fast (25% year-on-year in Q1 FY25); India is also described as the company's 'global innovation hub' where new products are proven before worldwide rollout.
- **New products (RCS, WhatsApp, Viber, email, IP-based messaging)** — Richer chat-style business messaging — e.g. WhatsApp ticketing for metro rail as a Meta premier partner, and Coca-Cola in UAE — growing at ~43% a year from FY22 to FY26 and consistently faster than the company overall (14% year-on-year in Q1 FY27); this is the mix shift management is betting on.
- **MNO Solutions (firewall and network APIs)** — Products sold to mobile operators rather than enterprises: AI-driven SMS and voice firewalls that filter grey/fraudulent traffic (the Vodafone Idea deal, its largest, went live April 2024) and network APIs sitting in the operator's backend for silent verification, SIM-swap detection and device verification — described as a high-margin, recurring revenue stream.
- **OCEAN (omnichannel engagement platform)** — The company's own software layer for running campaigns across all channels from one place, with campaign management, reporting, audience management and white-label options — positioned as the vehicle for moving 'up the value chain' from selling connectivity to selling customer-engagement software.
- **Heltar (AI conversational engagement, acquired July 2026)** — A recently-agreed slump-sale purchase of an AI-driven, low-code chatbot and automation platform spanning WhatsApp, RCS and voice, bought to close the conversational-AI gap quickly and cross-sell into existing single-channel clients; management says it is not material to the balance sheet and is funded from internal cash.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Route Mobile earns a Weak rating — 31 out of 100, one star — built from 47 on business quality, 18 on multibagger fit and 18 on risk safety. The genuine positives are narrow but real. The business converts capital into cash at a decent clip: every ₹100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings produced ₹21 of operating cash this year, and Return on Capital is the only quality area comfortably in positive territory (0.33). Management talks in a low-key, technical, substance-led way across 17 earnings calls, without promotional posturing, even when correcting market rumours — the 'Out of limelight' check is one of only two positives in the whole 34-check framework. There is also a real product cadence behind the 'Innovation Dominance' partial fit: a Bengaluru R&D centre, the OCEAN platform, RCS and WhatsApp channels, Network APIs and the Heltar acquisition, with non-SMS revenue compounding roughly 40-43% a year. And two decades of direct connections to mobile operators — 280-plus directly, 450-plus including BICS — give the 'Cost to Replicate' pattern something concrete to point at. Overall resilience is judged SOUND.

The trouble is that the single trait defining this company drives both its best pattern and its worst risks. Route Mobile is a genuinely high-volume, low-price business: 24.8 billion transactions at 29.4 paisa in one quarter, rising to 175 billion messages a year, and management deliberately took a loss-leading price to win a large public-sector bank. That is why 'Low Price Plus' is the only LIKELY FIT on the multibagger screen. But the same fact is why 'Pricing Power' and 'Brand Strength' are outright NO FITs, why the 'Intangible benefits' and 'Pricing, Mix and Volume' checks both score negative, and why 'Good Enough Goods' is a HIGH RISK. Realisation per billable transaction fell from 35 paisa to about 30, the company keeps only about ₹23 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs, gross margins sit near 21-23%, and EBITDA margin has slid from roughly 12.5% to 9.5%. A telecom operator undercut the company on a large deal. In short, the low-price model is being run without the cost-obsession culture that would make it a durable advantage — it looks less like a chosen strategy and more like the only available one. The operator connections cut both ways too: they are the stated replication barrier and the silver lining on the 'Good Enough Goods' risk, yet the international long-distance revenue they serve is in secular decline, and the very operators involved are the 'New Entrants' threat (ELEVATED) that management answered defensively when analysts pressed from 2022 onward.

The second thread running through all three records is dependence — on a shrinking core market, on a few relationships, and on a parent company. Management itself calls A2P SMS, the founding product, a business in secular decline as enterprises move to WhatsApp, RCS and over-the-top channels; that makes 'Technological Innovation' and 'Shifting Consumer Preferences' risks, sinks the 'Structural end-market growth' check, and pulls Industry Structure to -0.5. The product pivot is the answer, but new products are still only about 8% of revenue, so the innovation cadence that supports 'Innovation Dominance' is also the scramble that defines the technology risk. 'Stakeholder Concentration' is HIGH: the Vodafone Idea firewall deal was the load-bearing item in FY25 guidance, and disruption at a single high-margin customer knocked down Q1 FY27 gross margin and net revenue retention — which has fallen from 125% to 105% to 98%, undermining both the 'Recurring Revenue' and 'Market Share Gainers' partial fits. Because the product is discretionary enterprise spend that gets cut first in cost-optimisation cycles, 'Toll Roads' is a NO FIT and cyclicality stays on watch. Growth has averaged about 28% a year but is lumpy, and guidance of 40-50% for FY23 and 18-22% for FY25 was not met, which is what holds 'Corporate Culture' to partial despite genuine candor about the mix transition and integration. Finally, independence is gone: Proximus holds 83.1%, the founders have exited, and the chairman, CEO, CFO and chief strategy officer were all imported from the parent with no visible internal pipeline. That ownership is the silver lining against operator entry — BICS brings 900-plus operator relationships — and simultaneously the reason two management checks score negative. Capital allocation compounds the discomfort: a ₹1,200mn buyback launched in 2022 near peak valuations, a payout policy diluted from up to 40% of free cash to up to 20% of profit, and ₹1,300cr sitting idle with no clear framework.

Route Mobile is a cash-generative, honestly-run business trapped in a declining, price-deflating core market, now controlled by a parent that supplies both its strategy and its people. The numbers that would change the story are the ones where the pivot either starts paying or does not: gross margin and realisation per transaction stabilising rather than sliding, non-SMS revenue growing from 8% of the mix into something material, and net revenue retention turning back above 100%. Until then the record reads as a low-price volume business with thin pricing power, concentrated relationships and no independent decision-making — which is what a 31 out of 100 describes.

*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**What the calls show:** The model is genuinely high-volume/low-margin (24.8bn transactions at 29.4 paisa in Q1FY23 rising to 175bn messages in FY26 at ~22% gross margin) and management took a deliberate strategic low-price call to win a large PSU bank, but no cost-obsession culture or itemised, hard-to-copy cost advantage is ever described.

- ✅ Keeps ₹23 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 1.2× a year with 28% returns on capital — thin margins made up on volume, the low-price-winner profile.

*"Sells once, earns forever"*

**What the calls show:** The FY26 strategy call frames firewall/Network API sales to MNOs as a recurring managed-services stream and cites net revenue retention (125% in Q1FY23, 105% in Q1FY25, 98% in FY27), but there is no discussion of deferred revenue, advance billing or customer float funding the business — in fact receivables/DSO were a drag in FY23.

> "our firewall and network API products are building into a high margin, recurring revenue stream that sits alongside the enterprise business" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ About 75 days of cash is tied up in the trade cycle — customers are not funding the business.
- ✅ Capital spending is about 1% of sales — growth needs little capital.
- ❌ Operating cash was positive in 9 of 11 years — not fully consistent.

*"Out-inventing the competition"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 2 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** There is a genuine, repeated product cadence — Bengaluru R&D centre of excellence (FY23), capitalized omnichannel/AI-ML build at Masivian, OCEAN, RCS/WhatsApp, Network APIs, the Heltar acquisition (FY27) — with non-SMS revenue compounding ~40-43%, but gross margins are ~21-23% (too low to fund outsized R&D), R&D spend is never disclosed as a growing line, and new products are still only ~8% of revenue.

> "RCS, WhatsApp, and AI enabled messaging have grown at a 43% compounded annual growth rate over the period FY22 to FY26" — *management, on an earnings call*

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

## What to watch

- Non-SMS revenue moving beyond ~8% of the total — the 'Shifting Consumer Preferences' and 'Technological Innovation' risks turn on whether the ~40-43% compounding in OCEAN, RCS/WhatsApp, Network APIs and Heltar actually replaces the declining A2P SMS pool.

- Use of the idle ₹1,300cr and a stated payout framework — the 'Distribution to shareholders' check scores negative on the 2022 peak-valuation buyback and the policy dilution from 40% of free cash to 20% of profit; a clear, disciplined framework under Proximus ownership would lift the weakest quality area.
- Whether any senior leadership is developed internally rather than imported from the parent — the 'People matter' and 'Independent, long-term and tenacious' checks both score negative on the same fact, and nothing in the record yet suggests a pipeline.
