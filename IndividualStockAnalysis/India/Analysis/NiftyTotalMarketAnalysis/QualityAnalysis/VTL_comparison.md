### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 4 weakened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: Beyond the 'Delivering Excellence. Since 1965' letterhead, the FY26 calls contain no discussion of A&P spend, brand premium, distributor leverage or adjacency launches — the evidence is silent rather than negative. (The long view had said: There is a genuine reputational franchise — 'Delivering Excellence. Since 1965.', a diversified blue-chip customer base and brands preferring large organised vendors under China+1 (Aug 2024) — but no A&P spend, no premium pricing and no consumer brand; the reputation buys order flow, not price.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Consistent value-added product push across all four calls — melange, compact, cellulosic, functional/circular yarns, the ReNova recycling plant and the newly commissioned Vardhman Performance Fabrics — but no R&D spend, share-of-industry-R&D or per-product revenue impact is disclosed, and it has not yet translated into pricing power. (The long view had said: Regular named capability additions — Vardhman Performance Fabrics for technical/performance wear commissioned FY26, multi-fibre flexibility, contamination-free and value-added yarns, Budhni fabric line (Aug 2024, Jan 2026, May 2026) — but no R&D spend disclosure, no share-of-industry-R&D, and gross margins are commodity-set rather than innovation-funded.)

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Leadership is stable and long-tenured (Neeraj Jain through the window, elevated to MD by Q4), disclosure of adverse conditions is candid and specific, and the capex promised in Q1 — fabric expansion, ReNova, Performance Fabrics — was commissioned broadly on the stated schedule by Q3, though ramp guidance slipped from Q4 FY26 to Q1 FY27. (The long view had said: Consistent long-tenured team (Neeraj Jain, Jhamb, Thapar, Bansal, Malhotra on every call from 2022 to 2026), candid bad news — hedging losses, stopped spindles, tariff hit, 'margins remain under pressure' — explicit conservatism in capex sizing, and promises delivered: the FY24-announced INR2,000–2,500 crore programme reported on-track in Aug 2024 and ~90% of spinning modernisation completed by May 2026.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Nothing in the four FY26 calls touches licence fees, AMC/service revenue, renewals, installed base or customer advances as a funding source; the business discussed is outright sale of yarn and fabric. (The long view had said: Across all 16 calls there is no discussion of licence fees, AMC/service attach rates, renewals, installed base or customer advances as a funding source — the evidence is simply silent on recurring revenue.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Management repeatedly describes a structural raw-material cost disadvantage versus Vietnam/Bangladesh driven by MSP and the 11% import duty, i.e. an input-cost handicap rather than a hard-to-copy cost advantage; no itemised cost-culture savings are discussed in any of the four calls. (The long view had said: Repeated cost-culture evidence across the years — internal cost-saving initiatives, ~INR2,500 crore modernisation/debottlenecking capex, biomass boilers and green power targeted at cost, and scale as one of India's largest efficient mills (Aug 2024, Jan 2026, May 2026) — but the low-cost position is undermined by India's structural cotton-price disadvantage, and management concedes margins depend on industry utilisation, not a copy-proof cost edge.)
- **Forward Integrators**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Management explicitly rules out moving toward the end customer, positioning itself as a supplier to garment and home-textile exporters; no own stores, franchising or D2C is mentioned in any FY26 call. (The long view had said: Movement downstream is real but small and hesitant: vertical integration fibre→yarn→fabric→garment, and the FY26 reversal of its earlier stance to double garment capacity on the back of the EU FTA — yet management itself calls it 'very, very miniscule', and there are no own stores, franchising or D2C.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Cost to Replicate (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 5 unchanged. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Yarn is a largely undifferentiated product where price is set by surplus capacity — yarn prices fell 2-3% purely on duty relaxation and excess spinning capacity, and lower-cost mills in Vietnam, Bangladesh and Indonesia enjoy a $0.03-0.04/lb structural raw-material advantage plus duty-free cotton. (The long view had said: Management concedes yarn is a basic commodity whose price is set internationally and cannot carry a domestic cost premium, so cheaper Vietnamese/Brazilian-cotton-fed mills enjoy a $0.03-0.08/lb structural edge; the defence is cost and mix, not brand.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Preference movement shows up as an opportunity the company is already positioned for rather than an eroding demand base: it has introduced functional, performance and circular yarns for activewear and lifestyle, commissioned a performance-fabrics plant, and is taking green power from 9% to ~50% by FY27. Man-made-fibre substitution is a live variable but was described this year as pushing synthetic prices up, not displacing cotton demand. (The long view had said: Management repeatedly notes end-demand migrating away from pure cotton toward man-made and blended fibres and toward performance wear, and has invested to follow it; it also cited post-COVID wardrobe shifts changing which fabrics brands buy.)

**That worsened:**

- **New Entrants**: QUANT FLAG → WATCH. Why now: The threat is structurally advantaged competing countries rather than a new domestic format: brands shifted sourcing to Bangladesh, Vietnam, Cambodia and Sri Lanka under US tariffs, and Chinese mills swung India's yarn demand by ~20 million kg a month depending on Xinjiang bans and Bangladeshi unrest. (The long view had said: The calls discuss cost competition from existing spinning nations (Vietnam, Bangladesh, Pakistan) and excess global capacity, but no structurally different new-format competitor entering the industry.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
