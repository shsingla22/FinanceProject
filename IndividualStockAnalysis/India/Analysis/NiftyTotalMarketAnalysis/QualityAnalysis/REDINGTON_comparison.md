### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: B2B reputation is real and repeatedly cited — VARINDIA No.1 IT distributor in India for a third consecutive year with a widening lead over No.2, plus a 'most trusted distributor' award (Nov 2025, Feb 2026) — but there is no heritage/A&P/premium-pricing brand economics, only channel trust. (The long view had said: Redington distributes other companies' brands (Microsoft, AWS, Autodesk, Red Hat, Fortinet, Palo Alto) and earns no consumer premium; there is no A&P spend, heritage story or premium-pricing evidence in any of the 18 calls — its standing is a vendor/reseller reputation and a ranking, not brand pricing power.)

**That weakened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Classic thin-margin/high-turn distribution economics with visible cost discipline: PAT margin 1.3-1.4%, working capital days cut to 28-31, opex growing slower than revenue (opex/revenue down 17bps in FY26) and ROCE at 22% (Nov 2025-May 2026). What is missing is any claim that low price plus differentiation is hard to copy. (The long view had said: Across all four years management describes an explicit cost-and-turns culture: a consolidated shared-services centre processing 300,000+ orders a quarter (Aug 2022), opex growing slower than revenue in FY25 and FY26, best-in-class working capital of 25 days in Q2FY25 and 30 days in Q4FY26, interest cost cut 59% ex-Arena, all delivering 22% ROCE on ~1.3% net margins — thin unit economics rescued by asset turns and cost discipline.)
- **Forward Integrators**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Redington is moving deeper into services and platform-led distribution, not toward owned stores, franchising or D2C; its customers remain partners and resellers, with 'direct-to-retail' meaning retail channel execution rather than owned retail. (The long view had said: Redington is moving from brick-and-mortar distribution toward owned digital routes to market — an omni-channel shift flagged in FY23, platform/marketplace-led distribution, direct-to-retail execution in India in Q4FY26 and up-country reach growing 45% — but it owns no stores, franchises or D2C consumer channel; it is forward integration within B2B distribution only.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
