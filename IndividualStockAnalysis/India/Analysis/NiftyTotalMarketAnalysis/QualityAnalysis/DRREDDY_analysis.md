# Dr. Reddy's Laboratories Ltd. (DRREDDY) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: PHARMA*

Dr. Reddy's Laboratories is an India-based (Hyderabad-headquartered) pharmaceutical company that manufactures and sells medicines — mostly generic copies of drugs whose patents have expired, plus the active chemical ingredients that go into them. It sells to pharmacy chains, wholesalers, hospitals, doctors and other drug makers across the US, Europe, Russia and other emerging markets, and India, with quarterly consolidated revenue running roughly Rs. 6,300-8,100 crore (about US$775-920 million) over the period of these calls. Money is made by winning regulatory approvals and launching new products (7 US launches in one quarter, 10 in Europe, 31 across emerging markets), then selling volume against steady price erosion; the occasional exclusive or 'complex' launch — lenalidomide capsules, semaglutide, bosutinib with 180-day exclusivity — can swing profits sharply in either direction. Alongside the core generics business it is building newer engines: consumer health brands, biosimilars, peptides, contract manufacturing services for other companies, and in-licensed innovative drugs it distributes in selected markets.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Global Generics** — The main business — off-patent finished medicines sold under the company's own or partner labels, carrying gross margins in the 52%-65% range and made up of the four geographic units below.
- **North America Generics** — Generic medicines sold in the US, the single largest unit at $351 million in Q2 FY23 rising to $463 million in Q1 FY25 per quarter, driven by new launches including lenalidomide, and the swing factor behind most of the company's growth and decline.
- **Europe** — Generics across European countries, a smaller unit at about €52 million a quarter in FY23 growing around 10% year-on-year, with roughly 10 new product launches per quarter.
- **Emerging Markets (including Russia)** — Generics sold across Russia, CIS and other developing markets, around Rs. 1,225 crore a quarter, with Russia the biggest piece and a source of volatility from currency swings and channel-stocking cycles.
- **India** — Branded prescription medicines sold at home, around Rs. 1,150 crore a quarter and ranked #10 by value on IQVIA's moving-annual-total, where management is growing big brands, in-licensing drugs (Vonoprazan, toripalimab) and divesting non-core brands.
- **PSAI (Pharmaceutical Services and Active Ingredients)** — Sells bulk active pharmaceutical ingredients and development/manufacturing services to other drug companies — much smaller (about $81 million a quarter) and far less profitable, with gross margins swinging between roughly 4% and 23%.
- **Consumer Healthcare** — A newer over-the-counter/wellness business built by acquisition and partnership — the Nicotinell nicotine-replacement brands bought outside the US in 2024, and a nutraceuticals joint venture with Nestlé in India.
- **CDMO / Aurigene Pharmaceutical Services** — A contract development and manufacturing arm that makes drugs for other companies, including a 70,000 sq ft biologics facility opened in Hyderabad in 2024.
- **Biosimilars, peptides and innovation** — Future growth engines funded by R&D of roughly 7%-9% of sales, covering copies of biologic drugs (e.g. a denosumab partnership with Alvotech), peptide products such as semaglutide, and in-licensed novel therapies and devices like Nerivio for migraine.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

- **Capex for growth (not maintenance) — Neutral** *(from the conference calls)*: Capex is modest and flat-to-declining across the period (Rs251cr in Q2FY23, Rs491cr in Q1FY25, Rs307cr in Q1FY27) with growth spend routed mainly through acquisitions and in-licensing rather than plant, and the one visible growth capex — the semaglutide API scale-up — failed validation.

- **Advertisement and promotion — Neutral** *(from the conference calls)*: SG&A has risen persistently (26.3% of sales in Q2FY23 to 36% in Q1FY27) with 'targeted investments in the branded businesses', but management itself frames the return as still-to-come productivity rather than demonstrated brand payback.
