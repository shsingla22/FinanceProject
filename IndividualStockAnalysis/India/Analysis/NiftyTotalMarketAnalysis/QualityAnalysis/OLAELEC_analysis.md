# Ola Electric (OLAELEC) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Ola Electric is an Indian company that designs, builds and sells electric two-wheelers — mainly electric scooters and, more recently, electric motorcycles — to retail consumers across India, from premium models down to mass-market ones priced under about ₹1 lakh (management also notes reach into Tier 3/Tier 4 upcountry markets). It earns money by manufacturing and selling these vehicles through its own network of retail stores and servicing them afterwards; revenue was ₹1,718 crores in the June 2024 quarter and ₹2,253 crores for full-year FY26 on 173,794 deliveries. Management stresses that the company is 'vertically integrated' — it develops and makes its own motors, batteries, battery cells, electronics and software in-house rather than buying them in, which it credits for gross margins of 38.5% in Q4 FY26, above even petrol-engine (ICE) two-wheeler makers. It reports itself in two segments, Automotive and Cell, and says it has now built capacity for about 1 million vehicles a year and ~6 GWh of cell manufacturing, with the heavy investment phase behind it and revenue potential of ₹15,000–20,000 crores from that capacity.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Automotive segment (Auto)** — The core and by far the larger business — making and selling electric scooters (Gen 1, Gen 2 and now Gen 3 platforms, with Gen 3 at ~80% of sales by mid-2025) and electric motorcycles; it reached its first EBITDA-positive month in June 2025 and delivered ₹213 crores of operating cash flow and ₹173 crores of free cash flow in Q4 FY26, with no further capex needed to scale to a million units.
- **Cell segment (Cell Gigafactory)** — An in-house battery-cell factory producing Ola's own 4680-format cells (~72,500 cells in Q3 FY26) that go into its own vehicles; capacity has been built from 1.4 GWh to about 5–6 GWh funded by an SBI-consortium term loan, and management says it is still in the investment phase but should move to sustained revenue growth and eventually cash generation.
- **Motorcycles / bikes** — A newer product line within the automotive business launched in August 2024 and deliberately scaled up slowly — in roughly 200 stores by mid-2025 and planned for all stores by the festive season — aimed at motorbikes, which management says are about two-thirds of the Indian two-wheeler market.
- **Retail stores and service network** — The company's own front-end distribution and after-sales service arms, which it counts as part of its vertical integration; a service execution problem hurt brand trust and sales in FY26, and a 'hyper service' fix cut service backlogs from 14 days to 7–8 days, while store-network rationalisation helped halve quarterly operating expenses from ₹844 crores to ₹428 crores year on year.
- **Energy storage (stated as an adjacency, not yet a reported business)** — Management describes energy storage as a future extension of the cell factory once it reaches critical mass, and in May 2026 frames Ola as building across 'electric mobility, cell manufacturing, and energy storage' — but the calls give it no revenue or segment numbers.

### The story in depth

Ola Electric scores 29 out of 100 — a Weak, one-star rating — and the arithmetic tells you where the trouble sits: the business-quality framework gave it 32, the multibagger screen 25, and risk safety 30, so nothing rescues anything else. The single worst area is return on capital, at the bottom of the scale: every ₹100 of shareholders' money and borrowings in the business produced ₹-13 of operating cash this year, every ₹1 of assets turned over just ₹0.29 of sales, and ₹43 of every ₹100 of sales was lost at the operating line. Industry structure and management also score negative, with the calls acknowledging both petrol-engine incumbents and new EV startups continuing to enter. Only two checks stand out as genuine strengths: the company collects cash from customers 91 days before it has to pay suppliers, so buyers effectively fund the working capital, and the end markets — converting petrol two-wheelers to electric, domestic battery cell manufacturing and grid storage — are in real long-term expansion helped by government policy.

The patterns and the risks are, to a large degree, the same facts read from two directions. The one strong pattern fit is Forward Integrators: Ola owns its entire store and service network with no dealer layer, books the leases in its own operating costs, and sells order-to-delivery direct. That same choice to own everything is what produces the partial fits on Cost to Replicate (the only working Gigafactory in India, 2.5 GWh scaling to 6 GWh, with about ₹5,300 crore sunk into EV-specific plant and R&D) and Low Price Plus (in-housing motors, ABS, electronics and cells, operating costs cut from ₹844 crore to ₹428 crore heading toward ₹300-350 crore). But that identical ₹5,300 crore and the one-million-unit, 6 GWh footprint is exactly what makes Cyclicality a High Risk: FY26 deliveries were 173,794 units against a million units of capacity, and quarterly revenue fell from ₹1,718 crore in Q1 FY25 to ₹470 crore in Q3 FY26 — which is also why the asset-turn check scores at the bottom. The same duality runs through innovation (three vehicle generations in roughly four years lifted gross margin from 10% to 38.5%, but left Gen 1 and Gen 2 as legacy carrying over ₹500 crore of FY25 warranty cost, so the Innovation Dominance pattern and the Technological Innovation risk share one root), through the brand (industry-leading recall and search share support a partial Brand Strength fit while failing the out-of-limelight and security-by-obscurity checks outright, alongside a self-congratulatory tone flagged under the halo-effect caution), and through policy (the secular end-market tailwind that earns a positive check is the same government support that makes Government Dependency a High Risk — subsidy worth 8-10 margin points in FY25, and a Q4 FY26 gross margin of 38.5% that becomes 33.5% without production incentives).

What the company does not have is the set of traits that usually make a compounding story work. Pricing Power is a clear no — management says selling prices are flat, margin gains come from taking cost out rather than charging more, and recent gross margin was spent on aggressive growth, meaning discounting. Market Share Gainers is a clear no in the opposite direction: share went from 48.6% in Q1 FY25, when roughly one in two electric two-wheelers sold in India was an Ola, to quarterly deliveries of 32,680, with management no longer defending short-term share — the New Entrants risk and that failed pattern are one and the same event. Recurring Revenue and Toll Roads both fail because the after-sale relationship appears in the records only as warranty provisions, service backlogs and remediation cost, never as repeat revenue, and customers left in large numbers when service broke down; the same owned-store network that wins the Forward Integrators fit therefore carries the cost of every service failure directly. The offsets are real but modest: overall resilience is judged Sound, the risks tied to customer taste, trend-dependence and dependence on any one dealer or supplier are all Low, capital allocation is the one quality area scoring slightly positive, and the calls do disclose bad news, including the ₹250 crore warranty provision. Taken together, this is a business with a genuine and expensive physical position in a genuinely growing market, whose economics — negative returns, no pricing power, shrinking share and a heavy reliance on government support — do not yet justify what has been spent.

### Forward Integrators — STRONG FIT
*"Owns the shop, not just the product"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence and the numbers agree (1 of 2 checks computable supporting).

**What the calls show:** Ola is a textbook forward integrator: a fully company-owned D2C store and service network with no dealer layer, leases carried in its own OpEx, direct order-to-delivery booking, and management naming D2C one of its three standing strategic priorities in every call from Aug 2024 through May 2026 — though the FY26 reset showed the cost of owning that front end.

> "since our business model in the front end is a fully company -owned model, we've added lease expenses to our operational exp enses" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Operating margin moved from about -10830% (early years) to -35% (recent years) — the widening-profitability signature of pricing power / lock-in.
- ❌ Operating margin of -43% — too thin for a protected franchise.

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 1 check supporting).

**What the calls show:** Genuine cost obsession is documented and itemised across the calls — Project Lakshya, in-housing motors/ABS/electronics/cells to strip BOM, consolidated OpEx cut from Rs 844cr to Rs 428cr toward Rs 300-350cr, breakeven reset to 20-25k units/month, and zero marketing spend — combined with a value proposition (range-to-price index ~50% above peers). > "our OpEx is a ctually half... we'll actually get our OpEx down to about 100 -120 crores a month" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹31 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 0.3× a year — not the high-turnover, low-margin machine this pattern needs.

*"Could a newcomer copy this?"*

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Government Dependency, Technological Innovation, New Entrants, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — the only stress check that could be computed is clean.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.8× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 3 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Classic over-investment-into-a-boom pattern: ~Rs 5,300 crore sunk into 1 million units of vehicle capacity and 6 GWh of cells, against FY26 deliveries of only 173,794 and quarterly revenue collapsing from Rs 1,718 crore (Q1 FY25) to Rs 470 crore (Q3 FY26), with consolidated gross margin swinging from 13.7% to 38.5% year-on-year and the CFO calling Q3 FY26 a 'structural reset'. Demand is a discretionary durable tied to an industry penetration cycle that management itself says decelerated, and Q4 FY26 commentary flags 'commodity pressures' — sales and margins have fluctuated enormously across the nine calls.

> "As EV penetration growth slows down and we identified gaps in service execution, we made a deliberate choice to realign our retail footprint, cost structure and operating model" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Capex cycle now declared complete with high fixed-cost operating leverage and a lowered breakeven (~20,000-25,000 units/month); vehicles are a retail replacement/flow purchase rather than customer capex equipment*

- ⚠️ Year-to-year sales growth swings by ±16567 percentage points — boom-and-bust revenue, not a steady compounder.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from -32228% to -25% (swinging ±13127.2 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 10900% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 32% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Policy is embedded in both the P&L and the growth thesis across all nine calls: FAME/EMPS subsidy of ~Rs 10,000 a vehicle worth '8-10 points of margin' in FY25, Auto PLI and ACC PLI (Q4 FY26 gross margin 38.5% falls to 33.5% ex-PLI, and positive CFO is 'supported by... PLI inflows'), an ABS regulatory mandate reshaping BOM cost, and the cell business explicitly pinned on hoped-for future policy. Management openly frames the battery growth case on anticipated local-content mandates and PLI extensions that do not yet exist.

> "just like ALMM in solar, there will be ALBM, ALCM. There will be hopefully some extension in the ACC PLI. There will be domestic procurement for BESS deployments mandates coming soon" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Q1 FY26 gross margins were delivered 'largely without any incentive' and Q4 FY26 ex-PLI margin of 33.5% is still above ICE peers, showing the auto product economics stand up without subsidy*

### Technological Innovation — ELEVATED
*"Out-invented by the industry"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but the only computed fingerprint does not confirm it — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** The whole business is built on rapidly obsoleting platforms — three vehicle generations in roughly four years, with Gen 1 and Gen 2 already legacy and carrying Rs 500+ crore of FY25 warranty cost, plus a Gigafactory committed to the 4680 format that management itself says is only a waypoint to 4600 and 46120 within 12-24 months. Heavy fixed infrastructure built around one cell chemistry/format in an industry where battery technology moves fast is precisely the incumbent's trap.

> "4680 is not the end of it. In fact, it's the start of it... in the next 12 to 24 months, 4680 goes to 4600 goes to 46120" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Continuous in-house R&D cadence (rare-earth-free motor, in-house ABS, generational cell roadmap) means the company is the one iterating rather than defending a static format; vertical integration lets it absorb changes internally*

- ⬜ Insufficient sales history.
- ✅ Operating margin (-43%) remains near its historical peak (-25%) — no collapse in the premium.

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** An electric scooter is not a complex, brand-protected niche product, and management repeatedly frames its position as defending value against cheaper alternatives — noting margins are earned 'despite not charging 20% higher than market' and leaning on a range-to-price index chart to justify the proposition. Rivals selling adequate scooters with better service reach demonstrably took volume (Q3 FY26 deliveries of 32,680 versus 1.25 lakh two years earlier), which is the good-enough mechanism at work; cost leadership from vertical integration is the defence rather than a defensible premium.

## What to watch

- Monthly volumes against the stated 20,000-25,000 unit breakeven — the Low Price Plus cost-out story and the High-Risk Cyclicality verdict both turn on whether the fixed-cost base gets filled
- Gross margin excluding production incentives holding near the 33.5% level, and what happens to operating cash flow without that support — the direct test of the Government Dependency risk
- Quarterly deliveries and share direction versus the 32,680 low — this is what would move the Market Share Gainers pattern off no-fit and soften the New Entrants risk
- Return on capital and asset turn moving off -13% and ₹0.29 of sales per ₹1 of assets, since these are the lowest-scoring quality checks and the reason the rating is Weak
- Warranty provisions and service costs after the ₹500-crore-plus FY25 legacy-generation charge — the Technological Innovation risk and the Recurring Revenue no-fit both hinge on whether the installed base becomes a cost or an asset
