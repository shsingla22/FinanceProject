# JSW Cement (JSWCEMENT) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

JSW Cement makes and sells building materials in India — mainly bagged/bulk cement and GGBS (ground granulated blast furnace slag, a cement-like powder made by grinding a waste product from steel blast furnaces), plus a small amount of clinker sold to others. It earns money by manufacturing these at grinding and clinker plants and selling them by the ton: FY26 volumes were 13.96 million tons for revenue of INR6,512 crores, with cement fetching about INR4,673/ton and GGBS about INR3,682/ton in Q4 FY26. Buyers are a mix of retail/'trade' customers through a network of 13,000+ dealers (about half of cement sales) and direct institutional customers — ready-mix concrete (RMC) producers, large EPC contractors, housing developers and government infrastructure agencies (metros, airports, expressways, ports). It operates in South, West and East India, has just entered the North with a new integrated plant at Nagaur, Rajasthan (commercial operations March 2026), and has a clinker joint venture in Fujairah, UAE; it is part of the JSW Group and repeatedly stresses having the lowest CO2 emission intensity in the industry (268 kg per ton in FY26).

**The parts of the business:**

- **Cement** — Ordinary and premium cement made by grinding clinker with slag and other materials, sold largely through dealers to retail and project buyers — the biggest line at roughly 54% of volume in 9M FY26 (2.35 million tons in Q4 FY26, up 12% year-on-year), with about 51-53% of it sold in the higher-priced 'trade' channel.
- **GGBS** — Ground granulated blast furnace slag, a cementitious powder ground from steel-plant slag and sold mostly to the ready-mix concrete industry as a partial replacement for ordinary cement — about 42% of volume (5.18 million tons in FY25) and the company says it is India's single largest GGBS producer with roughly 84% market share; management treats cement and GGBS as one reporting segment and does not disclose GGBS margins separately.
- **Clinker (sold externally)** — The semi-finished input to cement, produced at its own clinker units and sold to third parties — the smallest line at about 4% of volume in 9M FY26.
- **Slag sourcing / JSW Steel link** — Not a revenue line but central to how it operates: it buys blast-furnace slag under long-term contracts from JSW Steel and other steelmakers, with several grinding units placed next to steel plants to cut freight — when supply was interrupted (a Dolvi blast-furnace shutdown in Q1 FY26, slag shortages in Q4 FY26) costs rose and volumes had to be rerouted.
- **Fujairah clinker JV (UAE)** — A 2.31 MTPA clinker joint venture in the UAE that supplies clinker to the West India operations; it is not consolidated into the main accounts and shows up as a share of profit (INR6 crores in Q4 FY26), with a new 1.65 MTPA grinding unit in Fujairah approved at USD 39 million.
- **Capacity expansion programme** — An ongoing build-out from 21.60 MTPA of grinding capacity today toward about 46 MTPA of grinding and 13.04 MTPA of clinker, via new units at Nagaur (Rajasthan), Mansa (Punjab), Sambalpur, Vijayanagar, Dolvi, Hatta and Uttar Pradesh — capex ran about INR1,962 crores in FY26 and it is the main use of cash and reason for the INR3,635 crore net debt.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

JSW Cement earns a 51 out of 100 overall score, or "Decent" (three stars), built from 65 on business quality, 34 on multibagger fit and 46 on risk safety, with resilience judged SOUND. The strongest part of the story is a genuinely unusual niche: the company created the Indian market for GGBS, a slag-based substitute for ordinary cement, and now holds roughly 84% of it, with GGBS making up 41-42% of volumes. That franchise sits inside end markets that are expanding on their own — Indian cement demand is expected to grow 7.5-8.5% a year to FY30, and GGBS faster still as green construction substitutes in. The company has consistently taken share out of that growth: 16.7% volume growth a year over a decade against 6.15% for the industry, and 12% cement volume growth against 8% for the industry in the fourth quarter. Two other traits stand out. Customers pay before suppliers need to be paid, by 136 days, so the customer base effectively funds day-to-day operations. And management is candid — it flagged a ₹1,466 crore non-cash charge and asked investors to use adjusted profit, disclosed the Punjab environmental clearance delay and its cause, and owned the Dolvi slag shortfall with the volume impact quantified.

The eleven-pattern screen lines up with this, but it also shows where the same traits cut both ways. Market Share Gainers is a likely fit and is the clearest of the eleven, because the share gain is explicit, repeated and won on cost and green credentials. Brand Strength and Innovation Dominance are also likely fits — the A-category brand launch in the North with television, print and hoarding spend, 57% premium share within trade sales, and named innovations such as microfine GGBS, waste-heat recovery and captive solar, with an industry-best 258-268 kg of CO2 per ton. But the screen is honest about the limits: branding spend was actually cut year-on-year to lift margins, which sits awkwardly beside the advertising check that credits deliberate brand-building, and no research spend is disclosed to back the innovation claim. The two outright failures matter most. Pricing Power is a NO FIT because GGBS realisation has been flat to down for years (₹3,761 in FY24, ₹3,773 in FY25, ₹3,684 in the first nine months of FY26) and cement realisation fell 3.9% in one quarter — management is consciously declining price increases to buy penetration. That is the same decision that produces the share gains, so the pattern the company scores best on is being paid for with the pattern it fails. Toll Roads is a NO FIT for a related reason: cementitious material is 42-45% of a ready-mix producer's raw material cost, and those producers openly re-compare GGBS against fly-ash mixes and switch.

The risk review turns those same facts into the two live threats. Stakeholder Concentration is ELEVATED because the GGBS franchise — the differentiated 42% of volumes and the source of the mini-monopoly and the hard-to-copy base — depends on blast-furnace slag from JSW Steel, with grinding units co-located at its plants and pricing set by a five-year contract in which JSW Steel initiates the change. The moat and the dependency are the same asset. Good Enough Goods is ELEVATED for the same reason Pricing Power failed: GGBS competes head-on with cheaper fly ash, and the buyer is the ready-mix producer optimising a cost-driven mix design, not the end customer. Separately, this is a capital-hungry business at a demanding moment. Every ₹1 of assets produces only ₹0.45 of sales, grinding utilisation is 62%, net debt is ₹3,635 crore, and annual capex runs above ₹2,000 crore, most of it for growth rather than upkeep (₹1,962 crore against ₹506 crore in FY26) as capacity rises from 21.6 towards 41.85/46 MTPA — but the return on that new money is not yet proven, which is why Return on Capital is the one quality area scoring negative. Cyclicality is ELEVATED on top of that, and Government Dependency is the single HIGH RISK item, since project economics lean on state subsidy schemes in Rajasthan, Punjab, Uttar Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh. The offsetting comfort is real: the negative working capital reduces how much of the build has to be externally funded, technology and fashion risk are LOW, and the one big preference shift in play — construction moving to low-carbon materials — runs in the company's favour, with 77.4% of the portfolio already green cementitious products.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, Cyclicality, Stakeholder Concentration, Good Enough Goods, New Entrants. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.7× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 122% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ✅ Returns on capital moderated from about 11% to 8% — lower, but still well clear of the deep slide this check looks for.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Project economics lean heavily on state subsidy schemes (Rajasthan 25% capital subsidy, Punjab 75% SGST for 7 years worth ~INR375-400 crore, UP 100% net SGST for 12 years, MP IPA), demand is framed around central and state infrastructure capex, and policy risk is live on both sides — analysts probed how a GST cut from 28% to 18% would shrink the incentive pool, while the Mansa (Punjab) plant slipped because environmental clearance stalled ahead of state elections, forcing a INR430 crore re-allocation of capacity to Nagaur. Limestone mining leases add a further permission-dependent layer.

> "because Punjab is getting a bit delayed... there are elections due in Punjab sometime in Feb next year. So things are a bit slow on the government side" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Demand is spread across rural/urban housing and industrial-commercial as well as infra, and incentives recognised in P&L are still small (INR3.47 crore in Q4 FY26) relative to earnings*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Cement is a price-taking commodity whose realisations swing quarter to quarter (cement realisation -3.9% quarter-on-quarter in Q3 FY26, then +4.8% quarter-on-quarter in Q4; GGBS prices had to be cut in FY25 when demand dropped), input costs track petcoke/freight cycles, and the company is spending ~INR2,000-2,300 crore of capex a year into a 21.6 to 46 MTPA expansion while carrying net debt that was 4.3x TTM EBITDA at listing. Demand is also explicitly hostage to macro shocks — April FY27 was 'relatively soft due to the inflationary pressures arising from the Middle East war situation'.

> "the prices came under significant threat and the GGBS demand reduced. We had to correct the GGBS prices marginally" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: GGBS is a consumable input into RMC with relatively stable realisation (Rs 3,239 to 3,684/MT over FY22-9MFY26) and long-term slag contracts; expansion is being funded partly by IPO equity with leverage falling to 2.9x*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 6 years with swings of ±14 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 11% and 22% (±4.5 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 20% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 30%).

### Stakeholder Concentration — ELEVATED
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but the only computed fingerprint does not confirm it — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** The GGBS franchise — 42% of volumes and the differentiated part of the business — depends on blast-furnace slag from JSW Steel, with grinding units co-located at JSW Steel plants and pricing set by a five-year contract in which JSW Steel unilaterally initiates price discovery every 2.5 years. That dependency has already hit results twice in three quarters: a Dolvi blast furnace shutdown forced third-party slag purchases in Q1 FY26, and Q4 FY26 slag unavailability at Dolvi cost ~120,000 tons of volume and raised lead distance and logistics cost.

> "there were some temporary slag availability issues at the Dolvi unit, as a result of which we had to reroute supplies... around 1.2 lakh tons of volumes is what got impacted" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Multi-year contracts secured with JSW Steel plus similar contracts with other steel producers in East India; distribution is broad (13,000+ dealers, 6,000+ direct customers, ~75% of new North dealers multi-brand)*

- ✅ Customers pay in about 48 days (was 57) — no sign of weakening bargaining power in collection terms.

> "we have consciously not increased the prices despite the cement prices having gone up. That's primarily being done because we want to drive adoption or penetration of GGBS" — *management, on an earnings call*
