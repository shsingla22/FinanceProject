Now: The opposite of obscure — a listed MNC subsidiary running well-attended investor meets with a dedicated new IR function and heavy analyst questioning. | The long view had said: This is the listed arm of the world's largest agri-input company, top-5 country for the group, with global CEO visits and heavy investor/regulatory attention — the antithesis of an ignored niche.

Now: The assessment is reasonably robust because management itself separates outcomes from skill — attributing FY25's EPS drop to China pricing and input costs and H1 gains to input stabilization and weather, not managerial brilliance. | The long view had said: Management itself separates results from skill by attributing swings to monsoon, Ukraine energy costs and Chinese pricing, which limits halo risk but also shows much of the outcome is not management-driven.

**Checks that regressed:**

Now: No evidence of switching costs or captive aftermarket — farmers reallocate freely and Chinese generic supply directly capped the company's pricing, the opposite of a lock-in. | The long view had said: Some system lock-in via the corn-seed-to-herbicide pull-through and hybrid loyalty, but no regional monopoly or true captive aftermarket — farmers can and do switch.

And from the calls: Asset-light model delivering 33% ROCE that expanded 500bps over four years, protected by owned brands (Roundup, Dekalb, Nativo) and a proprietary germplasm position.
- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: After direct input costs, the company keeps ₹41 of every ₹100 of sales — pricing power customers are happy to pay for. Now: The proposition is reliability of inputs — quality seed and crop protection that performs under climate volatility — and the corn franchise's price-plus-volume growth shows farmers pay for that certainty. | The long view had said: The core proposition is certainty — genuine product, agronomic advice and consistent hybrid performance in a market with a documented trust gap and counterfeit problem.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: UNKNOWN on the long view, UNKNOWN on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The three calls discuss seeds and crop-protection product sales only; no licence fees, AMC/service contracts, subscriptions, attach rates, renewals or deferred-revenue funding are mentioned anywhere. (The long view had said: Across all three meets the revenue model is described purely as seasonal, weather-dependent product sell-through (liquidation of crop protection and corn seed) with no licences, AMCs, subscriptions, renewal or attach rates; cash discussion is about collections and receivables, not customer advances or deferred revenue funding the business.)

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Named heritage brands (Dekalb, Roundup, Nativo, Laudis, Council Activ) and a 125-year India presence are cited as progressing through a bad season, but the calls show no market-share-plus-premium-price combination — the opposite happened in FY2025 when generic competition capped pricing. (The long view had said: Repeatedly anchored on heritage and consistency — 125+ years in India, 50 years of Roundup, 25 years of Dekalb — with sustained brand-building spend, a named top-brand stable (Dekalb, Roundup, Nativo, Laudis, Council Activ) that 'continued to progress well' through bad weather, and an anti-counterfeit scan feature in FarmRise precisely because the brand is worth faking.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Innovation is the stated identity and the corn-seed portfolio doubled in five years (15-16% CAGR) on the back of hybrid technology, but no R&D spend, pipeline economics or share-of-industry-R&D is disclosed in these calls, and the Indian entity is a distributor of global Bayer innovation rather than the R&D owner. (The long view had said: Named launches recur across the whole period with stated revenue/margin impact — Vayego, Laudis and Council in 2023, fast hybrid turnover 9108 to 9208, Bicota and Etcio Star in 2025 — and the Nov 2025 turnaround plan explicitly rests on 'backing on our R&D pipeline' with launches chosen for profitability profile, though India-level R&D spend is never disclosed since the pipeline is the parent's.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
