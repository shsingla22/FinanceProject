## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 30 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 2 improved, 5 regressed, 23 unchanged. 2 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them).

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Allocation | Good (+0.80) | Good (+0.80) | +0.00 |
| Return on Capital | Good (+1.33) | Good (+1.33) | +0.00 |
| Growth | Good (+1.17) | Good (+1.17) | +0.00 |
| Management | Good (+1.17) | Good (+0.83) | -0.33 |

| Customer Benefits | Good (+0.75) | Good (+1.00) | +0.25 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: A ₹50 billion Sri City greenfield plant (RAC, compressors, refrigerators) is under construction and funded entirely from internal accruals, on top of routine capex of 2-2.5% of revenue, clearly separating growth capex from maintenance. | The long view had said: Clearly separated growth capex (₹50bn Sri City plant doubling capacity by FY29, ₹6.57bn already deployed) versus ~2-2.5% of revenue maintenance capex, fully internally funded with a 100% capital subsidy, but incremental ROIC is unproven and return ratios have moderated.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: Deep multi-channel distribution reach extended into under-penetrated regional markets, combined with a direct service/AMC organization, gives strong accessibility and customer intimacy. | The long view had said: Deep dealer intimacy and reach — a robust offline distribution network, direct trade credit support during the downturn, and consumer research conducted household-by-household to design the Essential range.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Advertisement and promotion** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: A&P appears mainly as reactive 'festive go-to-market investments' that pressured margins rather than as disciplined, quantified brand-equity building, though brand strength is repeatedly cited. | The long view had said: A&P/channel spend is deliberate and brand-supportive (festive GTM, Cricket World Cup marketing, channel depth) and coincides with share gains, but a large part is trade promotion that cost ~1.1% of margin and management itself is rationalising promotional intensity.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Results were weak on the year (1.0% revenue growth, margin compression) while share gains were real, so the record is mixed and hard to attribute cleanly to management skill versus brand inheritance from the parent. | The long view had said: The record is testable against a bad year rather than a flattering one — FY26 revenue grew only 1.0% and margins fell, yet market share rose in every category, so operating skill is separable from the results.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.1 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Brand, manufacturing scale and BEE-compliance capability are real barriers, but margins compressed sharply under commodity/FX pressure and heavy channel support, suggesting only partial pricing rationality. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.9 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Deep backward integration (compressors, heat exchangers, PCBs, up to 86-inch panels), BEE regulatory hurdles and industry-wide price increases point to high barriers and rational pricing, offset by heavy trade promotion intensity that management is now rationalising.
- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Management quantifies misses plainly (EBITDA margin down to 4.8% in Q3, ROE/ROCE moderation, working capital build) and explains causes, though weakness is heavily attributed to external factors like weather, GST timing, FX and geopolitics. | The long view had said: Management owns misses plainly and quantifies them (Q3 below internal targets; Q4 margin bridge broken into 1.1% channel promotion, ~1% currency, 0.2% e-waste) and voluntarily explains the ROE moderation and the APA tax settlement.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Indian consumer durables is a competitive multi-brand market where LG must take price hikes cautiously and fund heavy trade support, indicating limited oligopoly pricing comfort despite category leadership. | The long view had said: The category behaves as a concentrated oligopoly in which LG sets prices and rivals follow, and the fragmented retail end customer has little negotiating power, though trade channel promotion pressure persists.

*Unchanged: Intangible benefits; Security by obscurity; Asset turn; Gain in market share; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Cyclical market growth; Assurance benefits; Distribution advantage; Structural end-market growth; Mini-monopolies; Disciplined stewards; Research and development; Geographical expansion; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Cost of working capital; Return on capital (headline); Share donators; Independent, long-term and tenacious; People matter (talent development); Out of limelight; Technology advantage; Profit margin; Customer types (retail vs corporate).*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 0 weakened, 7 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Explicit, quantified and rising share across categories in Q2 FY26 (washing machines 33.4%, refrigerators 29.9% +1pp, RAC 17.4% +0.5pp, TV 27.5% +1.4pp, side-by-side 43.2% +4.5pp), reaffirmed as continued YTD share gain in Q3 and category leadership in Q4 — though the gains coincided with margin compression and extended trade credit, which is the quality caveat. (The long view had said: Explicit, quantified share gains are reported every quarter across categories — refrigerators 29.9% (+1.0%), RAC 17.4% (+0.5%), TV 27.5% (+1.4%), side-by-side 43.2% (+4.5%) — and were sustained through a demand downturn; the caveat is that part of the gain was bought with festive/channel promotion spend that compressed margins.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (LIKELY FIT); Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Management is actively defending the mass end with its own cheaper line — the Essential Series 'combining affordability and value for money' aimed at first-time buyers in under-penetrated markets — which is a tacit acknowledgement that cheaper good-enough appliances are taking the entry segment; margins were also squeezed by 'incremental festive go-to-market investments to support our distributors and retailers'. (The long view had said: Home appliances are exactly the terrain where cheaper good-enough brands attack, and LG's own actions show the pressure: heavy channel promotion and go-to-market investments, careful language about keeping 'price positioning remain competitive', and the launch of an aggressively priced sub-INR20,000 Essential range to defend the entry tier. Offsetting this, brand awareness is high (consumers choose LG directly, no intermediary controls the sale), share rose in every category through the downturn, and price hikes of 2-3% on appliances and 7-13% on ACs stuck with competitors following.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Across all three FY26 calls, sales and margins swung sharply on weather, season, commodity prices and FX: EBITDA margin went 12.4% (Q2 FY25) to 8.9% (Q2 FY26) to 4.8% (Q3) and back to 11.7% (Q4), with full-year revenue growth of just 1.0% and ROE/ROCE explicitly 'moderated'. Demand in compressor-based categories (AC, refrigerators) is repeatedly described as hostage to a 'cooler than expected summer', festive timing and input-cost inflation, while the company simultaneously commits INR50 billion to the Sri City plant. (The long view had said: Demand is weather- and season-driven rather than customer-capex driven — a cooler summer, early monsoon and post-Diwali softness swung H1 FY26 revenue flat and cut EBITDA margin from 12.4% to 8.9% (Q2) and 7.7% to 4.8% (Q3), before Q4 rebounded to 11.7% on a hot summer; management itself notes 55% of AC business lands in Q4. This is seasonal/consumer-discretionary volatility in a flow-type replacement-and-penetration market, not a commodity or customer-capex cycle, and commodity exposure runs through input costs (copper, aluminium, FX) rather than selling prices.)
- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Policy is cited in every call as a primary swing factor: the August 2025 GST rate-cut announcement deferred then released purchases, the 2026 BEE rating transition drove inventory build and then the Q4 recovery, an INR705 crore Maharashtra incentive was booked, and the entire export-doubling plan is explicitly conditional on India-US tariff rationalisation to 18% and the India-EU FTA. B2B growth is partly credited to 'strong order inflows from government infrastructure projects', and the information display business was hit by tariffs and low infra spending. (The long view had said: Policy repeatedly and materially moves the P&L: the GST rate cut deferred then released purchases, BEE rating changes forced 7-13% price resets, e-waste recycling targets rising 60%→70% cost ~0.2% of margin, the new Labour Code hit Q3, and upside is pinned on India-US tariff rationalisation and the India-EU FTA plus an INR705 crore Maharashtra incentive and a 100% capital subsidy over 20 years at Sri City. Government also appears as a B2B customer in information display and system AC tenders. But this is a consumer-brand business whose pricing and volumes are set by the market, not a regulated tariff/subsidy-fed model.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (ELEVATED).*

*Unchanged: Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
