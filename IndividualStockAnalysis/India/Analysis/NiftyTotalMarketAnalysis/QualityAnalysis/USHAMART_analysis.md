# Usha Martin (USHAMART) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Usha Martin makes steel wire ropes and related steel wire products — thick, engineered steel cables used in equipment like mining machinery, offshore oil rigs, elevators, cranes, fishing vessels, and port infrastructure — and sells them to industrial customers, global OEMs and end users through its own sales network, channel partners and distributors. Roughly three-quarters of revenue comes from wire ropes, with the rest from plain wire/strand and LRPC (a prestressing steel strand used in construction), and the company says its profit increasingly depends on selling "value-added" ropes that need real engineering know-how rather than commodity product, which lets it charge higher realizations and pass through steel price swings. It manufactures mainly at a large plant in Ranchi, India, plus overseas units including Brunton Shaw in the UK and Usha Martin in Thailand, and sells worldwide — international markets were 55-57% of revenue, with Europe, the Americas, the Middle East and Southeast Asia all named. Revenue was about INR 3,691 crore in FY26 with roughly 19% operating EBITDA margin; the company also tracks earnings per tonne of steel sold (around INR 34,100 per tonne in FY26) and, having sold its earlier steel business, now runs debt-free with a net cash position.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Wire Rope** — The core business — steel ropes for mining, oil and offshore, elevators, cranes, fishing and ports — accounting for around 72-73% of total revenue, with 'value-added' engineered ropes making up about 71% of that segment.
- **Wire (including Wire & Strand and the 'GALSTAR' line)** — Plain and specialty steel wire, including aluminium-zinc-coated GALSTAR wire for rockfall barrier protection; the fastest-growing part lately, up around 24% in FY26.
- **LRPC** — Low-relaxation prestressed concrete strand — a more commodity-like construction steel product that is the smallest and most volatile of the three lines (down about 20% in Q4 FY26), with a newer higher-value 'plasticated LRPC' version being developed.
- **Ocean Fibre / Oceanmax (synthetic slings)** — A newly commercialised synthetic rope and sling business for offshore lifting that sits alongside the steel ropes; still small but management says the brand is established and expects meaningful scale-up from FY27.
- **Rigging shops and services (Europe, Middle East, Southeast Asia)** — Local service centres that cut, splice and fit ropes for customers — described as a way to add value, win new accounts (about 60 new customers in Saudi Arabia) and improve customer stickiness and margins rather than as a large revenue line.
- **Brunton Shaw UK** — The UK manufacturing arm and brand, being restructured into a specialist hub making only large-diameter oil and offshore ropes, with other European demand now served by direct exports from the Ranchi plant in India.
- **'One Usha Martin' program** — Not a product line but a group-wide cost and integration drive — a single SAP system, shared procurement and logistics, and fewer regional silos — credited with lower fixed employee and admin costs and better working capital.

### The story in depth

Usha Martin's story starts with a deliberate choice about what kind of business it wants to be. Rather than chasing tonnage, management has been steadily walking away from general-purpose ropes and black LRPC where realisations are unattractive, and pushing capital and capacity toward safety-critical value-added ropes for mining, elevators, cranes and oil and offshore work. The quality framework rewards this directly: growth is mix- and realisation-led rather than volume-led, with EBITDA per tonne rising from roughly INR 30,100 to INR 34,100 and margins from 17.2% to 19.1% while rope volumes grew only about 5%. Customers in these segments buy proven reliability and field-performance record, explicitly not price, and after direct input costs the company keeps INR 49 of every INR 100 of sales. The balance sheet has moved the same way, from net debt of INR 124cr in FY24 to net cash of INR 332cr by FY26, with growth capex of INR 245cr and INR 198cr funded from internal accruals and no acquisitions, alongside ROCE improving to 20.6%.

The multibagger screen picks up the same trait from a different angle. Pricing Power is a strong fit because realisations rose even when steel prices fell, and in FY26 rising steel, gas and logistics costs were passed straight through while margins still expanded, taking EBITDA per tonne to roughly INR 39,500 by Q4 FY26. The Toll Roads pattern is a likely fit for the same reason the quality checks score assurance benefits highly: the value-added ropes are engineering-specified and approval-gated, with long qualification cycles and low price sensitivity once a customer converts. Corporate Culture is the other strong fit, with the same leadership throughout, family ownership, a stable team, and bad news disclosed and quantified rather than buried, including the INR 4 crore redundancy, the INR 13 crore Wage Code cost, the 900-tonne Middle East volume miss and margin shortfalls against the 18% target. Most other patterns land only as partial fits, and the common reason is scale of proof rather than absence of evidence: new products such as GALSTAR, plasticated LRPC and Ocean Fibre synthetic slings exist and Ocean Fibre turned cash positive in its first year, but the contribution is still small; brand, channel and recurring-revenue evidence is described qualitatively without attach rates, renewal metrics or marketing spend behind it.

The risks are, in large part, the same facts read from the other side. Good Enough Goods is elevated precisely because the commodity end of the range faces cheaper substitutes head-on, which is what management is retreating from; New Entrants is a watch item because lower-cost Asian manufacturers have entered general-purpose ropes in the US and pressure is acknowledged in Europe, with management stating the retreat is deliberate policy rather than losing on price. That retreat is what keeps volumes broadly flat to plus 5% for three years and is exactly why Market Share Gainers scores only partial despite real share evidence: domestic 65-70%, US moving from 2-3% to sub 5% with US revenue share going from 7% to 9%, Europe at 8-9%, and around 60 new Saudi rigging customers. Cyclicality is elevated because demand tracks steel input prices and capex-driven end markets, though the pass-through ability and the 71-73% value-added rope mix soften it. Government Dependency sits on watch given infrastructure, port and urbanisation-driven demand plus trade-policy swing factors, offset by 57% international revenue and no regulated tariff or subsidy income. Technological Innovation and Stakeholder Concentration are both low, the first because the company owns the one genuine substitute technology through Ocean Fibre, the second because no single customer, licensor or supplier drives results. The unresolved weak spot is working capital: roughly 190 days of cash sits between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, which is why return on capital scores lowest of the seven quality areas at 0.33 and why the overall rating lands at 62 out of 100, or Decent, from 69 on business quality, 48 on multibagger fit and 68 on risk safety, with resilience judged sound.

## What to watch
