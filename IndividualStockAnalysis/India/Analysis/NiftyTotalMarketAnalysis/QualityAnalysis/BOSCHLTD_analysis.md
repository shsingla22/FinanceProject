# Bosch Ltd. (BOSCHLTD) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: AUTOMOBILE*

Bosch Limited is an India-listed company (Bangalore head office, majority-owned by Robert Bosch entities in the Netherlands and the US) that mainly makes and sells automotive parts — fuel injection and engine management systems, pumps, nozzle holders, electronic throttle bodies, fuel injectors and exhaust gas sensors — to vehicle manufacturers in India across cars, heavy and light commercial vehicles, tractors, two- and three-wheelers, plus some exports. Beyond parts for new vehicles, it earns from selling spares into the vehicle repair/aftermarket, from power tools sold to consumers and trade, from building/security systems, and from R&D engineering services billed to OEMs and to Bosch Germany, with smaller income from leasing space at its Spark.NXT campus. Revenue is essentially product sales, so results track Indian automotive production volumes and regulatory changes such as new emission norms; FY '25-'26 revenue was about INR 200,347 million with EBITDA of about INR 26,503 million and profit after tax of INR 27,702 million. Management has been reshaping the portfolio — selling the Project House Mobility Solutions, OE/OES Diagnostics and most Building Technologies businesses, and in April 2026 agreeing to buy group company Bosch Chassis Systems India (braking and safety systems) for cash as a 100% subsidiary.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Mobility business** — The umbrella automotive segment covering everything sold into vehicles and vehicle servicing; it is by far the dominant part of the company and grew 16.9% in FY '25-'26.
- **Power Solutions** — The largest division, making powertrain/propulsion parts — diesel and gasoline injection, engine management systems, common rail for gensets, throttle bodies, EGT components — and it crossed INR 130 billion of net sales in calendar 2025, growing 17.6% in FY '26.
- **Mobility Aftermarket** — Sells replacement parts and consumables — diesel systems, rotating machines, lubricants — into the vehicle repair market; a steady mid-single-digit grower (3.7% in FY '26, 8.4% in H1 FY '25).
- **Two-Wheeler and PowerSports** — Supplies fuel injectors, exhaust gas sensors and engine management systems (including the world's first CNG bike EMS) to motorcycle makers; the fastest-growing part recently, up 69.1% in FY '26 on the OBD2 norms rollout.
- **Consumer Goods** — The non-automotive power tools business — grinders, drills, cutters, spares and accessories sold through traditional trade and e-commerce — a modest but consistent grower (6.4% in FY '26).
- **Building Technologies** — Installed video surveillance and communication systems for customers and grew about 20% in FY '25, but its Video Solutions, Access and Intrusion and Communication Systems lines were sold during FY '25-'26, giving a large one-off profit.
- **Income from services (R&D services)** — Engineering and R&D work billed to vehicle OEMs and to Bosch Germany, recognised only when the customer's start-of-production dates are hit, so it is lumpy quarter to quarter (INR 1,701 million recognised in Q3 FY '23).
- **Other operating income** — Small side income from lease rentals — notably leasing out the Spark.NXT smart campus — plus export incentives and miscellaneous recoveries.
- **Bosch Chassis Systems India (being acquired)** — A group company described as India's market leader in braking and safety systems — ABS, ESP, airbag controllers, sensors and conventional actuation — with a main plant at Chakan and about 2,000 people, bought all-cash to become a 100% subsidiary and expected to lift both revenue and margins.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## The verdict: Decent — 59 out of 100 ★★★☆☆

### The story in depth

Bosch's core strength is that its parts are hard to copy and hard to swap out. The quality framework gives its highest marks to the things that protect the business rather than the things that grow it: it holds default-supplier status in fuel injection and now in braking and safety, customers buy it for reliability and guaranteed supply rather than price, and once a part is designed into a vehicle the calibration and embedded software inside it make changing supplier costly. Profit margins have moved within about three points over the years, which fits a market with rational pricing and few new arrivals, and the balance sheet is debt-free with only about thirteen days of cash tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. The multibagger screen reads the same evidence and lands on its two strongest verdicts here: 'Cost to Replicate' (ten generations of anti-skid control development, years of loss-making anti-lock brake investment before it paid, extremely low defect rates on safety-critical parts) and 'Corporate Culture' (the same chief executive and finance chief across the whole 2023-2026 stretch, conservative capital allocation, and an acquisition that was independently valued and priced). This is why the framework's industry-structure and customer-benefit areas score best of all.

The uncomfortable part is that the same trait powering the most attractive pattern also powers the single worst risk. The screen's 'Toll Roads' verdict rests on Bosch supplying small-value but essential engine management, injection, sensor and safety systems whose demand is set by law — the emissions transitions, tractor norms, on-board diagnostics, fuel-economy rules and mandatory stability and anti-lock braking. The risk review looks at the identical facts and calls Government Dependency a high risk, because growth is repeatedly explained by rule changes rather than by customers wanting more: two-wheeler revenue rose 69.1% in the latest year essentially because a diagnostics rule took effect on 1 April 2025. Regulation is a reliable customer, but it arrives in steps and does not repeat. The same doubling-up shows in pricing: the screen only gives 'Pricing Power' a partial verdict because margins improved (from 11.6% to 12.4% at the half-year, then profit up 14.7% on 10.8% revenue growth) but management credits local sourcing, product mix and volume rather than putting prices up. Its refusal to price down — 'we will not go and destroy the market' — earns a clear 'no fit' on the low-price pattern and simultaneously feeds the Good Enough Goods risk, since in the independent repair market it is the workshop, not the driver, who picks the part, and that channel has stagnated on weak diesel spares. Market-share gains are claimed rather than measured, which is why that pattern too stays partial, and the framework's growth and return-on-capital areas score lowest of all, the latter at zero.

The forward risks all trace back to what made Bosch strong in the first place: its historic stronghold was diesel injection, and management itself says diesel has fallen from 48% to 18%. That single fact drives three separate warnings — technology change, shifting customer preference, and new entrants — and management's own answer is revealing, because the remedy was to buy a chassis business that does not depend on which engine a vehicle uses. That deal scores only moderately in the framework for exactly the reason it also appears in the risk review: it is a sizeable purchase from the parent company, which decides what is moved into and out of the listed entity, while research income is billed partly to the German parent and electronics come from a group company. So the fix for the technology risk deepens the dependence-on-parent risk. Demand also swings with Indian vehicle production, with stock destocking, heavy truck weakness and then a 23.3% mobility rebound all inside the record. Against that, innovation keeps arriving with revenue attached — a hydrogen engine test facility, the world's first compressed-gas motorcycle engine management system, puncture detection and gear-shift software — and the aftermarket and service network of roughly 500 car locations and a hundredth two-wheeler workshop gives some repeat business, though management never sizes it, which is why the recurring-revenue pattern stays a likely rather than confirmed fit. The combined verdict is a 59 out of 100 'Decent' three-star rating, with sound resilience: a genuinely well-protected business whose safety score is the weakest of the three inputs because its growth engine and its biggest vulnerability are the same engine.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** Long-tenured leadership continuity (Mudlapur and CFO Karin Gilges across the whole 2023–2026 span), a debt-free 'very strong balance sheet', measured conservative capital allocation with an independently valued, conservatively priced related-party acquisition, and candid admissions of past failure (the chassis business 'was bleeding like crazy'; the divested PJMS solutions business 'continues to be quite negative even today') plus disclosed aftermarket weakness.

> "10 years ago, most of this room would have kicked us if we had done it, because this company was bleeding like crazy" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Cost to Replicate — STRONG FIT
*"Could a newcomer copy this?"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** The replication barrier is concrete and articulated: ten generations of ESP development, decades of loss-making ABS investment before profitability, 'extremely high PPM quality levels' on safety-critical parts, deep multi-year OEM co-development through each emission transition, and India's largest captive auto engineering centre (~22,000 people) supplying embedded software — none of which a funded newcomer rebuilds in a few years.

> "we have a completely captive, the largest captive engineering center of any auto company in India, close to 22,000 people work in that company" — *management, on an earnings call*

> "This campaign is designed to create battery lock-in effect by deploying targeted online and offline promotions across 25 key industrial clusters" — *management, on an earnings call*

*"Small cost, can't skip it"*

## What to watch

- Whether revenue can grow between regulatory deadlines — the 'Toll Roads' pattern and the high Government Dependency risk both hang on the 69.1% two-wheeler jump that came from the April 2025 diagnostics rule.
- Any sign that price, not just local sourcing and mix, is lifting margins — that would move 'Pricing Power' from partial to a real strength rather than a volume story.
- Further shrinkage of the diesel base beyond the 48%-to-18% fall already disclosed, which is the shared root of the Technological Innovation, Shifting Consumer Preferences and New Entrants risks.
- How the Bosch Chassis Systems purchase performs and whether more assets are moved in or out by the parent — the same deal sits behind both the merger-and-acquisition check and the Stakeholder Concentration risk.
- Recovery or continued stagnation in the independent aftermarket and diesel spares, the exposed flank named in the Good Enough Goods risk and the reason the recurring-revenue pattern is unconfirmed.
