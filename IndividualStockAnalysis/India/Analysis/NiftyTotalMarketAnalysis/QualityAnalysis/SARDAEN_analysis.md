# Sarda Energy (SARDAEN) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Sarda Energy & Minerals is an Indian company that makes steel products (iron ore pellets, billets, wire rod) and ferro alloys, and separately generates and sells electricity, describing itself as an integrated 'Energy plus Minerals' platform. It sells steel mainly into the domestic Indian market and exports a large share of its ferro alloys (about 115,000 tonnes worth roughly INR875 crores in FY24), while its power business earns money from a 600 MW independent thermal plant selling into the market and on medium/long-term contracts, and from small hydro plants selling to the Chhattisgarh state distribution company under long-term fixed-tariff agreements. Its edge is owning its own inputs: captive coal mines (in Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh and an Indonesian joint venture), captive power plants and pellet-making, so fuel and power costs are lower and more stable than peers'. Operations are centred on Raipur and Raigarh in Chhattisgarh, with a ferro alloys plant at Vizag, hydro projects in Sikkim, Chhattisgarh and Uttarakhand, and coal interests in Madhya Pradesh and Indonesia; consolidated revenue was about INR1,360 crores in the December 2025 quarter.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Steel (iron ore pellets, sponge iron, billets, wire rod)** — Makes iron ore pellets and long steel products (billets and wire rod) at Raipur, selling almost entirely in India rather than exporting; roughly 35–40% of its pellets are used in its own steel-making, and management says margins here are thin and price-driven, with cost control coming from captive coal and power.
- **Ferro alloys (Sarda Metals & Alloys Limited)** — A subsidiary with furnaces at Vizag (a third furnace added in FY23) producing ferro alloys sold to steelmakers, historically heavily exported — around 27,000–34,000 tonnes a quarter, INR200–300 crores per quarter — though by FY26 management shifted more volume to the domestic market for better realisations.
- **Mines and minerals (coal and iron ore)** — Owns and operates captive/commercial coal mines — Gare Palma IV/7 in Chhattisgarh (being raised from 1.68 to 1.8 million tonnes a year, plan approved up to 5.2 million tonnes), Shahpur West and newly won Senduri in Madhya Pradesh, Kalyani, Gare Palma IV/5, a Bartunga JV, and an Indonesian JV mine producing about 1 million tonnes a year — plus a letter of intent for the Surjagarh iron ore mine; by Nov 2025 it had six coal mines, two producing.
- **Thermal power – independent power plant (IPP, ex-SKS Power, 600 MW)** — A 600 MW coal-fired plant at Raigarh next to its own coal mine, acquired through insolvency proceedings (still under Supreme Court challenge as of Feb 2026), running at 80–90% load factor at roughly INR5 per unit, with 200 MW medium-term and 100 MW long-term offtake tied up and an expansion under approval — the single biggest driver of recent profit growth.
- **Hydropower** — Small hydro plants — 113 MW in Sikkim, Gullu in Chhattisgarh, a project in Uttarakhand, and the new 24.9 MW Rehar plant commissioned July 2025 at a fixed INR7.42 per unit under a 40-year PPA — selling to Chhattisgarh's distribution company; generation rose 28% to 621 million units in the first nine months of FY26, with three more 25 MW projects in the pipeline.
- **Captive power (thermal and solar)** — In-house coal-fired generation at Raipur (about 328 million units in Q3 FY26, almost all self-consumed) that feeds the steel and alloy plants cheaply, now being modernised with energy-efficient turbine-generator replacements and a 50 MW captive solar plant due in early FY27.
- **Energy segment overall** — Management groups thermal, hydro and captive power together and says the energy segment contributed about 70% of operating profit in Q2 FY26, making it now more important to earnings than the steel and alloys businesses.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Sarda Energy is a vertically integrated commodity producer — coal, steel, pellets, ferro alloys and electricity — and the strongest part of the record is how the people running it behave rather than what the business inherently is. Management scores highest of the seven quality areas (0.83), and the Corporate Culture pattern is the single STRONG FIT in the whole screen for the same reason: across FY23–FY26 the family and CFO team said what they would do and then did it — the third Vizag furnace ahead of schedule in August 2022, the Rehar hydro plant commissioned in July 2025 as guided, record production of coal, pellet, ferro alloys and power, and credit ratings lifted from CRISIL A+ to AA-. The 'independent, long-term and tenacious' check (+2) rests on the same evidence, including chasing the SKS Power resolution through the NCLT, the NCLAT and the Supreme Court from 2023 to 2026. That patience shows up in the numbers: roughly INR500cr a year of growth capex plus the ~INR2,000cr SKS acquisition funded out of internal accruals, with 9M FY26 profit after tax up 59%, a 17% cash return on capital, and a 31% operating margin — alongside a modest but coherent shareholder-return policy (the 811,108-share buyback at Rs.1,500 in June 2022 and the dividend raised from 75% to 100%).

What the company does not have is any structural protection, and this is where the patterns and the risks turn out to be two views of one fact. Competitive Advantage is the weakest quality area at -1.0; network effects score -2 because there is no platform of any kind, and pricing power, toll-road economics, forward integration and market-share gains are all NO FIT — management is an explicit price taker whose every quarter is explained by realisations moving with the cycle, with steel at five-year lows in Q3 FY26 and IEX power at INR3.33 against INR3.79. The one genuine edge is cost, not price: captive coal and a washery, captive pellets, captive power, 50MW of solar, energy-efficient TG sets, a railway siding for cheaper coal movement, and the option to divert captive power to merchant sale when steel spreads are thin. That same cost-led position is why Low Price Plus is only a PARTIAL fit, and it is also the stated silver lining under both the New Entrants and Good Enough Goods risks — the defence against imports and against undercutting is being low on the cost curve, because the billets, wire rod, pellets and silico-manganese carry no brand premium to defend (Brand Strength shows nothing at all in the transcripts, and Innovation Dominance is a bare quant signal with no R&D ever disclosed).

The energy pivot is the clearest case of one trait driving a pattern and a risk at once. Six coal blocks won at auction, hydro sites, and clearances management says take at least two years plus forest clearance are exactly what gives the Cost to Replicate pattern its real, time-based barrier, and the 40-year Rehar PPA at INR7.42 a unit plus the February 2026 thermal offtake are what earn Recurring Revenue its PARTIAL credit — long-duration contracted income in a business with no subscriptions. But those same assets sit inside the two HIGH RISK channels: Government Dependency, because auctions, clearances, regulated cost-plus hydro tariffs and a litigated CSPDCL transmission-charge demand set the terms, and Cyclicality, because everything outside the contracted energy book still moves with steel, ferro alloy, manganese, coal and merchant power prices — as when the May 2022 export duty 'changed the whole demand-supply dynamics' and FY24 revenue fell from INR4,212cr to INR3,869cr purely on realisations. The contracted power that lengthens revenue duration also narrows the customer list to CSPDCL and two IPP offtakes (200MW + 100MW), which is why Stakeholder Concentration sits on WATCH. Even the positive cyclical read — the +2 'cyclical market growth' check, with steel prices up 10–15% off multi-year lows and safeguard duties turning India a net exporter — is the upswing of the very exposure the risk review flags. Add a 96-day gap between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and the combined rating lands at 49 out of 100, a Mixed two-star verdict from 54 on business quality, 43 on multibagger fit and 48 on risk safety, with resilience judged SOUND.

The honest way to hold this is as a well-run, self-funded, low-cost operator that is deliberately buying its way toward steadier earnings, not as a business with a moat. The strengths are almost entirely execution and capital discipline; the patterns confirm there is no pricing power, brand, platform or measured share gain to fall back on; and the risks are largely the price of the same strategy — commodity prices and government process. What would move the verdict is therefore not a better quarter in steel but evidence that the contracted energy share of earnings is growing enough to blunt the cycle, or evidence that it is not.
