**Checks that regressed:**

- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: End markets are a mixed bag rather than a clear cycle bottom — CV and wind accelerating while mining, steel and cement stay weak and global demand is called uncertain. | The long view had said: The export/Class-8 cycle bottomed around Q4 FY24 as management called it, and the recovery has since materialised alongside a picking-up CV cycle, so the company sits on the upswing rather than the peak.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Growth is organic and self-funded from operating cash flow with an investable surplus generating other income, no leverage or large M&A, and capex continued through a soft demand year. | The long view had said: Purely organic capacity building funded from operating cash with an investable cash surplus, explicit refusal to buy volume at bad margins, and counter-cyclical investment through the FY24-25 export downturn.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: Clear leadership position in Indian rail freight bearings with railway-board qualification, supported by an aftermarket/distribution channel that is ~18% of revenue. | The long view had said: Roughly 50% share of Indian railway bearings with a dedicated centre of excellence, plus an embedded aftermarket position running roll shops inside steel plants around the clock.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: Customers in rail, mining and CV buy qualified reliability through formal approval processes, so trust and certification precede price in the purchase decision. | The long view had said: Customers buy reliability and uptime above all — zero-failure aerospace/defence requirements, longer bearing life and rail safety are the explicit purchase criteria.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: A distribution front-end at ~18% of revenue growing 8.5-11% plus hands-on senior sales engagement with large accounts indicates strong reach and customer intimacy. | The long view had said: Deep physical intimacy with customers through on-site roll shops in steel plants and direct senior-level selling, giving service reach that substitutes for price competition.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: The bearing industry is concentrated, but the calls show OEM customers with real negotiating power, resisting and dragging out price pass-through for two or more quarters. | The long view had said: Industrial and rail bearings are a stable few-player oligopoly with technical qualification barriers, but OEM customers still push back hard on price and the auto aftermarket has commoditised.
- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Rail is a durable, government-backed structural driver (DFC, Vande Bharat platform replacement, metros in multiple cities) plus localization/China+1 tailwinds, but management repeatedly caps expectations at slow-and-steady rather than secular acceleration. | The long view had said: Core end markets are in durable secular expansion — Indian rail track/wagon/Vande Bharat build-out, low per-capita steel consumption, plus sunrise defence, aviation MRO and food-processing demand.
- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Management gives full segment-wise revenue splits, quantifies one-off Labor Code and ramp-up drags, and openly admits only 10% of required price increases have been recovered, though it leans on 'ex-one-time' framing and dodged a direct utilization-target question. | The long view had said: Management quantifies each margin headwind to the basis point and openly admits missing its own utilisation target and the incomplete price pass-through rather than deflecting.

Now: Customer PPAP qualification cycles create real switching costs — even Timken's own new plant must re-qualify part by part before commercial sale, which equally deters displacement of an incumbent. | The long view had said: Customer PPAP and qualification cycles plus decades of incumbency create heavy switching costs and 'natural supplier' status on critical applications.

Now: Results are modest enough (8.6% revenue growth, PBT up ~3%, margins down year-on-year) that no halo is being generated; growth appears largely market- and volume-driven rather than clearly attributable to managerial skill. | The long view had said: Results are attributable to identifiable structural drivers (rail capex cycle, parent technology and export sourcing) that management itself separates from operating skill, so the assessment is not resting on a halo.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 4 weakened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared.

*Foundation test: UNKNOWN on the long view, UNKNOWN on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Toll Roads**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Bearings are a small-value but mission-critical component in wagons, locomotives, cement/steel/wind equipment, and every new line must clear customer PPAP qualification before commercial sale — a real switching barrier — but management never quantifies its share of customer cost or claims customers don't switch, and the May 2026 pricing fight shows customers do push back hard. (The long view had said: Across Dec-2022, May-2024 and May-2026 calls the bearing is described as a small but mission-critical component whose failure is unacceptable (rail safety, aviation, steel roll shops), sold on life and engineering rather than price, and locked in by PPAP/customer approval and testing regimes in a handful-of-players industry.)
- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Over the last four calls management delivered the promised Bharuch capitalisation and rail expansion, disclosed weak news candidly (only ~INR12–15 crore of Bharuch sales in Q3, only 10% of price recovery achieved, labour-code and ramp-up hits bridged explicitly), and funds growth from operating cash — though the multi-year record needed to confirm long-term culture is outside this window. (The long view had said: Sanjay Koul led the calls consistently from 2022 through FY26, the CRB/SRB plant promised in Nov-2022 was tracked on schedule in May-2024 and capitalised and shipping by FY26, capital is committed only against an explicit return test (asset turn >2x), and shortfalls are admitted plainly rather than spun.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The four calls break revenue into rail/mobile/distribution/process/export front-ends only; no attach rates, AMC/service contracts, renewal rates, deferred revenue or customer advances are ever discussed, and when an analyst asked about replacement vs OEM demand management answered with segment volumes rather than any recurring-stream disclosure. (The long view had said: There is a genuine replacement/MRO annuity behind the installed base (distribution 17-19% of sales every year, plus embedded service presence inside customer plants), but across all 15 calls management never discusses licence fees, AMC contracts, attach rates, renewals or customer advances/deferred revenue as a funding source.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The May 2026 call shows the opposite of pricing power: facing steel, grinding wheel, coolant and currency inflation, management had recovered only 10% of the increase, expects a two-quarter lag, and describes customer resistance — price follows cost with a drag rather than leading it. (The long view had said: Price realisation is a standing management effort — pushed even when steel prices softened (May 2024) — but the May 2026 call shows recovery is negotiated, lagged and incomplete rather than routine above-inflation increases, with OEMs resisting.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

*Not comparable: Forward Integrators (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 4 unchanged; 2 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Rail is the single largest front-end at ~24-25% of revenue and management explicitly ties it to government spending, railway-board tendering and even payment cycles between the railways and wagon builders; separately, export prospects are repeatedly framed around US tariffs and India-US/EU trade deals rather than company action. The exposure is real but bounded — three-quarters of revenue sits in mobile, distribution, process and intercompany exports. (The long view had said: Rail is the single largest front-end (23% of FY26 sales, 34% of Q4FY24) and its demand is set by Indian Railways budgets, mega wagon tenders, electrification and Vande Bharat programmes; management repeatedly frames the outlook around 'the intent of the Rail Mantralaya', flags elections as a challenge, and in FY26 ties export/Bharuch loading to India-US and India-EU trade deals and BIS norms.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Around 20% of revenue is 'export intercompany' — i.e. sales routed through the parent Timken Company's global system rather than an independently won customer base — and the parent also determines the product mandate (the GGB plain-bearing line, and the Board-approved merger of Timken GGB into TIL). Loading of the new Bharuch plant is expected to come from parent-directed supply-chain realignment, concentrating dependence on one relationship. (The long view had said: Two relationships drive a large share of results: exports are reported as 'export intercompany' to the Timken parent (21% of FY26 sales, INR707 crore) with volumes decided by the parent's global sourcing and priced via a BAPA, while rail effectively means one buyer, Indian Railways, where the company claims ~50% share; the parent also supplies the technology, the traded imported bearings (~35% of sales) and the GGB business being merged in.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Not comparable: New Entrants (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
