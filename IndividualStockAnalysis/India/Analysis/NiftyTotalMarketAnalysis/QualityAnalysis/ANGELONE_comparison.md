Now: Some stickiness exists via demat custody and the assisted partner network, but the calls present no evidence of meaningful switching costs in a market where account portability is easy. | The long view had said: Switching costs in discount broking are low — demat accounts are portable and clients routinely hold multiple brokers — with only soft cohort stickiness offered as evidence of lock-in.

Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.1 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Barriers are low in digital broking and the dominant industry variable in the window is regulatory change rather than any structural entry barrier protecting incumbents. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±11.8 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. ## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 1 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
