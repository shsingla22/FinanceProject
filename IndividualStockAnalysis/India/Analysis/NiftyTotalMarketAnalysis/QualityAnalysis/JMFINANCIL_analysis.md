# JM Financial Ltd. (JMFINANCIL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

JM Financial Limited is an India-based financial services group, headquartered in Mumbai and listed on the BSE and NSE, that advises companies on raising money and buying/selling businesses, lends and invests its own capital, and manages and distributes investments for wealthy and retail savers. It earns money three ways: fees and commissions from investment banking deals (IPOs, QIPs, block trades, M&A and private equity advisory), broking and wealth/asset management; interest and recovery income from loans it makes and from distressed loans it buys; and, increasingly, fees from arranging (syndicating) credit deals for outside investors rather than holding them on its own balance sheet. Its customers are Indian corporates and promoters, real estate developers, institutional and high-net-worth investors, retail investors reached through 70-odd branches and roughly 900 franchisees, and low-income home buyers. In FY26 the group reported revenue-driven profit after tax of about INR1,202 crores on a net worth of about INR10,605 crores (book value roughly INR111 per share), after a strategic pivot announced in May 2024 that shrank its wholesale lending book and shifted the group toward fee-based businesses.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Corporate Advisory and Capital Markets (CACM) — investment banking and institutional equities** — Advises companies on IPOs, QIPs, block deals, M&A and private equity transactions and runs an institutional stockbroking desk; it is the flagship fee business, with FY26 net revenue of about INR789 crores, operating PAT of INR347 crores on only INR829 crores of capital employed (about 47% ROE), 41 capital market deals worth roughly INR95,000 crores closed in FY26, and 55 IPO filings worth about INR1,40,000 crores in the pipeline; management says it ranked #1 in QIPs in calendar 2024 and #2 among Indian banks in M&A/PE advisory.
- **Wealth and Asset Management (earlier called 'Platform AWS' — Asset management, Wealth, Securities)** — Manages and distributes investments for high-net-worth clients (private wealth), mid-tier 'elite' clients and retail savers through independent financial advisers, plus retail broking, margin funding and portfolio management; wealth AUM was about INR1,10,000 crores in Dec 2024 with recurring AUM of roughly INR31,000 crores in FY26, FY26 net revenue INR775 crores and operating PAT INR132 crores, and the group is deliberately spending on 1,046 sales staff, branches and technology to grow it.
- **Asset Management (mutual fund and alternative funds), reported within Wealth and Asset Management** — Runs mutual fund schemes and is launching alternative investment funds (a pre-IPO fund, a real estate fund and a credit fund); it is still small and loss-making — non-liquid mutual fund average AUM of about INR10,500 crores in FY26, mutual fund management fees of roughly INR44 crores (up 65%), and a loss after minority interest of about INR30 crores for FY26.
- **Private Markets (private credit — corporate, bespoke, real estate and distressed credit — plus investments)** — Lends to and invests in companies, promoters and real estate developers, buys and recovers distressed loans through its ARC, and holds private equity fund and REIT stakes; since the 2024 pivot it is shifting from holding loans on its own books (wholesale book fell from INR7,529 crores in Mar 2024 to INR4,207 crores by Dec 2024) to originating deals and syndicating them to investors, and it delivered FY26 operating profit before tax of INR742 crores and PAT of about INR543 crores on roughly INR6,600 crores of capital employed, helped by over INR270 crores of recoveries.
- **Affordable Home Loans (home finance business)** — Makes small home loans (and earlier loans against property) to lower-income borrowers through a branch network that has grown to 151 branches and over 33,000 customers; AUM was about INR3,460 crores in FY26, up 22% year-on-year, with FY26 revenue up 25%, collection efficiency near 99%, gross NPA under 1%, and management targeting 25%+ annual growth for the next three years.
- **Legacy segments as reported before the FY26 format change** — Until the new segment format took effect, the group reported an Investment Bank segment, a Mortgage Lending segment (wholesale real estate lending plus retail/affordable mortgages, once the biggest piece of a INR14,670 crore loan book in Sep 2022), a Distressed Credit segment (ARC and alternative credit, about INR11,349 crores of AUM in Sep 2022), and Platform AWS; these have since been folded into CACM, Private Markets, Wealth and Asset Management, and Affordable Home Loans.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

JM Financial is best described as a well-run firm in the middle of a deliberate rebuild, which is why the combined rating lands at 40 out of 100 — a Mixed, two-star verdict built from 59 for business quality, only 10 for multibagger fit and 42 for risk safety, with underlying resilience judged SOUND. The clear strength is the people running it: Management scores highest of any area (1.33), with top marks for candor, disciplined stewardship and independent, tenacious long-term thinking. Since May 2024 they shrank a ₹10,000 crore wholesale lending book by roughly 90%, cut borrowings from ₹16,145 crores to ₹12,143 crores, brought debt to equity down to 1.1x, took provision cover to 93% early and on purpose ('it's just good to clean it up'), and beat their own recovery guidance with ₹270 crores against ₹250–300 crores promised. They were equally open about the bad news — the lost deals after the March 2024 SEBI order, the bad-loan spike in affordable housing, and separating a one-off tax refund from real operating profit. The same discipline shows up in the Corporate Culture pattern, which is graded PARTIAL largely on this delivery record.

The catch is that the very actions that earn those management marks are what depress the returns. Return on Capital is the weakest area (-0.67), with the headline check at its floor: every ₹100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings produced just ₹3 of operating cash this year. Shrinking the lending book, provisioning heavily and funding a stated two-year investment phase all sit behind that number. The investment itself is visible and real — affordable-housing branches went from 75 to 151, the wealth relationship-manager base grew 41%, plus digital and technology spend — but the Capex-for-growth check gives only a middling score because the payoff is still unproven. That same spending is why the Low Price Plus pattern is marked NO FIT: management repeatedly flags margin compression from 'increase in investments both in talent as well as infrastructure', with wealth operating profit after tax falling to ₹19 crores from ₹30 crores. Assets are heavy (₹1 of assets generates ₹0.16 of sales) and about 124 days of cash sits tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, though the pivot from balance-sheet lending toward fees and syndication is genuinely lighter on capital in the capital-markets arm. Growth has averaged about 11% a year and margins are fat at ₹63 of operating profit per ₹100 of sales, but both swing year to year (margins by roughly ±7.7 points), which points to costs and conditions management does not control.

On the pattern screen the company fits nothing cleanly — eight PARTIAL verdicts, three NO FIT and no outright matches, hence the score of 10. The reason connects directly to the risk review. Its most defensible asset is a multi-decade investment-banking franchise and client relationships, but management's own account admits the edge is people-based and mobile, so Competitive Advantage scores only 0.33 and Cost to Replicate stays PARTIAL. Pricing Power is NO FIT because prices are falling, not holding: wholesale mortgage yields dropped from above 15% to 13.5–14%, roughly 100 to 125 basis points, and broking yields fell as volume shifted into derivatives — the same evidence that makes Good Enough Goods an ELEVATED risk, since credit is close to a commodity. The biggest single trait cuts both ways. Being tied to the capital-market cycle is the top-scoring growth check, because the firm sits at what management calls the FY26 bottom with a record ₹140,000 crore IPO pipeline waiting on foreign-investor flows to return; it is also the HIGH RISK entry, because when those windows shut the earnings go with them — investment-banking segment profit collapsed to ₹39 crores in the fourth quarter of FY26. The hedge against it is the recurring-revenue push, which is why that pattern is the strongest PARTIAL: recurring assets under management up 33% to about ₹33,100 crores, now 29% of the total, alongside mutual fund assets up threefold to around ₹13,800 crores. That shift is also why Shifting Consumer Preferences is rated LOW — domestic ownership of Indian equities has risen from about 8% to 18% over twenty years and systematic investment flows remain strong. Regulation, not government contracts, is the gatekeeper (the SEBI order, RBI approval for consolidating the credit and asset-reconstruction arm), and lumpiness lingers in single ₹50–150 crore loan tickets and roughly ₹685 crores of bad loans concentrated in a few property exposures — being addressed by spreading into 33,000-plus affordable-housing customers at an average ticket of about ₹12 lakh.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Technological Innovation, New Entrants, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 1.1× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 127% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ✅ Returns on capital moderated from about 14% to 10% — lower, but still well clear of the deep slide this check looks for.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 2 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Earnings are tied to the capital-market cycle at almost every level: IB revenue depends on IPO/QIP windows that shut when FPIs sell (Q4 FY26 segment PAT collapsed to INR39 crores), brokerage and transactional wealth income swing with volumes, and the wholesale real-estate book rode a cycle from INR10,000 crores down to INR1,000 crores with INR800 crores of provisions. Management itself concedes the business can only be judged 'peak-to-peak', which is the analytical trap of cyclicals — you cannot know where in the cycle current margins sit.

> "The performance for the quarter ended March 2026 was impacted by lack of primary issuances amidst headwinds emerging from geopolitical issues." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate pivot toward recurring revenue (recurring AUM now 29% of wealth AUM, growing 33% Y-o-Y), steadily compounding affordable home loans (AUM +22-23% Y-o-Y, GNPA 0.5%), and countercyclical investment — hiring aggressively and taking full provisions while competitors were 'disrupted'.*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 4 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 53% and 79% (±7.7 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 18% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 1% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Technological Innovation — ELEVATED
*"Out-invented by the industry"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Broking and distribution — a large share of the Wealth segment's revenue — sit in an industry reshaped by digital-first platforms, and management is spending continuously on digital/technology and branch-plus-franchisee reach to keep pace, with wealth margins compressed by that spend. But no call describes a platform shift making JM's model legacy, and the innovation discussed is incremental rather than a format change.

> "There is a lot of investment also that is going in towards the digital experience." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Small-scale, continuous innovation cadence (digital experience, data/securitisation platform build) rather than a defensive response to a new technology platform; advisory, syndication and distressed credit are relationship businesses that are hard to automate away.*

- ⚠️ Growth has stalled: sales compounded at 16% a year in the first half of the record but only 6% recently — demand may be structurally fading.
- ✅ Operating margin (63%) remains near its historical peak (79%) — no collapse in the premium.

## What to watch

- Whether the record ₹140,000 crore IPO pipeline actually converts — the Cyclical market growth check and the HIGH RISK Cyclicality verdict both hinge on FY26 proving to be the bottom rather than a plateau.
- Whether recurring assets under management keep climbing past 29% of the total — this is the single lever that would turn the PARTIAL Recurring Revenue pattern into a genuine cushion against deal-driven earnings swings.
- Whether the 3% cash return on capital lifts as the two-year investment phase ends — the Return on Capital check is the largest single drag on the quality score, and the Capex-for-growth check is being given the benefit of the doubt until returns appear.
- Whether wealth-segment profits recover from ₹19 crores — falling from ₹30 crores while spending on talent, technology and branches is what drives both the Low Price Plus NO FIT and the ELEVATED Technological Innovation risk.
- Whether lending yields stabilise after the fall from above 15% to 13.5–14% — further compression would confirm the Good Enough Goods risk and keep Pricing Power at NO FIT, while stabilisation would strengthen the Cost to Replicate case.
- Whether the ₹685 crores of concentrated property bad loans keep resolving on guidance, and whether affordable housing keeps broadening the customer base — the test for the Stakeholder Concentration WATCH and for the stewardship record that carries the Management score.
