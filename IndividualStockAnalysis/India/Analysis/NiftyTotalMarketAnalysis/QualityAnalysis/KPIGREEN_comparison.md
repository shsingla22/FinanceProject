**Checks that improved:**

- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹36 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±2.8 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹36 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±9.1 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.8 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Barriers are low and pricing is set by competitive bidding, with tariffs squeezed further as ISTS waivers step down; management's own comfort with falling panel and battery prices signals a deflationary, price-competitive industry. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±9.1 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Entry barriers are low outside the land/evacuation constraint and pricing is set by competitive bidding rather than rational discipline, with returns compressed to 12-14% IRR.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Poor → Weak. Now: Presentation is heavily promotional — 'landmark', 'record-breaking', 'India's first', a chairman's-office spokesperson opening calls with mission rhetoric, and headline MOU announcements (Botswana INR36,000 crores) far ahead of execution. | The long view had said: Highly promotional posture: a steady stream of headline MOUs (Botswana, South Korea, Delta, Gujarat) with little follow-through, and growth commitments made by the CMD on television.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: Indian renewable EPC/IPP is a crowded, reverse-auction market with powerful counterparties — the company works as a BoS subcontractor to Adani Green and bids into GUVNL/SJVN/NTPC tenders, so the end customer holds the negotiating power. | The long view had said: The sector is fragmented and competitively tendered against far larger players (Adani, NTPC, SJVN), while the key counterparty GUVNL is a monopsony discom that sets tariffs.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Poor → Weak. Now: Growth is overwhelmingly volume/capacity-led (56% FY26 revenue growth from new megawatts) with tariffs fixed by competitive bidding under 25-year GUVNL PPAs and price-variation clauses that pass through cost, not pricing power; mix shift toward IPP annuity is the only positive lever. | The long view had said: Growth is purely volume (megawatts) driven; IPP tariffs are locked by 25-year PPAs and CPP is sold at a discount to grid, with price-variation clauses used only to defend, not expand, margins.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: Massive, almost entirely growth-oriented capex — ~INR5,500 crores across the 250MW solar/370MW hybrid/150MW wind IPP builds plus ~INR1,000-1,100 crores for BESS, with fixed assets rising to INR5,427 crores and total assets more than doubling to INR9,882 crores in FY26; returns are stated but modest (13-14% IRR on BESS). | The long view had said: Growth capex is enormous and rising (assets doubling yearly, capex funded 2:1 debt:equity) but incremental returns are falling, with new IPP/BESS projects underwritten at only 12-14% IRR and ROCE sliding from 18-19% to 13-14%.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: The customer base is almost entirely corporate/government (GUVNL, SJVN, NTPC, Adani, Aditya Birla, captive industrial users), matched with reliability and evacuation-readiness, but with no switching-cost lock-in once a contract is delivered. | The long view had said: Customer base is corporate and government, correctly served with TCO and reliability plus 25-year lease/O&M lock-in rather than brand intangibles.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Some regional entrenchment in Gujarat via a 6,275-acre land bank, 3.2GW of evacuation capacity and 108 project sites gives a local advantage, but PPA counterparties face no switching costs once a plant is built and sold on 25-year fixed terms. | The long view had said: A genuine regional advantage exists in South Gujarat through scarce, pre-secured land and grid evacuation approvals that new entrants cannot quickly replicate, plus 25-year captive contracts that lock customers in.

Now: The opposite of obscure — a listed, heavily promoted company in India's most capital-attracting sector, courting international lenders, rating agencies, a subsidiary IPO and a sovereign MOU. | The long view had said: Renewable energy in India is the opposite of an ignored niche — it is a capital magnet with heavy analyst attention and constant tender competition, though KPI's South Gujarat CPP niche is somewhat less crowded.

Now: Management deflects rather than engages on hard questions — the margin-compression query was answered by switching to a standalone basis that showed improvement, and the EPS/dilution concern was redirected to share price and 'market dynamics'. | The long view had said: Mixed — the CFO answers hard questions on ROE decline, project cost overruns and interest burden directly, but the promoter deflected a basic question on competitive advantage and MOU progress questions get non-answers.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 1 weakened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is B2B execution reputation — a 100 MW repeat order from Avichal Power, repeat balance-of-plant awards from Adani Green (534 MW then 1.13 GW), and management crediting 'customer trust' — but no heritage story, no A&P spend, no premium pricing and no consumer brand to stretch into adjacencies. (The long view had said: No heritage narrative, A&P spend, distributor leverage or consumer-facing brand discussion appears in any of the eight calls; customer trust is referenced only generically.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: The company keeps adding new technology segments each year (BESS, floating solar, green hydrogen/green ammonia JV with AHES Korea, EV charging, IBM Maximo-based NOC and robotic O&M) and converts them into named orders, but no R&D spend is disclosed, no share of industry R&D is claimed, and the technology is licensed or partnered in rather than developed — this is adoption, not R&D-led dominance. (The long view had said: KPI is a project developer and EPC contractor, not a technology owner — it explicitly does not manufacture panels, discloses no R&D spend in any call, and its 'technology' items (IBM Maximo NOC, Delta MOUs, BESS/hydrogen MOUs) are bought-in or nascent, with management conceding the new products cannot yet be costed.)
- **Forward Integrators**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: KPI has moved down the chain from EPC contractor to owning generation assets, land, evacuation infrastructure, and in FY26 obtained both CERC interstate and GERC intrastate trading licences giving direct market access — a genuine step toward the end customer — but there is no retail/franchise/D2C dimension and no disclosure of what share of revenue this direct channel carries. (The long view had said: No own stores, franchising, licensing or D2C dimension exists in this business model and none is discussed; the CERC/GERC trading licences are wholesale market access, not forward integration to an end consumer.)

**That weakened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The calls contain no cost-culture evidence — no itemised savings, no no-frills choices, no discussion of passing scale savings to price. The only cost remark is opportunistic (benefiting from cheaper imported panels in a tariff price war), which is not a replicable cost advantage. (The long view had said: The captive proposition is cheaper-than-grid power delivered fast (3–6 months vs peers' 18) with an 85% generation guarantee, and margins hold up (PAT 16–17%) — but across all eight calls there is no cost-obsession culture, no itemised savings, and no evidence the cost edge is hard to copy rather than a function of Gujarat land and policy timing.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: A large share of revenue is customer-capex work — CPP/EPC and balance-of-plant supply for others' capacity expansion (Adani, SJVN, Aditya Birla) — which is the dangerous form of customer cyclicality, and the company is itself investing hard into the boom (total assets more than doubling to INR9,882 crores, fixed assets INR5,427 crores). Input-commodity swings (silver, panel and battery prices) and even weather cut through results, with management attributing weaker H1 power sales to the rainy season. (The long view had said: The CPP/EPC leg (the majority of revenue for most of the period) is capex-linked — it is built when customers install captive plants — and input commodities swing (silver/panel prices, 'battery prices are fluctuating'); the revenue-mix shift alone moved EBITDA margin from 47.6% to 32.65% in FY23, and inventory rose 4x as the company stocks panels ahead of orders. Countering this, the IPP book is a fixed-tariff annuity.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The output is an undifferentiated commodity — electrons sold into reverse-auction tenders and EPC work won on price — and management itself stresses it is 'not a manufacturer of panels', i.e. it assembles third-party hardware with no proprietary product. The guided 13-14% IRR on the BESS project, dependent on VGF and falling battery prices, shows returns are set by the tender, not by any defensible premium. (The long view had said: The core offering is undifferentiated EPC of bought-in panels won through competitive tenders — KPI is 'not a manufacturer of panels' — so returns are set by bid tariffs (13-14% IRR on the GUVNL BESS, a stated floor of 12%), and when asked in FY23 what its USP was, management could only answer speed and 'style', not a protected product advantage.)
- **Fashion Risk**: QUANT FLAG → LOW. Why now: Demand is functional infrastructure, not taste-driven: revenue comes from 25-year PPAs and contracted EPC work with utilities and industrial customers, and no call attributes growth to a trending product, brand or celebrity moment. (The long view had said: 1 numeric fingerprint is present but the calls offer nothing either way — numbers alone are only ever a flag to investigate, never a verdict.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: GUVNL is named in every call as counterparty to the 250 MW solar, 370 MW hybrid, 150 MW wind and 445/890 MWh BESS — i.e. essentially the whole ~INR5,500 crore IPP capex programme rests on one state discom, and the SBI Project Finance Unit provided the single INR3,200 crore term loan behind it. On the EPC side a single customer, Adani Group, scaled from 534 MW at Khavda (Q3) to 1.13 GW of balance-of-plant orders by Q4. (The long view had said: IPP economics hinge on one counterparty — GUVNL — for the 250MW solar, 370MW hybrid, 150MW wind and 445/890MWh BESS awards, with management defending it as 'top most A rated discom' rather than pointing to a diversified offtaker base; the CPP/EPC side has likewise become anchored on a few large names (Adani Green BOP 1.13GW, SJVN, CIL, NTPC). Geography is also concentrated in Gujarat.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (QUANT FLAG).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
