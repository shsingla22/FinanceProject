# Senco Gold (SENCO) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Senco Gold (brand name "Senco Gold & Diamonds") is an Indian jewellery retailer that designs and sells gold jewellery, diamond/studded jewellery, silver and platinum to everyday retail consumers — mostly for weddings, festivals like Dhanteras and Akshaya Tritiya, gifting and daily wear. It makes money by selling jewellery through a chain of physical showrooms, earning a margin on the metal plus making charges on the craftsmanship, so revenue rises both with gold volumes and with gold prices; roughly two-thirds of sales come from its own stores and about a third from franchisee-run stores. Its stronghold is Eastern India, especially West Bengal/Kolkata, and it has been expanding into North India, Central India and Tier 2/3/4 towns — the network grew from about 140 stores in mid-2023 to 196 by December 2025, with 200+ targeted. Full-year FY26 revenue was about Rs.8,430 crore, up roughly 33% year-on-year, with management guiding to around 7.5% EBITDA margin and 4%-4.5% PAT margin on a normal year.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Gold jewellery** — The core product — plain gold jewellery in 22-carat and, increasingly, lighter-weight and more affordable 14-carat and 9-carat pieces; it is the large majority of sales (the diamond/stud share is only about 10%-11%, implying gold and other metals are the rest).
- **Diamond / studded jewellery (the "stud ratio")** — Diamond-set jewellery, which management repeatedly calls the key profitability lever because it carries better margins than plain gold; it has run at roughly 9%-11% of sales versus an industry average near 15%, and raising this ratio is a standing strategic goal — in FY26 diamond sales grew 32% in value and 9% in volume.
- **Silver and platinum** — A smaller supporting product line sold alongside gold, growing at roughly mid-teens percentages in value in the periods discussed.
- **Company-owned company-operated (COCO) showrooms** — Stores Senco owns and runs itself, contributing about 65% of revenue in Q3 FY26 and carrying higher profitability than franchise sales, with roughly 8-10 new ones opened per year.
- **Franchisee stores** — Asset-light stores run by partners, about 33% of revenue in Q3 FY26; management wants to push this model harder because it lets them scale into smaller Tier 2/3/4 towns without tying up their own capital.
- **Old gold exchange** — Customers trade in their existing gold and put it toward new jewellery — a major source of volume that rose from about 24% of purchases in FY24 to roughly 44% of FY26 revenue (about 50% in Q4 FY26), and which also supplies recycled gold instead of imports.
- **Swarna Yojana monthly instalment scheme** — A savings/advance-booking plan where customers pay monthly instalments toward a future jewellery purchase; as of Q1 FY24 it had about 80,000 customers and around Rs.200 crore of customer deposits outstanding, giving the company both committed future sales and low-cost funding.
- **Digital / online channel** — Website and online presence used partly for direct sales but mainly to generate leads that convert in stores — management estimated about 12% of business was "digitally led" in Q1 FY24; other channels together were about 2% of revenue in Q3 FY26.
- **In-house design and product development** — A design studio function management calls its core identity ("a house of design") — roughly 6,000-9,000 new gold designs and 3,000-6,000 diamond designs launched per quarter and over 1.5 lakh designs in FY26, tilted toward lightweight pieces that fit customer budgets at high gold prices.
- **Sennes Fashion (new-format subsidiary)** — A separate company set up in FY25 to open a handful of stores selling lab-grown diamonds, leather bags and perfumes — described as a small, long-term diversification bet rather than a current earnings driver.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Senco Gold is a jewellery maker that also owns its shops. It runs its own factories (about three-quarters of the jewellery is handmade) and sells through a network that has grown from 140 stores in August 2023 to 196 by February 2026, with 200-plus targeted and franchisees contributing about a third of revenue. That combination is why the strongest pattern in the screen is Forward Integrators, a clear fit. Around it sit two genuinely helpful outside forces: Indian jewellery as a whole is expanding from roughly $58bn toward $80bn, and the organised share of it is climbing from 33-38% to 45-50% as hallmarking and GST squeeze small unorganised jewellers who lack capital and execution ability. Senco is visibly picking up those customers - a 24% five-year revenue CAGR, roughly half of buyers being first-timers, FY26 revenue of Rs.8,430 crore at 33% growth. Management also scores well on candour: it volunteered the Rs.57 crore duty hit and the Rs.89 crore hedging loss with slide-level workings, accepted an investor's rebuke over a growth-rate mix-up, and has repeatedly beaten its own conservative guidance (20-25% guided, 33-35% delivered). Management is the highest-scoring quality area at 1.2.

The trouble is that several of the same traits that create the patterns also create the risks. Growing by opening more inventory-heavy stores builds a barrier that is expensive to copy, but it is also why about 259 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and why the headline return on capital is negative - every Rs.100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings produced Rs.-15 of operating cash this year. Growth and cash generation are pulling against each other, which is why Return on Capital scores -0.33 while Growth scores 0.67. The same tension shows up in the customer-advance schemes (Swarna Yojana, and later advances with the gold rate fixed): they fund the business cheaply, which is why Recurring Revenue rates a partial fit, but they are tied to the same working-capital problem rather than being a true renewal stream. Gold itself cuts both ways. Making charges are levied on value, so they rise automatically with the gold price - that is the pricing power - yet management explains both revenue and margin through the gold cycle in all twelve quarters, FY26's 33% growth came largely from a roughly 60% gold price rise while volumes actually fell, and reported EBITDA has swung down to 3.4-3.9%. Cyclicality is rated elevated for exactly the reason pricing power looks good. Policy is the third example: hallmarking rules opened up the 9-carat line and are pushing customers toward branded players, while the FY25 customs duty cut alone cost Rs.57-60 crore of EBITDA. Government dependency is the one high risk, and it is inseparable from the industry tailwind.

What is thin is the moat itself. Competitive Advantage scores 0.0 and Industry Structure 0.17. Brand strength and market-share gains are only 'likely' fits, not proven ones - the company never gives its own market-share figure - and the cost-to-replicate barriers, while real (Bengal heritage, the karigar network, the store base), are ones management itself concedes are under attack from newly listed rivals spending IPO and QIP money to open competing stores. Because the gold content is a transparent commodity, the entire premium rests on making charges of 15-16% that must be actively defended, and Senco admits it has to price lower and fund discounts where it is the challenger brand, such as Dehradun. That is why the advertising spend scores only 1: it converts into repeat business, but part of it is defensive discounting. Two patterns do not apply at all - Friendly Middleman and Toll Roads - because demand comes from festive and wedding footfall, not from a professional who recommends the product. Consumer preferences are shifting toward lighter, 9-carat and 14-carat pieces, and Senco has moved first on those, but that keeps volumes structurally declining. Growth of about 30% a year is also lumpy year to year, so persistence scores -1. The combined rating is 50 out of 100 - Decent, three stars - built from 58 on business quality, 41 on multibagger fit and 46 on risk safety, with resilience judged strained.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Policy repeatedly and materially moves earnings: the FY25 customs duty cut cost Rs.57-60 Cr of EBITDA and Rs.41 Cr of PAT, the FY26 duty hike is now creating inventory gains, BIS 9-carat hallmarking permission opened a product line, mandatory hallmarking/HUID is the stated driver of the unorganized-to-organized shift, and in May 2026 the Prime Minister's statement on curbing gold imports directly caused footfalls and demand to drop within 7-10 days.

> "we have taken into account Rs.58-60 Cr duty cut impact on the overall numbers" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Not a licensed/tariff-set business — pricing and demand are market-driven, and current policy direction (old gold exchange, hallmarking) is a tailwind rather than a headwind*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across all 12 quarters management explains both revenue and margins primarily through the gold-price cycle: FY26's 33% top-line growth is credited to a ~60% gold price rise while volumes fell, and reported EBITDA has swung from 3.4-3.9% (Q2/Q3 FY25, after the customs-duty cut and Rs.89 Cr hedging loss) to 13.7% in Q4 FY26 — with management itself splitting the 10.8% 9M margin into ~7.8-8% 'business margin' and 2-2.5% 'price rise'. Working capital is also cycle-geared: inventory rose 61% to Rs.5,296 crore and hedge ratios were cut from 90% to 40-50% under margin-call and liquidity pressure.

> "the gold prices have moved up by almost 60% from the beginning of the year" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/occasion-linked demand (weddings, Dhanteras, Akshaya Tritiya) rather than customer capex; old gold exchange now ~50% of transactions, which management says partially neutralises price risk; countercyclical store additions (~20/yr) through the volatility*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 0 of the last 7 years with swings of ±25 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 5% and 11% (±1.8 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.

## What to watch

- Whether the working capital cycle shortens from ~259 days and the negative -15% cash return on capital turns positive - the 'cost of working capital' and 'return on capital' checks are the two weakest scores and the main drag on the rating.
- Whether volumes stop falling if gold prices stall - the Cyclicality risk means FY26's 33% growth leaned on a ~60% price rise, so the Pricing Power and Market Share Gainers patterns are untested without that tailwind.
- Whether making charges hold near 15-16% as newly funded rivals expand - erosion would confirm the 'Good Enough Goods' and 'New Entrants' risks and further weaken the already-zero Competitive Advantage score.
- The next policy move on customs duty or hallmarking - Government Dependency is the single HIGH risk, and a single duty change already cost Rs.57-60 crore of EBITDA.
- Whether store additions of 18-20 a year keep earning the FY26 22% ROCE rather than drifting to management's 15.5-16% sustainable guide - this is what makes the 'capex for growth' check and the Forward Integrators pattern worth paying for.
