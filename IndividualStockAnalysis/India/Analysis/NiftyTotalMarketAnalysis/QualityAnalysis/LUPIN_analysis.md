# Lupin Ltd. (LUPIN) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: PHARMA*

Lupin Limited is a Mumbai-headquartered pharmaceutical company that makes and sells medicines — mostly generic copies of off-patent drugs, plus branded prescription medicines, the active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) that go into them, and more recently biosimilars and 'complex' products like inhalers and long-acting injectables. It earns money by selling these to two broad customer groups: in the US and other developed markets it sells generics into the trade/distribution channel where price erosion is constant and profits come from being early or one of few players (for example being the only generic on Tolvaptan), while in India and emerging markets it sells branded prescription medicines promoted by a field force of nearly 12,000 sales reps to doctors across cardiac, respiratory, diabetes, GI and other therapies. Growth comes from a steady stream of new product launches out of an R&D pipeline (over 50 active products, R&D at about 7.5% of sales in FY26), backed by its own manufacturing plants that must stay in good standing with the US FDA. In FY26 total revenue was about INR 27,958 crores with EBITDA margins of 29.7%, with the US at roughly USD 1.3 billion and the rest spread across India, Europe/Canada/Australia, and emerging markets like Brazil, Mexico, the Philippines and South Africa.

**The parts of the business:**

- **US business (North America)** — Sells generic and increasingly 'complex' medicines — respiratory inhalers (Albuterol, Tiotropium), complex injectables (Risperdal Consta, Glucagon, Liraglutide), and now its first biosimilar (Pegfilgrastim) — into the US market; it is the single largest and most volatile piece, at USD 1,318 million in FY26 (up ~40%), having swung from a weak USD 121 million quarter in mid-2022 to a record USD 371 million quarter in early 2026.
- **India branded formulations / prescription business** — Sells branded prescription medicines to Indian doctors through a sales force of nearly 12,000 reps across cardiac, respiratory, diabetes, GI and vitamins, growing about 1.2–1.3 times the Indian pharma market (10.6% in FY26 vs IPM 9.9%), with chronic-disease products now 66–67% of the portfolio and over 80 launches planned in coming years.
- **Other Developed Markets (Europe, Canada, Australia)** — Sells branded and generic products in Europe, Canada and Australia — Luforbec and NaMuscla in the UK/Germany, Zaxine in Canada — contributing about 11–12% of total sales and growing 13.3% in FY26, with European sales crossing USD 200 million and an acquisition of VISUfarma being added.
- **Emerging / Growth Markets (LATAM, APAC, South Africa)** — Sells medicines in Brazil, Mexico, the Philippines and South Africa, the fastest-growing block at roughly 35–49% growth, driven by a Brazil turnaround (Dapagliflozin growing 99–113% in local currency) and a push into diabetes and metabolic drugs.
- **API business (API Plus SBU)** — Makes and sells the raw active drug ingredients used in finished medicines, both for its own use and to outside buyers; a smaller, steadier line run under the CFO, growing single digits (about 7% year-on-year in Q1 FY25).
- **Trade generics / Global Institutional Business (GIB)** — Sells unbranded generics and supplies government and institutional tenders; the India trade generics arm was carved into a wholly owned subsidiary in 2024 for sharper focus, and GIB grew 11.8% in FY26 though quarterly tender timing can make India's reported numbers lumpy.
- **Biosimilars and specialty/innovation pipeline** — A newer growth engine covering biosimilars (first US approval Pegfilgrastim from the Pune biologics plant, four planned in three years), 505(b)(2) improved-formulation products, and in-licensed innovative drugs such as the Gan & Lee GLP-1 partnership — small today but where a rising share of the R&D spend is being directed.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Every check, area by area

**Capital Allocation — Neutral (+0.25)** · 4 of 6 checks had evidence

- **Research and development — Excellent** *(from the conference calls)*: R&D rose to INR 2,063 crores (7.5-8% of sales, ~70% complex) and demonstrably converted into launches — Tolvaptan, Risperdal Consta from the in-house Nanomi platform, Pegfilgrastim biosimilar, 15 launches in FY26 and 50+ planned over three years.
  > "R&D at INR 535 crores is 7.5% of sales... with almost 70% of our R&D directed towards complex portfolio" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Merger and acquisition — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: Deals have been small complementary bolt-ons (Anglo French Drugs, Southern Cross Australia, VISUfarma ophthalmics, Valorum licensing) rather than transformational, and consolidated margins expanded through the period, though acquisition economics are never disclosed.
  > "complete the acquisition of VISUfarma, which we expect to close this quarter" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Distribution to shareholders — Neutral** *(from the conference calls)*: Net cash swung from INR 310 crores to INR 4,636 crores with no articulated buyback or dividend policy — cash is explicitly earmarked for specialty M&A instead.
  > "we continue to explore strategic allocation of our capital to ensure the long term mission of the company including on the specialty front" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Cost of working capital — Poor** *(from the financial statements)*: About 181 days of cash stays stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers — a long cycle that ties up capital.
- *Not assessed (the evidence was silent — never guessed): Capex for growth (not maintenance); Advertisement and promotion.*

**Return on Capital — Good (+0.67)** · 3 of 3 checks had evidence

- **Return on capital (headline) — Excellent** *(from the financial statements)*: For every ₹100 of capital in the business (shareholders' funds + borrowings), it generated ₹25 of operating cash this year — a 25% cash return on capital.
- **Asset turn — Neutral** *(from the numbers and the calls together)*: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹0.73 of sales — a moderate asset intensity. And from the calls: Working capital was cut from 147 days in Q1 FY23 to 87 days in FY26 with ROCE of 28.4%, but this is a plant-heavy manufacturer whose turns improved through discipline rather than an asset-light model with a protecting moat.
  > "87 days of net working capital as compared to 110 days recorded last year. ROCE for the year translates to 28.4%" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Profit margin — Neutral** *(from the financial statements)*: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹29 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±8.1 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
