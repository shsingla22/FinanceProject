**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Barriers are genuinely high — balance sheet, AAA credit rating, and demonstrated capex-deployment capability are prerequisites, which management explicitly frames as the competitive lever — but tariff-based competitive bidding caps pricing rationality. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±21.3 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Capital intensity, credit rating requirements and execution capability are real entry barriers, and management pledges bid discipline over volume — but the TBCB auction format itself is inherently price-competitive rather than rationally priced.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹29 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹29 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±21.3 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: AEML is the sole urban distribution utility for its Mumbai and Mundra licence areas and each transmission line is an exclusive long-dated licensed asset, with management explicitly viewing even smart metering as a perpetual rather than contract-limited franchise. | The long view had said: The Mumbai and Mundra distribution licences are exclusive-in-practice service territories where AEML has been the country's top-rated utility for four straight years, giving a monopoly-like standing with its captive consumer base.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: The Indian transmission and metering capex cycle is clearly in an identified upturn with over INR1.5 lakh crore of projects awaiting bidding, though AESL is already well into that upcycle rather than positioned at its bottom. | The long view had said: Demand is described as steady rather than cyclical — a temporary FY25 slowdown from delayed metro and data-centre commissioning, with growth expected to normalise around 5%, so there is no identifiable cycle bottom to exploit.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Expansion is entirely domestic state-by-state contract capture (Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Telangana, Andhra pipeline) with no international footprint or brand-travel evidence discussed. | The long view had said: Expansion is domestic but credible and executed — second distribution licences sought in Navi Mumbai, Saurashtra and western UP, plus a widening state-level transmission bidding footprint across Maharashtra, Rajasthan, UP and Karnataka; no premium brand travelling internationally.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Excellent → Good. Now: Physical reach and service capability rivals struggle to match — 28,000 circuit km, two urban distribution licences and the country's largest metering deployment machine — in a business where service availability is contractually vital. | The long view had said: Physical reach rivals cannot match — ~28,000 circuit km of transmission, India's largest smart-metering platform running at a pace management says equals all other operators combined, and 5GW of contracted green capacity plus 3,500MWh of storage to serve C&I.

Now: Transmission is a concentrated field of a handful of credible bidders, but assets are awarded by competitive reverse auction (AESL wins only ~29% of what is bid) so the end customer sets price, not the incumbents. | The long view had said: Private transmission is a concentrated field where AESL alone takes ~29% of bid projects and end consumers have no negotiating power over regulator-set pooled tariffs, though tariff-based competitive bidding keeps rivalry live at the award stage.

Now: Revenue is regulated tariff or auction-determined with zero image- or taste-driven pricing power; even the growth businesses are priced on spreads and service fees. | The long view had said: There is no image- or taste-driven pricing power — electricity is a commodity sold at regulator-determined tariffs that are being lowered, with efficiency gains recycled into RAB rather than margin.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Explicit and rising share numbers across the year: ~29% of FY26 transmission projects bid, order pipeline growing INR60,000cr (Oct 2025) to ~INR78,000cr (Jan 2026), and the largest smart-metering platform in India at 1.34 crore installed, rising to a 4.7 crore portfolio with the IntelliSmart acquisition. Share is being won on execution and lower cost of capital, not visibly on impaired returns. (The long view had said: Share gain is documented and rising across the whole period — growing 4% vs market 1% in Q1FY24, ~20% of transmission bid wins in FY25, ~29% in FY26, network from 19,778 to ~28,000 ckm, and the largest smart-meter platform in India (83 lakh installed in FY26, roughly matching all other players combined) — and it is explicitly disciplined, with bids taken only where ratings and returns are not compromised.)

**That weakened:**

- **Toll Roads**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Transmission assets are won through tariff-based competitive bidding (AESL won ~29% of projects that went to bid) and distribution/transmission tariffs are regulator-set, so there is no 'customers pay without negotiating' dynamic; no certification or standards anchoring appears in any of the four calls. (The long view had said: Transmission is a literal toll road — the tariff is pooled at state level and charged to DISCOMs as a small, non-negotiable slice of delivered power cost under 35-year TSAs, and 99.7% availability earns incentive income (Rs 100cr in 9M FY25). But it is a regulated/bid-determined toll, not a rational oligopoly with pricing discretion, and each new asset is re-competed.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

*Not comparable: Innovation Dominance (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 2 unchanged; 4 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Revenue growth is driven by the sector's capex cycle — the transmission ordering pipeline (INR60,000cr rising to ~INR78,000cr and INR1.5 lakh cr identified) and DISCOM metering tenders — and analysts explicitly flagged that transmission bidding had slowed in Q3FY26, so future capitalisation depends on someone else's investment programme. Against that, income from commissioned assets is annuity-like and availability-linked rather than price-driven. (The long view had said: The core T&D business is annuity-like and operations-linked — availability of 99.7% earning fixed incentives, RAB growing from Rs 5,400cr to Rs 10,500cr — but the new Energy Solutions platform books merchant margins that management concedes swing with monsoon, season and exchange prices, and the transmission order flow itself is a bid-driven capex cycle (pipeline swinging from Rs 17,000cr to Rs 55,000cr to Rs 150,000cr).)
- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: Grid infrastructure is not an innovation-race industry, and across the four calls no competitor platform or format threat is discussed defensively; instead management is the adopter of incremental technology — Mumbai HVDC commissioned, India's largest smart-metering deployment, district cooling — which is the value-adding, small-scale innovation cadence rather than a disruptive reset. (The long view had said: By Apr 2026 analysts were directly asking whether co-located battery storage substitutes for HVDC transmission capacity at policy level, which would attack the core asset class; management conceded the debate is live rather than dismissing it, redirecting to intrastate opportunity.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (ELEVATED); Good Enough Goods (ELEVATED); Fashion Risk (QUANT FLAG); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 8 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
