**Checks that improved:**

- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹15 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±1.4 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹15 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±6.4 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: There is share to be taken from the informal/unorganised segment as the market formalises, and management explicitly frames a large share opportunity for Pizza Hut, though Sapphire has recently been the donator rather than the taker in pizza. | The long view had said: Mixed: Sapphire takes share from weaker operators in Sri Lanka, but in Indian pizza it is itself the donator, losing revenue (-7% FY26) to a more aggressive Domino's.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: There is little halo risk to correct for — reported results are weak (Pizza Hut restaurant EBITDA negative, group PBT near zero) and management attributes the KFC upturn to specific interventions rather than to the environment, making skill separable from luck. | The long view had said: The strong communication, ESG rankings and Gallup award create a halo that must be separated from delivery, since underlying results were poor for ~10 quarters and the recovery is partly external and partly vendor-subsidised rather than purely management skill.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: Product innovation intensity was explicitly increased and recent launches (Dunked, ShaWOWrma, Double Chicken Dynamite) are cited as contributing to KFC's SSSG recovery, though the innovation engine for KFC is owned by Yum!, not Sapphire. | The long view had said: No R&D as such; menu innovation intensity was consciously raised (Melts, Momo Mia, Dunked, ShaWOWrma, Double Chicken Dynamite) with a disciplined 4-9 month test-then-adopt rule, but innovation is menu-level and largely franchisor-supported rather than proprietary technology.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Excellent → Neutral. Now: Thin evidence: retention was addressed via an ESOP modification costing INR6.6 crores, while the merged entity's new capability leaders are being hired externally rather than promoted, suggesting no demonstrated internal pipeline for these functions. | The long view had said: Gallup Exceptional Workplace recognition requiring top-quartile global engagement for 4-5 consecutive years, #1 QSR in India on DJSI social/governance, and internal CEO elevations (Deepak Taluja, Vikrant Vohra) point to a systematic talent engine.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Good → Weak. Now: Sapphire owns no mind-space monopoly — the brands belong to Yum!, and management concedes Pizza Hut must still buy its way into consumer consideration; only Sri Lanka approaches a leading position. | The long view had said: KFC occupies a near-monopoly position in the Indian fried-chicken category ('undisputed leader') and Sapphire is the #1 QSR in Sri Lanka, though the brand equity belongs to Yum! and competition in fried chicken is emerging.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: Growth capex continues (73 KFC opened in FY26, 9 in Sri Lanka, count up to 1,074) but incremental returns are poor — consolidated adjusted PBT was just 1.1% in Q4FY26 — and Pizza Hut India expansion was deliberately halted at zero net adds for CY25. | The long view had said: Sustained growth capex — store count roughly doubled from ~550 (Dec-21) to 1,074 by Jun-26 with 60-80 KFC openings a year and management claiming similar payback in smaller towns — but incremental returns have diluted (KFC restaurant EBITDA fell from ~20.3% to 16.3-16.9%, new stores open at 80-85% of average ADS), so it is growth-heavy but not high-incremental-ROIC.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Genuine operating discipline — refusing to expand Pizza Hut until the brand is fixed and closing as many stores as it opened — is offset by the framework-violating decision to do a transformational merger rather than grow organically. | The long view had said: Genuine discipline — a written M&A filter with no forced deals, cautious Pizza Hut store openings for two straight calendar years, and a stated willingness to cut expansion if new-store strike rates drop — partly offset by the recent large Devyani merger.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: KFC is capturing share with 17% system growth and positive SSSG, but Pizza Hut lost share through five consecutive negative-SSSG quarters and management concedes it is only aspiring to a share play, not executing one. | The long view had said: KFC is outgrowing the market (17% system growth, 5% SSSG) and Sri Lanka is consolidating a #1 QSR position with six straight quarters of double-digit SSSG, but Pizza Hut has been donating share to a more aggressive Domino's for years.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Calls are dense, slide-by-slide operational detail with no personality cult, but the company does indulge in self-promotional superlatives such as its ESG and QSR ranking claims. | The long view had said: Consistently low-key, numbers-first calls run by the same CEO/CFO pair for five years with no personality promotion; the only self-praise is institutional (DJSI, Gallup) rather than personal.
- **Advertisement and promotion** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: A&P is now demonstrably driving KFC (advertising plus the INR99 meal produced the best SSSG in 14 quarters), but the company admits Pizza Hut's innovations and value offers failed purely for lack of marketing support, so spend has been inadequate and uneven rather than durably brand-building. | The long view had said: A deliberate 75-100bps step-up in KFC marketing plus the INR 99 'permanent value layer' finally converted into positive SSSG after ~3 negative years, though the equivalent Pizza Hut marketing investment has not yet arrested a -7% revenue decline.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Management has persisted with one consistent, self-authored Pizza Hut playbook across at least eight quarters of poor results and has kept rebuilding Sri Lanka through COVID and political turmoil, though independence is structurally capped since Yum! runs KFC marketing and innovation. | The long view had said: Demonstrated tenacity across a full cycle: delivering by bicycle through Sri Lanka's fuel crisis, holding a transaction-first strategy through COVID and currency shocks, and persisting through three years of failed campaigns until the KFC recruitment mix worked.

*Unchanged: Merger and acquisition; Candor; Asset turn; Technology advantage; Distribution advantage; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Network effects; Structural end-market growth; Convenience benefits; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Barriers to entry and rationality; Intangible benefits; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Geographical expansion; Security by obscurity; Return on capital (headline); Cyclical market growth; Cost of working capital.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 2 weakened, 7 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Sapphire operates powerful franchised brands (KFC, Pizza Hut, and a stated No. 1 QSR position in Sri Lanka) but does not own them — KFC marketing and innovation stay with Yum! — and Pizza Hut India revenue fell 7% for FY26 with management admitting the brand lacks marketing clout. (The long view had said: KFC is genuinely powerful (Sri Lanka is the No.1 QSR with steady share consolidation, and management says they win 'really, really strong' in direct competitive fights), but the brands are licensed from Yum! rather than owned, Pizza Hut has posted negative SSSG for most of FY24–FY26, and premium pricing is absent.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Q1 FY27 shows a genuine 2% price increase in both KFC and Pizza Hut with SSSG still positive and gross margin up 160/80 bps, but the last 12 months are dominated by entry-price value constructs and discounting to buy transactions, which is not the profile of durable pricing power. (The long view had said: The record shows the opposite of pricing power: price increases repeatedly lag inflation (9% vs mid-teens in FY23), 2.5 years of negative SSSG, and the FY26 recovery was bought with a permanent value layer, BOGO offers and vendor cross-subsidy rather than price.)

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Leadership is stable (Purohit as Group CEO and Jain as CFO since FY2022) and management discloses failures promptly — admitting the Epic Saver campaign missed and Pizza Hut remains challenged — but the last 12 months show FY26 adjusted EBITDA down 9% against repeated turnaround promises, so delivery lags the stated plan. (The long view had said: Purohit and Jain have fronted every one of the 18 calls since FY22; bad news is disclosed promptly and without spin (Maldives impairment taken in Q2FY25, Pizza Hut losses called out quarter after quarter, 'it has taken us time to find the right marketing mix'), guidance given in Dec-21 was delivered by Dec-24, and management refuses to chase M&A or store growth without meeting stated criteria.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: KFC delivered 15-17% revenue growth with 73 new restaurants in FY26 and Sri Lanka claims consolidated No. 1 QSR status, but Pizza Hut India added a net zero stores in CY25 with SSSG of -12% and -7%, and no explicit India market-share numbers are given. (The long view had said: Sri Lanka shows durable share leadership and six consecutive quarters of double-digit SSSG, and KFC India doubled its store count from Dec-21 to Dec-24 as promised; but India same-store sales were negative for roughly ten quarters and Pizza Hut ceded ground to a more aggressive Domino's, so system growth came from new stores rather than winning share.)

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 1 worsened, 7 unchanged. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The entire business is a franchisee of a single licensor, Yum! Brands, across KFC, Pizza Hut India and Sri Lanka; management confirms Yum! retains marketing and innovation for KFC and Pizza Hut's underperformance is explicitly blamed on marketing support the franchisee does not control, and the Devyani merger deepens rather than diversifies this single-relationship dependence. (The long view had said: The entire business is two Yum! master-franchise licences (KFC, Pizza Hut) plus a Sri Lanka territory; analysts directly questioned what Yum!'s global sale of Pizza Hut means for the Master Franchise Agreement, and even store-opening plans are set on Yum!'s calendar year. Margin support in FY26 also leaned on a few named vendor partners (fries, beverages).)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Fashion Risk (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
