**Checks that improved:**

- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹19 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹19 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±4.1 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Capital intensity and clinical talent create real entry barriers, and pricing behaviour is rational — walking away from low-yield business rather than chasing volume. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±4.1 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Capital intensity and scarce clinical talent are real entry barriers and pricing has been rational (ARPP up 8-10% with no discounting), but clinician poaching and government scheme pricing are pressure points.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Good → Weak. Now: Talent has been a source of damage rather than strength — leadership transitions caused two quarters of Kerala softness and clinician departures hurt K&M, with hiring described reactively. | The long view had said: Consistent, multi-year talent agenda — Aster Academy and nursing awards to fight 35% nurse attrition in 2022, and 100+ clinicians onboarded in FY26 with measurable MRR — though CEO turnover and clinician exits in K&M show gaps.
- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Weak. Now: The defining transaction is a transformative merger with Blackstone-backed Quality Care that roughly doubles the company (5,199 to 10,620+ beds) — the opposite of the small, simple bolt-on the framework favours, though the acquiree is strong and complementary and combined margins have expanded 116bps. | The long view had said: The Quality Care merger is a transformational, near-equal-size deal rather than a small bolt-on, though the acquiree is already strong and improving (FY26 EBITDA +24%, margin +136bps) and small clean-ups like the Aster Aadhar stake to 99% are sensible.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Neutral → Weak. Now: Headline strength leans heavily on presentational choices — proforma combined figures for an unconsummated merger, 'normalised' PAT excluding merger and labour-code costs, and ex-Kasargod margins — making skill hard to separate from framing. | The long view had said: Real operating skill is visible (material cost down 25.3%→21%, procurement synergies worth INR 85 crore), but the heavy award recitation and proforma/ex-item presentation make it necessary to discount the narrative gloss.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: Indian hospitals are fragmented and institutional payors hold real negotiating power, evidenced by Aster having to exit low-yield schemes and rationalise schemes at a volume cost. | The long view had said: Indian hospitals remain fragmented with several national chains and no stable oligopoly, though consolidation via the Aster-QCIL merger is moving the structure in that direction.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Neutral → Weak. Now: Capacity addition is described as calibrated and capital-efficient, but the year's central act is a company-doubling merger rather than the organic focus and avoidance of big M&A the parameter requires. | The long view had said: Capex is phased and demand-linked with a net cash balance sheet (INR 1,041 crore), but the organic discipline is offset by a very large QCIL merger, the opposite of the 'no big M&A' criterion.

Now: The value proposition is clinical certainty in high-acuity work — oncology, cardiology, DBS and robotics — where trust, not price, drives the choice, evidenced by rising complexity mix. | The long view had said: The core purchase is clinical certainty, evidenced by universal JCI/NABH accreditation, top national rankings, and first-of-kind transplants and complex procedures that pull patients regardless of price.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Clear runway and evidence of capture — pushing to top-3 in Bengaluru with 2,580 beds, 28 cities post-merger, and MVT share gains of 41-64% YoY, in a market still dominated by unorganised providers. | The long view had said: In a highly fragmented market Aster is not dominant and is explicitly targeting top-three status, with the combined platform reaching 10,620+ beds across 28 cities and growing revenue 14-18%, well above market.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 3 weakened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: NO FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: The calls mention adoption of robotics, DBS and minimally invasive procedures lifting case mix, but disclose no R&D spend, pipeline, or share-of-industry-R&D evidence on which this pattern turns. (The long view had said: No R&D spend is disclosed in any of the 18 calls; the 'innovation' cited is purchased capability — Elekta LINACs, Intuitive Surgical robots, a Bain-built app and data lake — all available to any well-funded competitor, which contradicts R&D-led dominance.)

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: ARPOB rose 14% in Q1 FY26 and inpatient ARPP grew 8-10% YoY in every quarter of FY26 without volume loss (volumes +9-15%), with 'disciplined pricing' and discontinuation of low-yield schemes cited — but management attributes most of the uplift to specialty/CONGO case mix rather than like-for-like price increases. (The long view had said: Realisation rises every year without volume loss: ARPOB +12% YoY in Q1 FY25 explicitly on 'price hike', then ARPP IP +8-10% YoY in each of FY26's quarters alongside 9-15% volume growth, with margins expanding — part mix/acuity-led (CONGO mix, oncology, robotics, MVT), which is pricing-for-value rather than pure list-price power.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Volumes are visibly clinician- and referrer-driven: Q2 FY26 attributes the Kerala turnaround partly to re-engaging MVT partners and referral networks, and Q3 FY26 blames K&M softness on 'a few clinician movements', but no trade-training or channel-lock-in programmes are described. (The long view had said: Clinicians clearly drive demand while insurers/patients pay — QCIL quantifies revenue per hired doctor cohort and Aster attributes a soft K&M quarter to 'clinician movements' — and Aster Academy trains doctors/nurses; but there is no evidence of low price sensitivity or professional-driven lock-in beyond doctor recruitment.)
- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Aster controls its own delivery channel and is integrating adjacent services directly to the patient — labs (revenue +17-18% YoY), pharmacy, and home care being folded into the AsterCare/Aster Health app — but no franchising, royalty, or own-store economics are quantified. (The long view had said: Aster has moved toward the consumer with 217 branded retail pharmacies and 229 patient experience centres by Q1 FY25, an Aster Pharmacy brand licensed to a third-party operator, and the MyAster D2C app; but management explicitly frames these as an ecosystem support for hospitals rather than a growth engine, and no franchise royalty or D2C revenue share is given.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
