**Checks that improved:**

- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Good. Now: A 3,412-branch network weighted to rural and semi-urban India, plus tab banking and vernacular WhatsApp channels, gives real reach that a new or digital-only rival cannot replicate quickly — though larger PSU peers match it. | The long view had said: A credible pan-India network of 3,412 branches skewed rural, but it is an order of magnitude smaller than SBI/PNB and readily matched by peers, so reach is adequate rather than unmatchable.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: The benefit mix is well matched — a retail/agri/MSME-led book (65% RAM) served by sovereign-backed assurance and convenience channels rather than by price, which is the right fit for its retail mass-market base. | The long view had said: Correctly tilts toward retail/RAM (65% of book) where relationships are stickier and yields better, but offers retail no intangible pull and has no corporate switching-cost lock-in — corporate relationships turn purely on price.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: There is modest deposit stickiness from a rural/semi-urban branch franchise (61% of branches) holding CASA steadily at 37–38% for eight quarters, but no genuine switching-cost lock-in on the asset side. | The long view had said: Some rural stickiness from 61% rural/semi-urban branch mix, but switching costs are minimal — management concedes ~30% of its 4 crore accounts are inoperative and it must actively chase quality CASA.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Good → Weak. Now: Growth is almost entirely volume-led with adverse pricing — yield on advances fell from 8.76% to 8.06% and NIM guidance was cut to 2.8–2.9% on repo cuts; only the RAM mix shift (65% of book) partly offsets it. | The long view had said: Growth is volume-led but with a deliberate and effective mix shift into higher-yield RAM (61% → 65-66%) that lifted NIM from 2.84% to ~3.10%, plus explicit refusal to write underpriced corporate loans.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Weak. Now: The bank sits near the benign top of the credit cycle rather than the bottom — GNPA down to 2.17%, net NPA 0.27%, credit cost 0.62% and PCR ~98% leave little further tailwind from asset-quality normalisation, while rate cuts are compressing NIM. | The long view had said: The bank rode the PSU credit cycle from its trough (GNPA 7.89% in FY22 to 2.17% in FY26) but is now at the favourable end — credit cost 0.62% and slippage 0.78% leave little cyclical upside ahead, only ECL provisioning of Rs2,500-3,000cr to absorb.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Weak. Now: Technology is bought-in catch-up rather than proprietary edge — Murex treasury, vendor APM, data-centre consolidation and journey digitisation are table stakes the bank is late to, funded by a ~Rs.900 crore IT budget. | The long view had said: Technology has moved from laggard to competitive — 4.8 app rating, AI contact centre, Murex treasury — but it is bought-in catch-up on a Rs1,000cr budget, replicable by any peer, not a multi-product proprietary edge.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: UCO is a low-profile, lightly-followed small PSU bank, but a listed government bank in a heavily regulated and capital-attracting industry is not an ignored niche. | The long view had said: A 90.95% government-held small PSU with thin analyst participation (calls run largely by one or two retail-facing analysts) and a share price the analyst says does not reflect the results — genuinely under-followed.

*Unchanged: Candor; Research and development; Structural end-market growth; Distribution to shareholders; Out of limelight; Halo effect (caution); Intangible benefits; Barriers to entry and rationality; Share donators; Gain in market share; Asset turn; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Assurance benefits; Geographical expansion; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Capex for growth (not maintenance); Network effects; Disciplined stewards; Mini-monopolies; Convenience benefits; People matter (talent development).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Unknown on the long view, Unknown on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The book is deliberately granular and getting more so — RAM rose to 65-66% of advances, Q3 slippages of Rs.419cr were all RAM with the majority in sub-Rs.10 lakh accounts, corporate SMA-1/2 was only Rs.316cr, and total >Rs.1cr SMA is 0.66% of advances. The one lumpy event flagged (a mid-corporate MSME slip in Q1) was a watch-list account, not a defining relationship. Against that, the earning base has been deliberately broadened toward a highly granular retail franchise.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
