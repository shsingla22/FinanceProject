**Checks that improved:**

- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Poor → Neutral. Now: No bolt-on acquisitions in the window; the only inorganic move is a 51% greenfield JV with the Zambian government — a new, unbuilt asset rather than an already-strong complementary business — with M&A only flagged as an option for the cash pile. | The long view had said: The Parabolic/Pure and Cure API acquisition was a distressed asset that has bled for years — EBITDA losses of ~40cr on tiny sales in FY23, -44cr in FY25 and -40cr in FY26 — with turnaround repeatedly promised and repeatedly missed, the opposite of a bolt-on where the acquiree is already strong and OPM improves.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Akums is a listed company that is the self-described leader of its industry with regular sell-side hosted calls from Ambit and ICICI Securities, so it is neither an ignored niche nor a heavily crowded name. | The long view had said: Far from under the radar — since its 2024 IPO the company is covered on every call by Ambit, ICICI, Citi, HSBC, Fidelity, Franklin Templeton and JM, in a CDMO space that is currently a favoured capital-allocation theme.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Regulatory compliance (EU GMP, DCGI, ANVISA) is a real barrier in the CDMO segment, but pricing behaviour elsewhere is plainly irrational — a broad-based, multi-quarter API price collapse that forced Akums to exit product lines. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±4.4 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Regulatory compliance is raising entry barriers on the formulation side, but the API business sits in an outright price war with 30%+ cephalosporin erosion and two straight years of double-digit basket declines, so industry rationality is mixed.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Good. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹12 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹12 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±4.4 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Net cash of over INR1,600 crore with no leverage, no large acquisitions, counter-cyclical investment through a weak H1, and disciplined shrinking of the loss-making API and trade generics segments. | The long view had said: Debt was cut from INR418 crore to INR213 crore, the group now sits on net cash of INR1,682 crore, and loss-making trade generics/API lines were rationalized rather than chased for top line — though the standing intent to deploy cash inorganically is a watch item.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: There is little halo risk to strip out — reported PAT actually fell to INR256 crore in FY26 — while the skill claims are checkable hard milestones (EU GMP certificates, DCGI approvals, first EU shipments) rather than results-derived narrative. | The long view had said: Separating results from skill is genuinely hard here: the headline balance sheet strength comes largely from a EUR 100 million customer prepayment rather than operations, FY26 PAT fell to INR256 crore, and the H2 volume surge is admittedly unexplained by management itself.
- **Candor** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Management named the miss and its cause without spin, pre-empted investor concern directly, and disclosed segment-level losses in API and trade generics quarter after quarter. | The long view had said: Management cut its own CDMO guidance mid-year, flatly labelled the API year a miss, quantified segment losses and provisions, and admitted it cannot explain the volume upturn rather than claiming credit — though it declined to give segment splits early on.

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
