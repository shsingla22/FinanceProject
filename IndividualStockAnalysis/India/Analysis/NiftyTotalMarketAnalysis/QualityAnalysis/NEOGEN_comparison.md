### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: No heritage story, A&P spend, distributor leverage or consumer-brand adjacency is discussed in the last-12-month calls; the business is sold on qualification and technology, not brand. (The long view had said: No consumer brand or A&P, but management consistently frames 25-30 years of batch consistency and credibility with OEM and regulated-pharma customers as the reason foreign incumbents rather than local copycats are the real competition (Aug 2022); this trust does not translate into premium pricing.)
- **Low Price Plus**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The stated positioning is Indian cost plus Japanese reliability, and management expects to be a 'highly cost-competitive global source'; but the last 12 months show cost escalation, not a cost culture — Pakhajan Phase 2 capex revised up to INR 1,367 crore, finance cost up 64% year-on-year in Q1 FY27, and no itemised savings discipline is described. (The long view had said: Management repeatedly concedes Chinese salt prices run 20-50% below Neogen's contracted prices (May 2026), and there is no cost-culture or itemised-savings language across the 20 calls; the company competes on non-China supply security, not cost leadership.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Volume-led growth accelerated through the window (8% in Q2 FY26 to 34% in Q1 FY27, inorganic up 158%, guidance raised to INR 950–1,050 crore) and management spent on toll manufacturing specifically to hold customers during the Dahej outage — but no explicit market-share percentages or comparisons versus named competitors are given. (The long view had said: Claimed 60-75% of India's specialty lithium carbonate imports (Aug 2022), rising CSM mix from ~10% to 15-18%, and by Aug 2026 the only giga-scale localised battery-materials supply chain in India; offset by admitted share loss to Chinese competition in advanced intermediates and agro (Nov 2024).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Stressed on the long view, Stressed on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Management repeatedly frames the operating backdrop in classic cycle terms — overcapacity, pricing volatility and subdued end-demand — while simultaneously committing INR 428 cr (Dahej Ph-1) and INR 1,367 cr (Pakhajan Ph-2) of capex whose revenue depends on India's ACC gigafactory commissioning schedule, i.e. on someone else's capex cycle; the core pharma/F&F/organo-lithium book is consumable, operations-linked demand with working pass-throughs, which bounds the exposure. (The long view had said: Across all four years management explains revenue and margins primarily through lithium and bromine price swings (75% revenue growth in FY23 on lithium price pass-through, inorganic revenue -24% in Q2FY25 purely on lower lithium, +145%/+158% in FY26-Q1FY27 as prices rebounded), while the growth engine is battery materials sold into customers' gigafactory capex ramp — funded by ~INR 1,800 crore of capex that has pushed net debt to INR 1,295 crore and forced a INR 600 crore QIP and promoter infusion. Pharma/agro destocking cycles and 'weak demand, oversupply' commentary compound it.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: The battery vertical's entire domestic case rests on one named relationship — 'a prominent giga-scale Indian manufacturer' whose PPAP approval is cited every quarter — with the international book at just four customers under provisional approval, and the technology on a single JV partner (Morita) whose $20m contribution has slipped from H1 FY27 to 'Q2 and Q3'; the established base business shows no such single-name dependence. (The long view had said: The battery ramp depends on a countable handful of names — four international salt customers, four domestic gigafactory cell makers, with the INR 200 crore salt guidance resting largely on one long-term formula contract — while inputs come from a narrow base (South-America lithium contracts, Dead Sea Bromine) and a single JV partner, Morita, supplies both the technology and a $20mn equity cheque. Earlier calls show the same pattern in the base business (a single five-year bromine-derivative contract with one pharma customer).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL); Fashion Risk (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
