# Nazara Technologies (NAZARA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Nazara Technologies is an Indian-listed company that owns and runs a portfolio of gaming and gaming-adjacent businesses, mostly through acquired subsidiaries it calls its 'Friends of Nazara' network. It makes money from consumers who subscribe to or spend inside its mobile games (like the kids' learning app Kiddopia and the kids' virtual world Animal Jam), from selling PC and console games it publishes (such as Human Fall Flat), from tickets and spend at its physical entertainment centres in India, and from advertising and sponsorship revenue on its sports/gaming content sites and esports events. Customers are mainly consumers in the US and other Western markets for the games, plus brands and advertisers who pay for sponsorships and digital ad services. Management describes the group as spanning India, the Middle East, Africa, Europe and North America, with FY26 revenue of about INR 1,829 crore and EBITDA of about INR 255 crore, and says the business has shifted decisively toward higher-margin owned gaming IP, which supplied roughly 90% of FY26 EBITDA.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Gaming (the core segment)** — The main business, covering mobile games, PC/console publishing and offline gaming centres; FY26 revenue grew 107% to INR 1,072 crore with EBITDA of INR 265 crore at a 24.7% margin, contributing about 90% of group EBITDA versus 56% a year earlier.
- **Mobile gaming** — Subscription and in-app-purchase games run by acquired studios, worth INR 713 crore of revenue and INR 137 crore of EBITDA in FY26, up 38% and 33% year-on-year.
- **Kiddopia (Gamified Early Learning)** — A paid subscription learning app for young children, sold mainly through app stores at roughly $8.4 per month ARPU; it was long a top-two title in Apple's kids category, suffered years of rising user-acquisition costs after Apple's privacy changes, and only returned to subscriber growth in Q3 and Q4 FY26.
- **Animal Jam (WildWorks)** — A free-to-play online kids' virtual world monetised through in-game purchases and subscriptions, worth about INR 95 crore of revenue and INR 19 crore of EBITDA in FY24, with margins expanding in FY26 and a version soft-launched on the Roblox platform.
- **Narrative games (Fusebox Games)** — Story-driven mobile games built on licensed reality-TV brands such as Big Brother, Bigg Boss, Traitors and Love Island; management calls it a growing engine with more IP launches planned for FY27.
- **PC and console publishing (Curve Games)** — Publishes and sells premium PC/console titles — most notably Human Fall Flat at 58 million lifetime units and Wobbly Life — generating INR 261 crore of FY26 revenue at a 39% EBITDA margin, the group's most profitable line.
- **Offline gaming (Smaaash and Funky Monkeys)** — Physical family entertainment and kids' play centres in India that earn footfall-based revenue; INR 99 crore of revenue and INR 27 crore of EBITDA in FY26, with new Funky Monkey centres being added and a 'Smaaash 2.0' revamp underway.
- **Skill-based real money gaming (Open Play)** — Real-money card/skill gaming, a small line at INR 37.4 crore revenue and INR 2.1 crore EBITDA in FY24, whose profitability was hit by the October 2023 GST change; it is barely mentioned in the later calls.
- **Esports / NODWIN Gaming** — Organises esports tournaments and youth live events and IPs (DreamHack, Starladder, Comic Con India) and sells sponsorships and media rights; once the largest revenue line at 55% of FY24 revenue, it was deconsolidated in FY26 and is now a ~46-47%-owned associate that swung from a INR 14 crore loss in FY25 to a INR 21 crore profit in FY26.
- **Absolute Sports / Sportskeeda** — Ad-supported sports and gaming content websites (Sportskeeda, Pro Football Network, PrimeTimer) that earn digital advertising revenue; Sportskeeda grew 60% to INR 196 crore revenue with INR 65 crore EBITDA in FY24 and stayed profitable in FY26 through cost cuts despite weaker traffic after Google search updates.
- **Ad Tech (Datawrkz)** — A digital advertising technology and services business earning fees from advertisers, mostly in the US; revenue fell from INR 153 crore in FY23 to INR 104 crore in FY24 as it dropped low-margin clients, then grew about 32% in FY26 with improving margins as it shifted to higher-margin tech products.
- **Bluetile and BestPlay** — The group's largest acquisition to date, completed in FY26 and consolidating from FY27, adding casual gaming scale, AI-native development capability and a rewarded engagement network.
- **Centers of Excellence** — Not a revenue line but an internal shared-services layer in user acquisition, data analytics, AI, growth and product that management credits for Kiddopia's turnaround and says every owned and newly acquired game plugs into.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Nazara's appeal starts with where it sits and how it is built. The structural end-market growth check is one of the few to score full marks: global gaming and digital advertising are expanding, and India is number one in game downloads while sitting outside the top ten in how much money is made from those players — a long runway. The economics look attractive on paper too: the intangible benefits check notes the product has essentially no direct input costs, so nearly all of every ₹100 of sales is free for people, marketing and profit. Management gets credit for candour, owning bad news in plain language — calling the Freaks4U deal a mistake, writing off ₹50 crore of goodwill on an IP that missed its projections, and conceding Sportskeeda has not recovered. That honesty is also what carries the Corporate Culture pattern to a partial fit, alongside founder-and-family leadership, a CFO in place since FY21, and a net cash balance sheet.

The trouble is that the same features that make the business attractive also make it fragile, and the patterns show it. Because there are almost no input costs, there is also almost nothing stopping anyone else — the barriers to entry check is the worst in the file at -2, noting margin swings of about ±13 points and pricing dictated by outside gatekeepers rather than by the industry. That is the same fact as the two HIGH risks: Apple's IDFA change permanently broke Kiddopia's customer-acquisition economics, and Google's search algorithm updates cut Sportskeeda's revenue by about 38% in FY26. So the Pricing Power pattern only half-holds — Kiddopia did raise prices 10–15% in 2022 with no damage and room left versus rivals, but across FY24–FY26 revenue per user barely moved (from $7.34 to $7.45) and growth came instead from spending more to buy users. Management runs that spending with discipline (payback inside two years, acquisition cost cut from $37.5 to $35.8 while scaling), which is why the advertising check scores positive, but it is buying growth rather than being handed it. The Innovation Dominance pattern — a steady stream of named launches — is likewise the flip side of Fashion Risk: NODWIN culls 60% of new IPs, growth is credited to whatever is trending, and the narrative games are built on whichever reality-TV show is popular. Market Share Gainers is contradicted from within: gaming rose from 56% to 90% of group profit, yet Kiddopia lost subscribers for years and Sportskeeda shrank.

Put together, the numbers have not yet rewarded any of it, and that is what pulls the rating down to 32 out of 100, or Weak, with resilience marked STRAINED. Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹-2 is operating profit; capital in the business generated only 4% in operating cash this year; and sales growth averaging about 24% a year has been lumpy rather than steady. Meanwhile the company has been a serial issuer rather than a returner of cash — ₹950 crore of equity raised in FY24 alone, with no dividend or buyback policy and 'value unlock' meaning selling non-core stakes. The genuine hedges against platform dependence exist but are small: the Forward Integrators pattern rests on Funky Monkey opening one to two owned centres a month with breakeven inside a year and excellent returns on that spending, yet it is a ₹99 crore slice of a ₹1,829 crore business. Three of the eleven patterns — Friendly Middleman, Toll Roads and Low Price Plus — find no support at all. The honest reading is a company in a good market, run by candid people who admit their errors, that has not yet shown it controls its own distribution, its own pricing, or its own returns.

### Innovation Dominance — LIKELY FIT
*"Out-inventing the competition"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and every one of the 2 computable checks supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** Named launches each year (Playground, Love Island/Big Brother narrative games, Wax Heads, Sovereign Tower, Dragon Shelter, Animal Jam on Roblox) plus AI-native capability from Bluetile and the Centers of Excellence, but R&D spend is never disclosed or benchmarked as a share of industry, and PC/console revenue was flat at INR 261 crores because the prior owner had stopped signing games.

> "We expect to launch at least 6 new releases in this current year... increasing our development spend to build that pipeline." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Essentially no direct input costs — typical of software and internet platforms — leaving ample room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ✅ Near-total retained margin alongside 24% yearly growth — the profitable-innovation flywheel: margins fund the spending that drives the growth.

**What the calls show:** Across the 18 calls the business is repeatedly reset by platform-level technology changes it does not control: Apple's IDFA change permanently broke Kiddopia's user-acquisition economics from FY22, Google Core algorithm updates cut Sportskeeda revenue ~38% in FY26 with management admitting four quarters of limited recovery, and the portfolio must chase new platforms (Roblox) and AI-native development to stay relevant.

### Stakeholder Concentration — HIGH RISK
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**What the calls show:** Distribution and monetisation sit with a handful of gatekeepers — Apple and Google app stores set the UA rules and take the platform margin, Google search traffic determines Sportskeeda's revenue, and a growing share of narrative games depends on licensed third-party reality-TV IP (Love Island, Big Brother, Bigg Boss, Traitors) whose renewal is outside Nazara's control.
