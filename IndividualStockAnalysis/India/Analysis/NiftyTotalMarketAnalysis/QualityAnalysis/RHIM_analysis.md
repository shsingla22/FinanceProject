# RHI Magnesita (RHIM) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

RHI Magnesita India Limited (formerly Orient Refractories, listed in India as RHIM) makes and sells refractories – the heat-resistant bricks, shapes, mixes and flow-control parts that line the furnaces, ladles, kilns and vessels in which metal and cement are made. Its customers are heavy industry, above all steel producers (integrated mills, secondary steel makers and public-sector plants such as Tata Steel and SAIL) plus cement, iron-making, non-ferrous and energy-infrastructure customers. It earns money by supplying these consumable linings on repeat, increasingly bundled with services – total refractory management, automation, robotics, digital monitoring and recycling under its '4PRO' platform – and it supplements its own output with goods traded/imported from its global parent group. It operates mainly in India from a set of local plants (Bhiwadi, Jamshedpur and, after the Dalmia and Hi-Tech acquisitions, western and southern sites), with capacity above 5 lakh tonnes a year, roughly 30% domestic market share, exports historically around 17-18% of sales, and record revenue crossing INR 4,000 crores in FY26.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Steel** — Refractory linings and solutions for steelmaking – ladles, tundishes, electric arc furnaces and related vessels – described by management as 'our largest business segment' and the main driver of FY26 growth via ladle solutions, EAF projects and tundish/ladle slide gate work.
- **Flow Control** — Products that control the flow of molten metal (slide gates, nozzles and similar), largely built up via the Hi-Tech Chemicals acquisition completed in January 2023; management repeatedly cites 'good momentum in Flow Control' as a growth and margin contributor in both domestic and export markets.
- **Iron making (blast furnace, taphole clay, pellet, DRI, coke oven)** — Refractories and project work for the iron-making side – blast furnace cast house and runners, taphole clay, pellet plants, DRI kilns and coke ovens; still 'a small business if we talk in totality' with lower per-tonne realisation, but a fast-growing order-book area with a dedicated Jamshedpur centre of excellence (~10,000 tonnes capacity, fully commissioned by 2027).
- **Cement / industrial segment** — High-alumina and other refractories for cement kilns and general industrial customers – a lower-realisation but meaningful volume business that management says provides 'diversification to our portfolio'; the company's presence here was strengthened by the Dalmia (DOCL) acquisition, which gave it a foothold in a segment where it had traditionally been 'less prominent'.
- **4PRO (total refractory management)** — The company's integrated solutions platform – 'total refractory management with sustainability inclusive' – combining products with automation, robotics, digital monitoring, scanning, process optimisation and recycling; management names 4PRO wins as a specific driver of record quarterly revenue and is signing long-term agreements under it to become 'the partner of choice rather than simply another supplier'.
- **Traded goods / imports from the parent group** — Refractory products bought in (largely imported from the global RHI Magnesita group) and resold in India – a business management sized at roughly INR 500-550 crores in early 2023 and said it expects to shrink over time as more 'local-for-local' production replaces imports.
- **Exports** — Sales of Indian-made refractories outside India, cited at about 17-18% of sales in FY23 with an ambition to reach 24-25%; the calls describe it as hit by the Russia-Ukraine war, weak European demand and, by FY26, 'weaker export demand amid geopolitical uncertainties' that contributed to a goodwill impairment on the RHIM IR unit.
- **HPI (Hydrocarbon Processing Industry) – legacy RESCO** — A newer, smaller line supplying refractories to hydrocarbon/energy processing customers, brought in from the group's U.S. RESCO legacy business and being 'solidified' in India, where management says it is still establishing a clear growth road map.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*
