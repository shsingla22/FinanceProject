### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 7 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 4 weakened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Promoter-family leadership is stable and long-tenured, FY26 delivered 30% revenue growth with EBITDA guidance surpassed, dividend raised to INR11, and interest costs kept flat while capex ran INR2,700 crores over two years; against that, the INR3,000 crore FY26 defence guidance reaffirmed in the Q3 call came in at INR2,634 crores after Pinaka slipped — disclosed openly, but a miss. (The long view had said: Promoter-family led with an unusually stable team (Manish Nuwal, Suresh Menon, both joint CFOs present on calls from Jan 2023 to May 2026), heavy long-horizon reinvestment (capex guided up INR800cr→INR1,200cr in FY25, INR2,700cr over two years, INR2,050cr planned FY27) and candid disclosure of slippage — the Pinaka order delay was owned quarter after quarter, and FY26 defence landed at INR2,634 crores against a maintained INR3,000 crore guidance, while the FY26 EBITDA guidance was beaten.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Licensed explosives manufacturing across 9 countries, a 30-year record and an INR18,000 crore qualified defence order book with programmes like Pinaka are hard for a funded newcomer to replicate quickly, but management never articulates the replication barrier itself — it is inferred from the asset base rather than stated. (The long view had said: The barrier is concrete and articulated by management: licensed explosives manufacturing plus multi-year customer qualification — the export build-out took roughly 2018–2022 of greenfield plants before revenue moved, and defence products need years of DRDO/services trials ('it may take 1 year or it may take 2 years') before commercialisation, so money alone cannot compress the clock.)
- **Toll Roads**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Explosives are a small but operationally critical input to mining and infrastructure customers, and the Q3 FY26 call frames the offering as specialised products plus technical services that entrench position, but no call quantifies the <1-2% of customer cost point, switching behaviour or certification/standards anchoring. (The long view had said: Explosives are a small but mission-critical consumable for miners, and Solar stresses long qualification cycles and product approvals that make customers sticky in a two-or-three-player oligopoly ('we have competed with the world number 1, number 2'); but management never quantifies its share of customer cost and prices are contractually indexed to ammonium nitrate, which is unlike a true toll.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: International revenue grew 43%/21%/35%/32% across the four FY26 quarters with new-market penetration and loss-making units like South Africa turned around, and defence rose to 33% of the Q4 basket, but management never gives share percentages or comparisons against named competitors. (The long view had said: The record shows persistent expansion — international revenue from ~INR800 crores pre-2021 to INR3,800 crores in FY26 with local plants in Zambia, Tanzania, Zimbabwe, Ghana, Turkey, Kazakhstan — and unfazed talk of new entrants like Kalyani, but management explicitly cannot supply market-share numbers and frames domestic capacity growth as utilisation, not a share-gain strategy.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (STRONG FIT); Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Bulk explosives could in principle be commoditised by cheaper local suppliers, but the four calls show the opposite happening: raw material consumption fell from 53.5% to 48.7% of sales and FY26 EBITDA margin reached 27.95% with FY27 guided at current margins, which management attributes to a deliberate shift up the value chain into specialised explosives, technical services and defence rather than to price defence against cheaper substitutes. (The long view had said: Bulk commercial explosives are a substantially commoditised, tender-priced product where realisation tracks ammonium nitrate rather than brand, and Solar competes head-on with the global number one and two on price-driven CIL/SCCL contracts. The premium end — specialised formulations, technical/blasting services and qualified defence energetics with high entry barriers — is where mix shift is driving margin, which limits but does not remove the commoditisation pull on the domestic explosives base.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The growth engine has shifted to defence deep-tech — UAVs, loitering munitions, Bhargavastra and Rudrastra — a segment where product generations turn over fast and today's platform can be superseded; the calls show Solar on the innovating side (successful tests, repeat UAV and multi-mode grenade orders) rather than defending a legacy format, and the core explosives business shows steady incremental innovation, not platform risk. (The long view had said: Across 15 calls no competitor platform is discussed defensively and no installed base is described as legacy; instead the company shows a steady cadence of self-funded product development (SEBEX, chaff/flares, 23/30mm and 155mm ammunition, loitering munitions, Bhargavastra) plus automation and process investment, i.e. it is the innovator in its niches rather than an incumbent defending an old format.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Cyclicality (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
