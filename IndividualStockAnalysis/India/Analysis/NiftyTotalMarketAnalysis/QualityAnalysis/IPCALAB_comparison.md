**Checks that improved:**

- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Weak → Good. Now: The year shows organic focus with no new M&A, minimal capex, the legacy EU fine settled and cash rebuilt at Unichem, and a deliberately restrained launch cadence. | The long view had said: Working capital discipline is genuinely tight, but the framework's core test — no big M&A — is failed by Unichem plus underperforming Krebs Bio and Lyka Labs stakes that have diluted consolidated returns for three years.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹21 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±1.4 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹21 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±4.9 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: There is evidence of price stickiness on legacy molecules but no regional monopoly, captive aftermarket or switching-cost mechanism is described. | The long view had said: There is little switching-cost lock-in — Unichem lost major US molecules purely to competitor price cuts and must buy them back at lower prices, showing customers move on price alone.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: The record survives skeptical decomposition — standalone margins rose to 25.18% on identifiable pricing/mix drivers and guidance was beaten, while the acquired arm visibly underperforms, so results are not being read as uniform managerial brilliance. | The long view had said: The impressive margin arc (19.5% to 25.2% standalone) is partly exogenous — softer input costs and mix — and the Unichem/Lyka record shows the skill does not travel beyond the core Indian branded business, so results should not be read as uniform management skill.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Management runs an explicit 5-6% annual price-rise policy with no erosion on old molecules, and attributes the FY26 gross-margin expansion primarily to mix shift toward domestic/branded/chronic. | The long view had said: A standing 5-7% annual price-rise policy plus explicit attribution of the 2.5pp FY26 margin gain to mix shows pricing/mix leadership, though ~12% domestic growth still implies a meaningful volume component.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.4 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Domestic branded generics show rational pricing with 5-6% annual increases and no erosion, but the US generic side saw high-volume products lost on price/share competition in the same year. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±4.9 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Indian branded pricing is rational with orderly 5-7% annual increases and API cost pass-through, but the US generic and institutional tender segments show discount-driven share losses and a 24% institutional decline.

Now: R&D at ~4% of sales funds generic filings and line extensions, and for the key GLP-1 opportunity the company is seeking in-licensing rather than its own technology. | The long view had said: Backward integration into APIs and intermediates (including internalising two Chinese imports and 20-25% cost cuts on three major APIs) plus five biosimilar candidates gives a real cost/tech edge, though R&D intensity at 3.7% is moderate.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 7 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 7. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Ipca beat IPM growth in both acute and chronic in every quarter of the window (Q3: 15% chronic vs 12% market) and inched domestic share up to 2.09% by MAT March '26, with US consolidated sales +14% and West Africa +41% for 9M — gains achieved alongside rising, not falling, margins. (The long view had said: Consistent, quantified share gain over the whole period: IPM market share 1.91% (MAT Jun-23) to 2.01% to 2.08% to 2.09% (MAT Mar-26), ranked 16th and fastest-growing of the top 20, beating IPM in both acute and chronic every quarter, with growth earned on rising margins rather than bought.)

**That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No discussion of licence fees, AMC/service contracts, subscriptions, attach rates, renewals or customer advances funding the business anywhere in the four calls. (The long view had said: No discussion of licences, AMC/service contracts, subscriptions, attach rates, renewals or deferred revenue as a funding source across any of the calls; the model is product sales (domestic brands, exports, API, tenders).)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Margin improvement is attributed to product mix, currency and stable input costs rather than any cost culture or itemised savings; management explicitly positions on branded/promotional margins, not low price — 'even in generic businesses, we have very good margins'. (The long view had said: There is a genuine cost culture — 20-25% cost reduction on three major APIs, internalising two China-sourced intermediates, Unichem utility savings and air-to-sea freight shift from ~40% to ~4-5% — and material cost/sales fell 3.36pp in 9M FY26; but the savings are kept as margin rather than passed through as a low-price scale proposition.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (LIKELY FIT); Toll Roads (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Friendly Middleman (STRONG FIT); Pricing Power (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: No single customer, licensor or supplier is named as driving results across the four calls; revenue is spread across India (INR3,817cr), export formulations (INR2,083cr), US (INR1,567cr), API (INR1,396cr) and many geographies (CIS, West Africa, LatAm, Middle East, East Asia, Europe), and India itself runs through ~20 marketing divisions. The nearest thing to concentration is product-level, not stakeholder-level — Unichem losing share on four US molecules. Diversification is genuine but each channel still has single points of failure.)

(The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and all 2 computed fingerprints are clean.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Technological Innovation (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
