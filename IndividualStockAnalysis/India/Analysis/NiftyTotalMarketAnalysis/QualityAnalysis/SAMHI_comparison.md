## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 28 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 6 improved, 4 regressed, 18 unchanged. 6 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them).

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |

| Return on Capital | Weak (-0.67) | Neutral (+0.00) | +0.67 |
| Growth | Good (+0.67) | Good (+0.83) | +0.17 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Land, approvals and city-centre scarcity create real entry friction — management notes their Navi Mumbai cost is below replacement cost — and pricing has been rational, with rate-led RevPAR gains rather than discounting. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±25.7 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Capital intensity and long development cycles keep new supply very low in core cities, and pricing behaviour has been rational with rates being pushed up rather than discounted, including passing through the GST/ITC cost.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹35 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹35 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±25.7 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Good. Now: Physical portfolio density across India's five largest office markets is a reach rivals would need years and heavy capital to replicate, but booking distribution itself is outsourced to the brand partners. | The long view had said: Distribution reach is real but rented — global brands act as the bridge to the customer — with SAMHI's own edge limited to portfolio density across four core Indian office markets.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: Accessibility is the entire thesis — assets are deliberately sited inside or adjacent to the office corridors, airports and infrastructure nodes where their corporate demand originates. | The long view had said: Accessibility is the explicit siting strategy — hotels are concentrated in India's largest office and aviation micro-markets, giving proximity-led convenience to the corporate traveller.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Credible domestic expansion completing presence in India's five largest office markets — first Mumbai entry at Navi Mumbai, a Noida lease with Ingka/IKEA, and a third Hyderabad Financial District asset — but this is city-level replication of a known playbook, not premium-brand international travel. | The long view had said: Expansion is deliberate deepening within four existing Indian office markets (~76% of asset income) plus Noida/Navi Mumbai rather than genuinely new geographies, and there is no international footprint.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Micro-market clustering gives a local advantage — three assets across all three price points in Hyderabad's Financial District — but hotel guests face essentially no switching costs. | The long view had said: Strong micro-market positions (HITEC City, Whitefield, ORR Bangalore) confer local scarcity while supply is tight, but hotel guests face essentially zero switching costs and corporate rates are renegotiated annually.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Management set explicit annual commitments and reported against them, quantified the GST hit and each disruption line by line, and voluntarily improved disclosure — but leans heavily on 'adjusted' bridges to a 19–20% growth narrative. | The long view had said: Management consistently volunteers bad news and quantifies it — flat ACIC revenue, lagging F&B outlet income, GST margin hit of ~200bps, INR 45-52 crores of event-driven revenue loss — and separates reported from adjusted numbers.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: SAMHI owns real estate but not the customer relationship — every asset trades under a licensed Marriott or IHG flag, so no monopoly-like position exists in the guest's mind. | The long view had said: SAMHI holds a dominant share of specific brand footprints in India (71% of Holiday Inn Express, 40%+ of Fairfield) but the customer-facing brand equity belongs to the licensor, so there is no monopoly in the guest's mind.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: Corporate guests do buy reliability, but the assurance is delivered by the international flags SAMHI licenses rather than by anything proprietary, and the calls give no evidence of price being secondary to trust. | The long view had said: The core proposition is branded reliability for business travellers — global brand standards and Marriott-managed operations — evidenced by the franchise-to-managed conversion deliberately shedding low-rated business for higher-rated corporate demand.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: The customer base is correctly identified as corporate business travel tied to office absorption, but there is no lock-in or switching-cost mechanism described — corporate rate contracts are renegotiated annually. | The long view had said: The customer base is predominantly corporate/business travel, matched with reliability and brand-standard assurance and annual contract negotiation, but with no real switching-cost lock-in on the guest side.

*Unchanged: Distribution to shareholders; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Network effects; Cost of working capital; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Disciplined stewards; Out of limelight; Cyclical market growth; Security by obscurity; Merger and acquisition; Asset turn; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Intangible benefits; Gain in market share; Structural end-market growth; Halo effect (caution); Return on capital (headline).*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 2 weakened, 4 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: the calls are silent and the numbers are mixed (1 of 2 checks supporting). (The long view had said: Across all 13 calls revenue is transactional room-nights measured by RevPAR/ARR/occupancy, repriced daily; there is no discussion of licence fees, AMCs, renewal rates, attach rates or deferred revenue/customer advances funding the business — in fact SAMHI pays fees to brand owners rather than collecting them.)
- **Brand Strength**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Every brand named on these calls — W, Westin, Tribute, Fairfield, Sheraton, Holiday Inn Express, Outdoor Collection by Marriott Bonvoy — belongs to Marriott or IHG, not to SAMHI; SAMHI is the owner/investor that licenses them and pays for the affiliation. It captures real distribution and premium-positioning benefit from that brand access, but the heritage and consumer trust are a rented asset, so no own-brand pricing moat is evidenced. (The long view had said: SAMHI owns no consumer brand — it licenses Marriott, IHG and Hyatt brands and even moved ACIC from Marriott franchise to Marriott managed, so the heritage, A&P and loyalty distribution sit with the operators; SAMHI's identity to investors is 'smart hotel investments', not a consumer promise.)

**That weakened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: LIKELY FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: the only computable check points this way but the calls offer no evidence — numbers alone are only ever a screening hint, never a confirmed fit. (The long view had said: The only innovation asset disclosed is the proprietary SAMHI Intel asset-management analytics tool (Nov 2023) used for demand-compression yield insights; there is no disclosed R&D spend, product pipeline or named annual launches driving pricing power.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Room count is rising materially (4,900 operational plus ~1,900 under development/rebranding, ~1,450 net additions, taking the portfolio past 6,300) and Navi Mumbai completes presence across India's five largest office markets, with a claimed three-price-point cluster in Hyderabad's Financial District. But no explicit market-share percentages or comparisons to named competitors appear in any of the four calls, so this is inventory growth rather than demonstrated share gain. (The long view had said: Real inventory-led share gain — third-largest room owner in India within 12 years, dominant share of specific brand systems (71% of India's Holiday Inn Express, >40% of Fairfield), 4,800 to 4,900 operational rooms with 1,900 more under development toward INR 3,000 crores revenue by 2030 — but the gain is bought with acquisitions and capital (GIC, Ingka leases) rather than shown as persistent competitive share numbers against named rivals.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Pricing Power (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 12 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
