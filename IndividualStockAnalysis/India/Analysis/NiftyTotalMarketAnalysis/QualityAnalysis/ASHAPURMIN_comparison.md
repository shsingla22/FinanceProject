## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 29 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 8 improved, 3 regressed, 18 unchanged. 2 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them). 1 was newly assessable in the recent view.

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Allocation | Good (+1.00) | Good (+1.00) | +0.00 |
| Return on Capital | Neutral (+0.00) | Neutral (+0.00) | +0.00 |
| Growth | Neutral (+0.33) | Neutral (+0.50) | +0.17 |
| Management | Good (+0.83) | Good (+0.83) | +0.00 |
| Industry Structure | Neutral (-0.50) | Neutral (+0.33) | +0.83 |
| Customer Benefits | Neutral (+0.00) | Neutral (+0.50) | +0.50 |
| Competitive Advantage | Neutral (+0.50) | Weak (-0.67) | -1.17 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Weak → Good. Now: The company operates in unglamorous industrial minerals and in a high-risk West African jurisdiction that most non-Chinese capital avoids, and only began investor outreach in FY26. | The long view had said: Guinea bauxite is the opposite of an ignored niche — it is the focus of intense Chinese capital, with 90% of the country's business done by Chinese companies and over 200mt of exports this year.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Weak → Good. Now: The India business enjoys a locational quasi-monopoly with captive mines, plant and seaport inside a 100km radius plus seven new bentonite leases, but there is no evidence of customer switching costs. | The long view had said: There are no switching costs or captive aftermarket in the core business — bauxite is sold almost entirely to Chinese refineries at index-linked prices with no fixed multi-year pricing, so customers can substitute freely.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: Guinea bauxite is not a stable oligopoly for Ashapura — Chinese players hold ~90% of the business and the company is an index-linked price taker to large refinery customers. | The long view had said: This is the opposite of a favourable oligopoly: Ashapura is a sub-scale supplier into a single end market where the customer sets index prices, and supply discipline is absent — the reopening of suspended Guinean leases alone crashed realisations from $70 to $60.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: The Guinea entry since 2016 has been executed with real infrastructure and is now producing volume, and India products reach 80+ countries — credible and well-executed, though it is commodity rather than brand-led. | The long view had said: The Guinea entry is a credible, hard-won geographic expansion executed over 9-11 years to become the only non-Chinese operator with sustainable standing, plus India exporting processed minerals to 80+ countries — but it is resource-access expansion, not a premium brand travelling.
- **Candor** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Management openly conceded a shortfall in Q3 with specific causes, admitted the bauxite price worry as its own, and voluntarily flagged the labour-code exceptional item and India margin pressure. | The long view had said: Management openly owns misses — the Q3 volume shortfall against internal targets, a pre-warned 40-50% EBITDA moderation, iron ore commissioning running slower than expected, and the labour-code exceptional item — but repeatedly refuses to quantify capex splits, demurrage and cost breakups when asked directly.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: The pitch to customers is reliability of long-term supply backed by long-term logistics and freight tie-ups and established quality, though price remains the primary commodity variable. | The long view had said: Management positions Ashapura as a long-term reliable supplier and cites higher-grade reserves that can command a premium, but price plainly dominates the relationship since contracts float with the China index and volumes swing with spot economics.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Leading positions in Indian bentonite, bleaching clay and kaolin plus the first propant plant in India give a near-monopoly standing in narrow domestic mineral niches, though not in bauxite. | The long view had said: The India franchise holds leadership positions (largest bentonite, bleaching clay and kaolin producer in India, third largest globally in the first two) that resemble a niche mini-monopoly, but this is capacity leadership without demonstrated pricing power — India margins fell on raw-material costs — while 76-79% of revenue is undifferentiated bauxite.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Weak → Neutral. Now: The customer base is entirely corporate/industrial where TCO and reliability are the right levers, and Ashapura offers reliability, but there is no evidence of switching-cost lock-in. | The long view had said: The dominant customer set is corporate (Chinese alumina refineries) but the benefit offered is neither TCO/reliability lock-in nor switching costs — it is spot-indexed commodity supply, a poor match of benefit type to customer.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: There is no clear identification of weak competitors ceding share; the proposed quota is described as curtailing exports generally, and the India competitive set is never discussed. | The long view had said: Weak and constrained competitors are available to take share from — the proposed quota system is aimed at curbing the 70-80mt Chinese majors, and Ashapura's newer, higher-grade deposits let it earn a quality premium against long-operating mines.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Leadership is promoter-family based with succession running from Chetan Shah to Manan Shah, and no systematic talent development or internal pipeline is described beyond generic team appreciation. | The long view had said: Low attrition with 10-15 year average tenure across 2,200 employees and a 75-scientist research bench indicate real talent retention, though succession is presented as promoter-family (Chetan Shah to Manan Shah) rather than a described internal pipeline.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Weak. Now: The advantage claimed is resource and logistics based rather than technological; product upgrades are incremental processing steps and no R&D intensity is disclosed. | The long view had said: There is real applied-technology depth in the India minerals business (multi-product R&D converting $40/t bentonite into $400/t bleaching clay), but it is confined to roughly a quarter of revenue while the dominant Guinea business carries no technology edge at all.

*Unchanged: Pricing, Mix and Volume; Cyclical market growth; Gain in market share; Return on capital (headline); Asset turn; Distribution advantage; Cost of working capital; Structural end-market growth; Disciplined stewards; Halo effect (caution); Convenience benefits; Out of limelight; Profit margin; Barriers to entry and rationality; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Research and development; Intangible benefits; Capex for growth (not maintenance).*

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these — identical by construction): Persistence of growth (Weak); Distribution to shareholders (Neutral).*

**Newly assessable in the recent view:**

- **Network effects** — Poor: No two-sided network exists — this is a bulk mineral extraction and export business with no self-reinforcing user dynamics mentioned in any call.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: The four FY26 calls describe bauxite/iron-ore export volumes and mineral product sales only; no licence fees, AMC/service contracts, attach rates, renewals, installed base or customer advances are discussed anywhere. (The long view had said: No discussion of licence fees, AMC/service contracts, subscriptions, attach rates, renewals or customer advances across any of the four calls — revenue is cargo-by-cargo bauxite shipments and mineral product sales.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Across FY26 the company more than doubled Guinea bauxite exports to ~8 million tons from 3.5 million, grew India revenue ~25% in H1, and did so with rising rather than bought margins (EBITDA margin 10.8%→14.9%, FY26 revenue +105%); it also claims leadership positions in bentonite, bleaching clay and kaolin and is the one non-Chinese operator with standing in a market where Chinese firms do 90% of the business. (The long view had said: Volume share gain is real and quantified — Guinea exports rose from 3.5mt (FY25) to ~8mt (FY26) targeting 15mt by FY27-28, and it is the only non-Chinese operator with standing in Guinea, plus largest bentonite/bleaching-clay producer in India and third largest globally — but the gain came with EBITDA/tonne halving to $5.9 and FY27 volume guidance already trimmed to 10-12mt, which is exactly the 'share bought with profitability' caveat.)

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Nothing in the four calls points to brand-based premium or A&P investment; the product is an index-priced commodity plus B2B industrial minerals, and the only 'reputation' claim is being a recognised reliable supplier, not a priced brand. (The long view had said: Management claims brand standing in the India minerals business as a China-Plus-One alternative and points to exports to 80+ countries, but there is no A&P spend, heritage narrative, premium pricing or distributor leverage evidence — and 76-80% of revenue is undifferentiated Guinea bauxite sold ~100% to China.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Guinea was 76% of Q3 revenue and is sold overwhelmingly into the Chinese alumina complex, with mining, transport and handling now outsourced under a long-term tie-up to a Chinese contractor (China Railway) and freight to a single long-term shipping counterparty; management explicitly frames pricing around Chinese New Year and US-China trade deals. The nascent iron ore stream is sold to one local beneficiation plant. (The long view had said: Guinea is ~76-79% of both top and bottom line, and management confirms that bauxite goes 'almost all to China' — a single end-market whose smelter policy and New Year cycle set pricing; mining, logistics and transshipment are outsourced to Chinese majors/China Railway, and the entire iron ore ramp depends on one local beneficiation plant partner.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (QUANT FLAG); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); New Entrants (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
