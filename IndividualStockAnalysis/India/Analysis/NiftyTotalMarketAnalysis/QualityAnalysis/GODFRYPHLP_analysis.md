## The verdict: Mixed — 48 out of 100 ★★☆☆☆

- **Risk safety (88/100):** started from a clean 100; 1 numbers-only flag (−4); the balance sheet shows one stress signal (−8) → 88 of 100. Caution: only 1 of the 8 risk channels could be tested on this evidence — treat this as provisional, not a clean bill of health.

### The story in depth

Godfrey Phillips India comes out of the three reviews as a genuinely profitable business that struggles to turn that profitability into cash returns. It keeps ₹45 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs and ₹25 of every ₹100 as operating profit, which the quality framework reads as real pricing power that customers are willing to pay for — the one clearly positive area in the whole scorecard (Customer Benefits scores +1). But for every ₹100 of capital in the business, only ₹8 of operating cash came back this year. The bridge between the two numbers is working capital: roughly 230 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, the single worst check in the framework at -2, and it drags the Capital Allocation area to -2.0 on its own. Sales have compounded at about 9% a year, but lumpily, and each ₹1 of assets produces ₹0.77 of sales. The combined rating of 48 out of 100 — 45% × 47 for business quality, 30% × 16 for multibagger fit, 25% × 88 for risk safety — lands at Mixed, two stars, with resilience marked STRAINED.

The same fat margins that earn the quality credit are also what the multibagger screen picks up, and what the risk review flags. Pricing Power and Innovation Dominance both register as QUANT SIGNAL — the numbers point that way — while Brand Strength, Recurring Revenue, Friendly Middleman and Forward Integrators come in as PARTIAL. None of the nine patterns reaches a full fit, and Toll Roads, Low Price Plus and Market Share Gainers are outright NO FIT, which is why the multibagger contribution is only 16 out of 100 and drags the overall score down hardest. Crucially, every single pattern verdict carries the same note: no conference-call judgement available. So the screen is running on numbers alone, with no management commentary to confirm or contradict them. The one risk raised, Good Enough Goods, sits in exactly the same position — one numeric fingerprint is present, the calls say nothing either way, and the review itself is explicit that numbers alone are a flag to investigate, never a verdict. Pricing power and the risk of being a merely-good-enough product are two readings of the same margin profile, and neither has been settled.

That silence is also why the risk safety score of 88 should be read carefully: only one of eight channels flagged anything, but the checks that would normally be settled by management's own words — Competitive Advantage and Management both come back as null in the quality areas — simply have no evidence behind them. What the numbers do say is that the moat is not steady. Operating margins swing by about ±5.1 points a year, which the framework treats both as a hint of price wars, cost shocks or new entrants disturbing the industry (Barriers to entry scores 0) and as a sign that key costs sit outside management's control. Industry Structure and Growth both score 0. So the story that connects all three reviews is a company with visible product-level strength and a light risk register, whose earnings quality is undermined from two directions at once: a very long cash cycle that swallows the profit before it becomes a return on capital, and margins that move enough year to year to question how protected that profit really is. Mixed is the honest label — this is not a broken business, but the case for it rests on numbers that no one has yet had to explain.

## What to watch

- The 230-day cash cycle (Cost of working capital, -2) — shortening it is the single fastest route from a 25% operating margin to a better than 8% cash return on capital.
- Whether the ±5.1 point margin swing (Barriers to entry, Profit margin) narrows or widens — steadier margins would support the Pricing Power and Brand Strength patterns; wider swings would confirm cost shocks or new entrants.
- Any conference-call commentary at all — every pattern verdict and the Good Enough Goods risk flag are currently marked 'no conference-call judgement available', and Competitive Advantage and Management score null for the same reason.
- Whether the Good Enough Goods flag is confirmed or dismissed — it and the Pricing Power signal are competing readings of the same 45% retained-after-input-costs figure, and only one can be right.
- Whether the ~9% sales growth becomes less lumpy (Persistence of growth, 0) and asset turn improves from ₹0.77 of sales per ₹1 of assets — both would lift the Growth area off zero.
