# RBL Bank Ltd. (RBLBANK) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

RBL Bank is a mid-sized Indian private-sector bank that takes deposits from the public and lends the money out, operating through a network of about 562 branches (18 added in the Q3 FY26 quarter alone) plus the branch-correspondent touchpoints of its wholly owned subsidiary RBL Finserve (RFL), along with call centres and digital channels including a unified mobile app. Its customers range from salaried and small-ticket retail borrowers and microfinance groups in rural areas, to small and mid-sized companies and larger corporates; roughly 59% of its loan book is retail and 41% wholesale. It makes money mainly on the spread between what it earns on loans (net interest income of INR1,657 crores in the Dec-2025 quarter, at a net interest margin of about 4.63%) and what it pays on deposits (cost of deposits about 6.2%), plus fee income of roughly INR959 crores a quarter from products such as credit cards. As of the Jan-2026 call it had about INR1,03,086 crores of net advances and INR1,19,721 crores of deposits, operates in India (including a GIFT City unit), and Emirates NBD of the UAE has agreed to invest about US$3 billion for a 60% stake, with Emirates NBD's India branches to be merged into the bank once approvals come through.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Wholesale banking (including commercial banking)** — Lends to companies — with 'commercial banking', meaning mid-sized and small corporates, described as the key engine growing about 30% year-on-year, alongside selective large-corporate lending; wholesale is roughly 41% of advances and management says it contributes meaningfully to advances, low-cost deposits and profits, with near-zero or negative net slippages.
- **Secured retail** — Loans to individuals and small businesses backed by collateral — home and affordable housing loans, secured business loans (over INR10,000 crores), tractor finance (over INR3,000 crores), secured working capital (over INR2,000 crores), gold loans and 'wheels'/vehicle finance — now about 34% of advances, growing 24-25% year-on-year with quarterly disbursals above INR5,000 crores, and recently turned profitable at the operating level.
- **Credit cards (and personal loans)** — Unsecured card lending where customers spend about INR7,000 crores a month; new-card sourcing through the Bajaj Finance partnership stopped in November 2024, but the bank crossed 1 lakh new cards in a single month by Q3 FY26 and cards-in-force grew again, though card bad-loan slippages (INR539 crores in the quarter) remain the bank's biggest problem area.
- **JLG / microfinance** — Small group loans (Joint Liability Group) to low-income, largely rural borrowers, sourced through subsidiary RBL Finserve; it went through heavy stress in 2024-25 but is now disbursing about INR700 crores a month with early-bucket collection efficiency of 99.5% and much lower slippages (INR130 crores in the Dec-2025 quarter).
- **Branch banking and retail liabilities (deposits)** — Gathers deposits from retail customers through branches — 66% of total deposits are branch-led and 'granular' deposits (accounts under INR3 crores) are 51.5% of the INR1,19,721 crore deposit base — and branches increasingly also originate loans, at about INR400 crores of disbursals a month.
- **RBL Finserve (RFL), wholly owned subsidiary** — The bank's business-correspondent and field distribution arm, originally the microfinance feet-on-street, now also sourcing affordable housing loans, small micro-LAP and individual loans to credit-tested JLG borrowers, described as gaining traction and potentially a meaningful contributor to secured loan sourcing.
- **Treasury and GIFT City** — Manages the bank's own investments (bond gains added to other income in Q1 FY26) and runs an international banking unit at GIFT City that has begun offering new products to retail clients; management says both continue to perform well, without giving a size.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**What the calls show:** The five calls are dominated by a credit cycle: JLG/microfinance slippages spiking (net slippage INR521cr in Q3FY25 vs INR231cr prior quarter), card slippages elevated for 6+ quarters, PAT collapsing to INR33cr in Q3FY25, NIM sliding from 4.9% to 4.5% then recovering to 4.63% — earnings and margins swing violently with the consumer credit cycle, and management itself frames CGFMU cover as protection 'if and when there is another cycle'.
