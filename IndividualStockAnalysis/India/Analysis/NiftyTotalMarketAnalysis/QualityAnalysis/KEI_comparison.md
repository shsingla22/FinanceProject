Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing discipline is evident — margins expanded through a commodity spike with retail prices revised every 15 days and B2B price-variation clauses, implying no discount war; entry barriers are high only in EHV. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.3 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Multiple well-capitalised new entrants have entered the wires segment and pricing is a formulaic metal pass-through rather than a defended price point, indicating entry barriers are low in the retail-facing part of the business.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Growing 20%+ against an industry growing far slower, with B2C dealer sales up 25-29% and exports up 45-95%, implying clear share capture from a non-dominant base. | The long view had said: KEI has ample runway in a ~INR75,000cr market and is taking share from weak dealers, but it admitted losing relative ground in FY26 because its own capacity was maxed out while industry grew faster.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: FY26 revenue growth of 20.7% came largely from copper/aluminium price pass-through (copper +16.85%) with net volume up only 6.21%, but margin expansion to 11.81% and rising EHV mix show genuine mix benefit rather than pure commodity inflation. | The long view had said: Headline growth is largely metal-price pass-through rather than pricing power (FY26: value +20.7% but net metal volume only +6.2%), though mix is genuinely improving via EHV, exports and B2C lifting EBITDA margin to 11.81%.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Good. ### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 0 weakened, 9 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Q2 FY26 shows 22% wire & cable value growth of which 15% was volume and the balance price, with retail prices reset every 15 days and price-variation clauses on B2B orders — but management frames this as commodity pass-through that leaves margins unchanged, not price rises above cost inflation; genuine margin expansion (EBITDA 10.92%→11.81% in FY26) came alongside a 16.85% copper cost rise. (The long view had said: Pricing is explicitly a pass-through of copper/aluminium, revised fortnightly up or down and built into tender price-variation clauses — not price increases above cost inflation; the Feb 2026 '15% wire, 10% cable' rise was purely metal-driven.)
- **Forward Integrators**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: KEI moves downstream through its EHV EPC/turnkey division (Q4 FY26 EHV EPC sales Rs.106 cr vs Rs.32 cr) which controls project delivery to the end customer, but there are no own stores, franchising, royalties or D2C e-commerce — retail reach is entirely through third-party dealers. (The long view had said: KEI sells through independent dealers and 26 depots with no own stores, franchising, royalties or D2C; the B2C shift is distributor-led wholesaling, not forward integration to the end customer.)

*Unchanged: Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Low Price Plus (QUANT SIGNAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls management frames demand almost entirely in terms of customers' capex programmes — solar/wind build-out, T&D projects, data centres, metros/railways, real estate — and its own INR2,000 crore Sanand expansion is a classic expansionary-cycle bet (delayed 6 months on phase 1, ~9 months on phase 2, depreciation load landing FY28). Input costs are pure commodity (average copper +16.85%, aluminium +9.91% in FY26), but realised margins were stable-to-rising every quarter (11.49% → 11.43% → 12.0% → 12.21%), so the classic cyclical margin whipsaw is not visible in the last 12 months. (The long view had said: Across all 15 calls demand is framed in terms of customer capex programmes (solar developers, T&D utilities, RDSS, thermal/pumped storage, real estate, data centres) and headline growth is substantially metal-price inflation — FY26 net sales grew 20.66% while net metal volume grew only 6.21% and copper averaged +16.85%; KEI is also committing ~INR2,000 crores of greenfield capex (Sanand) plus new Bhiwadi/Baroda land at a visible cycle peak. Bounded, however, because margins have not swung with the cycle — EBITDA margin rose steadily from ~10.4% (FY23) to 11.81% (FY26) through both copper up- and down-moves — and B2C distribution is now 54-56% of sales.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The largest and fastest-growing block is B2C house wire and LT cable sold through ~2,125 dealers (56% of Q4 sales, +29%), a channel where the dealer/electrician intermediary influences the choice and where pricing is reset every 15 days off commodity movements — the structural setup in which cheaper good-enough substitutes attack a branded premium. The calls contain no discussion of KEI defending its price premium, and margins in fact expanded to 12.21% in Q4, so the exposure is real but not yet visible in results. (The long view had said: House wire and LT cable are BIS-standard, relatively simple products sold through an intermediary channel where dealer margin and stocking drive the choice — management, when asked what makes a dealer stick with KEI, fell back on approvals, brand and depot stocking rather than any product moat, and is simultaneously facing cheaper new wire entrants and 'exchanging from the weak hands to the stronger hands' in the channel. Protection exists at the top end but not across the retail book.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Results are driven by a broad base — approximately 2,125 active working dealers as of 31 March 2026 with B2C at 54% of sales, and an order book spread across domestic cable, EHV, export and EPC with no single named customer whose renewal management is questioned. Only one customer is named at all (Reliance's Jamnagar polysilicon plant), as an illustration rather than a dependency. (The long view had said: Supplier side is concentrated by management's own admission — 85% of metal is bought from just Hindalco and Vedanta — and an analyst pressed on channel concentration, with ~100 of 2,125 dealers said to drive 70-80% of sales; no single end-customer, however, is ever cited as driving results.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
