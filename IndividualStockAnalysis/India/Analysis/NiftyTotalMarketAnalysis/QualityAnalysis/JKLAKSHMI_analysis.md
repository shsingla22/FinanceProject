# JK Lakshmi Cement (JKLAKSHMI) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

JK Lakshmi Cement is an Indian cement maker: it digs limestone from its own mines, burns it into clinker at its plants, and grinds that clinker into bagged and bulk cement that it sells to builders, contractors and infrastructure projects. Its home base is Rajasthan (registered works at Jaykaypuram, Sirohi, with mines at Sirohi, Udaipur and a newly acquired lease at Nagaur), and it sells mainly across North India (Rajasthan, Haryana, western UP), the West (Gujarat, Mumbai — where a new Surat grinding station started in September 2025) and a smaller position in the East. Money comes from tons sold times the price per ton, so management talks constantly about volume, realisation per ton, the mix between dealer/retail 'trade' sales (roughly 49%–56% of volume across the calls) and lower-priced institutional 'non-trade' sales, and about pushing higher-priced 'premium' products (about 21% of trade volume in FY23, around 30% by FY25). Profit is then driven by squeezing costs — fuel (pet coke and coal), power, and freight measured as average 'lead distance' (down from ~395 km in FY23 to ~372 km) — plus greener inputs like alternative fuels and renewable power.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Cement (the core business)** — Manufacturing and selling grey cement — roughly two-thirds blended cement and one-third OPC (ordinary Portland cement) in FY23, with a stated goal of moving blended to 72%–75% — and this is overwhelmingly where the revenue and profit come from.
- **Premium products** — A higher-priced branded slice of the cement range that management deliberately pushes to lift realisation per ton; it grew from about 14% to over 21% of trade sales volume during FY23 and to roughly 30% by Q1 FY25.
- **Trade vs non-trade channel** — Not a separate product but the way management splits the same cement: 'trade' means dealer/retail bags at better prices, 'non-trade' means bulk institutional sales to projects and onsite ready-mix plants — the trade share ran about 49%–56% and swings in it visibly move average realisation.
- **Ready-mix concrete (RMC)** — A concrete-mixing operation that sells ready-mixed concrete to sites; it was about Rs. 52 crore of revenue in one FY23 quarter, so a small add-on next to cement.
- **Non-cement revenue** — A catch-all 'other products' line — about Rs. 116 crore in a FY23 quarter (including the Rs. 52 crore of RMC) — clearly a minor contributor alongside cement.
- **Udaipur Cement Works Limited (UCWL)** — A separately listed cement subsidiary that JK Lakshmi controls, whose Udaipur expansion (clinker line targeted for around March 2024, grinding a month or two later) was being ramped toward 60% utilisation by end-FY25; a merger of UCWL into JK Lakshmi was proposed at the board in mid-2024.
- **Limestone mines and leases** — The raw-material base that underpins everything — reserves of roughly 15–20 years at Sirohi (lease renewal due 2030), 40–50 years at Udaipur plus an adjacent new lease, and a Nagaur lease said to support a future 5–7 million ton plant for 45–50 years.
- **Grinding stations and logistics assets** — Split grinding units and in-plant railway sidings — including the Surat grinding station commissioned 22 September 2025 — that let the company grind clinker closer to customers, reach new markets and cut freight/lead distance.
- **Cost and sustainability levers** — Ongoing efficiency programmes — alternative fuels and raw materials (AFR at ~12% of fuel in Q1 FY25 vs 7% the prior year), renewable power (~48% vs 39%), digital/supply-chain optimisation and headcount productivity that cut employee cost — which management treats as the main defence when cement prices are weak.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*
