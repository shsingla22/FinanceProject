# Cams Services (CAMS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

CAMS (Computer Age Management Services) is the back-office and technology plumbing behind India's mutual fund industry: it acts as a registrar and transfer agent (RTA), meaning it processes investor transactions, keeps the records of who owns which fund units, handles new purchases, SIPs, redemptions and new fund offers, and runs the investor-facing apps and platforms that sit on top of that. Its customers are asset management companies (fund houses) — it serves 4 of the top 5 AMCs by equity assets — and it earns money mainly as a fee on the assets under management it services, so revenue moves with fund AUM (over INR55 lakh crore serviced, roughly 68% of industry AUM, and about 67% of industry equity assets), plus per-transaction and platform fees. Around this core it has built a set of adjacent 'non-MF' businesses — KYC registration, payments, alternative-fund servicing, insurance repository, account aggregation, pension records — which management is deliberately scaling and which now contribute roughly 15% of total revenue. It operates in India (including GIFT City funds), with the mutual fund business still the bulk of revenue and margins in the mid-40s percent EBITDA.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Mutual Fund (MF) services / core RTA business** — Keeps the official records and processes every buy, sell, SIP and new-fund-offer for the fund houses it serves — by far the biggest part of the company (around 85% of revenue), servicing INR55 lakh crore of AUM at ~68% industry share and processing over 107 crore financial transactions a year, and it is being rebuilt from scratch on a new Google Cloud platform.
- **CAMS KRA** — A KYC registration agency that stores and verifies investor identity records so investors don't have to re-do KYC with every provider — originally serving mutual funds, now also brokerages and depository participants; it grew over 100% in one quarter in FY25 and became the second-largest KRA after taking over NSE's KRA business.
- **CAMSPay** — The payments arm that collects money from investors for financial firms — notably UPI AutoPay for SIP mandates, where CAMS says it was first to market and is now a top BFSI player — and it has added a payment gateway and a payment aggregator licence; growth of 23%–59% year-on-year in recent quarters.
- **Alternatives (AIF / PMS services, incl. CAMS WealthServ)** — Does the same record-keeping and fund-administration work for alternative investment funds and portfolio management services, plus a digital client-onboarding platform (WealthServ); it services over INR3 lakh crore of AUM, about 50% of the outsourced market, growing 16%–25% a year with roughly 250 onboarding mandates signed.
- **CAMSRep (insurance repository) and Bima Central** — Holds insurance policies in electronic form for policyholders and gives them an app (Bima Central) to manage their portfolio, plus tools that helped insurers trace and settle unclaimed benefits; it has crossed 1 crore electronic insurance accounts and ~1.3 crore policies at about 40% market share, but management repeatedly says the revenue is still small and 'scratching the surface'.
- **Account Aggregator (CAMS Finserv)** — A consent-based service that lets a consumer share their financial data with lenders or advisers, sold alongside an analytics product called Amaze for personal finance and lending use cases; market share climbed from 2%–3% to the low-to-mid teens, but revenue was still only about INR1–2 crore a quarter as of FY25.
- **NPS CRA (pension record-keeping)** — A central record-keeping agency licensed by the pension regulator for the National Pension System, launched in 2022 as the first cloud-based CRA; it quickly reached over 10% share and the number-two position in direct-to-consumer eNPS sales, with corporate and distributor channels in pilot at the time.
- **MFCentral and myCAMS (investor-facing digital platforms)** — Free investor-facing platforms — MFCentral, built jointly with the other RTA, lets investors see and transact across all their mutual fund holdings in one place, and myCAMS is described as the largest mutual fund app in the country with over 5.25 million users; they serve mainly as ecosystem hooks and as a source of APIs sold to fintechs and advisers rather than large direct revenue.
- **Digital / platform stack for capital markets** — A bundle of supporting services sold across the ecosystem — eKYC, e-signing, digital onboarding, anti-money-laundering checks, validation, reconciliation products and loan-against-mutual-funds enablement for banks and NBFCs — which management frames as making CAMS a 'full stack' platform for capital markets rather than as a separately sized revenue line.

### The story in depth

CAMS earns an unusually high grade — 83 out of 100, or Outstanding — because one structural fact does most of the work: it sits in the record-keeping layer of Indian mutual funds with roughly 68% of industry assets under service, four of the top five equity fund houses, half the outsourced alternative-fund market and 40% of the insurance repository. That position is what the quality framework scores as a mini-monopoly and what the pattern screen recognises three separate ways. It is a toll road, taking a few basis points out of a fund house's 140-200 basis point expense ratio for work the fund cannot stop paying for. It is recurring revenue, because the hard part is winning the fund-house logo once (six were taken live in FY26, including Carnelian, Neo and Oaklane) after which asset-based fees, KYC registry, repository and payments income simply keep arriving. And it is expensive to replicate: a challenger would need a SEBI licence, three decades of accumulated investor records and the migration of more than five crore folios out of a system fund houses cannot afford to see break. The financial evidence matches — ₹45 of operating profit on every ₹100 of sales, margins up from 43.5% to 46.5%, and ₹42 of operating cash for every ₹100 of capital employed.

The same three traits that create the patterns also create most of the risks, and it is worth being blunt about that. Because the service sits in the back office, the end investor never chooses CAMS and never sees the bill — the fund house decides and keeps any saving. That is the classic 'good enough' setup, and it has already partly played out: a company-wide price reset with fund houses was flagged in January 2025 and absorbed through the following two quarters, the KYC registry business took a price-down in FY26, and the pattern screen rates pricing power an outright non-fit, noting management defends price rather than raising it. The narrowness of the customer universe compounds it — about 31 licensed fund houses in total, so a handful of named relationships carry the bulk of revenue, which is why stakeholder concentration is the one risk rated Elevated rather than merely Watch. The regulatory anchoring that keeps competitors out also means SEBI ultimately sets what fund houses can afford to pay: the removal of the 5 basis point exit load immediately prompted questions about another round of contract renegotiation. And because fees are linked to assets, market swings pass straight through — assets fell from ₹58.5 lakh crore to ₹55.1 lakh crore in Q4 FY26. This is exactly why the quality scores are lowest on Growth and Industry Structure even as returns on capital score highest.

What holds the story together is management's response to precisely these pressures, which is why culture is rated a strong fit and candor, stewardship and long-term tenacity all score full marks. The same leadership has been in place across the whole 2022-2026 window, bad news is volunteered rather than extracted — loss-making account aggregator and pension units, slower insurance, a disappointing non-fund contribution — and stated goals have been hit repeatedly. The strategic answer to the fee-pressure problem is diversification away from the single regulated revenue pool, and it is working slowly but visibly: non-fund revenue has gone from about 9% to 15.3% of the total against a stated 20% target, built from products incubated since FY21-22 such as the KYC registry, payments, account aggregator, Bima Central and WealthServ. The answer to the fintech threat is to build the new digital layers itself rather than cede them, which is why the new-entrant risk stays at Watch. The answer to a core platform management openly calls several decades old is a ground-up rebuild on Google Cloud, funded through the profit and loss account with no debt and no transformational acquisitions — though that re-platforming is itself the technology risk. Meanwhile share keeps creeping up on every measure disclosed (equity assets 60% to 67%, equity net sales 71% to 76%, SIP collections 57% to 64%), and costs grew just 9% against an 11% target. The overall risk picture is judged sound: pressure on price is real and recurring, but nothing in the record threatens the position itself.

## What to watch

- Another price reset with the large fund houses — the Stakeholder Concentration risk is already rated Elevated, and the removal of the 5 basis point exit load has already prompted questions about fresh renegotiation.
- Non-fund revenue stalling below the stated 20% target after reaching 15.3% — this is the main hedge against the Government Dependency risk of a single SEBI-set fee pool.
- Yield compression from the drift into passive funds, which the finance chief attributes 'entirely' to mix — the core of the Shifting Consumer Preferences risk and the reason Pricing Power is scored a non-fit.

- Any reversal in the share numbers that underpin the Market Share Gainers pattern — equity assets at 67%, equity net sales at 76%, SIP collections at 64% — or a break in the 9%-versus-11% cost discipline that has been holding margins at 46.5%.
