### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 4 weakened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Margins expanded (FY26 EBITDA 18.33% vs 17.52%; Q1FY27 20.40%) with 'calibrated pricing actions' against steel inflation, but management explicitly de-emphasises price as the lever and describes the FY26 topline as flattish — no evidence of price rises above cost inflation without volume loss. (The long view had said: Every year the calls describe explicit, calibrated price increases (3–4% Apr-2022, a 1-Jan-2025 increase, three increases in H1 CY2026) alongside volume growth of 17% YoY in Q1FY27 and a steady mix shift to higher tonnages; margins rose from ~9% to >20% over the period, though management concedes some June-2026 increases meet resistance and are not fully recovered.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Q1FY27 crane/CE volumes grew 17.25% and revenue 22% against an industry only just normalising, and management claims leadership in sub-30-35t pick and carry where Chinese players are absent; but no explicit share percentages are given, and in heavy cranes the Chinese are described as gaining aggressively. (The long view had said: Across the 2022–2026 calls management repeatedly quantifies dominant and rising share in pick-and-carry and tower cranes, targets 70%, and has bought out two low-end competitors to consolidate the segment; share gain is backed by profit expansion (EBITDA 9.3% in Q1FY23 to 20.4% in Q1FY27), not bought with price.)
- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Promoter-family led, debt-free with dividend, capex 'completed as planned', and the Feb 2026 guidance of a flattish topline with improved margins was in fact delivered in the May 2026 FY26 print — while softness was disclosed candidly; against that, the agri division has been carried at ~1% margins without a stated remedy. (The long view had said: Stable family leadership (Sorab/Vyom Agarwal) with the same CFO since FY2022, prompt owning of shortfalls (backhoe 500cr FY24 target flagged as likely missed in the very call it was raised), delivery on the multi-year margin goal, conservative guidance discipline, and preponing MSME vendor payments at the cost of reported CFO.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: LIKELY FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: The four calls discuss only equipment unit sales, segment revenue and volumes; there is no discussion of AMC/service revenue share, attach rates, renewals, installed-base monetisation or customer advances as a funding source. (The long view had said: Service, spares and product support are named as one of the three pillars of competitive edge, implying an aftermarket stream off a large installed base, but across 18 calls management never quantifies AMC/spares revenue share, attach or renewal rates, or customer advances as a funding source.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Across all four calls, technology appears only as incremental, value-adding product upgrades — intelligent tower cranes, AI-assisted pick-and-carry cranes, telehandlers, aerial work platforms — plus a technology-access JV with KATO; no analyst asks about a disruptive platform shift and no part of ACE's installed base is described defensively as legacy. (The long view had said: The BS-III to BS-V / CEV-V emission transition materially reset the product economics and depressed the core Hydra crane volumes for roughly a year as retail buyers turned skeptical of electronic engines, showing regulation-driven technology change can hit the installed format; ACE's own answer is incremental — higher-tonnage models and licensing KATO's technology rather than developing heavy-crane technology itself.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: ACE sells capital equipment bought out of customers' capex budgets, and the last 12 months show exactly the swing described: FY26 revenue flat/down after a prebuying-inflated FY25, H1 softness from the CEV-V emission transition, extended monsoon and slow project mobilisation, then recovery in Q3/Q4 and a 19% YoY Q1FY27; management also flags a strong seasonal 40-45%/55-60% H1-H2 split and elevated, volatile steel input costs. Capacity utilisation of only ~60% after a completed capex programme is the classic expansion-into-a-soft-patch pattern. (The long view had said: ACE sells capital equipment bought out of customers' capex — management says ~50% of sales go indirectly to crane rental companies whose buying swings with sentiment, and the record shows violent swings: backhoe industry down 30-40%, EBITDA margin moving from 9.3% (Q1FY23) to 20.4% (Q1FY27), FY26 with no growth at all, and every call framing demand and margins around steel/commodity prices and infra capex cycles. Guidance is repeatedly withheld or hedged because the cycle is unreadable.)
- **New Entrants**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Chinese OEMs are a structurally different competitor entering on price plus financing, and management concedes they took 20-25% of the Indian tracked-excavator market in five years and have been 'very aggressive' in truck and crawler cranes for 5-8 years; ACE's response — a JV with KATO to compete in heavy cranes — is itself framed as building its own capability against them. Exposure is bounded because the core profit pool is untouched. (The long view had said: Asked directly about competitive intensity in FY27 and about Chinese dominance, management said intensity is unchanged and that international rivals already manufacture in India; ACE pre-empted the low-price attack by acquiring two small discount crane makers in 2022 and running them as separate cheaper brands, and its pick-and-carry share has kept climbing toward ~65-70%.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
