**Checks that improved:**

- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Weak → Good. Now: There is little halo risk to strip out — reported outcomes are modest (10.36% ROE, 2.78% GNPA) and management explicitly attributes NIM movement to the external repo cycle rather than claiming skill. | The long view had said: Headline records were repeatedly flattered by non-recurring or cycle items rather than operating skill — a Rs.80 crore tax-refund interest, an RBI-mandated AFS accounting change worth Rs.32.74 crore of PAT, and treasury write-backs.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Weak → Good. Now: A 38-year insider took over as MD and held one consistent strategy (RAM growth, CASA build, bulk-deposit reduction) across all four calls, accepting a weak H1 to deliver record aggregate business by Q4. | The long view had said: Three different CEOs across the period (interim Sekhar Rao → Srikrishnan, the first outside hire, who exited → Raghavendra Bhat) meant repeated strategy resets rather than one long-term plan executed tenaciously.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Bank licensing limits new entrants, but the calls describe industry-wide yield compression and costly bulk-deposit competition that forced the bank to walk away from business, indicating limited pricing rationality. | The long view had said: Licensing is a barrier but pricing behaviour is irrational — the bank itself cut headline home-loan rates and granted ROI concessions to defend a repricing book.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Entirely organic, with deliberate shrinkage of low-yield IBPC/corporate exposure (Rs.4,057cr to Rs.1,707cr), refusal of costly bulk deposits (6.6% to 4.2% of deposits) and accelerated provisioning to lift PCR — no M&A anywhere in the year. | The long view had said: Purely organic with prudent moves — accelerated provisioning, cutting bulk deposits from 5.3% to 4.2%, exiting low-yield opportunistic corporate/IBPC exposure — but the earlier decision to chase low-yield IBPC/corporate volume for optics undercuts the discipline claim.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: The bank is a price-taker in a fragmented market, forced to pass through repo cuts on 77% of its book while competing for deposits, and it chose to shrink corporate lending because pricing there was unattractive. | The long view had said: Indian banking is a fragmented, hyper-competitive market of PSBs, large private banks, SFBs and NBFCs where the customer holds the negotiating power and openly shops the bank's book.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Leadership was filled internally after nearly four decades of service, and the call rosters show a deep, named functional bench including a Chief Human Resources Officer who sits on investor calls. | The long view had said: Mixed: the bank had to go outside for a CEO for the first time in its history (a pipeline admission) but subsequently elevated internally and built out a deep functional bench including a dedicated CHRO.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: Management is deliberately re-matching customer types — exiting opportunistic large-corporate lending where it has no leverage and building granular retail/RAM assets and sub-Rs.3cr deposits. | The long view had said: The strategic reorientation toward retail/MSME does match the branch-trust benefit set, but the corporate book was built opportunistically on price rather than switching-cost lock-in and is now being unwound.
- **Candor** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: The Q4 call opens with a line-by-line scorecard of guidance versus actuals that openly reports the misses on advances, full-year NIM, ROE and restructured book alongside the beats. | The long view had said: The FY26 call opens with an explicit line-by-line guidance-versus-actual scorecard that owns the misses (advances Rs.83,340cr vs Rs.84–85,000cr guided; cost-to-income 56.34% vs 55–56%), and management admits when it lacks an answer.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: The bank is building accessibility through branch banking, a merchant payment app and MSME ecosystem tie-ups with business facilitators, but presents no evidence its reach or sales force is superior. | The long view had said: A 940-branch retail network with expanding regional loan-processing centres and direct customer access is the bank's clearest customer benefit.
- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Weak. Now: Despite the 'Your Family Bank' positioning, there is no pricing power — yield on advances fell from 9.28% to 8.78% over the year and margins sit below 3%. | The long view had said: There is real legacy brand goodwill ('Your Family Bank', a 100-year unbroken profit record) but it converts to no pricing power, as falling yields and NIM demonstrate.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Neutral → Weak. Now: Advances de-grew through H1 FY26 (Rs.74,267cr in June to Rs.73,644cr in September) with aggregate business down Q-o-Q, and the FY26 recovery still missed the bank's own advances guidance, so the year shows share loss then catch-up rather than capture. | The long view had said: Ample runway as a ~Rs.1.9 lakh crore small bank, and FY25 showed ~20% advances growth, but the book went backwards for two quarters in FY26 before recovering — no consistent share capture.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Weak. Now: The same product pipeline (pre-approved personal loans, surrogate-based lending, supply chain finance, merchant payment app) is announced as 'planned in the coming quarters' in Aug 2025 and still pending in Nov 2025 and Feb 2026, indicating low development throughput rather than consistent new-product yield. | The long view had said: A steady product/tech development pipeline exists (CBDC programmable payments, NFC QR, LOS revamp, SHG and agri products) but it is catch-up feature parity rather than proven high-efficiency innovation.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Good → Neutral. Now: The bank works through a branch-banking network and regional collection centres, but the calls give no branch-count expansion or reach data suggesting rivals cannot match it. | The long view had said: Its densest asset is physical reach — 940 branches plus 15 regional retail processing centres and government treasury tie-ups — meaningful within Karnataka though sub-scale against national rivals.

*Unchanged: Cyclical market growth; Technology advantage; Mini-monopolies; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Structural end-market growth; Asset turn; Out of limelight; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Security by obscurity; Assurance benefits.*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Unknown on the long view, Unknown on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Management repeatedly describes exiting large/mid-corporate advances that were 'low-yielding' and 'opportunistically deployed for better yields than Treasury' — i.e. it could only win that business at commoditised, near-Treasury pricing — and yield on advances slid to 8.78% while cost of deposits stayed near 5.5%. Growth is concentrated in gold, housing and vehicle loans, undifferentiated products where the borrower shops on rate. (The long view had said: Loans and deposits are undifferentiated and the bank has visibly no pricing power: yields on advances fell 9.95% → 9.52% → 8.71% as existing borrowers took cheaper competing quotes and the bank granted ROI concessions, then it explicitly repriced housing and MSME loans down to 'competitive' levels to restart growth — a commoditised offering defended by price rather than by niche or scale protection.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The four calls document deliberate de-concentration on both sides of the balance sheet: the IBPC/food-credit book was cut from INR4,057 crores to INR1,707 crores, corporate advances degrew ~12% year-on-year, and funding was granularised with bulk deposits falling from 6.6% to 4.2% of total deposits while retail term deposits (<INR3 crore) grew to INR67,648 crores. No single named customer, counterparty or channel is cited as driving results. (The long view had said: Deposit and retail franchise is granular across 940 branches and bulk deposits were cut to 4.2% of total, but the asset side shows real single-name sensitivity — Q3FY26 GNPA deterioration was attributed to one borrower — plus heavy geographic anchoring in Karnataka.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Cyclicality (WATCH); Technological Innovation (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Unknown on the long view (none of the stress checks could be computed.) vs Unknown on the one-year view (none of the stress checks could be computed.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 8 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
