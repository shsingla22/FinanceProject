### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 2 weakened, 8 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Toll Roads**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: MMA is a small-dose octane additive for gasoline blenders, which superficially fits the 'magic ingredient' shape, but every call shows the opposite of toll-road economics: pricing is contested by Chinese and domestic players, tariffs are absorbed by Aarti, and agro/fluoro prices are set by Chinese dumping rather than by conversion lock-in. (The long view had said: Some toll-road traits are real — critical intermediates, customer qualification cycles that slow switching, and integrated go-to supplier status in benzene/toluene chains — but the defining test fails: 2.5 years of Chinese dumping forced price deflation across the whole portfolio, so customers clearly do negotiate and switch.)
- **Forward Integrators**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Management uses 'forward integration' and 'moving closer to the customer' in the value-chain sense (PEDA downstream of its own Ethylation, the Superform JV), not the pattern's sense — there are no owned stores, franchise royalties or D2C economics anywhere in the calls. (The long view had said: Directionally the company is moving downstream toward the customer — application-led Advanced Materials pivot, PEDA/downstream integration, and the Superform and RESL JVs — but there are no stores, franchises, royalties or D2C economics; this is value-chain integration, not the retail/franchise version of the pattern.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 3 worsened, 3 unchanged. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **New Entrants**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Competition is framed defensively in three of four calls — MMA pricing pressured by 'both China and domestic Indian players' despite Aarti's first-mover position, and MPDA underperforming on 'heavy competition from China' — but these are low-cost capacity additions in existing formats rather than a structurally new competitive model resetting the industry. (The long view had said: Chinese overcapacity acts as a structurally different low-cost entrant that reset industry economics for 2-2.5 years — management says pricing pressure is 'across the board', forced them onto Tier-2/Tier-3 customers to move volume, and they admit pushing utilisation 'even at the expense of a slight compromise on the margins'.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: As a B2B intermediates maker Aarti shows no eroding demand base; the end-preference shifts named in the calls run in its favour — PDCB/polymer volumes growing on EV-driven PPS demand — and it is investing ahead of the sustainability shift via the Re Aarti circularity JV. (The long view had said: The largest single application, MMA (~40% of revenue), is a gasoline blending/refinery-optimisation product whose demand base is tied to internal-combustion fuel consumption, while the company simultaneously cites EV adoption as the growth driver for its PDCB/PPS chain — the same transition cuts both ways, and management has not addressed the gasoline side.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: All four calls tie revenue and margins to commodity input and refining spreads — benzene/aniline fell 15-20% in Q1 FY26 causing Rs 30 crore inventory valuation losses and a 16% QoQ revenue decline, then the same feedstocks rose over 60% by Q4 FY26, inflating working capital and net debt; MMA economics are explicitly indexed to gasoline-naphtha cracks, and management itself flags 'ongoing volatility in the refining product margin'. (The long view had said: Across all 15 calls results swing with commodity feedstock and end-market cycles — benzene/aniline/toluene/methanol up 60% in Q4 FY26, EBITDA guidance repeatedly withdrawn or missed (Rs 1,100cr FY23 guide, refusal to commit in FY25), and ~40% of revenue now sits in an energy/MMA business explicitly priced off gasoline-naphtha cracks; capex ran Rs 1,500-1,800cr/yr in the boom and is being cut to Rs 700-800cr for FY27 after returns disappointed.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Cheaper substitutes are visibly compressing the premium across the portfolio: agrochemical and pharma pricing 'subdued due to persistent dumping by China', high-value fluoro product margins hit by aggressive Chinese pricing, and MPDA underperforming — management concedes contribution margins have converged across end applications, so profit now depends on operating leverage rather than product premium. (The long view had said: A large part of the portfolio is bulk isomer-level chemistry (NCB, DCB, PDA, MPDA) with no brand protection, where cheaper Chinese material takes share directly — MPDA 'continues to underperform due to heavy competition from China' and management concedes margin recovery only happens in pockets where global utilisation is tight.)
- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Every call in the window is dominated by government policy as the swing factor: a 25% US tariff hit the 15-20% of revenue sold into the US and cut polymer volumes, the India-US trade deal then restored them, and management explicitly pins expected margin recovery on China's 'anti-involution' policy and the India-EU FTA — plus a Rs 15 crore exceptional charge from India's new labour code with more provisions pending government notification. (The long view had said: Pricing and volumes are repeatedly reset by policy the company does not control — US tariffs on MMA/PDCB/NCB (50%+ to 18%), the India-US trade deal, India-EU FTA, China's VAT-rebate removal under anti-involution, and a Rs 15cr one-time labour-code charge — though no revenue is subsidy- or tender-dependent and there is no fixed-infrastructure/regulated-tariff exposure.)

*Unchanged: Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
