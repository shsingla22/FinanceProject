### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 2 worsened, 3 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: A large part of the portfolio is bulk intermediates where the customer's choice is price-led: management repeatedly reports volumes holding while price is conceded to Chinese and domestic competitors, and states that contribution margins have converged across end applications so mix no longer differentiates profitability — the profit lever is operating leverage and cost, not premium. The stated pivot away from bulk is an admission of the exposure. (The long view had said: A large part of the portfolio is bulk isomer-level chemistry where the buyer's decision is price, and cheaper Chinese equivalents have compressed margins for years despite stable volumes — MPDA 'continues to underperform due to heavy competition from China', PDA needs a 'structural long-term solution', and Aarti has had to fall back on Tier-2/Tier-3 customers with weaker pricing power. Management's own pivot 'from bulk products toward high-value, application-led solutions' is an admission the commodity layer is not defensible.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Where end-demand preferences are shifting, the portfolio is on the right side of them: PDCB/polymer volumes are growing on PPS demand from EVs, and the circularity JV (Re Aarti) targets sustainable chemistry. No volume decline in the window is attributed to changing end-user habits. (The long view had said: The calls themselves surface both sides of an energy-transition shift: roughly 40% of revenue is a gasoline fuel-additive application whose demand depends on refinery blending economics, while the polymer chain (PDCB/PPS) is growing specifically on EV adoption. The near-term commentary is all cyclical, not habit-driven, so the erosion is prospective rather than visible in volumes today.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: All four FY26 calls describe results as driven by commodity price swings rather than volume: Q1 saw benzene/aniline fall 15-20% causing Rs 30 crore inventory valuation losses and a 16% QoQ revenue decline, while Q4 saw the same inputs rise over 60%; the largest segment (energy/MMA, ~40% of revenue) is explicitly priced off gasoline-naphtha crack spreads. Revenue swung -16%, +21%, +11% QoQ within one year, and management itself flags spread volatility as an ongoing uncertainty. (The long view had said: Across all four excerpts the business is described in cycle/commodity terms — benzene, aniline, toluene, methanol prices moving 'over 60%', margins swinging with gasoline-naphtha cracks, and an energy application that is now ~40% of revenue and explicitly 'seasonal' and volatile; this sat alongside a Rs 3,000 crore FY24-25 capex programme and Rs 1,500-1,800 crore annual spend, i.e. expansion at the very point margins were compressing, with management later admitting it 'pushed volumes sometimes even at the expense of a slight compromise on the margins'.)
- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Chinese overcapacity operating as a structurally lower-cost entrant is cited as a margin problem in every call — fluoro products, agrochemicals, MPDA and MMA all show volume held but price conceded. Management's answer is cost optimisation and market expansion rather than pricing power, and it is openly hoping an external policy shift (China's 'anti-involution') will restore rationality. (The long view had said: Chinese producers with structurally different cost economics reset pricing across Aarti's portfolio for 2-2.5 years — management calls the pressure 'across the board', worst in agrochemicals, and admits 'it's even nothing to do specific with Aarti, the broader chemical industry is struggling'. This is incumbent overcapacity rather than a new business format, and by FY26 the anti-involution policy is reversing some of it.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
