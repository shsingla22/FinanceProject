### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

**Checks that improved:**

- **Candor** (Management): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Management proactively normalises comparatives for one-off PLI recognition, separates out an INR150 crores fair-valuation gain and an INR41 crores exceptional item, and names its problems plainly — magnet shortages, labour and gas availability, Europe not growing. | The long view had said: Management is forthright about external problems (Red Sea, labour shortages, commodity inflation, Bangladesh, April supply disruption) but repeatedly refuses to quantify EV investment, EV profitability or the employee-cost split when pressed, and rarely concedes an internal mistake.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Growth is predominantly volume-led (Q1 FY27 volumes +28% vs revenue +38%), with mix/premiumisation contributing the gap, and margin gains of only 30-60bps suggest pricing power is real but limited — GST cuts were fully passed through rather than retained. | The long view had said: Growth is predominantly volume-led — FY26 volumes +24% against revenue +30%, leaving only modest price/mix contribution, and pricing power is used defensively, recovering only about a third of commodity inflation.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing conduct looks rational with no discount war mentioned and stable promoter ownership, but the EV transition has visibly lowered entry barriers — TVS is fighting for VAHAN share in newly contested EV categories. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±3.1 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Scale, distribution and homologation create real barriers in ICE, but EV has drawn new entrants and a major rival has resorted to a discounting spree, so pricing rationality is not assured across the industry.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: TVS is a large-cap, heavily covered listed company running quarterly calls with Goldman Sachs and other institutions on the line — the antithesis of an ignored niche. | The long view had said: This is a heavily covered large-cap: quarterly calls feature Goldman Sachs, UBS, Nomura and Axis Mutual Fund, the antithesis of an ignored niche.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: Accessibility is a genuine edge — captive TVS Credit reaching nearly 62,000 touchpoints and 2.6 crore customers, with finance penetration explicitly cited as the enabler of EV adoption. | The long view had said: Accessibility is a deliberate build — EV rollout from urban to semi-urban to rural through the existing dealer network, charging tie-ups with Tata Power, CESL and Jio-BP, and TVS Credit's ~62,000 touch points putting finance at the point of sale.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Good → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹15 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹15 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±3.1 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Good → Excellent. Now: Distribution is repeatedly named as the driver of results — strengthened international channels behind 33% export growth across Africa, LatAm and Asia, layered on a captive finance network of ~62,000 rural and semi-urban touchpoints. | The long view had said: Distribution is a genuine and deepening strength — export growth explicitly attributed to distributor strengthening across Africa, Asia and LATAM, plus finance reach at ~62,000 touch points — though domestically TVS's network still trails the market leader's.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Growth is organic with no new acquisitions in the window, balance-sheet discipline is externally validated by the CARE upgrade to AAA, and the captive NBFC is run on stated risk-calibrated underwriting with improving GNPA. | The long view had said: Growth has been overwhelmingly organic with tight working-capital discipline (under 30 days dealer stock, cash-and-carry), counter-cyclical investment in EV and software through the 2022-23 downturn, no large acquisitions, and balance-sheet strength confirmed by the CARE upgrade to AAA.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Record headline numbers are substantially aided by exogenous tailwinds — GST 2.0, a 125bps repo cut, good monsoon — and management attributes them to its own execution, though the consistent outperformance versus industry growth is genuine skill that survives the discount. | The long view had said: The record results are supported by a repeatable, verifiable mechanism — consistent outperformance versus stated industry growth rates over 16 quarters and 270bps of margin expansion built on mix and cost reduction — rather than a single cyclical tailwind, though the relentlessly upbeat commentary warrants discounting.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: Two-wheeler buyers face negligible switching costs and management competes on features and price points rather than lock-in; the only captive element is TVS Credit financing, which is a convenience not a switching barrier. | The long view had said: Two-wheeler buyers face negligible switching costs, but there is partial captivity through the spare-parts aftermarket (INR930 crores a quarter) and TVS Credit financing roughly 44% of the company's own two-wheeler sales.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. Now: Capex is clearly growth-directed — capacity expansion to meet unmet export demand plus new Norton production lines at Hosur and Solihull — funded by rising operating free cash flow, though no explicit incremental ROIC disclosure. | The long view had said: Capex is clearly growth-directed — a 1.5 million-unit two-wheeler capacity addition plus EV 2W (40k→50k+/month) and 3W (20k→30k) expansions — alongside rising margins (10.2% in Q2FY23 to 13.1% in Q4FY26) and free cash flow up 47% to INR3,805 crores, implying high incremental returns.
- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Weak. Now: Norton is the opposite of the ideal bolt-on — a distressed heritage brand bought in 2021 that has absorbed five years of investment with first products only now reaching market and no evidence yet of margin contribution. | The long view had said: Deals are small and complementary (40% of Nkars pre-owned vehicles; Hyundai JV for EV 3W) but Norton was a distressed brand bought in 2021 that took five years and continuous investment before its first products shipped — the opposite of an already-strong bolt-on, though group OPM still improved.
- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Two structural legs are visible — EV penetration rising (3-wheeler EV already ~32%) and emerging-market motorisation in Africa/LatAm — though the core Indian ICE 2-wheeler market itself is mature and cyclical. | The long view had said: Multiple secular drivers are quantified and compounding: EV two-wheeler penetration rising from 6.2% to over 10.6% monthly, EV three-wheeler penetration crossing 40%, and scooterisation moving from 38% toward 40%+ of the domestic market.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Norton demonstrates tenacity — five years of sustained investment before first product, now in production at both Solihull and Hosur — as does the 13-year HLX export build that just crossed 5 million units. | The long view had said: The 2022 promise of a full 5-25kW EV portfolio, in-house capability and international expansion was pursued through loss-making early years and delivered — EV volumes from 16,000 a quarter to 130,000, plus a patient five-year Norton rebuild — with management repeatedly defending spend that analysts questioned.
- **Advertisement and promotion** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. Now: Management explicitly flags stepped-up brand-building investment alongside evidence that the brands convert (6.5 million Apache riders, HLX crossing 5 million with the last million in one year), but no spend quantum or efficiency data is given. | The long view had said: Management explicitly treats A&P as investment rather than cost and the payback shows in brand milestones — Jupiter 5 million, HLX 5 million (last million in one year), iQube 1 million — plus the new premium Paddock channel and LATAM brand-building.

*Unchanged: Geographical expansion; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Cost of working capital; Technology advantage; Intangible benefits; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Out of limelight; Gain in market share; Asset turn; Assurance benefits; Network effects; Return on capital (headline); Research and development; Cyclical market growth; Share donators; Mini-monopolies.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Q4 FY26 introduces a battery-as-a-service subscription and the TVS Connect Fleet platform alongside a large captive financing book (TVS Credit, INR 32,053 crores, 2.6 crore customers), but management never discusses attach rates, renewal rates, AMC revenue share or deferred revenue as a funding source — so the recurring layer is nascent rather than proven. (The long view had said: Spare-parts revenue is disclosed every call and grows steadily (INR670cr in Q2FY23 to INR930cr in Q2FY25), dealer dispatches are cash-and-carry with under 30 days stock (customer-funded working capital), and BaaS battery subscription was launched in FY26 — but management never discusses attach rates, AMC share, renewals or deferred revenue as a funding source.)

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Q4 FY26 shows only conditional, input-cost-driven pricing ('wherever we are able'), and in Oct 2025 the company 'passed on the full benefit of GST rate reduction to our customers across the right portfolio' — i.e. prices fell; margin gains (12.7%→13.1%) come from premiumisation, mix and cost work rather than demonstrated above-inflation price rises. (The long view had said: Price increases are taken repeatedly and explicitly without volume loss — ~1% in Q2FY23, and in FY26 roughly a third of a 3-5% commodity shock was offset by price while volumes grew 24-28%; Jupiter 110 ran a waiting list in Oct 2024 and in Jul 2026 TVS gained share against a discounting scooter rival while holding a premium.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Named launches land every quarter with commercial traction (Orbiter V1/V2, Ntorq 150, Apache RTX, King Kargo HD EV and CNG, four Norton models into production at Solihull and Hosur), but no R&D spend figure, share-of-industry-R&D, or pipeline economics are disclosed in the window, so the funding-flywheel half of the pattern is unevidenced. (The long view had said: Named new products arrive every single year with visible revenue impact (Ronin and iQube 2022, Jupiter 110 and Apache RTR 310 in 2024, Orbiter, iQube S, King Kargo CNG and the Norton Manx/Atlas range in 2026), funded by an openly higher-than-peer employee and depreciation load for in-house software, electronics and digital capability — ~500 specialists disclosed in Oct 2024.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Stressed on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **New Entrants**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: EV-native entrants reset two-wheeler economics, yet across four calls management never discusses competitors defensively or frames discounting as a competitive response; instead it reports growing well ahead of industry in every segment while building the new format itself. (The long view had said: EV-native entrants reset the two-wheeler economics during this window, and by Jul 2026 an analyst asks directly about a major rival on a 'discounting spree' in scooters; in Oct 2024 analysts probed whether Raider needed repositioning 'in light of recent competitor events'. TVS answers from strength but the competitive structure of the segment has clearly changed.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The relevant preference migration — buyers moving from ICE to electric and toward premium, connected products — is one the portfolio has already pivoted into, with EV volumes and premium launches growing faster than the base business rather than eroding it. (The long view had said: The demand mix is migrating structurally — scooters from ~38% toward 40%+ of the market, EV penetration from ~3% to 10.6%, premium/super-premium growing while management concedes 'the challenge will continue in the economy category' where inflation-hit buyers sit. The shift is real, but TVS's portfolio is positioned on the winning side of it.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Supplier concentration is the live channel — rare-earth magnet availability visibly capped EV volumes in Q2 FY26 (EV grew only 7%), and Q4 FY26 added labour, gas and on-time raw-material supply disruptions; customer and distribution concentration, by contrast, is negligible. (The long view had said: Across four years no single customer, licensor or distributor is named as driving results; revenue is spread over domestic retail, Africa/Asia/LATAM exports (33% growth, 4.68 lakh units in Q1FY27) and ~62,000 TVS Credit touchpoints. The only concentration flavour is supplier-side wobbles (semiconductors in 2022, Tier-2 labour/gas in 2026), which were episodic and multi-vendor, not single-source.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Good Enough Goods (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Technological Innovation (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Stressed on the long view (2 of the 3 stress checks are flagged — multiple warning lights at once.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
