# Jubilant Foodworks Ltd. (JUBLFOOD) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSUMER GOODS*

Jubilant FoodWorks runs quick-service restaurant chains, selling pizza, fried chicken, doughnuts, coffee and Chinese-style food to everyday consumers who order for delivery, dine-in or takeaway. It operates franchised international brands — chiefly Domino's Pizza — under master-franchise rights in India, Turkey, Sri Lanka and Bangladesh, plus its own brands, and makes money mainly from food sold at company-owned stores, supported by its own ordering app, delivery riders and in-house 'food tech' commissary factories that supply the stores. In Turkey, where roughly 88% of stores are run by franchisees, it earns instead by supplying and servicing those franchise partners, so management reports both 'system sales' (all end-customer sales including franchise stores) and its own revenue. As of the latest call it operates close to 3,600 stores across five brands and six countries (~2,530 of them in India), with consolidated quarterly revenue of about Rs. 24.4 billion, EBITDA margin around 20.5%, and growth driven by opening 230–250 new stores a year toward a stated goal of a 5,000-plus store business.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Domino's India (India segment)** — The core business by far — around 2,530 pizza stores across 400-plus Indian cities, delivering roughly Rs. 18 billion of the group's Rs. 24.4 billion quarterly revenue, with delivery the main and fastest-growing channel and dine-in/takeaway the acknowledged weak spot.
- **Turkey business (DP Eurasia) — Domino's Turkey** — About 700-plus Domino's stores in Turkey, overwhelmingly franchisee-run and therefore 'asset-lite'; acquired and consolidated from FY25, it now runs at high margins, generates steady cash and pays dividends back to the parent to service the debt raised to buy it.
- **COFFY (Turkey)** — The group's coffee-shop chain in Turkey, which crossed 100 stores in 2024 and keeps adding stores (18 in one recent quarter); still small in sales terms versus Domino's Turkey but described as a key growth brand.
- **Popeyes (India)** — The fried-chicken chain, grown from a Bangalore launch to 73 stores, now posting high double-digit like-for-like growth and called out by management as an emerging new growth engine, though still loss-making enough to drag group margins.
- **Dunkin' (India)** — The doughnut-and-beverage chain, a small part of the portfolio where management is adding a handful of stores and pushing beverages like bubble tea to get it to positive restaurant-level profitability.
- **Hong's Kitchen (India)** — The company's own Chinese-food QSR brand, described as India's first, at roughly 33 stores with entry-price items around Rs. 99 — the smallest brand and, with Popeyes and Dunkin', part of the 'new business' drag on profits.
- **Sri Lanka and Bangladesh operations** — Smaller overseas Domino's markets that management reports as delivering high double-digit top-line growth and improving profitability, but which are not broken out with meaningful revenue figures on these calls.
- **Supply chain / 'food tech' factories** — In-house commissary plants running a complex multi-temperature forward and reverse supply chain that make and distribute dough and ingredients to the store network — not sold separately, but described as a core cost and quality advantage.
- **App, data and advertising** — The own-brand ordering apps and loyalty programme (over 20% year-on-year growth in monthly transacting users) that cut reliance on third-party platforms, plus a small but new advertising income stream from post-order page ads, which crossed 10 active clients in one recent quarter.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Jubilant Foodworks earns its four-star, 66-out-of-100 "Strong" rating on the back of an unusually well-built delivery machine. Its strongest single trait is distribution: a commissary-backed, multi-temperature supply chain feeding roughly 3,600 stores across six countries, with an owned delivery fleet and around 2,530 India stores in 400-plus cities reaching sub-20-minute service and tier-3/4 towns. That same asset base is exactly what makes three multibagger patterns fit strongly. Forward Integrators fits because the company makes, delivers and sells through its own commissaries, its own stores and its own app, with delivery at 76% of the mix. Market Share Gainers fits because the network grew from 1,701 Domino's India stores in 2022 to about 2,530 in 2026, with 1,000 more planned in three years and roughly two-thirds of the pizza category. Innovation Dominance fits because every call names launches with stated commercial impact. The economics behind it are genuinely good: about 26 paise of operating cash per rupee of capital, roughly 72 of every 100 rupees of sales left after direct input costs, and customers paying 86 days before suppliers must be, so the customer effectively funds the business.

The catch is that several of these strengths are the very same fact as the risks. The pattern screen flags Low Price Plus as a likely fit — eight straight quarters with no price rise while absorbing record inflation, free delivery, minimum order value cut from Rs.149 to Rs.99, a Rs.99 lunch thali and Rs.49-79 price points aimed at idli and dosa occasions. Read the other way, that same behaviour is why Pricing Power scores as no fit at all and why New Entrants is the one risk rated elevated: aggregator and quick-commerce platforms have repeatedly reset the economics, and the company's answer has been to spend margin rather than raise prices. Innovation Dominance and Fashion Risk are likewise two readings of one habit — growth leaning on a rolling sequence of trending launches, with an analyst directly raising category fatigue. The heavy fixed-cost density that makes 20-minute delivery possible is what makes input-cost swings bite: cheese at ten-year highs took margin to 24.3% (down 170 basis points) in FY23 and to a trough near 19.3% in FY25. And Brand Strength is only a partial fit for the same reason Stakeholder Concentration is on watch — every brand it runs, Domino's, Popeyes and Dunkin', is licensed from someone else, and Domino's India drives the overwhelming share of results.

The composite score reflects that split: 68 for business quality, 78 for pattern fit, but only 50 for risk safety, with overall resilience judged stressed. Within quality, the customer-facing and capital-allocation areas score well — capex of Rs 700-850 crore a year is overwhelmingly for new stores, commissaries and technology, with cost per store cut about 20% for three straight years — while growth (0.5) and industry structure (-0.17) drag. Sales have compounded at roughly 15% a year but lumpily, and the check on being an overlooked business scores -2 because this is a heavily covered large-cap tracked quarterly by multiple brokers in a capital-magnet category. Six of eight risk channels sit at watch or worse; only technology disruption and government dependency are low, the latter because revenue is entirely consumer-funded with pricing set by the company. The honest picture is a business with a real, hard-to-copy delivery moat and a genuinely cash-generative model, currently paying for that moat by holding prices down against better-funded platform competition, with the unsolved dine-in decline — four consecutive quarters of falling dine-in orders and management still naming it a problem in May 2026 — sitting unresolved alongside it.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

*"A new player changes the rules"*

**What the calls show:** Aggregator/quick-commerce platforms have repeatedly reset the economics and JFL has responded with margin investment: two years of free delivery from FY25, eight straight quarters of no price increase, and in FY26 cutting minimum order value from Rs.149 to Rs.99 explicitly to match Swiggy/Zomato — with management conceding average ticket size fell as a result and Q4FY26 LFL collapsing to 0.2%.

## What to watch

- Whether the price freeze ever ends — the Low Price Plus pattern turns into a permanent margin tax if the Pricing Power check stays at no fit and only ~120bps of increase is achievable.
- Aggregator and quick-commerce pressure, the sole elevated risk (New Entrants) — watch whether free delivery and the Rs.99 minimum order value stay permanent costs or get rolled back.
- Margin recovery through the input-cost cycle (Cyclicality risk) — EBITDA troughed near 19.3% in FY25 after cheese hit ten-year highs; a repeat would test the fixed-cost density behind the Cost to Replicate barrier.
- The unsolved dine-in decline (Shifting Consumer Preferences) — four straight quarters of falling dine-in orders and delivery at 76%; the smaller 600-700 sq ft store format is the stated fix and needs to prove out.
- Diversification away from one licensed brand (Stakeholder Concentration, and the partial Brand Strength fit) — whether Popeyes, Dunkin', COFFY and Turkey grow enough to matter against Domino's India.
