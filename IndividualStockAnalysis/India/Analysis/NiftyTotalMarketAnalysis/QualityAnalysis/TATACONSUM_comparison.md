### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 5 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No single customer, licensor or supplier is named as driving results in any of the four calls; instead the calls document a deliberate broadening of the route to market — a full national go-to-market rebuild with 270 distributors transitioned and 160 net new added, plus three newly incubated channels (food services INR170cr ARR, vending INR100cr, pharmacy INR30cr across 42,000 outlets). (The long view had said: The international tea business sells through a handful of large retail chains, which delayed pass-through of price increases in FY23, and the Middle East shipping disruption via Dubai transshipment hit a whole quarter's exports — but the India base has been deliberately widened from 1.3m direct outlets toward 1.5m plus new channels.)

**That worsened:**

- **Government Dependency**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Policy and duty changes repeatedly moved results in-window: the GST rate transition caused an end-of-Q2 FY26 demand hiccup in Capital Foods, Organic India and Soulfull; US tariffs hit the ~20% international part of Capital Foods in Q3 FY26; Brazil and Venezuela actions blipped coffee costs; and an LPG shortage hit south-India tea shops in Q1 FY27. None of this is regulated pricing or government-contract revenue, so exposure is real but bounded. (The long view had said: No revenue is regulator-, tariff- or subsidy-set; government touchpoints raised on calls are peripheral and mostly incidental — 5% GST on packaged staples (framed as a benefit versus unorganised players), US export tariffs that had largely cycled through by Q1FY27, and an LPG shortage hitting roadside tea shops.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The core demand base is drifting: India tea volumes grew only 2-4% in the last two quarters, UK strategy is explicitly to hold share in declining black tea while growing fruit, herbal and specialty, and consumption is migrating online. Management is pivoting hard and successfully into the new preferences — matcha, RTD green tea with L-Carnitine, Kombucha Zero, Slimcare, Copper+, Organic India — with growth businesses reaching 36% of India revenue and growing 47% in Q1 FY27. (The long view had said: Rather than eroding, the demand base is being actively repositioned toward where preferences are moving — fortified and low-sodium salt, millets/ragi atta, organic (Organic India), plant protein, cold-pressed oils and Kombucha — with growth businesses scaling from ~20% to 36% of the India business and growing 47% year-on-year; volume softness is attributed to heat waves, not changing habits.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 1 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
