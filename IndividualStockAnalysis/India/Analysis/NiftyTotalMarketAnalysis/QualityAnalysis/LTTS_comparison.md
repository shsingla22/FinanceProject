### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 2 weakened, 4 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Every call names new platforms with revenue impact and rising disclosed IP — 1,600+ patents and 216 AI patents in Q2 FY26 rising to 1,757 and 244 by Q1 FY27, with PLxAI, Qguard.ai, AiNexus, AgenticIQ and Ainfonix launched and monetised, plus Munich EI Centre and Anthropic/Databricks/NVIDIA/MIT partnerships; management explicitly credits this for margin mix and deal wins. (The long view had said: Innovation cadence is genuine and disclosed every quarter — 25 patents/quarter in FY23 building to 1,757 cumulative filings by FY27 (244 in AI), with named launches each year (AnnotAI, AiCE/AiCH, SDx framework, AgenticIQ, Ainfonix, PharmaWiseAI) and 1,033 patents co-authored with clients. But R&D spend is never quantified, gross margins are services-level, and the innovation has not yet converted into pricing power or accelerating growth.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Q2 FY26 disclosed that licence revenue from its AI platforms is only ~1% of trailing-12-month revenue with a medium-term goal of 5%, and the business is otherwise multi-year services annuity (dedicated offshore centres, $50Mn+ run-rate accounts) rather than product-upfront-plus-recurring; no attach rates, renewal rates or customer-advance funding are discussed. (The long view had said: Revenue is project/T&M engineering services (T&M mix 73% in Q1FY23, still 64.9% in Q1FY27) with no license fees, AMC, attach rates or renewal-rate disclosure; working capital is strongly positive — combined DSO ran 114-125 days in FY25 and 77-83 days even after improvement — so customers do not fund the business.)

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: L&T parentage, claimed #1 position in Industrial and a top-2 SDV analyst rating, plus MIT Media Lab/NVIDIA/Anthropic association, give credibility that wins large deals; but there is no consumer-style heritage, A&P spend or premium-pricing evidence in these calls. (The long view had said: L&T parentage plus third-party validation (ISG leader FY23, Everest leader FY25) and repeated empanelments with aerospace majors and hyperscalers give real credentialing, and MIT Media Lab / NVIDIA / AWS / Google / Anthropic partnerships extend it — but there is no premium pricing or A&P-driven brand economics, and revenue growth decelerated to low single digits by FY26.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management claims share gains in US automotive/SDV and shows FY26 large-deal TCV of $855Mn, up 40% year-on-year, with mix shifting to 80% OEM revenue; but no explicit share percentages versus named competitors, and headline revenue growth of 5% (8.3% continuing) with two sequentially declining quarters undercuts persistent share capture. (The long view had said: Management cites 12.4% five-year CAGR versus 8% industry (Zinnov) and specific consolidation wins ('gain market share over competition' from a European Tier-1, share gain in US SDV), but the trajectory weakened materially — guidance fell from 13.5-15.5% (FY23) to 8-10% (FY25), the $1.5bn aspiration was repeatedly deferred, and FY26 continuing revenue grew 8.3% with Q4 flat year-on-year.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (QUANT SIGNAL); Forward Integrators (QUANT SIGNAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 5 eased, 0 worsened, 2 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Across the four calls revenue swings with clients' R&D/capex budgets — Auto 'program pauses and muted decision-making' in Oct 2025, furlough-driven weakness in Q3, Europe deferring new-model investment — and USD CC growth fell from 10.4% year-on-year to 1.9% year-on-year within the window; Plant Engineering demand is explicitly tied to customer greenfield/brownfield capex. Bounded by a large annuity-like base of sustenance/modernization work and multi-year large deals ramping. (The long view had said: Demand is explicitly tied to customer capex and R&D budget cycles — Plant Engineering growth 'led by new capex projects', Industrial Products swinging on customer inventory gluts, Auto swinging on EV programme spend, and Industrial now riding AI data-centre capex — and the damage shows in the numbers across the five years: USD growth decelerated from 23.4% year-on-year (Q1FY23) to 0.3% year-on-year (Q4FY26) and EBIT margin slid from 18.3% to 14.1% FY26 before recovering to 15.7%.)
- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: LTTS sells engineering effort into an industry being reshaped by GenAI/Agentic AI, and management spent the whole year restructuring around it — Lakshya 31, an 'AI-first delivery model', divesting SWC and exiting non-strategic businesses to pivot to Engineering Intelligence — while clients push for shorter, cheaper development cycles, which is where effort-based pricing gets squeezed. No evidence in the calls of the installed base being displaced by a rival platform, and the innovation cadence is continuous rather than a bet-the-company shift. (The long view had said: The core people-per-hour ER&D model sits directly in the path of GenAI: management concedes 'automation' has broken the headcount-to-revenue link, headcount has been flat (~23,600 to 23,845) since FY25 while the whole strategy has been rebuilt around 'Engineering Intelligence', AI-led delivery, and 'nonlinearity of revenue' — a large-scale platform shift that resets the industry's pricing basis, not a small value-adding innovation.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: ER&D services face persistent commoditisation pressure that management repeatedly acknowledges — European clients prioritising cost optimisation and shifting to low-cost countries, vendor consolidation on price, and offshore/high-value centre structures — and the whole Lakshya 31 response is to abandon commoditised revenue for high-end work, which is itself a defence of the premium. (The long view had said: Core engineering services are substitutable and priced against cheaper offshore capacity — clients repeatedly frame spend as 'cost optimization', 'cheaper better faster' India delivery and vendor consolidation, and the margin record bears it out: EBIT fell from 18.6% (Q4FY22) to 14.1% (FY26), with the Tech segment at 11.5-12.6% versus Sustainability's 29%, forcing a deliberate exit from low-margin, non-strategic work.)
- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Every named engagement across the four calls is with private-sector buyers — global OEMs, energy majors, CPG/beverage companies, Emerson, hyperscalers, medtech and pharma firms — with no pricing, tariff or subsidy set by a regulator, and the one policy/public-infrastructure-adjacent unit (Smart World & Communication) was classified as discontinued and divested in FY26. (The long view had said: The SWC (Smart World & Communication) business carried direct government exposure — a Maharashtra government cyber-security deal, collections delayed by general elections, and chronic H1/H2 seasonality — and secondary policy exposure runs through EV/renewables subsidy-driven demand and oil & gas clients; management resolved this by classifying SWC as discontinued and divesting it in March 2026.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The main end-demand migration touching LTTS is the vehicle shift to EV/software-defined architectures, and the calls show the portfolio already pivoted onto that side of the shift — proprietary EV platform iDrive, a top-2 SDV rating, and North America SDV share gains — rather than a demand base eroding under it. (The long view had said: The underlying technology preference of end-consumers shifted under the company's investment: LTTS built capability around EV, and management admits on the Jul-2026 call that the EV strategy 'didn't play out as the way' as demand moved to hybridisation, while EV spend earlier gave way to SDV — the demand base moved even though execution held.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 8 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
