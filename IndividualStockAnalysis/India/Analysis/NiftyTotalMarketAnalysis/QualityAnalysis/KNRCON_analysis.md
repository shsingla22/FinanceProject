# KNR Constructions (KNRCON) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

KNR Constructions (KNRCON) is a Hyderabad-headquartered civil construction contractor that builds infrastructure — mainly highways and roads, plus irrigation works, water pipeline schemes and, more recently, mining development — for government customers such as NHAI/MoRTH and various state governments, with a small share from private clients. It earns money in two ways: as a plain construction contractor paid a fixed contract price for building the asset (EPC), and by winning Hybrid Annuity Model (HAM) road concessions where it puts its own equity into a project company (SPV), builds the road itself, and then earns construction revenue plus annuity/toll cash flows, and later sells the completed SPV — for example the December 2025 agreement to sell four HAM SPVs to Indus Infra Trust for about INR 1,543 crores against roughly INR 567 crores invested. Revenue visibility comes from an order book that stood at about INR 8,586 crores in mid-2022, dipped to INR 4,922 crores by mid-2024, and recovered to about INR 11,903 crores by March 2026 (including newly won HAM projects), typically executable over roughly 2-3.5 years. Its work is concentrated in southern and central India — Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Kerala — with one toll road in Bihar and stated ambitions in Maharashtra and the northeast.

**The parts of the business:**

- **EPC road projects** — Straight construction contracts where a government body pays KNR to build or widen roads, flyovers and bridges (e.g. a INR 319 crore iconic bridge for Musi Riverfront Development Corporation, a INR 50 crore Hyderabad flyover); roads were about 29% of the order book in December 2025 and 49% by March 2026.
- **HAM (Hybrid Annuity Model) road projects** — Road concessions won by KNR and housed in its own subsidiary SPVs, which then award the construction to KNR — so-called 'captive' order book (44% in 2022, 37% in 2024, 16% in Dec 2025, 39% in Mar 2026); it requires KNR to inject equity (INR 734 crores invested of a revised INR 952 crores as of March 2026) and is the company's most capital-hungry business.
- **Irrigation projects** — Construction work on state irrigation and lift-irrigation schemes (Palamuru, Kaleshwaram and JV packages), which was about 26% of the order book in 2022 and has shrunk to roughly 14-19% by 2025-26, and which management flagged as a payment-delay problem (about INR 850 crores outstanding in mid-2022).
- **Pipeline projects** — Water/pipeline-laying contracts, mostly for state government clients, forming a smaller slice of the order book — around 22% in mid-2024, 12% in December 2025 and 7% by March 2026.
- **Mining projects** — A newer diversification into mining development work that had become the single largest order-book component by late FY26 — about 40% in December 2025 and 30% in March 2026 — though management excludes it when quoting the order book's execution period.
- **Toll / operating road assets and asset monetization** — Completed concessions generate cash — the Bihar Muzaffarpur-Barauni project collected about INR 10-12 crores of toll per quarter — and finished HAM SPVs are sold to infrastructure trusts (KNR Palani alone brought INR 205 crores of consideration plus INR 90 crores of upstreamed cash against INR 64.4 crores invested), recycling capital back into new projects.
- **JVs, associates and arbitration claims** — Income also comes from joint ventures and associate companies outside the main contracting business — for instance a INR 60.8 crore arbitration award from the Patel KNR joint venture and a INR 14.5 crore dividend from associate Patel KNR Infrastructures, both booked in Q1 FY25 — which are lumpy and can materially swing a quarter's profit.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

**Capital Allocation — Neutral (+0.00)** · 3 of 6 checks had evidence

- **Merger and acquisition — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: No acquisitions at all; instead disciplined asset recycling — the Indus Infra Trust SPV sale realised INR 205 cr plus INR 90 cr cash upstream on INR 64.4 cr invested in KNR Palani, and a INR 1,543 cr package for four SPVs.
  > "The company has invested INR64.40 crores in the form of equity and sub debt in that SPV. Pursua nt to this transaction, the company has r eceived a consideration of INR 205.05 crores" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Cost of working capital — Neutral** *(from the financial statements)*: About 39 days of cash stays stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers — a long cycle that ties up capital.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance) — Weak** *(from the conference calls)*: Capital has gone into captive HAM SPV equity (INR 952 cr) and a large equipment fleet whose returns have collapsed — equipment now sits idle against falling turnover, and the company is recycling SPVs rather than compounding growth capex.
  > "equipment is very huge with us and that is not performing to the required level" — *management, on an earnings call*

**Return on Capital — Weak (-1.00)** · 3 of 3 checks had evidence

- **Profit margin — Good** *(from the financial statements)*: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹26 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±5.6 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Return on capital (headline) — Poor** *(from the financial statements)*: For every ₹100 of capital in the business (shareholders' funds + borrowings), it generated ₹-2 of operating cash this year — a -2% cash return on capital.
- **Asset turn — Poor** *(from the numbers and the calls together)*: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹0.33 of sales — a fairly asset-heavy business. ## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Government Dependency, Stakeholder Concentration, New Entrants, Good Enough Goods, Technological Innovation, Shifting Consumer Preferences, Fashion Risk. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.5× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ⚠️ Only about -30% of recent operating profit became cash (was 123%) — profits are getting stuck in paper, an early stress signal.
- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 9% to 22% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is entirely a function of the government's road capex award cycle, not of any operations-linked flow demand: standalone revenue fell from ~INR890cr/quarter (Q1FY23) to INR535cr (Q4FY26) and standalone EBITDA margin from 18.5% to 5.3%, with FY26 margin at 8.5%, because NHAI awarding paused for two years while fixed staff and equipment costs stayed in place. Management itself attributes both the volume collapse and the compressed bidding margins to the awarding gap.

> "all the orders on hand were executed and because of the big gap that has come in NHAI, for 2 years there has been very less job inflow that has caused us to bid in such a way" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 11 years with swings of ±21 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 14% and 34% (±5.6 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 61% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 10% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Essentially the whole business is government-determined: order book is ~80% state government plus central government with only 1-3% private clients, revenue depends on MoRTH/NHAI awarding volumes and budget allocations, and policy changes directly reset economics (HAM upfront payment cut from 40% to 20%, price-adjustment cycle changed from 3 months to 1 month, Bharatmala Phase-1 deadline extended by 6 years). The FY26 earnings collapse followed purely from the ministry slowing awards, and INR670cr of Telangana irrigation dues remain stuck on the state's fiscal position.

> "The Highway sector in particular experienced a slowdown, primarily due to moderation in the project awarding by the ministry. This led to a lower construction and execution activity during the financial year." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Attempted diversification into mining, railways/high-speed rail, metro, pipeline, solar and BESS — but these are also government/PSU-tendered, so the diversification is across departments, not away from policy-driven revenue*

### Stakeholder Concentration — HIGH RISK
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 1 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** A single counterparty class drives results, and single relationships have repeatedly damaged the P&L and balance sheet: Telangana irrigation receivables were INR850cr outstanding in Aug 2022 and INR670cr still stuck on Kaleshwaram Package 4 in Jun 2026, requiring management meetings with the state Finance Minister; separately the whole FY25-26 order drought traces to one client, NHAI, pausing tenders. Order-book concentration also swings violently (mining alone was 40% of the book in Dec 2025, and it cannot start because of forest clearance and a single Gram Sabha).

> "we have one project in Kaleshwaram Package 4, where almost INR670 crores of debtors is pending... from government of Telangana irrigation project, where we have stuck with some of the debtors" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate broadening of client base across NHAI, TANSHA, MSRDC, GHMC, Musi Riverfront Corp and multiple states (Kerala, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, MP, Odisha, Rajasthan), plus asset monetisation to Indus Infra Trust releasing INR1,543cr*

- ⚠️ Customers now take about 79 days to pay, up from 42 — lengthening collection terms are how weak bargaining power against concentrated customers shows up in the numbers.

### New Entrants — ELEVATED
*"A new player changes the rules"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Management repeatedly describes a bidding field that has been reset by aggressive entrants chasing a shrunken tender pool, forcing KNR to accept structurally lower margins — new HAM wins are being underwritten at 11-12% EBITDA versus the 18-20% historically earned, and management concedes the bottom line cannot return to earlier levels. This is price aggression rather than a genuinely new-format competitor, which bounds the risk.

> "this time whatever the order which is coming up it's coming at a tight competition, cannot be expecting like a level last year. So, turnover can go, but I don't think the bottom line will be to that tune which we were giving in the earlier days because now the market is entire changed" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Selective bidding discipline — avoiding sub-INR500-800cr tenders because 'it's highly crowded and there is no good bid that is happening there' — and using back-to-back/JV arrangements with Patel Infra, NCC and BOT developers to access work without competing head-on*

- ✅ Operating margin held (about 15% then 28%) — no drift down under competition.
- ⚠️ Growth has stalled: sales compounded at 21% a year in the first half of the record but only -1% recently — demand may be structurally fading.

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED

**What the calls show:** KNR's historic premium rested on execution reputation, but in an L1 tender market the buyer awards on price and that premium has visibly eroded: sustainable EBITDA guidance has been cut from 13-14% to 9-10%, and management calls the current level embarrassing. There is no niche or scale moat protecting the price point, though the company's pre-qualification credentials and completion track record still gate entry on larger packages.

> "I think EBITDA, if you talk about EBITDA, I'm very shameful to say that it is around 10 kind of level now we are getting" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Pre-qualification and bid-capacity requirements plus a CRISIL AA/A1+ rating restrict who can bid larger packages, and KNR is 'the people most wanted by the bigger contractors' for back-to-back work — a partial protection of its position*

- ✅ Operating margin (26%) remains near its historical peak (34%) — no collapse in the premium.
- ✅ Operating margin held (about 15% then 28%) — no drift down under competition.
