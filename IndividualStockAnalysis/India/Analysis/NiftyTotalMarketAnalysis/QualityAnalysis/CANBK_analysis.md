# Canara Bank (CANBK) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

Canara Bank is an Indian government-linked (public sector) bank headquartered at 112 J C Road, Bengaluru, listed on the BSE and NSE under the code CANBK, and run by a Managing Director & CEO plus several Executive Directors. It takes deposits from the public (about Rs. 16.1 lakh crore as of June 2026) and lends that money out as loans (about Rs. 12.9 lakh crore), giving a total 'global business' of roughly Rs. 29 lakh crore; borrowers range from individual home, vehicle and gold-loan customers to farmers, small businesses and large corporates. It earns mainly from the spread between interest charged on loans and interest paid on deposits — net interest income crossed Rs. 10,215 crore in a single quarter — topped up by 'non-interest' income such as fees and commissions, treasury (bond and investment) gains, PSLC commission, and cash recovered from loans it had earlier written off (roughly Rs. 6,500 crore a year). Business is sourced through its own countrywide branch network ('brick and mortar outlets'), it also participates in government credit schemes routed through the Jan Samarth portal, and it holds stakes in subsidiaries including Canara HSBC and Canara Robeco, whose stock-market listings produced a one-time Rs. 1,930 crore gain.

**The parts of the business:**

- **RAM credit (Retail, Agriculture and MSME)** — The bank's priority engine of small-ticket lending to individuals, farmers and small businesses — management has deliberately pushed it from about 55% to roughly 57-58% of the total loan book, and it stood at Rs. 7.65 lakh crore growing over 21% a year in the June 2026 quarter.
- **Retail credit** — Loans to individual customers — the fastest-growing piece at Rs. 3.20 lakh crore and 35.9% growth, driven by home loans, vehicle loans and a non-agricultural gold loan product introduced for city customers at a higher rate and lower loan-to-value.
- **Housing loan** — Home loans, the biggest single retail product at Rs. 1.29 lakh crore growing about 17-18%; management says it is one of the few banks steadily gaining market share here and does not buy loan portfolios from others.
- **Vehicle loan** — Car and vehicle finance at Rs. 27,315 crore growing about 26% — a book management notes the bank historically never grew in double digits until recently.
- **Gold loan** — Loans against gold, which crossed Rs. 1 lakh crore back in 2022; split between an agriculture version yielding about 8.75% and a retail city version yielding over 9%, after the bank stopped agri gold lending in metro cities to be more conservative.
- **MSME credit** — Working capital and term loans to micro, small and medium enterprises, at Rs. 1.69 lakh crore growing about 15%; it is also where management admits most of the loan stress sits (Rs. 1,333 crore of one quarter's Rs. 2,771 crore of new bad loans).
- **Agriculture** — Farm and allied lending including agri gold loans and term loans, part of RAM; growth here was deliberately slowed when the bank pulled back from metro agri gold lending.
- **Corporate credit** — Large-company and project lending — roughly 44-45% of the book, spread across road infrastructure (Rs. 50,000 crore of HAM projects underwritten in 2022), AAA/AA-rated NBFCs, iron and steel, real estate for housing, engineering, construction and chemicals; the bank says it is deliberately choosy, with about 78% of rated exposure at A or above, and has at times shed low-yielding corporate loans.
- **Treasury / investments** — The bank's bond and equity investment book, which produces lumpy gains or losses — over Rs. 1,000 crore of treasury income in one quarter, but Rs. 800 crore of mark-to-market losses in another when bond yields rose and markets fell.
- **Fee-based and other income** — Commissions and charges on banking services, including PSLC commission, running around Rs. 1,900 crore in a quarter and growing in the mid-teens.
- **Recovery from written-off accounts (TWO recovery)** — Cash collected on loans already fully written off, a steady profit contributor of roughly Rs. 1,500-1,600 crore per quarter and about Rs. 6,500-6,800 crore per year.
- **Subsidiaries (Canara HSBC, Canara Robeco)** — Associated financial-services companies in which the bank holds stakes; their stock-market listings delivered a one-off Rs. 1,930 crore gain in the March 2026 quarter.
- **Government-scheme lending (ECLGS 5.0)** — Credit extended under a government emergency guarantee scheme — the bank identified about Rs. 90,000 crore of eligible accounts and roughly Rs. 18,000 crore of potential extra lending, of which over Rs. 11,000 crore was sanctioned and Rs. 10,000 crore disbursed by July 2026.

### The story in depth

Canara Bank's clearest strength is that it is genuinely winning business and has cleaned up its loan book at the same time. Advances grew 17.97% year-on-year in July 2026 against a system pace of roughly 11-12%, retail grew about 36%, and management explicitly claims rising housing share from a small base of roughly 4-5%. That growth is aimed at end markets — retail, housing, vehicle, MSME and gold credit — that have expanded 17-33% through the period, which is why both the 'Gain in market share' and 'Structural end-market growth' checks score positively and the Market Share Gainers pattern is judged real rather than cosmetic. It is also growth the bank built itself: no portfolio buyouts, negligible co-lending, all retail and MSME business originated through its own branches. That self-sourcing is what earns the partial credit on Forward Integrators and supports the 'Disciplined stewards' check, alongside a capital ratio of 17.17% and a decision to provide the full Rs 560 crore against a stressed public-sector account before any actual default. The delivery record backs it up — the 90% coverage target was beaten at 94.76%, the RAM mix target was met, and July 2026 guidance was beaten line by line — which is why Corporate Culture rates as a partial fit.

The problem is that almost none of this converts into better economics, and the same traits that create the strengths also create the weaknesses. Growth is volume-led, not price-led: advances rose 17.97% while net interest income rose only 13.39%, and margin was merely 'protected' at about 2.52% — the worst in its peer group, with roughly 1.04% return on assets. That is why Return on Capital scores the worst possible mark and why three patterns — Pricing Power, Low Price Plus and Recurring Revenue — are outright no-fits. The bank cannot set its loan prices, because they move with the policy rate, and it cannot get cheap money either: current and savings deposits have stayed under 30% of the base, bulk money costs 7.5-7.8%, and management concedes households are moving savings into mutual funds and equities. So the bank buys volume by paying up for deposits, which is the New Entrants and Shifting Consumer Preferences risk and the 'Barriers to entry and rationality' and 'Intangible benefits' negative checks describing one single fact from different angles. The same picture explains why it had to walk away from Rs 22,500 crore of underpriced corporate loans — it is a price-taker on both sides of the balance sheet, which is the Good Enough Goods risk. Its two real barriers, sovereign ownership and dense South India branches anchoring a Rs 2.45 lakh crore gold-loan book, give it deposit trust and a niche customers prefer to visit in person, but neither produces a price premium — hence Brand Strength and Cost to Replicate both stop at partial.

The risks compound rather than offset the above, and several are the flip side of the strengths. The improved asset quality — bad loans down from 6.98% in 2022 to 1.57%, credit cost down to 0.49%, coverage at 94.76% — means the bank sits near the top of the credit cycle, not the bottom, so the 'Cyclical market growth' check is negative and a new expected-loss provisioning charge is still ahead; the tailwind that flattered recent earnings is largely spent. Government ownership is rated the single HIGH RISK item because it sets the terms on both sides: it underwrites deposit trust and boosts FY26-27 credit growth through a state guarantee scheme, but it also puts the bank's economics in the state's hands, and it explains the leadership churn of four different chief executives across 17 calls that sinks the 'Independent, long-term and tenacious' check. Individual large accounts still swing reported numbers — Rs 1,240 crore on Future Group, a Rs 3,800 crore state public-sector exposure that forced the Rs 560 crore provision — which keeps concentration on watch even as the shift toward retail and small-business lending (55% to about 59% of the book) gradually broadens the base. The unrealised 2022 overseas ambition and continued South India concentration weigh on 'Geographical expansion'. Netting it out, business quality scores 45 and risk safety 60, producing an overall 50 out of 100 and a 'Decent' three-star grade: a bank executing well operationally within a structure that caps what that execution can be worth, with overall resilience still unproven.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Canara Bank is a majority government-owned PSU bank whose economics are set by the state and the regulator at almost every line: deposit-raising rests on sovereign guarantee, credit growth in FY26-27 is explicitly boosted by the government's ECLGS 5.0 scheme (Rs 90,000 cr identified, Rs 11,000 cr sanctioned via the Jan Samarth portal), a recurring ~Rs 2,500 cr of other income comes from selling PSLCs (a regulator-created market arising from priority-sector mandates), lending yields reset mechanically with RBI repo moves, and a single RBI accounting change (ECL) imposes a Rs 10,000-12,000 cr provisioning bill plus a ~1.2% CRAR dent. Across all four years management frames both growth and profitability in terms of policy actions (repo transmission, penal-interest reclassification, HTM/AFS norms, ECL floors) rather than franchise decisions.

> "since we have a central government sovereign guarantee for rising deposits, we are not facing any difficulty" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Partial: the shift of the book toward granular RAM/retail (55% to 57%+ of credit, retail growing 33-36%) reduces reliance on PSU/government-directed corporate lending, and capital adequacy of 17%+ lets it absorb policy shocks like ECL without a capital raise.*

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Lending is a flow business tied to customer operations, and the trajectory across 17 calls shows the credit cycle turning firmly in the bank's favour (GNPA 6.98% in Jul-22 to 1.57% in Jul-26, credit cost 0.90% to 0.49%, slippage ratio 0.60%) — but earnings still swing on the rate and market cycle rather than on the franchise: treasury income fell from Rs 1,617 cr to Rs 654 cr in one year, an Rs 800 cr MTM loss appeared on a bond-yield move, and one-off listing gains of Rs 1,930 cr flattered a prior quarter. ## What to watch
