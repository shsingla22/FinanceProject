# PG Electroplast (PGEL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

PG Electroplast (PGEL) is an Indian contract manufacturer of home appliances — it builds room air conditioners, washing machines, air coolers and televisions in its own factories and sells them to consumer-durable brands, which then put their own names on the products. It works in two ways: OEM, where it simply assembles to a client's design, and ODM, where it designs and develops the product platform itself and then makes it (management says roughly 80% of its AC product sales are ODM). It makes money on the volume of finished appliances shipped, so revenue is highly seasonal — the March-quarter summer AC season is by far its biggest — and margins depend on commodity prices, the rupee, and how fully its plants are loaded. It operates only in India, from plants in Greater Noida, Supa, Rajasthan and Sri City, and in FY26 did about ₹5,288 crore of consolidated revenue with ₹194 crore of net profit (versus ₹4,869 crore and ₹291 crore in FY25).

**The parts of the business:**

- **Product business (finished appliances)** — The core of the company — complete, ready-to-sell appliances made for brand customers; it grew from a minority of sales in FY23 to about 80–82% of total revenue by FY26 (₹1,412 crore of the ₹1,717 crore Q4 FY26 revenue).
- **Room air conditioners (RAC)** — The single largest line — indoor units, outdoor units and complete units supplied to leading Indian brands; it was ₹932 crore or 66% of total revenue in Q3 FY26, grew 9% for full-year FY26 despite an industry that shrank ~15%, and management calls it the biggest contributor to product revenue.
- **Washing machines** — Semi-automatic and fully automatic machines made at multiple locations; smaller than ACs (₹194 crore in Q3 FY26) but the fastest-growing line, up 52% for full-year FY26.
- **Air coolers** — A seasonal summer product added later than ACs and washers; it grew 267% in H1 FY25 off a small base but declined about 8% in FY26, so it remains the smallest of the three main product lines.
- **Television / electronics (incl. Goodworth Electronics JV)** — LED TV assembly and electronics work, now largely run through the joint venture Goodworth Electronics, which did ₹670 crore of revenue in the first nine months of FY26 at thin margins (₹16.7 crore EBITDA) — a competitive, low-margin business the company entered with very small capex.
- **PG Technoplast (100% subsidiary)** — The wholly owned subsidiary that houses a large share of manufacturing operations, reporting ₹1,067 crore of revenue in Q3 FY26 out of ₹1,412 crore consolidated.
- **Refrigerators (Sri City, under construction)** — A new plant in Sri City in South India with 1.2 million units of planned capacity, targeted to start commercial production in Q4 FY27 at 30–40% loading and become a meaningful revenue stream in FY28.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

PG Electroplast is winning share convincingly in a market that is genuinely growing, and it says so honestly. Its air-conditioner revenue went from Rs.297cr in FY22 to Rs.1,118cr in the first half of FY25, and it still grew 27% in the first nine months of FY26 while the industry shrank 15-20% — the one pattern in the screen rated a clear fit is Market Share Gainers. The demand backdrop supports this: fewer than one in ten Indian households owns a room air conditioner, with a path toward 3-4 crore units a year by FY31-32, and brands are steadily handing production over to outside manufacturers rather than doing it themselves. Management is the strongest part of the whole record (scoring 0.83 out of a possible 1.0 among the quality areas), and the candour check is near-perfect: they flagged the disappointing fully-automatic washing machine push as early as FY23 and itemised each FY26 setback, including a Rs.300cr production loss tied to LPG supply. Growth spending has climbed relentlessly — roughly Rs.130-140cr in FY23, Rs.370-380cr in FY25, Rs.700-750cr in FY26 — and nearly all of it is going into new capacity and making more of the product in-house, not just keeping the lights on. Yet the combined rating is only 41 out of 100, a Mixed two-star verdict, and the resilience read is Strained. Understanding why means seeing that most of the good news and most of the bad news come from the same source.

That source is cost leadership. It is the company's stated core advantage in every phase, and it earns the screen's second-best result — a likely fit with the Low Price Plus pattern, backed by a detailed cost programme covering lease rationalisation, moulding consolidation, systems and low-cost automation. But being the cheapest maker is exactly why six of the eleven patterns are outright no-fits and why the competitive advantage area scores negative (-0.5). The company keeps only Rs.18 of every Rs.100 of sales after direct input costs, which is thin, and there is no image or taste that lets it charge more — the intangible benefits check is at the bottom of the scale. Management openly disclaims managing to a percentage margin and passes commodity moves through with a one-to-two-month lag, so the Pricing Power pattern fails outright; in FY26 it could not pass on roughly 250 basis points of input inflation, which is the clearest proof of the point. The customers own the brands, so Brand Strength fails. The company supplies the whole finished appliance rather than a small critical ingredient, so Toll Roads fails. Sales are one-time product shipments with no service or subscription tail, and the business is working-capital hungry rather than customer-funded, so Recurring Revenue fails. The same cost-first identity produces the Good Enough Goods risk at Elevated: the brand in the middle makes the buying decision, the end consumer has never heard of PG Electroplast, and the product is defended on price rather than on being different. Genuine engineering work happens — new air-conditioner platforms for revised energy ratings, roughly 80% of AC sales from its own design solutions, new washing machine and cooler platforms — but with modest R&D spending it only earns a partial fit on Innovation Dominance rather than a real technical moat.

The second link runs from the growth itself to the risk register. Because the company sells into a seasonal, weather-driven category through a handful of brands, the very volume-led expansion that wins share also makes results swing hard: after 100%-plus product growth through FY23-FY25, FY26 saw operating profit fall from Rs.519cr to Rs.442cr and net profit from Rs.291cr to Rs.194cr on an industry that shrank about 15%, with five million units of unsold stock sitting in the channel. That is why Cyclicality is rated High Risk, and why the persistence-of-growth check scores at the bottom despite an average growth rate near 37% a year — the growth is real but lumpy. Concentration is the other High Risk, and it sits directly on top of the company's best defensive move: the two largest new investments, the refrigerant plant at Sri City and the two-million-unit compressor plant at Supa, are underwritten by a single unnamed anchor customer. Backward integration is what makes the Cost to Replicate pattern even partially credible, and it depends on one buyer. Funding it is the third link: with capex at Rs.700-750cr and repeated equity raises including a prior share sale and a fresh Rs.1,500cr enabling approval, existing holders are being diluted rather than paid, which is why the shareholder-distribution check is negative and capital allocation scores only 0.2. Three further watch-level risks — government policy (incentives were about Rs.30cr of the Rs.250cr FY25 profit guidance, and a GST rate-cut announcement froze sales), brands bringing production back in-house under those same incentives, and a washing machine business anchored in a possibly flat semi-automatic segment — all press on the same open question. Against that, management points to a cyclical trough now passing: channel stock normalised, better April and May sell-out, a GST cut and a longer season. The honest reading is a capable, fast-growing, transparently run manufacturer with a strong tailwind and a weak position in its own value chain, where the next few periods will show whether scale finally converts into durable pricing strength or simply into more volume at Rs.18 of gross retention.

*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**What the calls show:** Share gain is documented and sustained across the whole period: AC revenue from Rs.297cr (FY22) to Rs.1,118cr in H1FY25 (+143%) to ~11-12% of all AC manufacturing in India and ~20% of the outsourced market by FY26, with +27% 9M FY26 growth against a 15-20% industry decline and no client lost in five years — gained on cost/quality, not on credit or loss-making pricing.

> "we have been able to grow our RAC business by 27% in 9 months of FY '26 despite the industry posting an almost 15% to 20% decline" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Sales compounding at 37% while margins held or improved (3% → 9%) — growth that was WON, not bought with price cuts.

### Low Price Plus — LIKELY FIT
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern, but the numbers push back (0 of 1 check supporting) — held one step below a full confirmation.

**What the calls show:** Cost leadership is the stated core moat in every phase — Nov-2024 'cost leadership while striving for product leadership', Feb-2026 repeats it, and May-2026 details an itemised cost programme (lease rationalisation, molding consolidation at Salarpur, SAP, low-cost automation) alongside high turns (net fixed asset turnover >6x, ROCE 18.6%) on thin ~8% EBITDA margins.

> "These initiatives are aimed at preserving and extending our cost leadership, which is a very core competitive mode for us in our outsourcing business" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹18 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 0.9× a year — not the high-turnover, low-margin machine this pattern needs.

### Corporate Culture — PARTIAL

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Stakeholder Concentration, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.2× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ⚠️ Only about 16% of recent operating profit became cash (was 98%) — profits are getting stuck in paper, an early stress signal.
- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 5% to 16% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Results swing violently with the AC season, weather, channel inventory and commodity/forex cycles: FY23-FY25 saw 100%+ product growth and upgraded guidance, then FY26 EBITDA fell from INR519cr to INR442cr and PAT from INR291cr to INR194cr on a ~15% RAC industry decline, while capex kept scaling to INR700-750cr — classic expansion into a peak. Q4FY26 alone lost ~INR420cr of revenue to LPG/truck disruptions and ~250bps of gross margin to input inflation the company could not pass through.

> "The RAC industry... experienced a decline of approximately 15% during FY26. This was against a backdrop where just 12 months ago when we and the rest of the industry were guiding for strong double-digit growth." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Products are consumer flow/replacement goods rather than customer capex equipment; management invests through the cycle (compressor and refrigerant plants) while the industry retrenches, and washing machines (+52% in FY26) partially offset AC seasonality.*

- ⚠️ Year-to-year sales growth swings by ±56 percentage points — boom-and-bust revenue, not a steady compounder.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 1% and 10% (±3.2 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 14% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 23%).

### Stakeholder Concentration — HIGH RISK
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 1 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Both ends are concentrated: the two largest new investments — the Sri City refrigerant plant and the Supa rotary compressor plant — are underwritten by a single unnamed 'anchor customer', and revenue depends on a handful of brands whose channel-filling decisions swung Q3FY26 and Q4FY26 sharply. On the supply side management stated critical components (compressors, motors, copper tubing, aluminium foil, controllers) must be imported from China with no domestic alternative, and Chinese New Year timing forces months of pre-buying.

> "We have already entered into a tie-up with our anchor customer for this facility... We have received approval and commitments from our anchor customer for this facility as well." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Client base has broadened steadily (8 AC brands by FY23, 16 new orders, continued additions), management claims no client lost in five years with rising wallet share, and the compressor/refrigerant plants are explicit moves to 'greatly secure our supply chain' away from China.*

- ⚠️ Customers now take about 76 days to pay, up from 33 — lengthening collection terms are how weak bargaining power against concentrated customers shows up in the numbers.

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** PGEL sells an unbranded manufacturing service where the intermediary — the brand — controls the choice, the end consumer has zero awareness of PGEL, and the product is defended on cost rather than differentiation; analysts openly ask whether refusing to concede price will cost share to other OEMs, and FY26 showed the pricing power is thin (only 10-15% of a much larger cost increase passed through, 250bps gross margin hit). EBITDA margins run ~8% with electronics as low as 2-4%.

> "every OEM player are currently renegotiating their pricing with the OEM players. So how the margin will be secured as OEM player has very thin margins?" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Management cites cost leadership plus scale, quality and product leadership as switching friction ('we have not lost a single client' in five years, market share up within clients), and backward integration into compressors/refrigerant is intended to defend unit economics structurally rather than by price.*

- ✅ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs held near 19% (was 13%) — no gross-level price erosion.
- ✅ Operating margin (7%) remains near its historical peak (10%) — no collapse in the premium.
- ✅ Operating margin held (about 3% then 9%) — no drift down under competition.

### Government Dependency — WATCH
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Policy repeatedly moves results: PLI incentives were ~INR30cr of the INR250cr FY25 profit guidance, the entire IT-hardware/laptop JV opportunity is stalled pending government import restrictions, the 15-Aug-2025 GST rate-cut announcement froze sales for over a month, and BEE rating transitions pulled production into Dec-25 and hollowed out Jan-Feb. *Silver lining: Diversified across RAC, washing machines, coolers, TV JV, moulding and electronics so no single policy line dominates; management declined to build the business case on the IT-hardware PLI while it remains unresolved.*

## What to watch

- Who the anchor customer is behind the Supa compressor and Sri City refrigerant plants, and whether that commitment holds — this is the core of the High Risk Stakeholder Concentration verdict and of the partial Cost to Replicate fit.
- Whether input-cost inflation gets passed through in future seasons, after the roughly 250 basis points it could not recover in FY26 — the crux of the negative Pricing, Mix and Volume check and the Pricing Power no-fit.
- Whether the cyclical upturn management identified actually lands — normalised channel inventory, better April/May sell-out, the GST cut and a longer season — which is what separates the positive Cyclical market growth check from the High Risk Cyclicality verdict.
- Whether the five-year record of zero client losses survives brands in-sourcing under policy incentives, which is the New Entrants watch risk testing the Market Share Gainers pattern.
- How the Rs.700-750cr capex programme gets funded from here, given the Rs.1,500cr enabling approval and the negative shareholder-distribution check — further dilution would keep capital allocation stuck near its current 0.2 score.
