# Torrent Power Ltd. (TORNTPOWER) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: ENERGY*

Torrent Power is an Indian electricity company that generates power, distributes it to end customers, and is building power-transmission lines. It sells electricity to households and businesses in the areas where it holds a distribution licence or runs a distribution franchise, and sells output from its power plants either under long-term contracts with state power buyers (such as MSEDCL in Maharashtra and MP Power Management Company) or in the short-term 'merchant' market, plus occasional resale of LNG fuel. Its money comes mainly from regulated returns on the money it invests in the distribution and transmission networks (a regulator-set return on equity, recently 13-15%), from fixed-cost recovery and merchant margins on its power plants, and from cutting distribution losses. As of 31 March 2026 it had about 5.1 GW of generating capacity — 2.7 GW gas-fired, about 2 GW renewables and 362 MW coal — operating in Gujarat (Ahmedabad, Surat), Maharashtra, Agra in Uttar Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh, with a further 4 GW of renewables, 3 GW of pumped-storage hydro, a 1.6 GW coal plant and transmission lines under construction.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Licensed distribution business** — Supplies electricity to customers in areas where the company holds a distribution licence (Ahmedabad, Surat and others) and earns a regulator-set return on the assets it builds; management calls it the operational benchmark of the group, with distribution losses of 2.34% described as among the lowest in India, and it absorbed roughly INR 1,600 crore of the FY26 capex together with the franchise business.
- **Distribution franchisee business** — Runs the electricity distribution of city networks owned by state utilities under a franchise arrangement — Agra being the flagship, where losses fell from 58.77% at takeover in 2010 to a record-low 6.94% — and is a smaller capex item (about INR 250-275 crore a year) but a steady contributor through loss reduction.
- **Thermal generation (gas-based)** — Operates 2.7 GW of gas-fired plants (SUGEN, UNOSUGEN, DGEN) whose power is sold under short-term/merchant contracts such as the NVVN tenders, and which swings the profits most — it added about INR 293 crore of quarterly contribution in Q2 FY26 on strong merchant sales, but lost money when LNG prices were high; fuel cost is largely passed through and LNG cargoes are contracted ahead for summer demand.
- **Thermal generation (coal-based)** — A small existing 362 MW coal capacity plus a new 1.6 GW coal plant in Madhya Pradesh won from MP Power Management Company at INR 5.83 per unit (fixed cost INR 4.22, variable cost passed through), with boiler-turbine-generator and balance-of-plant orders placed and about INR 700 crore spent in FY26; the pending Nabha Power acquisition adds to this side.
- **Renewable generation** — About 2 GW of solar and wind in operation with roughly 4 GW more under construction, sold mostly under long-term contracts such as the 367 MWp MSEDCL project and a 500 MW round-the-clock (FDRE) solar-plus-wind-plus-battery project at INR 4.87 per unit; it is by far the biggest area of spending, about INR 6,500 crore in FY26, with 1.2-1.5 GW expected to be commissioned each year going forward.
- **Pumped storage hydro / energy storage** — A 3 GW pumped-storage hydro programme, including a first-of-its-kind-in-India 40-year, 2,000 MW energy storage facility agreement with MSEDCL; still under construction, with civil, hydro-mechanical and electro-mechanical contracts awarded and environmental clearance received, and described as a 4-5 year project.
- **Transmission** — Builds and owns high-voltage lines — the Khavda project was commissioned in the March 2026 quarter and Solapur is still under construction — a smaller business at roughly INR 250-550 crore of capex a year.
- **City gas distribution** — Pipes natural gas to city customers; the calls mention only that India's largest green-hydrogen-blended-with-natural-gas initiative in the city gas distribution sector was commissioned in the September 2025 quarter, so it is a minor part of the story in these calls.

### The story in depth

Torrent Power's core is a protected retail electricity business, and almost every strength in the record traces back to it. The company holds a legally exclusive supply licence in Ahmedabad, Surat and Dahej, where customers have no alternative supplier and effectively cannot switch — both the mini-monopolies and broken-competition checks score the maximum. It has also earned that position operationally rather than just inheriting it: licensed distribution losses of 2.34% are at global benchmark levels, and the Agra franchise saw its aggregate technical and commercial losses fall from 58.77% at the 2010 takeover to a historic low of 6.94%, which is why the framework rates its long-term tenacity and distribution reach at the top of the scale. That same operating discipline is what the multibagger screen picks up as a partial Low Price Plus fit and a partial Cost to Replicate fit — the wires, the statutory licences, the 40-year 2,000MW storage agreement with MSEDCL and finite pumped-storage sites genuinely cannot be copied, but management never frames any of it as a barrier and no share of the savings is shown reaching the customer as a price weapon. Weak state-owned DISCOMs are the natural donors of share here, and the record makes that literal: Agra's losses at takeover and the privatisation of five to six UP circles are direct transfers of customers from a worse operator to a better one, which is also what supports the partial Market Share Gainers reading (footprint moving from 4.8GW to 5.1GW, renewables pipeline 3.1GW to 4GW, a 1.6GW coal award and the Nabha acquisition) even though no actual market-share numbers are given.

The uncomfortable part is that the exact trait producing those strengths also produces the biggest risk. Exclusivity in distribution is granted by the state, and it comes bundled with returns the state sets: the licensed business moved from a fixed 14% return to a 13-15.5% incentive-linked framework, fuel is a pass-through, and quarterly profit swings are dominated by regulatory true-up and carrying-cost orders — with a INR171cr disallowance on UNOSUGEN power purchase costs showing that tariff outcomes get contested downward, not raised at will. That is why the screen marks Pricing Power as no fit and the risk review marks Government Dependency as the single high risk: they are the same fact seen from two sides. The partial offset is that being the best operator is exactly what earns the incentive milestones inside that framework, so quality of execution is the one lever that is genuinely in the company's hands. The second tension is capital. Growth capex is large and accelerating — roughly INR6,500cr of renewables plus INR1,600cr distribution, INR700cr coal and INR550cr transmission in FY26, with FY27 guided much higher against a 4GW renewable, 3GW pumped-storage and 1.6GW coal build-out. That earns a positive mark for growth capex and a 17% cash return on capital, but it is funded 70:30 debt to equity and by a maiden INR3,500cr share sale, with no dividend policy, buyback or distribution framework mentioned anywhere. Shareholder distribution therefore scores negative, Recurring Revenue is a clear no fit because growth consumes capital rather than throwing it off, and capital allocation is the weakest quality area at 0.25.

Where the company steps outside its protected patch, the advantages thin out. Renewable and commercial-and-industrial bidding is crowded — management concedes rivals are going aggressively on leverage, describes its own C&I bidding as already aggressive, and says it walks away when a threshold return is not met — which is why new entrants rate as an elevated risk even though the licence areas themselves stay legally sealed. The thermal and merchant fleet is openly cycle-driven: LNG at $12-17 suppressed merchant sales in Q4 FY25, a favourable window added INR304cr of thermal contribution in Q2 FY26, and by May 2026 gas at $16-20 made merchant sales very difficult, which also undercuts any Toll Roads reading since electricity is a large, price-sensitive line item for industrial buyers rather than a trivial one. The 2.7GW gas fleet, a third of installed capacity, already runs at only 30-35% utilisation and is being displaced economically by cheaper renewables plus storage, forcing a re-platforming toward firm-and-dispatchable renewable and battery hybrids. Meanwhile customers are quietly moving behind the meter — Ahmedabad and Surat both showed year-on-year unit declines, with rooftop self-generation raised as the cause and the company itself booking INR58cr of rooftop solar installation incentives — though regulated distribution profit is stated not to depend on unit growth, which softens the blow. Add concentration in a handful of named counterparties (NVVN for the entire gas merchant tender, MSEDCL for pumped storage and a 367 MWp solar project, MP Power Management for the coal contract) and the picture is a financially sound operator — net debt to equity of 0.40 and net debt to EBITDA of 1.41x, resilience judged sound — that is genuinely excellent at what it does but structurally capped in what it can earn. That is what produces the Mixed, two-star rating: 61 on business quality, 50 on risk safety, and only 16 on multibagger fit, blending to 45 out of 100.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, New Entrants, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.7× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Virtually every earnings driver across all five calls is set by government or regulator: licensed-distribution ROE/ROCE moved from a fixed 14% to a 13-15.5% incentive-linked MYT framework, quarterly PBT swings are dominated by true-up and carrying-cost orders (a INR273 crore credit in Q3 FY26 reversed by a INR171 crore UNOSUGEN power-purchase-cost disallowance in Q4 FY26), and the gas fleet's profitability depended on Section 11 imposition and NVVN tenders. Growth is likewise anchored in state contracts (MSEDCL 2 GW storage agreement, MP Power 1.6 GW coal LOA at INR5.83/kWh, UP privatisation circles) and fixed infrastructure — the textbook profile the risk warns about.

> "Section -11 was imposed first time on the gas -based power plant by the government, contributing significantly to the bottom -line." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Regulated returns are cost-plus and the company is a best-in-class operator (2.34% distribution loss, Agra AT&C down to 6.94%), which earns incentive milestones rather than losing them; but this reduces severity, not dependency.*

### New Entrants — ELEVATED
*"A new player changes the rules"*

**What the calls show:** Renewable and C&I bidding is crowded enough that management concedes peers are 'going aggressively' on leverage and that it must walk away on threshold IRR, and it describes itself as 'already aggressive... in all the bids' for C&I consumers — the classic sign of returns being competed down by better-capitalised entrants rather than an incumbent setting price.

> "if you look at the peers also going aggressively" — *management, on an earnings call*

## What to watch

- Whether the new 13-15.5% incentive-linked return framework and pending true-up orders land favourably, and whether disallowances like the INR171cr UNOSUGEN item repeat — the Government Dependency high risk is the main swing factor in the whole verdict.
- Whether FY27 capex, guided much higher, comes with a stated distribution policy or further equity raises — the negative Distribution to Shareholders check and the 0.25 capital allocation score only improve if cash starts returning to owners rather than being absorbed.
- Whether the company holds its threshold-return bidding discipline as renewable and C&I competition intensifies, or starts winning volume at weak returns — this is the pivot point of the Elevated New Entrants risk and the Corporate Culture pattern.
- Whether renewable commissioning actually converts the 4GW pipeline and the UP circle bids into measurable share — Market Share Gainers is only a partial fit because footprint is expanding but no share figures exist, and FY26 commissioning ran low.
- Whether the 2.7GW gas fleet's 30-35% utilisation keeps sliding and whether unit declines in Ahmedabad and Surat continue as rooftop solar spreads — together the Technological Innovation and Shifting Consumer Preferences watch risks would erode the earnings base the monopoly checks are built on.
