### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 2 weakened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Management gives explicit, repeated and rising share numbers — market share doubled from 2.4% (CY21) to 4.8% (CY25), largest residential developer in India by bookings for the third consecutive year, top-two in all five leading markets — and the gain is accompanied by rising profit (FY26 PAT +32%) and a 62% step-up in construction spend rather than bought with discounting. (The long view had said: Share has roughly doubled from 2.4% (CY21) to 4.8% (CY25) with GPL the largest listed residential developer by bookings for three consecutive years and Top-2 in all five leading markets — and it is profitable share, not bought: gearing 0.37x against a 0.5x self-imposed cap, economic interest up from 50% to 87%, and management says it walked away from land deals priced above its underwriting.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: FY26 booking value grew 16% against 5% volume growth, implying meaningful realisation gains, and management explicitly withholds prime inventory to capture price increases; but no explicit price-increase percentages or elasticity discussion is given, and Q2 FY26 gross margin was flagged by analysts as weak, so the evidence is directional rather than demonstrated pricing power. (The long view had said: Aug 2022 documents repeated 4–6% quarterly price hikes absorbed without volume loss, and over FY21–FY26 booking value compounded ~40% against ~20% volume growth, implying sustained realisation gains — but this rides an industry-wide housing upcycle rather than firm-specific pricing power, and management concedes froth in markets like Gurgaon is 'fading out'.)

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: The Godrej name plainly travels — a first-ever Hyderabad entry did ~INR2,600 crores in one calendar year and a first Indore project did INR400+ crores — and management leans on ESG/quality rankings (No.1 globally on GRESB and DJSI) as trust markers; but the calls contain no A&P spend, heritage narrative or explicit premium-pricing-plus-share evidence, so this is inferred from launch outcomes rather than stated. (The long view had said: The Godrej name is repeatedly cited as the reason landowners and JV partners transact with GPL — enabling an asset-light JV/BD model no peer matches — and it travels across geographies (Mumbai, NCR, Bengaluru, Pune, Hyderabad, Ahmedabad, Kolkata) and price points from Panipat plotted development to Worli ultra-luxury, with premium positioning intact.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls the revenue model is one-time residential unit sales recognised on OC/delivery — collections are milestone-linked construction payments, not renewals or AMC/subscription streams; no attach rate, renewal rate or installed-base density is ever discussed. (The long view had said: Real-estate development is a one-time sale, but customer advances/collections genuinely fund construction (collections of INR19,965 crores in FY26 vs booking value INR34,171 crores, driving OCF of INR7,830 crores), and a small annuity/rental stream is emerging. There is no license/AMC/renewal/attach-rate economics anywhere across the 16 calls.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT); Innovation Dominance (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Pricing and demand are not government-set, but launch timing — and therefore reported bookings — sits at the mercy of local approvals: the NCR de-growth in FY26 was attributed to a large Gurgaon acquisition whose approvals did not arrive within the year, the Delhi Ashok Vihar project 'has been delayed for some years', and Yeshwanthpur approval landed only three weeks before quarter-end. Real but bounded: it shifts revenue between periods rather than setting the economics of the business. (The long view had said: Revenue timing is gated by state approvals throughout all four years: Ashok Vihar was guided for Q3 FY23 and was still unlaunched in May 2026 because of tree-removal litigation in NCR courts; Maharashtra elections were flagged as an approval-delay risk in FY25; and NCR's FY26 sales de-grew because a large Gurgaon launch could not get approvals within the year. Management repeatedly says launch guidance is 'a combination of approval timeline'.)
- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The product itself (homes) is not technologically disruptable, but an analyst on the Q4 FY26 call explicitly flagged AI-related disruption to IT-hub employment as a demand risk for Bengaluru and Pune — together roughly INR12,500 crores of FY26 bookings — and management did not rebut the AI premise, answering instead on approvals and launch timing. That is a real second-order exposure via the end-customer's income base rather than via GPL's own technology. (The long view had said: The exposure is second-order but real and now explicitly raised: analysts in May 2026 asked about 'AI-related disruption in demand' for Bengaluru and, sentimentally, Pune, where GPL's largest growth markets rest on an IT/GCC employment base. Management rebutted rather than dismissed it, and conceded it must stay watchful; nothing threatens the company's own product or method.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW); Good Enough Goods (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 2 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 2 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 8 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
