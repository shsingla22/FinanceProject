# Time Technoplast (TIMETECHNO) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Time Technoplast makes plastic-based packaging and containers, plus a growing line of lightweight composite gas cylinders, and sells them almost entirely to other businesses (about 92-93% of sales are B2B) — chemical, pharmaceutical, paint and similar industrial customers who need drums and containers, and oil/gas companies such as government-owned oil marketing companies (e.g. IOCL) that buy its LPG and CNG cylinders. It earns money by manufacturing and selling these products in volume; raw material is mainly polymer, and price changes are passed on to customers through agreed monthly or quarterly revisions, so profit is driven by volume and capacity utilisation rather than resin prices. Roughly 65% of sales come from India and 35% from overseas operations spread across 10 countries where it manufactures only the packaging products, so the higher-margin value-added lines are an India story. In FY26 it reported consolidated net sales of about INR 6,114 crores, EBITDA of INR 901 crores and profit after tax of INR 469 crores, with EBITDA margin around 14.7%.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Industrial Packaging ("established products")** — The core business of making industrial drums and containers for chemical, pharma and similar customers — described as about 76% of sales in FY25 and roughly 70% by FY26 as value-added products grew, still growing steadily at about 10% a year with confirmed packaging orders of around INR 400 crores for the current calendar year across domestic and international markets.
- **Value-added products** — The higher-margin basket — mainly composite cylinders and IBCs (intermediate bulk containers) — which rose from 20-22% of total sales in FY23 to 29-30% by FY26, grew 18% in FY26 versus 10% for established products, and takes the bulk of the capex (INR 172 crores of FY26's INR 370 crores).
- **CNG Composite Cascades (Type IV cylinders)** — Lightweight composite cylinders bundled into cascades for transporting CNG, the fastest-growing line — roughly INR 308-310 crores of revenue in FY24 and growing 22-23% in FY26, with an order book of about INR 165-195 crores; capacity is being expanded and a larger 250-litre cylinder is ready to replace the current 156-litre size.
- **LPG Composite Cylinders** — Composite cooking-gas cylinders in sizes from 5 kg to about 22 kg, made for over a decade and supplied since 2022 to government-owned oil companies such as IOCL (which at one point booked 60-70% of capacity) plus exports to several countries; the calls put this at roughly INR 200 crores of revenue in FY24.
- **Other composite products** — A widening range beyond LPG and CNG — oxygen cylinders and composite products for the automotive sector — plus fire extinguishers in 6 kg and 9 kg sizes that management says are developed and go commercial from April 2026; sizes and timing are given but no revenue numbers.
- **Overseas business (10 countries)** — Manufacturing operations outside India that make only the packaging products, contributing about 35-37% of sales and growing slightly faster than India in recent years, at EBITDA margins (around 14.4%) now roughly level with India's.
- **NED Energy (batteries)** — A subsidiary that makes VRLA batteries for power, solar and UPS uses; the calls mention it once, in Aug 2022, for an MoU with Tesla Power USA Inc. for orders worth about INR 100 crores, and it is not discussed as a segment in later calls.

### The story in depth

Time Technoplast is a manufacturer with a solid, unexciting core and a genuinely interesting edge. The quality work scores it highest on the shape of its industry and its competitive position (both full marks in those areas) and nearly as high on management (0.8). Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹15 is operating profit, and that figure has moved only about ±0.8 points a year — the framework reads this steadiness as evidence of cost control and of an industry where competitors price rationally rather than fight for volume. The end markets are described as being in long-term expansion: CNG cascade rollout, the switch from steel to composite LPG cylinders, hydrogen, PE pipes for water and gas infrastructure, and mandated recycled-content rules. Management has been in place a long time (the same two names across all 16 calls), has paid dividends for 30 years, cut debt from INR647 crores to INR266 crores, and openly admitted missing its own target (FY26 ROCE of 18.9% against a 20% goal). Research and development is not a slogan here — Type IV CNG cascades, LPG and oxygen cylinders, 6/9 kg composite fire extinguishers due April 2026 and hydrogen drone cylinders have moved from the lab to commercialisation, and capex has roughly doubled from about INR175 crores in FY25 to INR370 crores in FY26 with about half of it aimed at these value-added lines rather than at simply keeping the existing plants running.

The multibagger screen tells you where that edge stops. Seven of the eleven patterns come back a partial fit and four are outright no fits, which is why the pattern score is only 10 out of 100. The partial verdicts almost all hang on the same 25% of the business: the composite cylinder side, where PESO and CPRI approvals, a decade of supply history with the oil marketing companies and a 1,080-cascade automated plant create a real barrier, and where a decade of new products keeps arriving. That same certification wall is what earns partial credit for the toll-road, cost-to-replicate, innovation and market-share patterns at once — it is one trait doing four jobs. The other 75% of revenue, the drums, jerry cans, pails and pipes, is plain conversion work, and it is what pulls every one of those verdicts back from a yes. The four flat no fits reinforce the point rather than adding new information: there is no pricing power, because pricing is an agreed pass-through formula and revenue actually grows slower than volume when polymer prices fall (9M FY26 volume up 15% against revenue up 11%); there is no recurring revenue, because everything is a one-off product sale; and there is no brand or middleman effect, because 92% of sales go straight to industrial buyers on formula pricing. The quality framework reaches the same conclusion from a different direction, marking pricing and mix down to -1 and noting that mix shifting to value-added products, now 29-30% of sales, is the only positive lever the company controls.

The risk review shows the strengths and the dangers running through the same pipe. The value-added franchise that carries the growth story is also the single high risk: the entire LPG composite cylinder business depends on tenders from state oil companies — at one point 60-70% of capacity was booked by IOCL, with expansion waiting on HPCL and BPCL — while CNG cascades depend on the government's gas infrastructure push. The concentration risk is the same story told at the customer level: a small CNG order book of INR175-195 crores and exclusive tie-ups such as Monbat. The board-committed joint venture with private gas distributors is the one move that would loosen this, and it is also what earns the company its partial credit as a forward integrator. Elsewhere, the itemised cost programme — solar power deals across five states now worth about INR11 crores a year with roughly a one-year payback, robotic automation, unit consolidation, falling finance costs — is simultaneously the evidence for the low-cost pattern and the evidence for the good-enough-goods risk, because defending profits through cost cuts is what a business does when it cannot raise prices. Cyclicality is flagged as one to watch for exactly the reason pricing power fails. Against all this sit two hard numbers the quality checks penalise: about 161 days of cash tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and only ₹5 of operating cash generated per ₹100 of capital employed, which is why the capital allocation and return-on-capital areas score 0.2 and 0.33 while everything else scores well. Resilience is judged strained, and the combined rating of 48 out of 100 — 45% weight on business quality at 67, 30% on pattern fit at 10, 25% on risk safety at 60 — lands at Mixed, two stars. One caveat worth stating: entry risk was marked no signal, meaning the calls simply never addressed it, not that the threat has been ruled out.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Toll Roads — PARTIAL
*"Small cost, can't skip it"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 2 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Packaging is a small but operationally critical input for chemical/pharma customers, and the composite business is anchored in certification (PESO approvals, CPRI certification, OMC acceptance, Indian Institute of Packaging quality norms, PCR recycling compliance) — but the repeated admission that prices are reset monthly/quarterly with polymer shows customers do negotiate, which is not toll-road behaviour.

> "newer sizes being developed for which the respective PESO approvals are being pursued" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Operating margin 15%, swings of ±0.8 points, median return on capital 14% — good but not the near-monopoly profile of a toll road.
- ❌ Operating margin of 15% — too thin for a protected franchise.

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 1 check supporting).

**What the calls show:** There is a genuine, itemised and delivered cost culture — solar PPAs across five states now yielding ~INR11 crores annualised with ~1-year payback, unit consolidation, robotic automation, plant siting next to customers to cut logistics, finance cost heading from INR90-100 crores to INR25-30 crores — but management frames these as margin/ROCE levers, not as savings passed to price to win volume, and asset turns are modest with a ~98-day cycle.

> "Power purchase agreements across Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Gujarat and West Bengal have already started generating annualized benefits of about INR11 crores" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹29 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 1.1× a year — not the high-turnover, low-margin machine this pattern needs.

### Innovation Dominance — PARTIAL
*"Out-inventing the competition"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 2 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Product development is regular, named and increasingly commercialised — Type IV CNG/LPG cylinders, 250-litre CNG, oxygen cylinders (approved 2/4/9 kg), hydrogen drone cylinders, 6/9 kg composite fire extinguishers, automated IBC lines, OPz/VRLA batteries — and it shows up in mix (value-added share 20% in FY23 to 29-30% in FY26 at ~18-20% contribution); but R&D spend is never disclosed or quantified, several items (fire extinguisher, 14.2 kg LPG) have slipped for years, and the innovation buys mix, not pricing power.

> "value added products recorded a strong growth of 18%... the contribution of value-added products to total sales increased to 29%, up from 27%" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹29 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — limited pricing headroom.
- ❌ Thin retained margin (29% of sales) alongside 9% yearly growth — the flywheel is incomplete.

## What to watch

- Flow of new LPG tenders from HPCL and BPCL, and whether the board-approved joint venture with private gas distributors actually starts — this is the direct test of the Government Dependency high risk and of the Forward Integrators partial verdict.
- Whether value-added products keep climbing past the current 29-30% of sales, since the Gain in Market Share check and the Innovation Dominance and Cost to Replicate patterns all rest on that segment growing faster than the commodity 75%.
- Cash return on capital rising from today's ₹5 per ₹100, and the 161-day working capital cycle shortening, as the INR370 crores of FY26 capex comes on stream — the Return on Capital (headline) and Cost of Working Capital checks are the two biggest drags on the score.
- Whether revenue growth stops trailing volume growth (currently 15% volume against 11% revenue) — the evidence behind both the Pricing, Mix and Volume check at -1 and the outright Pricing Power no fit, and the mechanism behind the Cyclicality watch.
- Whether the cost programme keeps delivering at the pace shown (solar at about INR11 crores a year, automation, falling finance cost), because under the Good Enough Goods risk that programme is the only thing holding the ~13-15% margin on established packaging in place.
- Any first sign of a structurally different competitor, given that New Entrants was scored No Signal — silence in the calls, not proven safety.
