Now: Capacity raised from 80,000 to 100,000 tonnes and sold at >90% utilisation with FY26 volumes up 20% while global EAF steel output was flat to down — explicit share capture, with headroom against the industry's next-largest 70,000-tonne plants. | The long view had said: HEG runs at 90%+ utilization while global majors sit at 50-55% and Indian peers at 60-65%, and it is the only producer adding capacity, implying continued share capture in a 4-5 player market.

Now: HEG is a cost and scale leader but explicitly a price taker facing 'sustained pricing pressure' and cautious customer procurement — no monopoly-like position in the customer's mind. | The long view had said: Graphite electrodes are a fungible input sold against 3-4 global rivals with no aftermarket or mindshare lock-in; customers freely push back orders and price is set by global supply-demand.

Now: No evidence of switching costs or captive aftermarket; electrodes are a consumable sold into a competitive global market where management stresses cost position rather than lock-in. | The long view had said: No regional monopoly or captive aftermarket — HEG competes on price in 40 export markets, faces antidumping/CVD actions in the US, and orders are re-bookable across suppliers every 3-6 months.

Now: Brownfield expansion from 80,000 to 100,000 tonnes already completed and running at >90% utilisation, with a further 15,000-tonne expansion on track for early 2028, plus a 20,000 TPA anode materials plant — all funded from internal accruals with no long-term debt. | The long view had said: Sustained growth capex — Rs 1,200 cr 80k→100k tons completed and stabilized, a further 15,000-ton expansion targeted for early 2028, plus a Rs 1,750 cr anode plant — but the anode project's own stated payback of 9-10 years without subsidy shows incremental ROIC is not uniformly high.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 4 strengthened, 1 weakened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: FAIL on the long view, FAIL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Cost leadership is the repeated core message in every call of the window — world's largest single-location plant (100,000t vs next two at ~70,000t), 'cost structure amongst the lowest', and industry-highest utilisation of 90%+ sustained through a weak pricing year, with FY26 EBITDA margin still rising 17%→19% on 20% volume growth. (The long view had said: Consistent, verifiable cost leadership: the world's largest single-location electrode plant (100,000t vs next two at ~70,000t), run at >90% utilisation for four straight quarters while foreign peers sat at 50-55% and Indian peers at 60-65%, with FY26 profit growth attributed to 'better control on input costs and focused monitoring on all fixed costs'. It is scale-and-cost advantage in a commodity rather than the 'low price plus differentiation' formula, and unit economics still swing with the cycle (Q4 FY26 operating loss).)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: No brand, heritage, A&P spend, premium-pricing or adjacency-under-the-same-name discussion appears in any of the four calls; the product is sold on cost and availability. (The long view had said: the calls are silent and the numbers are mixed (1 of 2 checks supporting).)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Technology language exists only in the to-be-demerged Greentech arm (silicon/graphene anode doping, in-house BMS with academic partners); for the core electrode business no R&D spend, pipeline or named new products are disclosed in the last 12 months. (The long view had said: There is genuine product-development effort in the graphite-anode venture — an 18-month pilot plant testing 28 raw-material permutations and claiming to beat global spec — but no R&D spend is ever disclosed, no named product launches with revenue impact, and the core electrode business is described purely in cost/scale terms.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Management explicitly frames the 80,000→100,000t expansion running at 90%+ utilisation as share gain while EAF steel output is flat, backed by FY26 volumes +20% and revenue Rs 2,153cr→Rs 2,569cr, with a further 115,000t expansion targeted for early 2028 — and the gain came with margin expansion, not degradation. (The long view had said: Volume grew 20% in FY26 while global crude steel output fell ~2%, and HEG is expanding 80k→100k→115k tons when 'there is nobody who has even announced anything as yet' — but management explicitly refuses to disclose market-share numbers, so the gain is inferred from utilisation and capacity rather than stated.)

**That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The four calls discuss only electrode tonnage, utilisation and realisations; there is no mention of service contracts, AMC, subscriptions, attach rates, renewals or customer advances funding the business. (The long view had said: the calls are silent and the numbers are mixed (1 of 3 checks supporting).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 2 worsened, 4 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Electrodes are a substitutable industrial input where sustained pricing pressure is the recurring complaint and management declines to disclose realisation on competitive grounds, deflecting an analyst who pressed on it and on the UHP/HP mix — a sign price, not brand, is the battleground. The defence offered is cost position rather than any protected premium. (The long view had said: Pricing is set by industry supply-demand rather than brand: announced hikes repeatedly fail to stick when demand is weak, and HEG's own defence is cost leadership rather than a defensible premium — it competes as the low-cost producer against larger Western peers running at 50-55% utilisation.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No single customer, licensor or geography is named as driving results in any of the four calls; management repeatedly cites customer diversification and roughly two-thirds of sales exported to 30-35 countries. The only supplier-side flag is a passing mention of needle coke cost inflation, not dependence on a named counterparty. (The long view had said: Customer side is genuinely broad (70-75% exports to 35-40 countries for 25-30 years, ~20% Middle East absorbed without volume loss during the war), but the input side is narrow: needle coke is bought from a handful of suppliers on quarter-by-quarter negotiation and the electrode-to-needle-coke spread is repeatedly named as the driver of margins.)

**That worsened:**

- **New Entrants**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: On the core side, sustained low-price Chinese supply is the recurring pressure on realisations, though management points to industry supply discipline holding; on the new Greentech side HEG is entering a field where more than 150 GWh of Indian cell capacity has already been announced, so the anode-materials and BESS pillars will be built into a crowding entrant field rather than an established position. (The long view had said: The calls affirmatively describe a four-to-five player global oligopoly with multi-year build times and no announced new capacity outside HEG's own; competitive pressure comes from existing peers' idle capacity and Chinese steel exports, not from a structurally different entrant.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: NO SIGNAL → LOW. Why now: Demand is industrial and functional, and the one preference shift management discusses — end-market and regulatory preference for low-carbon steel — expands rather than erodes HEG's demand base by pushing steelmakers toward the EAF route that consumes its product. (The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and the only computed fingerprint is clean.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
