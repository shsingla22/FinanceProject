# Hindustan Petroleum Corporation Ltd. (HINDPETRO) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: ENERGY*

HPCL is an Indian state-linked oil refining and marketing company: it buys crude oil, refines it into fuels like petrol (MS), diesel, LPG cooking gas, bitumen and lubricants, and sells them across India to motorists, households and industrial customers. It earns money on two legs — the refining margin between crude cost and product prices, and the marketing margin on selling through its own network of roughly 22,000 retail fuel outlets and to about 9.3 crore LPG customers. It runs refineries at Mumbai (expanded to 9.5 million tonnes) and Visakhapatnam (expanded from 8.3 to 15 million tonnes), plus a new refinery-cum-petrochemical complex in Barmer, Rajasthan, and moves product through its own pipeline and terminal network. Scale is large and volatile: it sold over 13 million tonnes in a single quarter, posted quarterly revenue of about INR 123,000 crores and a record FY26 standalone profit of INR 17,175 crores, but swung to a loss the very next quarter when crude prices and supply chains turned against it.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Refining** — Turns crude into fuels at its Mumbai, Visakhapatnam and now Rajasthan (HRRL) refineries — roughly 6.6 million tonnes processed in a quarter at over 100% capacity utilisation, and management treats widening the crude basket and lifting throughput as a core profit lever.
- **Marketing (retail and direct sales)** — Sells petrol, diesel and other fuels through about 22,000 retail outlets nationwide plus direct supply to industrial customers; management runs programmes like 'Abhyuday' to push more volume through 4,500 selected pumps and says it has been gaining market share against private competitors.
- **LPG (cooking gas)** — Supplies bottled cooking gas to roughly 9.3 crore customers — described as a mass-market, home-delivery business, with a new premium on-demand variant 'HP Navya' being piloted in cities through partners like Swiggy.
- **Pipelines and logistics** — Owns pipelines and import terminals (Hassan–Cherlapally, Vijayawada–Dharmapuri, Mangalore import terminal and LPG cavern) used to move crude and products cheaply — framed as a way to cut logistics cost rather than a separate revenue engine.
- **Petrochemicals** — Sells petrochemical products under its own brand; a small presence today that management expects to become substantial once the Rajasthan refinery's petchem section (73% complete in Aug 2025) ramps up by end-FY27.
- **Natural gas** — Covers the gas chain end to end — a 5 million tonne LNG storage and regasification terminal at Chhara, a share in gas transmission pipelines, city gas distribution in 23 geographies, and direct gas sales to industry, backed by long-term supply deals such as a 10-year contract with ADNOC from 2028.
- **Biofuels / green business** — Described as the country's largest blender of ethanol into petrol (about 12% blending), with two sugar mills in Bihar, compressed biogas (CBG) plants starting in Uttar Pradesh, and green hydrogen at Vizag — being housed in a wholly owned green-business subsidiary.
- **Lubricants** — A lubricants business that management said was being carved out into a separate company, pending Government of India approvals as a PSU.
- **Refinery investments (HMEL, MRPL)** — Stakes in two other refining companies that feed into consolidated profit — they lifted FY26 consolidated PAT to INR 18,047 crores versus INR 17,175 crores standalone.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Hindustan Petroleum comes out of these three reviews as a well-run company sitting in a business that will not let it keep what it earns. The management scores are the strongest part of the record: the candor check scores a full 2 because the company admits its own failures rather than dressing them up — it called Abhyuday 1.0 a failure on the record, disclosed the HRRL fire and the RUF catalyst clogging promptly, withheld guidance rather than inventing one, and flagged coming Q1 losses upfront. The promises it does make have been beaten: the Samriddhi cost programme promised ₹1,000 crore, was raised to ₹1,500 crore and delivered ₹1,691 crore, with operating cost per tonne falling from ₹1,438 to ₹1,344, and debt-to-equity came in at 0.8 against guidance of 1.1. Headline returns look strong too — ₹30 of operating cash for every ₹100 of capital employed — and only about 14 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. That is why the management area scores 0.83 and return on capital 0.67, the two highest scores in the whole framework.

The trouble is that almost every strength here doubles as a weakness once you ask who sets the price. The physical network — roughly 22,000 fuel outlets, 9.3 crore LPG customers and owned pipelines like Hassan–Cherlapally and Vijayawada–Dharmapuri — scores 2 on distribution advantage and is what makes Forward Integrators a likely fit and Cost to Replicate a partial one: a refiner that owns the whole road to the customer, backed by a ₹73,000 crore refinery that still took a decade to build. But owning the road does not mean setting the toll. The genuine cost culture that makes Low Price Plus a likely fit is undercut in the same breath, because the low price is set by government and by refining margins, not chosen by the company. The same fact drives the risk review's dominant finding: Government Dependency is the one HIGH RISK, with LPG sold below cost at ₹167 a cylinder, under-recoveries of ₹10,900 crore in FY25 and ₹2,148 crore in Q1 FY26 waiting on a cabinet compensation decision, and even the FY27 capex figure described as agreed with the Government of India. It is also why four of the eleven patterns — Pricing Power, Toll Roads, Recurring Revenue and Friendly Middleman — are flat no-fits, why the company keeps only ₹13 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs, why network effects score -2, and why competitive advantage is the only negative area score at -0.33. HPCL even buys 21-34% of the product it sells from rival refiners, which tells you how little the product itself differentiates.

The third thread is timing, and it cuts both ways. Earnings swing violently — a record ₹17,175 crore standalone profit in FY26, up 133%, then an outright loss in Q1 FY27 as Brent moved from $110 to $85 to $96 — which is why Cyclicality is rated ELEVATED. The same volatility is what makes the cyclical market growth check score positive: the company is visibly at a trough with new capacity in HRRL and the RUF unit ramping into any recovery, though nobody can say how long the trough lasts. The growth capex that should power that recovery is also where execution has been weakest — that check scores only 1, because HRRL ran years late and was then hit by a fire, the RUF unit suffered catalyst clogging, and capex is now being deferred. Supply is the other fragile link: term crude contracts sitting on the far side of the Strait of Hormuz delivered almost nothing in Q1 FY27, even as the company has broadened its crude basket. The remaining risks — energy transition, private entrants like Reliance and Nayara, commodity sameness, shifting delivery habits — all sit at WATCH rather than acute, and only fashion risk is genuinely LOW. Netting it out, the combined rating is 46 out of 100, or Mixed, from 57 on business quality, 26 on multibagger fit and 50 on risk safety, with underlying resilience judged SOUND. In plain terms: an honest, cost-disciplined operator with an irreplaceable distribution network, whose earnings belong more to crude prices and cabinet decisions than to anything management does.

Whether the cabinet actually compensates the LPG under-recoveries (₹10,900 crore in FY25, ₹2,148 crore in Q1 FY26) — this is the single biggest swing factor under the HIGH RISK Government Dependency finding.

Whether HRRL and the RUF unit run reliably after the fire and catalyst clogging, and whether deferred capex restarts — the weak point behind the growth capex check scoring only 1.

Debt-to-equity drifting back up from 0.8x toward the old 2.33x — the headroom the Cyclicality risk relies on as its silver lining.

Whether Samriddhi-style savings keep coming after the ₹1,691 crore delivered — the sole evidence supporting the Low Price Plus pattern.

Whether Abhyuday 2.0's 100-150bps outperformance across 4,900 outlets turns into real share gain against Reliance and Nayara, rather than the conceded volume growth of FY26 — the test for both Market Share Gainers and the New Entrants risk.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

**Overall:** the company partly passes the foundation every multibagger shares (steady cash + high returns on capital + growth), 2 of 3 testable checks passing, and fits 2 of the 11 patterns: Low Price Plus, Forward Integrators.

### Low Price Plus — LIKELY FIT
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and the only computable check supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** A genuine and now-delivered cost culture exists under Kaushal — Samriddhi promised ₹1,000cr, revised to ₹1,500cr and delivered ₹1,691cr, with opex/MT falling from ₹1,438 to ₹1,344 and opex/turnover from 1.54% to 1.45% — but the low price is set by government/industry pricing, not by a hard-to-copy cost advantage passed to customers, and Q1 FY27 losses show the cost edge does not protect returns.

> "in FY '25, the number was 1,438 per metric ton, and this has dropped to 1,344" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹13 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 2.2× a year with 19% returns on capital — thin margins made up on volume, the low-price-winner profile.

### Forward Integrators — LIKELY FIT
*"Owns the shop, not just the product"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern, but the numbers push back (0 of 2 checks supporting) — held one step below a full confirmation.

**What the calls show:** HPCL is a refiner that owns the route to the customer end-to-end — ~22,000 retail outlets and 9.3 crore LPG customers in Nov 2023, dealer programmes and non-fuel retail thereafter, and by Jul 2026 a direct/quick-commerce LPG channel via Swiggy scaling to 200-250 cities, with self-sufficiency in product by year-end tying refining to its own shelf space.

> "In Bangalore, we are piloting it with Swiggy... pan-India, about 200 to 250 cities by Diwali" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Operating margin moved from about 4% (early years) to 6% (recent years) — no widening trend.
- ❌ Operating margin of 7% — too thin for a protected franchise.

### Brand Strength — PARTIAL
*"The name itself sells"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 2 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Real brand assets exist — lubricant market leadership (~650 TMT), own-brand petrochemical marketing, a top-20 India brand ranking, and the HP Navya premium LPG adjacency launched Jul 2026 — but there is no evidence of premium pricing or sustained A&P leverage translating brand into margin.

> "very proud that we have been listed in top 20 brands in India across all sectors" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹13 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — limited pricing headroom.
- ❌ Operating margin of 7% — too thin for a protected franchise.

### Innovation Dominance — PARTIAL
*"Out-inventing the competition"*
