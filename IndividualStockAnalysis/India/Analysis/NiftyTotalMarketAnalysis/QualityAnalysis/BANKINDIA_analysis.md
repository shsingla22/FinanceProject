# Bank of India (BANKINDIA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

Bank of India is an Indian commercial bank that takes deposits from the public and lends the money out at higher interest rates — the gap between the two (net interest income) is its main source of earnings. It serves individuals, farmers, small and medium businesses (MSMEs) and large corporates, offering savings and current accounts, term deposits, home, education, vehicle and personal loans, farm credit, MSME working-capital loans and big-ticket corporate finance. Alongside interest, it earns fee and other 'non-interest' income from services such as payments, cards, cash management, and gains on its treasury/investment book. It is mainly a domestic Indian bank — roughly Rs.8.25 lakh crore of its Rs.9.58 lakh crore of deposits are domestic as of June 2026 — with overseas operations including Kenya and a presence at GIFT City IFSC in Gujarat; total 'global business' (deposits plus advances) was about Rs.17.55 lakh crore in June 2026, and net profit was Rs.10,527 crore for FY26.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Deposits (CASA and term deposits)** — Collecting money from customers through low-cost current and savings accounts (CASA) plus fixed/retail term deposits, which funds all its lending; global deposits stood at Rs.9.58 lakh crore in June 2026 with CASA of Rs.3.02 lakh crore (a 36.68% CASA ratio, down from ~44% in 2022), and management repeatedly calls building this low-cost deposit franchise its top priority — including new Zonal Deposit Centres at every zonal office.
- **RAM advances (Retail, Agriculture and MSME)** — Small-ticket lending to individuals, farmers and small businesses — home, education, vehicle and personal loans, farm credit, MUDRA and MSME working capital — which is the bank's declared growth engine, at Rs.3.93 lakh crore in June 2026, up ~20% year-on-year and making up 58.30% of all advances.
- **Corporate advances** — Larger loans to companies (manufacturing, infrastructure, roads and similar), the remainder of the loan book outside RAM (roughly 40%); management says it continues to onboard new corporate clients but the emphasis has shifted toward RAM, and its new Strategic Business Branch in Mumbai targets pool buyouts, co-lending, TReDS and supply-chain financing.
- **Treasury / investments** — Managing the bank's own portfolio of securities, which produces trading gains or mark-to-market losses that swing non-interest income — e.g. a Rs.295 crore MTM loss partly offset by Rs.147 crore of security sales in the June 2022 quarter, and a drop in treasury income that pulled down operating profit that year.
- **Non-interest / fee income** — Fees and charges from everyday banking services, cards, payments and collections, plus recoveries from written-off loans; it came to Rs.9,874 crore in FY26 (up 10%) versus Rs.25,172 crore of net interest income, so it is a meaningful but secondary earnings stream.
- **Digital banking and payments** — Online and app-based banking through the BOI Omni Neo app and an e-platform for straight-through loan and account opening, UPI payments, QR sound-boxes, virtual RuPay debit cards, digital savings accounts, Digital Banking Units and Bharat Connect biller services for corporates — presented as an efficiency and customer-acquisition channel rather than a separate profit line.
- **International operations** — Overseas branches and units — the calls specifically mention a Kenya business with its own mobile app and M-PESA integration, and an investment with six other banks in CCIL-IFSC Ltd for foreign-currency settlement at GIFT City; the gap between 'global' and 'domestic' numbers implies roughly Rs.1.3 lakh crore of overseas deposits and Rs.1.2 lakh crore of overseas advances in June 2026.
- **Subsidiaries and associates (RRBs, BOI Services Ltd)** — The bank sponsors three Regional Rural Banks (which have been given e-commerce-enabled debit cards) and owns BOI Services Ltd, which is being used to put dedicated field staff on the street to revamp cash management services and pull in deposits.
- **Asset quality / recovery function** — A large ongoing effort to control and recover bad loans — restructured COVID-era books, slippage containment and recovery from written-off accounts (about Rs.40,000 crore of write-offs mentioned in 2024) — which has taken gross NPAs from 9.30% in June 2022 to 1.81% in June 2026 and net NPAs to 0.51%, directly driving the profit growth management highlights.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** As a PSU bank, fortunes are set by policy at almost every line: RBI's LCR draft, DCCO and ECL norms directly hit margins, CET-1 and credit cost (management sizes ECL at 0.5% p.a. of capital for five years); growth products are government schemes (PMAY, Mudra, PM-Surya Ghar, ECLGS 5.0 worth Rs.10-12k crore of new credit); pricing is repo/EBLR-benchmarked; deposit raising targets government and PSU accounts; even FCNR(B)/OFCB fundraising depends on RBI windows and hedging subsidy, and PSU merger speculation is answered with 'it is only for the Government to answer'.

> "we expect to fund around 10-12 thousand crores under the ECLGS scheme" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: None material — diversification is within a wholly regulated business; the government guarantee on ECLGS cushions credit risk but deepens policy dependence*
