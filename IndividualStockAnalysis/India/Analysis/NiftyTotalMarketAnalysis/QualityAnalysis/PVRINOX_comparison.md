**Checks that improved:**

Margins have moved about ±3.5 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹32 is operating profit. - **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Neutral. Now: No acquisitions in the window at all — the only portfolio action was a disciplined all-cash divestment of the non-core 4700BC snacking brand, which is prudent but gives no evidence of bolt-on M&A skill. | The long view had said: The defining transaction was the transformational PVR-INOX merger — the opposite of a small complementary bolt-on — and post-deal margins deteriorated (FY25 loss of INR152 crores, 72 screen exits) before recovering, with subsequent activity being divestment (4700BC to Marico) and portfolio rationalisation.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: Much of the profit surge is attributable to an industry-wide record box office rather than company skill, but the margin achieved at 350-400bps lower occupancy than pre-COVID is genuine self-help that survives the discount. | The long view had said: FY26's record results ride an exogenous content cycle — the industry itself hit an all-time high with Bollywood up 55% — so results substantially reflect the slate rather than proprietary management skill, making the halo risk high.

Now: Textbook stewardship in the window: leverage eliminated to a net cash position, capex intensity cut, loss-making screens exited and non-core assets sold, with no large M&A. | The long view had said: Since the merger management has cut net debt ~90% to a net cash position, exited loss-making screens, held cost discipline for several years and pivoted to capital-light growth with no further large M&A.

Now: There is a mild flywheel where scale makes the circuit the preferred partner for producers and developers, but no genuinely self-reinforcing two-sided network that strengthens with each additional user. | The long view had said: No two-sided network is evident; scale brings buying and booking leverage but audiences derive no benefit from other audiences, and the subscription programme piloted in 2022 is never referenced again as a network asset.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 5. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 5 weakened, 5 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Promises made early in the window were delivered later in it: net debt fell 619cr (Sep-25) to 365cr (Dec-25) to 161cr (Mar-26) to a net cash 80cr (Jun-26), the capital-light pivot hit its stated mix, loss-making screens were exited, non-core 4700BC was divested, and management discloses awkward items promptly (weak Q3 advertising, the 44.6cr labour-code provision) while measuring itself on ROCE (10.2%) under long-tenured founder leadership. (The long view had said: The FY24-FY26 promises were delivered — free cash flow, debt down ~90% to a net cash INR80 crore, ROCE to 10.2%, and honest closure of 70+ loss-making legacy screens — but the earlier record is weaker (Jul 2022's 'debt will not rise' preceded a leveraged post-merger period) and senior leadership has churned heavily, with CFO, chairman and MD roles all turning over across FY21-FY25.)

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Across FY26 and Q1 FY27, ATP and SPH rose 8-10% while admissions simultaneously grew 8-10%, which is price realisation without volume loss; but the increase is partly premium-format mix, the GST cut was fully passed through rather than retained, and Q2 FY26 ATP grew only 2%. (The long view had said: Across the full period ATP and SPH rise every year without losing volume — FY26 ATP INR280 (+8%) and SPH INR147 (+10%) alongside 10% footfall growth, and Q1 FY27 again +8%/+9% — driven by explicit F&B price hikes (70% value / 30% volume), AI-assisted dynamic pricing and premium formats.)
- **Brand Strength**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Management points to brand trust, premium formats and preferred-partner status with mall developers and producers alongside 31% box office share at above-industry ticket prices; however no heritage narrative, A&P spend or measured brand-driven share gain is disclosed in these calls. (The long view had said: By FY26 management frames the company as a scale consumer brand — ~40% of India's multiplex screens and 31% of box office, preferred partner for mall developers and producers — and explicitly monetises the brand through franchising, while the brand also carries adjacencies (live sport, concerts, alternate content).)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: LIKELY FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: The four calls discuss ticket, F&B and advertising income only — no licence fees, subscriptions, AMC/service contracts, attach rates, renewals or deferred revenue as a funding source are mentioned, so the evidence is silent. (The long view had said: The only genuinely recurring streams discussed are small and late-arriving: FOCO management fee income (~INR10 crore in FY26, run-rate INR13-14 crore) and a subscription programme that was piloted in Jul 2022 but never resurfaces as a disclosed revenue line; no attach rates, renewal rates or deferred-revenue-as-funding discussion appears across the 15 calls.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Scale leadership is explicit — ~40% of India's multiplex screens and 31% of box office, with ~100 gross screen adds a year and 44% of FY26 additions in underpenetrated South India — but the calls give a share level, not a rising share series versus named competitors. (The long view had said: Share is high and management cites 40% of multiplex screens and 31% of box office, with expansion into underpenetrated South India and Tier 2/3 towns, but the step-change came from the INOX merger rather than organic gain, and an analyst notes footfalls stayed flat at 14-15 crore on a rolling basis despite ~100 more screens since the merger.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Cost discipline is real (18% EBITDA margins at ~28% occupancy vs 350-400bps higher pre-COVID, merger synergies), but the model is premium rather than low-price: ATP and SPH are at record highs and rising 8-10%, and growth comes from premiumisation, not passing savings into price. (The long view had said: There is a real, sustained cost culture — renegotiated rentals, unit-level electricity control, capital-light capex intensity down 24% — plus deliberate cheap weekday/morning/front-seat pricing for value seekers, but the core strategy is premiumisation, not low price with high asset turns.)

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Fashion Risk**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Demand in any given quarter rests on whether particular titles catch fire — management attributes results to specific hits (Saiyaara, Mahavatar Narsimha, Dhurandhar, Kantara Chapter 1, Su from So) and its FY27 confidence rests on an unproven slate (Ramayana, King, Avengers: Doomsday). The exposure is bounded because it is a rotating portfolio of dozens of titles across languages rather than one taste bet, and the underlying cinema-going occasion is repeat-purchase. (The long view had said: Demand rests entirely on shifting audience taste in films and stars — the 2022-24 stretch saw Hindi cinema underperform while South content and re-releases carried the load, and management explicitly pinned hopes on 'Ranbir Kapoor, Ranveer Singh, Shahrukh Khan, Salman Khan' returning; that reversed sharply with Bollywood up 55% in FY26. The company owns no brand that can go uncool, and the wide portfolio of ~1,500 releases a year spreads the taste risk.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Results swing violently with the film release calendar rather than any capex cycle: FY26 EBITDA doubled to INR968 crores with margins going 8.4%→14.4% and PAT swinging from a INR152 crore loss to a INR386 crore profit, while Q3 advertising fell simply because there were four marketable films versus eight a year earlier. The last-12-month calls are an unambiguous upcycle peak (all-time-high box office in CY2025 and FY26), so current margins cannot be treated as a normalised base. (The long view had said: Not a commodity or customer-capex cycle, but earnings swing violently with the film slate — FY25 Q2 EBITDA of INR207cr vs INR447cr a year earlier, a FY25 loss of INR152cr, then a record FY26 with EBITDA doubling to INR968cr; management itself frames the business as hits and misses against a heavy fixed rent base. The pivot to capital-light/FOCO growth and screen exits shows they are deliberately reducing the over-investment leg of the cycle.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Technological Innovation (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
