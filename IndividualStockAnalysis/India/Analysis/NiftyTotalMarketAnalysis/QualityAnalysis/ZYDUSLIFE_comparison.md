### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 7 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 5 weakened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: India branded formulations outgrew the market in all four quarters (9%, 9%, 14%, 15%) with chronic mix rising 43.7%→46.3% (620bps over three years), oncology leadership retained, and management claims a top-3 position in US generics — share gained on rising margins (FY26 EBITDA 31.2%, +80bps) rather than bought. (The long view had said: Share gain is quantified and repeated across the whole 2022-2026 arc: India branded moved from lagging (Aug 2022) to beating IPM 'for yet another quarter' at 14-15% with chronic mix rising 41.2%→45.3%→46.3% (+620bps in 3 years), US volume share gains lifted the business past US$1bn to top-3 in generics, and international markets grew 38-45% — all while margins rose, so the share was not bought.)

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Promises made in Q1/Q2 FY26 ('committed to achieve our targeted top line growth and profitability') were delivered by Q4 ('ahead of our expectations… highest ever operating profit'), with long-tenured leadership (Sharvil Patel, Ganesh Nayak) and a conservative balance sheet (net cash Rs 56.3bn in Q1; net debt/EBITDA 0.5x after heavy M&A); no instance of management owning a mistake appears in the window. (The long view had said: Promises made on old calls were delivered on later ones: the Aug 2022 pledge of '20% plus EBITDA margins for FY23' was met and then exceeded (27.5% FY24, 31.2% FY26), the FY25 guidance of maintaining 27.5% and 30+ launches was honoured, and leadership has been stable (Sharvil Patel MD, Ganesh Nayak on every call 2022-2026) with a conservative balance sheet — net cash in FY24 and only 0.5x net debt/EBITDA in FY26 despite several acquisitions; FDA 483 observations were disclosed promptly rather than buried.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The India branded formulations business (37% of revenue, growing 9-15% ahead of market) is inherently prescription-led with doctors choosing and patients paying, and MedTech is explicitly sold around surgeons; but the calls never describe trade training programmes or channel-relationship investment that would make the lock-in explicit. (The long view had said: The India branded formulations business (consistently 14-15% year-on-year growth, outperforming IPM) is prescription-led through doctors, with super-speciality leadership in nephrology/oncology, and the May 2026 call describes engagement around the prescriber plus patient support programmes; MedTech (Amplitude, Andy robotic system) is sold to surgeons. But the calls never describe trade training programmes or channel investment as an explicit moat.)
- **Pricing Power**: LIKELY FIT → NO FIT. Why now: The FY26 calls show the opposite of pricing power in the core businesses: US growth is attributed to volume and new launches against 'increased competitive intensity in key products', and new India launches are pitched on lower therapy costs rather than price increases. (The long view had said: Pricing power is real only in pockets — consumer wellness gross margin expanded 377bps on 'calibrated price increase' and management now talks of premiumisation — while the US generics engine (roughly 40-47% of revenue) is structurally deflationary with erosion offset by volume and launches.)
- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The Comfort Click acquisition (Q2 FY26) is an explicit move toward the end consumer through e-commerce/D2C, extended to Poland, Finland and Portugal by Q3; but there is no own-store, franchise-royalty or D2C revenue-share disclosure for the core pharma business. (The long view had said: The Comfort Click/WeightWorld acquisition is an explicit move to a digital-first D2C wellness platform across UK/EU/US, and Sentynl gives owned front-end commercialisation in US rare disease; but there is no own-store count, franchise royalty, or D2C revenue-share disclosure.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls management discusses product revenues, launches and acquisitions; there is no mention of attach rates, service/AMC contracts, renewals, subscriptions, installed base or customer advances funding the business. (The long view had said: Across all 17 calls the revenue discussion is product sales (generics, branded formulations, consumer brands, devices); no attach rates, AMC/service share, renewal rates or deferred revenue/customer advances as a funding source are ever discussed.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (STRONG FIT); Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 5 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The calls show a continuous, small-scale value-adding innovation cadence rather than an installed base going legacy: 505(b)(2) launches (Beizray), rare-disease approvals (Zycubo), world-first Nivolumab biosimilar, first indigenous Aflibercept, trivalent flu vaccine, semaglutide pens, ADC Phase III and Saroglitazar NDA. No analyst or management discussion of a competing platform threatening the existing asset base; AI is described as an internal efficiency tool. (The long view had said: The core is a small-molecule generic base (top-3 US generic player) exposed to the industry-wide shift toward biologics, biosimilars and GLP-1s, which is why management is buying its way in (Pembrolizumab/Ranibizumab licences, a US biologics facility, Nivolumab and Aflibercept biosimilars, semaglutide, robotic surgery). The response so far is a steady cadence of incremental, funded innovation rather than a defensive scramble.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No single customer, licensor or supplier is named as driving results in any of the four calls; growth is repeatedly described as broad-based across geographies and segments, and FY26 added whole new revenue legs (Comfort Click, Amplitude, Sentynl rare disease, international formulations up 38–45%). (The long view had said: Results are repeatedly driven by a handful of named products rather than named customers — Asacol/Mesalamine, Revlimid and Mirabegron dominate analyst questioning every year, with Revlimid now running off and Mirabegron's exclusivity riding on a jury trial; the new bio-CDMO leg similarly rests on one partner, Agenus BOT/BAL. Breadth is improving fast, but individual product cliffs still move the P&L.)

**That worsened:**

- **Fashion Risk**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The fastest-growing consumer piece is explicitly trend-exposed — gummies, collagen, Shilajit resin, sports nutrition, pet VMS — and management credits growth to launching into 'high-growth wellness categories', where taste can move without warning; consumer wellness was roughly a fifth of Q4 FY26 revenue. Exposure is bounded because the bulk of the group is prescription and chronic pharma. (The long view had said: The demand base is functional and repeat-purchase — prescriptions in cardiology, oncology, nephrology and dermatology, with growth credited to therapy focus and pipeline rather than any trending product or celebrity moment. The only taste-exposed items (gummies, shilajit resin) sit inside a broad multi-brand wellness portfolio.)

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
