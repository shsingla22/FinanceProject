# Bajaj Finserv Ltd. (BAJAJFINSV) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

Bajaj Finserv Limited (BFS) is an Indian holding company that owns and runs a group of financial-services businesses, selling insurance, loans and other money products to Indian retail customers, small and large businesses, and government-sponsored schemes. It does not really sell anything itself; its income is the consolidated income of its subsidiaries — premiums and investment income from its general (non-life) and life insurance arms, and interest income from its lending arms — with the parent company's own standalone results described as small and only discussed 'where material'. Its consolidated total income was about INR 38,508 crore in the March 2026 quarter with consolidated profit after tax of about INR 2,539 crore. It operates in India, with premium accounted under Indian GAAP for the insurers and Ind AS at group level; a long-running joint venture with Germany's Allianz SE ended when Bajaj bought out Allianz's 23% stake in January 2026 and the insurers bought back the residual 3% in March 2026, making both insurance businesses 100% Bajaj-owned.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Bajaj General Insurance Limited (earlier Bajaj Allianz General Insurance, 'BAGIC')** — The non-life insurer, selling motor, retail and group health, crop, commercial lines (fire, marine, engineering, liability) and travel insurance through bancassurance and agency channels; it is one of the two core businesses the call spends most time on, ranked first among private players on premium in Q3 FY26 with quarterly gross written premium of about INR 7,389 crore, investment assets of roughly INR 35,500–36,400 crore, and a combined ratio management repeatedly claims is among the best in the multi-line market.
- **Bajaj Life Insurance Limited (earlier Bajaj Allianz Life Insurance, 'BALIC')** — The life insurer, selling savings and protection policies — par, non-par savings, term, annuity and unit-linked (ULIP) plans — to individuals; it is the other core business, with gross written premium of about INR 9,237 crore in the March 2025 quarter and value of new business of INR 549 crore, and from FY25 has been running a 'Bajaj Life 2.0' strategy shifting from fast growth to 'sustainable and profitable growth'.
- **Bajaj Finance Limited (BFL)** — The group's lending business and, on the numbers given, the largest profit contributor — total income of about INR 11,917 crore and profit after tax of INR 4,480 crore in the March 2025 quarter at roughly 19% return on equity — though it holds its own separate earnings call, so BFS management takes only 'high-level' questions on it.
- **Bajaj Housing Finance Limited (BHFL)** — A home-loan/housing finance subsidiary described as another 'material' subsidiary that, like Bajaj Finance, reports on its own call, so the BFS call gives it almost no separate detail.
- **Bajaj Finserv Direct Limited** — One of the group's 'emerging companies', run by its own MD/CEO who joins the call from FY25 onwards; the excerpts name it as part of the emerging-business grouping but give no size figures.
- **Bajaj Finserv Health Limited** — Another 'emerging company' in the group with its own MD/CEO on the calls from FY25; the excerpts identify it as a health-related venture within the group but provide no financial detail.
- **Bajaj Finserv Asset Management Limited (Bajaj Finserv AMC)** — The group's asset-management (fund) arm, the newest of the 'emerging companies' — its MD appears on the calls from FY26 — again with no size disclosed in these excerpts.
- **Bajaj Finserv Limited standalone (the parent)** — The listed holding entity itself, which prepares the group's Ind AS accounts and holds the stakes in the operating companies (for example 77.33% of each insurer after the Allianz buyout); its standalone results are discussed only 'where material', implying they are small next to the subsidiaries.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Bajaj Finserv comes out of the three reviews as a genuinely well-run group that happens to sit in a hard industry. The quality framework's best marks go to distribution (+2), described as the clearest durable edge through multiline agency, an unusually diversified bancassurance base, and direct and digital reach; to end markets that are clearly still expanding, with lending assets under management compounding from Rs 2.3 lakh crore in FY23 to Rs 5.1 lakh crore in FY26 on a 10-crore customer franchise; and to a management team willing to stay with a plan for years, including a five-year effort to lift policy renewals and the BALIC 2.0 product restructuring that was carried through at the cost of near-term topline. Those same traits are what the multibagger screen picks up: Corporate Culture is the one STRONG FIT, resting on unchanged senior leadership across all ten earnings calls and on bad news being disclosed and quantified rather than buried, and Market Share Gainers is a LIKELY FIT backed by named, repeated gains — life insurance new-business share rising from 6.4% to 7.4% of the private market and growing 22% against an industry 12%, general insurance growing 12% against an industry 9% in FY25 and ranking first among private players on premium in Q3 FY26. The combined rating of 54 out of 100 — 'Decent' — is built from a reasonable business-quality score of 61, a much weaker multibagger fit of 41, and a middling risk-safety score of 56.

In several places the very thing that creates a strength is also what creates a risk, and the records say so directly. Distribution is the top-scoring advantage and it supports the Friendly Middleman pattern, with partner counts rising from 96 to 103 and explicit spending on agent training — but that same reliance on intermediaries who control where a policy is placed is the core of the 'Good Enough Goods' risk, where general insurance is described as largely price-shopped and pricing pressure runs through the whole period. Scale is the second example: the growth in assets under management that drives the Market Share Gainers verdict is the same balance-sheet weight that earns a -2 on asset turnover, where each Rs 1 of assets produces only Rs 0.20 of sales, and it sits alongside a -2 on return on capital, where the business generated negative Rs 11 of operating cash for every Rs 100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings this year. Regulation is the third: the BALIC 2.0 restructuring that the framework praises as evidence of tenacity was itself forced by new surrender rules, and Government Dependency is the single HIGH RISK — the IRDAI '1/n' rule made FY25 figures non-comparable and inflated the claims-and-costs ratio, with a crop insurance court order and a labour-code charge as further examples of outside decisions resetting reported numbers.

What is missing is as informative as what is present, and it explains why the multibagger fit is the weakest of the three scores. Two patterns are outright NO FIT. Pricing Power fails because the record shows the opposite — motor business given up in large cities on price in FY23, group health pricing 'continuously under pressure', and crop and motor exposure cut in Q4 FY26 on elevated pricing pressure. Toll Roads fails because insurance premium is the customer's whole purchase, negotiated continuously and won through tenders in crop and government health. Seven other patterns land only at PARTIAL, including recurring revenue, cost discipline, innovation and cost-to-replicate — each has real evidence behind it (renewal premium growth of 29%, 20.9% and 18% across recent quarters; operating costs falling as a share of income at both lending arms; a health rider passing 10 lakh members) but none rises to a clean fit. The area scores line up with this: industry structure at -0.5 and return on capital at -1.0 drag against competitive advantage at +1.3 and management at +1.0, and overall resilience is judged STRAINED. Three risks sit on WATCH — earnings swinging on investment mark-to-market, with consolidated profit up 5% as reported in Q4 FY26 versus 24% excluding those marks; technology spending on AI and a software migration that pressured cost ratios and cut revenue at Bajaj Markets; and new-format competitors, with standalone health insurers growing retail health 32% against the group's 11.2%. The clearest good news on risk is that the largest relationship exposure, the Allianz joint venture, was resolved on the group's own terms without operational disruption, leaving stakeholder concentration rated LOW.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

**Overall:** the company partly passes the foundation every multibagger shares (steady cash + high returns on capital + growth), 1 of 3 testable checks passing, and fits 3 of the 11 patterns: Corporate Culture, Brand Strength, Market Share Gainers.

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** Leadership is strikingly stable across all ten calls (Sreenivasan, Singhel, Chugh, Sahni), bad news is disclosed promptly and quantified rather than buried — the Osmanabad crop court order, the Labor Code hit, the accelerated ECL provision, MTM losses, persistency dips and the planned Bajaj Markets revenue de-growth — and promises get delivered: the Q1 FY23 pledge to fix motor loss ratios showed up in Q2/Q3 FY23, and BALIC 2.0 launched in H2 FY25 produced record VNB and 24.5% NBM by Q4 FY26. Balance sheets stay conservative (solvency 266–359%, Tier-1 above 20%).

> "If you recall in Q1, when we had a spike in our loss ratios, we ha d said that we will be taking corrective action to bring down the loss ratios . And typically... these take six to nine months to play out" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Brand Strength — LIKELY FIT
*"The name itself sells"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and the only computable check supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** The Bajaj name is stretched across adjacencies — Finserv Health, Bajaj Markets, Bajaj Finserv AMC (fastest to Rs.30,000cr in 2.5 years), and the rebranding to Bajaj General/Bajaj Life after the Allianz buyout — supported by a consistency promise ('Caringly Yours', lowest grievance ratio, highest NPS) and a 10-crore customer franchise. No A&P spend, heritage narrative or premium-pricing evidence is offered, so it stops short of full brand strength.

> "BAGIC continues to have the lowest grievance ratio in the industry and the highest NPS consistently year -on-year" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ⬜ No input-cost data.
- ✅ Operating margin of 38% moving only ±4.7 points a year — the steady, protected profitability these patterns produce.

### Market Share Gainers — LIKELY FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern, but the numbers push back (0 of 1 check supporting) — held one step below a full confirmation.

**What the calls show:** Explicit, repeated share gains over the full period: BALIC's private-market IRNB share rose 6.4%→7.4% in 9M FY23 and grew 22% vs industry 12%; BAGIC grew 12% vs industry 9% in FY25 and ranked first among private players on GDPI in Q3 FY26; BFL AUM compounded from Rs.2.31 lakh cr (Dec 2022) to Rs.5.10 lakh cr (Mar 2026). Critically, the share was not bought with underwriting or credit quality — GNPA stayed ~1% and they repeatedly ceded price-competitive motor/crop/group-health business.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Regulation and government business repeatedly and materially reset reported economics: the IRDAI '1/n' rule made FY25 numbers non-comparable and inflated the combined ratio, new surrender regulations muted BALIC's new business and forced a full product restructuring (BALIC 2.0), a GST change cost ~5% of NBM in a quarter plus input tax credit, the new Labour Code took Rs.380 crore gross off one quarter, RBI's Digital Lending Directions forced Bajaj Markets to re-align its model, IRDAI mandates an Ind AS transition, and the Allianz buyout needed CCI/IRDAI approval. On top of that, tender-driven crop and government health business is volatile and loss-making enough that management repeatedly strips it out and has cut exposure.

> "Underwriting losses stood at INR 96 crores for the quarter versus about INR 3 crores... impacted largely by elevated claims arising from our government health business" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate diversification away from policy-driven revenue — tactical reduction of crop and government health exposure, with core commercial/motor/retail health lines reported and managed separately.*

## What to watch

- Return on capital: the -2 check showing -11% cash return on capital is the single biggest drag on the rating — a move back to positive operating cash on capital employed would lift the weakest area score (-1.0).
- Pricing pressure: the Pricing Power NO FIT and the 'Good Enough Goods' WATCH risk both hinge on price-shopped motor, group health and crop lines — evidence of premiums holding without giving up volume would be the first real change.
- Regulatory resets: Government Dependency is the only HIGH RISK, driven by the '1/n' rule, surrender regulations and the crop court order — whether the deliberate reduction of crop and government health exposure actually shrinks this sensitivity.
- Retail health share: the New Entrants risk cites standalone health insurers growing 32% against the group's 11.2% — closing that gap, or failing to, tests the Market Share Gainers pattern where it is weakest.
- Renewal book strength: Recurring Revenue is only PARTIAL and rests on renewal premium growth of 18-29% tied to persistency — if renewal growth slows, the main offset to the mark-to-market earnings swings under the Cyclicality risk weakens.
