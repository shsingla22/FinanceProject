### The story in depth

Maharashtra Scooters comes out Mixed — 45 out of 100, or two stars — and the arithmetic behind that shows exactly where the tension sits: business quality scored 40, multibagger fit scored just 19, and risk safety scored 84. The quality picture is genuinely split rather than merely average. On the good side, Customer Benefits and Capital Allocation both score the full +2. The company collects its cash 0 days before it has to pay suppliers, so customers effectively fund the business — a negative working-capital position the framework calls a rare strength — and the product carries essentially no direct input costs, leaving nearly all of every ₹100 of sales available for people, marketing and profit. On the poor side, Growth, Industry Structure and Return on Capital are all negative. Every ₹1 of assets produces only ₹0.01 of sales, and every ₹100 of capital employed generated just ₹1 of operating cash this year — a 1% cash return. So the business keeps almost all of what it bills, but it bills very little relative to the money tied up in it. Overall resilience is marked STRAINED.

The same handful of traits show up on both sides of the ledger, and that is the heart of the story. The near-total absence of input costs — ₹99 of operating profit out of every ₹100 of sales — is what earns the top mark on Intangible Benefits, and it is also the number feeding the three patterns that scored best on the multibagger screen: Pricing Power, Innovation Dominance and Market Share Gainers. But all three are logged as QUANT SIGNAL only, meaning the numbers point that way and no conference-call judgement was available to confirm it. Four more patterns — Recurring Revenue, Friendly Middleman, Brand Strength and Forward Integrators — come in PARTIAL, and Toll Roads and Low Price Plus are NO FIT. That thin, unconfirmed evidence is why multibagger fit scored only 19. Equally, the ±71.4 point annual swing in profit margin does triple duty: it holds the Profit Margin check at a neutral 0 despite the extraordinary headline margin, it drives the -2 on Barriers to Entry and Rationality as a hint of price wars, cost shocks or new entrants, and it explains why 41% average annual sales growth still scores -2 for Persistence — the growth is lumpy year to year. Swings that large suggest key costs sit outside management's control.

The risk review is the most reassuring of the three inputs, and it deserves the least weight in the story. Only two of the eight channels flagged anything: Cyclicality, with three numeric fingerprints, and Fashion Risk, with one. Both are marked QUANT FLAG, and the review is explicit that numbers alone are a flag to investigate, never a verdict — in both cases the calls offered nothing either way. So the 84 risk-safety score is high largely because nothing could be confirmed, which is the same evidence gap that capped the pattern screen. It is worth noting that the Cyclicality flag is almost certainly reading the same ±71.4 point margin volatility that the quality framework already penalised twice, so it is not an independent worry so much as a second sighting of the first one. Two quality areas, Competitive Advantage and Management, could not be scored at all. Taken together: a business that keeps nearly everything it earns but earns very little on the capital inside it, with attractive-looking patterns that no one has yet been able to verify and risks that no one has yet been able to rule out.
