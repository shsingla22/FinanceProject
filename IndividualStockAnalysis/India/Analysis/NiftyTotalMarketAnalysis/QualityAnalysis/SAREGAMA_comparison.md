Now: Debt-free, internally funded investment, exit from the low-yield in-house film business, and a documented refusal to overpay for a hot album under peer pressure. | The long view had said: Funding is from internal accruals and existing QIP money with no new raise, cash reserves are healthy, they invested counter-cyclically through a profit plateau and walked away from an overpriced Dhurandhar 2 — offset slightly by a widening M&A footprint (Pocket Aces, Bhansali).

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 5. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: The 180,000-song heritage catalogue is a genuine provenance asset being re-marketed to a new generation via GenAI videos, and the Bhansali tie-up buys prestige association; but the calls show consumer pull sitting with artists and film albums rather than the Saregama name, with no A&P or brand-premium disclosure. (The long view had said: Genuine heritage (the Lata/Kishore-era catalogue, 'the richest IP catalogue' in India) and name-stretch into adjacencies — Carvaan, Carvaan Live, Yoodlee, the UN40 festival — but the brand does not command consumer premium pricing; monetisation runs through platforms and artists rather than the Saregama masterbrand.)
- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Across the window management flagged the Q2 shortfall openly, attributed it to release phasing, and then delivered (H2 music +26% vs H1 +8%); it held its 5-year-payback rule by publicly refusing Dhurandhar 2 despite peer pressure, funded the INR 325 crore Bhansali deal entirely from internal accruals with no debt, and Mehra/Chaturvedi have led continuously since FY21/FY22 with a consistent 'judge us on rolling 12 months' framing. (The long view had said: Strong candour and stability — Vikram Mehra MD and Pankaj Chaturvedi CFO throughout, public admission that the Punjabi strategy 'never worked out' twice, Pocket Aces breakeven promised in Aug 2024 and delivered in FY26, and an on-call commitment to add rolling-12-month disclosure after an analyst challenged the narrative; offset by the Aug 2024 promise of 30% ex-Carvaan CAGR and doubled PBT being quietly reset to 20-23%, and by the 2022 extension of content useful life from 6 to 10 years that flattered PAT.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management repeatedly targets leadership and has locked structural supply - 30-40% of its Hindi film music now comes via Bhansali without bidding, and it claims the top position in Haryanvi after the Nav deal - but the calls give no hard, rising market-share percentages, and the gain is largely prospective. (The long view had said: Share ambition is explicit and quantified across the whole period — 'no. 2 label to the no. 1 label', a stated goal of 25-30% of all new Indian music releases, no.1 in Bhojpuri/Gujarati (Aug 2022), leadership in Haryanvi via the Nav deal, artists managed rising 150→270→300+ — and it is share bought with discipline, not price, as shown by refusing Dhurandhar 2 on payback grounds.)
- **Toll Roads**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Music content is not a trivial line item for its customers - labels take roughly half of streaming platform revenue, and Saregama itself negotiates hard on price (refusing Dhurandhar 2), which is the opposite of an unnegotiated toll on a tiny cost share. (The long view had said: Music IP is a critical, non-substitutable input to every streaming platform and Saregama is deliberately un-dependent on any single buyer ('we have very consciously worked in a fashion that there is no overdependence on any one player'), with the catalogue framed as an inflation-linked infrastructure asset; but content is the largest, not a <1-2%, cost line for its customers, so the classic toll-road economics only partly apply.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Cost to Replicate (STRONG FIT); Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 2 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Monetisation runs through a handful of platforms and the calls show the damage concretely — Airtel Wynk's closure sat in the denominator for three consecutive quarters and management tracked its exit quarter by quarter, while forward growth is explicitly pinned on subscription decisions taken by 'the 2 big platforms'. Content supply is now also deliberately concentrated: roughly 30-40% of Hindi film music is to come from one counterparty, Bhansali Productions. (The long view had said: A single partner's exit — Airtel Wynk shutting in Nov 2024 — dragged the music growth denominator for four-plus quarters and pulled reported growth from ~23% to 18%, and management declines to disclose platform-level revenue split while a handful of platforms (YouTube, Spotify, Saavn, Instagram) and Sun TV drive licensing. On the supply side, the new Bhansali Productions minority stake concentrates A/B+ Hindi film music access in one relationship for 24–30 months.)
- **Cyclicality**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Revenue is release-phasing driven rather than commodity- or capex-cyclical: management repeatedly asks to be judged on a rolling 12-month basis, video degrowth in Q2FY26 was called 'nothing but a cyclical issue', and content spend guidance fell twice in the year (INR 320-330cr to INR 275-300cr to INR 235cr actual) because Love & War and Paradise slipped out of FY26, producing H1 music growth of 8% vs H2 of 26%. (The long view had said: Revenue is consumption-linked — streaming, catalogue royalties and subscription, not customer capex or commodity prices; management repeatedly frames music licensing as the steady base and the catalogue as a long-duration asset (5-year payback, then 55–75 years of returns). Real lumpiness exists but comes from event tours (FY25 Diljit India tour non-repeat) and album release phasing (H1 FY26 +8% vs H2 +26%), not an industry cycle.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Good Enough Goods (ELEVATED).*

*Unchanged: Technological Innovation (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Fashion Risk (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

*Not comparable: Government Dependency (NOT ASSESSED → LOW — could not be assessed on the long view).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
