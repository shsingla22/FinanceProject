# Supreme Industries Ltd. (SUPREMEIND) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: INDUSTRIAL MANUFACTURING*

Supreme Industries is an Indian manufacturer of plastic goods, selling roughly 522,000 tonnes of product for about Rs 7,582 crore of net product turnover in the first nine months of FY26. Its biggest business by far is plastic pipes, fittings and valves used in farm irrigation, household plumbing, drainage and government water schemes, alongside plastic packaging films and sheets, industrial moulded components and material-handling crates, and consumer items like premium furniture and bathware. It makes money by buying polymer resin (PVC, PP, polyethylene) and converting it into branded finished products sold to farmers, builders, plumbing dealers, appliance makers, packers and oil marketing companies — so profits swing with polymer prices, which can create inventory gains or losses. It operates only manufacturing plants in India, spread across roughly 36 sites in 15 states with some exports, funds all its capital spending (about Rs 1,200 crore this year) from its own cash, and in August 2025 bought Wavin's Indian pipe business with a technology licence from Wavin B.V. of the Netherlands.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Plastic Piping System** — Pipes, fittings, valves and complete plumbing/drainage/irrigation systems (including CPVC, PEX, PERT, electrofusion fittings, PP silent pipes under the 'Serene' brand and polyethylene gas pipes) — clearly the core business, the one management guides on separately at 15-17% volume growth, with installed capacity heading to about 1 million tonnes a year by FY26.
- **Packaging Products** — Cross-laminated films (tarpaulins and fabricated 'made-up' products), protective packaging (foam/customized packing plus consumer items like sports goods, yoga mats and toys) and performance packaging films; the protective packaging division alone is targeting a Rs 1,000 crore revenue milestone this year.
- **Industrial Products** — Industrial components moulded for appliance makers (washing machines, air conditioners, coolers, refrigerators) plus a material-handling business selling crates for fisheries and fruit-and-vegetables, dustbins, soft-drink crates, PP hollow sheets and Bubble Guard Board; it is the weakest segment lately, flat to down in recent quarters.
- **Consumer Products** — Premium plastic furniture (chairs, cabinets) sold through a growing showroom network — 319 showrooms by June 2024 — and bathware; a smaller segment whose growth has swung between -7% and +8%.
- **Composite LPG Cylinders** — Lightweight composite cooking-gas cylinders supplied on letters of intent to state oil companies IOCL (2.31 lakh units) and BPCL (2 lakh units, with a repeat order), plus exports and a first order of CNG cascade cylinders — a small but strategically pushed niche.
- **Value-added products** — Not a separate factory but the premium slice of sales management tracks across all divisions, at Rs 1,118 crore in the December 2025 quarter versus Rs 961 crore a year earlier, and the stated focus for improving margins.
- **Profile Windows** — A new line making plastic window frames at Kanpur Dehat and Kharagpur, with commercial production expected from February 2026 and an initial sales focus on U.P., NCR and Haryana.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Supreme Industries earns its "Decent" 3-star rating (59 out of 100 = 45% x 66 business quality + 30% x 33 multibagger fit + 25% x 78 risk safety) on the strength of how it is built and funded rather than on what it can charge. The two highest-scoring quality checks are distribution and stewardship: 36 manufacturing sites across 15 states put product close to demand, backed by direct distributor relationships, 319 furniture showrooms and the 266 dealers added with Wavin; and across all 14 calls every capex programme — from roughly Rs 700 crore in FY23 to Rs 1,500 crore of commitments in FY25 and about Rs 1,200 crore in FY26, taking piping capacity from 740,000 to about 1 million tonnes — was paid for out of internal accruals, with borrowings described as small and temporary and investment continued right through the FY26 margin squeeze. That self-funded machine converts into Rs 20 of operating cash for every Rs 100 of capital employed and Rs 14 of operating profit on every Rs 100 of sales, with margins moving only about plus or minus 1.9 points a year. The third full-marks check, 'share donators', is the demand-side twin of the same story: weak rivals whose imitation products 'proved complete failure' with customers returning, an 85% non-organised segment to convert, and one loss-making competitor simply bought outright.

The multibagger screen is where that picture thins out, and mostly for one reason: the very distributor-led, volume-led design that creates the distribution advantage also blocks the patterns that depend on owning the customer or the price. Not a single pattern is a clean fit — 'Low Price Plus' is the closest at LIKELY FIT, supported by the volume/turns model, the rivals' accusation of aggressive pricing in July 2024 and the post-Wavin staff and overhead cuts, but with no itemised cost culture set out on the calls. Seven patterns land at PARTIAL for closely related reasons: 'Friendly Middleman' and 'Forward Integrators' are held back because the core piping business sells through the trade with no installer training, prescription programmes or D2C, even as furniture showrooms grew from 308 to 319 and window profiles will go through company fabrication; 'Brand Strength' has the premium push and value-added turnover rising to Rs 1,118 crore in Q3 FY26 but no brand spend or heritage narrative; 'Innovation Dominance' has a genuine annual cadence — PEX, PERT, PVC-O, electrofusion fittings, PP silent pipe with Poloplast, composite LPG and CNG cylinders, window profiles — but much of it is licensed or collaborative rather than proprietary; 'Market Share Gainers' shows repeated above-industry growth (pipes up 55% against a 27% market in Q1 FY23, CPVC up 30% in 9M FY26) yet management will not quantify its own share; 'Cost to Replicate' rests on the site network, the 1 million tonne capacity and the exclusive seven-year Wavin/Orbia licence for India and SAARC, while management itself concedes competitors churn out look-alikes. 'Recurring Revenue' is PARTIAL only because every sale is a one-time product sale with no service contracts or renewals. The two outright NO FITs, 'Toll Roads' and 'Pricing Power', share a single cause: pipes are a price-elastic, polymer-linked spend where dealers destock on price moves and the company must offer price protection.

That missing pricing power is also the company's biggest risk, which is why 'Cyclicality' is the one ELEVATED item. Realisations follow polymer prices instead of running ahead of cost inflation — volume growth persistently outpaces value growth (Q2 FY26 pipes up 17% in volume against 11% in value; 9M FY26 up 10% against 3%) — and the resulting swing is visible in the margin line, 12.24% in Q1 FY23 on falling PVC, above 15% in FY25, back to 12.1% in 9M FY26 after a Rs 100–120 crore raw-material hit. The partial offsets are that demand is consumption-linked housing, plumbing, agriculture and packaging rather than customers' own capex, and that the debt-free balance sheet lets management keep investing through the downturn — the same stewardship that scores full marks. Three risks sit at WATCH and each is the shadow of a stated strength: cheap look-alikes ('Good Enough Goods') and waves of new converters ('New Entrants') are the flip side of the abundant weak competitors the 'share donators' check counts as an opportunity, and the price aggression alleged by rivals under 'Low Price Plus' is precisely the competitive response management is accused of triggering. 'Government Dependency' is bounded — management says plainly it sells to distributors, not government — but Nal Se Jal demand faded, infrastructure allocations are blamed for the first-half FY26 piping shortfall, and the cylinder business runs on IOCL and BPCL letters of intent. Technology obsolescence, stakeholder concentration and fashion risk are all LOW, helped by eight product segments and a broad channel, and 'Shifting Consumer Preferences' returned no signal at all. Overall resilience reads SOUND: the weakest quality areas are industry structure at 0.0 and capital allocation, customer benefits and growth at 0.5 each, which is consistent with a well-run, well-financed volume business operating in a commodity-priced industry it does not control.
