### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 30 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 3 improved, 10 regressed, 17 unchanged. 2 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them).

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |

| Return on Capital | Weak (-0.67) | Neutral (+0.00) | +0.67 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.3 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: The 21.9mn sqft / 123-gateway / 4,730-centre asset base is a hard entry barrier, and pricing has been rational rather than discount-led — PTL yields rose ahead of tonnage in every quarter of the year while margins expanded across all segments. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±34.5 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Capital and density barriers are high and consolidation has removed entrants, but pricing rationality is qualified because Delhivery deliberately positions itself as the price aggressor rather than restraining pricing.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Poor → Neutral. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹6 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±1.3 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹6 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±34.5 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: PTL growth is genuinely yield-led (revenue growth consistently above tonnage growth), but Express — two-thirds of revenue — grew 24% on revenue against 43% volume in Q3 and 46% against 72% in Q4, so the dominant segment is volume-led with falling realisations. | The long view had said: Growth is overwhelmingly volume- and utilisation-led, with management explicitly disclaiming yield increases and describing itself as the source of price pressure in a commodity industry; only PTL shows genuine mix/yield gains.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Good → Weak. Now: Nothing in the calls suggests a monopoly-like hold on the customer's mind or a captive aftermarket; Delhivery competes for share of wallet with other 3PLs and platform captive arms on every account. | The long view had said: Delhivery holds a near-unique position in heavy goods and in integrated express-plus-LTL capability where rivals cannot make money at its prices, but it self-describes the core service as a commodity.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Calls are substantive and metric-dense with the presentation handed to functional heads rather than the CEO, but Delhivery is a high-visibility listed IPO story with a prominent founder-CEO, so this is neutral rather than low-profile. | The long view had said: Calls are dense, operational and metric-led with no promotional framing, and management publicly declined the fashionable quick-commerce narrative — though the CEO remains a visible industry figure covered by every large broker.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: Accessibility is a genuine strength — near-universal pin-code coverage, 4,730 delivery centres, new sub-two-hour and intracity on-demand services, and continued direct salesforce investment to reach customers city by city. | The long view had said: Unmatched accessibility across 18,838 pincodes and 4,730 delivery centres, combined with a deliberately expanded direct sales force reaching cities previously served only indirectly.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. Now: R&D is visibly producing shippable products within the year — TransportOne SaaS, Freight Index One, Delhivery International air economy, plus robotics/AI and a first autonomous VTOL drone field mission — though revenue contribution from these is not yet quantified. | The long view had said: Sustained R&D from US patents in 2022 through TransportOne SaaS, Freight Index One, VTOL drones, robotics and AI in 2026, with a direct traceable line from tech output to cost savings and margin expansion.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: CFO succession was handled internally — Vivek Pabari promoted from Head of Corporate Finance to replace a 13-year veteran — and a broad bench of segment heads (PTL, SCS, business) presents directly on calls. | The long view had said: Clear internal pipeline — the 2022 IR head became CFO and another became Head of PTL — alongside Delhivery Academy training, structured driver programmes and expanded employee benefits and safety spend.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Domestic pin-code reach is already saturated and flat (~18,830), and international expansion is early and tiny — cross-border revenue of Rs.38cr a quarter via FedEx/Aramex partnerships plus a newly launched air economy product. | The long view had said: Domestic pincode coverage is already near-saturated (18,435→18,838), so expansion is incremental — new PTL sales cities, intra-city services in Mumbai/Hyderabad and a new international air product rather than a travelling premium brand.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: The customer base is corporate (e-commerce platforms, D2C brands, SMEs, enterprise SCS mandates) and the offer correctly emphasises reliability and cost, but the corresponding switching-cost lock-in that this parameter requires is not evidenced. | The long view had said: The customer base is predominantly corporate, where the TCO/reliability pitch is correctly matched, and the long tail of 51,000 customers including ~10,000 SMEs reduces marketplace concentration risk.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Excellent → Good. Now: Management positions technology as the core differentiator with concrete multi-product output — AI across network and facilities, automation, robotics R&D, SaaS and drones — though superior customer benefit versus rivals is asserted rather than demonstrated. | The long view had said: A genuine multi-product tech edge — patents, proprietary sorting and dispatch systems, auto-docking optimisation, LLM/multimodal AI across operations, robotics and Delhivery Labs — repeatedly converted into measurable cost advantage.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Multi-year tech/engineering investment and 'long cycle bets' in local, cross-border, rapid and financial services are being funded from core cash flows rather than dilution, showing willingness to invest through a period when profitability was under scrutiny. | The long view had said: Founder-CEO since 2011 has stuck with the PTL business through a disastrous integration to 11%+ margins and continues funding long-cycle bets from core cash flows rather than chasing quick-commerce fashion.
- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Management pre-explains negative optics rather than burying them — flagging the deliberate supply-chain revenue decline as a chosen exit, disclosing integration costs against a stated envelope, and separating PAT before and after those costs. | The long view had said: The SpotOn failure was disclosed with a line-by-line cost bridge and an admission of the timeline miss rather than being buried, and later calls flag optically flattering items (depreciation change, ESOP reversal) unprompted.

*Unchanged: Capex for growth (not maintenance); Halo effect (caution); Share donators; Intangible benefits; Assurance benefits; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Disciplined stewards; Network effects; Gain in market share; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Merger and acquisition; Structural end-market growth; Asset turn; Return on capital (headline); Cost of working capital; Distribution advantage; Security by obscurity.*

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 3 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Named launches recur each quarter — Delhivery Direct in five cities, Delhivery International air economy, TransportOne SaaS, Freight Index One, autonomous VTOL drone trials, AI/robotics — but R&D spend is never quantified and new-service investment is a modest Rs 14 crores a quarter against Rs 2,300+ crores of revenue. (The long view had said: Named innovations land every year with stated P&L impact — US patents for Addfix/UAID (FY23), automated sortation and 46-ft tractor-trailers, Delhivery Direct app, dark-store/rapid network, TransportOne SaaS, autonomous VTOL drone trial and road train, AMRs/AS-RS and LLM deployment across operations (FY26) — and management ties the tech spend directly to unit-cost savings.)
- **Low Price Plus**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The FY26 calls show a genuine scale-and-cost engine — structural cost advantages, capex intensity down 7.8%→4.7%, receivables at 11 days, ROIC to 16% and first free cash flow a year early — but management never describes passing savings through to price as a deliberate strategy, so only part of the pattern is evidenced. (The long view had said: The consistent thread from FY23 losses to FY26 is cost leadership at scale — itemised network savings (auto-docking trucks, utilisation, haulage), CapEx intensity down from 7.8% to 4.7%, working capital to 11 receivable days, ROIC to 16% — with management framing pricing power as the ability to earn where rivals cannot.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across four calls there is no heritage story, no A&P spend, no consumer-brand premium — growth is attributed to network scale, the Ecom Express acquisition and service levels, and pricing is negotiated contract by contract. (The long view had said: Delhivery has become the default name in Indian 3PL and uses Delhivery Direct/Delhivery International to build consumer brand perception, but there is no heritage story, no sustained A&P spend, and no premium price attached to the name across the calls.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 0 worsened, 6 unchanged; 1 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls demand is operations-linked flow volume — parcels shipped by e-commerce, D2C and SME customers in their day-to-day trade, not customer capex — and it compounded steadily (208m → 246m → 295m → 306m shipments) with margins expanding rather than swinging; the only variability discussed is intra-year seasonality (Q1 low, Q3 festive peak), which management planned for. (The long view had said: Demand is a flow/operations-linked service (parcels shipped by customers' daily commerce, not their capex), but the calls show real seasonal and demand volatility — Q1 troughs, festive Q3 peaks, an e-commerce market management calls volatile, and fuel-price/consumption headwinds; the FY23 episode where retained capacity against a volume shortfall swung adjusted EBITDA from +4% to -12.5% shows how quickly operating leverage cuts both ways.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
