# Ambuja Cements Ltd. (AMBUJACEM) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CEMENT & CEMENT PRODUCTS*

Ambuja Cements is an Indian cement maker — part of the Adani group since 2022 — that produces and sells cement and clinker across India, and calls itself the world's ninth largest building material solutions company. It makes money by mining limestone, firing it into clinker at large kiln plants, grinding it (mostly with fly ash into 'blended' cement) at grinding units near its markets, and selling bags and bulk cement through two channels: 'trade' (dealers and retailers serving individual home builders, about 74% of sales by Q4 FY26) and 'non-trade' (direct bulk sales to infrastructure and construction projects). Profit comes from the gap between selling price per tonne and cost per tonne, so management talks constantly about realisation per bag, cost per tonne (about INR4,400 for FY26, targeted lower), fuel, power and freight. It operates plants and grinding units across Gujarat, Rajasthan, Punjab, Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Jharkhand, Bihar, West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh and the southern states, with FY26 sales of 73.7 million tonnes, capacity of 109 million tonnes, EBITDA of about INR6,539 crores and PAT of about INR2,647 crores, and it says it is debt-free.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Cement (Ambuja standalone)** — The core business — making and selling grey cement under the Ambuja brand; it is the parent company and the largest single piece, with standalone quarterly revenue of about INR4,218 crores as far back as Q3 FY23.
- **ACC Limited (listed subsidiary)** — A separately listed cement company Ambuja controls and reports alongside itself; together the two form the bulk of 'Adani Cement', and ACC is now proposed to be merged into Ambuja under the 'One Cement Platform'.
- **Sanghi Industries** — An acquired cement company (Gujarat-based) folded into the group and amalgamated with Ambuja in FY26; part of roughly 19 million tonnes of acquired capacity that was running well below par — cement utilisation about 57% in Q4 FY26 — and is being turned around.
- **Penna Cement** — An acquisition completed to strengthen the group in southern India (consolidated from August 2025, amalgamated with Ambuja in FY26); like Sanghi it is a fixer-upper, at about 46% cement utilisation in Q4 FY26 and needing heavy maintenance capex.
- **Orient Cement** — Another acquired cement business, consolidated for 11 months of FY26 and still in the process of being merged into Ambuja; management credits the acquired assets collectively with utilisation rising from 37% to 58% year-on-year in Q3 FY26.
- **Premium cement products** — Higher-priced branded cements such as Ambuja Kawach and ACC Gold, sold through the trade channel at better realisation; these grew to about 35-36% of trade sales volumes by FY26, up from 24% in Q1 FY25, and are management's main lever on price.
- **Blended cement** — Cement mixed with fly ash and other additives rather than pure clinker — about 86% of sales volumes; it is both a cost lever (lower clinker factor, 65% by Q4 FY26) and a green lever.
- **Ready-mix concrete (RMC)** — A smaller downstream business selling ready-mixed concrete, including a green product line called ECOMaxX; the calls describe it as growing but give no size figures.
- **Captive and green power** — Waste heat recovery systems and renewable energy plants (about 900 MW built, heading to 1,122 MW by FY27) that supply the plants' own electricity; green power was about 32-37% of the power mix, and surplus power is currently being sold into the market until approvals allow captive use.
- **Alternative fuels and waste (Geoclean / AFR)** — Burning industrial and municipal waste as substitute kiln fuel — around 9% of the fuel mix, with a target of 30% thermal substitution — which cuts fuel cost and supports the sustainability story.
- **Logistics and distribution** — Not a revenue line but a major cost centre management runs like a business unit — own GPWIS rail rakes, hub-and-spoke grinding units, shorter lead distances and direct-from-plant dispatches, with freight running around INR1,300 per tonne.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Ambuja scores 52 out of 100 — a Decent, three-star business — and the way that number is built tells the story: 58 for business quality, 62 for risk safety, but only 33 for multibagger fit. The quality work shows a company that is very good at two things and unproven at a third. It serves a market in genuine long-run expansion (Structural end-market growth scores +2, tied to India's roughly 1.1x GDP cement demand and a large infrastructure and housing pipeline), and it is taking share inside that market faster than anyone else (Gain in market share scores +2, from about 14% to 16.6%, volumes growing at twice the industry rate, capacity from 67.5mt to 109mt, against a stated 20% target). That is why Customer Benefits scores a perfect 1.0 and Growth 0.83. But Return on Capital sits at 0.0, Capital Allocation at 0.17 and Industry Structure at 0.0 — the company is growing well without yet showing that the growth pays. Only one of the eleven multibagger patterns is a STRONG FIT, and it is the same one the quality checks reward: Market Share Gainers.

The single trait that produces the strongest pattern also produces most of the weaknesses, and this is the central tension in the file. Share was won largely by buying it — Sanghi, Penna, Orient, Tuticorin — and the Merger and acquisition check scores -1 precisely because these were large, repeated deals for weak assets that needed overhaul, not small bolt-ons. The same purchases show up in the Asset turn check at -1: every ₹1 of assets produces ₹0.39 of sales, with acquired capacity running at just 46-58% utilisation against ₹6,000-7,500 crore of annual capex. The Capex for growth check gives +1 for scale and for funding it all from internal accruals while staying debt-free, but says plainly that incremental returns are unproven. Corporate Culture is only a PARTIAL fit for the same reason: the disclosure is unusually candid, including openly owning missed cost targets in Q4 FY26, yet the promise-versus-delivery record is weak, with the 140mt-by-FY28 goal among the slippages — which is also why Management is the one quality area scoring below zero at -0.17. And the very scale and visibility that make the consolidation work possible score -2 on Security by obscurity: this is the most scrutinised consolidation story in Indian cement, the opposite of a quiet compounder, even as that same scale and the licence-gated limestone blocks keep New Entrants a LOW risk and give Cost to Replicate its PARTIAL standing.

The second tension is between what the brand spending achieves and what it does not. Advertisement and promotion scores +1 and Intangible benefits +1 — after direct input costs the company keeps ₹83 of every ₹100 of sales — and the push has genuinely moved premium cement to 35-36% of trade sales, up from 24%, which is exactly why Shifting Consumer Preferences is a LOW risk: the one preference shift in the market is running in the company's favour and is being captured. But that effort has not turned into price. Brand Strength is only PARTIAL because realisation barely moved (Rs 254 against Rs 255), and Pricing Power is an outright NO FIT: the FY26 calls document realisations rising Rs 1 a bag while industry costs rose Rs 400-500 a tonne, with prices described as rollback-prone. Toll Roads is likewise NO FIT — cement is a big-ticket, price-sensitive line in a construction budget, not a small critical input. The same commodity character drives Good Enough Goods to ELEVATED, since the contractor or dealer largely picks the bag and non-trade realisations run up to Rs 31 a bag below trade, and it feeds the HIGH Cyclicality risk, where EBITDA margin once swung from 6.1% to 14.4% in a single quarter on fuel cost alone. The cost programme meant to offset this (Low Price Plus, PARTIAL) is real and itemised but has slipped on delivery. Against all that, resilience is still judged SOUND: the balance sheet is debt-free with large cash and an AAA rating, demand is 64-65% housing rather than customer-capex-linked, and five of the eight risk channels are LOW or WATCH. The two WATCH items are worth naming — a demand narrative that leans on government programmes and inputs that depend on state coal and limestone auctions, and a supply side anchored on the promoter group, including the 16 lakh tonne coal contract and Adani adjacencies that dominated analyst questioning.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

**Overall:** the company partly passes the foundation every multibagger shares (steady cash + high returns on capital + growth), 2 of 3 testable checks passing, and fits 2 of the 11 patterns: Market Share Gainers, Innovation Dominance.

### Market Share Gainers — STRONG FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence and the numbers agree (1 of 1 check computable supporting).

**What the calls show:** The most clearly delivered promise of the period: share moved from ~14% (Aug 2024, with a 20% FY28 target) to 16.6% by Q3 FY26 with volumes growing at 2x industry and 19% over nine months, capacity from 67.5mt to 109mt; the caveat is that much was bought via acquisitions (Sanghi, Penna, Orient) whose utilisation remains 46-57% and which have dragged costs above target.

> "our highest ever quarterly sales volume at almost 18.9 million tons, up 17% and the market share improved to 16.6%" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Sales compounding at 14% while margins held or improved (17% → 16%) — growth that was WON, not bought with price cuts.

### Innovation Dominance — LIKELY FIT
*"Out-inventing the competition"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and every one of the 2 computable checks supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** There is real technical investment — Coolbrooks RotoDynamic Heater (claimed first commercial-scale globally), an Indo-Swedish carbon capture pilot with IIT Bombay, the CiNOC AI operations centre and 'R&D-backed customized cements' — but R&D spend is never disclosed or quantified, there is no named annual product cadence with revenue impact, and the innovation is cost/ESG-directed rather than margin-funding.

> "our R&D -backed customized cement solutions, you will hear it very soon" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹83 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ✅ High retained margin (83% of sales) alongside 14% yearly growth — the profitable-innovation flywheel: margins fund the spending that drives the growth.

### Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) — PARTIAL
*"Sells once, earns forever"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls are silent and the numbers are mixed (1 of 3 checks supporting).

- ❌ About 37 days of cash is tied up in the trade cycle — customers are not funding the business.
- ❌ Capital spending is about 18% of sales — growth is capital-hungry.
- ✅ Operating cash was positive in 12 of 12 years — the reliable cash engine recurring-revenue businesses show.

### Friendly Middleman — PARTIAL
*"The expert sells it for you"*

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Cement demand, pricing and costs swing sharply across the 16 calls: EBITDA margin moved from 6.1% to 14.4% in one quarter (Feb 2023) on fuel cost alone, cost per tonne oscillated (Rs 4,437 → Rs 4,500 → below Rs 4,000 → Rs 4,500 'peak'), and by May 2026 management conceded prices could not be raised against a Rs 400-500/t industry cost shock while demand guidance was cut to 5-5.5%. The 2022-25 expansionary phase (67.5 → 140 → 155 mt targets) was explicitly recalibrated in FY26 to 119 mt with the end-state target pushed from FY28 to 'FY30', the classic over-investment-then-retreat pattern.

> "industry is still under the relentless pressure and not able to pass on the price" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Debt-free balance sheet with large cash, demand is consumption/housing-linked (64-65% housing) rather than customer-capex-linked, and capacity was added counter-cyclically through the downturn*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 3 of the last 10 years with swings of ±37 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 13% and 21% (±2.4 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 26% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 10% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.
