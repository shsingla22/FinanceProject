# Ambuja Cements Ltd. (AMBUJACEM) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CEMENT & CEMENT PRODUCTS*

Ambuja Cements is an Indian cement maker — part of the Adani group of companies — that manufactures and sells bagged and bulk cement (mostly blended cement, plus clinker) across India, and describes itself on recent calls as the world's ninth-largest building material solutions company. It sells through two channels: the "trade" channel of dealers and retailers serving housing and individual builders, which now accounts for roughly 74% of volumes and fetches better prices, and the "non-trade" channel serving infrastructure and bulk/institutional buyers. Money is made on volume times price per bag/tonne less cost per tonne — management runs the business on EBITDA per tonne (FY26: 73.7 million tonnes sold, EBITDA of INR6,539 crores or INR887 per tonne, PAT of INR2,647 crores), so most of the effort described on the calls goes into cutting cost per tonne (fuel, power, freight) and pushing higher-priced premium products. It operates plants, clinker lines and grinding units spread across Gujarat, Rajasthan, Punjab, Chhattisgarh, Maharashtra, West Bengal, Jharkhand, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh and southern India, with capacity grown from 67.5 million tonnes at the 2022 Adani takeover to 109 million tonnes, targeting 155 million tonnes by March 2028, and stays debt-free with a large cash pile.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Cement business (grey cement — trade and non-trade channels)** — The core and overwhelmingly dominant business: making and selling cement (86% of the mix is blended cement, which uses less clinker and so costs less) to dealers/retail homebuilders under "trade" and to infrastructure and bulk buyers under "non-trade" — trade is about 74% of volumes and prices about INR31 per bag higher than non-trade.
- **Premium products (Ambuja Kawach, ACC Gold)** — Higher-priced branded cement grades sold within the trade channel, called "blockbuster" super-premium products by management; they rose from 24% of trade volumes in FY25 to about 35-36% in FY26 (up 31% in absolute terms), and are the main lever for lifting realisation per bag.
- **Group cement companies / "One Cement Platform" (ACC, Sanghi Industries, Penna Cement, Orient Cement)** — Acquired cement companies that Ambuja reports with — Sanghi and Penna have already been merged into Ambuja, while ACC and Orient are in the process — with the goal of running them as one platform; Sanghi and Penna alone add 19 million tonnes of capacity and are the current problem children, running at only ~57% and ~46% utilisation and dragging on cost.
- **Captive and green power (waste heat recovery + renewables)** — Self-generated electricity for the plants — waste heat recovery grown from ~40 MW at takeover toward 186 MW, plus a renewable energy fleet of about 900 MW heading to 1,122 MW by FY27 — which now supplies roughly a third of power needs; it exists to cut power cost per tonne, though some of it is currently being sold into the open market until approvals allow captive use.
- **Alternative fuels and waste co-processing (Geoclean / AFR)** — Burning industrial and municipal waste as substitute kiln fuel instead of coal and pet coke, marketed as the Geoclean waste-management offering; the share of alternative fuels in the fuel mix was around 9% in FY25 against a 30% target, and its purpose is both lower fuel cost and the company's sustainability claims.
- **Ready-mix concrete (RMC)** — A smaller adjacent business selling ready-mixed concrete, including a green variant branded ECOMaxX; mentioned only briefly in the early 2023 call as a growing area, with no size given in later calls.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Ambuja Cements comes out of the three reviews as a genuinely large, well-financed grower that has not yet turned its size into superior returns — a combined score of 48 out of 100 (Mixed, 2 stars), built from 54 on business quality, 26 on multibagger fit and 62 on risk safety, with overall resilience judged sound. The strongest evidence sits on the growth side: market share climbed from about 14% at FY25 toward 16.6% in Q3 FY26 against a stated 20% target, volumes grew at twice the industry rate and 19% over nine months, and capacity went from 67.5mt to 109mt while staying debt-free. Management also scores well on straight talk — it volunteers cost misses, quantifies one-off items so periods can be compared, admits competitors have a cheaper power position and flags its own kiln efficiency gap. Two of the eleven multibagger patterns are likely fits — Market Share Gainers and Innovation Dominance — and the innovation cited (waste-heat recovery and 900MW of green power, alternate fuels, the CiNOC AI operations centre, the Coolbrook kiln electrification, ECOMaxX concrete) is the kind that lowers cost rather than changing what the customer buys.

The awkward part is that the same actions that produce the strengths also produce the weaknesses. Share was gained substantially by buying it — Sanghi, Penna, Orient and Tuticorin were large distressed purchases rather than small strong bolt-ons — and those assets still run at 46-57% utilisation, with post-deal margins hurt by maintenance capex nobody expected. That directly explains why the company sold 73.7mt against 109mt of capacity, why every ₹1 of assets generates only ₹0.39 of sales, and why the scorecard gives zero for both competitive advantage and return on capital even as growth scores well. The brand story runs into the same wall from the other direction: advertising behind the Adani Cement transition, Kawach and ACC Gold has lifted premium cement to 35-36% of trade sales and grown it 31% year on year, yet the spend showed up as a visible cost drag while Q4 FY26 realisation was ₹254 a bag against ₹255 a year earlier. So Brand Strength and Low Price Plus rate only partial, and Pricing Power is scored as no fit outright — the record shows price hikes that get rolled back and management saying in May 2026 that a ₹400-500 per tonne industry cost increase could not be passed on. The one clear customer-facing plus, keeping ₹83 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs, has not yet reached the bottom line.

The risk review lands in the same place rather than adding a separate worry. Cyclicality is the single high risk, and it bites precisely because the cost programme is still unfinished: profit per tonne moved from ₹807 to ₹1,060 to ₹718 and back to ₹887 as construction demand and imported fuel prices swung. Good-enough-goods risk is elevated for the identical reason Pricing Power failed — cement is close to a commodity, non-trade buyers pay up to ₹31 a bag less than trade, and premiumisation is working inside the trade channel but not defending overall price. Two items sit on watch: government auctions and budgets set several inputs (limestone blocks, captive coal, infrastructure outlays), softened by management's statement that 75-80% of demand is private housing and commercial; and the input side leans on the promoter group, including a 16-lakh-tonne coal contract and reliance on Adani logistics — the same group relationship that supplies the port, jetty and rake advantages behind the partial Cost to Replicate score, and the same headline Adani takeover that makes this the opposite of an under-the-radar stock, covered by every major broker. Four risks are low — new entrants, technology, shifting preferences and fashion — because limestone leases and heavy capital needs bar entry, the top five players' share rose from about 45% in 2018 to 60% by FY25, and the shift toward blended and green cement is one the company is leading with blended at 86% of volumes. In short: the downside is well protected and the balance sheet is clean; what is missing is proof that the bought capacity earns its keep.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across the 16 calls demand and margins swing with the construction/infra capex cycle and with imported fuel prices: EBITDA margin moved 6.1%→14.4% (Q3FY23), EBITDA/t went INR807 (Q1FY25) → INR1,060 (Q2FY26) → INR718 (Q3FY26) → INR887 (FY26), cost/t swung INR4,250→INR4,500 on a West Asia war shock, and the aggressive up-cycle capacity plan (67.5→140→155 mt) was itself recalibrated in May 2026 to FY30 with capex cut to INR6,000-6,500 cr as demand guidance fell from 8% to 5-5.5% — over-investment at the peak followed by a step back.

> "industry may grow at around, say, 5% to 5.5%... despite the circumstances of costs gone up, unfortunately, industry is still under the relentless pressure and not able to pass on the price" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Cement is a consumption/flow product (~65% housing, largely individual housing repeat demand) rather than customer capex equipment, and the company invested through the downturn while remaining debt-free*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 3 of the last 10 years with swings of ±37 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 13% and 21% (±2.4 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 26% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 10% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

## What to watch

- Utilisation of the acquired plants — Sanghi and Penna style assets still at 46-57% against the 73.7mt-of-109mt overall gap; a sustained climb here is what would repair the negative asset-turn check and the zero score on return on capital, and would confirm the Market Share Gainers pattern rather than the negative merger-and-acquisition check.
- Realisation per bag versus the premium mix — premium is 35-36% of trade sales and growing 31%, but Q4 FY26 realisation was ₹254 versus ₹255 a year earlier; realisation rising alongside that mix would start to reverse the no-fit Pricing Power verdict and the elevated good-enough-goods risk.
- Delivery on the stated cost-per-tonne targets (kiln fuel, waste-heat recovery, 900MW green power, lead distance, rail rakes) — the Low Price Plus pattern is partial only because delivery has lagged the ₹4,000-per-tonne goal, and hitting it is the main defence against the high cyclicality risk that swung profit per tonne between ₹718 and ₹1,060.
- Credibility of capacity and timing guidance — the candour check is one of only two full-marks scores, but the corporate-culture pattern is held to partial by the 140mt-by-FY28 promise becoming 155mt and then slipping, plus the 3-6 month capex delay; another unexplained reset would pull down the management area, which already scores only 0.17.
- Related-party input reliance — the 16-lakh-tonne coal contract and dependence on Adani group logistics keep stakeholder concentration on watch; wider third-party sourcing, or conversely a deepening of group dependence, would move both this risk and the group-adjacency support underneath the partial Cost to Replicate pattern.
