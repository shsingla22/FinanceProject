# Schaeffler India Ltd. (SCHAEFFLER) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: INDUSTRIAL MANUFACTURING*

Schaeffler India Limited, which management calls "the Motion Technology Company," makes and sells mechanical components that help machines move — bearings, clutches and other transmission parts, and related engineered products. It is the Indian arm of the Schaeffler Group, a business the calls say has been in motion technology for over 75 years, and it sells to two broad sets of customers: vehicle makers (two- and three-wheelers, passenger cars, commercial vehicles, tractors, and off-highway names like John Deere, Toyota Kirloskar, Daimler India, Escorts Kubota) and industrial customers (power plants such as Adani Power, wind-turbine generator maker ELIN, Indian Railways and metros, Voith Turbo, Sandvik Mining, Nord Drivesystems). It earns money by winning long-running supply programs with these customers and then shipping parts to them quarter after quarter, plus a replacement-parts business selling to garages and workshops after vehicles are on the road. It is headquartered in Pune, listed on the BSE and NSE, manufactures in India, and also exports; recent quarterly revenue has run around INR 2,500–2,700 crores with roughly 19% EBITDA margins and about 12–13% net profit.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Automotive Technologies (OEM business)** — Supplies parts built into new vehicles — especially transmission products such as clutches, hybrid applications and planetary gear shafts — and management describes it as the core engine of the company, with new business wins in this area repeatedly cited as what feeds revenue two to three years out.
- **Vehicle Lifetime Solutions (automotive aftermarket)** — Sells replacement and repair parts — timing kits, front-end auxiliary drives, wipers, lubricants and BS-VI-related products — to workshops and garages once vehicles are in service, and is described as a strategically important growth area that management wants growing faster than the rest, including an ambition to double aftermarket sales by 2030.
- **Industrial business** — Sells bearings and engineered components into sectors like power generation, wind energy, railways and metros (including insulation-coated bearings), mining and industrial drives; the calls describe it as a meaningful second leg alongside automotive, growing in single digits in the most recent quarter.
- **Exports** — Ships products from India to overseas Schaeffler customers and markets; it was a drag when demand was muted in 2024 (management cut back capex partly because of it) and a positive contributor again by mid-2026.
- **Koovers (KRSV Innovative Auto Solutions)** — A Bengaluru-based business-to-business e-commerce platform selling auto spare parts to workshops, acquired outright in 2023 — small relative to the group (about INR 77 crores of sales that year, 7,000 workshops, 1.8 million SKUs, mostly in south India) but bought as the digital route to grow the aftermarket business.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Schaeffler India comes out of the three reviews as a Strong business, scoring 67 out of 100 (45% x 70 on business quality, 30% x 56 on multibagger fit, 25% x 76 on risk safety), with an overall resilience read of SOUND. The core of the story is consistency: sales have compounded at about 12% a year with very little variation from year to year, operating margin sits at roughly 18 rupees of every 100 rupees of sales and has moved only about half a point either way, and the business generated a 21% cash return on the capital employed in it. That steadiness is not an accident of the cycle — it comes from selling certainty rather than cheapness. Customers hand out awards for zero-defect quality (Toyota Kirloskar), supply-chain resilience (Daimler) and reliability in railway and wind-generator applications, and the checks on customer benefits and industry structure score at the top of the range. The framework reads flat margins over many years as the signature of a rational industry with few new entrants.

The same trait that produces the strength also caps it, and this is where the patterns and the risks meet. Being deeply embedded in customer programmes — multi-year co-development, zero-defect qualification, 80% localisation — gives the toll-road-like anchoring that lets the company grow faster than its end markets (Automotive Technologies up 30.8-33% against underlying vehicle production of about 15%, on rising content per vehicle). But that embeddedness means prices are negotiated with those same large customers rather than set by Schaeffler: the Pricing Power pattern is judged NO FIT, with the July 2026 call reporting input, LPG, freight and currency costs landing on the bottom line and cost recovery still under discussion, and an analyst question on price indexation drawing no claim of an automatic pass-through. Low Price Plus is also NO FIT, because cost work is described as protecting the company's own margin, not as savings handed to customers. So the stable margin should be read as disciplined cost and mix management inside a rational industry, not as the power to raise prices at will. The Cost to Replicate and Innovation Dominance patterns land only at PARTIAL for related reasons: the barriers are real but not obviously impossible to copy, and the parent group controls technology and product mandates.

The weakest link is what the company does with its cash, and one decision drives most of it. Capital Allocation scores lowest of all quality areas at 0.33, almost entirely because of the 2023 Koovers purchase (142.4 crore rupees, paid in cash) — a loss-making startup that nearly three years on still runs at -13.4% EBITDA and drags the consolidated margin below the 19.3% standalone level, earning a negative mark on the merger and acquisition check. Yet the very same deal is what supports two of the better pattern verdicts: Forward Integrators and Friendly Middleman, because it bought a B2B e-commerce platform and 7,000 workshops and moved the company closer to the mechanic who actually chooses the part. It is also management's answer to the New Entrants risk, since digital platforms are a structurally different way of distributing auto parts. Read together, this is a bet that is strategically coherent and financially unproven so far. Around it sit eight risks, six rated WATCH and none rated high: cyclicality (revenue was roughly flat in CY24 at +0.5% growth), the powertrain transition away from the ICE-linked clutches, cam phasers, timing kits and alternator pulleys that generate today's content gains, policy-anchored demand in railways, wind and power, and intercompany exports to the parent group at about 16-17% of sales that swing hard with the parent's own demand. Offsetting all of this is the one STRONG FIT pattern, Corporate Culture: Harsha Kadam has led throughout, the 30-50% dividend policy was honoured at 45% in a weak year, e-axle localisation was followed through, and bad news is disclosed promptly.
