# Elecon Engg.Co (ELECON) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Elecon Engineering is an Indian engineering manufacturer that makes two main things: industrial gearboxes and gear-based power transmission products, and large material handling equipment such as conveyors, wagon tipplers, stacker reclaimers, crushers, feeders and port equipment. Its customers are industrial and infrastructure companies — cement, steel, sugar, power, marine, mining, ports and fertilizer plants — plus OEMs that build machinery, so it is a business-to-business capital-goods supplier rather than a consumer company. It earns money by booking orders and then designing, manufacturing (management stresses everything from drawing and design through manufacturing, testing and quality control happens under one roof) and delivering equipment against them, with a reported open order book of about INR 1,518 crores as of June 2026 and consolidated FY26 revenue of roughly INR 2,341 crores. It operates from India with overseas subsidiaries serving customers in more than 95 countries (85+ in earlier calls); domestic sales were about 71% of Q1 FY27 revenue and overseas about 29%, with a stated goal of taking overseas to 50% by FY30 and a recently added step-down subsidiary in Mexico.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Gear Division** — Designs and manufactures industrial gearboxes and gear/power-transmission products — both standard catalogue items and custom engineered ones — for steel, cement, sugar, power and marine customers; it is by far the bigger business, described as the company's cornerstone at roughly 85% of revenue in Q1 FY25 and still about 63-80% in FY26/FY27 quarters (INR 416 crores revenue in Q1 FY27), with management claiming a leadership position in India's organised industrial gear market and status as one of Asia's largest gear solution providers.
- **MHE Division (Material Handling Equipment)** — Designs and builds large, complex, high-capacity bulk material handling systems — conveyors, wagon tipplers, stacker reclaimers, crushers, feeders and port equipment — for power, steel, cement, mining, ports and fertilizer customers; it is the smaller but faster-growing arm (around 15% of revenue in Q1 FY25, rising to roughly 37% in Q4 FY26 before easing to about 20% in Q1 FY27 at INR 105 crores), and management says it was restructured to focus only on equipment supply and aftermarket service after loss-making legacy turnkey projects were cleared, which returned it to profitability.
- **Overseas / international business (subsidiaries and exports)** — Sells the same gear and MHE products abroad through foreign subsidiaries and a distribution network spanning 95+ countries, supported by OEM tie-ups and brand-building; it contributed about 29% of consolidated revenue in Q1 FY27 (INR 151 crores) versus roughly 27-34% in FY25, and the company targets 50% of revenue from overseas by FY30.
- **Aftermarket and service** — Supplies spares, life-cycle support and aftermarket services alongside new equipment — management describes end-to-end life cycle support in gears and calls aftermarket services one of the two things the MHE division now concentrates on — though the calls give no separate revenue figure for it.

### The story in depth

Elecon makes industrial gearboxes and material-handling equipment, and the quality work says the business itself is in good shape: competitive advantage scores full marks, the structure of its industry scores highly, and management scores well. The concrete things behind that are easy to name. The company has been net-debt-free since FY23 and sits on roughly INR700 crore of net cash. It has stayed organic, walked away from the risky material-handling EPC work in favour of products and after-sales, and has pushed exports hard — from 85 countries to more than 95, with overseas sales rising from 27% to over 34% of the group total and a new step-down subsidiary in Mexico. It leads the organised gear market in India while holding what management itself calls a tiny share globally, so there is genuine room ahead, and it has been converting that room rather than just talking about it. After a weak FY26, when customers deferred their own spending and both revenue and margins suffered, the company goes into FY27 with a record open order book of INR1,518 crore, up 36.8% on the year, and order intake up 23% — the look of a company at the bottom of a cycle rather than the top.

Where the story thins out is when you ask whether any of this is the kind of durable advantage that compounds for years. The multibagger screen is blunt about it: of eleven patterns, only pricing power is even a likely fit, eight are partial, and two do not apply at all. Even the pricing power is fading rather than building — in 2022 the company was pushing cost increases straight through to customers and gross margins widened, but by Q1 FY27 gear margins had slipped to 17.9% from 18.8%, meaning the pass-through no longer works the way it did. The other patterns keep running into the same wall: the ingredients are real but never sized. Brand-building spend is credited with opening export markets but is never quantified. R&D is called 'the only constant in the company' and has lifted engineered products to 45% of the mix with patents filed, yet the spend is never disclosed and no product is tied to revenue. After-sales is described as a genuine second stream but is never measured across seventeen earnings calls. OEM partnerships are the route into foreign markets but nothing shows how sticky they are. The barrier to copying Elecon — 75 years of design know-how and drawing-to-testing manufacturing under one roof — is a real answer, but plants run at only 56-60% utilisation, which cuts against the idea that capacity itself is scarce. That gap between credible strengths and unproven, unmeasured ones is exactly why the pattern score comes in at 18 while business quality scores 63.

The risks are largely the same traits viewed from the other side. The single high risk is cyclicality: about 80% of gear demand is customers building new capacity in cement, steel, power and mining, and only about 20% is replacement — which is precisely why the record order book reads as a cycle turning rather than as structural growth, and why the FY26 shortfall happened at all. The two hedges against that cycle are the same two things the patterns give credit for: the roughly 20% replacement and after-sales work at better margins, and the export push, which is also what softens the government-dependency risk from PSU tenders, ports, thermal power and defence orders. The push into higher-margin engineered products is likewise the direct answer to the 'good enough goods' risk, since the other 55% of the gear mix is catalogue product where competition bites harder. Lumpy orders cut both ways too — one power order was 27% of a single quarter's intake and a first Navy order visibly dented gear margins, so the same big wins that build the order book also make any single quarter unreliable. The clearest genuine weakness sits in capital allocation, the one negative area: about 115 days of cash is tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, the INR700-800 crore cash pile earns only about 8% while the company generates INR300-350 crore a year after tax, and shareholders get a token INR1.50 final dividend with no buyback and no stated policy. The 2010-11 European acquisition was written down to nothing with a INR102 crore goodwill charge in FY26, which both explains management's retreat from foreign deals and, because they disclosed it plainly, counts in their favour on candour. Put together — solid business, weak evidence of a durable moat, one big cyclical exposure, and cash that is safe but idle — the combined score lands at 50 out of 100, a Decent three-star verdict with sound resilience.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Pricing Power — LIKELY FIT
*"Raises prices, keeps the customers"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and every one of the 2 computable checks supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** Early in the period management did pass through inflation aggressively (Jul 2022: short offer validity, went back to customers for increases, gross margins expanded), but by FY26-FY27 pass-through failed — Q1 FY27 gear margins fell to 17.9% from 18.8% and MHE lost ~2.5-3% margin purely on input cost, and management said high input costs were lengthening enquiry-to-order conversion. Conditional pricing power exists only in replacement/engineered/defence repeat orders.

> "within gear the margin growth has not happened because whatever growth that we could see that has been dragged by raw material price increase" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹52 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ✅ Operating margin moved from about 14% (early years) to 24% (recent years) — the widening-profitability signature of pricing power / lock-in.

### Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) — PARTIAL
*"Sells once, earns forever"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (2 of 3 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Aftermarket/replacement is a real but small and unquantified stream — MHE realigned to 'product supply and aftermarket services' (Jul 2024) and Q4 FY26 credits margins to 'a balanced contribution from equipment sales and aftermarket services'; but across 17 calls there are no attach rates, AMC/renewal shares, installed-base density metrics or deferred-revenue-as-funding discussion, and the Jul 2022 call put replacement at only ~20% of demand.

> "Normally, the ratio is 80:20, 80 is fresh and 20 is replacements and then normally we see that the replacements are where the profit margins are even higher." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ About 115 days of cash is tied up in the trade cycle — customers are not funding the business.
- ✅ Capital spending is about 5% of sales — growth needs little capital.
- ✅ Operating cash was positive in 12 of 12 years — the reliable cash engine recurring-revenue businesses show.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK

> "everyone was trying to defer their capex investment because of the current geopolitical scenario" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: ~20% replacement/aftermarket demand at higher margins, MHE aftermarket services, net cash of ~INR700 crores and a INR400 crore capex programme maintained through the FY26 downturn (countercyclical investment)*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 4 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.

## What to watch

- Whether the record INR1,518 crore order book actually converts into revenue and margin recovery, or whether the Cyclicality risk (80% fresh-capex demand) reasserts itself and the FY27 upturn stalls.
- Whether gear margins stop slipping from the 17.9% level — a further fall would downgrade Pricing Power from likely fit to failed and confirm the Good Enough Goods pressure on the 55% catalogue mix.
- Any move on the Distribution to shareholders check: a stated dividend policy, a buyback, or the ~INR700-800 crore net cash being deployed instead of earning ~8% would lift the negative Capital Allocation score.
- Whether the engineered-product share keeps rising past 45% and whether R&D spend or after-sales revenue is ever disclosed — the missing numbers are what hold Innovation Dominance and Recurring Revenue at partial.

- Concentration in order intake — a repeat of single awards like the power order at 27% of a quarter's intake, or defence orders denting margins, would push Stakeholder Concentration from watch to a live problem.
