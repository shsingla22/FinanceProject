### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 8 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Share gain is repeated and quantified against a weak industry: Officer's Choice gained share in a degrowing mass-premium segment in Q2 and Q3 FY26, P&A salience rose from 39.7% to 48.2% of volume, and Q1 FY27 volumes beat an industry growing in low single digits — with international reach expanding from 14 to 39 countries, and margins rising rather than being bought with discounting. (The long view had said: Share gain is documented repeatedly and quantitatively across the seven calls: P&A salience 25% (FY18) to 37.3% (FY24) to 48.2% of volume in Q1 FY27, Officer's Choice gaining share while the Mass Premium segment de-grew in H1 FY26, ICONiQ taking share from legacy brands, and export footprint from 14 to 39 countries — with margins expanding rather than being bought.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Across all four calls Officer's Choice holds mass-premium leadership and is India's top exported whisky, while ICONiQ White scaled to 10.7mn cases in FY26 and is billed the world's fastest-growing millionaire whisky brand for three straight years; sustained A&P (the 'So Smooth Must Be Magic' campaign, Shreyas Iyer collaboration, packaging refreshes) and stretch into the ABD Maestro luxury range confirm brand-led economics. (The long view had said: Real heritage and stretch exist — Officer's Choice since 1988, India's top exported whisky, four millionaire brands, extensions into Blue/brandy and Zoya flavour variants — but the durability test is being failed by three of the four legacy brands, which need packaging resets and 'brand reset' programmes after high-teens volume declines.)

**That weakened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Named launches recur every quarter — Rangeela Vodka, YELLO Designer Whisky, AODH Irish Whiskey, Zoya Gin, Arthaus, a reformulated Sterling Reserve B7 blend, a 10-brand Maestro portfolio in one year — but this is brand/design innovation via a 'build, buy and partner' model with no disclosed R&D spend or technological barrier. (The long view had said: Named launches with measurable revenue impact appear every year: ICONiQ White (2022) to 10.7m cases in FY26, Zoya gin plus flavour extensions at 30% of that brand's volume, 10 ABD Maestro brands built in one year, The Collective 34-year malt over 50% pre-sold, and a new deluxe vodka; rising gross margins (37% FY24 to 48.2% in Q4 FY26) fund the A&P behind them, though rupee R&D spend is never disclosed.)

*Unchanged: Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 1 worsened, 2 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Sales run through state-controlled buyers/corporations, and a single one — Telangana — was material enough that its licence auction and delayed payments dented volumes and working capital for two consecutive quarters before 'progressive normalization' in Q4FY26. Growth is also concentrated in one brand: ICONiQ White contributed 10.7m of the year's cases and most of the incremental volume. (The long view had said: Results are repeatedly driven by a single state and a single brand: Telangana was ~10 of 31.7 million cases and ~65% of PET consumption, and its receivable stretch (60 to 150 days) alone flattened FY24 volumes; growth now rests overwhelmingly on ICONiQ White (10.7m of ~36m cases in FY26) while the other three millionaire brands decline.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: No evidence of cheaper substitutes or channel own-label eroding the franchise in these calls; the opposite is visible — Officer's Choice gained market share in a declining segment while its gross margin rose from over 40% to about 45%, and company gross margin expanded to 48.2% in Q4FY26 alongside selective price increases. (The long view had said: Cheaper local brands and value price points press the mass-premium core (Maharashtra Made Liquor, 'value price points in the whisky category' in Telangana and Andhra), and in a state-controlled retail channel the shop, not the brand, often steers the purchase; so far the premium has held.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The consumer shift toward premium spirits is eroding the legacy Mass Premium base — the segment degrew at industry level in H1FY26 and was flat year-on-year in Q4FY26 — but ABD is on the right side of it, with P&A rising from 39.7% to 48.2% of volume and 59.3% of value in a year. (The long view had said: The mass-premium whisky base is structurally eroding as consumers trade up — industry mass-premium degrowth in H1FY26, Officer's Choice needing efforts to 'arrest decline' — but ABD is executing the pivot successfully, with P&A salience rising from 25% (FY18) to 47.2% volume / 57.3% value in FY26 plus the ABD Maestro luxury build.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Demand is a consumable, operations-style flow business — ~9m cases sold every quarter with little swing — but margins in this window were explicitly levered to input cycles: FY26 gross margin expansion was credited to a 'favourable commodity and packaging cost environment', and Q1FY27 took a ₹24 crore hit from global supply chain disruption that cut EBITDA margin from a like-for-like 14.7% to 12.2%. (The long view had said: This is a flow/consumable business — repeat consumer demand, not customer capex — with volumes growing 6-8% and margins expanding steadily across seven post-listing quarters; management has invested through the period (PET plant, malt and ENA distilleries) rather than cutting back, and input costs are described as neutral to favourable.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Fashion Risk (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
