- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Good. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹14 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±1.4 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹14 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±3.0 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.4 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Licensing, distillery capex and state route-to-market create high entry barriers with no new entrants cited and no discount war in evidence, but pricing rationality is set by regulators, not the industry. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±3.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Licensing, excise and per-state bottling infrastructure are high entry barriers and pricing is administered rather than discount-driven, but management flags rising local brands and aggressive Maharashtra-made-liquor pricing as encroachment on that rationality.

Now: State-licensing regimes create regional strongholds and channel lock-ins — Officer's Choice Blue as a 'regional power brand', plus hard-won CSD/defence listings — though consumers themselves face no switching costs. | The long view had said: State-subject regulation and local bottling create regional pockets of advantage (Telangana distillery serving ~10m of 31.7m cases), but end-consumer switching costs are negligible and states can and do reshape markets overnight, as Maharashtra and Telangana licensing showed.
- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: Premium expansion uses a small, asset-light 'Build, Buy and Partner' model with no large or complex acquisition in the window, and the resulting portfolio is margin-accretive to mix. | The long view had said: Management repeatedly signals only selective, synergistic bolt-ons and has used an asset-light 'build, buy and partner' route for the luxury portfolio, but no actual acquisition or post-deal OPM evidence is presented across the seven calls.

Now: Calls are workmanlike and metric-led with no promoter showmanship; the celebrity element (Shreyas Iyer) is attached to brands, not to management. | The long view had said: Disclosure is largely operational and substance-led, but the incoming MD used his first call and a media interview to raise guidance to high-teens growth and an 18% EBITDA margin, which cuts against low-profile understatement.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 8 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Share gain is claimed and quantified repeatedly and against a weak category: Officer's Choice gained share in Q2, 9M and Q3 FY26 despite mass-premium segment degrowth, P&A salience rose from 39.7% to 48.2% of volume, Q1 FY27 volumes grew 6.2% versus a broadly flat industry, and country coverage went 14→39 in about two years — and it is accompanied by rising, not falling, margins. (The long view had said: Share gain is documented repeatedly and against a weak industry: P&A salience rose 39.7% (Q2 FY25) to 47.1% to 48.2% (Q1 FY27), Officer's Choice gained share while the mass premium segment degrew, ICONiQ is 'winning market share from legacy brands', and international footprint went 14 to 39 countries in two years on a higher-margin, lower-working-capital model — i.e. share not bought with profitability.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Across all four calls the company shows brand-led share gain plus premiumisation: Officer's Choice leads mass premium and is India's top exported whisky at ~45% gross margin, while ICONiQ White scaled to 10.7 million cases in FY26 and 3.1 million in Q1 FY27, with sustained A&P (nationwide 'So Smooth Must Be Magic' campaign, Shreyas Iyer collaboration) and name-stretch into Officer's Choice Blue. (The long view had said: Real heritage and stretch — Officer's Choice since 1988, four millionaire brands, extensions into Blue/Brandy, Sterling Reserve B7/B10, Zoya flavour variants — and ICONiQ White scaled to 10.7 million cases; but brand durability fails the test on the legacy portfolio, with OC Blue and Sterling Reserve requiring 'brand reset' and repackaging after multi-year declines.)

**That weakened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is regular, named new-product activity with revenue impact — three ABD Maestro launches in Q3 FY26 (Rangeela Vodka, YELLO, AODH) building to a 10-brand portfolio in one year, plus reformulated SRB7 and packaging refreshes — but this is marketing/design-led NPD; no R&D spend, R&D share of industry, or technical pipeline is disclosed in any of the four calls. (The long view had said: Named launches with quantified revenue impact recur every year across all seven calls — ICONiQ White (2.3m cases FY24 to 10.7m FY26), Zoya gin plus flavour extensions, 10 ABD Maestro brands in FY26, a 34-year single malt, deluxe vodka next — supported by the Aurangabad R&D centre and rising gross margins funding A&P, though absolute R&D spend is never disclosed.)

*Unchanged: Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: A single state's route-to-market repeatedly swung consolidated results — Telangana's licence auction and delayed payments were discussed in three of four calls, and Q4 FY26 recovery was explicitly credited to 'progressive normalization in the Telangana'. Product concentration is also real: ICONiQ White alone was 10.7m of ~36m FY26 cases and 3.1m in Q1 FY27, with Officer's Choice the other profit pillar. (The long view had said: Telangana is both the largest market (~10 of 32 million cases in FY24, ~65% of PET consumption, sole distillery and new PET plant sited there) and the customer whose non-payment stalled H2 FY24 growth; on the product side ICONiQ White (10.7m cases FY26, 3.1m in Q1 FY27) now supplies essentially all growth while the other three millionaire brands decline. Concentration sits on both the customer/state side and the single-brand side.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Mass premium whisky is a low-differentiation, price-point-led category where consumers trade down as soon as shelf price moves — Maharashtra's price changes hit 'consumer affordability and buying behavior' and offtake fell, and ABD itself is retreating from unprofitable volume via 'profitable state brand mix optimization'. Bounded, though: management is not defending a premium against own-label or cheap challengers anywhere in the calls, and premium mix and gross margin are both rising. (The long view had said: The mass-premium end, still over half of volumes, faces cheaper local brands and management is now explicitly 'driving growth at value price points'; in a state-controlled shop channel the consumer's choice is heavily shaped by shelf availability and price slabs rather than brand pull.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The consumer shift here is toward premiumisation and ABD is on the winning side of it: P&A rose from 39.7% of volume in Q2 FY25 to 48.2% of volume and 59.3% of value by Q1 FY27, with management stating premiumisation is a structural trend. The legacy mass premium base is flat-to-declining, but management attributes that to state policy and deliberate SKU-profitability pruning rather than eroding habits, and Officer's Choice still gained share with gross margins improving to ~45%. (The long view had said: The demand base under the legacy mass-premium flagship is eroding structurally — industry mass premium whisky degrew in H1 FY26 and Officer's Choice needs efforts to 'arrest decline' — while consumers migrate to premium and to Indian-origin brands; the pivot is being executed rather than merely promised.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Fashion Risk (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
