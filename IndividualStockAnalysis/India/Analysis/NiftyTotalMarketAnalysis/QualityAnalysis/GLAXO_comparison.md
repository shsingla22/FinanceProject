- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Good. **Checks that regressed:**

And from the calls: Asset-light model (CMO manufacturing, imported vaccines, no capex commentary) delivering 61% ROCE alongside brand and innovation-led moats.

Now: Prescribers buy GSK for reliability and clinical evidence (RUBY-1 data, 100-year presence), though the CMO fire that disrupted supply for three quarters is a real dent in that assurance. | The long view had said: The proposition is squarely reliability and trust — physicians and self-payers choose the original over cheaper equivalents, and vaccines are bought for certainty of quality and clinical data.

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 1 worsened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Supplier concentration did real, quantified damage this year: a fire at a single contract manufacturer constrained supply for three-plus quarters and held full-year FY26 revenue growth to 2% against underlying 5-6%. Layered on that is total dependence on the parent GSK for every growth asset (Shingrix, Trelegy, Nucala, Zejula, Jemperli), plus a visibly weak 'distributed portfolio' tail. (The long view had said: A fire at a single contract manufacturer disrupted Calpol/Cobadex supply for four consecutive quarters, costing INR25–30 crores a quarter and shaving 3–3.5% off topline growth, with management having to retract its 'supply issues are behind us' assurance a quarter later; separately the entire innovative pipeline (Shingrix, Trelegy, Nucala, Zejula, Jemperli, Blenrep) is sourced from the parent, and vaccine consignments depend on imported shipments and single-point regulatory release.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Technological Innovation (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
