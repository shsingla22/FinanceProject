# Jain Resource (JAINREC) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Jain Resource Recycling buys scrap metal from around the world and melts and refines it into usable metal, which it then sells to industrial buyers. Its main products are copper and copper-alloy products (about 55% of FY26 revenue), lead and lead-alloy ingots (about 40%) and aluminium products (about 5%), with smaller streams of tin, plastic and, soon, antimony recovered as by-products. It earns money on the spread between what it pays for scrap and what it gets for the finished metal, with sale prices linked to LME benchmark prices and largely hedged through short futures positions to protect that margin. It buys scrap from more than 120 countries, processes it at plants in the SIPCOT industrial complex at Gummidipoondi, Tamil Nadu (head office in Chennai), and sells mostly for export (62% of FY26 revenue, chiefly to China and other overseas markets) with the remaining 38% sold in India; FY26 revenue was about INR 9,543 crore with PAT of about INR 347 crore.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Copper and copper alloy products** — Recycles copper-bearing scrap into copper and copper-alloy products; the largest and most strategically important part of the business at roughly 55% of FY26 revenue (52% in 9M FY26), earning management-cited EBITDA of roughly INR 42,000-51,000 per tonne.
- **Lead and lead alloy ingots** — Recycles lead-acid battery scrap into lead and lead-alloy ingots, contributing about 40% of FY26 revenue and described as having a faster cash-collection cycle than copper.
- **Aluminium and aluminium products** — The smallest of the three main metal streams at roughly 4-5% of revenue, part of what management calls its diversified multi-metal portfolio.
- **Value-added copper products (Jain Green Technologies, Unit 3)** — A new forward-integration plant making copper anodes, cathodes, wire rods, busbars/profiles and tin-silver coated products; described as the company's biggest 'profitability driver', costing about INR 95 crore in Phase 1 funded from internal accruals, with anode production started March 2026 at 800 tonnes/month and the other lines commissioning through FY27.
- **Tin recovery** — Extracts tin as a by-product of lead-acid battery recycling; capacity was raised during the year from 125 to 500 tonnes per annum via an extra vacuum distillation furnace, described as already scaling and improving realisations.
- **Antimony extraction** — A planned unit to separate antimony from lead-antimony bullion, called a new concept in India and a profitability driver, with about INR 20 crore capex, 1,000 tonnes/month of bullion processed for roughly 100 tonnes/month of antimony, targeted for Q3 FY27.
- **Jain C&Y Circular Solutions (Ahmedabad copper scrap JV)** — A joint venture with C&Y/CNY Group to set up a 72,000 tonne-per-annum copper scrap processing plant (motor/alternator scrap, cable scrap and other scrap) yielding about 25,000 tonnes of processed copper a year; described as a volume-growth project starting operations from September 2026.
- **Kuwait battery recycling investment (Abraj Al-Khaleej)** — A board-approved 25% equity stake in a Kuwaiti company to build a 2,000 tonne-per-month battery scrap plant, aimed at securing lead raw material and a Middle East presence, targeted for Q3 FY27 but delayed by shipping disruption.
- **Plastic recycling** — Plastic from battery and cable scrap is currently recycled in-house at the copper and lead plants; a dedicated standalone plant on about six acres, costing roughly INR 15 crore, is planned for Q3 FY27 to support the company's zero-waste model.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Jain Resource is a fast-growing metals recycler whose entire economic model rests on turning assets over quickly rather than charging a premium price. Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹2.82 of sales, and the business generated ₹9,543 crore of revenue on a capex base of roughly ₹100 crore a year, with reported ROCE of 25.3% and ROE of 30.4% despite a PAT margin of only 3.6%. That is why the one screen it most nearly passes is the Low Price Plus pattern — thin margins carried by high volume — and why growth checks score positively: volumes rose about 26.5% in FY26 on 48% revenue growth, copper capacity is still only 65% utilised, and the end market itself is scored as being in secular expansion, supported by constrained mined supply, decarbonisation, EPR-driven formalisation and India emerging as a recycling hub. Sourcing across 120+ countries with 62% of revenue from exports, plus new ventures in Kuwait and Ahmedabad, adds geographic reach on top of that volume story.

The problem is that the same trait that produces the high returns also removes the protection around them. Because the company keeps only ₹9 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs and posted an FY26 EBITDA margin of 5.9%, there is no premium to defend — which is exactly why the Pricing Power, Brand Strength, Toll Roads and Innovation Dominance patterns are all marked No Fit and why the Good Enough Goods risk sits on Watch. Prices are set by a market formula the company cannot influence: it buys at about 80% of the copper LME price and sells at roughly 98–99%, and that gap narrowed from about 19% to as low as 16%. When Chinese buyers unilaterally moved the realisation formula down after their buying push ended, copper EBITDA per tonne fell through the year from ₹38,500 to ₹62,400 to ₹42,000 and then to about ₹14,000, Q4 EBITDA margin dropped to 3.5% from 7.2%, and full-year guidance was reset from ₹50,000–55,000 per tonne in February to ₹30,000–33,000 in May. This is the Cyclicality risk, the only one rated Elevated, and it is inseparable from the pricing checks that score worst. The same China-anchored offtake that drove the volume growth behind the Market Share Gainers pattern is also the reason Stakeholder Concentration sits on Watch, with receivable delays traced to material reaching China. It also explains the one blemish on the otherwise creditable Corporate Culture read: management beat its own 20% growth guidance with 38–48% growth and disclosed the Iran shipping and machinery delays promptly, but its confident February per-tonne guidance had to be cut sharply three months later.

The small capital base cuts the same way. Total FY27 capex guidance of only ₹115–120 crore across antimony, plastics, Ahmedabad and Kuwait, funded from internal accruals, is a sign of discipline, but it also means the assets are cheap to copy, which is why Cost to Replicate is only a Partial fit and why the New Entrants risk is on Watch — management answered a direct question about its moat with a list of selling points rather than structural barriers, and acknowledged that foreign recyclers are relocating into India, with the C&Y venture as the example. The response to all of this is the Jain Green Technologies programme in anode, cathode, wire rod and busbar, with anode production started in March 2026: it is the reason Forward Integrators registers as a Partial fit and it is also named as the main defence against cyclicality, though the framework notes it is midstream integration rather than a route to the end customer, and that the new processes for antimony, tin distillation and value-added copper are licensed or bought in rather than internally developed, with no R&D spend disclosed. The most immediate warning sign is that headline profitability and cash have parted company: on shareholders' funds plus borrowings, the business produced a negative 21% cash return this year, with working capital running at 82 days in the third quarter — growth is currently consuming cash even as the reported returns look strong. The combined rating lands at 48 out of 100, or Mixed, from 44 on business quality, 33 on multibagger fit and 74 on risk safety, with overall resilience judged Sound: a genuinely growing operator in a growing market, but one whose earnings belong to the metal price rather than to the company.
