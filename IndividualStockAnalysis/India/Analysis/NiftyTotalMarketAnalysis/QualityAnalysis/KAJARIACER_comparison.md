Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.8 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Structural cost pressure (gas prices, Iran-linked supply issues) has shut Morbi capacity and raised the entry bar, and the industry took broad 12-17% price increases together rather than discounting — though Kajaria itself discounted in Q3. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Entry barriers are low (a plant costs only ~Rs.100-120cr) and pricing has been chronically irrational, with realizations falling ~9% over three years and Morbi historically cutting prices until a gas shock forced a hike.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 4 strengthened, 1 weakened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Innovation shows up only as mix shift — converting a 9.1 msm Gailpur line from ceramic floor tiles to glazed vitrified, plus SKU rationalisation — with no R&D spend, pipeline or named-launch revenue disclosed on any call. (The long view had said: There is regular product/technology upgrading — Continua+ slabs from Sacmi at Srikalahasti (2022), GVT mix shift, conversion of a 9.1 msm Gailpur ceramic line to GVT (FY26) — but no R&D spend is ever disclosed, no pipeline or industry-R&D-share language, and the innovation is capex-led capability rather than margin-funded invention.)
- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Management convened a dedicated December 2025 call to disclose a fraud at the Kerovit Global step-down subsidiary that its own new controls had surfaced, admitted the earlier lapse plainly ('a lapse on our part... we ignored that'), appointed E&Y as forensic auditor, and stated an explicit disclosure principle; cost-saving and unification promises made in Oct 2025 were visibly delivered by Q4 FY26 under stable family leadership. (The long view had said: Positives are real: stable family leadership with succession under way (Chetan/Rishi to Vice Chairman/MD, grandson Kartik on calls), debt-light balance sheet funding capex from internal accruals with a 40-50% dividend policy, and prompt disclosure of a subsidiary fraud with an E&Y forensic audit and tightened signing authority; against that, guidance was repeatedly missed and eventually abandoned, and the fraud itself is a control failure.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: No discussion of licence fees, AMC/service contracts, renewals, attach rates or customer advances funding the business in any of the four calls. (The long view had said: Across all 17 calls the model is one-off product sale of tiles/bathware/adhesives — no licence fees, AMC, subscriptions, attach rates or renewal language anywhere; working capital is positive and discussed as a drag (57 days in Jul-2022, 59 in Jul-2024, 64 in Dec-2025, 51 in Mar-2026), the opposite of customers funding the business.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Q4 FY26 showed 12-13% price increases in North (16-17% Morbi) alongside 11% volume growth, but management framed it as gas-cost pass-through rather than value pricing, and Q3 FY26 realisation fell ~2-2.4% on inventory-liquidation discounts — pricing follows input costs, it does not lead them. (The long view had said: The record shows price deflation, the pattern's disqualifier: own-manufactured realisation fell ~9% from Rs.401/sqm in Q3FY23 to Rs.365/sqm in Q3FY26, discounts were given in Q3FY26 to clear SKUs, and the FY26 12-17% hikes were pure gas-cost pass-through, not value capture.)

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Brand is stretched across Kajaria tiles, Kerovit bathware and Gresbond adhesives with adhesives more than doubling (INR21cr to INR44cr in Q4), and ~INR24 crores of quarterly A&P is disclosed — but FY26 ad spend was cut year-on-year and the brand did not prevent flat tile volumes for three quarters. (The long view had said: Brand equity is repeatedly the stated lever for both price and share (2% hike in May-2022, share gains in a flat industry), backed by sustained A&P of ~Rs.100 crores a year, ~1,700 dealers with 380 exclusive showrooms, and stretch into bathware (Kerovit), adhesives and plywood under the same name — though the ply adjacency was shut by FY26, showing the stretch has limits.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Tiles are a low-complexity product Kajaria does not even need to make itself — it outsources ceramic and polished vitrified tiles and negotiates purchase prices down — and cheap Morbi output is the classic good-enough substitute, with the dealer/contractor intermediary largely controlling what the end-customer buys. The last 12 months affirmatively show pricing power holding, though: 12-17% price increases passed through to cover gas with 'some added margin' and EBITDA at 19.19%, with brand spend being raised rather than cut. (The long view had said: Own-manufactured tile realization fell ~9% from Rs.401/sqm (Q3FY23) to Rs.365/sqm (Q3FY26), and management concedes it cannot price up against the Morbi cluster's cheaper equivalent product; in Q3FY26 it gave discounts to liquidate SKUs. The choice is intermediated by dealers and increasingly by architects/interior designers, exactly the Nobel Biocare structure where the intermediary captures the premium.)
- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Tiles demand tracks Indian construction/renovation activity and the calls show a soft patch (flattish tile revenue in Q2 and Q3 FY26) followed by an 11% volume rebound in Q4, while margins swung from ~10-12.5% in FY25 quarters to 19.19% in Q4 FY26; input gas is an openly commodity-priced, volatile cost (North gas INR55.5/SCM in Q4 to INR62.5 in April, West INR46.6 to INR79) forcing 12-17% price hikes. Demand is however operations/renovation-linked retail rather than tied to any single customer's capex programme, which bounds the cycle. (The long view had said: Across all 17 calls the P&L swings with construction demand and with gas, a commodity input management repeatedly refuses to forecast: gas moved Rs.34→Rs.69 (FY23), back to ~Rs.37 (FY25), then to Rs.84 landed in Morbi by Apr-2026, and EBITDA margin ranged from 15.2% (Q1FY23) to 10.0% (Q4FY25) to 19.2% (Q4FY26) with volumes flat for most of FY26. Management withdrew margin and volume guidance twice ('please do not talk about this subject', and again in May-2026), which is itself an admission that the cycle, not the franchise, sets near-term outcomes.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Results are driven by a wide dealer network rather than any named customer, licensor or supplier; the whole FY26 narrative is about restructuring and broadening that base — one salesperson per dealer post-unification, tail-end dealers churned out, white-space dealers added, and a new 18-20 person architect/influencer team created. No analyst raises a single-relationship renewal. (The long view had said: No customer concentration — ~1,700 dealers, 380 exclusive showrooms, and no region above 35% of sales — but supply is concentrated: roughly 27–28% of material is outsourced from the Morbi cluster, and when Morbi shut in Mar-2026 management flagged 'outsourcing is still a little problem' and could not say how much material it would receive.)

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
