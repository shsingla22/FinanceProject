# Sky Gold & Diamonds (SKYGOLD) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Sky Gold & Diamonds (formerly Sky Gold Limited) is a Mumbai-based jewellery manufacturer, set up in 2008, that designs and makes gold jewellery — its specialty being lightweight 22-carat pieces made by the casting process, using imported machinery from Germany, Turkey and Italy. It sells almost entirely B2B: the buyers are big organized retail chains such as Malabar, Joyalukkas, Kalyan, GRT, Senco, Reliance Jewels, CaratLane, Aditya Birla, PN Gadgil, Thangamayil and Damas, plus large wholesalers, so its products end up in thousands of showrooms rather than its own stores. It earns money on the 'making charge' it adds on top of the gold — a thin margin business (gross margin around 6%-8%, EBITDA near 5%) where profit depends on volume of gold processed and on shifting the mix toward fancier, higher-charge designs; management stresses that gold price is a pass-through it does not control. It manufactures in Mumbai and a larger leased Navi Mumbai facility, sells mainly across India, and has a growing export/international arm (Middle East/GCC, UAE, Singapore, Malaysia), with revenue scaling from about INR796 crore in FY22 to a Q4 FY26 quarterly run rate of roughly INR1,911 crore and a stated FY27 target near INR8,100 crore.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Core B2B gold jewellery manufacturing (Sky Gold parent)** — The main business: designing and casting lightweight 22-carat plain gold jewellery — chains, bracelets, bangles, pendants, earrings — for large retail chains and wholesalers, and it is the bulk of revenue (guided at INR2,700 crore of the INR3,300 crore FY25 target), with monthly gold processing rising from about 250 kg to 345 kg between FY24 and Q2 FY25.
- **Value-added / studded, diamond and lower-carat jewellery** — Higher-margin studded, diamond, 9/14/18-carat and rose-gold ranges, backed by a diamond design studio in Andheri near SEEPZ; management says these value-added products went from 4%-5% of revenue three years ago to more than 50% by FY26, and it is the main lever for improving margins.
- **Subsidiaries — Star Mangalsutra and Sparkling Chains** — Acquired companies making mangalsutra and chains that widen the product range and were said to lift the addressable market by about 65%; they were guided to add roughly INR600 crore in FY25 (INR150-350 crore per quarter) on top of the parent.
- **Design, merchandising and co-creation** — A roughly 150-person design and merchandising team (doubled over three years) with a library of lakhs of designs that co-creates products with retailers — sketches, prototypes and samples before mass production — which management calls its real differentiator, citing a high design-selection rate, lower unsold inventory, and awards from CaratLane, Aditya Birla, De Beers and the World Gold Council.
- **Exports / international business** — Sales to overseas markets including the GCC, UAE, Singapore and Malaysia, including onboarding Damas (a large GCC chain, requiring 21-carat product) and a Dubai operation called Sky Souk; exports were INR63.9 crore, about 9% of sales, in Q2 FY25.
- **Dealership / distribution network** — A newer channel selling through distributors and dealers to reach smaller retailers beyond the big chains, described as the next growth area and credited with better order visibility and working-capital efficiency.
- **Custom cell and digital ordering app** — A large made-to-order unit that produces one-off pieces when end customers bring designs to a retailer (small volume but strengthens retailer relationships), and an app where clients browse the design bank and place orders directly, already carrying 7%-8% of orders by early 2026.
- **Vesteria / US B2C (early-stage, 2023)** — A tie-up with Varanium to launch its own 18- and 22-carat brand online for US women — the only consumer-facing venture mentioned, described as just launched in FY23 and not discussed in later calls.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Sky Gold's story starts with growth, and on that count the record is unambiguous. Sales went from ₹796 crores in FY22 to an exit run-rate of ₹7,650 crores, monthly volumes from 250 kg to over 345 kg, and exports from 1-2% of sales to 11%, while the customer list grew to include nearly every major organized chain — CaratLane, Malabar, Kalyan, Reliance, Damas and others. This is the one pattern the screen calls a strong fit, Market Share Gainers, and it rests on two supports the quality checks also flag: an end market of roughly $90 billion growing 12-15% a year with a durable shift from unorganized to organized players, and a visible pool of share to take, because unorganized manufacturers are being squeezed by the working capital cost of higher gold prices while Sky Gold has capital available. Growth is also increasingly the right kind — value-added products went from 4-5% of revenue to over half, gross margin widened by 245 basis points to 8.45%, and management dropped volume guidance in favour of value. Talent has been built to match, with the design and merchandising team doubled to about 150 people, senior hires from Titan and elsewhere, ESOPs for long-serving staff and an internal promotion to CEO.

The same traits that produce the growth also produce most of the concerns, and they are not separate stories. The business is asset-light — every ₹1 of assets throws off ₹2.87 of sales, off leased facilities — and that high-turnover, low-margin shape is exactly why the Low Price Plus pattern is a likely fit, backed by a genuinely itemised cost culture: gold loss cut from 1.5% to 0.5% (worth 100 basis points of margin), jewellery made 20-30% lighter, delivery 30-40% faster, working capital days down from 75 in November 2024 to 59. But low margin is a thin cushion. After direct input costs the company keeps only about ₹8 of every ₹100 of sales, which the quality framework scores as weak pricing power, and the headline return on capital is negative: -₹2 of operating cash for every ₹100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings this year. The 8.45% margin comes largely from a back-to-back hedged gold pass-through model, and management credits the recent improvement mainly to cost work rather than to charging more — which is why Pricing Power scores only a partial fit despite making charges rising from ₹300 to ₹500-600 per gram back in February 2023.

Where the company sits in the chain explains the rest. Sky Gold manufactures under retailers' brands: the shopper buying a Malabar or Kalyan piece does not know who made it, its own collection names are acknowledged as unknown to consumers, and roughly 90% of chain sourcing is outsourced across a wide field of competing manufacturers. That single fact makes Brand Strength a clear non-fit, keeps Friendly Middleman only partial, and is the source of both the Good Enough Goods risk and the Stakeholder Concentration risk — the business started with its top five customers at 70% of revenue, Malabar the largest, and still sells to a finite set of chains that control shelf space. Its defence is design and service depth: a nine lakh design library, 25 jewellery projects, co-creation with retailers, one of India's biggest custom cells, an ordering app taking 7-8% of orders. Yet design taste turns over in one or two months, so the same design-led engine behind the partial Innovation Dominance fit is also the Fashion Risk. And nothing here would stop a well-funded newcomer — the barriers named are capital, machines, designers and relationships, all rebuildable, as Sky Gold's own sevenfold scaling since FY23 demonstrates, hence Cost to Replicate scores no fit. Gold price is the connective thread on the risk side too: it drives the share gains from weakening rivals and it is the swing factor behind cyclicality, having risen 60-70% then corrected 15%, with policy adding to it after the Prime Minister's call to curb gold imports softened industry demand. Resilience still reads as sound — hedging removes the metal exposure, demand is occasion-led, and there is no gold metal loan exposure — and the combined rating lands at 56 out of 100, Decent, with solid risk safety (80) offsetting middling business quality (59) and a low multibagger fit (33).

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is denominated in a commodity whose price management repeatedly discusses as the swing factor — gold up 60-70% in a year, then a 15% correction, with demand softening on spikes; by Jun 2026 management withdrew volume guidance entirely because of gold price volatility. But demand is consumer flow demand (weddings, festivals, gifting) rather than customer capex, and every gram is back-to-back hedged, so this is commodity-linked volatility rather than a capex cycle.

> "we would like to avoid volume guidance primarily because of the volatility in the gold price, which is beyond our control" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/occasion-led consumer demand rather than customer capex; back-to-back hedging ("we do not take any single gram there is no exposure"); countercyclical advantage as unorganized manufacturers struggle with working capital when gold spikes*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 0 of the last 4 years with swings of ±26 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
