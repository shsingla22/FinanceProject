# Premier Energies (PREMIERENE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Premier Energies is an Indian manufacturer of solar equipment — it makes solar cells and solar modules (panels), and is building out the steps above and below that in the chain: ingots and wafers, aluminium frames for panels, battery energy storage systems, inverters, and, through its Transcon acquisition, electrical transformers. It sells mainly to buyers in India's domestic solar market — utility-scale project developers, government-linked schemes such as the PM Surya Ghar rooftop programme and KUSUM, and other module makers who buy its cells — with exports a very small slice (under 3% of revenue in Q2 FY25 and less than 1% of the order book by mid-2025; planned US cell plans were put on hold). It makes money by producing and selling this hardware in volume from its own plants at Sitarampur (Telangana) and Naidupeta (Andhra Pradesh), earning higher margins where it is backward-integrated and where it supplies DCR (domestic content requirement) cells and modules, of which it says only two or three Indian players are capable. FY26 revenue was INR 8,026 crores, up 20.7%, with an operational EBITDA margin of 30.4% and PAT of INR 1,510 crores; the order book stood at INR 14,010 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Solar cells** — Manufactures solar cells (Mono PERC historically, now TOPCon, including a 1.2 GW G12R line and newly launched Zero Busbar cells) both for its own module plants and for outside sale — cells rose to about 39% of a quarter's order intake, and capacity is set to reach 10.6 GW.
- **Solar modules (panels)** — Assembles finished solar panels, including DCR and non-DCR versions and new All-Black modules, sold on 25-30 year warranties; capacity is going to 11.1 GW with a 5.6 GW Sitarampur plant completed in FY26, making the company one of India's largest and most integrated cell-and-module makers.
- **Ingot and wafer** — A backward-integration step to make the silicon wafers that cells are cut from — first announced as a 2 GW plant, later a 10 GW line under construction at Naidupeta, still pre-revenue.
- **Aluminium frames** — A 36,000 tonne-per-year plant for the metal frames around its own modules (captive use), costing about INR 260 crores and due to commission by December 2026, expected to add 100-150 basis points to EBITDA margin.
- **Battery energy storage systems (BESS)** — A planned 12 GW vertical under the company's 'Mission 2028' roadmap, targeted to start contributing to revenue from FY27, aimed at a fast-growing Indian storage tender market.
- **Inverters** — A planned 3 GW inverter manufacturing vertical, also part of Mission 2028 and expected to start earning revenue from the beginning of FY27.
- **Transformers (Transcon)** — A 51%-owned acquisition completed in December 2025 making low-voltage, inverter-duty and increasingly higher-value MV/HV/EHV transformers; FY26 revenue of INR 423 crores and PAT of INR 45 crores, with capacity going to 16.75 GVA by July 2026 and management targeting over INR 1,000 crores of revenue in two to two-and-a-half years.
- **KSolare** — A further acquisition management said was due to close around February 2026; the calls give no detail on its size or activity beyond that.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

> "our foray into aluminium frame manufacturing business by setting up a 36,000 metric ton per annum facility for captive consumption" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 2 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is tied directly to customers' capex programmes (IPP/utility-scale project build-outs, rooftop scheme installations) and margins are riding an abnormal DCR-scarcity peak that management itself expects to soften as industry cell capacity arrives; input costs (silver, copper, polysilicon, wafers) swing results, with a Q1 FY26 inventory write-down on falling Chinese cell/wafer prices cutting operating EBITDA from 32.6% to 30.1%. On top of this the company is committing ~INR12,000 crores of capex over three years and letting net debt rise into the peak — the classic expansionary over-investment pattern.

> "as more cell lines come up and competition catches up, there would be a softening of EBITDA margins from where they are today" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Some flow-like, operations-linked demand from rooftop (PM-Surya Ghar) and replacement/consumable-style volumes; brownfield expansion at 30-40% lower capex and a stated discipline of debt/equity ~1 and debt/EBITDA ~1.5*

- ⚠️ Year-to-year sales growth swings by ±61 percentage points — boom-and-bust revenue, not a steady compounder.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from 4% to 30% (swinging ±10.5 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 19% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 46%).

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Essentially the entire margin structure is policy-created: DCR cells command ~USD0.23-0.24 versus ~USD0.155-0.16 for non-DCR, ALMM-2 from June 2026 converts the market to domestic cells, and demand comes from PM-Surya Ghar, KUSUM and government-linked tenders while ALMM-3 timing dictates the INR5,900 crore ingot-wafer plan. Management has deferred BESS cell backward integration outright pending government localisation clarity and deferred US plans pending IRA/tariff clarity, and the sector also depends on anti-dumping duty petitions — small policy changes would reset economics, exactly the solar-subsidy mechanism to be wary of.

> "we would want to wait for more clarity from Government of India... As of today, it doesn't make sense for us to invest on backward integration because the market is still now open to China" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Some diversification into transformers (Transcon), BESS and inverters, and growth in the private C&I/open-access segment that is now roughly equal in size to utility-scale*
