# Supriya Lifescience (SUPRIYA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Supriya Lifescience is an Indian bulk-drug maker that manufactures active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) — the chemical substances that go inside finished medicines — and sells them to pharmaceutical companies rather than to patients. It makes money by producing these chemicals at large scale at its plants in Maharashtra (Lote Parshuram in Ratnagiri, plus a newer site at Ambernath) and selling them to roughly 1,700 customers in about 128 countries, with exports making up around 82-83% of revenue; Europe is its biggest market (about 40% of FY26 revenue), followed by Latin America (~24%) and North America (~6%). Its products span therapy areas management lists as anaesthetics, anti-histamines/anti-allergics, anti-diabetics, anti-anxiety, vitamins, ADHD and, more recently, cardiovascular; margins are helped by 'backward integration', meaning it makes its own key raw materials for products accounting for ~74-76% of sales. In FY26 it reported revenue of INR 828 crores (up ~19%), EBITDA margin of 35.5% and profit after tax of INR 209 crores, and it guides to roughly 20% annual growth and an INR 1,000 crore revenue milestone in FY27.

**The parts of the business:**

- **APIs (bulk drugs / prime APIs)** — The core business — manufacturing and selling active pharmaceutical ingredients across anaesthetics, anti-histamines, anti-diabetics, anti-anxiety, vitamins, ADHD and cardiovascular therapies; management says the top therapeutic areas contributed about 91% of FY26 revenue, so this is essentially the whole company today.
- **CMO / CDMO (contract development and manufacturing)** — Making molecules to order for other drug companies, from big pharma to innovators, using Supriya's large-scale and hazardous-chemistry capability; management describes the business as shifting from purely 'prime APIs' to a combination of APIs plus CMO/CDMO, with the newly commissioned Ambernath facility (to be capitalized in Q4 FY26) called a key milestone for this segment, but no separate revenue figure is given.
- **Backward integration** — Not a separate product line but the way it competes — making its own key starting materials instead of buying them; it covered the top 12 of about 38 products (~70% of sales) in 2022 and about 74-76% of revenue by FY26, and management credits it for the high gross margins and for keeping competitors out of new products.
- **Exports / regulated markets** — The main channel — exports are 82-83% of sales, and management is deliberately shifting the mix toward 'regulated' markets (Europe, North America) where prices and returns are higher; Europe went from 33-43% of revenue in earlier years to ~51% in one quarter of FY25 and ~40% for full-year FY26, and the Lote plant received a USFDA inspection report with a Voluntary Action Indicated classification in FY26.
- **Protein project (A21 site)** — A newer product line aimed initially at Indian formulators plus export samples to Australia and Southeast Asia; management says it is still progressing and will take a couple more quarters before contributing meaningful revenue.
- **Formulations and new capacity (Patalganga, Isambe, Module E)** — Planned expansion rather than current revenue — capacity was doubled to about 1,020 kilolitres via Module E, and the new Patalganga site (all clearances received, ~INR 200 crores of Phase 1 capex) will add two API/advanced-intermediate blocks and two formulation blocks, taking the company beyond bulk chemicals into finished formulations over time.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Supriya Lifescience is a bulk drug ingredient maker that sells almost entirely to other pharmaceutical companies — 82% of sales are exports, spread across roughly 1,700 customers in 128 countries. What customers actually buy is certainty: a plant that passes GMP/cGMP and USFDA audits and makes its own key starting materials, so supply does not fail. That is the highest-scoring part of the quality framework (Assurance benefits, +2), and it explains why the same three ideas keep reappearing. Once a customer writes Supriya into its regulatory filings, the relationship typically lasts five to ten years, which is the basis of the Partial monopoly / switching costs check (+2) and of the Toll Roads pattern. The competitors being displaced are non-GMP, non-ISO Chinese suppliers that global drug makers are actively trying to move away from — an unusually weak set of Share donators (+2), and the reason the Market Share Gainers pattern is judged a likely fit, with Europe rising from 10% of revenue in early FY22 to 51% in Q1 FY25 and 40% for FY26. Management has funded all of this out of its own cash — debt to equity of 0.01, no working capital limits drawn for years, no acquisitions, and capacity built through the FY23-24 downturn — which is why Corporate Culture is the single strongest pattern in the screen.

The interesting part is that the trait doing the most work is also the source of the biggest risks. Making its own key starting materials (76% of sales by FY26, up from 70% in Aug 2022) is what lets Supriya undercut China, and it is the core of both the Low Price Plus and Cost to Replicate patterns — yet both are only partial fits, because the defence management offers is that it can match 'current Chinese prices'. That is a cost argument, not a pricing argument, and it is exactly why the Good Enough Goods risk sits on Watch: the price benchmark is set by someone else. The screen is blunt about this — Pricing Power is a no fit, because per-kilo prices have been flat for years, growth has come from volume, and margins were guided down from 39% to 33-35% as new products must first scale in cheaper semi-regulated markets. That same margin behaviour shows up on the risk side as Cyclicality, rated Elevated, with EBITDA margins swinging from about 53% in Q2 FY22 to 31% in Q1 FY23, back to 39% in Q1 FY25, and now guided lower. Similarly, the move into larger-volume molecules is what makes Market Share Gainers credible, but it is also precisely what the New Entrants risk flags, with the Chairman conceding that competing head-on with Indian manufacturers would be difficult. And on funding: the business runs about 167 days of cash tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers (Cost of working capital, -2), which is the same fact that rules out the Recurring Revenue pattern — there are no customer advances or renewals financing the business, only one-off product shipments.

Put together, this is a well-run business whose economics do not yet match the quality of its execution. The framework rates Capital Allocation and Return on Capital at zero: spending is heavily growth-oriented — capacity doubled to 1,020 KL, INR140-160cr at Ambernath, INR152cr of capex in FY26, INR200cr earmarked for Patalganga Phase 1 — but the returns on that spending are still lagging, which is why Capex for growth scores only +1. The risk review is reassuring on the things that usually sink a company: government dependency is low (the company declined the PLI scheme outright), technology disruption is low, and fashion risk is low, giving a risk-safety score of 78 and an overall resilience read of Sound. Where the record is thinner is concentration — the top three products are about 44-45% of revenue, the single DSM contract is discussed on every call, and the 300-tonne cardiovascular order rests on three or four customers — offset only partly by the 3-4 new launches a year the doubled R&D spend is producing. The combined rating lands at 60 out of 100, or 'Decent': strong business quality at 63 and high risk safety at 78, dragged down by a multibagger fit of just 41, since six of the eleven patterns are outright no fits and three more are only partial. Owning this is a bet on a disciplined, conservatively financed supplier steadily taking share from weaker Chinese competition — not on a business that can raise its own prices.

*"A culture that compounds"*

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

## What to watch

- Margin direction versus the 33-35% guidance — the Cyclicality risk is rated Elevated precisely because margins have already swung between 53% and 31%; a further slide would confirm the Pricing Power 'no fit' verdict rather than a temporary mix effect.
- Returns on the Ambernath, Patalganga and Module E spending — Capital Allocation and Return on Capital both score zero today, and the Capex for growth check notes incremental returns are lagging; evidence that this capex is earning its cost would lift the weakest part of the rating.
- The 167-day working capital cycle (Cost of working capital, -2) — whether growth makes it worse, since there are no customer advances funding the business (Recurring Revenue, no fit).
- Concentration in the top three APIs (44-45% of revenue), the DSM contract and the three-to-four-customer cardiovascular book — the Stakeholder Concentration risk is on Watch, and loss of any one of these would test how much the new-launch cadence actually de-risks the portfolio.
- Whether Chinese pricing or new Indian entrants erode the cost advantage — both the Good Enough Goods and New Entrants risks are on Watch, and the Low Price Plus and Cost to Replicate patterns are only partial fits, so a sustained price war would remove the main defence management cites.
- Continued clean regulatory outcomes and new CEP/USDMF approvals — the Assurance benefits (+2) and switching-costs (+2) checks and the Toll Roads pattern all rest on customers keeping Supriya in their dossiers; a failed audit would undo several strengths at once.
