# Alembic Pharma (APLLTD) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Alembic Pharmaceuticals is an India-based drug maker that manufactures and sells medicines — both finished formulations (tablets, oral liquids, injectables, oncology products, and animal health products) and the active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) that go into medicines. It earns money two ways: selling branded medicines to doctors and chemists in India through its own field sales force, and selling generic copies of off-patent drugs in the US and other regulated overseas markets, where it must first get regulatory approvals (ANDAs from the US FDA) for each product and then launch and defend market share against price erosion. Overseas is the larger half of the business — the US is its single biggest territory, followed by other export markets in Europe, Asia and rest-of-world — and it spends heavily on R&D (guided at roughly INR 600-800 crores a year recently, about 9-11% of sales) to keep filing and launching new products. Group revenue was about INR 6,229 crores in FY24 and running near INR 1,850-1,900 crores a quarter by FY26, and from Q4 FY26 it has also started selling its own branded product (Pivya, an antibiotic) directly in the US.

**The parts of the business:**

- **India Branded Business (branded formulations)** — Branded prescription medicines sold to Indian doctors and patients across specialty areas like gynaecology, gastroenterology, antidiabetic, cardiology and ophthalmology plus acute/anti-infective and respiratory products; about INR 2,200 crores in FY24 and INR 652 crores in the December 2025 quarter, growing only 4-7% a year and, management admits, lagging the market.
- **Animal Healthcare / Veterinary** — Medicines for animals, sold within the India business and repeatedly singled out by management as one of its strongest and fastest-growing pieces (about 35% growth in one early quarter, and still called an important growth driver in FY26).
- **US Generics** — Generic versions of off-patent drugs sold in the United States, the company's biggest single market — roughly $47-56 million a quarter in earlier years — built on 15-25 product launches annually from a filing pipeline that had reached 270 cumulative ANDAs by late 2025, and constantly squeezed by price erosion.
- **Ex-US Generics / International formulations (Europe, Canada, ROW)** — The same generic formulations sold in regulated and rest-of-world markets outside America; smaller than the US (about INR 182 crores in a mid-2022 quarter) but the fastest-growing export line lately, up 23% in FY24, 36% in rest-of-world markets in Q3 FY26 and 20% for FY26.
- **API business** — Bulk active pharmaceutical ingredients sold mainly to other drugmakers in regulated markets (about INR 233 crores in a mid-2022 quarter); volumes hold up well but pricing has been a persistent drag, with management guiding to high-single to low-double-digit growth for FY27.
- **US Branded / specialty business** — A newly started business selling its own branded drug in the US — Pivya, an oral antibiotic for uncomplicated urinary tract infections in women, launched in the March 2026 quarter under licence with a high royalty payout — described as a deliberately small, measured entry that hurts near-term profit but is meant to build a specialty platform over several years.
- **R&D and manufacturing facilities** — The cost engine behind everything else: R&D of roughly INR 475-800 crores a year focused on injectables, peptides, oncology, oral solids and early-entry filings (First-to-File, Day-1, NCE-1), supported by newer plants including injectable and oncology facilities at Karakhadi and a formulations site at Indore whose rising utilisation management ties directly to future margins.

### The story in depth

Alembic Pharma scores 36 out of 100 — Mixed, with resilience judged STRAINED — and the reason is that its genuine strengths and its genuine weaknesses are the same few facts seen from different sides. The company keeps about 73 rupees of gross profit on every 100 rupees of sales, which the quality framework credits as real customer benefit (Intangible benefits, +1), and it has grown sales about 12% a year on average, right inside the band the framework prizes (Persistence of growth, +1). But almost none of that comes from being able to set its own prices. The Pricing Power pattern is a flat NO FIT: price erosion in the high single to low double digits shows up in every management call from August 2022 through May 2026, and gross margin has already drifted from roughly 74-75% down to 71-72%. The Pricing, Mix and Volume check (-2) says the same thing in the language of the numbers — growth is almost entirely more units and new launches, offsetting a price line that falls every single year. The high margin is therefore a level, not a defence.

What holds the growth up is the research and launch engine, and this is where one trait produces both the best pattern and several of the worst risks. R&D of INR 475 crores in FY24 rising to a guided INR 750-800 crores by FY27 (8-11% of sales), 270 cumulative ANDA filings, 25-27 US launches a year, and a named push into peptides, injectables, ophthalmics and Day-1 filings is enough for Innovation Dominance to be the one LIKELY FIT on the whole board. But the same pipeline that generates the launches is, by the record's own account, the thing competitors are also building: Cost to Replicate is NO FIT because FDA-approved Baroda plants and an ANDA pipeline are replicable by any funded entrant, and New Entrants is ELEVATED because a widening field of Indian suppliers chasing the same US molecules has structurally reset the economics — management has actually lowered its internal return thresholds for new R&D projects because returns fell. So the launch engine must keep running simply to stand still, and every launch it produces enters a market that the framework rates as having no oligopoly (-2), no switching costs (-2), no network effects (-2) and low barriers with irrational pricing (-1). That is the Good Enough Goods risk at HIGH, and it is why Toll Roads and Low Price Plus are both NO FIT: there is nothing that stops a customer switching, and the stated strategy is deliberately not to compete on price. Industry Structure at -1.33 is the framework's blunt summary of the same picture, and the ±6 point swing in profit margins connects straight to the ELEVATED Cyclicality verdict, where EBITDA fell to INR 9 crores in Q1FY23 before recovering to INR 263 crores by Q4FY24.

The third thread runs through capital and the India business, and it also cuts both ways. The FY22-24 build of four new facilities put roughly INR 300 crores a year of costs through the profit and loss account with very little revenue behind it for years — the Capital Allocation area scores -0.83 and Return on Capital sits at zero — though that cycle is now finished, with FY26 spend limited to maintenance and debottlenecking at INR 300-350 crores. Alongside it, about 381 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers (-2), and roughly INR 3,000 crores of net working capital is discussed only as a drag, never as customer money funding the business, which is why Recurring Revenue is only PARTIAL. That same dependence on a small set of consolidated US buyers shows up as ELEVATED Stakeholder Concentration, where one logistics change moved a quarter from $70 million to $47 million. In India, the company chose restraint — promotional spend deliberately held back under the UCMP marketing code with no field-force expansion (-1) — and the cost of that choice is visible in three separate places at once: Brand Strength, Friendly Middleman and Market Share Gainers are all only PARTIAL because India growth of 4-7% has trailed the wider market for three to four years, and the UCMP code itself is part of the WATCH-rated Government Dependency risk alongside the FDA remediation at Karakhadi that suppressed US revenue for about two years. On the credit side, Management scores positive (+0.33): the promoter family has been stable throughout, bad news has been disclosed promptly, R&D is gated on returns and the balance sheet is conservative. The genuine bright spots are all in the silver linings rather than the core — ophthalmic leadership in the US, ex-US regulated markets compounding around 20% a year, ROW up 36%, animal health, and the first US branded launch (Pivya, via Alembic Therapeutics with its own field force) which for now costs 100-150 basis points of margin. Meanwhile the risks that would normally frighten a pharmaceutical investor are absent: Technological Innovation and Fashion Risk are both LOW, because demand rests on prescriptions across many therapy areas and no platform shift appears anywhere in fifteen calls. The verdict is not a broken company — it is a well-run company in a structurally poor place.

## What to watch

- Whether gross margin stabilises above the current 71-72% or keeps drifting down — continued slippage would confirm the Good Enough Goods risk (HIGH) and settle the Pricing Power NO FIT permanently.
- Whether India branded growth actually catches up to the wider market, as promised in the Q1 FY27 call — that single outcome moves Brand Strength, Friendly Middleman and Market Share Gainers off PARTIAL and reverses the -1 on advertisement and promotion.
- Whether the step-up in R&D to INR 750-800 crores by FY27 produces revenue at the old return thresholds or only at the lowered ones — this is the test of whether Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT) is being outrun by the New Entrants risk (ELEVATED).
- Whether the 381-day cash cycle and the ~INR 3,000 crores of net working capital start to shrink now that capex has dropped to maintenance levels — the cost-of-working-capital check is the single worst score (-2) in the capital allocation area.
- Whether the higher-barrier categories — ophthalmics, complex injectables, peptides, Day-1 filings — plus the ex-US markets growing around 20% become large enough to set the group's economics, rather than remaining silver linings attached to a commoditised core.
