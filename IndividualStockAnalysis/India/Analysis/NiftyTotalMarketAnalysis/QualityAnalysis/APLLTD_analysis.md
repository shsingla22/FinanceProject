# Alembic Pharma (APLLTD) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Alembic Pharmaceuticals (NSE: APLLTD, BSE: 533573) is an India-based drug maker headquartered in Vadodara, Gujarat, that manufactures and sells finished medicines plus the active chemical ingredients that go into medicines. It earns money three ways: selling branded prescription medicines (and animal health products) to doctors and chemists in India; selling generic copies of off-patent drugs, mainly in the US but also in Europe and other rest-of-world markets; and selling bulk active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) to other drug companies, largely in regulated markets. The business runs on a pipeline model — it spends heavily on R&D (roughly INR 475-800 crores a year, about 8-12% of sales) to file ANDAs with the US FDA (270 cumulative filings by Q3 FY26) and launch a stream of new products each year, while running FDA-inspected plants for oral solids, injectables and oncology. Consolidated revenue was about INR 6,229 crores in FY24 and grew to roughly INR 7,300+ crores by FY26 (quarterly run-rate around INR 1,850 crores), with gross margins management targets at 70-75%.

**The parts of the business:**

- **India Branded Business (branded formulations)** — Branded prescription medicines sold to Indian doctors through a field sales force, spanning specialty therapies (gynaecology, gastroenterology, antidiabetic, cardiology, ophthalmology) and acute/anti-infective and respiratory products; it is one of the largest single pieces at about INR 2,200 crores in FY24 and INR 652 crores in the December 2025 quarter, though growth has lagged the overall Indian market at 4-7%.
- **Animal Healthcare (veterinary)** — Medicines for animals sold within the India business, repeatedly called out by management as a strong and consistent growth driver (roughly 35% growth in one early quarter) even when other India segments were flat.
- **US Generics** — Generic versions of off-patent drugs sold to US buyers and distributors, historically the company's biggest single territory at roughly $47-56 million a quarter (about INR 367 crores in Q1 FY23), driven by 15-25 new product launches a year and constantly squeezed by price erosion.
- **Ex-US Generics (Europe, Canada and rest-of-world)** — The same generic formulations sold outside the US, reusing the same product dossiers filed for the US; smaller than the US at around INR 182 crores in Q1 FY23 but the fastest-growing part lately — up 23% in FY24, 36% in ROW markets in Q3 FY26, and 20% for full-year FY26.
- **API business** — Bulk active pharmaceutical ingredients sold to other pharma companies, mostly into regulated markets, at roughly INR 233 crores a quarter in FY23; volumes have held up but pricing has been a persistent headwind, with management guiding to high-single to low-double-digit growth for FY27.
- **US Branded business (new)** — A newly started, deliberately small specialty push into selling its own branded drug in the US, beginning with Pivya (an in-licensed first-line oral antibiotic for uncomplicated urinary tract infections in women) launched in Q4 FY26; it currently costs money rather than making it, with management framing it as a multi-year build rather than a quick scale-up.
- **R&D and manufacturing platform** — The engine behind everything else — INR 600-800 crores of annual R&D aimed at complex areas (injectables, peptides, oral solids, oncology, drug discovery) and early-entry First-to-File / Day-1 / NCE-1 launches, supported by newer plants (Karakhadi injectables, an oncology facility, Indore) whose rising utilisation management says is the main lever on margins.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Every check, area by area

**Capital Allocation — Weak (-1.00)** · 6 of 6 checks had evidence

- **Distribution to shareholders — Neutral** *(from the conference calls)*: Distribution is a plain, modestly rising dividend (INR8 to INR11/share in FY24) with no buyback despite a de-rated stock and no articulated payout framework.
  > "The Company has recorded a higher dividend of INR 11 per share, which is 550%" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance) — Weak** *(from the conference calls)*: A large FY21-23 growth capex cycle (4 new facilities, ~INR300cr/yr of fixed cost incl. depreciation) delivered poor incremental returns — F2/F3 were still only 40-60% utilised three years later — and capex has since dropped to maintenance/debottlenecking of INR300-350cr.
  > "we have commercialized 4 new facilities and the yearly expenses on these 4 facilities are about INR 300 crores, including the depreciation... whereas the revenue from these facilities are still very small" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Advertisement and promotion — Weak** *(from the conference calls)*: India promotional investment was ramped up in FY23 with promised topline payoff, yet the branded business grew only 5-7% and stayed below market for 3-4 years, and management concedes its promotional (UCPMP/doctor-spend) posture cost it growth.
  > "the whole doctor spend scenario in the context of UCMP, possibly we were too conservative and extremely sensitive to it relative to the market" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Research and development — Weak** *(from the conference calls)*: R&D has run at 8-15% of sales for years with INR350cr of Aleor R&D written off, projects cut because returns fell, and management admits IRR thresholds have been lowered while US growth stayed single-digit — low long-run R&D efficiency.
  > "we have reduced R&D because we cut out some projects because the returns had come down in the U.S. market compared to 5 years back" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Merger and acquisition — Weak** *(from the conference calls)*: No bolt-on acquisition track record; the one consolidated venture (Aleor) required successive write-offs of capitalised R&D totalling ~INR350cr before merger, and growth deals since are in-licensing (Pivya) with high royalty outgo.
  > "Aleor has further written-off Rs. 115 crores out of which CWIP, out of capitalized R&D to P&L... Total is about Rs. 350 crores spent over last 3-4 years" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Cost of working capital — Poor** *(from the financial statements)*: About 381 days of cash stays stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers — a long cycle that ties up capital.
