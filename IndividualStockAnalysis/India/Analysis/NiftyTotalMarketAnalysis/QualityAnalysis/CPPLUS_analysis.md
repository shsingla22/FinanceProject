# Aditya Infotech (CPPLUS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Aditya Infotech Limited (listed as CPPLUS) makes and sells video security and surveillance equipment in India under its own brand CP PLUS — CCTV cameras of every type (analog HD, IP network, Wi-Fi, 4G, thermal, explosion-proof, AI), the recorders that go with them (DVRs and NVRs), plus cables, power supplies and accessories so a customer can buy the whole kit from one place. It sells to four customer groups: small and medium businesses (about 60% of revenue), government and large enterprise projects (about 30%), and homes (about 10%), reaching them mainly through a wholesale channel of 1,000-plus distributors and 2,000-plus system integrators across 500-plus cities, plus 141 exclusive 'Galaxy' franchise stores and online marketplaces. It earns money largely by manufacturing hardware in India and selling it at a margin — helped by making more of the parts itself — with a smaller and newer layer of cloud-storage, AI video-analytics and monitoring services on top. The business is essentially India-only; FY26 revenue was Rs 4,220.8 crore with EBITDA of Rs 579 crore and adjusted PAT of Rs 368 crore, and management says its share of India's surveillance market reached roughly 45% by Q3 FY26.

**The parts of the business:**

- **CP PLUS brand (own-brand products)** — The core own-brand range of cameras, recorders and allied security gear that management says contributed 86-87% of total company revenue in FY26, within which IP (network) products were about 73-75% of the mix as customers shift away from older analog HD.
- **SMB segment** — Sales of cameras and recorders to small and medium businesses through distributors, system integrators and franchise stores — described as the largest slice at about 60% of revenue.
- **Government and large enterprise projects** — Bigger tendered deployments for smart/safe city schemes, public safety and critical infrastructure plus large corporates, described as roughly 30% of revenue and an area where the company is adding vertical-focused salespeople.
- **Consumer / home segment** — Retail products such as ezyKam smart Wi-Fi home cameras, 4G cameras for places without wired internet and CarKam dashcams, sold online and offline; about 10% of revenue but the place where the CP PLUS brand name matters most.
- **Professional range** — Higher-end products for institutional buyers — AI network cameras with facial recognition and video analytics, number-plate recognition cameras for traffic, GPS-enabled mobile NVRs for trains and buses, police body-worn cameras and control-room displays.
- **Services and solutions** — A software/services layer — CP PLUS Cloud Storage for backup video, CP PLUS AI for video analytics, and the OnVigil AI-IoT monitoring platform — small today but the direction management is pushing, including a Qualcomm partnership to move from 'hardware-led surveillance' to AI analytics.
- **Manufacturing (AIL Dixon, Kadapa)** — The in-house factory in Kadapa, Andhra Pradesh — originally a 50:50 joint venture with Dixon Technologies, bought out in September 2024 to become a wholly owned subsidiary — which management calls India's largest single-location surveillance plant, with capacity grown from 1.5 million units a month at listing to 2.5 million by end-FY26.
- **Backward integration / component localisation** — A push to make more of the input parts itself rather than import them — an enclosures and housing plant at Kadapa targeting 30 million units a year, a CCTV lens assembly line scaling toward 1 million lenses a month, and a cable joint venture with Orient Cables in Rajasthan due to start up in FY27.
- **R&D centres** — In-house product design and engineering, originally in Noida and later Ahmedabad, with a Bangalore centre coming and a wholly owned Taiwan subsidiary set up for chip-level and embedded work; described as central to holding STQC certification leadership and building AI products.
- **Multi-brand strategy (EYRA, NEXIVUE)** — Two new lower-priced brands launched at IFSEC in December 2025 aimed at the mass-market and unorganised end of the market, launching through Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 — a way to compete below the main CP PLUS price points.
- **Non-CP PLUS business** — The remaining roughly 13-14% of revenue outside the CP PLUS brand; the calls trace this to the company's origins as a distributor of global IT brands from 1995 onward but give no further detail on its current size or composition.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## The verdict: Decent — 64 out of 100 ★★★☆☆

In one breath: the quality framework finds a good business today (72/100); it strongly fits 3 of the 11 patterns long-term winners share; the risk review found 1 high risk (Government Dependency). Weighing those together gives 64 out of 100 — decent.

**The exact arithmetic:** Overall = 45% × 72 (business quality) + 30% × 63 (multibagger fit) + 25% × 52 (risk safety) = 64 out of 100 → Decent (3 stars).

- **Business quality (72/100):** The 34-check quality framework scored the business +0.89 on its −2 (poor) to +2 (excellent) scale, with 97% of checks backed by evidence; mapped onto 0–100 that is 72 points.
- **Multibagger fit (63/100):** the foundation test partly passed (+10); 3 patterns fit strongly (Friendly Middleman, Pricing Power, Market Share Gainers) (+45); 1 likely (+8) → 63 of 100.
- **Risk safety (52/100):** started from a clean 100; 1 high risk (Government Dependency) (−20); 5 worth watching (−20); the balance sheet shows one stress signal (−8) → 52 of 100.

### The story in depth

Aditya Infotech (CP PLUS) comes out of these three reviews as a genuinely good operating business that has just lived through an extraordinary year. Nine of the ten notable quality checks score full marks: sustained advertising has built what the framework calls the only real consumer brand in a business-to-business category; capacity was scaled from 1.5 to 1.9 to 2.5 million units a month inside FY26 and paid for out of the company's own cash flow; IPO money cut gross debt by 89% to 48 crore rupees; research centres grew from one site in Noida to Ahmedabad, Taiwan and a planned Bangalore; and management repeatedly volunteered unflattering facts on its calls, including that its cheap inventory is now used up and that profit growth will lag revenue growth. The multibagger screen agrees, giving strong marks to three patterns: the company sells only through more than 1,000 distributors and 2,000 system integrators rather than to end users, it has been able to raise prices (6-8% in January 2026, then staged monthly increases with average selling price guided up 20-25% into FY27), and it has taken own-brand market share from about 25% at the IPO to roughly 45.4% by Q3 FY26 against its own 36% projection, with margins widening from 6.9% to 18% rather than shrinking. The end market is forecast to compound at 16.5% to FY30, with units nearly doubling from 39.7 million to 75 million.

The uncomfortable part is that the strengths and the biggest risk are the same fact. The step-change in share and margin dates from the April 2025 MeitY/STQC rule that disqualified Chinese brands, which is why the risk review calls government dependency a HIGH RISK: the pattern screen's two best scores, market share gains and pricing power, both rest on a policy decision the company did not make and cannot control. The same vacuum that CP PLUS filled is now drawing in roughly 30 certified brands, including about a dozen global names holding around 10% and 18 domestic ones, which is why new entrants is flagged as a WATCH. Selling only through the trade is likewise double-edged: it earns a strong fit on the middleman pattern, but because installers and integrators choose the product in a category one analyst described as historically deflationary, it also drives the 'good enough goods' risk — the company itself launched the EYRA brand purely to fight the cheap unorganized tail, which is part of why the low-price pattern only rates a partial fit. Buying chipsets and memory three quarters or more ahead reads as disciplined counter-cyclical stewardship in the quality framework and as supplier concentration in the risk review, since the parts come from a handful of global fabs, half of which have moved off DDR3. And the price increases that power the pricing-power score are exactly why the toll-road pattern is a clear no-fit: management itself models customers trading down from entry-level IP cameras to analog as prices rise.

The one place the record is plainly negative is cash returns. For every 100 rupees of shareholders' funds and borrowings in the business, it produced about one rupee of operating cash this year — a score of -2, and the reason the return-on-capital area comes out below zero while every other area is positive. That is the arithmetic of a company pouring its own cash into capacity, forward-bought components and inventory faster than the profits come back, which is also why the risk review labels overall resilience STRAINED. Several patterns stall at 'partial' for a related reason — the numbers simply are not disclosed: recurring offerings such as cloud storage, the OnVigil platform and the Qualcomm revenue-share exist but attach and renewal rates are never given; research spending is never quantified; and the 141 co-funded Galaxy stores come without store economics, with consumer sales under 10% of revenue. Weighted together — 72 for business quality, 63 for pattern fit, 52 for risk safety — the combined score is 64 out of 100, a 'Decent' three-star verdict. The honest reading is a well-run, brand-led, share-gaining manufacturer whose recent numbers were amplified by a regulatory reset, whose margin swing over four quarters (8.7% to 12.6% to 18%) has not yet been tested without that help, and whose capital is not yet earning a return worth paying up for.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** The entire step-change in fortunes is policy-created: the MeitY/STQC mandate from April 2025 disqualified Chinese brands, taking CP PLUS's own-brand share from ~25% to ~45.4% and EBITDA margin from 6.9% (Q1FY25) to 18% (Q4FY26); on top of that ~30% of revenue is enterprise/government projects (smart city, railways worth 'INR2,000 to INR3,000 crores', NHAI toll removal, PSU system integrators), and BIS/STQC certification gates every SKU launch. A dilution, delay or reversal of the certification regime — or a change in which countries' semiconductors are permitted — would unwind the margin and share gains directly.

> "MeITY has mandated that from April 9, 2025, all network CCTV cameras sold in India must be STQC certified." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Revenue is spread across four end-segments with SMB (60%) the largest and the company does not bid tenders directly, selling only through distributors and system integrators*

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Margins and revenue have swung violently within four quarters (EBITDA margin 8.7% → 12.6% → 18%; Q4 revenue +45.5%), driven by a one-off regulatory reset, low-cost inventory gains now 'exhausted', and a semiconductor/memory commodity cycle management says will keep costs rising until 2027 — the classic peak problem of not knowing how much of current margin is cyclical. Demand also leans on customer capex: government project spend (railways, NHAI), 'new establishments' and smart-city programmes, roughly 30% of revenue.

> "Critical components such as SoC, DDR, flash, and sensors remain under severe supply pressure, resulting in extended lead times and rising procurement challenges" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Underlying demand is broad, underpenetrated and largely replacement/expansion-led across SMB (60%), and the company is investing countercyclically — capacity 1.5mn → 2.5mn units/month, housing, lens and cable backward integration funded from internal accruals while the small-player tail cannot secure supply*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 6 years with swings of ±19 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 5% and 13% (±2.8 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 0% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 3%).
