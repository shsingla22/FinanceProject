# APL Apollo Tubes Ltd. (APLAPOLLO) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: METALS*

APL Apollo Tubes makes steel pipes and tubes — mostly "structural" steel tubes used to build things — by buying hot-rolled steel coil (management calls the company India's largest steel buyer) and rolling it into tubes, sheets and roofing products at plants across India (Sikandrabad, Hosur, Murbad, Raipur, with new units at Siliguri, Gorakhpur and Ahmedabad) plus a plant in Dubai. It sells mainly to the construction market through a network of distributors, dealers and retailers, pitching its tubes as a replacement for conventional building materials like rebars/TMT bars, steel angles and channels, wood, aluminium, cement and concrete, and cheap scrap-steel pipe; it also sells directly into large projects such as hospitals and commercial towers. Money is made on volume times a spread per ton: management runs the business on tons sold (about 2.3 million tons in FY22 rising to roughly 3.55 million tons in FY26) and on EBITDA per ton, guided at around INR4,500 in FY23 and raised to about INR5,500 by FY26, with the premium APL Apollo brand fetching INR3,000–4,000 a ton more than the base category. It is capital-light on working capital (negative working-capital cycle, ~37% ROCE in FY26), funds its expansion from its own cash flows, and is targeting 8 million tons of capacity by FY28 and 10 million tons by 2030.

**The parts of the business:**

- **General / standard structural steel tubes (the base business)** — The bread-and-butter hollow steel sections sold through the dealer channel; this is the volume backbone that the older plants supply, and where the company's cheaper "SG" brand competes head-on with small players and sponge-iron pipe makers.
- **APL Apollo brand (premium pricing)** — The branded tube range that the company deliberately prices INR3,000–4,000 per ton above the market; management says twelve months of higher pricing stuck, making this the main driver of the jump in EBITDA per ton by FY26.
- **Value-added and innovative products from the Raipur plant** — A roughly 1.1 million-ton sellable-capacity plant in Chhattisgarh making four products — heavy structural tubes, roofing sheets, super-light structural and coated thicker sheets — that were running at about 61% utilisation in Q1 FY25 and carry better gross margins than the standard range.
- **Heavy structural tubes** — Large tubes meant to replace conventional steel construction for buildings, seeded by projects like the Delhi hospitals and a 15-storey NCR commercial tower, with a pipeline of ~50 projects worth about 200,000 tons cited in 2022; utilisation was about 58% in Q1 FY25.
- **Roofing sheets** — A Raipur product line and the best-utilised of the four at roughly 89% in Q1 FY25.
- **Super-light structural** — A lighter structural tube line from Raipur, running around 52% utilisation in Q1 FY25.
- **Galvanized and colour-coated / coated products** — Coated tubes and sheets that management flags as higher-margin (helped in FY26 by steel shortage), though output depends on gas availability — coated thicker sheet was at about 48% utilisation in Q1 FY25.
- **Dubai operations (international)** — Four tube mills abroad with about 300,000 tons of capacity making sizes from 15mm x 15mm up to 300mm x 300mm; utilisation was ~30% in Q1 FY25 and about 40% in FY26 due to the Middle East crisis, but margins there are described as better.
- **"Patra" (low-end commodity) segment** — A cheap, thin-material segment the company is deliberately shrinking — cut to under 30% of that segment's earlier volume by FY26 — because it drags down margins versus branded and innovative products.
- **Planned super-specialty segment** — A future 2 million-ton addition targeted for 2030 through joint ventures with Japanese, Korean, European and American partners, aimed at EV, aerospace, petrochemical, oil-and-gas and heavy-engineering tubes; as of Jan 2026 it was still only at the talking-to-targets stage.

### The story in depth

APL Apollo comes out of all three reviews as one consistent machine: sell steel tubes cheaper than anyone else can, keep almost no capital tied up doing it, and use the cash to build more capacity. The quality framework gives full marks to the economics this produces — every ₹1 of assets generates ₹2.61 of sales, cash from customers arrives 12 days before suppliers have to be paid, and every ₹100 of capital in the business threw off ₹36 of operating cash this year, with return on capital running at 33-37%. The multibagger screen picks up the same engine from the other side and calls it a strong fit on two counts. 'Low Price Plus' rests on being India's largest steel buyer, DFT mill technology, regional plants that cut freight from ₹2,600 to ₹1,150 a ton against a ₹750 target, and power use down from 92 units to 84. 'Market Share Gainers' is the visible result: roughly 55% of the primary structural tube market in 2022 with a 60-75% target, volumes up from about 2.3mt to 4.2mt, and expansion from 5mt toward 8mt of capacity and a stated 10mt goal for 2030 — all of it organic, funded from internal cash flow, and taken from net debt to ₹15bn net cash. The scores for capital allocation (1.33) and return on capital (1.33) are the highest in the framework, and the risk review's 'SOUND' resilience verdict leans on the same balance sheet.

The catch is that the trait making the cost story work also creates the two elevated risks. Buying more steel than anyone else in India gives negotiating power, but it also means the input side sits with a handful of primary mills — the risk review flags a raw-material shortage in Q4FY26 tied to mill curtailments, which is why 'Stakeholder Concentration' sits on watch. More importantly, buying and reselling steel in volume ties the whole business to steel prices: across all 17 calls, demand, volumes and margins are described in terms of HRC direction — volumes lost to destocking when steel fell 13% in Q1FY23, guidance cut in FY25 amid deflationary steel and inventory write-downs, and disruption again in Q4FY26. That is why 'Cyclicality' is elevated, and it is also why 'Toll Roads' is a clear no-fit: the product is a big, visible line item in construction cost, so the primary-versus-secondary price gap of ₹15,000-20,000 a ton decides whether customers buy at all. The other elevated risk, 'Good Enough Goods', has the same root — cheap scrap-steel 'Patra' tubes let dealers pocket an arbitrage that customers could not judge on quality, freezing the general segment from 0.9mt in FY20 to 1.1mt, while small melters in the Raipur, Mandi Gobindgarh and Chennai belts grew into an industry the size of the addressable market ('New Entrants', on watch).

What holds the two sides together is the brand, and it is the newest part of the story. Advertising and promotion scores full marks because campaigns expensed since FY25 turned into a price premium the market has actually accepted — ₹3,000-4,000 a ton, described as 'the new normal' after twelve months — and pricing and mix, not volume, drove the last two years of profit growth, lifting EBITDA per ton from about ₹4,200 to over ₹5,500 even as volumes wobbled. That premium is simultaneously the 'Brand Strength' pattern (a likely fit), the silver lining on 'Good Enough Goods', and the reason 'Pricing Power' is only a partial fit: the 2022-24 calls describe heavy discounting and full pass-through of steel costs, so the pricing power is roughly a year old rather than long-standing. The company is also fighting the cheap end directly with its own lower-priced SG brand while narrowing the price gap from ₹20/kg to about 5%. The other partial fits share this shape — real advantages that are not yet structural. 'Cost to Replicate' is a capital-and-time barrier (5mt installed where no rival services more than 20,000-30,000 tons a month), not an unrepeatable one, and 'Friendly Middleman' rests on informal market-building through architects, structural consultants and fabricators rather than a formal tie. That is why competitive advantage (0.67) and management (0.67) are the weakest quality areas, and why a business with 36% cash returns lands at 72 out of 100 — Strong, on a healthy end-market where steel tubes are permanently displacing wood, concrete, rebar and angles at a cited 15-16% a year, but with the moat still being built.

*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**What the calls show:** Consistent scale-plus-cost story across the years: largest steel buyer in India with buying leverage, DFT technology, regional plants to cut freight, itemised savings (freight INR2,600 to INR1,150 to a INR750 target, power 92 to 84 units), plus the SG fighter brand to win the base segment — low EBITDA/ton (INR4,200-5,500) but 33-37% ROCE and negative working capital.

- ✅ Keeps ₹15 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 2.6× a year with 24% returns on capital — thin margins made up on volume, the low-price-winner profile.

### Market Share Gainers — STRONG FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence and the numbers agree (1 of 1 computable checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Persistent, quantified share gain over the full period — ~55% of the primary structural tube market in 2022 targeting 60-75%, systematic conversion of the secondary/scrap-steel tube pool as the price gap collapsed from INR20/kg to ~5%, volumes from ~2.3mt to a 4.4mt December run-rate, plus international expansion via Dubai; gains were partly bought with discounting in FY25 but not with credit risk (working capital stayed near zero and went negative).

> "we shall be taking market share from the secondary scrap steel because now the pricing gap between our products and the scrap steel pipe is like 5%, which used to be 20%" — *management, on an earnings call*

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.1× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 106% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 21% to 26% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across all 17 calls management frames demand, volumes and margins in terms of HRC/steel commodity price direction — Q1FY23 volumes lost to destocking as steel fell 13%, FY25 guidance cut amid deflationary steel and inventory write-downs, and Q4FY26 again disrupted by a steel price spike, shortage and channel destocking; EBITDA/ton has swung from ~INR4,200 to INR5,500+ on price cycles, and the demand end (construction/capex) is itself cyclical.

> "whatever fall has to happen, it will happen by Q2, and we'll see all the pain from July to September months" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Countercyclical capex (Raipur, Dubai, 5mt→8mt) funded from internal cash flows while the cycle was weak; net-cash, negative working capital balance sheet; consumable-like construction demand rather than customer capex equipment*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 11 years with swings of ±17 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 5% and 9% (±1.1 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 4% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 6%).

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** The Nobel Biocare mechanism is explicit here: cheaper low-grade scrap-steel ('Patra') tubes captured the general segment because dealers pocketed the price arbitrage and end-customers lacked quality awareness, freezing APL's general volumes for four years; management has had to defend its premium via brand campaigns, consumer education and a fighter brand, and Q4FY26 still flags 'risk from Patra'.

> "the manufacturers and the dealers are taking benefit of price gap... we are also taking initiatives like making the consumer aware about the poor quality of scrap steel" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Brand premiumisation has held — INR3,000-4,000/ton APL Apollo premium described as 'the new normal' after 12 months; value-added and super value-added products (60-65% of mix) are technically out of reach of scrap-steel tube makers; SG Premium fighter brand caps share loss and the general segment has been cut to under 30%*

- ✅ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs held near 14% (was 15%) — no gross-level price erosion.
- ✅ Operating margin (8%) remains near its historical peak (9%) — no collapse in the premium.
- ✅ Operating margin held (about 7% then 7%) — no drift down under competition.
