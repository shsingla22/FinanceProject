**Checks that improved:**

- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Weak → Good. Now: There is a genuine pool of impaired competitors — 10-15% of the tanker fleet under sanctions and 500-700 OSVs in cold layup that would need heavy capex to return — from whom compliant modern tonnage can take work. | The long view had said: No weak competitors to harvest — GESHIP is itself the seller of ships to lower-tier operators, and in offshore the 500-700 cold-stacked vessels are latent supply rather than share to be taken.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Poor → Weak. Now: No technology differentiation is claimed anywhere in the window; the fleet is standard tonnage bought and sold on the second-hand market like any other owner's. | The long view had said: No technology differentiation is claimed in four years of calls; the only technical initiatives cited are fuel-consumption/emissions savings that every owner pursues, and assets are explicitly described as commoditised and liquid.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: Profit margins swing by ±5.7 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Barriers are effectively nil — any owner with capital can order tonnage, and management documents order books climbing to ~20% crude/product, 27-29% LPG against negligible scrapping, i.e. classic supply indiscipline. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±9.1 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Capital floods in whenever returns are good — order books went from 3-4% for crude tankers to ~20%, LPG to 29%, and asset prices rise simply because 'money is chasing good ships', the definition of no entry barrier and no supply discipline.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: Mixed cycle position: tankers are post-spike at record NAV with the order book built up to ~20% and supply 'kicking in' in CY2027-28 (late-cycle), while the offshore rig/OSV market looks early-cycle with a 2.5% order book and Aramco recalling rigs. | The long view had said: The company sits near the top of the shipping cycle, not the bottom — best-ever quarterly and annual profits, asset prices up 10-20%, order books rebuilt from 3-4% to ~20% — though the offshore/jack-up segment is still mid-recovery from its trough.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: No monopoly-like position in a customer's mind — freight is a commodity priced off Suezmax/MR benchmarks — with only a partial exception in the India-suitable offshore fleet that wins repeat local charters. | The long view had said: A pure commodity spot-freight business with no monopoly position in any customer's mind; the company deliberately chooses maximum spot exposure over any captive relationship.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Weak → Neutral. Now: Customers are corporate (oil majors, charterers, ONGC-type offshore buyers) and the company does supply reliability and contract coverage, but it has no switching-cost lock-in on the spot-traded majority of its fleet. | The long view had said: Customers are corporates (oil majors, Indian PSUs, ONGC, Aramco) but the relationship carries no lock-in or TCO advantage — Aramco simply cancelled rigs and Indian tenders were shelved.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Good. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹58 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±5.7 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹58 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±9.1 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.

Now: The stock itself is under-followed (persistent 25-30% discount to NAV), but the underlying sector is anything but ignored by capital, with order books building across every segment. | The long view had said: A quietly-run, under-followed Indian shipowner that persistently trades at a 25-40% discount to net asset value with retail investors dominating the Q&A — capital is clearly not crowding in.

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 12 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
