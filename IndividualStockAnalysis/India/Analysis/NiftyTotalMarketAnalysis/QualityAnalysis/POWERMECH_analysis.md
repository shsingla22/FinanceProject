# Power Mech Projects (POWERMECH) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Power Mech Projects is an Indian construction and services contractor that builds and then looks after large industrial plants and infrastructure — historically power stations, and increasingly roads, railways, water schemes, metros and mines. It sells its labour, equipment and project-management skill to power utilities and industrial groups (NTPC, DVC, state electricity boards, BHEL, Adani, Vedanta, JSW, Coal India and railway authorities), earning money on contracts it wins by tender: civil and structural work, erecting mechanical and electrical equipment, full turnkey EPC packages, and long-running operation-and-maintenance (O&M) contracts that provide recurring, steadier revenue. Roughly 94-95% of revenue is domestic, with a small international arm (including Africa) focused on short-term, higher-margin O&M work; the power sector supplies about 57-70% of revenue and non-power infrastructure the rest. In FY26 it reported total revenue of about INR 6,107 crore, EBITDA of INR 750 crore (12.3% margin) and profit after tax of INR 412 crore, sitting on an order backlog of about INR 55,151 crore including its mine contracts, or about INR 15,899 crore excluding them.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Civil (including railways and water)** — Concrete, structural and site construction for power plants plus railway track and depots, metro depots, roads and water-distribution schemes — the biggest revenue line for several years (INR 2,352 crore in FY24) and the largest order backlog at roughly INR 9,100 crore, though management repeatedly flags delays in certifying water-division bills.
- **Mechanical / erection** — The company's original core: installing boilers, turbines and other heavy mechanical equipment inside power plants and industrial sites — around INR 692 crore of FY24 revenue and a backlog of about INR 2,959 crore in early 2026, growing again on the back of new coal-plant ordering.
- **Operation & maintenance (O&M)** — Running and servicing power plants and other assets after they are built, in India and overseas; management calls this the stability engine because the revenue and margins repeat year after year — INR 1,109 crore in FY24 with a backlog that jumped from INR 600 crore to about INR 2,200 crore in one year and has stayed near INR 2,500 crore since.
- **Electrical** — Electrical installation work, the smallest line and long a weak spot (only INR 53 crore of FY24 revenue), but revived by railway-electrification jobs and the electrical content inside EPC packages, lifting the backlog past INR 1,000 crore.
- **EPC (FGD and balance-of-plant)** — Full turnkey packages where Power Mech takes design, procurement and construction responsibility rather than just a work package — started with a INR 6,100 crore-plus flue-gas desulphurisation order from Adani in 2022 at roughly 11% EBITDA margin, and extended in FY26 to a large balance-of-plant EPC for BHEL's 800 MW Singareni thermal project, where the company does about 40% of the value in-house and buys in the remaining 60% of engineering and equipment.
- **MDO (mine developer and operator)** — Long-duration contracts to develop and run coal mines; two such contracts dominate the reported backlog — the difference between the ~INR 55,000 crore total and the ~INR 16,000 crore executable book — and they began contributing revenue from FY24 onwards as a future growth and margin driver.
- **International operations** — Overseas projects and services, including an Africa business set up under a dedicated MD/CEO; it has shrunk from about 17% of revenue in FY23 to roughly 5-6% by FY26 as management deliberately narrowed it to short-term, high-margin O&M work.
- **New energy and other new ventures** — Recent entries described as future growth engines: a 250 MW / 1,000 MWh battery storage project in West Bengal on a build-own-operate basis (INR 800 crore capex, INR 103 crore a year for 15 years, Power Mech leading a consortium with 51%) which was later cancelled by the West Bengal electricity department, a 16 MW solar plant in Bihar, and a Mumbai Monorail O&M contract that marks its entry into metro-rail maintenance.

### The story in depth

Power Mech is, on this evidence, a well-run and honestly financed contractor rather than a franchise business. Management is by far the strongest area of the quality work (0.83 of the checks in that area), and it lines up with the only pattern rated a strong fit: a founder-promoter who has led throughout, family succession in place, and promoter money put into the company to keep lenders comfortable, with debt kept low through the build-out (0.21x equity in FY24, 0.32x in FY26). The operating record supports the second-best pattern, market share gainer: the operations-and-maintenance installed base went from about 67,000 MW in 2022 to about 75,000 MW by February 2026, headcount from 32,000 to 48,000, and revenue from INR 3,618 crore in FY23 to INR 6,107 crore in FY26 — roughly 15% a year on average, lumpy year to year but inside the steady band the framework rewards. Cash discipline matches: only about 19 days of cash sits between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and growth spending is deliberately targeted (FGD equipment, mine development, a first integrated balance-of-plant EPC package) rather than routine.

The awkward part is that the very thing producing the growth is also what caps the quality score. The work is won through lowest-price tendering from a handful of very large buyers, so the company gains share by being competitive on price and execution — and for exactly that reason the framework gives zero on both competitive advantage and return on capital, marks the industry structure down hard (customers such as BHEL, NTPC, Adani and SAIL hold the negotiating power), and rates five patterns as no fit: pricing power, toll roads, friendly middleman, low price plus and innovation dominance. Margins tell the same story from the other side: earnings margins have sat in a narrow 11% to 12.4% band across FY23 to FY26, growth has come from volume and order book rather than price, and a bid was lost outright on price at IISCO Burnpur. This is also the 'good enough goods' risk rated elevated — undifferentiated manpower and equipment sold into price-led tenders. The genuine offsets are partial, not absent: roughly 18,000 skilled operations engineers, 75,000 MW of operating experience and pre-qualification credentials are hard for a newcomer to assemble quickly, and reputation has real value (contracts awarded without performance guarantees, a credit rating upgrade from A to A+, a long BHEL relationship that keeps converting into orders) — but none of it converts into charging more.

The risks cluster around where demand comes from, and they overlap with the same strengths. Government dependency and customer concentration are both rated high risk: the demand story is explicitly the national capex plan and the customer list is dominated by state-owned buyers, while two names — Adani and BHEL — have driven results and guidance for four years, with management saying the annual target depends on them. That is the same relationship depth that earns partial credit under brand strength and cost to replicate. Cyclicality is elevated because revenue tracks customers' capex programmes, and the framework itself notes that although the company positioned into thermal at the bottom of the cycle, much of the 54,740 MW upturn is already visible rather than still ahead. Two slower-burning risks sit on watch: the core skill base is coal thermal, and management itself flags plant utilisation falling from 58% toward 30-40% as renewables scale, which is why the move into battery storage, pumped storage, metro maintenance and mine development matters — the same move that earns partial credit as forward integration, though one INR 800 crore storage build-own-operate plan was later cancelled. The recurring maintenance stream is the main shock absorber (backlog up from INR 600 crore to INR 2,522 crore by Q3FY26, plus 25-year mine contracts), but it is annuity-like rather than true subscription economics. Two other checks go the wrong way: overseas revenue shrank from 17% to 5-6% despite repeated stated ambitions, and cash has flowed from shareholders to the company (promoter allotment and a share sale to fund mining) with no dividend or buyback policy stated — the flip side of the promoter support that scores so well on culture. Netting out, resilience is judged sound but the overall rating is Mixed at 44 out of 100: a capable operator in a structurally unattractive industry.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** The demand narrative is explicitly the government capex plan (NIP, INR 12.5 lakh crore central capex, Jal Jeevan drinking-water schemes, railway budget), and the customer list is dominated by PSUs and state utilities — BHEL, NTPC, DVC, Singareni, Coal India/CCL, SAIL, NMDC, KPCL, state gencos. ## What to watch

- Customer concentration (high risk): whether Adani and BHEL stop being the names that determine the annual target — the FGD and balance-of-plant order flow is the single most reversible driver here.
- Pricing power (no fit) and 'good enough goods' (elevated): any sustained move in earnings margin above the 11-12.4% band held since FY23 would be the first real evidence that qualification barriers, not price, are winning the work.
- Recurring revenue (partial fit): whether the maintenance backlog keeps compounding past INR 2,522 crore and renews on performance terms — this is the only offset to the elevated cyclicality risk.
- Technological innovation and shifting demand (both watch): how plant utilisation actually behaves as it slides from 58% toward 30-40%, and whether the storage, pumped-storage, metro and mine-development extensions turn into revenue after the INR 800 crore storage project was cancelled.
- Distribution to shareholders (negative check) and geographic expansion (negative check): a first stated dividend or buyback policy, or international revenue recovering from 5-6% back toward the earlier 17%, would each mark a change in how capital and ambition are being converted into returns.
