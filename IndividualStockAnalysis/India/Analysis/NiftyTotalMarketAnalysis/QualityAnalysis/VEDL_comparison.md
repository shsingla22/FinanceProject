Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Entry requires mineral concessions, environmental/forest clearances and billions in capital — barriers evident in the company's own dependence on EC/FC approvals for Sijimali and Kuraloi — but pricing rationality is externally determined. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±16.8 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. ### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 3 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Cost to Replicate**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Parts of the portfolio are genuinely hard to replicate — Hindustan Zinc's first-quartile Rampura Agucha/Gamsberg ore bodies, Sijimali bauxite, and government-auctioned critical-mineral blocks are finite, licence-gated assets a funded newcomer cannot recreate — but aluminium smelting, merchant power and steel are replicable with capital, and returns still swing with LME prices. (The long view had said: The one pattern with a concrete, articulable barrier: finite, permitted, world-scale orebodies and fully backward-integrated infrastructure that a funded newcomer cannot rebuild — 662 Mt zinc R&R at Zinc International, 3,000 Mt at Liberia, 1.4 bn barrels R&R, a 5 Mtpa single-location alumina refinery, captive bauxite and coal — which management frames as a >$50bn replacement cost.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls revenue is described purely as tonnes sold at LME-linked prices (aluminium, zinc, silver, oil, power units) with no licence fees, AMC/service attach, renewals or installed-base economics; the only contracted streams mentioned are merchant power PPAs, not customer-funded recurring revenue. (The long view had said: Across all 15 calls the discussion is entirely about commodity volumes, cost per tonne, LME prices and capex; there is no mention of attach rates, AMC/service revenue, renewals, installed base or customer advances funding the business.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Over four calls there is no R&D spend, pipeline or new-product revenue disclosure; growth is explicitly attributed to capacity commissioning and debottlenecking — process capex, not innovation-led differentiation. (The long view had said: There are named product launches with a differentiation angle — Restora and Restora Ultra low-carbon aluminium and EcoZen, 'Asia's first low-carbon zinc product' — plus a 90% value-added product target, but R&D spend is never disclosed or quantified and no revenue impact from innovation is given across any call.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 11 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
