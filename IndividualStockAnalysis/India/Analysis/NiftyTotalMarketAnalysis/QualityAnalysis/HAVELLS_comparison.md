**Checks that improved:**

- **Candor** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Management volunteers unflattering detail — inventory overhang, consumer schemes, under-absorption of overheads — and proactively flags that unusually strong lighting and cable margins were year-end one-offs that will normalise. | The long view had said: Management proactively flags one-offs and inventory gains rather than claiming them as structural and admits inability to forecast, but has not squarely owned the repeatedly missed 2022 promise that Lloyd margins would normalise 'by Q4'.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: This is a heavily covered large cap with multiple brokerages hosting and probing the calls each quarter — the opposite of an ignored niche. | The long view had said: Havells is the opposite of under-the-radar — a mass-media advertiser doubling ad spend and covered by JP Morgan, Nomura, Citi, CLSA, Jefferies and Kotak on every call.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing behaviour looks rational and industry-wide during the year (5-10% hikes for BEE, copper and currency passed through and absorbed), but discount schemes were still needed in the off-season and entry barriers are not discussed. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.8 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Entry barriers are modest with deep-pocketed new entrants (Reliance via BPL/Kelvinator) and constant competitive intensity, and management itself concedes it must balance price recovery against share defence.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Excellent → Neutral. Now: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.53 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: The year was marked by elevated working capital in cables and Lloyd that drained cash, alongside heavy plant and land capex — a capital-intensive rather than asset-light profile. | The long view had said: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.53 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: Working capital discipline is visible — inventory down to 63 days with management targeting further improvement and debtor days at 13 via channel financing — combined with a brand/distribution moat, though Lloyd's seasonality and new plants add asset intensity.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: Categories are openly contested with the analyst citing intense competition and the company having to fund consumer schemes to move channel stock — no monopoly-like hold on the customer's mind is evidenced. | The long view had said: Strong branded positions in switchgear (38-41% contribution margins sustained for years) fall short of monopoly-like standing, and the aftermarket service model is a cost centre rather than a captive profit pool.
- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Renewables is scaling rapidly on sector tailwinds (now a separate reporting segment) and industrial/infrastructure cables remain strong, though core consumer electricals are mature and cyclical. | The long view had said: Core end markets are in secular expansion: an under-penetrated AC market at an inflection point with first-time buyers, plus a policy-supported renewables/BESS/EV-charging ramp now carved out as its own segment.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Excellent → Good. Now: A broad multi-channel network — distributors at over half of sales plus modern trade and e-commerce — let the company manage a large inventory correction and push staggered price hikes across all categories, though the same channel also amplified the destocking pain. | The long view had said: A multi-brand, multi-category national distribution and service reach — Havells/Crabtree/Standard/REO/Lloyd across omni-channel, deep rural penetration and CARE 360 service — is the asset the company explicitly leverages into every new category including renewables.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Management claims share held or gained on secondary sales, but an analyst notes top-line growth lagged industry across Lloyd, ECD and cables, and fans/coolers/wires saw degrowth — the claim is unverified. | The long view had said: Genuine runway remains as Lloyd's shares are small in most categories and renewables is nascent, and management consistently claims share retention or gain even while pushing through steep price hikes.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: Innovation is asserted generically and a refreshed refrigerator portfolio was launched, but no R&D spend, pipeline or efficiency evidence is given in these calls. | The long view had said: R&D is being institutionalised (dedicated INR200 cr R&D centre, in-house inverters, BLDC/energy-efficiency transitions, premium launches across segments) and management explicitly links new premium products to innovation spend.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Management refuses to manage to quarterly optics, treats advertising and capacity build as multi-year investments, and says so explicitly while declining to give short-term guidance. | The long view had said: Management has stuck to the Lloyd turnaround across four years of analyst pressure, refuses quarterly-margin framing and guidance, and frames advertising and capacity as multi-year investments.

*Unchanged: Out of limelight; Profit margin; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Merger and acquisition; Technology advantage; Convenience benefits; Halo effect (caution); Intangible benefits; Assurance benefits; Cost of working capital; Advertisement and promotion; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Return on capital (headline); Disciplined stewards; Geographical expansion; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Cyclical market growth; Customer types (retail vs corporate).*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 6 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Promoter-family led, stable board across all four calls, with bad news disclosed promptly (declining fans/AC/cooler revenue, elevated working capital, lower other income) and prior guidance delivered — Oct 2025's promise that channel inventory would normalise by end-Q3 was borne out in the Jan 2026 call — alongside explicit conservatism on working capital and capex. (The long view had said: Long-tenured family leadership (Anil Rai Gupta chairing every call from Jul 2022 to Jul 2026), consistent no-guidance discipline, candour on one-offs (flagging that Q4 lighting's 37% contribution should normalise to 30–32% and that cable margins included inventory gains), and long-horizon spending on capex, brand and R&D; against that, the Jul 2022 promise of 'normalized margins' at Lloyd by Q3–Q4 FY23 was still unfulfilled in Jul 2026 with contribution margins back in high single digits.)

**That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: None of the four calls (Oct 2025–Jul 2026) discuss AMC/service contracts, attach rates, renewals, installed-base monetisation or customer advances as a funding source; the discussion is entirely primary/secondary product sell-in. (The long view had said: Across the four excerpted calls (Jul 2022, Jul 2024, Apr 2026, Jul 2026) management never discusses AMC/service contracts, attach rates, renewals or deferred revenue as a funding source; after-sales service is raised only once by an analyst and deflected as 'operational'.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Pricing Power (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Every call in the window shows revenue and margins swinging on commodity prices and weather rather than on structural demand — Q3 growth was 'led by an accelerated growth in our cables business, driven by volume expansion and commodity price inflation', Q4 cable margins spiked on copper/aluminium inventory gains with only 6% volume growth, and Lloyd margins collapsed twice on a short summer plus under-absorption of manufacturing overhead. Exposure is bounded because the portfolio is consumer replacement/flow demand rather than customer-capex equipment, and management describes capex and working capital as deliberately conservative. (The long view had said: Results are repeatedly moved by copper/aluminium swings and channel stocking/destocking (Q1FY25 wire destocking on the June 2024 price crash; Q4FY26 inventory gains on copper/aluminium; Q1FY23 cable margin hit), plus heavy summer seasonality in Lloyd where 65-70% of AC sales fall in two quarters; but the portfolio is largely replacement/operations-linked consumer and building products, not customer capex equipment.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The categories are price-sensitive and intermediated — lighting has already been through structural ASP erosion, wires and switchgear are bought largely on electrician/dealer recommendation, and management repeatedly weighs how much of raw-material inflation it can pass on without losing share, admitting Q1 FY27 growth lagged the industry after 'sharper price hikes'. The counterweight is a deliberate, funded brand-pull strategy — advertising spend more than doubled year-on-year in Q1 FY27 — aimed precisely at keeping the premium defensible. (The long view had said: The Nobel Biocare mechanism is visible in switches/switchgear, where cheaper competitors buy dealer shelf-space with long credit and the electrician/dealer intermediary steers a low-brand-awareness purchase — contribution margins have drifted down from ~41% post-COVID toward the 38% end — and management concedes it must 'balance' passing on costs against holding share in a competitive market.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Technological Innovation (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 8 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
