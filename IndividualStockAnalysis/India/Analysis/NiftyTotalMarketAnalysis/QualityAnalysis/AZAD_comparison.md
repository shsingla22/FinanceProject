### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 6. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 1 weakened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Toll Roads**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls management describes small, mission-critical rotating components inside OEM turbines and engines where the cost of failure dwarfs the part price, made by only a handful of qualified suppliers, with heavy certification anchoring (NADCAP accreditation for coatings, first-article inspections, serial production audits) — the classic critical-but-tiny-input toll road. (The long view had said: Repeatedly framed as a certification-anchored niche supply into a handful-of-players oligopoly (GE/Mitsubishi/Siemens = 70% of energy; Boeing/Airbus = 91% of aero) where qualification, not price, decides the sale and converted customers cannot leave — the Feb 2026 tariff answer is the purest expression of pass-through pricing power. The one unproven element is the classic '<1-2% of customer cost' test, which management never quantifies.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: the only computable check points this way but the calls offer no evidence — numbers alone are only ever a screening hint, never a confirmed fit. (The long view had said: Reputation with OEMs is real and awarded (MHI '2024 Partner of the Year', single-source 8-year nozzle-vane contract in May 2026), and it clearly stretches across GE's nuclear/gas/steam/hydrogen divisions — but this is B2B qualification credibility, not heritage brand with A&P spend or distributor leverage.)
- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Promises made in the Aug 2025 call were delivered by May 2026: 25-30% top-line guidance reiterated all year and landed at ~30% (INR590cr vs INR453cr), the capex/lean-factory roadmap produced four dedicated plants, and management openly flagged ramp-up difficulty and a multi-year stabilisation timeline (FY27 stable, FY28 full utilisation) rather than over-promising; founder-led with a stable core team through the window. (The long view had said: The promise-to-delivery record is good: the Feb 2024 promise that PAT/finance cost would normalise post-IPO was delivered; FY26 was pre-labelled a 'year of stabilization' in May 2025 and Feb 2026 and closed exactly that way with INR590cr revenue (~30%, matching the 30%+ guide), and management flags bad news (ramp-up costs, GST credit stuck to H1 FY28, working capital not yet normalised). Against that: senior leadership is unstable — MD Murali Bhupatiraju was introduced in May 2025 and is absent by FY26 calls, the management roster shows repeated chairman/MD churn, and May 2026 admits ongoing role replacements.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Order book grew from INR6,000cr (Q1) to INR6,500cr+ (Q3), with the MHI contract expanded to INR1,387cr, a Safran MOU, Pratt & Whitney progression and an 8-year single-source MHI award — management frames this as wallet-share gain against US/Europe/China/Japan incumbents, but gives no explicit market-share percentages. (The long view had said: Explicit and rising wallet-share trajectory from a <1% base in Feb 2024 to documented expansion by Feb 2026, with order book climbing from ~INR6,000 crores (May 2025) to INR6,500 crores, new marquee accounts added each year (Rolls-Royce, then Safran and Pratt & Whitney), aerospace scaling INR43.8cr → INR80.7cr → INR101cr — and the share is being won on qualification, not on discounted price or loosened terms.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Cost to Replicate (STRONG FIT); Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
