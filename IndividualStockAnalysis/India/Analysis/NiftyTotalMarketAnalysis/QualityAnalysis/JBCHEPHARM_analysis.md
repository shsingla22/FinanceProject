# J.B. Chemicals & Pharmaceuticals Ltd. (JBCHEPHARM) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: PHARMA*

J.B. Chemicals & Pharmaceuticals ("J.B. Pharma") is an India-based drug maker that sells branded prescription medicines under its own brand names — Rantac, Metrogyl, Cilacar, Cilacar-T, Nicardia, Azmarda, Sporlac, Razel and an acquired eye-care range — to doctors and chemists across India through a large field force of medical representatives, with cardiology and other chronic therapies as its biggest area. Alongside India it exports formulations to South Africa (both government tenders and the private market), Russia/CIS, the US and other branded export markets, and it manufactures products on contract for other companies (CDMO), where management says it is a global leader in lozenges, especially herbal and medicated ones, plus liquid formulations. Money comes from selling pills, syrups and lozenges at a gross margin that has climbed from roughly 63% to about 69-70%, with operating EBITDA margins guided at 27-29%; growth comes from pushing volumes and prices on big brands, buying brand portfolios, and winning new CDMO clients. The split is roughly 58% India / 42% international; in FY26 India revenue was about INR2,461 crore, international formulations INR1,154 crore and CDMO INR445 crore. Torrent Pharma bought a controlling stake in January 2026 and a full merger was in its final stages as of the May 2026 call.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Domestic formulations (India branded business)** — Branded prescription medicines sold to Indian doctors/pharmacies — the largest part at roughly INR620 crore a quarter and INR2,461 crore in FY26 (about 58% of revenue); management says it is the fastest-growing company among the top 25 in the Indian pharma market, with six brands in the industry's top 300 and a chronic portfolio (cardiology especially) that crossed INR1,000 crore.
- **Chronic / cardiology portfolio (within domestic)** — The high-margin core of the India business — hypertension, cholesterol and heart-failure drugs like Cilacar, Cilacar-T, Nicardia, Razel and Azmarda — where the calls say J.B. ranks 8th in a roughly $3 billion Indian cardiology market and grew 19% in FY26 versus 14% for the industry.
- **Acquired brand portfolios (Sanzyme/Sporlac, Azmarda, paediatrics, ophthalmology)** — Brands bought from other companies and plugged into J.B.'s sales network — e.g. the Sporlac franchise at INR122 crore growing 33% CAGR, Azmarda at about INR75 crore, four paediatric brands worth INR33 crore of sales, and an ophthalmology range added in January 2024; a meaningful growth driver but individually much smaller than the core brands.
- **Trade generics** — A low-margin unbranded generics line inside India that was about 7-8% of India sales until Q3 FY26 and was then deliberately discontinued after the Torrent takeover, which is why reported India growth slowed to 2% in Q4 FY26 while the branded business still grew 8%.
- **Franchisee business (within domestic)** — A smaller India channel run through franchise partners, contributing INR108 crore of revenue in Q3 FY26 and growing about 11% year-on-year.
- **International formulations (exports)** — Finished medicines sold in South Africa, Russia/CIS, the US and other branded export markets — about INR306 crore in Q3 FY26 and INR1,154 crore for FY26; in South Africa management deliberately cut low-margin government tender sales so the higher-margin private market rose from 40% to about 70% of that country's business.
- **CDMO / CMO (contract manufacturing)** — Making lozenges (herbal and medicated, for immunity and cough-and-cold) and liquid formulations for other companies' brands, including consumer players in Europe — around INR445 crore in FY26, roughly a quarter to a third of international revenue, described as a business where J.B. has global leadership in lozenges and which is seasonally stronger in the first half of the year.
- **API** — A small side line selling active pharmaceutical ingredients (the raw drug substance), worth only about INR21 crore in Q3 FY26 — the least important of the reported categories.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

J.B. Chemicals & Pharmaceuticals comes out of all three reviews as the same kind of business: a prescription-led Indian branded generics company built on decades-old brands, sold by a 2,500-person field force to cardiologists, nephrologists, physicians, paediatricians and eye doctors. The numbers back the story up. Sales have grown about 12% a year with very little year-to-year wobble, the company has been the fastest grower in the top 25 of the Indian pharma market (12% against the market's 9%, and 19% against 14% in chronic medicines), and after direct input costs it keeps ₹69 of every ₹100 of sales, with ₹27 of every ₹100 left as operating profit. Capital is working hard too — ₹17 of operating cash for every ₹100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings. The multibagger screen found five outright strong fits (prescription middleman, pricing power, brand strength, market share gains and corporate culture), the risk review found no high-severity channel and rated resilience sound, and the combined rating lands at 81 out of 100, or Outstanding.

The interesting part is that the same few traits produce both the strengths and the worries. The doctor-decides model is exactly what makes the 'friendly middleman' pattern a strong fit — prescriptions rose 57% in a year and are the headline number management reports — but it is also precisely why the 'good enough goods' risk stays on watch: the same molecules are sold more cheaply by many companies, and the person choosing is the prescriber, not the patient paying. Brand heritage works the same way. Nicardia holding roughly 90% of its market and legacy names sitting in the industry's top 150–300 support the brand and pricing-power patterns, and price increases of about 7% alongside volume growth have lifted gross margin from 63% to around 70%. Yet management itself expected 50-plus competitors and price erosion on Azmarda once its patent expired, which is the new-entrant risk in plain sight. The CDMO lozenge arm is a third example: claimed global leadership in herbal and medicated lozenges with all five top OTC customers on the roster is what earns partial credit as a toll road and a mini-monopoly — but resting 12–13% of revenue on a handful of named partners, with that segment flat in FY26 and new-product delays moving group numbers, is why stakeholder concentration is on watch. Even the margin story cuts both ways: walking away from low-margin South Africa tenders and the trade generics channel lifted margins and is why the screen rules out any 'low price plus' character, but it also shows the company retreating from price-competitive ground rather than defending it.

Against that, the softer spots are real but mostly narrow. The quality framework scores competitive advantage at zero and capital allocation near zero, and the heaviest single negative is working capital: about 151 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, which is the same fact that makes recurring revenue a clear no-fit — there are no subscriptions or renewals, and the company funds the float rather than its customers doing so. Operating margins have swung about 4.5 points a year, which suggests some important costs sit outside management's hands. Innovation is only a likely fit rather than a confirmed one because R&D spending is never disclosed and new products contribute roughly 3% of growth through line extensions; forward integration shows up only in the numbers with nothing in the calls to confirm it. What holds the whole thing together is the track record on promises: EBITDA guidance was raised step by step from 24–26% to 27–29% and met each time, ₹548 crore of debt was repaid to a net cash position of about ₹1,200 crore, return on capital moved from 21% to 27%, and operating cash flow more than doubled — which is why the culture pattern is a strong fit and why the watch-rated risks read as things to monitor rather than reasons to stay away.
