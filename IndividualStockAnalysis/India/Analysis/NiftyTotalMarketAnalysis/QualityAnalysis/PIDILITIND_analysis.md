# Pidilite Industries Ltd. (PIDILITIND) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CHEMICALS*

Pidilite Industries is an India-based maker of adhesives, construction chemicals and related specialty chemicals, sold both to ordinary households and tradespeople (carpenters, contractors, painters, applicators) and to other manufacturers. Its consumer-facing products include glues and DIY products, waterproofing products such as the Roofseal roof coating, tile-care products under the Roff brand, and art and craft materials sold heavily to schools; its industrial side supplies pigments and chemicals to footwear, leather and textile makers. It makes money by selling branded products through a very wide distribution network — including more than 7,500 'Pidilite ki Duniya' rural outlets and direct coverage of tens of thousands of villages — earning margins that swing with the price of its main raw material, Vinyl Acetate Monomer (VAM), and with the pricing it takes. The bulk of sales are in India (standalone revenue of about INR 3,272 crores in the March 2026 quarter out of consolidated INR 3,700-odd crores per quarter, and INR 14,553 crores for FY26), with smaller subsidiaries in Asia, Africa and the Americas plus exports to the US and EU.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Consumer & Bazaar (C&B)** — The branded retail business — adhesives, construction chemicals including waterproofing, DIY and art-and-craft products sold to households, painters, carpenters and contractors through retail shops; it is the larger and most-discussed half of the company, growing volumes around 9-15% in recent quarters and singled out by management as the strong, predictable core.
- **Business to Business (B2B)** — Sales of chemicals and industrial adhesives to other manufacturers — including pigments, and supplies to footwear, leather and textile makers — the smaller segment, with domestic B2B growing at mid-teens rates recently while its export portion was hit hard by tariffs and geopolitics.
- **Exports (within both C&B and B2B)** — Shipments abroad, mainly pigments to the US plus sales to the EU; management says the share of business is 'not that significant', but it fell 13.5% in the December 2025 quarter on US tariff issues and was disrupted again in March 2026 by Gulf/West Asia supply-chain problems.
- **International subsidiaries** — Operating units in Asia, Africa and the Americas (the US and Brazil/Pulvitec businesses were exited during FY24); they grew revenue roughly 6-9% in recent quarters with improving margins, and are a modest add-on to the India business.
- **Domestic subsidiaries (incl. Nina waterproofing)** — Indian group companies covering project-type waterproofing work under the Nina brand plus mostly consumer-and-bazaar businesses; small relative to the parent, growing about 4-5% recently, with Nina down 16% in the March 2026 quarter due to difficulty getting work sites.
- **Art & craft materials** — Stationery-type craft products whose sales are tied to schools being open — the business roughly doubled when schools reopened after the pandemic disruption.
- **New/exterior product initiative** — A new exterior wall product described as sitting between paint and cement, being piloted in select geographies; in southern European markets similar products are 15-20% of the exteriors market, but management calls it very early days.
- **NBFC (lending) pilot** — A small non-banking finance venture launched in one city with limited capital, lending to customers on a six-month trial basis before deciding whether to scale it.
- **BuildNext (construction solutions platform)** — A construction-solutions platform Pidilite had invested in and then transferred to JSW One in a share swap in FY26, leaving Pidilite with what management calls an insignificant shareholding in JSW One.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Pidilite comes out of all three reviews as the same company seen from three angles: a set of household names — Fevicol, Dr. Fixit, Fevikwik, Araldite and now Roff — sold through the people who do the work rather than the people who pay for it. The quality framework gives top marks to mini-monopolies, brand-derived pricing power, advertising treated as brand capital, and the assurance customers buy when the cost of a failed joint or a leaking wall dwarfs the cost of the product itself. The multibagger screen reads the same facts and returns strong fits on Brand Strength, Friendly Middleman, Cost to Replicate and Innovation Dominance. These are not four separate strengths but one loop: heavy, sustained advertising builds a name the homeowner asks for by name, which gives the carpenter, applicator and architect a reason to specify it, which gives the 14,000 branded rural outlets and roughly 28,000 directly covered villages something worth stocking, which in turn pays for the next round of advertising. The financial evidence sits underneath it — ₹56 of every ₹100 of sales left after direct input costs, and ₹25 of operating cash for every ₹100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings.

The screen's weaker verdicts point at the same place the quality scores are thinnest. Growth is the lowest-scoring quality area, and correspondingly Market Share Gainers only rates a partial fit: the company talks convincingly about taking share from unorganised and smaller rivals and about eight-plus quarters of double-digit rural growth, with underlying volume growth rising roughly 100–120 basis points a year to 11.1% in FY26, but it never publishes a share number or names the rivals it is beating. Pricing Power is a likely rather than strong fit for a related reason: the price moves described are mostly raw-material pass-through in both directions — 14–18% up in FY23, mid-single-digit rollbacks in FY24, 12–15% on Fevicol in April–May 2026 — with management openly choosing volume over price. Recurring Revenue is only partial because nothing in the record describes subscriptions, service contracts or renewals; the small lending pilot is an experiment, not an attached revenue stream. Low Price Plus is a clear non-fit, and that is a compliment here: this is a premium-priced business running a 20–24% profit corridor, not a thin-margin, high-turnover one. The management and capital-allocation marks tie the picture together — repeated candour about bad news (Kerala demand weakness, exports down 13.5%, Nina waterproofing down 16%, a conceded decline in asset turns) and a consistently organic approach that refused a big-bang entry into paints, kept the lending pilot to a single city and reinvested margin gains into demand generation.

Five of the eight risk channels come back low, and the three flagged for watching are, tellingly, the flip side of the very traits that earn the strong pattern fits. Cyclicality is the sharpest: input costs swing violently — the key raw material moved from $2,230 a tonne in Q1 FY23 to $1,022 in Q1 FY25 and $830 in Q3 FY26, then back to roughly $1,800 by May 2026 against a 40–50% basket inflation — and because the company deliberately absorbs rather than fully passes on cost, margins move with them; the cushion is that 70–75% of the portfolio is repair and renovation spread across residential, commercial, infrastructure and government work. New Entrants is the second watch item, and it exists precisely because the categories are attractive: management concedes rivals appear whenever input costs turn benign, category boundaries are porous in both directions, and Pidilite's own move into paints puts it against what it calls a 300-pound gorilla. Good Enough Goods is the third, and it is the direct shadow of the Friendly Middleman strength — an intermediary chooses the product, a large unorganised fringe crowds fast-growing categories like tile adhesives, and price-capped lines such as art materials at ₹10, ₹15 and ₹20 could only absorb about 60% of inflation. Technology, government, customer concentration, consumer preference and fashion risks are all rated low, which is why overall resilience reads as sound. The combined rating of 86 out of 100 — 45% weight on business quality at 76, 30% on pattern fit at 100 and 25% on risk safety at 88 — lands at Outstanding, and the arithmetic shows where it comes from: a perfect score on the pattern screen and a clean risk profile carrying a business-quality score held back mainly by growth disclosure and the limits of the competitive-advantage evidence.

## What to watch

- Cyclicality risk: whether margins hold near the 20–24% corridor now that the key input has swung back toward $1,800 a tonne after touching $830, given the stated policy of under-pricing inflation to protect volume.
- Market Share Gainers (partial fit): underlying volume growth has climbed roughly 100–120 basis points a year to 11.1% — if that stalls, the share-gain story loses the only hard number supporting it, since no explicit share figures are ever given.
- New Entrants risk: how the paints entry against the '300-pound gorilla' progresses, and whether paint majors selling exterior coatings as waterproofing erode the Dr. Fixit position.
- Good Enough Goods risk: pass-through in price-capped lines such as art materials, where only about 60% of inflation could be recovered, and whether the unorganised fringe regains ground in tile adhesives as that category grows 15–20%.
- Disciplined stewards check and Forward Integrators (likely fit): the organic, no-big-bang record — a swapped-away construction platform, a single-city lending pilot — would be undercut by a large acquisition or a capital-heavy channel build.
