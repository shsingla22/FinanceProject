# Manappuram Finance Ltd. (MANAPPURAM) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

Manappuram Finance is an Indian non-bank lender (NBFC) whose main product is the gold loan: small, short-tenure cash loans given to households and small businesses against their own gold jewellery held as collateral, with typical ticket sizes around INR 56,000 and durations of roughly 84 days, lent at a conservative loan-to-value (about 65% of gold value) and at yields in the ~19-22% range. It makes money on the spread between what it charges borrowers and its cost of borrowing from banks, capital markets and securitisation, and it serves customers largely at the 'bottom of the pyramid' through a branch network across India plus an online gold loan channel (74% of the gold book by late 2024). Around this core it runs a group of other lending arms — microfinance, vehicle and equipment finance, affordable home loans and MSME/secured business loans — so consolidated assets under management grew from about INR 30,760 crore in mid-2022 to INR 63,798 crore by March 2026. Gold loans dominate and have grown more dominant over time, moving from 67% of consolidated AUM in 2022 to 53% in late 2024 and back up to 80% by Q4 FY26 as gold prices rose and the non-gold books were deliberately shrunk.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Gold loan business (standalone Manappuram Finance)** — Lends cash against gold jewellery pledged by retail customers — the company's flagship and 'core growth engine', at INR 50,953 crore AUM and 80% of consolidated AUM by March 2026 (up 99% year-on-year), serving over 26 lakh customers, and now split under new RBI rules into consumption loans for household needs and income-generating loans for small businesses.
- **Asirvad Microfinance Limited (microfinance subsidiary)** — Small unsecured group loans to low-income, mostly rural borrowers, plus its own small gold loan book; it peaked around INR 12,149 crore AUM in late 2024 but was barred by the RBI from fresh disbursement in October 2024 over pricing and borrower-assessment lapses, and after tightened underwriting and geographic pullback shrank to about INR 6,091 crore by Q3 FY26 while still running losses (INR 156 crore in that quarter).
- **Vehicle and Equipment Finance** — Loans for commercial vehicles (about half the book), passenger cars, two-wheelers and farm equipment; it grew fast to INR 4,848 crore by Q2 FY25 but was later scaled down, with car loans and farm equipment loans paused, as management prioritised asset quality over volume.
- **Manappuram Home Finance (affordable housing)** — A separately run housing finance arm giving home loans to lower-income buyers, small relative to the group at INR 1,692 crore AUM in Q2 FY25 and earning only a few crore of profit per quarter; described in FY26 as stable but with muted growth.
- **MSME and allied lending (including on-lending)** — Business loans to micro, small and medium enterprises — management's stated aim is secured credit lines to MSMEs — at roughly INR 1,113 crore in 2022 rising to INR 2,963 crore by Q2 FY25 (including an INR 816 crore on-lending book), and subsequently scaled back along with vehicle finance pending stronger systems and controls.
- **Co-lending partnerships** — A newer origination channel launched in January 2026 in which Manappuram lends jointly with partners, described as a group priority that extends reach into geographies where it has few branches and improves returns for its subsidiaries.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Manappuram is a gold-backed lender operating in what management calls India's largest retail lending asset class, where roughly 65% of the market still sits with unorganised lenders — a genuinely long runway that earns a positive mark on structural end-market growth. The everyday appeal of the product is real and functional: households borrow against family jewellery for school reopenings, the sowing season and small-business working capital, which is why the review scores customer-facing risks low across the board (Fashion Risk, Shifting Consumer Preferences and Stakeholder Concentration are all LOW, on a granular book of 25.2 lakh gold customers at about INR1.27 lakh average ticket with 3.16 lakh new customers added in a single quarter). The company also owns its whole route to the customer — branches plus a direct app — and runs quiet, numbers-focused calls with no celebrity positioning, scoring positively on being out of the limelight. But the quality scores split sharply by area: Competitive Advantage and Customer Benefits are mildly positive, while Industry Structure is the weakest at -0.67 and Capital Allocation is negative. The combined rating lands at 50 out of 100 (64% x 49 on business quality, 36% x 52 on risk safety) — Decent, three stars — and resilience is recorded as unknown.

The single trait that shapes most of this report is that a gold loan is an undifferentiated product in a crowded market, and it drives a pattern verdict and a risk verdict at the same time. On the pattern side it is why Pricing Power and Low Price Plus are outright no-fits and why Brand Strength only gets a partial: the franchise pulls in 3-4 lakh new customers a quarter, but it carries no price premium — Manappuram abandoned above-market pricing and benchmarked to competitors, with gold loan yields falling from roughly 25-26% in Q1 FY22 to 21.7% in 2022 to 18.3% by FY26. On the risk side the identical fact is logged as Good Enough Goods at HIGH RISK, with New Entrants on WATCH given banks lending at 90% LTV, fintechs and IIFL's return, and it shows up again in the negative checks on intangible benefits, on pricing/mix/volume, and on barriers to entry, where teaser schemes and indiscriminate lending were curbed by regulation rather than by economics. The same doubling-up appears in growth: headline gold AUM rose 99.1% year-on-year in FY26, but tonnage grew only 2.8% and customer numbers fell from 26.55 lakh to 25.2 lakh, so the Market Share Gainers pattern is a no-fit and Cyclicality sits on WATCH for the same reason — the balance sheet inflates and deflates with a commodity price, not with volume. Cost to Replicate is a no-fit on the company's own admission of a level playing field. The bright spot in this cluster is technology, rated LOW risk: the online gold loan book went from 74% to 92% of the total, alongside paperless journeys and AI branch security — though the innovation pattern is only partial because much of the work is regulator-prompted catch-up.

The second thread is diversification and the regulator, and here too one story explains several verdicts. The move into unsecured microfinance through Asirvad is what turns the M&A pattern negative, what makes the stewardship check negative (an RBI disbursement ban from 22 October 2024 for pricing and underwriting deficiencies, followed by an INR136 crore vehicle-loan write-off), and what makes Government Dependency the report's other HIGH RISK — the September 2024 gold-loan circular and the April 2026 collateral rules point the same way, with the regulator, not the market, setting the economics. The fallout also explains why geographical expansion scores negative: management explicitly shrank MSME and vehicle finance rather than expanded, with co-lending the only incremental reach. Asirvad's AUM fell 39% year-on-year and vehicle finance 37%, and the bench had to be rebuilt almost entirely from outside — group CFO, compliance officer, general counsel and heads of technology, risk, audit, operations, HR and two businesses — which is why the talent check is negative too. Against that, two things pull the other way. Microfinance appears to be at the bottom of its credit cycle: the new book is 59% of the portfolio with 99.4% collection efficiency and Asirvad returned to a small profit in Q4 FY26, the one positive cyclical check. And the culture pattern, while only partial because of repeated undelivered promises such as the 2022 hope of around 5% return on assets, credits candid disclosure of the ban, the write-off and segment stress, conservative capital at 21-31% CRAR and unbroken dividends. In short: a durable, growing, low-glamour demand base with no pricing power, a balance sheet that moves with the gold price, and a management record marked by one costly diversification that the regulator halted.
