### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 4 strengthened, 1 weakened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Across all four calls management frames value as the mainstay (2for79/2for99, Rs.99 cafe) paired with relentless, itemised cost work — in-house broiler in 250+ then all restaurants, solar, e-coolers, renegotiated IT contracts, supply chain moved closer to stores — lifting gross margin to 69.9% (+2.1% y/y) and restaurant EBITDA +25.7% while holding entry price points. (The long view had said: Genuine cost obsession is itemised year after year — in-house broiler in 250 restaurants halving utilities, solar, supply-chain clustering to cut transport, 25% G&A cut in Indonesia — paired with an explicit low-price value ladder (2 for INR50 → INR79/99); but returns remain thin (company EBITDA only 5.8% in FY26) and scale trails larger QSR peers, so the 'high turns, strong returns' half is unproven.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: RBA operates a licensed global brand (Burger King, Whopper) rather than owning heritage it created, and its own Popeyes portfolio is failing at 24-25 stores; brand strength shows in 11-12 quarters of positive SSSG and a 6.3% Q4 SSSG in a 'difficult and tough sales environment', but the equity is rented, not owned. (The long view had said: RBA operates a global heritage brand with sustained A&P (celebrity campaigns, festival platforms) and claims consumer leadership on burgers in Indonesia, but it licenses rather than owns the brand and the brand demonstrably does not command premium pricing — it is sold through 2-for-79 value offers, and Popeyes shows the brand name alone does not travel.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The four calls describe a company-operated QSR P&L (dine-in, delivery, digital orders) with no licence fees, AMC, subscription or deferred-revenue stream discussed; the evidence is silent on any recurring layer. (The long view had said: Across all 16 calls the revenue model is purely transactional restaurant sales (ADS, SSSG, delivery mix); there is no license fee, AMC, subscription or deferred-revenue stream discussed, and the app/CRM is described as acquisition-and-engagement, not contracted recurring revenue.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is genuine premiumisation (Kings Collection 2.0 at Rs.200, Korean LTOs 'selling higher than INR200' at higher APC) and delivery discounts cut for a 2-point margin gain, but the volume engine is still deep value at Rs.79/99 held flat for over a year — this is mix and discount management, not repeated above-inflation price increases. (The long view had said: The trajectory is the opposite of pricing power: value is the stated 'fulcrum' and 'mainstay' of strategy for over a decade, ADS fell from INR131k in Jul-2022 to INR117k in Q3FY26, and SSSG is explicitly traffic-led rather than price-led, with margin gains coming from cost-out and discount reduction, not price rises.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Store count rose from 577 to ~600 with 67 net adds y/y and nearly doubled since FY22, alongside positive SSSG while management repeatedly calls the environment tough — implying share gain — but no explicit market-share figures or comparisons versus named competitors are given in any of the four calls. (The long view had said: India shows persistent outperformance — 328 stores (Jun-2022) to ~600 (Mar-2026), 11-12 consecutive quarters of positive SSSG with positive traffic in a slowing market — but management never cites market-share numbers versus named competitors, and the international leg went backwards (46 Indonesian stores closed, portfolio cut to 149; Popeyes stuck at 24-25).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The entire business runs on brands licensed from a single franchisor (Burger King, Popeyes) and a material sales channel sits with a handful of delivery aggregators whose economics management negotiates directly; the Indonesian arm is also mid-transition to 'new promoters', with the Popeyes portfolio explicitly earmarked to move to 'someone else's portfolio'. Bounded because no licence-renewal issue was raised on any call and dine-in traffic is being deliberately grown as the primary channel. (The long view had said: The entire business is a licensed master-franchise of two brands owned by a single foreign licensor (Burger King, and Popeyes under a 300-restaurant MFDA), so the franchisor relationship is existential in exactly the Safilo sense; on top of that, third-party delivery aggregators now carry 43-44% of India sales, and a single new promoter (Inspira Global) is taking ~35% control via INR900 crore equity plus INR700 crore warrants.)
- **Cyclicality**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Demand is everyday food consumption, not customer capex or a commodity cycle; even in what management called a 'very difficult and tough sales environment' (Q3 FY26) the India business delivered its 11th consecutive quarter of positive SSSG and kept investing counter-cyclically, adding 67 net restaurants year-over-year and moving from 577 to ~600 stores. Discretionary consumer softness is the only cyclical element visible and it did not break volumes or margins over the four calls (Q4 SSSG 6.3%, best in 12 quarters). (The long view had said: QSR food is a repeat-purchase flow product, not a customer-capex or commodity business, and RBA delivered positive SSSG in 11 of the last ~12 quarters through what management repeatedly calls a weak demand environment; but the company's own aggressive expansion cycle (328 stores in Aug 2022 to ~600 by Mar 2026, plus the Indonesia acquisition) produced visible over-investment damage — 46 Indonesian stores closed as unprofitable and Popeyes stranded sub-scale at 24-25 outlets.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The preference shifts visible in the calls — toward digital ordering, delivery, cafe/coffee occasions and premium menus — have been met with a completed portfolio pivot rather than eroding demand: cafes installed in ~90% of restaurants, 91-92% digital orders, a premium Kings Collection 2.0 and quarterly premium LTOs, and 12 consecutive quarters of positive SSSG. No health/nutrition-driven volume decline is mentioned. (The long view had said: The channel shift to convenience genuinely re-shaped the business — delivery went from ~39% to 43-44% of sales, forcing a lower-margin mix management spent years repairing — and the menu is continuously reformulated around Indian veg/health/regional preferences (no-onion-no-garlic Katra store, no synthetic colours, paneer and veg value tiers); the pivot has so far been executed, not lost.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Government Dependency (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Fashion Risk (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 2 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 2 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
