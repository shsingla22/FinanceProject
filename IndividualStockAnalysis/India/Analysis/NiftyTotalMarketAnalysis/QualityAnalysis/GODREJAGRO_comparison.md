### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 25 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 4 improved, 1 regressed, 20 unchanged. 7 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them).

| Return on Capital | Good (+1.00) | Good (+1.00) | +0.00 |
| Growth | Good (+0.67) | Good (+0.83) | +0.17 |

| Customer Benefits | Neutral (+0.50) | Good (+0.75) | +0.25 |
| Competitive Advantage | Neutral (+0.50) | Neutral (+0.50) | +0.00 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: The mix is well-matched — retail consumers get branded Yummiez/Jersey value-added products while farmer and B2B customers get reliability-led feed and crop inputs, both showing improving salience. | The long view had said: The customer base is a mixed bag — millions of retail farmers served on functional (not intangible) benefits, plus corporate CDMO clients at Astec with genuine stickiness, but no coherent single benefit-match.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: The oil palm business operates under India's zone-allocation regime giving effective regional exclusivity over Fresh Fruit Bunch procurement, evidenced by area expansion translating directly into 16% FFB volume growth. | The long view had said: Oil palm plantations confer regional captive processing catchments through state allocations and mill proximity (Mizoram/Northeast), but the rest of the portfolio has no switching-cost lock-in.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Oil palm zoning creates a genuine barrier, but feed and dairy face low entry barriers and input-cost-driven pricing, with dairy margins collapsing to 3% on milk procurement costs the company could not pass through. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.4 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Low barriers and irrational pricing are evident in agrochemicals, with sustained Chinese dumping crushing export realizations and channel over-stuffing requiring credit-hygiene clean-ups.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Animal Nutrition volumes grew 15% in Q4 and 12% in Q3 with cattle feed up 21-24%, explicitly described as significantly ahead of industry growth, in a large fragmented feed market leaving ample runway. | The long view had said: Repeated share gains in animal feed (11% volume growth in Q1FY23, 15% in Q4FY26 'significantly ahead of industry'), with explicit runway in under-penetrated East/Central India, though share tracking is admittedly informal.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Management openly flags negatives — flat Crop Protection results, dairy margin compression, channel inventory overhang in Q1 base, and the Iran war overhang on guidance — and volunteers that Vegetable Oil pricing was a negative rather than claiming price-led growth. | The long view had said: Management repeatedly owns internal failures explicitly — admitting they 'goofed up big time' on crop protection processes, being caught unaware by the soymeal import policy, and stating plainly that visibility on enterprise products is limited.

*Unchanged: Structural end-market growth; Profit margin; Disciplined stewards; Share donators; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Cost of working capital; Security by obscurity; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Return on capital (headline); Asset turn; People matter (talent development); Distribution advantage; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Halo effect (caution); Intangible benefits; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Cyclical market growth; Technology advantage; Assurance benefits; Out of limelight.*

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these — identical by construction): Geographical expansion (Good); Persistence of growth (Good); Merger and acquisition (Good); Research and development (Good); Convenience benefits (Good); Advertisement and promotion (Neutral); Mini-monopolies (Weak).*

**That strengthened:**

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Low Price Plus (LIKELY FIT); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

**That worsened:**

*Unchanged: Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 13 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
