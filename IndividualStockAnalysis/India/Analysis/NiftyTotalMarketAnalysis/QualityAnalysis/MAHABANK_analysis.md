# Bank of Maharashtra. (MAHABANK) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

Bank of Maharashtra is an Indian state-owned commercial bank headquartered in Pune (head office "Lokmangal", Shivajinagar), listed on the BSE and NSE under the ticker MAHABANK, with the Government of India holding 73.60% of it as of March 2026. It takes deposits from the public — current, savings and term deposits, with a heavy tilt toward low-cost CASA (current and savings) balances that make up roughly 50-56% of total deposits — and lends that money out to retail borrowers (home, vehicle and gold loans), farmers, small businesses (MSME) and corporates. It earns most of its money from the gap between the interest it charges on loans (yield on advances around 9.05%) and what it pays on deposits (cost of deposits around 4.52%), producing a net interest margin near 3.85-3.95%; it also earns fee and other income, gains or losses on its bond investment portfolio, and cash recovered from loans it had previously written off. Total business (deposits plus loans) was about INR 3.36 lakh crore in mid-2022 and INR 4.76 lakh crore by mid-2024, growing further to add roughly INR 1,04,000 crore in the year to June 2026, with FY26 net profit of INR 7,019 crore.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Deposits (CASA and term deposits)** — The bank's funding base — current, savings and fixed deposits from retail customers plus a large book of government departments, ministries and entities that bank with it; management calls CASA its core strength, holding it above 50% (52.51% at March 2026) even amid what it called a "real war for deposits".
- **Retail lending** — Loans to individuals — home loans, vehicle loans and gold loans are the named focus products — and the fastest-growing part of the loan book, up 32% in FY26 (home loans 29%, vehicle 56%, gold 53%) and 25% year-on-year in Q1 FY27.
- **Agriculture (Agri)** — Farm lending, typically targeted to grow 15-16% a year; it dipped to low single digits during a deliberate portfolio rebalancing in FY26 before recovering to 13% for the year and 30% year-on-year by June 2026.
- **MSME lending** — Loans to micro, small and medium enterprises, growing 20% in FY25 and 11% in FY26 (also slowed by the rebalancing), then 23% year-on-year in Q1 FY27.
- **RAM book (Retail + Agri + MSME combined)** — The bank groups these three as "RAM" and manages the split against corporate lending as a board-level target, holding it at 63% RAM to 37% corporate.
- **Corporate lending** — Larger loans to companies — including mid-corporates in the INR 100-150 crore range, plus green and renewable energy, infrastructure and data centre projects that management calls "bullish sectors" — kept at roughly 37-41% of advances and growing 22% in FY26 and 30% year-on-year in Q1 FY27.
- **Treasury / investments** — The bank's bond portfolio, split between AFS (available-for-sale, marked to market) and HTM (held-to-maturity); it can add to or subtract from profit — a Rs. 200 crore depreciation hit and Rs. 160 crore drop in trading gains dented Q1 FY23 — and the bank manages it by keeping modified duration low (around 1.4).
- **Recovery and the written-off loan book** — A dedicated effort to collect on bad and already written-off loans — a roughly INR 20,000-21,000 crore written-off pool (about INR 7,500 crore of it in NCLT bankruptcy proceedings), plus settlement schemes for small accounts and auctions of repossessed property — which management calls a "gold mine" and which delivered INR 1,423 crore of recoveries in FY26 straight into operating profit.
- **Digital banking and channels** — The mobile banking app (being revamped into what management calls a "lifestyle banking solution") and 24/7 video KYC account opening including Sundays and holidays, used to onboard customers and win deposits rather than as a separate revenue line.
- **Co-lending and partnerships** — Joint lending tie-ups with highly rated NBFCs and partnerships with term-lending institutions to participate in infrastructure and renewable projects — described as a way to expand the loan book using partners' origination and appraisal expertise.
- **New Business Acquisition vertical** — A dedicated unit headed by a General Manager that chases government and institutional relationships for current and savings balances, including ERP/API integrations for their bulk transactions.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*
