### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 1 weakened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Share gain is the single most consistent thread across all four calls — Q1 shipments +13% year-on-year on regained share, Q2 +18% year-on-year at record revenue, Q3 record INR 1,092 crores, and FY26 the first INR 4,000 crore year in the Indian refractory industry — and it was won alongside positive cash generation (record INR 289 crore operating cash flow in Q3), not by buying volume at any cost. (The long view had said: Share reached ~30% in Feb 2023 but largely by acquiring Dalmia OCL and Hi-Tech rather than organically; FY25 shipments fell ~7% y-o-y while steel grew, and FY26 claims 'continued market share gains' with a 2% outperformance target — genuine leadership, but contested and partly bought.)
- **Low Price Plus**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: There is a genuine, itemised cost programme running through all four calls — recipe optimization, circular economy/recycling, energy efficiency, manpower planning, machine-level loading — but RHIM positions itself as the premium solutions partner de-commoditizing the category, not as a scale low-price player passing savings to customers. (The long view had said: Management repeatedly refuses the low-price route, ceding volume rather than matching imports (Nov 2024) and running cost programs to protect margin, not to pass savings to price; capacity utilisation of 61-67% is the opposite of the high-asset-turn model.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Some genuinely hard-to-replicate assets exist — global parent recipe/product technology transferred into India, India's only robotic caster installations, a nationwide service footprint across 9 refractory service locations, and embedded 4PRO contracts inside customer plants — but management itself concedes new domestic capacity and imports keep arriving, so a well-funded entrant is visibly not being kept out of the commoditized bulk of the market. (The long view had said: Management's own impairment rationale names new competitor capacity additions, import commoditisation and pricing pressure as structural — a well-funded entrant is demonstrably eroding the position, and the assets (kilns, capacity, mines) are buyable, as RHIM itself showed by acquiring Dalmia OCL.)

**That weakened:**

- **Toll Roads**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Refractories are indeed a small, mission-critical input to steel and cement, but the defining toll-road trait — customers paying without negotiating — is directly contradicted across all four calls, which describe overcapacity, import-driven commoditization and aggressive price behaviour rather than a rational oligopoly. (The long view had said: Refractories are a small but operationally critical input (guarantee/bonus clauses, 'ensuring uninterrupted supply', Tata's Agile Partner award), yet customers negotiate hard and Chinese dumping at $650-700 CIF forces price wars — management itself calls much of the category commoditized, so the no-negotiation toll economics only hold in the specialised segments.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Refractory demand is largely consumption-linked to steel and cement output (a flow/consumable base), but the last four calls repeatedly tie growth to customer capex — new converters, ladles, coke oven/DRI/pellet projects, EAF projects and cement plant expansions — and margins swung materially within the year (10.7% in Q2 to 13.7% in Q3 to 12.1% in Q4) on alumina/magnesia input cost movements, with Q4 revenue falling to INR 932 crores on a 'softer cement demand cycle'. (The long view had said: Across FY23–FY26 revenue and margins swung with customer capex and raw-material cycles: H1 FY25 revenue fell 8.8% because customer commissioning slipped, EBITDA margin moved from ~16% (H1 FY25) to 10.7% (Q2 FY26) to 13.7% (Q3 FY26) and 11.9% for FY26, and the CFO stated outright that the business is cyclical; growth is explicitly tied to steel/cement capacity commissioning and project orders (coke oven batteries, SMS expansions) rather than only to customer operations.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: New Entrants (HIGH RISK); Good Enough Goods (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
