# State Bank of India (SBIN) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

State Bank of India is a very large Indian bank: it takes deposits from the public — savings accounts, current accounts and term deposits — and lends that money out as loans, earning most of its income from the gap between the interest it charges borrowers and the interest it pays depositors (net interest income), plus fee and other non-interest income and gains on its bond portfolio. Its customers span ordinary individuals (home loans, personal loans, savings accounts), farmers, small and medium businesses, and large corporates, served through a branch network, a contact centre and its YONO digital app, which by FY26 had over 10 crore registered users and originated 66% of new savings accounts. It is primarily a domestic Indian bank but also runs foreign offices doing overseas lending and deposit-taking, and it owns a group of subsidiaries that management says keep adding value for shareholders. By FY26 it had a balance sheet of about Rs 76 trillion, roughly Rs 59 trillion of deposits, Rs 49 trillion of loans and Rs 18 trillion of investments, with net profit of Rs 80,032 crore, ROA above 1% and ROE of 18.5%.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Retail Personal advances** — Loans to individuals — home loans (management repeatedly calls itself the leader in home loans) and unsecured personal loans — the largest chunk of the non-corporate book at about Rs 13.7 trillion as of Q1FY25 and growing in the low-to-mid teens.
- **Agriculture (Agri) advances** — Lending to farmers and the rural economy, about Rs 3.09 trillion as of Q1FY25 and growing around 17% year-on-year.
- **SME / MSME advances** — Loans to small and medium-sized businesses, about Rs 4.43 trillion as of Q1FY25 and the fastest-growing of the three RAM segments at roughly 20% year-on-year.
- **Corporate Banking** — Lending to large companies, which management describes as rebounding and growing 13-15% a year in FY26; together with Retail, Agri and SME it makes up the whole domestic loan book.
- **International Banking / foreign offices** — Overseas branches that lend and take deposits outside India — advances grew 20% and deposits 12.7% in FY26 — a meaningful but smaller part of the bank than the domestic business.
- **Global Markets / Treasury (investment book)** — The bank's roughly Rs 18 trillion portfolio of government securities, state development loans and highly rated corporate bonds, which produces income but can swing profits — mark-to-market losses on this book cut operating profit by about a third in Q1FY23.
- **Deposits / liability franchise** — The funding side of the bank — savings and current accounts (CASA ratio about 39%, a cheap source of money) plus retail term deposits growing about 15% — which management calls a core focus as household savings shift toward market-linked products.
- **YONO (digital banking platform)** — The bank's app and digital operating model, with over 10 crore registered users, used to open accounts (66% of new savings accounts in FY26), sell products and cut the cost of serving customers; management aims to double users to 20 crore in 2-3 years.
- **Subsidiaries** — Separately managed group companies whose Managing Directors attend the results meets; management says they perform consistently and create value through digital expansion and product innovation, but the calls give no size figures for them.
- **Stressed Assets Resolution Group (SARG) / risk** — The unit that manages bad loans and recoveries; its work shows up in gross NPAs falling from 3.91% in mid-2022 to 1.49% by March 2026 and credit cost down to about 0.29%.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

State Bank of India earns a Strong (4-star) overall rating of 69 out of 100, built from 61 on business quality, 85 on multibagger pattern fit and 64 on risk safety. Almost everything good about the story traces back to one asset: the deposit base. Savings balances are three times those of the next largest bank, deposits total Rs 59 trillion, roughly 60,000 savings accounts are opened every day, and 10 crore customers use the YONO app, with 66% of new accounts opened digitally. That reach is why the framework scores Distribution advantage and Convenience benefits at the top of the scale, and why depositors accept a savings rate of just 2.5% — they are effectively buying sovereign-backed safety, which is what the Assurance benefits check rewards. The same asset produces four of the strongest pattern fits: Low Price Plus (the cheapest deposits in the system paired with real cost discipline through Project Saral and 10,000 Seva Sarathis, and the best loan quality in the industry at 1.49% gross bad loans), Cost to Replicate (no funded newcomer can rebuild this deposit book), Brand Strength (management declining to cut the savings rate to its own formula-derived level in FY23 rather than harvest the franchise), and Market Share Gainers (savings share rising from 20.2% in 2004 to 22.3% in 2014 to 26.27%, with credit growing 16.87% year-on-year, faster than the system, while asset quality improved rather than deteriorated).

The traits that create those strengths are the same ones that create the biggest risks, which is why the risk score sits well below the pattern score. Government ownership is what makes the safety promise credible and makes new banking licences nearly impossible to obtain — but it is also the single High Risk item in the review, because policy sets both sides of the margin: a formula governs the savings rate, repo-linked external benchmark pricing governs the loan book, and the Reserve Bank's expected-credit-loss transition from April 2027 is a pending capital event management has not put a number on. That same repo linkage is precisely why Pricing Power is scored a clear No Fit: loan yields reprice downward automatically when rates fall, fourth-quarter FY26 net interest income was explicitly hit by that pass-through, and management guides margin only to about 3% through the cycle rather than to rising prices. It also feeds the Cyclicality watch item, alongside a 32.79% drop in first-quarter FY23 operating profit on treasury losses and a roughly Rs 3,500 crore trading and valuation hit in the fourth quarter of FY26. State ownership caps the Corporate Culture pattern at Partial too: guidance discipline is genuinely good — 1% return on assets, roughly 3% exit margin and 50 basis points of credit cost promised and delivered, with the Chairman openly admitting a wrong internal bond-yield call, which is why the Candor check scores full marks — but leadership is state-appointed and turns over fast, with four chairmen and around a dozen managing directors across the period reviewed.

Two further tensions round out the picture. First, sheer size cuts both ways. It underwrites the moat, but it also means returns come from leverage rather than efficient use of assets — a Rs 76 trillion balance sheet earning about 1% on assets is why Asset turn and the whole Return on Capital area score at the bottom, and why Growth and Industry Structure score low: with savings balances already three times the runner-up's, the remaining runway for share gains is limited. Being the largest and most watched bank in India also means the Security by obscurity check is deeply negative — every major broker covers the quarterly calls on live webcasts, so there is no neglected corner here. Second, the deposit franchise that anchors the whole thesis is the thing under pressure. Households are moving savings into mutual funds and market-linked products, low-cost deposit growth lagged term deposits for years (up 2.59% against 12.20% in the first quarter of FY25), and management concedes this will reshape banking — this is the Shifting Consumer Preferences watch item, and it is the same competitive squeeze that shows up as the New Entrants watch item, with digital platforms and market instruments contesting household savings. The bank's answer is the technology spend that the Capex for growth and Research and development checks credit and that the Innovation Dominance pattern rates Partial: the new YONO drew 4 crore registrations in three months and low-cost deposits still improved to 39.46% even after the rate cut. But management itself has raised technology from an initiative to a systemic risk, naming AI tools that exploit weaknesses at scale — so the defence and the danger are, again, the same thing. Customer and funding concentration is rated Low, and commoditisation is rated Low because SBI has been gaining share while paying less, not defending a premium. Overall resilience is recorded as Unknown.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** SBI is a government-owned bank whose pricing, provisioning and product economics are set by policy: savings rate formula and EBLR repo linkage determine both sides of the margin, the RBI's ECL transition from April 2027 is a pending capital event management won't yet quantify, and growth prospects are framed around Union Budget capex, Viksit Bharat 2047 and government credit-guarantee schemes. Even the personal loan book leans on the state — roughly 85-90% of express credit goes to government, defence and quasi-government salary accounts.

> "out of this 95%, almost about 85% would be for the government employees only, that is defense and government employees" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Scale and diversification across retail, SME, agri, corporate, international and subsidiaries mean no single scheme dominates; but the regulatory determination of pricing and provisioning is structural and unmitigated*

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Bank earnings ride the credit and rate cycle: Q1FY23 operating profit fell 32.79% year-on-year on treasury MTM losses, Q4FY26 was dragged by a ~Rs 3,500 crore trading/valuation hit and NII compression from EBLR repo resets, and slippages are repeatedly explained as seasonal (agri/SME). But demand is operations-linked flow lending (retail, agri, SME, salary accounts) rather than customer capex, and asset quality has improved steadily across four years (GNPA 3.91% to 1.49%) with ROA held above 1% throughout.

> "our operating profit also has gone down and the net profit also has gone down as compared to the last quarter and mainly dragged the profitability, dragged by the treasury operation" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/operations-linked lending base, granular RAM book, 39% CASA and countercyclical buffer provisions (Rs 30,642 crore non-NPA provisions, Rs 3,500 crore COVID provision retained)*
