# Kalpataru Proj. (KPIL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Kalpataru Projects International (KPIL) is an India-based engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) contractor — it wins fixed-scope infrastructure and construction contracts, then designs, buys the materials for, and physically builds them for clients such as power grid utilities (e.g. PGCIL), state water departments, oil & gas majors (e.g. Aramco in Saudi Arabia), metro rail authorities, railways, and private property developers. Revenue is recognised as projects are executed against a large order backlog, so the order book is the main indicator of future sales: FY26 consolidated revenue was INR 27,143 crores off an all-time-high order book of INR 65,457 crores, with consolidated PBT margins of about 4.9% and PAT of INR 1,031 crores. Its biggest business by far is power transmission & distribution, followed by buildings and factories, with smaller water, oil & gas, urban infrastructure and railway units. Roughly speaking it works across India and international markets including the Nordics (subsidiary LMG Sweden), the Middle East, Africa, South America and Brazil (subsidiary Fasttel), and it is exiting non-core assets such as road toll SPVs and an Indore real estate project to put capital back into EPC. The company is the merged entity of the erstwhile Kalpataru Power and JMC Projects, combined from FY24.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Transmission & Distribution (T&D)** — Builds power transmission lines, substations and HVDC systems for utilities in India and overseas; the flagship and largest business, with revenue crossing INR 10,000 crores in FY25, +25% growth in FY26 and an order book of INR 28,572 crores at end-FY26 — management calls itself a global leader here.
- **Buildings & Factories (B&F)** — Constructs residential towers, airports, data centres, hospitals and industrial plants — increasingly on 'design-build' contracts where KPIL does the design too; the second-largest business, with FY26 order inflows of INR 11,460 crores (up ~40%) and an order book of INR 18,295 crores.
- **Oil & Gas** — Builds oil and gas infrastructure, mainly in the Middle East and anchored by Saudi Aramco projects; small but the fastest-scaling unit — revenue INR 1,758 crores in FY25 after doubling, then up another 55% in FY26, and management calls it a future major growth engine.
- **Water** — Executes water supply and pipeline work, largely government Jal Jeevan Mission schemes (including UP JJM); the problem child — INR 2,112 crores of FY26 revenue, the only unit that shrank, held back by delayed government payments, so the company is finishing existing jobs and bidding cautiously despite a ~INR 9,500 crore order book.
- **Urban Infrastructure** — Builds elevated and underground metro rail, tunnels (including TBM tunnelling in Kanpur and Bhopal) and elevated roads; a mid-sized unit that grew 49% in FY26 on metro execution, with FY26 order wins of about INR 2,000 crores.
- **Railways** — Rail infrastructure work; a small, deliberately shrinking-focus unit — about INR 1,019 crores of revenue in FY25 and 9% growth in FY26 — where management bids selectively because competition is intense.
- **International subsidiaries (LMG Sweden, Fasttel Brazil)** — Overseas T&D arms — LMG serves Sweden and the Nordics and has been a strong performer (FY25 revenue +79%, order book INR 3,535 crores), while Fasttel Brazil underperformed and KPIL wound up all its Brazilian projects and took a full provision on that exposure in Q4 FY26.
- **Non-core assets (road SPVs, Indore real estate)** — Legacy toll-road holding companies and a leftover real estate project that management is selling off rather than growing — the Vindhyachal road asset was divested in January 2026 at about INR 799 crores enterprise value (over INR 600 crores net cash), with the Indore inventory being fully monetised by March 2026, freeing capital for the core EPC business.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Kalpataru is a diversified engineering-and-construction contractor whose best qualities are consistency and honesty rather than any single dominant advantage. Sales have compounded at roughly 13% a year with unusually steady year-to-year growth, operating margin sits at about ₹9 on every ₹100 of sales and has moved only around ±1.7 points a year, and the company collects cash from customers about 94 days before it has to pay suppliers — so customers effectively fund the business. Management has been deleveraging (consolidated net debt down 50% to ₹915 crore, debt-to-equity 0.1x), selling non-core assets such as the Vindhyachal road and Indore real estate, and avoiding new large acquisitions. This is also the one place the multibagger screen returns an unqualified verdict: Corporate Culture is a STRONG FIT, because guidance on revenue, margin, working-capital days, order inflows and those divestments was set in advance and delivered, and bad news — the Saudi hits, the Fasttel losses, the water receivables, the road delays — was volunteered rather than extracted. The quality framework agrees, scoring Management and Growth highest at about 1.17 out of 2.

The growth engine and the biggest risks come from the very same source: a bigger, more international, more specialised order book. The book grew from ₹58,415 crore to ₹65,457 crore while the company moved into higher-value niches — high-voltage direct current transmission, tunnel-boring underground metro, Middle East oil and gas, data centres — with Sweden's LMG order book rising from ₹2,000 crore to ₹3,535 crore, Aramco oil-and-gas revenue up 55-100%, and 65% of FY26 transmission inflows coming from abroad. That is what earns Market Share Gainers and Innovation Dominance a LIKELY FIT, though the innovation is capability-building funded by roughly ₹3,000 crore of capex over five years and in-house design teams, not disclosed research spending. The same concentration that produced the growth is the risk: management itself said the oil-and-gas business is 'completely driven by one large client', which is why Stakeholder Concentration sits on WATCH, and the domestic side leans on government and state-utility ordering — PGCIL, PFC, REC, metro, railways and Jal Jeevan Mission water, where receivables already ran into four digits — which is why Government Dependency is the single HIGH RISK. Every revenue line depends on someone else's capital spending, keeping Cyclicality ELEVATED, partly offset by six businesses, roughly two-and-a-half years of order visibility and over 90% hedging on aluminium, zinc and copper. The overseas push also carries a scar: Brazil's Fasttel never delivered, lost money for years and ended in a full provision and wind-down, which is why the acquisition check scores negative (-1) even as geographic expansion scores positive.

What the record does not show is durable pricing power, and this is where the story caps out. Competitive Advantage scores zero and Industry Structure scores negative in the quality framework, and five of the eleven multibagger patterns are outright NO FIT — Pricing Power, Recurring Revenue, Friendly Middleman, Toll Roads and Forward Integrators. Margin gains came from choosing better bids and a richer mix, not from raising prices; about half the order book is fixed-price with commodity and currency risk hedged rather than passed through. Three more patterns land only as PARTIAL: the cost culture is real (8.3% EBITDA margin, return on capital past 21%, finance cost down to 1.8% of sales) but not clearly scale-driven; the brand is a prequalification credential with major clients, not a consumer name commanding a premium; and the barriers — HVDC, tunnel-boring and Aramco-scale credentials, a ₹3,000 crore owned equipment fleet — are genuine but this remains a bid-by-bid business. Hence the two remaining WATCH risks, which are really the mirror image of the discipline praised above: management concedes 8-8.5% margin is the industry norm and double-digit margins existed only pre-COVID, and it walks away from over-competed work rather than matching prices — ceding Railways for multiple years and admitting mistakes in Urban Infrastructure. That protects the margin but forfeits revenue. Netted out, the combined rating is 54 out of 100, a 'Decent' three-star business with SOUND resilience: a well-run, honestly managed, steadily growing contractor rather than a company that can charge what it likes.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

**Overall:** the company partly passes the foundation every multibagger shares (steady cash + high returns on capital + growth), 2 of 3 testable checks passing, and fits 3 of the 11 patterns: Corporate Culture, Innovation Dominance, Market Share Gainers.

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** The three-year record shows promises made and kept: FY25 and FY26 guidance on revenue, PBT margin, sub-100 NWC days, inflows and the Vindhyachal/Indore divestments were all set in advance and delivered, with net debt/equity at 0.1x. Bad news is disclosed promptly and unprompted — Saudi IBN Omairah legacy hits, full provisioning of Brazil, the persistent water receivable — under a stable Mohnot/Patodia team.

> "Today, I'm proud to report that we have fully delivered on each of these promises." — *management, on an earnings call*

### Innovation Dominance — LIKELY FIT
*"Out-inventing the competition"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and every one of the 2 computable checks supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** No R&D spend is ever disclosed; what exists is capability innovation — TBM underground tunnelling, HVDC, MEP-integrated and design-build EPC — funded by ~INR 3,000 cr of capex over five years and expanded in-house design/engineering teams, which management credits for better-margin wins.

> "nearly half of our B&F project portfolio has shifted towards design-build projects" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹66 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ✅ High retained margin (66% of sales) alongside 13% yearly growth — the profitable-innovation flywheel: margins fund the spending that drives the growth.

### Market Share Gainers — LIKELY FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern, but the numbers push back (0 of 1 check supporting) — held one step below a full confirmation.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** A large share of the order book is government/PSU-anchored — PGCIL, PFC and REC tenders, Union Budget capital outlay, metro rail, railways, and Jal Jeevan Mission water — and the policy channel has already inflicted real damage: JJM receivables ran into four-digit crores, depressed Water revenue for two straight years and dragged consol EBITDA in FY25 and FY26, with recovery dependent on state budget releases rather than on execution.

> "our outstanding is in 4-digit crores... we are hoping that, that would improve significantly in February, March" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Genuine diversification away from policy-driven revenue: international T&D was 65% of FY26 T&D inflows, plus private B&F (residential, data centres) and Aramco oil & gas; management has gone cautious on domestic Water and Railways bidding.*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Every revenue line is customer-capex driven — PGCIL/PFC/REC transmission tenders, Aramco oil & gas, metro rail, data centres, residential B&F — and management explicitly frames the T&D outlook as a capex cycle, while input commodities (steel, aluminium, zinc, copper, diesel) drive margin discussion in FY26. Order books swing with client capex programmes, not client operations, which is the dangerous form; the FY24→FY26 run has been the up-leg of that cycle (revenue INR 19,626cr → 27,143cr, consol PBT margin 4.1%→4.9%), so current margins are cycle-elevated by an unknowable amount.
