### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 3 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Unknown on the long view, Unknown on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: As an HFC the business is bound by RBI rules — the principal business criterion is managed tightly against the 60% threshold (61.71% → 60.88% over the year, i.e. steadily narrowing headroom) — and pricing is driven by policy repo transmission, while 9-10% of borrowings are NHB refinance. Revenue is not government-contracted, but the regulator sets the asset-mix constraint and the rate pass-through. (The long view had said: Fortunes are materially set by regulator and policy: the RBI's November 2025 consolidated circular quietly dropped an illustration and forced capital against all undisbursed home-loan/CF tranches, knocking Tier-1 down ~300 bps in one quarter with no clarity on when it reverses; the PBC (50%/60%) rule dictates portfolio mix and constrains where growth can come from; ~9-11% of funding is NHB refinance; and repo transmission on a ~Rs.17,000 crore repo-linked book plus PSU-bank rate aggression drive pricing directly.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Retail customers are granular, but the wholesale side is chunky — LRD is now 22.4% of AUM growing 44%, and management attributed an unusually strong prior-year quarter to just two REIT/large commercial transactions, showing individual counterparties can swing a quarter. Funding concentration is explicitly mitigated by 18 bank relationships. (The long view had said: No single customer, licensor or funder drives results — borrowing relationships span 17-18 banks across money market, bank and NHB channels, the retail book is granular (3.3 lakh banked customers), and BFL-sourced digital leads are a stable, disclosed 12-15% of home-loan disbursals with no distribution overlap; the only lumpiness is occasional marquee LRD/REIT transactions that swing a quarter's disbursements.)

*Unchanged: Good Enough Goods (HIGH RISK); Cyclicality (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

*Not comparable: New Entrants (NOT ASSESSED → WATCH — could not be assessed on the long view); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view); Fashion Risk (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
