**Checks that improved:**

- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Leverage cut from 4.15x to 3.88x then 2.14x post-infusion, excess liquidity deliberately run down, borrowing kept muted, and an explicit refusal to diversify into unrelated businesses. | The long view had said: Prudent leverage and liquidity discipline (3 months of liability repayment held as liquidity, leverage cut to 2.14x, borrowings repaid before growth deployment) and deliberate slowing of MSME when stress appeared, offset by one very large merger.
- **Candor** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: They quantify one-off drags (INR196.95 crores gratuity/Labour Code hit to cost-to-income), disclose NIM compression to 8.19% when it happened, and openly warn ROE will fall to ~13.5% post-dilution while refusing to over-promise credit cost gains. | The long view had said: Generally candid — owns the gold-loan slowdown causes, admits caution and past error in MSME, and acknowledges conservatism in NIM guidance — but occasionally deflects rather than engages on uncomfortable questions.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: The headline Q1 FY27 PAT jump of 59.79% is substantially a funding-cost and capital-structure effect rather than operating skill, and management itself pre-disclosed the ROE dilution, so results can be separated from ability. | The long view had said: Strong headline results owe materially to exogenous tailwinds — a CV upcycle, falling repo/funding costs (8.96% to 8.56%) and a large equity infusion lifting NIM — making it hard to separate skill from cycle, though credit cost held stable at ~1.66% through the period.
- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Management anchors to Indian credit demand growing ~2.5x GDP (~20%), a genuine secular financialisation trend, and the last-12-month record of 15-16% AUM growth is consistent with it. | The long view had said: End markets — used-vehicle finance, small-ticket MSME, gold and rural credit in an economy growing 6.6–7.8% with rising GST collections and infrastructure capex — are in secular expansion, though cyclically sensitive.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Weak. Now: There is no image- or taste-driven pricing power; the high yield compensates for credit risk (gross Stage 3 at 4.64%) and customers demonstrably shop on rate. | The long view had said: Sustained high margins (NIM 8.6–9.0%) and stated pricing power in its niche indicate real customer stickiness, but that power is eroding as rate cuts are passed through to retain business.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: Competition operates through rate undercutting rather than rational pricing, and the moat against new entrants is cost of funds — which is precisely why a 100bps funding advantage is treated as the core strategic prize. | The long view had said: Real barriers exist in capital, ratings and branch/collection infrastructure, but pricing is competitive rather than rational — the company competes by matching rates and passing on funding-cost declines.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Clear runway rather than dominance — only ~3% share of new-vehicle financing with a stated plan to double it in three years, alongside 15.26% AUM growth ahead of system credit. | The long view had said: Clear runway plus demonstrated capture: new-vehicle disbursement mix rose from ~10% to 16–17% by winning back customers previously lost to rivals, MSME targeted from 15% to 20% of book and gold from 2.5% to 5%.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Growth is entirely within India through the existing 3,225-branch network; no credible new-geography expansion is described, and MUFG's Asian footprint is the partner's, not Shriram's. | The long view had said: Credible domestic expansion of the ex-SCUF products from a 60% South concentration into the larger non-South market on the back of the merged pan-India branch network, though it is a multi-year work-in-progress and there is no international footprint.

*Unchanged: Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Security by obscurity; Asset turn; Convenience benefits; Cyclical market growth; People matter (talent development); Customer types (retail vs corporate); Out of limelight; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Distribution advantage; Mini-monopolies; Assurance benefits; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Distribution to shareholders; Merger and acquisition; Share donators.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 2 could not be compared.

*Foundation test: FAIL on the long view, FAIL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: There is genuine operating-cost discipline — cost-to-income fell from ~29% to ~25% across FY26 into Q1 FY27 while AUM grew ~15% — but the bank variant fails on the funding leg: this is wholesale-funded at ~8.56% cost of liabilities, not cheap deposits, and yields are high rather than low-price. (The long view had said: This is a high-yield, high-credit-cost niche lender, not a low-price scale player: NIM ran 6.91% (Q1 FY23) to 9.04% (Q1 FY27) with credit cost guided around 2% and cost of funds still 8.56%; the cost-to-income improvement in Q4 FY26 came from a flat headcount and lower branding spend, not an articulated cost culture.)

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: NIM expanded from 8.11% (Q1 FY26) to 9.04% (Q1 FY27), but management attributes the gap to falling funding cost and the MUFG equity infusion, and explicitly plans to cut lending rates to stop customers defecting to banks — that is rate competition, not pricing power. (The long view had said: Management claimed pricing power explicitly in Aug 2022 and delivered it through the cycle — NIM expanded from 6.91% to 9.04% across the 18 calls while rate cycles moved both ways, and they state credit cost is priced into lending rates; the only dilution is deliberately passing lower funding costs into new-vehicle loans from FY27.)
- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Conservative framing on guidance, liquidity kept at 3-6 months of repayments with LCR 262-335%, leverage cut to 2.14x, and the post-merger multiproduct promise visibly delivered at 16-17% growth; offsetting this, the CEO changed between the Oct 2025 and Dec 2025 calls with several MD-level changes in FY26, waved off as routine, and no mistake is owned on any call. (The long view had said: Leadership is unusually stable (Revankar, Parag Sharma and Sunder present on calls from FY23 to FY27), guidance is conservative and repeatedly met or beaten (15% AUM guidance held then exceeded; 18% reaffirmed in FY27), the balance sheet is run conservatively (excess provisions of INR6,730 crore over RBI norms in FY23, leverage 3.8x falling to 2.1x post MUFG), and they disclose unflattering items plainly, including the INR196.95 crore labour-code gratuity hit and the two-wheeler DSA accounting change.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 1 worsened, 6 unchanged; 1 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Unknown on the long view, Unknown on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Every call opens with GDP, monsoon, GST collections and OEM unit sales because the book is financing of vehicles, tractors and construction equipment — the borrower's capex asset. The last 12 months show the swing directly: construction equipment degrew 16% in Q4 FY26 and 8.2% for the full year on weak government infra spend, then returned to 8.8% growth in Q1 FY27, while Q1 FY27 flagged a 24%-below-normal monsoon and an IMD cut to 90% of average rainfall as a threat to rural demand. (The long view had said: The book is anchored to commercial-vehicle, tractor and construction-equipment demand, and management opens every call with OEM unit cycles, monsoon and fuel/oil price commentary — CE sales fell 16% in Q4 FY26, tractors swing with rainfall, and FY27 guidance was hedged on a 90%-of-normal monsoon and West Asia oil risk. Bounded because the core asset is used-vehicle lending against operators' running cash flows on fixed-rate contracted EMIs rather than new-capex financing, and AUM/NIM have compounded steadily (8.1–9.0% NIM) through the 2022–2026 span.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
