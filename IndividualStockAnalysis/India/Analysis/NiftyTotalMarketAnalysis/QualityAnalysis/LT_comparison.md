### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: L&T's name clearly carries enough weight to be stretched across adjacencies under the same brand (L&T Energy GreenTech, L&T Semiconductor Technologies, L&T Realty, L&T Vyoma data centres) and to attract global partners (General Atomics, Holtec, ITOCHU, ACWA, TenneT), but the calls contain no consumer-style A&P spend, premium pricing or heritage/consistency narrative. (The long view had said: L&T's institutional reputation is real and monetised through credibility with sovereign and private clients (BBB+ two notches above sovereign, MSCI upgrade BB→BBB→A, ENR top-200 ranking, first Indian corporate ESG bond), and the name is stretched into adjacencies (L&T Vyoma data centres, L&T Semiconductor, L&T Realty) — but there is no consumer A&P spend, premium pricing or distributor leverage evidence.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Evidence is mixed and mostly not product pricing: the one explicit price increase is the Hyderabad Metro fare hike (30% gross, 20% net), while P&M margins moved only 7.6%→8.1% and in Q4 FY26 management said it was seeking client relief for higher logistics and insurance costs rather than repricing at will. (The long view had said: Fixed-price EPC contracting means the calls repeatedly discuss cost headwinds compressing margins (Q1FY23 input escalation, FY26 Hydrocarbon cost overruns, Q4FY26 P&M margin down 50bps) rather than price increases; competitive pricing is explicitly named as the cause of margin loss.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Order inflow growth of 33%, 45% and 17% (a record Rs 1,356 billion in Q3) with the order book up 25%→31%→30% year-on-year plainly outpaces India's ~7% GDP and GCC's ~4% growth, implying share gain, but management never cites a market-share number or names competitors, so the gain is inferred rather than disclosed. (The long view had said: Across the four years covered the order book more than doubles from Rs 3.63 trillion (Jun'22) to Rs 7.40 trillion (Mar'26), with international share rising from 28% to 52% and private-sector domestic share from 17% to 39% — share gained on record inflows while working capital and ROE improved, not bought with balance-sheet quality.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: the calls are silent and no computable check supports the pattern. (The long view had said: Scale and execution efficiency are genuine and repeatedly cited as the margin lever (execution cost savings, operational efficiencies, NWC-to-sales falling from 22.9% in Q1FY23 to 4.1% in Mar'26 lifting asset turns), but there is no articulated cost-obsessed culture, no itemised savings programme, and margins are thin partly because of competitive bidding rather than a hard-to-copy cost advantage.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 2 unchanged; 4 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The calls show L&T buying into rather than defending against technology shifts — green hydrogen/ammonia BOO plants, offshore wind HVDC for TenneT, semiconductor power-module asset acquisitions from Fujitsu, hyperscale data centres (L&T Vyoma), and partnerships with General Atomics and Holtec; no analyst or management commentary treats any L&T platform as becoming legacy. (The long view had said: The energy transition is repricing parts of the portfolio — thermal power ordering was 'subdued' in FY23 and the Power segment shrank — but L&T has repositioned rather than been displaced, adding Green Energy, CarbonLite Solutions, offshore wind, semiconductors and data centres over the period.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: L&T's revenue is almost entirely EPC work tied to customers' capital expenditure — infrastructure, hydrocarbon, minerals and metals capacity expansion — so inflows swing with client capex cycles and oil prices; the near-term Hydrocarbon prospects pipeline collapsed from Rs 5.78 trillion (Jul 2025) to Rs 1.26 trillion (Jan 2026), and management repeatedly frames Middle East demand in terms of oil revenues and prices. Margins in the Projects & Manufacturing portfolio sit in a thin 7.6%-8.1% band, leaving little cushion when the cycle turns. (The long view had said: Revenue is almost entirely capex-linked EPC work — 90%+ of a Rs 7.4 trillion order book sits in Infrastructure and Energy, and management frames demand around government capex budgets, oil-price-driven Middle East spending and commodity/input-cost swings; margins visibly move with the cycle (P&M EBITDA 8.2% in Q1FY23, 7.6% FY25, 9.4% Q4FY26, Energy segment margin falling from 8.2% to 6.5% on cost overruns).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Good Enough Goods (ELEVATED); New Entrants (QUANT FLAG); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL); Fashion Risk (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 11 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
