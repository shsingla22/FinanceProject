# Max Financial Services Ltd. (MFSL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

Max Financial Services Ltd. (MFSL) is an Indian listed holding company whose business is essentially one thing: its life insurance company, Max Life Insurance — renamed Axis Max Life Insurance after Axis Bank took a stake (Axis Bank put in INR 1,612 crore in April 2024, lifting the Axis group's holding in Max Life to 19.02%, on the way to a planned 20%). It sells life insurance policies to Indian retail customers — term/protection cover, guaranteed and participating savings plans, unit-linked (market-linked) plans, annuity/retirement income products — plus group cover sold through lenders and employers. It earns money from the premiums customers pay and from investing that money, with profitability tracked as 'new business margin' on new sales (around 26%-27% guided in FY24, with quarters ranging from ~22% to ~28.6%) and helped by high policy renewal ('persistency') rates. Distribution is entirely India-centred — own agents, direct sales and online, plus bank partners led by Axis Bank and Yes Bank — with an added push at overseas-based Indians via a Dubai representative office and a GIFT City hub; as of Q3 FY26 the board has given in-principle approval to merge Axis Max Life into MFSL itself.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Max Life / Axis Max Life Insurance** — The life insurance operating company that is effectively MFSL's entire business, holding roughly 9.8%-10.4% share of India's private life insurance market and growing new-business sales at around 16%-20% a year, well ahead of the industry.
- **Protection (retail term and health)** — Pure life cover and health-linked riders sold to individuals — about 13%-15% of sales, and the fastest-moving line recently, with retail protection up 99% in Q3 FY26 helped by GST changes that cut customer prices.
- **Non-par savings (guaranteed savings)** — Fixed-return savings and endowment policies where the payout is guaranteed at the outset; roughly 18%-27% of the product mix across the period, with pricing adjusted as interest rates move.
- **Participating (par) savings** — Savings policies where customers share in the insurer's investment surplus via bonuses; a steady 17%-20% of the mix and used to keep the portfolio balanced.
- **ULIP (unit-linked plans)** — Market-linked investment-cum-insurance plans where returns follow chosen funds (including ESG, small- and mid-cap index funds); the single largest slice at about 35%-38% of sales.
- **Annuity / retirement** — Products paying guaranteed income after retirement, sold to both individuals and corporates; small but the fastest-growing line, up 122%-141% in recent quarters and about 10% of the Q3 FY26 mix.
- **Group credit life and group/corporate benefits** — Cover sold in bulk through banks, microfinance lenders and employers — loan-protection cover plus gratuity, superannuation and employee-benefit schemes — growing 45%-62% and supported by 20+ new partner tie-ups.
- **Proprietary channels (agency, direct, online)** — Max Life's own sales engine — an agent force that grew from ~61,000 in FY22 to ~1.42 lakh, plus direct sales staff and a fast-growing online business; described as the cornerstone of growth, compounding ~39% over three years.
- **Bancassurance / partnerships** — Selling policies across bank and broker counters, chiefly Axis Bank (~70% counter share) and Yes Bank (~58%), with newer bank and broking tie-ups now adding about 5% of individual sales.
- **NRI business (Dubai, GIFT City)** — Policies sold to non-resident Indians, contributing roughly 12%-13% of individual new-business premium, served through a Dubai representative office and a newly approved GIFT City office.
- **Max Life Pension Fund Management** — A small pension-fund management arm registered with regulator PFRDA, mentioned in FY23 as awaiting its commencement certificate — complementary to the retirement/annuity push rather than a major earnings source.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Max Financial Services earns an overall 60 out of 100 — "Decent" — from three separate readings that all point at the same engine: 69 for business quality, 64 for how well it fits proven multibagger patterns, and only 38 for risk safety, with resilience judged STRAINED. The engine is distribution. The quality framework gives its top mark to distribution advantage (a 46/54 split between the company's own channels and partners, 70% counter share at Axis Bank and 58% at Yes Bank, number-one counter share in three of the last six banks onboarded) and to market share gains from a roughly 10% base, growing two to three times faster than the private sector and the wider industry across FY24, H1FY26 and 9MFY26. That same trait is what makes the "Market Share Gainers" pattern a likely fit, with share up 61 basis points in FY24, 83 basis points to 10.1% in H1FY26 and 53 basis points in 9MFY26, and what makes "Friendly Middleman" a likely fit too, since almost all sales run through bank counter staff and an adviser force grown from about 61,000 in FY22 to roughly 1.42 lakh. Sitting on top of that is the strongest pattern in the screen, "Recurring Revenue": renewal premium grew 13% in FY24 and 17% in 9M FY26 to INR 15,591 crore, with 13-month persistency at 85% and 25-month persistency at an all-time high of 76%.

The uncomfortable part of the story is that the very strength that drives the patterns also creates the largest risks. Because the business is sold through other people's counters, Stakeholder Concentration is rated ELEVATED — management itself says the majority of sales come from the bank, counter share at Axis is 70%, and open architecture letting rivals onto those same counters slowed bancassurance growth to 8% in Q1FY23. The New Entrants risk is ELEVATED for a related reason: web aggregators and brokers already take 60% of online business, and an analyst flagged a bank chairman publicly targeting a move away from a high-cost distribution model. The company's answer is the "Forward Integrators" pattern — proprietary channels growing 32%, 28% and 52% in successive periods, a 39% three-year growth rate, online at 68%, 150 new branch units, an owned e-commerce site and an app — which is precisely the mitigation the risk review credits, with roughly 21% of policies issued online and number-one search ranking in term insurance. The same tension shows in pricing. "Pricing Power" is only a partial fit because the margin improvement from 19.7% in Q1FY23 to 26.5% in FY24 came from product mix and cost cutting rather than charging more, and management repeatedly passes interest-rate benefits back to customers. The risk review reads that behaviour from the other side and calls it Good Enough Goods (ELEVATED): term and non-par savings are comparison-shopped products where the intermediary picks the winner, and analysts have asked whether non-par pricing is getting too aggressive.

The single highest-rated risk is Government Dependency, and it is where the strengths in management quality do their real work. Regulation set both revenue and margin repeatedly: the IRDAI surrender-value consultation, then a GST input-tax-credit disallowance costing roughly 300 to 350 basis points of new business margin, INR 295 crore of operating expense and a INR 268 crore hit to embedded value. Against that, the framework awards full marks for candor — management quantified the GST damage, the proactive tightening of assumptions that produced a negative variance, and a one-off gratuity provision, all unprompted — and the "Corporate Culture" and "Low Price Plus" patterns record guidance being set and met through the shock, with itemised cost actions and renegotiated vendor and distributor terms recouping about 70 to 80% of the hit on a run-rate basis, and policyholder costs falling from 16.9% of premium to 13.8%. Elsewhere the picture is genuinely good: the product promise is certainty of payout and the company leads on exactly that, with the number-one claims paid ratio three years running at 99.34% to 99.51%, top-ranked persistency, a customer satisfaction score rising from 49 to 58, and "Innovation Dominance" a strong fit with named new products in every year of the calls. Technology, shifting consumer preferences and fashion risk are all rated LOW, and cyclicality only a WATCH, cushioned by the renewal base — though management concedes equity-market swings drive part of the business. The counterweights are structural rather than fixable: this is a capital-and-balance-sheet-heavy life insurer with INR 1.93 lakh crore of assets under management, 201% solvency and repeated capital raises, so every rupee of assets produces only 24 paise of sales, industry structure scores lowest of all the quality areas at 0.17, and the "Toll Roads" pattern gets a flat no-fit because this is a competitive consumer financial product with no captive position.

Cost to Replicate is only a partial fit for the same reason the concentration risk is elevated — the agent force, the accumulated share and the claims record are slow to rebuild, but management's own disclosures show the bancassurance channel is open to rivals.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, Stakeholder Concentration, New Entrants, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Regulation determines both revenue and margin repeatedly across the record: the IRDAI surrender-value consultation (FY24), the GST input-tax-credit disallowance that cost ~300-350 bps of new business margin and INR 295 cr of opex plus a INR 268 cr hit to embedded value, an RBI circular reshaping the banca channel, and the Insurance Act amendment (FDI to 100%, Section 35) that alone enables the proposed MFSL-Axis Max Life amalgamation. Even the GIFT City expansion waits on SEZ/IRDAI/IFSCA approvals.

> "the gross impact on margins is close to around 350 basis points due to GST" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Some ability to absorb policy shocks via distributor renegotiation, cost actions and product tweaks — ~70-80% of the GST hit mitigated on a run-rate basis within two quarters — but the exposure itself is structural*

## What to watch

- Axis Bank counter share slipping from the disclosed 70% — the core of the Stakeholder Concentration risk and the foundation under the Friendly Middleman pattern
- Proprietary and online channels failing to keep compounding at their recent 39% and 68% rates — this is the Forward Integrators pattern and the stated defence against both concentration and New Entrants
- Share gains falling back toward the industry rate after +61, +83 and +53 basis points — the Market Share Gainers pattern and the top-scored 'Gain in market share' check both rest on outgrowing peers two to three times over
- A further regulatory or tax change on the scale of the GST hit (300-350 bps of margin, INR 295 crore of cost) — Government Dependency is the only HIGH RISK channel and drives the STRAINED resilience call
- Renewal premium growth or persistency weakening from 17% and 85% — the Recurring Revenue pattern is the strongest fit in the screen and the main cushion against the Cyclicality watch item
- Non-par pricing turning more aggressive or margins held only by giving more back to customers — the Good Enough Goods risk and the reason Pricing Power is rated only partial
