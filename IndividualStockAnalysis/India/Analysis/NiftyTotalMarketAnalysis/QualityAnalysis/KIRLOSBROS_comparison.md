### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 0 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: The Kirloskar name carries genuine heritage — an old company with four-generation dealer relationships and a Government of India 'Appliance of the Year' recognition in FY26 — but across the four calls there is no A&P spend, no distributor-leverage economics and no premium-plus-share-gain proof, and FY26 domestic revenue actually declined 3%. (The long view had said: The Kirloskar name carries genuine Indian heritage and international recall (SPP, Rodelta), and management cites 'strong brand recall' plus a Government of India 'Appliance of the Year' recognition — but there is no sustained A&P disclosure, no market-share-plus-premium evidence across the 14 calls, and industrial demand is driven by tenders and order boards rather than brand pull.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: New-product cadence is real and named across the window — BEE 5-star/4-star retail pumps, ecolabel series, FGD and strategic-sector pumps, IoT/AR-VR, and canned motor pumps breaking a two-player American hold on retail petroleum — but R&D spend is never disclosed or shown growing, and mid-teens EBITDA margins are not the high-gross-margin engine the pattern describes. (The long view had said: The company self-funds R&D in genuinely hard areas — sodium pumps for the Fast Breeder Reactor, in-house primary heat transport pumps with India-controlled IP, AI/AR/VR service tools, new US product launches — but R&D spend is never quantified or shown growing, and there is no named-product revenue attribution or share-of-industry-R&D framing.)
- **Toll Roads**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Efficiency certification language recurs (BEE 4/5-star, ecolabel, European Minimum Efficiency Index) and pumps are mission-critical to power, oil & gas and marine customers, but the business is largely tender-driven where the company concedes it must be lowest bidder — the opposite of a niche supplier customers pay without negotiating. (The long view had said: Pumps are large capital equipment competitively tendered — orders hinge on government funding release (JJM), EIL approved-vendor lists and tender participation, and revenue is lumpy and project-dependent; nothing suggests a small-share-of-cost, non-negotiated spend.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: FY26 revenue was flat (+1%) with domestic down 3% and EBITDA margin sliding from 15.2% to 13.7%, with management explicitly framing the year as capital-goods-cycle behaviour and an 'overall slowdown'; demand sits largely on customer capex — power stations (coal, nuclear), oil and gas, building and construction, data centres — plus a weather-sensitive agricultural small-pump business hit by an extended monsoon. Offsetting this is a genuinely diversified segment/geography mix, retail small pumps and a growing aftermarket service/spares franchise. (The long view had said: Across the 2022-2026 calls revenue swings with customer capex cycles — oil & gas, mining, marine/defence, power and project pumps — and management itself frames results as 'in line with capital goods industry trends' and calls oil & gas and marine/defence 'lumpy'; the SPP UK history is the cautionary case where a capex-driven segment collapse pushed the unit into loss, and FY26 saw margins fall again on mix and adverse operating leverage.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No single customer, licensor or supplier is named as driving results in any of the four calls; revenue is spread across small pumps, industrial, power, oil & gas, marine & defence, building & construction and five-plus international entities (US, UK, Netherlands, Thailand, South Africa). The one channel dependence — multi-generation dealers — is explicitly managed by protecting dealer financial health rather than pushing inventory onto them. (The long view had said: Concentration bites mainly on the supply side — a single American engine supplier delayed 13-14 large offshore packages in FY25, and Kirloskar Ebara is constrained to EIL's approved vendor list for seals, motors and fabrication — while UK service revenue depends on a handful of named framework relationships (Thames Water excluded, United Utilities newly won) and Indian dispatches depend on a long-standing dealer network.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
