# Tega Inds. (TEGA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Tega Industries makes and sells the wear-parts and equipment that mining companies use to crush, grind and process ore — most importantly "mill liners" (including its DynaPrime range), the replaceable rubber/composite linings that sit inside grinding mills, plus other non-mill consumable products and related services. Its customers are mines, with the focus on copper and gold ore and a smaller amount of iron ore, so demand is tied to global mining activity; management repeatedly points to copper and gold demand growth and to mining investment in Latin America, North America and Africa. Most of the money comes from repeat, replacement-type sales of consumables that wear out and must be changed, sold at roughly 60% gross margin, with cost inflation passed on to customers with a lag of a few quarters; a smaller share comes from one-off equipment contracts. It is Indian-headquartered (Kolkata) but genuinely global, with manufacturing and sales across India, Latin America (a large new plant being built in Chile), Australia and Europe, and in June 2026 it completed the acquisition of Molycop alongside Apollo Funds as a minority partner. Revenue for FY26 was about INR 17,736 million (~5% growth) with EBITDA margins of about 22%, and an order book of about INR 12,060 million at March 2026.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Consumables business segment** — The core and much larger business — mill liners (including the DynaPrime range) and non-mill consumable products that wear out inside ore-grinding and processing circuits, sold as spares and "conversion" orders to copper, gold and iron ore mines; it contributed about 84% of group revenue in FY26 and carries the higher margin (EBITDA around 21% in the periods discussed).
- **Equipment business segment (Tega McNally Minerals)** — Designs and supplies mineral processing plant equipment — feeders, screens, hydrocyclones, spiral classifiers, thickeners and similar — won largely through project contracts; acquired in February 2023, it is the smaller and lumpier part at roughly 16% of FY26 revenue (INR 2,688 million, up 25%), with thinner margins (EBITDA ~13%, PBT ~8%) that only recently turned positive.
- **DynaPrime (within consumables)** — Management's flagship composite mill-liner product line, originally sold mainly in Latin America and then rolled out across geographies through customer trials that convert a mine's mill to Tega liners — it is called out separately as the fastest-growing piece of the consumables segment.
- **Service income** — Fees for servicing/installing at customer sites, mentioned as a distinct contributor that lifted revenue in some quarters, but small relative to product sales.
- **Molycop** — A large mining-consumables business acquired jointly with Apollo Funds and completed on 1 June 2026 after anti-trust clearances in 12 jurisdictions; management calls it a transformational addition that creates a bigger, more diversified global mining solutions platform, and its acquisition costs (INR 775 million) were a one-off drag on FY26 margins.
- **NMDC Donimalai project (within equipment)** — A consortium contract to design and commission a 7 million tonne per annum iron ore screening and beneficiation plant for NMDC, India's largest iron ore miner — total contract INR 872 million crore-scale value of INR 872 crores with Tega McNally's share about INR 120 crores over 26 months, cited as proof the equipment arm can win large orders.

### The story in depth

Tega sells wear parts into mills that grind ore, and the strongest parts of the record all point the same way: the end market is genuinely expanding, and customers value what Tega does beyond the part itself. The framework's two highest marks are structural end-market growth (copper demand up 40% by 2040 needing around 80 new mines and more than $250bn of investment, record gold demand against roughly 1% supply growth, and falling ore grades meaning more tonnes milled per ounce) and assurance benefits — Tega pre-built inventory and rerouted supply through COVID and the Red Sea disruption without a single supply failure, and won a six-year European contract on that proven reliability. That reliability plus the DynaPrime composite liner, which replaced metal liners and grew 157% year-on-year in Q1FY23, is what lifts two multibagger patterns to LIKELY FIT: pricing power, where 13-14% price increases came alongside 30% volume growth and gross margins held at 57-60% through raw material swings; and innovation dominance, funded by those same roughly 60% margins. It is also why the technological-innovation risk reads LOW — the industry's shift from metal to composite liners runs in Tega's favour, with Tega as the one doing the displacing.

The trouble is that several of these same traits are the source of the risks. The premium price and roughly 60% gross margin that prove the pricing-power pattern are exactly what makes 'good enough goods' the single ELEVATED risk: a high-priced consumable sold against a large scale competitor, Metso, invites a cheaper substitute, and analysts have pressed management directly on whether Metso could copy the product. When asked, management gave assurance rather than a concrete reason the product cannot be copied, which is why the cost-to-replicate pattern stops at PARTIAL despite real barriers in multi-year mill trials and a service footprint across all top-40 miners. The same tension shows in growth. The market-share-gainers pattern started strong but stalled — consumables grew 10% in FY24, 11% in FY25 and were flat in FY26 against an industry growing about 4.9% — and management denies losing share rather than showing it was held, which is also why the market-share check scores only 1. That gap between what was promised and what arrived (15% consumables guidance repeated for four years) is what keeps corporate culture at PARTIAL, even though leadership has been stable and disclosure prompt. Meanwhile the repeat-purchase story that would make this a toll road or a recurring-revenue franchise is described consistently but never quantified — no attach rates, no renewal rates — so both patterns stay PARTIAL. Cyclicality sits at WATCH for the same reason results move with copper, gold and iron-ore prices: group EBITDA margin fell from 24% in Q3FY25 to 14% in Q3FY26.

Where the record turns clearly negative is money — how it is tied up and how it is spent. Capital allocation scores worst of all areas, driven by the Molycop acquisition: a business roughly seven times Tega's revenue, growing only about 1-3% at around 12% EBITDA margin against Tega's own 22%, funded with INR1,500cr of new parent debt, $838mn of assumed debt and a INR2,000cr commitment. That broke the test for organic, prudently levered stewardship and dragged both the disciplined-stewards and M&A checks to their lowest score, with the earlier Tega McNally deal already a slow 18-24 month distressed turnaround. Underneath that, roughly 153 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and every ₹100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings generated only ₹6 of operating cash this year. Two smaller exposures compound it: the equipment arm at about 16% of revenue depends on state mining capex such as the NMDC Donimalai order, and individual relationships visibly move the P&L, including one European contract worth about INR10 crores a month. The offsets are real — 84% of revenue comes from a diversified private mining base, order books stood at INR1,206 crores at March 2026 with INR906 crores executable within twelve months, and demand is functional wear-driven consumption rather than taste-driven, which is why fashion risk, shifting preferences and technological displacement all read LOW and overall resilience is SOUND. Netting it out, the rating is 53 out of 100 — 45% weight on business quality at 58, 30% on multibagger fit at 26, 25% on risk safety at 78 — a Decent, three-star business: a strong product in a growing market, held back by a stalled growth trajectory and a balance sheet now carrying a very large, lower-margin acquisition.
