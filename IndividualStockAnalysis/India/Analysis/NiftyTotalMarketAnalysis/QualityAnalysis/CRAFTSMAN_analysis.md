# Craftsman Auto (CRAFTSMAN) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Craftsman Automation is an Indian contract manufacturer of machined metal parts, based in Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu, and listed on the BSE and NSE. It sells engine and vehicle components — machined engine blocks and cylinder heads, aluminium die-cast and machined parts, and alloy wheels — to vehicle and engine makers (commercial trucks, tractors, construction machinery, industrial engines, SUVs and two-wheelers), mostly in India but also exported to South America, Europe and North America. It earns money on 'value addition' — the machining work it does on the metal, with raw material (steel, aluminium) largely passed through to customers under price pass-through arrangements — so profitability depends heavily on running its plants at high capacity utilisation. It operates seven to eight-plus plants located near its customers across India (Coimbatore, Faridabad, Bhiwadi, Shoolagiri, Hosur, Gujarat and others), and runs three reported segments: Automotive Powertrain, Aluminium Products and Industrial & Engineering, with consolidated FY2024 turnover of about ₹4,452 crore and EBIT of about ₹619 crore (roughly 14% EBIT margin).

**The parts of the business:**

- **Automotive - Powertrain** — Machines engine and drivetrain parts (engine blocks, cylinder heads, gearbox-related parts) for commercial vehicles, tractors/farm equipment, construction machinery, industrial engines and some passenger vehicles, including exports of truck engine blocks; it was about ₹1,558 crore of FY2024 revenue and ₹292 crore of EBIT, and management says it is still a core growth business but no longer the one the company depends on. A newer 'stationary engine' line tied to data-centre/AI power demand has a first order book of $100 million, expected to reach that revenue level around FY2029-30.
- **Aluminium Products** — The largest segment (about ₹2,154 crore of FY2024 revenue and ₹322 crore of EBIT), formed in 2022 by merging the auto-aluminium and industrial-aluminium businesses; it die-casts and machines aluminium parts for two-wheelers, cars/SUVs and industrial uses, plus a fast-ramping alloy wheel business (Bhiwadi, Shoolagiri and Hosur plants, exiting March 2026 at a run rate of about 3 million wheels a year against 5.8 million installed capacity). It includes subsidiaries DR Axion (76%-owned, ₹1,246 crore turnover and ₹196 crore EBIT in FY2024) and Sunbeam, which is being restructured out of low-margin casting-only work; management's stated ambition is to take this business to $1 billion.
- **Industrial and Engineering** — The smallest segment (about ₹740 crore of FY2024 revenue and ₹49 crore of EBIT), combining a storage-solutions business selling racking/storage systems to e-commerce and similar customers (about ₹88 crore of the segment's ₹157 crore quarterly revenue in Q1 FY2023) with general engineering work; it is material-intensive so its EBITDA margin looks low, but the ROCE is good, and management describes it as the 'backbone' supporting the powertrain and aluminium businesses.

### The story in depth

The strongest thing about Craftsman Auto is the people running it and the direction they are pushing the business in. Management scored highest of any area in the quality work (1.2), built on candour (score 2) — the company openly owns deferred maintenance during the post-COVID ramp, idle Stage-5 capacity, capex overshooting its own guidance, and Sunbeam still sitting below target margins — and on a genuinely long-horizon, promoter-led style (score 2): land bought in stages over years, stationary-engine capability seeded from 2021 that will only start invoicing in FY27–29, and a foundry at Kothavadi built ahead of demand. That patience shows up in the growth area (0.83) and in the one pattern that comes closest to a clean fit, Innovation Dominance, where product and process development for off-highway and stationary engines was expensed years before revenue and has now converted into a firm $100mn order book aimed at FY29/30. The company is also nearer the bottom than the top of its cycle, with commercial vehicles muted for about three years and tractors only now recovering.

The trouble is that several of these same traits are simultaneously the source of the biggest risks. The refusal to cut capex through downturns — 'we have never cut back on capex' — is what underpins the Cost to Replicate pattern and softens the Cyclicality risk, but it is also why capex is running from ₹580cr in FY24 to roughly ₹1,000–1,100cr in FY26 while returns lag, and why the company sought permission to raise equity from the public to reduce debt. Deep, long-validated relationships with a few large customers create the Toll Roads traits and keep new entrants LOW, yet the same handful of names — Daimler with about 75% of that business from Brazil, DR Axion's tie to Hyundai/Kia, an unnamed SUV maker whose inventory correction moved results — is exactly why Stakeholder Concentration is ELEVATED, and why losing roughly 15% of the Daimler portfolio to Cummins engines matters. Buying share through Sunbeam, DR Axion and Fronberg made the Market Share Gainers pattern possible, and Sunbeam is also the clearest evidence for the Good Enough Goods risk: plain raw casting without machining could not hold its economics at legacy prices, forcing price resets and customer exits.

Where the record is genuinely weak is in what the company can charge and what it earns back. Competitive advantage scored zero. Pricing Power and Brand Strength are both explicit non-fits — pricing is cost-plus pass-through on aluminium and steel with a fixed value-add spread, described as a tailor being paid ₹1,000 for stitching — so the pass-through that cushions commodity swings is the same mechanism that removes any ability to price. Return on capital is negative (-0.33): every ₹100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings produced ₹8 of operating cash this year. Capital allocation scores 0.17, with no buyback or distribution policy anywhere in the record. Only one of eleven multibagger patterns is a likely fit, four are outright non-fits, and four of eight risk channels are ELEVATED, leaving resilience STRAINED. That is how a business with real engineering depth and unusually honest management lands at 45 out of 100 and a Mixed, two-star rating: the growth story is credible and the cash return on it, so far, is not.

- **Research and development — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: No formal R&D line, but sustained expensed product development for off-highway and stationary engines converted into a firm $100mn stationary-engine order book targeted for FY29/30, showing development spend does yield sticky new business.

- **Advertisement and promotion — Neutral** *(from the conference calls)*: As a B2B component maker there is effectively no brand-building A&P; the only promotional activity cited across five years is trade-show participation, which is customer-acquisition rather than durable brand equity.

### Innovation Dominance — LIKELY FIT
*"Out-inventing the competition"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and every one of the 2 computable checks supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** There is regular product/process development — off-highway and stationary-engine programmes with development costs expensed ahead of FY26 revenue (May 2024), backward integration into a hybrid foundry, automation investment — but R&D spend is never disclosed or benchmarked, and gross margins are commodity-linked rather than innovation-funded.

> "we have invested heavily for off-highway products machining, and also now we are also backward integrating... there have been only expenses which has been expensed off because these products are a long gestation for development" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹45 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ✅ High retained margin (45% of sales) alongside 25% yearly growth — the profitable-innovation flywheel: margins fund the spending that drives the growth.

> "we are using a mixture of our own equipment manufacturing for the special purpose machines, and very, very prudently we are adjusting the requirements to still be competitive and get more orders" — *management, on an earnings call*

*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**What the calls show:** Management claims a clear rank in storage (No. 2 in static racking, top-two in automated storage, Feb 2026) and is scaling aluminium toward a stated $1bn ambition, but much of the gain is inorganic (DR Axion, Sunbeam, Fronberg) and Sunbeam share came with unprofitable customers now being exited.

> "we are the second largest player in the country as far as the static racking. And we are among the two largest Indian players... on the automated storage" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Pricing Power — NO FIT
*"Raises prices, keeps the customers"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls contradict the pattern — that grounded reading overrides any supportive numbers (1 of 2 checks had pointed this way).

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Technological Innovation, Stakeholder Concentration, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across all 17 calls results swing with customer capex/production cycles and commodity prices: CV/tractor/off-highway volumes, repeated steep OEM inventory corrections (Q4 FY24), a 'lean year' of muted demand, and margins that management insists must be read against violent aluminium price moves — all while capex ran INR580cr (FY24) to ~INR1,000-1,100cr (FY26) with debt/EBITDA at 2.55.

> "there has been a major inventory correction going into Q4 by our customers, all our leading customers... some of the customers didn't lift material from middle of March" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Countercyclical investment — 'we have never cut back on capex' — plus three segments (powertrain, aluminium, industrial) and a pass-through mechanism on metal prices that protects value-add rather than headline margin*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 9 years with swings of ±21 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 15% and 28% (±4.4 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 14% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 29%).

### Technological Innovation — ELEVATED
