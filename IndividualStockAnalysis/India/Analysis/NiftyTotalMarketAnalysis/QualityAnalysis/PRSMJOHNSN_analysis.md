## The verdict: Strong — 67 out of 100 ★★★★☆

In one breath: the quality framework finds an excellent business today (90/100); no winning pattern is strongly confirmed yet; the risk review found nothing severe. Weighing those together gives 67 out of 100 — strong.

- **Multibagger fit (13/100):** the foundation test partly passed (+10); 1 numbers-only hint (+3) → 13 of 100.

### The story in depth

Prism Johnson comes out of this work as a solid, well-run business rather than an exciting one, and the arithmetic of the rating says exactly that: 45% x 90 for business quality, 30% x 13 for multibagger fit, and 25% x 92 for risk safety gives 67 out of 100, a Strong four-star result. The quality side does almost all the lifting. The company keeps Rs 64 of every Rs 100 of sales after paying for its direct inputs, which the framework reads as pricing power customers are willing to pay for; operating profit is Rs 9 out of every Rs 100 of sales and has moved only about 1.7 points a year; every Rs 1 of assets produces Rs 1.01 of sales; and customers pay in seven days before suppliers have to be paid, so the business is effectively funded by its own customers rather than by borrowed money. Put together, an asset-light balance sheet and negative working capital turn a fairly ordinary Rs 9 margin into a 16% cash return on the capital employed. Capital Allocation, Customer Benefits and Industry Structure all score 2.0; Return on Capital and Growth score 1.0.

The pattern screen tells the same story from the other side, and it is the weakest of the three legs by a wide margin at 13 out of 100. Only one pattern registers even a quantitative signal, Recurring Revenue from products sold upfront, and three others are partial fits: Pricing Power, Brand Strength and Innovation Dominance. The remaining five, including Toll Roads, Market Share Gainers and Low Price Plus, do not fit at all. The partial fits are not coincidental; they are the same facts already counted on the quality side, the Rs 64 kept after input costs and the barely-moving margins, showing up again under different names. The reason none of them is confirmed outright is stated plainly in the records: there is no conference-call judgement available for any of the eleven patterns, so the numbers point somewhere but management's own account is missing to corroborate them. The same gap shows up in the quality scoring, where Competitive Advantage and Management have no score at all. And the single hardest number in the file, sales growth averaging about 2% a year, explains why a business this steady still scores so low on pattern fit: the traits that make it dependable are not the traits that compound.

The risk review is the mirror image, a high safety score of 92 with resilience judged SOUND and only two items raised, Cyclicality and Fashion Risk, each carrying one numeric fingerprint and each explicitly labelled a flag rather than a verdict because the calls say nothing either way. This is where the strengths and the risks touch the same trait. A cyclicality flag would normally argue that earnings swing with the wider economy, yet the quality checks record margins moving only about 1.7 points a year and year-to-year sales growth described as very consistent, so the steadiness that earns points under Profit Margin and Persistence of Growth is also the main evidence against the cyclicality flag being real. The same is true of the negative working capital: customers funding the business is scored as a rare strength, and it is also what would soften a downturn, but it depends on customers continuing to pay early, which is exactly what a genuine cyclical turn would test. Both risk flags and the four unconfirmed patterns fail for the same reason, an absence of management commentary, which means the Strong rating rests almost entirely on the numbers and would move most if that missing evidence ever arrived.
