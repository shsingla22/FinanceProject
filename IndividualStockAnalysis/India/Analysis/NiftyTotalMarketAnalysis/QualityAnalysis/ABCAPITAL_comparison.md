**Checks that improved:**

Now: RBI/IRDAI licensing does deter casual entrants, but the window itself documents irrational pricing and lax underwriting in unsecured lending that forced ABCL to withdraw, and regulatory rule changes repeatedly reset economics. | The long view had said: Licensing is the only real barrier; the FY25 unsecured cycle showed irrational industry underwriting and over-leveraged borrowers that forced ABCL to tighten filters, and commission structures had to be realigned 'in line with most of the industry'.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Neutral → Weak. Now: Growth is overwhelmingly volume/AUM-led (portfolio +24%, disbursements +41% y/y) with only modest pricing support — NIM up just 13bps y/y to 6.12% — though mix shift did drive VNB margin from 7.5% to 14.2%. | The long view had said: Growth is overwhelmingly volume/book-led with NIM essentially flat over the period (6.98% FY24 → 6.28% → 6.06% → 6.12%), partly offset by genuine mix-led margin gains in life insurance (traditional to 70%, riders, VNB margin +380 bps).

Now: Distribution is the strongest structural asset — banca plus proprietary channels, 1,712 branches with co-location driving cross-sell, and it is what is actually delivering the share gains — though large private-bank and NBFC rivals match this reach. | The long view had said: Distribution is the clearest durable advantage — 1,712 branches plus 12 bancassurance tie-ups including Axis, IDFC First, Bank of Maharashtra and PSU/India Post partnerships, a captive ABG ecosystem contributing 17.6% of HFC retail disbursements, and partnership premium growing 26%.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 8 unchanged; 1 could not be compared.

*Foundation test: FAIL on the long view, FAIL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Margins are rising without volume loss — NBFC NIM including fee up 13 bps y-o-y to 6.12% alongside 24% AUM growth, life VNB margin up 380 bps y-o-y to 14.2% and health combined ratio improving to 111% while GWP grew 39% — but this comes from mix, rider attachment and rate cycle rather than any described ability to raise prices above cost inflation; management explicitly repriced only in reaction to regulation. (The long view had said: The trajectory shows price/spread compression, not pricing power: NBFC NIM fell from 6.98% (Q1 FY24) to 6.06–6.12% (FY26), commissions were realigned downward under the surrender guidelines, and VNB margin expansion came from product mix and rider attachment rather than price increases. No call cites an explicit price rise taken without volume loss.)

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Within this window management largely delivered what it guided: the promised HFC operating leverage arrived (RoA 1.46% in FY25 to 1.96% in Q3 FY26), credit cost came in at 1.23% against 1.2-1.3% guidance, and the earlier decision to tighten unsecured underwriting was disclosed as it happened rather than after the fact. Against that, the five-year record shows heavy churn — two MDs out in FY21, CEO and CFO exits FY22-23, chairman changes — so long-tenure stability is not established; leadership has only been settled under Mulye since FY2025. (The long view had said: Promises made in early calls were delivered in later ones — HFC operating leverage and RoA expansion guided from FY25 and delivered (RoA 1.53% Q2 FY25 to 1.96% Q3 FY26), credit cost held inside the guided 1.2–1.3%, and the FY25 tightening of unsecured underwriting was flagged in advance and then shown working. The senior team (Mulye, Bala, Rakesh, Pankaj, Kamlesh, Mayank) is unchanged across all eleven calls. Mild offset: the Q2 FY26 ₹500 cr Stage-3 sale that cut unsecured GS3 from 5.4% to 1.9% flatters the optics, though it was disclosed in detail.)

*Unchanged: Forward Integrators (STRONG FIT); Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

*Not comparable: Toll Roads (NOT ASSESSED → NO FIT — could not be assessed on the long view).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Unknown on the long view, Unknown on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No competitor platform is discussed defensively and no installed base is described as legacy; instead the calls show a steady, small-scale innovation cadence the company owns — ABCD D2C app (7.6mn acquisitions, 26+ products), Udyog Plus B2B MSME platform (~Rs 4,400 crore AUM), new-age scorecards and rule engines — and a deliberate migration away from third-party fintech sourcing to proprietary digital journeys. (The long view had said: Financial services faces fintech/platform pressure and the calls show continuous re-platforming (Finverse, ABCD, Udyog Plus, ABC Stellar, AI copilots, account aggregator at 62%), but this is incremental, value-adding innovation on a franchise whose core — balance sheet, licences, distribution — is not being made legacy by a single new format. No analyst or management discussion of an existential platform shift appears in the excerpts.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The only preference shift visible is the customer migration to digital/self-serve channels, and the calls show a successful pivot into it rather than an eroding demand base — a D2C app live for two years with 7.6mn acquisitions and 26+ products, a B2B MSME platform at ~32% of unsecured business loan AUM, alongside physical branch expansion into tier 3/4 towns. (The long view had said: The demand base is migrating channel-wise (digital onboarding 100%, 83% of renewal collections digital, D2C app as acquisition engine) and product-wise (ULIP down to 30% vs traditional/protection 70%, passives and SIPs displacing lump-sum active). Management is pivoting the portfolio with it rather than defending an eroding format.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Cyclicality (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
