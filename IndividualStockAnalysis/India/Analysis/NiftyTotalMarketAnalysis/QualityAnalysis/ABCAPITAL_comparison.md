### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 0 weakened, 9 unchanged; 1 could not be compared.

*Foundation test: FAIL on the long view, FAIL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Promises made early in the window were delivered later in it: the May-25 guidance of HFC RoA 2.0–2.2% and operating leverage tracked 1.46% → 1.59% → 1.82% → 1.96% with opex-to-book down 51 bps, FY26 credit-cost guidance of 1.2–1.3% came in at 1.23%, and management flagged unsecured stress and its own sourcing recalibration early and repeatedly rather than after the fact. (The long view had said: Bad news is disclosed early and acted on — the FY25 unsecured/personal-loan tightening was flagged, executed and shown working — and several multi-year promises landed (25% NBFC CAGR, credit cost inside 1.5% then 1.2–1.3%, HFC operating leverage and RoA path). Against that, the FY25 promise to 'maintain a VNB margin of about 17%-18%' was missed badly (7.4% H1 FY25, 11.6% H1 FY26, 14.2% Q3 FY26 against a restated >18% goal), and the leadership record shows heavy churn at CEO/MD/CFO level over the five years.)

*Unchanged: Forward Integrators (STRONG FIT); Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

*Not comparable: Toll Roads (NOT ASSESSED → NO FIT — could not be assessed on the long view).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged. Financial resilience: Unknown on the long view, Unknown on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The calls show deliberate broadening rather than dependence: sourcing was shifted away from third-party digital partners toward branches and proprietary journeys, the branch base grew from 1,623 to 1,712 in four quarters, and group-ecosystem reliance on Udyog Plus fell from ~50% to ~38% of disbursements. No single customer, licensor, distributor or funding relationship is named as driving results. (The long view had said: Real but bounded distributor and mandate concentration: life insurance growth is increasingly partnership-led (26% vs 8% proprietary in 9M FY26) with named banks — Axis, IDFC First, Bank of Maharashtra — driving the delta and Axis alone gated by zone access, while in alternatives the single ESIC mandate is roughly Rs 28,000 cr of Rs 32,663 cr PMS/AIF AUM and the ABG ecosystem supplies 17.6% of HFC retail disbursements and 38% of Udyog Plus. Customer-side concentration is negligible.)
- **New Entrants**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Despite operating in segments crowded by fintech lenders and new AMC entrants, management reports share gains everywhere in the window — MF share to 5.66%, SAHI health share up ~200bps to 14.5%, top-3 incremental HFC book growth — with no discounting or margin investment framed as a competitive response; NIM and VNB margin both expanded. (The long view had said: Digital-native lenders and fintech sourcing partners are a real presence — management repeatedly recalibrated sourcing away from digital partners toward proprietary channels in small-ticket unsecured, and the AMC has been fighting a market-share dip against lower-cost formats — but no structurally different competitor is described as resetting industry pricing, and the response has been building ABCL's own new-format channels rather than defensive discounting.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The one preference migration visible in the calls is the customer shift to digital/self-serve financial services, and ABCL is on the right side of it — a D2C app with 25+ products and 7.6m acquisitions in about eighteen months, plus rider-attachment and product-construct changes in life insurance to match what customers now buy. (The long view had said: The channel and product mix is migrating under the franchise — investors shifting from active to passive and ETFs (passive QAAuM Rs 38,600 cr, up 28% YoY; ETF AUM up 40% vs industry 24%) and service moving fully digital (100% digital onboarding, 83% digital renewal collections) — but the underlying demand base for credit, protection and savings is expanding, not eroding, so this is mix shift rather than demand destruction.)

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Cyclicality (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Unknown on the long view (none of the stress checks could be computed.) vs Unknown on the one-year view (none of the stress checks could be computed.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 1 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
