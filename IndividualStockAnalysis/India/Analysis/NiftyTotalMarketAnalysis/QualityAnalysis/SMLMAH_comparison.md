### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 8 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: FY26 volumes grew 17% vs industry 13% with revenue +18% and PAT +31%; cargo share +20bps and passenger +80bps for the year, +170bps in buses in Q4 alone, #3 in ILCV passenger, and the Q4 cargo dip was explained as deliberate order pre-poning into Q3 — with a stated path from ~6% to 10–12% share by FY31. (The long view had said: FY26 volumes grew 17% versus industry 13%, with cargo share +20bps, passenger +80bps for the year and +170bps in Q4 buses, and an explicit path from ~6% to 10-12% combined share by FY31; the Q4 cargo share dip was explained as order phasing rather than lost ground.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Real but modest barriers — a 450-touchpoint service network, homologation-heavy engine and ADAS development, and access to Mahindra's MRV engineering pool — yet management itself concedes SML had "limited capabilities of engineering" standalone and both brands face "network challenges which comes with the Challenger brand", so the advantage is bought scale rather than an un-replicable asset. (The long view had said: Nothing in the call is un-replicable by a funded competitor — the advantages cited (group R&D access, sourcing scale, dealer network expansion) are the standard assets of larger Indian CV incumbents, and management concedes SML lacked engine and ADAS engineering capability on its own.)

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The April 2026 call shows cost-recovery pricing, not pricing power: 1.3% commodity inflation in Q4 (~3.5% annualised) met with a 2–3% price increase from 15 April, and Q4 PAT grew just 2% on 16% revenue growth — margins were taken by input costs rather than defended by price. (The long view had said: A 2-3% price increase was taken from 15th April to recover ~1.3% Q4 commodity inflation, and no demand loss is reported, but this is cost pass-through with a lag rather than pricing above inflation — Q4 PAT grew only 2% on 16% revenue growth.)

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 3 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: This is a commercial-vehicle OEM whose demand management itself ties to GDP, replacement cycles, customer fleet expansion (i.e. customer capex) and diesel prices, with an industry that swung from ~2% growth in H1 FY26 to ~22% in H2 after GST reform; Q4 also took a 1.3% commodity cost hit (~3.5% annualised) from steel, aluminium, copper and polymers, so both revenue and margins move with the cycle. (The long view had said: Management explicitly ties demand to GDP/manufacturing/mining growth, diesel prices, GST-reform-driven swings (H1 flat ~2%, H2 +22%) and customer fleet-expansion/replacement cycles — trucks are their customers' capex, and commodity inflation (steel, aluminium, copper, polymers) hit Q4 margins so PAT grew only 2% on 16% revenue growth.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: SML competes as a sub-scale challenger at ~6-7% share against entrenched incumbents in a segment where product differentiation rests on TCO and service reach, and management concedes network gaps are a barrier to even entering the customer's consideration set — a position where cheaper 'good enough' rivals with better reach can hold price; the whole synergy programme is framed around cost efficiency and value engineering rather than premium defence. (The long view had said: Buyers here are professional fleet and institutional operators purchasing on total cost of ownership and uptime rather than brand image, and the company demonstrated pricing power by pushing through 2-3% increases in April to recover input inflation, with management explicitly competing on TCO/uptime leadership rather than defending a brand premium.)
- **Fashion Risk**: NO SIGNAL → LOW. Why now: Demand is functional and economics-driven — buyers choose on total cost of ownership, uptime, power-to-weight and after-sales reach, with replacement cycles and fleet expansion as the stated drivers; nothing in the call attributes growth to a trend, brand cachet or celebrity effect. (The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and all 2 computed fingerprints are clean.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (NO SIGNAL); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Technological Innovation (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
