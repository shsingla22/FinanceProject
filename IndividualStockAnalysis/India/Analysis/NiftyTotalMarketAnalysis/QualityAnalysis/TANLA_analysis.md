# Tanla Platforms (TANLA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Tanla Platforms is an India-based CPaaS (communications platform as a service) company: it acts as the middleman that carries business messages — SMS, WhatsApp and other OTT messages, RCS, plus small amounts of email and voice — from large enterprises to those enterprises' customers, such as bank transaction alerts and UPI notifications. Its customers are enterprises (banks and the top names in most verticals, per management) and, on the platform side, telecom operators like Vodafone Idea and Indosat; it earns money mainly per message delivered, buying delivery capacity from telcos and Meta and reselling it to enterprises, plus higher-margin licence/usage fees on its own software platforms. The bulk of revenue is India domestic messaging, with an international (ILD) messaging business and international platform deals alongside it. Management describes the underlying CPaaS industry as growing about 8–10% a year, with Tanla aiming to grow faster by taking share; group EBITDA margin was around 16% in FY26 versus roughly 20% levels discussed in FY23, and the company holds about ₹1,000 crore of cash.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Enterprise / Digital Platforms messaging (the Karix platform)** — The core, largest business — bulk A2P messaging for enterprises, historically almost entirely SMS (management said in FY24 that everything other than OTT is essentially SMS, apart from a very small email and voice component); it is volume-driven, competitively priced and lower-margin, and management says it is growing by winning net-new customers and increasing wallet share with existing ones, with a new client typically taking three to four quarters to fully ramp.
- **OTT messaging (WhatsApp via Meta, plus RCS)** — Delivering business messages over WhatsApp and other over-the-top channels rather than SMS — it scaled fast, from about 4% of consolidated revenue in FY23 to 12.5% in FY24 (about 18% in the March 2024 quarter), and Tanla says it is Meta's 'partner of the year' and the largest player in RCS, which it offers as a much cheaper alternative channel when Meta raises WhatsApp prices.
- **International / ILD messaging** — Messages sent to and from outside India; it went through a period of volume decline as traffic shifted to OTT (management said it had 'bottomed out' by Q4 FY24), and by FY26 management was deliberately steering the mix toward more profitable international routes to lift margins.
- **Platform business — Wisely (AI/ML firewall, Wisely ATP, Wisely Insights)** — Tanla's own licensed software sold to telecom operators and enterprises — an AI/ML-based messaging firewall (deployed with Vodafone Idea, and with Indosat internationally), Wisely ATP (an anti-phishing platform, signed with three banks including Bandhan Bank by April 2026), and Wisely Insights; it is much smaller than messaging but strategically central because it runs at 95%-plus gross margins, though sales cycles are long and growth has slowed versus a few years ago.

### The story in depth

Tanla runs an asset-light business that turns capital into cash efficiently: every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.18 of sales, the platform products carry 98.2% gross margins, capital spending is only about ₹100–150cr a year on a multi-thousand-crore revenue base, and ₹100 of capital employed generated ₹23 of operating cash this year. The balance sheet is debt-free with roughly ₹1,000cr of cash, and the company has paid a substantial dividend and bought back stock while the shares were heavily marked down. It is also clearly taking share — new customers including RBI and Bank of Baroda, more than half the wallet at a large e-commerce client, and visibly weak competitors to take business from, with Infobip close to collapse in India, Sinch underperforming, and Twilio's ValueFirst already bought and absorbed. That combination is why the quality framework scores competitive advantage at the top of its range and rates industry structure and return on capital well, and why the risk review calls overall resilience sound.

The trouble is that none of these strengths turns into the kind of durable pricing advantage the multibagger screen looks for. Not one of the eleven patterns is a clean fit: six are partial and five do not fit at all, and the same single fact explains most of the misses — roughly 75–80% of revenue is enterprise messaging, a commoditised product sold through competitive tenders. So 'Pricing Power' comes out as the pattern's exact opposite, with price erosion described year after year and FY26 volume growth that management says may not show up as higher revenue because prices keep falling. 'Toll Roads' fails even though regulators mandate the alerts, because customers still negotiate hard and a rival undercut the company out of a large account. 'Low Price Plus' fails because management refuses to bid low and there is no cost-cutting culture to fall back on, and 'Friendly Middleman' fails because sales are won directly in bids with no channel of recommenders. The parts that could break this pattern — the ₹395cr Digital Platforms segment, the telco firewall work that is genuinely hard to replicate, the Meta and Google 'Partner of the Year' standing and the exclusive Truecaller tie-up — are real but too small or too thinly disclosed to carry the whole company, which is why 'Recurring Revenue', 'Brand Strength', 'Innovation Dominance', 'Market Share Gainers', 'Corporate Culture' and 'Cost to Replicate' all land at partial rather than pass.

The risk review then names those same traits as the main dangers, which is the clearest link across the three analyses. 'Good Enough Goods' is rated high risk for exactly the reason 'Pricing Power' and 'Toll Roads' failed: a commoditised core, price erosion at every renewal, and EBITDA margin sliding from over 20% in FY22 to about 16% in FY26. 'Technological Innovation' is the second high risk, because the messaging base itself is moving — in-app notifications, international SMS volumes shrinking as traffic went to WhatsApp, Meta and RCS repricing, and analysts now asking about passkeys — and the company's answer to that shift, its innovation cadence, is precisely the strength the screen could only score as partial, since platform revenue grew in single digits. The winning relationships cut both ways too: share gains and marquee partners drive the 'Market Share Gainers' and 'Brand Strength' readings, while the risk review flags the same names as elevated concentration, with the Vodafone Idea firewall exit knocking platform growth from about 35% to 10% in a quarter, Truecaller running on a renewable two-year exclusive, and Meta setting terms on its own. On the spending side, the quality checks mark down both promotion and growth capex — go-to-market investment is visibly squeezing EBITDA without proportionate growth, and steady capex is funding a segment that has decelerated — which is the same margin story from a different angle and helps explain why capital allocation is the weakest quality area. Netting it out, the rating is Mixed at 44 out of 100: 61 for business quality, 25 for multibagger fit, and 38 for risk safety. A financially sound, share-gaining operator whose main product is losing price, with a high-margin platform business that has not yet grown large enough to change the picture.
