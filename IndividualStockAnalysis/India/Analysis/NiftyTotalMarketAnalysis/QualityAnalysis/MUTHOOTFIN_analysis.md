# Muthoot Finance Ltd. (MUTHOOTFIN) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

Muthoot Finance is an Indian non-banking finance company (NBFC) whose main business is lending small amounts of cash to ordinary people against their household gold jewellery, which the customer pledges as collateral and gets back on repayment. It earns money on the interest spread — it borrows from banks and from public issues of secured non-convertible debentures, and lends at higher rates, with very low credit losses (0.03% of AUM in H1 FY25) because loans are capped at 75% loan-to-value at origination and unredeemed gold can be auctioned as a last resort. Customers are a broad mix of salaried people, self-employed professionals, small business owners and traders across tier-1, tier-2 and tier-3 towns; the company deliberately finances only used household ornaments, never coins, bars or bullion. It operates through a branch-heavy network across north, east, west and south India — about 5,000 Muthoot Finance branches plus roughly 1,000 more at subsidiary Muthoot Money, with one small overseas arm in Sri Lanka — and consolidated assets under management grew from about INR 65,085 crores in Q3 FY23 to INR 1,81,916 crores by March 2026.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Gold loan business (standalone Muthoot Finance)** — The core business — loans against pledged household gold ornaments made through ~4,855-5,000 branches; it is by far the biggest part, at INR 86,164 crores of gold loans in H1 FY25 rising to about INR 1,39,658 crores by December 2025 and roughly INR 1,64,000 crores standalone by March 2026, with average ticket size around INR 88,000 and about 199 tonnes of gold held as collateral.
- **Non-gold loan portfolio** — Personal loans, home loans, vehicle loans and MSME loans started three to four years before FY23 and mostly offered to existing gold loan customers; management described it as roughly 12-13% of the book in early 2023 with an ambition to reach 15-20% over three to four years, but repeatedly said gold loans remain the first priority.
- **Belstar Microfinance** — A 66.13%-owned RBI-registered microfinance NBFC lending to small borrowers, with AUM of INR 9,625 crores in H1 FY25 that fell to INR 7,911 crores by December 2025 after a rough patch (a INR 160 crore first-half loss in FY26, later partly offset); it has since started opening gold loan branches — 81 in FY26, taking total branches to 1,300 — to diversify.
- **Muthoot Home Finance** — A wholly owned housing finance subsidiary that is small but growing steadily — AUM of INR 2,441 crores in H1 FY25 and INR 3,485 crores by March 2026, with FY26 profit after tax of INR 45 crores and gross NPA of 2.63%.
- **Muthoot Money** — A wholly owned RBI-registered NBFC, acquired in 2018, that originally did vehicle finance but has been running that down and pivoting into gold loans through about 1,000 branches; its gold loan AUM jumped from INR 3,900 crores to INR 9,794 crores in FY26 and profit after tax went from INR 12 crores to INR 338 crores after a INR 1,000 crore capital infusion from the parent.
- **Asia Asset Finance (Sri Lanka)** — A 72.9%-owned listed lending subsidiary in Sri Lanka — the only overseas operation mentioned — with a portfolio of about INR 950-960 crores equivalent and 91 branches, small relative to the group.
- **Muthoot Insurance Brokers** — A registered insurance broking arm that sells insurance rather than lends; a minor contributor, earning INR 23 crores of profit in the nine months to December 2025.
- **Borrowing / funding operations** — Not a customer-facing segment but central to how the company works — it funds its lending with bank borrowings and public issues of secured redeemable non-convertible debentures (for example INR 422 crores raised through its 28th and 29th public issues in Q3 FY23).

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Muthoot Finance lends money against household gold jewellery, and the records agree that what it really sells is trust. It was named India's most trusted financial services brand for the tenth year running, and the quality checks back that up with concrete operating detail rather than slogans: appraisal done by its own trained staff instead of outsourced agents, thirteen layers of branch security, roughly 1,000 auditors, and a stated policy of giving late borrowers time rather than rushing to auction their family gold. That trust is delivered through a physical network rivals cannot copy quickly — about 5,000 Muthoot Finance branches plus roughly 1,000 at Muthoot Money, gold collected from the customer's home by the company's own people, and the highest loan book per branch among peers at INR17.75 crores. Advertising has been sustained and rising, from the FY23 'Put Your Goal To Work' campaign through a deliberately larger budget in FY26. Together these earned the top marks in the framework for competitive advantage, distribution, convenience and assurance, and they are what make Brand Strength and Corporate Culture the two clear STRONG FIT patterns — the latter helped by an unusually settled owner-family board, the same six Muthoot directors and the same CFO on every call from February 2023 to May 2026, and fourteen straight years of dividends capped by a record 300% payout.

The interesting part is how often the same trait shows up as both a strength and a hazard. Muthoot is deliberately the expensive lender — charging up to 24% with blended yields around 20.8% by FY26 — while banks and newer NBFCs advertise gold loans at 9-10%. Management treats that cheap pricing as irrational rather than something to match, and the fact that it raised rates by half to one percentage point and still grew its gold loan book 50% is genuine evidence of pricing headroom. But the same premium is exactly what makes 'Good Enough Goods' a live risk: customers are repeatedly being offered a cheaper version of a fundamentally similar secured loan, and management is put on the defensive about the gap call after call. The Pricing Power pattern only rates PARTIAL for that reason, and because management itself denies running a pricing strategy. The branch-and-appraiser machine tells the same double story. Owning the whole customer interface is what earns the Forward Integrators and Cost to Replicate patterns — roughly 30,000 in-house-trained appraisers and a 1,000-strong audit team covering 5,000 branches — but that same physical intensity is expensive, with operating costs growing about 25%, and it is directly exposed to the regulator, since branch openings needed prior RBI permission for years. Growth carries the same duality: the acceleration from 4-5% in FY23 to 28% in FY25 to 50% in FY26 is what makes Market Share Gainers a real pattern, yet the risk review attributes much of it to the gold price surge itself, with tonnage actually falling quarter-on-quarter — the same cycle that produced the mid-single-digit FY23 slump. And the competitive entry that flattened growth in FY23, when new players priced at 6-9%, has not gone away; it is the single most repeated theme across all four years, and a large peer returned in FY25.

That is why the combined rating lands at 50 out of 100, a 'Decent' three stars, built from 45% × 70 on business quality, 30% × 30 on multibagger fit and 25% × 36 on risk safety. The quality picture is lopsided rather than uniformly good: competitive advantage scores full marks and customer benefits are strong, but growth, management, industry structure and return on capital all score low or zero in the framework's own areas, and capital allocation is only partial — the dividend record is unbroken but there is no sign of buying back shares when they are cheap. On the pattern side, only two of eleven are strong fits, five are partial, and the company is explicitly not a low-cost operator and shows no recurring-revenue model. Three of the eight risk channels — the gold price cycle, regulatory dependency, and new entrants — are rated HIGH, and they are not independent of each other: a regulator that sets the 75% loan-to-value cap, forced practice changes through the September 2024 circular, and controls branch expansion is shaping the same economics that new, cheaper-funded entrants are attacking. Against that, the risks that would normally worry a lender are genuinely low here: the customer base is about 97 lakh accounts with roughly 400,000 added each quarter, so no single counterparty matters, demand is functional and repeat with loans turning over roughly every four months, and customer acceptance of gold loans is broadening rather than fading. The runway is real too — banks hold about INR13 lakh crore of gold loans against roughly INR3 lakh crore for all NBFCs, and 25,000-30,000 tons of household gold sits unpledged. Notably, resilience is marked UNKNOWN in the records, so how this business behaves under genuine stress is not established by the evidence reviewed.

*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*
