### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 7 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 3 weakened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Toll Roads**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: BSE is a regulated Market Infrastructure Institution in a two-player Indian equities/derivatives market whose per-trade charge is a trivial fraction of a participant's cost yet is unavoidable to trade or list; transaction charges rose 87% to Rs. 3,795 crores in FY26 alongside record volumes, with the ecosystem anchored by SEBI regulation, the ICCL clearing corporation and the Core SGF. (The long view had said: Exchange/clearing charges are a fraction of a percent of a trader's or issuer's cost yet are unavoidable for market access, and BSE operates as one of two Market Infrastructure Institutions with pricing, SGF and 'true to label' charge structures anchored by SEBI regulation; transaction charges compounded from Rs. 2,030 crores (FY25) to Rs. 3,795 crores (FY26) as volumes grew. Management frames itself as regulated infrastructure rather than a profit-maximiser.)
- **Brand Strength**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Management leans explicitly on 150-year heritage and the trust the name carries — rebranding Asia Index Private Limited to BSE Index Services precisely because of the brand's pull, celebrating the 150th anniversary, and stretching the name across SME listings, index services, co-location and the new Nivesh Mitra investor app. The Sensex franchise is the brand asset a competitor cannot recreate. (The long view had said: A 150-year heritage no entrant can manufacture, with the Sensex and Bankex franchises repeatedly stretched into adjacencies under the same name — Sensex Next 30, BSE Focused IT/MidCap derivatives, StAR MF and now StAR NPS — plus 25 crore registered investor accounts and 16,663 investor awareness programmes in FY26 acting as the trust/distribution spend.)

**That weakened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: There is a visible cadence of new offerings driving revenue — Sensex derivatives expansion into non-expiry-day activity, index products for insurers/MLD issuers/foreign clients, co-location build-out and the Nivesh Mitra app — but R&D or technology spend is never disclosed or quantified, and no share-of-industry-innovation framing is offered, so the funded-innovation flywheel cannot be verified from these calls. (The long view had said: Named new products land every single year across the 16 calls — EGR and the bullion exchange tech (2022), Sensex/Bankex expiry redesign and single-stock derivatives (2024), StAR MF 2.0, Asia Index buyout followed by 50 new indices and two RBI-approved debt indices, StAR NPS (Apr 2026) — funded by high incremental margins, with technology investment quantified (ICCL throughput re-engineered from 3,000 to 27,000 trades/sec per member). It is incremental, repeated and revenue-visible rather than a single breakthrough bet.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: BSE has genuine annuity-like streams — annual listing fees, index licensing (Rs. 2.54 lakh crore of passive AUM tracking its indices as of Sep 2025), enhanced data dissemination and co-location subscriptions — and 'other operating income' grew 59% to Rs. 349 crores in FY26; but the calls never discuss attach rates, renewal rates, deferred revenue or customer advances as a funding source, and ~78% of operating revenue is volume-linked transaction charges rather than contracted recurring fees. (The long view had said: Every call pairs an upfront event fee (book-building/IPO fees, one-time listing) with an annuity behind it — annual listing fees from a 700-SME/255-new-listing installed base, colocation subscriptions, data dissemination and index-licensing fees linked to Rs. 2.7 lakh crore of passive AUM across 85 schemes — and the listing fee has been raised and re-priced over the years (Apr 2022 hike, Jul 2025 throttle framework). Clearing/settlement float (SGF and margin balances) is explicitly a treasury income source, i.e. customer money funding the business.)
- **Pricing Power**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Growth in the last twelve months is volume-led rather than price-led: FY26 index-derivatives average daily premium turnover grew ~118% while transaction charges grew 87%, implying realised yield per unit of turnover fell, and management never cites a price increase, premiumisation or negligible elasticity. Margin expansion came from operating leverage and mix, not from raising prices above cost inflation. (The long view had said: There are repeated, successful price actions that stuck without volume loss — the Apr 2022 listing fee hike, the May 2024 derivatives charge revision that 'significantly contributed' to results, and the Jul 2025 throttle framework that took colocation revenue from Rs. 74 crores to Rs. 171 crores. Against that, StAR MF realisation has structurally deflated (Rs. 3.05 per transaction trending toward Rs. 3 as volumes rise) and derivatives pricing is deliberately set below the competitor, so this is opportunistic repricing rather than durable above-inflation pricing power.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Cost to Replicate (STRONG FIT); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Revenue is entirely volume-linked to market activity, and the last four calls show a boom: revenue +59% to Rs 5,148 cr in FY26 with EBITDA margins expanding from 51% to 64% (39% to 59% in Q3 alone) — classic peak-cycle elevation whose durability cannot be sized, with cash volumes already flat-to-down while derivatives carried the growth. Bounded because transaction fees are an operations-linked flow product rather than customer capex, and revenue still set 13 consecutive quarterly records through a year of Rs 2.5 lakh cr of FII selling. (The long view had said: Revenue is a levered function of market buoyancy: Q1 FY23 saw 'muted' trading, falling treasury MTM and 20% net margin, while FY26 delivered Rs.5,148 crores with 64% EBITDA margin — a swing management attributes to buoyant markets, record IPO mobilisation and FPI/DII flows rather than any structural step-change alone. This is operations-linked flow revenue, not customer capex, but the amplitude across the 16 calls is large and the current point is plainly a cycle peak.)
- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The calls show a steady cadence of small, value-adding technology work — co-location expansion, Common Contract Note implementation, the Nivesh Mitra educational app, index platform build-out — with no defensive discussion of a competing platform technology or of BSE's own systems becoming legacy. (The long view had said: The business runs on a latency and throughput arms race — ICCL re-engineered real-time risk management and scaled from 3,000 to 27,000 trades per second per member, and colocation revenue depends on staying technically competitive — so falling behind on infrastructure would directly cost order flow. But no call shows a disruptive platform threatening the exchange model; innovation discussed is incremental and value-adding.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No single customer, member or licensor is cited as driving results across the four calls; instead management describes a widening base — 25 crore investor accounts with 3.53 crore added in FY26, 10 states above 1 crore each, 255 new listings, and index clients extended to insurers, MLD issuers and foreign clients. (The long view had said: Growth is overwhelmingly driven by one contract family — Sensex/Bankex index options — traded through a small set of large members, and the clearing arm's receivables are concentrated enough that a single Rs.80 crore NSE outstanding required an ECL provision in Q4 FY26; the Asia Index licensor dependence on S&P Dow Jones was a genuine single-relationship exposure until BSE bought the stake out in 2024.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Fashion Risk (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
