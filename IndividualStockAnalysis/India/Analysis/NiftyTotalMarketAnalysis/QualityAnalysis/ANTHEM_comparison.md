**Checks that improved:**

- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Good. - **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Good → Excellent. Now: A genuinely multi-technology platform — synthetic chemistry plus fermentation, biotransformation, peptide synthesis and high-potent oncology under one roof — is winning new projects on capability rather than transfers, which is a deep and differentiated edge. | The long view had said: Multi-modality technical breadth (custom synthesis, fermentation, peptides, ADCs with 15-20 payloads, RNAi, enzymatic biotransformation) plus continuous/green chemistry investment, though the leadership claim is largely self-asserted.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: The niches are specialised (biotransformation, fermentation, peptides, high-potent oncology) but the company is a 2025-listed name covered by JP Morgan, Citi, Nomura, HDFC MF and JM Financial on these very calls — it is no longer under the radar of capital. | The long view had said: Far from under-the-radar — a 2025 IPO with calls hosted by Nomura, JP Morgan, Citi, JM Financial and HDFC, in one of the most capital-attracting themes in Indian pharma.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: Headline results are flattered by items outside operating skill — Rs.156 Cr of other income (forex, RoDTEP, treasury) is included inside the Rs.990 Cr EBITDA and the 43.4% margin, and FY26's 31% profit growth sat on only 15–18% revenue growth, so the operating record is good but less spectacular than the reported ratios. | The long view had said: Headline results are flattered by non-operating items — Rs.156 cr of other income (forex/RoDTEP) inside EBITDA, a labour-code exceptional reversal and an abnormally low tax rate — while revenue missed guidance, so profit growth overstates underlying operating skill.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.1 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing appears rational with no discount war — margins expanded 420bps to 43.4% even in a soft-demand destocking year — and the capital, regulatory and qualification hurdles (peptides, high-potent, fermentation blocks) are high, though the calls do not discuss competitor entry directly. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±5.2 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Technical/regulatory barriers are real and no undue pricing pressure is reported, but new capacity and entrants are piling into the same peptide/GLP-1 pools with China competing hard on price.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: The evidence points away from oligopoly — customers actively multi-source and diversify supply chains, and destocking decisions were imposed on Anthem, indicating end customers retain negotiating power. | The long view had said: The CRDMO/peptide space is crowded rather than oligopolistic — aggressive Chinese competition, listed Indian peers building the same peptide capability, and many Indian companies betting on the same GLP-1 opportunity.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Good → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹39 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±2.1 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹39 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±5.2 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: After direct input costs, the company keeps ₹62 of every ₹100 of sales — pricing power customers are happy to pay for. And from the calls: Gross and EBITDA margins are high, but the calls attribute them to backward integration, yield optimisation and operating leverage rather than any image- or taste-based pricing power, and demand proved unsteady through destocking. | The long view had said: After direct input costs, the company keeps ₹62 of every ₹100 of sales — pricing power customers are happy to pay for. And from the calls: Pure B2B with no image- or taste-driven pricing power; the strong margin profile comes from backward integration, yield and cost control rather than intangible brand pull.

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
