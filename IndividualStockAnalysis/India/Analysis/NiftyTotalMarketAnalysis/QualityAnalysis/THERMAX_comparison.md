Of the 34 quality checks, 28 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 3 improved, 7 regressed, 18 unchanged. 3 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them).

**Checks that improved:**

- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Weak → Good. Now: Breadth of technology across supercritical boilers, ZLD/water, air pollution control, hydrogen, coal gasification and data-centre cooling is genuine, though commercialisation lags in several of these. | The long view had said: Management explicitly disclaims proprietary technology, and even in water it concedes the edge is missing and still an aspiration.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: The BOO/pay-per-use onsite utilities model (TOESL, First Energy) embeds Thermax in customer sites on multi-year contracts, creating real switching costs. | The long view had said: Switching costs are weak — Thermax had lost visibility of roughly two-thirds of its own installed base and is only now rebuilding the captive-aftermarket link through H-live.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: Growth is order/volume-led with pricing largely a commodity pass-through fight, though management shows genuine price discipline by walking away from underpriced projects. | The long view had said: Growth is order-volume and pass-through driven, not price/mix led; margins are set by commodity swings and competitive bidding, with management admitting price-taking pressure across cycles.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Services/chemicals and aftermarket exist as recurring revenue but nothing in the calls indicates a monopoly-like hold on the customer. | The long view had said: Near-monopoly in the TOESL biomass niche and a deliberate aftermarket/services build (AMC, spares, digital) growing 20-30% a year, which is the strongest moat-like pocket in the portfolio.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: Real growth capex into green solutions (~Rs 400 crore equity in First Energy/TOESL, ~250 MW on ground by FY26) with a stated 15%+ equity IRR hurdle, but the returns are not yet visible — First Energy is loss-making with a contractor-driven cost overrun in FY26. | The long view had said: Growth capex is real but modest — ~Rs.400cr equity in TOESL/First Energy opex assets at a 15%+ IRR hurdle and FY27 capex of ~Rs.250cr including boiler/cooling capacity lines — yet incremental returns have disappointed (First Energy loss-making, cost overrun down south).
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: International ambition is real (global data-centre boiler bid, TOESL taken international, international order pipeline called robust) but execution has been patchy — both international businesses booked losses in FY23. | The long view had said: Credible international traction — a Rs.450cr Middle East order, a Dangote order, SE Asia/Africa waste-to-energy pipeline and global data-centre boiler prospects — offset by persistently weak European subsidiaries.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: No weak competitors are identified as donating share; if anything rivals bid aggressively enough to take work away from Thermax. | The long view had said: Some share is being donated — China-plus-one shifts and global waste-to-energy leaders that have no manufacturing of their own now partner with Thermax.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Growth is organic, no large M&A appears, low-margin accounts are exited and unprofitable orders declined, with investment continued through a weak margin period. | The long view had said: Consistently organic (explicitly no M&A), hard IRR floors, negative working capital in projects and repeated willingness to walk away from underpriced work — including the largest project in company history.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: New adjacencies (data-centre cooling and boilers, ZLD, green utilities) give runway, but management explicitly has not quantified target share and offers no share-capture data. | The long view had said: Demonstrated share strength in chosen niches (≈40% in biomass/multifuel, 90%+ in TOESL) plus new share capture in data-centre cooling and boilers, though dominance in TOESL limits further runway there.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: Customers buy contracted reliability of utilities, and Thermax stepped in at its own cost when a subcontractor failed rather than let a project slip, which is assurance-type value. | The long view had said: The clearest strength — customers pay a premium for engineering certainty and execution reliability, giving Thermax a 'last look' and a 2-3 paise price premium in solar despite no technology edge.

*Unchanged: Cost of working capital; Profit margin; Research and development; Intangible benefits; Security by obscurity; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Barriers to entry and rationality; Convenience benefits; Return on capital (headline); Cyclical market growth; Halo effect (caution); Customer types (retail vs corporate); Candor; Asset turn; Structural end-market growth; Out of limelight; Distribution advantage; Oligopoly (vs duopoly).*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 0 weakened, 5 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: the only computable check points this way but the calls offer no evidence — numbers alone are only ever a screening hint, never a confirmed fit. (The long view had said: Thermax is repeatedly described as the trusted engineering partner customers give the last look to, supporting ~40% share in biomass/multifuel and 90%+ in on-site energy, and the name has stretched into solar, bio-CNG and data-centre cooling. But there is no heritage/A&P/consumer-brand machinery and the premium is worth only 'may be 2% to 3%'.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Management repeatedly frames investment in new technology areas — hydrogen, water, coal gasification readiness, and by FY26 data-centre cooling and boiler solutions opening a new revenue vertical — but no R&D spend, share of industry R&D, or named-product revenue contribution is ever disclosed, and gross margins are too thin (5–10%) to fund outsized innovation. (The long view had said: There is a genuine, sustained innovation culture — a new-energy division from 2021, home-grown coal gasification worked on 6-7 years, bio-CNG, biomass-to-hydrogen, the H-live IoT platform, solar-plus-wind-plus-storage — but it is not the profitable, self-funding kind: gross margins are thin, R&D spend is never disclosed, and coal gasification still had zero orders in May 2026, three years after being showcased.)
- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Across all four calls management discloses bad news promptly and unprompted — owning the Q1 FY23 margin miss, the FGD write-downs, admitting the chemicals business 'went through hell', flagging solar will stay loss-making for two years, and in May 2026 volunteering the First Energy contractor failure and cost overrun — while framing decisions on return on equity/IRR and refusing loss-making orders; the CFO Rajendran Arunachalam is the same person across the FY23 and FY26 calls, indicating long-tenured leadership. (The long view had said: Bad news is disclosed promptly and owned (FGD losses reserved, chemicals 'went through hell', First Energy cost overrun, the FY26 working-capital build acknowledged when an analyst spotted it), and capital discipline is real — walking away from thin-margin projects and investing for 'the next 20 to 30 years'. Against that, several multi-year promises are undelivered: 10% product margins, coal gasification commercialisation, and the profitability ramp in green solutions.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Cost to Replicate (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 8 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
