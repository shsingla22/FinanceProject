# Maruti Suzuki India Ltd. (MARUTI) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: AUTOMOBILE*

Maruti Suzuki India Limited makes and sells passenger cars — small cars, sedans, SUVs and its first battery electric vehicle — mainly to retail buyers in India, and also ships them abroad to roughly 100 global markets. It earns money by manufacturing vehicles at its own plants (including facilities at Kharkhoda in Haryana and Hansalpur in Gujarat) and selling them through a large dealer network of over 4,600 sales outlets and more than 5,900 service touch points across India. In FY26 it sold 2,422,713 vehicles, generating net sales of over INR 1.74 trillion (up 20.2%) and net profit of over INR 144.4 billion, and it paid a dividend of INR 140 per share. It is the market leader in Indian passenger vehicles and, on its own, accounted for 49% of India's total passenger vehicle exports during the year; it also earns investment income on a large surplus cash portfolio (over 99.5% AAA-rated), which swings with bond yields.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Domestic passenger vehicle sales (India)** — Selling cars to Indian buyers through its dealer network — the bulk of the business at 538,994 units in Q4 FY26 alone, with demand so strong that about 190,000 customer orders were still unserved at year-end.
- **Small cars / entry-level segment (18% GST bracket)** — Affordable small cars and sedans like the Dzire and WagonR, which led the second-half demand rebound after the government's GST cut; about 130,000 of the 190,000 pending orders sat in this segment, and the Dzire was India's top-selling passenger vehicle in FY26.
- **Mid-SUV segment** — Larger, higher-priced SUVs where the newly launched VICTORIS — the fastest mid-SUV to cross 50,000 cumulative sales and Indian Car of the Year — helped the company compete in one of the industry's fastest-growing segments; management has 7 more SUVs planned through the end of the decade.
- **Electric vehicles (eVITARA)** — Its first fully electric SUV, built on a dedicated new platform for India and about 100 export markets, currently small and limited by production capacity, supported by 2,000 company charging points across 1,100 cities and an aim of over 1 lakh charging points by 2030.
- **Exports** — Selling vehicles to overseas markets — a record 447,000 units in FY26 and 137,215 units in Q4 (about $1.24 billion of revenue in the quarter), making it India's leading passenger vehicle exporter with a 49% share of the country's PV exports.
- **Manufacturing capacity expansion** — Building out plants — a second plant at Kharkhoda (Haryana) and a fourth line at Hansalpur (Gujarat), each adding 250,000 vehicles a year this fiscal, with FY27 capex of INR 14,000 crores and a medium-term goal of 4 million units a year.
- **Treasury / invested surplus** — A large portfolio of invested cash (over 99.5% AAA-rated) that produces other income; rising bond yields caused a roughly INR 750 crore mark-to-market hit in Q4 FY26, which pushed net profit down 6.9% year-on-year despite record operating profit.

### The story in depth

Maruti's report card comes out at 63 out of 100 — "Decent", with resilience judged sound — and the reason is that the same handful of traits keep showing up on both sides of the ledger. The strongest thing about the business is its sheer reach and low cost of doing business: over 4,600 sales outlets and 5,900 service points across 1,100 cities, 26.5% of outbound vehicles now moved by rail (up from 5.1% in 2016), and a working capital position where cash comes in 19 days before suppliers have to be paid, so customers effectively fund the company. That combination is what makes the "Low Price Plus" pattern a strong fit — volume leadership at roughly 8.8% operating margins on deliberately lowest-cost sourcing — and it is also what makes the network expensive and slow for anyone else to copy. But being the scale-driven value player is precisely why two other patterns only partly fit: pricing power is qualified rather than proven (higher average selling prices came from a richer mix and smaller discounts, not list-price increases, and management would not commit to passing on commodity inflation), and brand strength stops short of a premium because there is no premium-pricing story to go with the 30 million cars sold and the heritage. The same trait that wins on cost is the one that caps what the company can charge.

The second thread runs from the tax change. GST reform is credited with turning the industry from a 0.4% decline in the first half into a 16.7% rebound in the second, permanently lowering the cost of buying a car and pulling first-time buyers from 42% of the mix to 51% — which is why both "structural end-market growth" and "cyclical market growth" score at the top of the quality framework. That very dependence is the single highest-rated risk in the review: government dependency, with 130,000 of the 190,000 pending orders sitting in the 18% GST bracket, and fuel-economy norms, ethanol blending mandates and flex-fuel policy all still to be settled. Cyclicality is rated elevated for the same reason — a year of "two distinct halves" with Maruti's own volumes swinging 17.9% between them, and margins moving 80 basis points against the company on input, energy and freight costs in the fourth quarter alone. Management's response has been to commit hard into that demand: INR 14,000 crore of capital spending planned for FY27 and roughly half a million units of new capacity added in a single year, decided while first-half volumes were still falling, funded from its own cash rather than borrowing and held in a surplus that is over 99.5% AAA-rated. The quality framework reads that as disciplined, counter-cyclical stewardship, and the pattern screen reads it as a strong corporate-culture fit — record profit of INR 144.4 billion, record INR 140 dividend, stable Suzuki-anchored leadership. It is genuinely both: prudent in how it is paid for, and exposed in what it is betting on, because the demand backing it was created by a policy decision.

The third thread is where the company is weakest, and it explains most of the "partial" verdicts. The market has moved toward SUVs and away from the small cars Maruti historically owned, and management openly says market share needs recovering — which is why "market share gainers" only partly fits despite 12.3% second-half domestic growth, and why the new-entrants risk is flagged: SUV-focused rivals reset the economics of a segment Maruti once dominated, and well-funded newcomers have taken share before. The company is pivoting rather than defending — seven more SUVs planned to the end of the decade, VICTORIS winning Indian Car of the Year and reaching 50,000 units faster than any mid-SUV — but the innovation pattern stays partial because research spending is never disclosed as a separate figure, and the technology risk stays on watch because a large existing petrol-and-diesel fleet and supplier base now face a shift to electric, with the first battery model on a brand-new platform and fuel-economy rules not yet finalised. Two things cushion all of this. Exports are a real diversifier and a genuine strength — an all-time-high 447,000 units, 49% of everything India exports in passenger vehicles, and a battery model designed for around 100 markets — and the customer base is about as spread out as it gets, which keeps fashion risk and the risk of being undercut by a cheaper rival both rated low. The exposure that remains is upstream, in rare-earth metal supply and West Asian energy and shipping routes, which management itself flags as needing contingency plans. Underneath the scores, the low marks on return on capital and industry structure are the quiet reminder that this is a strong operator inside a hard business.

### Low Price Plus — STRONG FIT
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence and the numbers agree (1 of 1 check computable supporting).

**What the calls show:** The classic low-price-plus profile is visible throughout the Apr 2026 call: volume leadership at ~8.8% EBIT margins with an itemised bps cost bridge, explicit lowest-cost sourcing, and a decade-long shift of outbound logistics to rail (5.1% to 26.5%) as a structural cost/carbon saving, all while the product is positioned as inherently more efficient than peers.

> "we are trying to arrange all the commodities at the lowest possible cost, diversify our supply chai n" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹28 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 1.2× a year with 19% returns on capital — thin margins made up on volume, the low-price-winner profile.

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** Stable Suzuki-anchored leadership (Ayukawa to Takeuchi, FY22-FY26) with conservative capital allocation — record INR144.4bn profit, record INR140 dividend, INR14,000cr capex funding a near-unheard-of 0.5mn unit capacity add, and a >99.5% AAA invested surplus — plus prompt disclosure of bad news including the INR750cr mark-to-market hit and the labour-code provision, and delivery of the previously promised Kharkhoda and Hansalpur lines.

> "the hardening of bond yields resulted in mark -to-market impact of approximately INR7.5 billion on our invested surplus" — *management, on an earnings call*

## What to watch

- Whether the 190,000 pending orders convert into deliveries — 130,000 of them sit in the 18% GST bracket, so any change to that tax treatment goes straight at the "government dependency" risk and at the two top-scoring growth checks built on it.
- Whether the roughly 0.5 million units of new capacity and INR 14,000 crore of FY27 spending find demand — the "capex for growth" check assumes they will; the "elevated" cyclicality risk is what happens if the second-half rebound does not hold.
- Whether market share actually recovers in mid-SUVs behind VICTORIS and the seven planned models — management concedes it must, and this is what separates a "partial" from a real fit on the market-share-gainers pattern and settles the new-entrants risk.

- Whether the company begins disclosing research spending and service or maintenance-contract revenue — both the innovation-dominance and recurring-revenue patterns are held back purely by absent disclosure, not by evidence against them.
