# PNC Infratech (PNCINFRA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

PNC Infratech is an Indian construction and infrastructure development company, headquartered in Agra, that builds highways, expressways, bridges, canals, water-supply systems and other public infrastructure almost entirely for government customers such as NHAI, the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways, and state bodies like MPRDC, Lucknow Development Authority and UP State Bridge Corporation. It makes money in two ways: as a plain contractor (EPC), where it is paid to build and books construction revenue against an order book — over Rs 22,000 crore of unexecuted orders as of March 2026 — and as a project developer (fund-based PPP projects on HAM, BOT-Toll and BOT-Annuity terms), where it puts in its own equity, builds the road, and then earns annuity payments or toll collections over a long concession. It regularly sells these completed road assets to infrastructure investors to recycle capital, such as the 12-asset divestment announced in January 2024 that finished with the sale of PNC Challakere to Vertis Infrastructure Trust in March 2026, and an earlier stake sale to Cube Highways. Work is done across Indian states including Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra and Karnataka, with a first step overseas in the form of two road bids submitted to Uzbekistan's Ministry of Transport.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Roads, highways and expressways (EPC)** — Straight construction contracts where the government pays PNC to build highways and expressways, including early-completion bonuses (Rs 37 crore on Purvanchal Expressway Package 6, Rs 56 crore on a Maharashtra project); this is the core business, with highway contracts alone at 62% of the order book in March 2026 and 16 standalone EPC projects worth over Rs 18,000 crore under execution.
- **HAM projects (Hybrid Annuity Model)** — Road projects PNC part-funds itself and is repaid by NHAI in instalments over a concession period — the biggest of its own-money businesses, once 23 projects worth over Rs 30,000 crore (called one of the largest HAM portfolios in the country) and, after the asset sales, 14 projects worth over Rs 17,200 crore with Rs 1,623 crore of equity required, Rs 1,081 crore already put in.
- **BOT-Toll and BOT-Annuity projects** — Older-style concessions where PNC built the road and collects toll or fixed annuity payments for years afterwards; a small residual part of the portfolio, down to 1 BOT-Toll and 2 BOT-Annuity assets by FY26, with the Gwalior-Bhind toll road getting a 2.8-year concession extension because traffic came in below target.
- **Water and irrigation (drinking water, canals)** — Building drinking-water supply systems, largely tied to the government's Jal Jeevan Mission, plus canal and irrigation work; a meaningful second leg that was 39% of the order book in mid-2022 and 18% by mid-2024, booking Rs 289 crore of drinking-water revenue in a single quarter (Q1 FY25), though the calls flag long-outstanding dues from state governments.
- **Coal mining contract** — A mine-related contract that management reports as its own order-book slice, at 15% of the unexecuted order book in December 2025 and 13% in March 2026.
- **Railways, airport and area development** — Newer diversification lines that management reports bundled with water and canal work, together making up around 25%-32% of the order book in FY26.
- **Bridges and flyovers** — Urban and state-level structure work won recently, including a Rs 559 crore bridge project for UP State Bridge Corporation (in joint venture) and a roughly Rs 200 crore four-lane Gomti River flyover for Lucknow Development Authority.
- **Renewable energy and storage** — The newest area, set up in Q3 FY26 through a wholly owned subsidiary, PNC Renewable Energy Private Limited, with an NHPC solar-plus-battery-storage (BESS) project awarded and to be executed through a step-down subsidiary; too new for the calls to give it an order-book share.
- **Asset monetisation and claims recovery** — Not a construction line but a real source of cash and profit in the calls — selling completed road SPVs to investors (Ghaziabad Aligarh to Cube Highways, the 12-asset package ending with PNC Challakere to Vertis) and collecting disputed claims from NHAI, such as Rs 515 crore under the Vivad Se Vishwas II scheme and a Rs 235 crore one-time settlement on the Agra Bypass award.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

**Return on Capital — Neutral (+0.00)** · 3 of 3 checks had evidence

- **Return on capital (headline) — Excellent** *(from the financial statements)*: For every ₹100 of capital in the business (shareholders' funds + borrowings), it generated ₹38 of operating cash this year — a 38% cash return on capital.
