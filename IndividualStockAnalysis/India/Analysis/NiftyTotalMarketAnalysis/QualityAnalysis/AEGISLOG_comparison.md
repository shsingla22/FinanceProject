### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 5 weakened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Promises made in earlier calls were largely delivered within the window (Mangalore 82,000 MT and Pipavav 48,000 MT LPG terminals commissioned, Haldia stake acquisition completed, IPO proceeds used to pay down debt), with a conservative balance sheet (INR5,939 cr cash, gearing capped at 0.6x / 3.5x EBITDA) and a consistent dividend. Against that, several timelines slipped quietly rather than being owned — KGPL was 'Q2 FY26', then Q4, then June 2026, then Q2 FY27, and JNPT liquids moved from end-FY26 to H1 FY27 — and no mistakes are acknowledged on any call. (The long view had said: Chandaria and Moledina front every call from Aug 2022 to Jun 2026, the 25% EPS CAGR guidance given in FY24 was beaten (32% over five years), the FY24-promised Pipavav and Mangalore cryogenic terminals and the ammonia terminal were commissioned as scheduled, and the balance sheet stayed conservative (cash INR150cr to INR5,939cr, gearing capped 0.6x) — the main blemish being repeated soft deferrals on the KGPL pipeline timeline.)
- **Toll Roads**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Port storage and evacuation infrastructure is a small, non-negotiable slice of an energy customer's cost base and is contracted on take-or-pay terms with names like L&T, Hindustan Zinc and HPCL; pipeline and VLGC-jetty connectivity (KGPL, JLPL, Kandla VLGC berth) makes the terminal hard to bypass once tied in. But management never quantifies its share of customer cost, and no certification/standards or oligopoly language appears. (The long view had said: Across all 14 calls Aegis charges throughput/storage tolls (~INR1,100/ton on LPG worth many multiples of that) on cargo that IOC/HPCL/BPCL and chemical majors cannot land without its jetties, tanks and pipeline links — evidenced by 77% liquid segment EBITDA margins, take-or-pay contracts and long-term exclusivity rather than price negotiation.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Volume growth is persistent and record-setting across all four calls — LPG throughput 5.15 mn tons (+14%) and distribution +45% in FY26, all-time-high throughput at Haldia in December, first VLGC at Kandla — and it is being won with rising margins, not bought. But no explicit market-share figures or comparisons against named competitors are given. (The long view had said: Terminal throughput grew 23% (FY24) and 14% (FY26) against national LPG import growth of only 8-10%, with explicit share-capture language at Kandla, entry into the East Coast via the Hindustan Aegis acquisition, and a network built across Mumbai, Kandla, Pipavav, Haldia, Mangalore, Kochi and JNPT.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: the calls are silent and no computable check supports the pattern. (The long view had said: Distribution margin per ton rose from ~INR4,000 to INR7,000 while volumes simultaneously grew 45-71%, and liquid realisations improved on mix — but management attributes this to energy-price volatility and procurement scale rather than deliberate above-inflation price increases, and concedes margins vary with product mix.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: the calls are silent and no computable check supports the pattern. (The long view had said: Consumer-facing brands exist (Aegis Pure Gas for commercial, Chhota Sikander for domestic cylinders, autogas) and get stretched into new geographies, but they appear once in 2022 and essentially disappear from later calls — no A&P spend, heritage story or distributor-leverage discussion.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 1 unchanged; 4 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Revenue is heavily levered to LPG sourcing/distribution volumes and pass-through product prices (FY26 LPG revenue INR7,689cr vs liquids INR644cr), and the company is mid-way through a very large capex build ($1.2bn cumulative, $5bn targeted by 2030, plus a potential INR20,000cr Vadhavan outlay) funded off record earnings — the classic expansionary over-investment set-up; Q4 FY26 also showed throughput exposure to external energy shocks. (The long view had said: Distribution margins — the main earnings driver — nearly doubled from ~INR4,000 to ~INR7,000/ton explicitly because 'energy prices have risen', and management insists this is sustainable, the classic cycle-peak claim; Q4 FY26 throughput was disrupted by the West Asia situation with country-wide LPG supply down 30-50%. This sits alongside a $1.2bn-by-FY27 and $5bn-by-2030 capex programme, i.e. capacity being committed at elevated margins.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Results and forward visibility rest on a handful of named counterparties repeatedly cited across all four calls — HPCL's exclusive Haldia terminaling to 2038, Hindustan Zinc for the entire Pipavav ammonia terminal, one 'large conglomerate' for >0.5mmt/yr of Pipavav petroleum products, L&T for Kandla ammonia, plus Jio-BP and Itochu — so single-relationship renewal risk is material at asset level. (The long view had said: Terminal volumes hinge on a handful of named counterparties: management confirmed Haldia volumes stayed depressed because 'BPCL is still not there', Kandla could not get a throughput agreement for quarters after commissioning, and the newest anchors are each single relationships — an exclusive HPCL terminaling agreement to 2038 at Haldia, one conglomerate for >0.5 mt/yr at Pipavav, Hindustan Zinc for a third of the ammonia terminal. Aegis Vopak also concentrates the liquid business in one JV partner.)
- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Nothing in the four calls is defensive about competing technology platforms or a legacy format; the innovation cadence discussed is incremental and additive — VLGC-compliant jetties, loading arms, rail gantries, pipeline connectivity — and management is positioning into the energy transition rather than being displaced by it. (The long view had said: Storage and evacuation infrastructure is not a high-innovation industry and the company's own changes are small-scale and value-adding (VLGC-compliant jetties, cryogenic tanks, rail gantries, pipeline connectivity), but the installed base is hydrocarbon-specific and competes with an expanding piped/compressed natural gas network that management discusses in cost-defence terms.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW); Good Enough Goods (LOW); New Entrants (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
