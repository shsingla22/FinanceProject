### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 3 weakened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Toll Roads**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Terminaling and evacuation is a small, non-negotiable slice of an energy customer's delivered cost but wholly critical to their operations, and the contracts are structurally toll-like: 15-year take-or-pay deals, an exclusive HPCL agreement to 2038, PNGRB-approved pipeline connections (JLPL, KGPL) and India's first independent ammonia terminal — assets customers connect into rather than switch away from. (The long view had said: Terminalling is a classic toll: throughput realisation of roughly INR1,100/ton on LPG worth many multiples of that, charged to a handful of oil majors (IOC, HPCL, BPCL) who cannot import without a compliant jetty and tankage, with take-or-pay and multi-decade contracts and pipeline (KGPL, Jamnagar-Loni) anchoring; the same asset earns 77% segment EBITDA margins in liquids (Q3 FY26).)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Volume growth is clearly outpacing the market — FY26 LPG throughput 5.15 mn t (+14%), distribution +45%, Q4 distribution +71% 'despite disruptions from the West Asia situation' — and the footprint is expanding to a seventh port plus Vadhavan, but no explicit market-share percentages or named-competitor comparisons are given in these calls. (The long view had said: Volumes have persistently outgrown the market — FY24 logistics throughput +23% and FY26 +14% against national LPG import growth of only 8–10%, with explicit share-capture claims at Kandla and new-coast entry via the Haldia/HALPL acquisition; the gains come with rising, not falling, margins so they are not bought.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No price increases are discussed anywhere in the four calls; margin gains are attributed to volume, operating leverage and product mix (JNPT 'average realization improving due to a better product mix'), which is mix, not pricing power — and the evidence is silent on elasticity or premiumisation. (The long view had said: Distribution margins rose from ~INR4,000/ton to ~INR7,000/ton in FY26 while volumes simultaneously grew 45%, and management claims the level is sustainable — but the driver is energy-price volatility plus procurement efficiency, not brand-led price increases, and liquid realisation swings with product mix and take-or-pay phasing (liquid EBITDA fell 5% in FY26).)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The calls carry no brand, heritage or A&P discussion; even the B2C LPG distribution growth is explained through partners and terminal infrastructure rather than any consumer brand pull. (The long view had said: Consumer-facing brands exist — 'Aegis Pure Gas' for commercial and 'Aegis Chhota Sikander' for domestic cylinders (Aug 2022 call) — and the Aegis name carries weight with global partners (Vopak JV, Itochu 10% stake, L&T MoU), but across 14 calls there is no heritage narrative, no A&P spend, and no distributor-leverage discussion; the business is B2B infrastructure.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Forward Integrators (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Cost to Replicate (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 1 unchanged; 4 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: LPG revenue is a price-pass-through of an imported energy commodity and Q4 throughput was held flat by external supply disruption, while the company is committing to a very large capex programme ($1.2bn now, $5bn by 2030) whose returns depend on sustained volumes; the liquid division's EBITDA fell 5% in FY26 on capacity phasing and the absence of take-or-pay income, showing segment earnings do swing. Demand is nonetheless operations-linked (cooking/industrial LPG throughput, petroleum storage) rather than tied to customers' capex cycles, and long-dated take-or-pay contracts underpin new assets. (The long view had said: Terminalling and distribution are operations-linked flow products with 8–10% structural LPG import growth and take-or-pay/1-year storage contracts, but distribution margin — the main FY26 profit driver — is openly a function of energy prices, jumping from ~₹4,000 to ~₹7,000/ton 'as the energy prices have risen ... because of the uncertainty involved', which is a cycle-peak margin management nonetheless calls sustainable, alongside a $1.2bn-to-$5bn capex build-out into that peak.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Individual named counterparties anchor whole assets — Hindustan Petroleum for Haldia through 2038, Hindustan Zinc for the Pipavav ammonia terminal, an unnamed 'large energy conglomerate' for over 0.5 MMT at Pipavav, L&T for Kandla ammonia, plus partners Vopak, Itochu and Jio BP — so a single relationship's loss or non-renewal would move a terminal's economics materially. Exposure is bounded by the multi-port, multi-product footprint and 45% growth in B2C distribution volumes. (The long view had said: Both ends of the chain are concentrated: throughput depends on a handful of PSU oil companies (the 2022 call shows Haldia volumes structurally impaired because 'BPCL is still not there'), Haldia is 'anchored by an exclusive terminaling agreement with Hindustan Petroleum through 2038', Pipavav liquids rest on one 15-year take-or-pay with a single conglomerate (>0.5 mt/yr) and the ammonia terminal on one take-or-pay with Hindustan Zinc; on supply, the West Asia disruption cut national LPG availability 50% in April 2026 and 30% in May.)
- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls the innovation discussed is incremental and value-adding — LPG loading arms, rail gantries, VLGC-compliant jetties, pipeline connections — with no competing technology platform or format obsolescence raised by management or analysts; management is positioning ahead of the energy transition via ammonia terminals rather than defending a legacy format. (The long view had said: The bulk industrial franchise exists because propane is currently cheaper than natural gas — a fuel-substitution position analysts probe repeatedly ('how is the LPG versus CNG price differential going on currently'), and management defends it on price rather than structural advantage; wider CGD/PNG rollout or electrification would strand LPG-specific cryogenic and bottling infrastructure.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW); Good Enough Goods (LOW); New Entrants (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 8 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
