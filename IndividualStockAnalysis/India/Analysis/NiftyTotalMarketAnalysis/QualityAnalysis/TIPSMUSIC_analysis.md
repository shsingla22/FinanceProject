# Tips Music (TIPSMUSIC) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Tips Music (formerly Tips Industries) is an Indian music label that buys and owns the rights to songs — mainly soundtracks it acquires from film producers, plus non-film singles it commissions — and then licenses that music out for other people to play. Its customers are digital platforms like YouTube, Spotify, JioSaavn, Instagram/Facebook, Moj/ShareChat and Wynk, which pay it a share of advertising and subscription money whenever its songs are streamed or used, plus brands and venues that pay to use the music offline. It makes money by spending cash up front to acquire song rights (roughly 20%-30% of revenue, about INR 90-100 crores in FY27) and then earning royalties on that library for years afterwards, including on decades-old 1990s hits that still stream heavily. It operates in India across Hindi and regional languages (Tamil, Gujarati and others), reported FY26 revenue of INR 375.5 crores with profit of INR 216.6 crores, and returns most of its cash to shareholders through dividends and buybacks.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Digital** — Licensing its songs to streaming and social platforms — YouTube alone is said to be 45%-50% of revenue — which together make up about 70% of total revenue; roughly 10%-15% of platform money comes from paid subscriptions and the rest from advertising.
- **Non-digital (brand publishing, public performance)** — The remaining ~30% of revenue, from licensing music to brands and collecting fees when songs are played in public venues; management said both did well through FY26.
- **Film music (content acquisition)** — Buying soundtrack rights from movie producers, paid in 4-5 instalments around the film's release, often across multiple languages — e.g. a two-movie PS1/PS2 deal covering five languages and about 60 songs; the FY27 slate is around five films including titles from Balaji Telefilms and Tips Films.
- **Non-film music** — Songs the company creates or acquires outside of movies, including recreated versions of old hits; historically the bigger share of releases by count (79 of 97 songs in Q1 FY25), though recent quarters have skewed back toward film songs.
- **Catalogue (music library)** — The back catalogue of older songs, especially the 1990s repertoire, which management calls the main driver of recent growth and which keeps earning on YouTube and Instagram decades later — e.g. a Khuda Gawah song drawing 3 billion Instagram views.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Tips Music comes out of all three reviews as the same kind of business: a very high-return, cash-generating owner of old songs. Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.04 of sales, the entire cost of new content is written off the day a song is released rather than spread over years, EBITDA margins run 65-70%, and every ₹100 of capital in the business threw off ₹74 of operating cash this year. Because almost nothing has to be reinvested to keep the machine running, the company is debt-free with ₹345cr of net cash and has been handing money back — a ₹46.62cr buyback in FY25, a ₹166cr dividend in FY26, and a stated commitment to distribute the whole FY26 profit of ₹217cr. That combination is why Return on Capital (1.33) and Customer Benefits (1.25) are the strongest parts of the quality scorecard, and why the multibagger screen scores highest of the three components at 79, carrying the overall rating to 70 out of 100, or Strong.

The single asset behind all of it is the copyright library — roughly 38,000-42,000 songs acquired since 1988, including the 90s Hindi repertoire. This is the clearest strength in the record and the reason 'Cost to Replicate' is a strong fit: a well-funded newcomer cannot rebuild that catalogue at any price, because the recordings and the era are finite. The same asset explains the 'Recurring Revenue' partial fit — new releases are only 10-15% of revenue, with the back catalogue supplying the rest — and it is the main thing holding down two risks, cushioning hit-rate dependence (Fashion Risk) with breadth rather than any single big bet, and making the business almost immune to economic cycles (Cyclicality: LOW). Alongside it sits a management pattern the screen rates a strong fit: family-controlled, stable leadership across all 17 calls, deliberately conservative accounting, a refusal to join bidding wars, a five-year line-by-line negotiation with YouTube, and a habit of under-promising — guiding 30% and delivering 61%.

The tension in the file is that the strengths and the risks run through the same two facts. First, the moat protects the old catalogue but not the market for new content: analysts flagged deep-pocketed international labels entering India and bidding up acquisition prices, and profit margins have swung by ±30.6 points — which is why Competitive Advantage scores negative (-0.33) and Industry Structure scores zero even while the replication barrier is rated strong. Second, Tips owns the songs but does not set their price. Per-stream realisation is fixed by the platforms (₹0.04-0.10 in India against ₹0.50-0.90 globally), which is exactly why Pricing Power is only a likely fit rather than a strong one, and why Stakeholder Concentration is ELEVATED: YouTube alone is 45-50% of revenue on an industry-pool payout the company does not control, with the Shorts deal renewal still unresolved into Q1 FY27. The same YouTube relationship that gives Tips its 158mn-subscriber direct channel (the 'Forward Integrators' partial fit) is the concentration. Listening is also migrating to short-form video, where monetisation is immaterial: total YouTube views fell 7.8% even as revenue grew. Against that, the structural tailwind is real — paid subscription is still only 10-15% of digital revenue in India versus over 50% globally and is compounding at 40-50%, pointing to a ₹7,000-8,000cr label revenue pool that a mid-sized challenger growing 20-32% against a single-digit industry is well placed to take share in.

## What to watch

- YouTube Shorts renewal terms (Stakeholder Concentration, ELEVATED) — unresolved into Q1 FY27 and sitting on top of the 45-50% of revenue YouTube already supplies.
