**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Poor → Neutral. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Entry barriers are low and pricing is not rational — NFO flows are migrating to index funds and thematic launches, and the flow contest is fought on fund performance and distributor incentives rather than stable economics. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±11.0 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Barriers are low and pricing irrational — successive regulator-mandated TER cuts (a further 5bps from April 2026), telescoping pricing as AUM grows, and an explicit race to 'extremely low-cost' passive products.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Poor → Neutral. Now: Core mutual fund AUM growth decelerated below industry (6% YoY in Q1 FY27 vs industry 15%; equity 10% vs a 21%-growing industry in FY26), so retail share is slipping even as huge low-fee institutional mandates (ESIC, EPFO) were won. | The long view had said: Four straight years of share loss is the single clearest trend — MF AAUM grew 14% YoY vs industry 21% in FY26 and equity 10% vs industry growth, and management's stated goal is still merely to stop the decline.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹64 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹64 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±11.0 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: The industry is top-heavy but not a stable oligopoly for ABSLAMC — pricing is regulated by SEBI's TER caps and the effective gatekeepers (banks, MFDs) hold the negotiating power over which schemes get sold. | The long view had said: Not a protected oligopoly — 40-plus AMCs compete and the end customer/distributor holds the negotiating power, with the AMC forced to hand ~65% of TER to distributors and absorb regulatory fee cuts.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Good. Now: Distribution reach is the firm's most defensible asset — 19,000+ pin codes, growing MFD activation (including MFDs now selling PMS) and bank channel penetration — though it is matched by the other large bank-backed AMCs rather than unmatched. | The long view had said: Distribution reach is broad and steadily widened, but it is matchable and not superior to bank-backed rivals — the company still has to win its way onto bank recommendation lists and is losing flow share despite the footprint.

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Revenue is a fee on market-linked AUM, and every call in the window narrates equity-market swings — Q4 FY26 saw 'a sharp transition from optimism to caution' with FII outflows and corrections, and Q1 FY27 flagged a marginal slowdown in SIP flows; but demand is operations-like recurring savings flow rather than customer capex, with SIPs ~44% of equity AUM providing sticky monthly inflows. (The long view had said: Revenue is a direct function of market-linked AUM and profits swing with mark-to-market on the treasury book — PAT fell to ₹103cr in Q1FY23 on MTM despite flat operating revenue, and Q4FY26 PAT dropped to ₹187cr from ₹228cr for the same reason, with SIP AUM falling ₹87,000cr to ₹76,000cr purely on MTM. This is market-cycle rather than capex-cycle exposure, and the recurring SIP book (40 lakh accounts, ~₹1,100-1,200cr/month) behaves like flow-product demand that cushions drawdowns.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Low-cost passive/index products are the classic good-enough substitute for active management and are visibly taking industry flows, while distributors and bank recommendation lists — not end investors — control fund selection, and SEBI's TER slabs mechanically cut fees as AUM scales; management's response is to join rather than defend a premium (launching passives, index funds now among its inflow drivers). (The long view had said: Index funds and ETFs are the classic good-enough substitute for active management, and the price gap is explicit in management's own numbers — 63-64bps on active equity versus ~8bps on ETF, with management conceding 'the margin could be lower' as passive scales. The intermediary controls the choice exactly as in the Nobel Biocare case: ~65% of TER is paid away to distributors, flows depend on getting onto banks' recommendation lists, and the April 2026 TER cut was absorbed by 'optimizing the commission structure' — repeated fee compression the AMC cannot refuse.)
- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Across the four calls technology appears only as incremental, value-adding cadence — a new investor app, partner app, WhatsApp servicing and a GenAI chatbot — with no defensive discussion of a competing platform, no legacy-format problem, and no analyst pressing on disruption of the AMC model. (The long view had said: The active-to-passive/ETF platform shift is the live disruption question — an analyst explicitly asked how the industry splits between active and passive over 3-5 years, and management answered defensively that 'active will continue to be the dominant asset class' while conceding passive margins are structurally lower (ETF yields ~8bps vs 63-64bps on equity). Exposure is bounded because ABSLAMC is building the new format itself: 52-54 passive products, ₹40,000cr+ passive AUM, ETF growth of 47-68% ahead of industry, a dedicated Head of Passives hired, plus GenAI chatbot and new investor/partner apps.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Two government mandates (EPFO, ESIC) drive essentially all of the headline 42% YoY AUM growth — excluding ESIC, PMS/AIF grew only 15-17% — so a single relationship's renewal now swings reported assets; on the distribution side management repeatedly credits flows to a handful of large bank recommendation lists. (The long view had said: Two government mandates (ESIC, EPFO) now account for the bulk of headline AUM — PMS/AIF jumped from ₹28,650cr to nearly ₹2 lakh crore 'largely supported by ESIC and EPFO', and the EPFO fixed-income mandate is a five-year renewable relationship. Distribution is also gated by a few large banks' recommendation lists ('our product got added to two of the large banks'), though the underlying retail base is broad — 1.11 crore folios, 19,000+ pin codes, MFD/digital/direct channels.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Fashion Risk (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
