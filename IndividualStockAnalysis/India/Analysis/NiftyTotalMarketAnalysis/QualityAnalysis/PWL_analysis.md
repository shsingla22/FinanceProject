# Physicswallah (PWL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Physicswallah (PWL) is an Indian education company that teaches students preparing for competitive entrance exams — JEE, NEET, UPSC and 40+ other exam categories — plus school-age learners from roughly grade 3 upwards. It gives lessons away free on the internet (about 250 YouTube channels, a business it started as a YouTube channel in 2016), which pulls students onto its mobile app where it sells cheap paid live online batches; management calls this a 'zero CAC' model because free content does the marketing. It also runs more than 300 physical 'tech-enabled' coaching centres for students who learn better in a classroom, and is starting to run and manage actual K-12 schools. Money comes almost entirely from course fees paid by students and parents across India — Hindi-belt states, southern states and regional languages — with revenue of INR1,082 crores in the Dec-2025 quarter (up 34% year-on-year) and INR2,980 crores over nine months, split roughly 51% online, 46% offline, 3% other.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Online** — Paid scheduled live classes ('batches') sold through the PW mobile app at very low prices (a year-long JEE/NEET course for about INR4,000, roughly INR10 a day), fed by free YouTube content; it is the largest part at about 51% of revenue in Q3 FY26 and grew 38% year-on-year, with 3.4 million daily active app users spending about 106 minutes a day.
- **Offline learning centres (Vidyapeeth, Pathshala and others)** — More than 300 physical coaching centres started in 2022 for students who get distracted studying online; about 46% of revenue and 26% growth, but only 7% of total learners, and roughly 80% of centre admissions come via the online funnel — a typical centre costs about INR2.5 crores, loses about 6% EBITDA in year one and reaches 13-15% margin by year three, with about 70-75 new centres planned next year at an outlay of INR200 crores.
- **Acquired/partner brands (Sarrthi IAS, Utkarsh Classes, Xylem)** — Regional and category-specific coaching businesses bought or partly bought to add geographies, languages and exam types — Sarrthi IAS in UPSC (40% stake now, 85% by FY28) did INR22 crores revenue and INR15 crores EBITDA in the quarter, Utkarsh Classes covers state government exams and turned to 6% pre-Ind AS EBITDA, and Xylem in Kerala should close the year around INR330-340 crores of revenue versus INR90 crores when acquired.
- **K-12 platform (Curious Junior and school management)** — A newer push into regular schooling — 'Curious Junior', an online tuition alternative for grades 3-10 that management calls the largest online tuition player and which roughly doubled EBITDA and enrolments, plus a school-management company running three schools (Indore, Gurgaon, Varanasi) with 8 more planned and a Tender Heart, Ranchi partnership; INR400 crores of capital has been allocated but it is currently under half a percent of revenue, expected to matter for profit only by FY29-30.
- **New online categories (state boards, vernacular, CLAT/NEET PG/IIT JAM)** — Early-stage exam and language expansions meant to repeat the JEE/NEET playbook — state boards launched June 2025 reached 175,000 paid learners and INR22 crores in nine months, five regional-language batches enrolled 94,000 paid students for INR24 crores, and three newly reported categories (CLAT, NEET PG, IIT JAM) together added INR32 crores.
- **AI initiatives** — Ongoing investment in AI people and products such as Aryabhata, PW Talks and Ask AI; no revenue is attributed to it, and it is described as a cost and efficiency investment with tech/product team costs up about 40% year-on-year.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Physicswallah earns its keep on reach and price. The quality framework gives its best marks to distribution — roughly 250 YouTube channels plus 30 regional ones feeding an app that supplies about 80% of offline admissions, letting the company choose where to put centres using pin-code data rather than guesswork — and to convenience, with 3.4 million daily users spending about 106 minutes a day and 300-plus tech-enabled centres placed where the online audience already sits. That same machine shows up in the multibagger screen as a strong fit on Brand Strength and Market Share Gainers: about 20% share in the early JEE and NEET categories (30-40% in the Hindi heartland), fifth in offline with roughly 7-8% of revenue while growing fastest, and market leadership in UPSC after the Sarrthi deal doubled that business. The end market is expanding on its own — a 6 crore-student test-prep pool growing 13-15% a year and a K-12 market four times larger — and the company still has under about 2% of the total opportunity, with plenty of weak competition to take it from: unorganised local tutors at INR20,000-25,000, slow traditional chains and non-tech local schools. Advertising spend is falling as a share of revenue (9.6% in FY25 heading to a guided 8-8.5% in FY26) at roughly a fifth to a quarter of what rivals spend, because the free YouTube community does the selling. Bolt-on acquisitions have worked — Xylem taken from INR90 crores to roughly INR330-340 crores — and management talks about its business with unusual frankness, volunteering centre-level economics, first-year losses, the ARPU decline and the fact that only about 40% of centres are profitable.

The interesting part is that most of the strengths and most of the risks are the same facts read from two sides. The free-community funnel that makes the low-cost model work — the Low Price Plus and Cost to Replicate patterns rest on it, with a year of live JEE/NEET prep at about INR4,000, roughly INR10 a day, against INR20,000-25,000 for a local teacher — is also a single distribution channel the company does not own, which is why the risk review flags Stakeholder Concentration and notes the brand is still anchored to its founder-teacher. The word-of-mouth brand built on YouTube personalities serving teenagers is what makes Brand Strength a strong fit, and it is also why Fashion Risk is rated elevated: management credits growth to community affinity rather than to anything that locks a student in. Being the cheap, good-enough option protects the company from being undercut, but it leaves almost no premium to defend — Pricing Power is only a likely fit because online revenue per user rose about 8% alongside about 30% enrollment growth while offline fell about 4%, and management openly refuses to charge more, treating falling prices as the mission. The quality framework's harshest mark says the same thing from the industry side: barriers to entry are low and pricing is not rational, with profit margins swinging by 38 percentage points. Low barriers protected the company on the way in and expose it now — the New Entrants channel points to free or near-free AI tutoring, and the Technological Innovation risk is rated high after analysts asked directly how the company competes with Gemini and OpenAI and got a defensive answer about its retrieval layer being more accurate. The Innovation Dominance pattern and the technology risk are, again, one fact: new launches with disclosed revenue and in-house AI models on one side, a fight with much larger AI providers on the other.

Put together, the picture is a genuinely advantaged distributor of education operating in an unattractive industry structure, which is why the combined rating lands at 64 out of 100 — Decent, three stars — from 64 on business quality, 72 on multibagger fit and 54 on risk safety, with overall resilience judged sound. The area scores explain the gap: capital allocation, competitive advantage and customer benefits all score positive, while return on capital and industry structure are negative and growth and management are only mildly positive. Growth has averaged about 102% a year but arrives lumpily, so persistence scores poorly even though the direction is right. The screen reflects the same unevenness — three patterns fit strongly, four are likely, three are only partial and two do not apply at all. The partial ones are informative: recurring revenue is real in cash terms, since year-long fees are collected upfront with Q4 the biggest collection quarter and revenue recognised as batches run, but it is not contractual; corporate culture gets credit for the founder's long horizon, roughly INR5,000 crores of net cash, an admitted mistake on the marketing cycle and a repeated promise to split online and offline profitability from 1 April 2026, but there is only a short public record to judge against. Most risks sit at watch or low — there is no government-set pricing, demand is annual household spending rather than anything tied to a customer's capital cycle, and the shift from local tuition to app-based learning runs in the company's favour, with state boards at 175,000 paid learners and vernacular at 94,000 showing it capturing that shift inside its own portfolio. What is being paid for here is scale and reach in a growing market; what is being accepted is thin pricing protection in a business anyone can enter.

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; the numeric fingerprints could not be computed, so the call evidence decides alone.

**What the calls show:** Analysts on the Feb 2026 call directly asked how PW can outperform Gemini and OpenAI in education, and management's answer was defensive — framing Gemini's entry into mock testing as 'validation' and claiming its RAG layer beats frontier models on accuracy. ## What to watch

- Whether the promised split of online and offline profitability from 1 April 2026 actually arrives, and what it shows — the Corporate Culture pattern is only a partial fit largely because this promise is still unproven, and the Candor check rests on the same disclosure habit.
- The share of centres that are profitable, currently disclosed at about 40%, alongside the Return on Capital area score of -0.33 — the offline build (70 more centres, INR200 crores) is the main use of the INR5,000 crore cash pile.
- Any sign the free YouTube funnel weakens, whether through platform changes, the founder's role, or falling engagement from the 3.4 million daily users at 106 minutes — this single unowned channel is the basis of the Distribution advantage check, the Cost to Replicate pattern and the Stakeholder Concentration risk at once.
- How the answer to free or near-free AI tutoring develops beyond the retrieval-accuracy claim — Technological Innovation is the one risk rated high, and it sits directly against the Innovation Dominance pattern.
- Whether pricing stabilises or keeps sliding — offline ARPU already fell about 4%, management refuses to monetise the headroom, and the Barriers to entry and rationality check scores -2 on 38-point margin swings; sustained price competition would undercut both the Pricing Power and Low Price Plus patterns.
