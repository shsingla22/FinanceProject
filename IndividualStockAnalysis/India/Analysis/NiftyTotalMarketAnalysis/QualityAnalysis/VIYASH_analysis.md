# Viyash Scientific (VIYASH) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Viyash Scientific Limited (listed in India as VIYASH, formerly SeQuent Scientific) is a pharmaceutical manufacturer that makes and sells both active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs, the chemical raw material in medicines) and finished-dose formulations, for both animal health and human health. It earns money by selling APIs and intermediates to other drug companies, by selling its own branded/generic animal medicines through its own field sales teams and distributors, by distributing other companies' products in some markets, and increasingly by contract development and manufacturing (CDMO) work for innovator drug companies. It is headquartered in Hyderabad, manufactures at nine USFDA-approved sites plus plants in Spain, Turkey and Brazil, and sells into roughly 150 countries, with key markets in Europe, the US, India, Turkey, Brazil and other emerging markets. The company took its current shape after the 2024-25 merger of SeQuent Scientific (animal health) with Viyash Life Sciences (human APIs and formulations); in Q4 FY26 it reported revenue of about INR 920 crore, split roughly INR 499 crore formulations and INR 384 crore API.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Animal health formulations** — Finished veterinary medicines for farm and companion animals sold in Europe (manufacturing and a direct sales force in Spain, plus Benelux, Sweden and distribution deals elsewhere), Turkey, Brazil and LATAM, and India (field force expanded to about 200 people); management calls all regions strong-growing and it was the original SeQuent business, contributing the bulk of formulation sales.
- **Human health formulations** — Finished human medicines aimed mainly at the US market, where the strategy shifted from mature high-volume generics toward more complex products made with the company's own in-house APIs and manufactured in India; management called FY26 "a great year" for this line and is building a dedicated potent lab for oncology products.
- **Human APIs (and intermediates)** — Bulk drug ingredients sold to other pharma companies — described as the company's "strongest core area" and "the biggest vertical," with a deliberate shift out of commodity intermediates and low-price markets (India, Bangladesh, Pakistan) toward complex, high-potency products for developed markets; APIs were about INR 384 crore of the roughly INR 920 crore Q4 FY26 revenue.
- **Animal health API** — Bulk veterinary ingredients, roughly 70-80% of which go to innovator animal-health companies; it was flat at about INR 350 crore for five years and reached a roughly INR 400 crore run rate in FY26, with management expecting strong growth from new product development and better plant efficiency.
- **CDMO / innovator business** — Contract development and manufacturing plus life-cycle-management work for innovator drug companies, started around FY25 using the group's existing plants and R&D; still small but described as growing well, with management guiding to 30-40% growth in innovator business the following year.

### The story in depth

Viyash Scientific comes out of this review as a competent, hard-working manufacturer rather than a company with a moat you can lean on. The strongest part of the record is what it can do: research is treated as the core of the business, with more than 200 scientists including over 20 doctorates, ten to twelve new products developed a year, roughly 60 commercial products plus 30 NDAs, and a new oncology potent lab. That capability is what earns the two best pattern verdicts — Innovation Dominance and Market Share Gainers both come out as likely fits — and it lines up with the highest-scoring quality area, competitive advantage. It also shows up in the numbers management can point to: gross margin widened by 321 basis points, and the review attributes that mainly to a shift into complex, high-potency and backward-integrated products rather than to charging more. Geography is doing real work too, with EU-approved sites in Spain and Turkey used to file and sell across Europe, a direct sales team added in Benelux and Sweden, and Brazil used as a base into Mexico and South America. The overall grade lands at 55 out of 100 — 'Decent' — built from 59 on business quality, only 26 on multibagger fit, and a comparatively high 84 on risk safety.

The most important thing this report can say is that the same trait keeps producing both a pattern and a risk. The move into complex and high-potency products, two dedicated high-potency plants and roughly 45% of volume products backward-integrated is simultaneously the evidence behind Innovation Dominance and the defence management has built against the Good Enough Goods risk — cheaper challengers attacking a portfolio that is still largely generic API and generic formulations. The low-cost manufacturing culture, plant shutdowns, network optimisation and the Mangalore divestment saving about $1 million a year read as the Low Price Plus pattern, but they are also the answer to the New Entrants risk, where low-cost Indian entrants are described as resetting the economics of the US human-health formulations business. The same tension sinks Pricing Power outright: the August 2022 call shows cost inflation could only be passed on slowly and contractually, and the FY26 calls describe US price competition forcing a change of strategy — so the margin gain came from mix and integration, not from pricing. Even the customer relationships cut both ways. Deep, decade-old innovator ties, nine USFDA-approved sites, EU and CEP approvals, 25 US drug master files and 18 to 24 month requalification cycles are what makes the business costly to replicate and hard to switch away from — and they are exactly why Stakeholder Concentration is flagged, with 65-70% of API sales sitting with large multinational customers and the CDMO franchise running off eight to ten innovators, about 90% of it from existing relationships. Management's own candour is the strongest management-side signal in the record, and it is candour about weakness: the Vizag fire, Turkey hyperinflation in 2022, poor US cost competitiveness, five flat years in animal-health API and missed customer expectations.

What keeps the score at 'Decent' rather than higher is the financial and shareholder side, not the operating story. Return on capital scores zero and capital allocation is negative. About 160 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, a long cycle that ties up capital. There is no dividend, buyback or distribution policy anywhere in the calls — free cash is explicitly earmarked for debt reduction and selective acquisitions. Sales have grown about 20% a year on average but lumpily, which is why persistence of growth scores negative even as the average looks good. Several patterns land only as partial because the supporting evidence is asserted rather than shown: market share is claimed qualitatively and never quantified, brand strength rests on branded-generic positions in Turkey, Brazil and India with no heritage story and no advertising disclosure, and Cost to Replicate is undercut by management's own description of the market as 'so crowded'. Toll Roads and Pricing Power find no fit at all. On the other side, resilience is judged sound, and four of the eight risk channels — cyclicality, technological disruption, government dependency and fashion — come out low, because demand is functional, prescription- and vet-driven, consumed continuously rather than tied to any customer's capital spending, and spread over 150 countries and 60-plus products. The company is emerging from a clear trough of post-COVID destocking, US price competition and five flat years in animal-health API, with the upturn visible in FY26. The demand base itself is shifting from farm animals to pets — management notes pet care has moved from about 40% to well over half of the developed market — and the company is spending to follow it through the Nourrie acquisition in Brazil, the Viyash merger and distributing Boehringer Ingelheim's companion-animal products in India. So the story is a business with genuine capability and a credible pivot, whose returns and shareholder payoff have yet to arrive.
