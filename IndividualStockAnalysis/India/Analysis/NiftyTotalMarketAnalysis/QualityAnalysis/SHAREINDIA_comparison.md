### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 3 weakened, 6 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: MTF interest and newly launched PMS/AIF AUM give annuity-like income streams the company explicitly calls sustainable (MTF book Rs.430cr Q2 FY26 → Rs.457cr Q3 → Rs.470cr Q1 FY27, PMS AUM Rs.150cr at launch), but across all four calls there is no discussion of attach rates, renewal rates, deferred revenue or customer advances funding the business. (The long view had said: Some annuity-like streams have been built and disclosed across the later calls — MTF book (Rs 239cr FY25 → Rs 424cr FY26 → Rs 470cr Q1 FY27), PMS AUM (Rs 100cr → Rs 150cr), uTrade subscriptions (+20% quarter-on-quarter, 5,231 clients) — but there is no attach rate, renewal rate or deferred-revenue/customer-advance discussion, and the bulk of profit still comes from prop/broking volumes.)
- **Brand Strength**: NO FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: No heritage story, no A&P spend, no premium-pricing or distributor-leverage discussion appears in the last-12-month calls; growth is attributed to product diversification and branch openings rather than brand pull. (The long view had said: No heritage, A&P or premium-pricing evidence; management concedes limited marketing reach and slow customer acquisition in Nov 2022 and, by Jul 2026, cannot explain why a 'low PE, high growth' stock is unloved — brand pull is absent from the record.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Strong relative outperformance in a consolidating industry (ADTO up 29% quarter-on-quarter to Rs.9,700cr in Q3 FY26, active clients +1% quarter-on-quarter while industry accounts shrank, Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue +31% and PAT +48% year-on-year) implies share gain, but management never cites an explicit market-share number or names competitors. (The long view had said: Share numbers are quoted (6%+ options, 5-7% commodity, 8-10% currency, 20% ISFC market-making in Nov 2022) and client/institutional counts rise (137→186→212 empanelments), but by Feb 2025 management describes share as merely 'stable' and volumes falling in line with the industry — growth by adding verticals, not by persistent share capture.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Distribution is direct — own branches in tier-3 cities and an in-house sales/retail team selling to end clients — with no prescriber, installer or independent-professional channel, and no trade training programmes mentioned in any of the four calls. (The long view had said: Authorised persons/APs are added as a channel (29 new APs in Q3 FY25) and third-party product distribution is being built, but the stated strategy is the opposite — company-owned Tier-3 branches going direct to the end customer, with no trade-training or channel-lock-in language.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The calls mention 'cost optimization' and lower cost of borrowing in passing but give no itemised savings, no no-frills positioning and no evidence of a hard-to-copy cost culture — the evidence is effectively silent. (The long view had said: There is a real cost-and-capital-efficiency thread — self-clearing instead of PCM, second membership working-capital gains, repeated 'cost optimization', and a claim of being most cost-effective on scale — but management explicitly refuses the discount-broking volume model, so scale-plus-low-price is not the strategy.)

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Cost to Replicate (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No single customer, licensor or counterparty is named as driving results in any of the four calls; revenue is spread across a retail broking base, prop/market-making, MTF, PMS, treasury and merchant banking, and management explicitly positions this breadth as the reason it withstood the regulatory shock. (The long view had said: A material part of demand rests on what retail traders currently find exciting — commodity options in gold/silver/crude are credited to recent volatility, and index option volumes collapsed once expiries were trimmed — so revenue follows trading fads rather than a fixed functional need.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Good Enough Goods (ELEVATED); New Entrants (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
