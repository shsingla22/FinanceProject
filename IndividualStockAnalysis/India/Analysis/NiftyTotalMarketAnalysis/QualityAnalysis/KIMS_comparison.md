### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 8 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Twenty-five years of heritage from the first Nellore hospital, and new units convert reputation into fast ramp (Thane doing 1,000+ surgeries in under six months, INR9 crores of monthly revenue ahead of expectation), but the brand is regional and demonstrably not portable without effort — Nashik's ramp lagged and management concedes the strength is clinical rather than consumer-marketing led. (The long view had said: The KIMS name is clearly portable — Sunshine was rebranded to KIMS within a week of control and management says the brand is building in Maharashtra — plus 20-year heritage and heavy awareness campaigns, but there is no evidence of brand-driven premium pricing; new units still stall until insurers empanel them.)

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Volume-led expansion is real and rapid — revenue +28.2% in FY26, 7 hospitals launched in 2025, 25 centres across 5 states — but management never cites market-share numbers or names competitors, and the share is being bought with heavy capex that cut PAT from INR415 crores to INR242 crores. (The long view had said: Footprint has expanded from ~3,600 beds in two states (2022) to 25 hospitals across 5 states with 7 launches in 2025 and FY26 revenue +28.2%, but no explicit market-share figures are ever cited, and the core Telangana cluster is conceded to be mature at high-single-digit growth against heavy competitor bed addition.)

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The calls affirmatively show pricing holding rather than being competed away by cheaper substitutes: FY26 ARPOB +14% and ARPP +11.4% alongside 15.4% IP volume growth, with new Karnataka units printing INR76,000 ARPOB and management guiding group ARPOB from ~INR42,000 toward INR50,000 — no discussion of defending a price premium or of cheaper alternatives taking share. (The long view had said: An intermediary rather than the patient increasingly sets price: insurer negotiations are explicitly adversarial on rates and gate access to volume, and tier-2/3 assets structurally earn far lower ARPOB (~INR17,000 versus INR70,000+ in Telangana), showing a cheaper-good-enough tier of care exists in the same catchments. The premium is defended by acuity rather than by brand pricing power.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Healthcare demand is flow-type and non-discretionary (IP volumes +15.4%, OP +25.4% in FY26), so there is no commodity or customer-capex cycle; but KIMS's own expansion cycle is producing exactly the over-investment margin damage the channel describes — FY26 EBITDA margin fell from 26.6% to 21.1% and PAT from INR415cr to INR242cr while revenue grew 28.2%, with nine units opened in one year and Q4 consolidated pre-Ind AS EBITDA down 33.5% year-on-year. (The long view had said: Demand is operations-linked and non-discretionary across 17 calls — emergency, transplant, oncology, cardiac and mother-and-child volumes, not customer capex; mature-cluster EBITDA has held near 28-30% throughout, and the only margin swings (26.6% FY25 to 21.1% FY26) come from KIMS's own greenfield drag, not from a demand or price cycle. Management kept investing through the 2022 post-COVID lull and through the FY26 profit dip rather than scaling back.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
