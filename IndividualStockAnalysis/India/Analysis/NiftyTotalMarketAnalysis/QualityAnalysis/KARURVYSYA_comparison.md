**Checks that improved:**

- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Weak → Good. Now: The bank appears past the trough of the rate-cut margin cycle, with deposit repricing tailwinds turning NIM from a 9bps decline in Q2 to expansion of 22bps and then to 4.26%, and corporate disbursements at a five-quarter high. | The long view had said: The bank is at a benign cyclical peak, not a trough — GNPA 0.74%, credit cost 0.33%, with management flagging geopolitical risk and taking a one-time INR163cr prudential provision, implying credit costs can only normalise upward.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Systematic capacity building is visible — a ~100-strong relationship manager cadre for the small business group with 72 already in place, plus branch sales and service executives and a dedicated CASA acquisition team. | The long view had said: Leadership depth has been built largely by lateral hiring rather than internal pipeline (Consumer Banking head resigned and was replaced externally; national sales managers recruited from market), though one internal elevation to Executive Director occurred and analysts have explicitly flagged key-man risk.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Volume drives the balance sheet, but earnings growth this year came from deliberate mix engineering — fixed-rate book raised 15%→23%, MCLR cut 29%→20%, credit substitutes and SDL preference — lifting NIM from 3.77% to 4.26% through a 125bps repo cut. | The long view had said: Mix shift genuinely drove profitability (corporate 45%→14%, RAM at 86%, fixed-rate book 23%→34% preserving NIM through 125bps of repo cuts), but pricing power is absent and management is now conceding price to retain relationships.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: The assessment is reasonably robust because management itself separates windfalls from operating skill — restating a 26% sequential operating profit jump as 14% once the INR139 crore recovery is excluded. | The long view had said: Record profits are genuinely skill-driven in part (mix shift, underwriting), but are also flattered by an exceptionally benign credit cycle and lumpy one-offs (write-off recoveries, tax-refund interest, SR recoveries) that management itself strips out.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Weak. Now: Growth is entirely within its existing domestic footprint and management explicitly flags the absence of any overseas or GIFT City presence as a competitive handicap in NRI business. | The long view had said: Credible but incremental expansion beyond the Tamil Nadu home base via institutional/state-government tie-ups, a Hyderabad retail branch and NEO's 40 locations; no overseas presence and it explicitly cannot offer GIFT City/leverage products.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: A multi-year strategy has been executed steadily — INR75,000 crores of business added over 3.5 years to cross the INR2 lakh crore milestone — with willingness to forgo business that does not fit the plan. | The long view had said: A multi-year restructuring executed with consistency across all 17 calls — ROA from 0.19% (Dec-2020) to 2.11%, GNPA 3.97%→0.74%, corporate mix 45%→14% — with guidance repeatedly met or beaten.
- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Weak. Now: As a deposit-funded bank the model is inherently balance-sheet heavy — INR1.05 lakh crore of advances funded by INR1.23 lakh crore of deposits — the opposite of asset-light, with returns driven by spread rather than turnover. | The long view had said: Bank balance sheets are inherently asset-heavy, and while returns are excellent (ROA 2.11%, NIM 4.26%), there is no structural moat protecting the spread — management concedes it must give up price to retain customers.

Now: Mixed evidence — KVB is taking share in mortgages and jewel loans and NBFC/MFI stress has opened space, but it is also donating accounts to more aggressive lenders on price. | The long view had said: Weaker incumbents exist to take share from, evidenced by the deliberate win-back campaign reclaiming customers lost to other banks and strong new-to-bank acquisition.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared.

*Foundation test: FAIL on the long view, FAIL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management defends spreads deliberately — NIM rose from 3.77% (Q2 FY26) to 3.99% and then 4.26% in Q1 FY27, above pre-rate-cut levels despite 125bps of repo cuts, and they walk away from underpriced business — but this is asset-mix and liability repricing discipline, not price increases without volume loss, since holding price costs them accounts and slows commercial growth. (The long view had said: Management explicitly concedes it cannot raise price without losing volume — the May-26 and Jul-26 calls say they must 'compromise on margins' in MSME and to retain existing LAP/commercial customers, and FY27 NIM guidance is set below the current 4.26% exit level.)

**That weakened:**

- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Although KVB sources mortgages via an 'open market channel' and co-lends with NBFCs, the calls repeatedly show the end borrower is highly price-sensitive — accounts are lost outright on rate, the opposite of the low-price-sensitivity that defines this pattern. (The long view had said: Origination genuinely runs through intermediaries — the NEO/open market channel, DSAs, connectors and DSTs, co-lending NBFCs and BNPL partners (DSA commission is a named opex line by FY26) — but the lock-in the pattern requires is absent: no trade training or professional reputation moat, and customers are visibly price-sensitive.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Nothing in the window articulates why a funded competitor could not replicate KVB's position; on the contrary, the Q3 and Q4 FY26 calls describe rivals taking accounts away on price and heightened competition forcing the bank to 'lie low' in housing and vehicle loans. (The long view had said: The replicable-in-a-few-years test is only half passed: a century-old Tamil Nadu branch and retail-household deposit base (72% of deposits) plus a demonstrably rebuilt underwriting culture would take a newcomer years, but the calls themselves show the advantage being competed away on price in LAP, housing, MSME and commercial, and the deposit franchise is the acknowledged constraint rather than a fortress.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Not comparable: Forward Integrators (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: As a bank, pricing and economics are directly set by RBI policy — the 125bps repo cut mechanically repriced the EBLR book and NIM, the FCNR(B) ceiling relaxation determined NRI deposit competitiveness, CGFMU guarantee coverage governs the MFI book, and PSLC sales and new labour codes flow straight into income and provisions. Deposit strategy also leans on state government bodies and institutional/government business.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
