# Life Insurance (LICI) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC) is India's largest life insurance company: it sells life insurance and savings/pension policies to individual Indian households and to employers and other groups, and is listed on the BSE and NSE while being regulated by IRDAI. Customers pay premiums — new-business premium in year one and renewal premium in later years — and LIC pays out on death, maturity or as pension/annuity income; in FY26 total premium income was Rs. 5,35,984 crore, of which Rs. 3,39,375 crore was individual business and Rs. 1,96,609 crore group business. It makes money two ways: the margin between premiums collected and claims/expenses paid (profit after tax was Rs. 57,419 crore in FY26), and by investing the money it holds until claims fall due — assets under management were Rs. 57,29,396 crore at March 2026. It operates in the Indian life insurance market, where it holds about 56.6% share of first-year premium (roughly 36.6% of individual business and 70.1% of group business), selling a suite of about 58 products almost entirely through a force of roughly 14.6 lakh individual agents, plus banks, alternate channels and online partners such as PolicyBazaar.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Individual business (Par)** — Traditional participating policies sold to individual customers, where policyholders share in the profits — historically the bulk of LIC's individual book (92% of individual APE in Q1 FY23), still the majority at Rs. 28,121 crore or 64.89% of individual APE in FY26.
- **Individual business (Non-Par)** — Non-participating individual products — guaranteed-return savings, term cover, annuity and unit-linked plans such as Jeevan Utsav, Jeevan Kiran, Jeevan Dhara-II, Dhan Vriddhi, Index Plus and Amritbaal — which management has deliberately pushed from 7-9% of individual APE in FY22-23 to Rs. 15,214 crore or 35.11% in FY26, and which they credit for rising VNB margins.
- **Group business (Pension & Group Schemes)** — Bulk life, superannuation, gratuity and fund-based schemes sold to employers and institutions rather than individuals — Rs. 1,96,609 crore of premium in FY26 and about 35% of total APE, a business where LIC has around 70% market share but which management notes carries lower VNB margins.
- **Renewal premium book** — The recurring premiums flowing in from policies sold in earlier years — Rs. 2,71,699 crore in FY26 — a large, stable income stream whose durability management tracks via persistency ratios (13th-month persistency was 75.75% in Q1 FY23).
- **Investments / Assets Under Management** — The in-house investment operation (run by a Chief Investment Officer and front/back-office teams) that manages the accumulated policyholder and shareholder funds — Rs. 57,29,396 crore at March 2026 — and supports the solvency ratio of 2.35 against a 1.5 regulatory minimum.
- **Agency distribution channel** — LIC's own tied sales force of about 14,57,045 individual agents (roughly 44% of all agents in the market), which sold 98.42% of policies and brought in 91.75% of new business premium in FY26 — overwhelmingly the main way LIC reaches customers.
- **Bancassurance and Alternate Channels (BAC)** — Sales through bank partners, alternate channels and digital tie-ups such as PolicyBazaar — small relative to agency (Rs. 3,341 crore of new business premium in the first nine months of FY26) but described as a fast-growing focus area, with some products like Bima Ratna launched specifically for this channel.
- **Riders and government scheme business** — Add-on covers sold alongside main policies (7 individual riders and 1 group rider as of March 2026, including accident-benefit and cancer-cover type products) plus participation in the government's Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana, which management reports separately from its 58-product commercial suite.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Life Insurance Corporation earns its "Decent" 54-out-of-100 rating (business quality 58, multibagger fit 55, risk safety 46) on the back of one very durable engine: a huge book of policies that keeps paying. In FY26, renewal premium from individual policies of ₹2,71,699 crore dwarfed new business of ₹67,676 crore, which is why the Recurring Revenue pattern is a strong fit and why sales have compounded at roughly 8% a year with unusually steady year-to-year growth. Customers pay before the company has to pay out, so working capital is negative and customers effectively fund the business. What customers are buying is certainty rather than a cheap price, and the record backs that up with 2.49 crore claims honoured and ₹2.80 lakh crore of maturity payouts in FY26. Sitting underneath all of it is an under-penetrated Indian life insurance market with a national expansion agenda the company is aligning its digital and rural distribution to. Management has also done broadly what it said it would at listing, which is why the Corporate Culture pattern registers as a strong fit: the non-participating share of individual new business went from 7.75% in the first quarter of FY23 to 18.32% in FY24 to 35.11% in FY26, the long-held ₹5,000 crore bancassurance ambition was met in FY26, and the overall expense ratio fell from 15.57% in FY24 to 12.42% in FY25 to 11.91% in FY26.

The awkward part is that the very features producing the strengths also produce the biggest risks, and in two places the same fact appears on both sides of the ledger. First, distribution: about 14.6 lakh tied agents sold 98.42% of FY26 policies and roughly 92% of new business premium. That force is what makes the Friendly Middleman pattern a strong fit and is a large part of why Cost to Replicate scores as a partial fit, since no newly funded entrant could assemble that reach or the rural footprint covering 59% of India's 2.45 lakh Gram Panchayats quickly. It is also, unchanged, the Stakeholder Concentration risk rated elevated, with bancassurance at just 3.61% of new business premium in FY24; the offset is that bancassurance and alternate channels are now growing 45% to 67% a year and have lifted their share of individual new business premium from 4.73% to 7.51%. Second, the product mix shift: moving into non-participating products lifted margin on new business from 13.6% to 21.2% and grew that measure 41.6%, and it is the direct answer to the risk that customer preferences are shifting away from the traditional participating savings core. But the same shift was accompanied by giving up market share rather than winning it. First-year premium share fell from 65.42% to 58.87% in FY24 to 56.66% in FY26, individual share from 43.86% to 36.6%, and agent-count share from 47.61% to 44.25%. That is why Market Share Gainers is a clear non-fit, why two separate quality checks penalise the company as a share donator, and why the New Entrants risk stays on watch as bank-led private insurers keep taking ground. It also caps the Brand Strength pattern at partial: the 65-year heritage and Fortune 500 rank 98 are real, but the pattern's key test is share, and share went the wrong way. Growth here has been mix-led rather than volume-led, with policy count up only 3.7% in FY26.

On price and returns the record is weaker still, and this is where the rating is held down. Pricing Power is a non-fit because the evidence runs the other way: in the first quarter of FY23 the company raised annuity benefits by 7% to 12% specifically to close the gap with competitors, knowingly compressing margin, which is the same undifferentiated, rate-competed dynamic behind the Good Enough Goods risk. Toll Roads does not fit either, since the product is a large discretionary savings outlay for the customer rather than a small unavoidable one. The balance sheet is structurally heavy, with ₹57.3 lakh crore of assets supporting ₹5.36 lakh crore of premium, so each ₹1 of assets generates only about ₹0.16 of sales, and cash return on capital was negative 15% this year, dragging the Return on Capital quality area to the worst score of any area. The company is also the opposite of an overlooked stock, being one of the most heavily covered names on the exchanges. Above all this sits the single high-rated risk, Government Dependency: expense-of-management limits produced a roughly ₹12,914 crore charge amortised over three years by regulatory approval, and surrender-value, commission and composite-licence rules remain pending, meaning the economics can be reset by policy rather than by management. Overall resilience is judged strained. The honest reading is a genuinely durable, cash-collecting franchise with real cost discipline and a credible product pivot, attached to a shrinking competitive position, weak returns on the capital employed, and a regulator with the ability to change the rules.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) — STRONG FIT
*"Sells once, earns forever"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence and the numbers agree (2 of 3 checks computable supporting).

**What the calls show:** Life insurance is structurally recurring: FY26 individual renewal premium of Rs 2,71,699 crore dwarfed new business of Rs 67,676 crore, and every call from Aug 2022 to May 2026 discloses persistency by cohort (13th/25th/37th/49th/61st month) and the in-force/claims base as core metrics. The stream is premium-funded in advance and needs no incremental capital, though persistency is only middling (13th month 74.6% in FY26, down from 75.75% in Q1FY23) and management admits some cohorts have not improved.

> "Renewal premium income of individual business for the year ended 31 March, 2026, was 2,71,699 crore rupees" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Customers effectively fund the business — cash arrives 0 days before suppliers are paid.
- ✅ Capital spending is about 0% of sales — growth needs little capital.
- ❌ Operating cash was positive in 5 of 8 years — not fully consistent.

### Friendly Middleman — STRONG FIT
*"The expert sells it for you"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence and the numbers agree (1 of 2 checks computable supporting).

**What the calls show:** Distribution is almost entirely intermediated by tied agents who select and sell the product: 98.42% of FY26 policies and 91.75% of new business premium came from the agency force of ~14.6 lakh agents, and LIC keeps investing in the channel (agency transformation project announced FY24, ANANDA agent app, Bima Sakhi recruitment/training reaching 3.45 lakh women across 59% of Gram Panchayats, Super Sales Saathi app). End-customer pull marketing is barely discussed by comparison.

> "Further, 98.42% of our policies in the year ended March 31st 2026 were sold by our agency force." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Operating margin of 5% — too thin for a protected franchise.
- ✅ Operating margin moved from about -0% (early years) to 5% (recent years) — the widening-profitability signature of pricing power / lock-in.

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** Across 17 calls the promises made at listing were tracked and delivered: non-par share of individual APE went 7.75% (Q1FY23) → 18.32% (FY24) → 35.11% (FY26), the long-held Rs 5,000 crore Bancassurance ambition was hit in FY26, expense ratio fell to a post-listing low, and VNB margin rose to 21.2% — with bad news owned on the same calls (persistency cohorts 'not shown any significant improvement', excess EoM charged to shareholders, wage-revision pension liability disclosed and amortised with regulator approval). The counterweight is leadership instability: chairperson/MD turnover has been continuous (Kumar → Mohanty → Doraiswamy, five MDs exiting across FY23–FY25) given the government-appointment structure.

> "we have delivered more than what was told to the shareholders at the time of listing" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL

## What to watch

- Whether the slide in market share stops or slows - first-year premium share of 56.66% and individual share of 36.6% in FY26 are the core of both the Market Share Gainers non-fit and the New Entrants risk.
- Pending regulation on surrender values, upfront commissions and composite licences, plus any further expense-of-management charges beyond the ₹12,914 crore already being amortised - the Government Dependency risk is the only high-rated one.
- Whether bancassurance and alternate channels keep compounding at 45% to 67% and lift their 7.51% share of individual new business premium meaningfully higher, which is the only real relief for the elevated Stakeholder Concentration risk from 98.42% agency-sold policies.
- Whether the 21.2% margin on new business holds without another round of benefit enhancements like the 7% to 12% annuity increase - repeat cuts would confirm both the Pricing Power non-fit and the Good Enough Goods risk.
- Any improvement in the negative 15% cash return on capital and the ₹0.16 of sales per ₹1 of assets - these two checks drive the weakest quality area and are what keeps overall resilience at strained.
