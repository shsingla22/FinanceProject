### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 3 unchanged; 5 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: No heritage/provenance story, A&P spend, distributor leverage or adjacency launches are discussed on any of the four calls. (The long view had said: There is genuine brand/heritage equity — Murugappa Group backing was cited as reopening supplier and customer relationships at AWUKO, Rhodius has 'seven decades' of legacy, and FY26 growth came partly from 'branding of established products in newer geographies' — but the calls show no premium pricing or sustained A&P spend, and the AWUKO brand purchase ended in a wind-up.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Management twice attributes standalone Electrominerals and Ceramics growth to volume plus price (Q1 FY26), and standalone PBIT margin rose to 15% in Q3 and 23.4% FY26 PBIT growth; but Q2 FY26 explicitly says prices were flat, consolidated Abrasives margins fell from 8.2% to 4.1%, and no explicit price-increase-without-volume-loss claim is made. (The long view had said: The record is one of price deflation, not pricing power: soft abrasive prices in FY23, 4-6% realisation drops in standalone electrominerals (Jul 2024), and 13% realisation falls at Foskor (Feb 2026/May 2026) that ultimately made the business unviable; price hikes are described as catch-up cost recovery, not value capture.)

**That weakened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: LIKELY FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: Only a passing reference to Metallized and Engineered Ceramics driving growth; no R&D spend disclosure, pipeline language, or named new products with revenue impact in the last-12-month excerpts. (The long view had said: Innovation shows up concretely — treated grains driving a doubling of electromineral exports (May 2026), a new semiconductor wafer-fab component facility (INR 66 crores), SOFC engineering ceramics up 30% on AI data-centre demand — but R&D spend is never disclosed or quantified as a share of sales across any of the four calls.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 2 worsened, 0 unchanged; 4 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Demand is consumable/operations-linked (abrasives, ceramics used in customers' running operations) rather than tied to customer capex programmes, but the last 12 months show real swing: standalone abrasives degrew 5.5% in Q1 FY26 on channel destocking and consolidated PBIT fell from INR444cr to INR301cr over 9M FY26, before a sharp H2 rebound (standalone H2 sales +14.4% sequentially, Q4 segments each +~22% year-on-year). Electrominerals pricing is discussed in volume-and-price terms typical of a mineral business. (The long view had said: Across 2022-2026 management frames demand through commodity prices and customer capex cycles — base-metal corrections in Aug 2022, EMD price realisation falling 4-6% on import pricing, zircon sand price volatility killing Foskor, and ceramics repeatedly missing guidance because customer 'projects are getting delayed' and shipments await customer inspection; consolidated PBIT margin swung from 13% (Q1FY24) to 7-8% (FY26). The abrasives/refractory consumable base tied to customer operations rather than capex cushions this, and capex was maintained at ~INR 350 crores through the downturn.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Two overseas units were written off in FY26 because they could not hold price against cheaper competition — Foskor Zirconia declared commercially unviable on 'intensifying global competition' after failing to earn sustainable profits since 2013, and Awuko is being voluntarily wound up (INR119cr exceptional) on mounting losses with no turnaround possible; standalone electrominerals growth in Q2 FY26 was volume-led with 'prices remained flat', consistent with limited pricing power in the commodity end. (The long view had said: This is the clearest and most repeated risk: cheaper Chinese product has attacked the premium at both ends of the portfolio — Foskor's realisation fell 13% 'because of the price pressure from Chinese competition' and the business was written down and exited after failing to earn sustainable profits since 2013, while retail abrasives faced years of questioning on Chinese dumping and standalone EMD realisation slipped 4-6% on low-price imports. Rhodius, acquired explicitly as the 'globally best quality' premium disc maker, ran a EUR 2.6 million FY26 loss, and AWUKO was wound up entirely; competitiveness in Europe is now partly leaning on anti-dumping duties and FTA tariff relief rather than on the premium itself.)

**That worsened:**

- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Government action is the single largest driver of the FY26 profit decline: the January 2025 sanctions on the Russian subsidiary VAW cut its rouble sales ~31% in H1 FY26 and accounted for roughly INR87 crores of the INR156 crore consolidated PBT drop, with INR104 crores of FY25 provisions on sanctioned-country deposits; separately Foskor Zirconia is being wound down partly on South African electricity cost escalation. (The long view had said: Fortunes are repeatedly moved by government policy rather than by the company: US sanctions on VAW Russia (Jan 2025) cut rouble sales 35% and drove ~INR 87 crores of PBT decline plus INR 104 crores of provisions, while on the upside EU anti-dumping duties on Chinese grains helped exports double, and management fields questions on China's export-rebate withdrawal, the EU FTA and GST rationalisation. Pricing is not regulator-set and end demand is industrial, which bounds the exposure.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Rhodius's switch of a single third-party logistics provider in Q1 FY26 disrupted fulfilment badly enough to cost close to EUR 5 million of sales and turn a small profit into a EUR 1.6 million quarterly loss, and management had to walk down full-year guidance twice as a result — evidence of dependence on one channel/logistics partner rather than a diversified distribution base. (The long view had said: No single customer, licensor or supplier is ever named as driving results across the 17 calls; revenue is spread over three segments, retail/industrial/precision channels, and multiple geographies, and management describes actively broadening the base through dealer appointments and new OEM onboarding. The only supplier-relationship stress cited was re-establishing AWUKO's lapsed suppliers post-insolvency, which was resolved.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 18 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
