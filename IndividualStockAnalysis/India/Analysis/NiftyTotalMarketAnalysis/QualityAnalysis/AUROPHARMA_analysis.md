# Aurobindo Pharma Ltd. (AUROPHARMA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: PHARMA*

Aurobindo Pharma is an India-based (Hyderabad) drug maker that mostly sells generic medicines — copies of drugs whose patents have expired — in pill/oral form, as injectables, and in other formats, plus the bulk chemical ingredients (APIs) that go into medicines. Its customers are overseas buyers first: the US is its single biggest market (about $1.6 billion of sales in FY26), followed by Europe (which crossed €1 billion in FY26), then a set of 'growth markets' such as Canada and India, and government/donor-funded antiretroviral (HIV) tender business. It makes money by manufacturing at large scale in its own plants and getting products approved with regulators (it had filed 838 ANDAs with the US FDA by mid-2024, ~668 approved), then competing largely on volume and reliable supply while prices in generics steadily erode. Group revenue was Rs. 33,653 crores in FY26 with an EBITDA margin of about 20.4%, and management is also spending on newer areas — biosimilars, peptides, vaccines, a China plant, and backward integration into antibiotic raw materials (Pen-G).

**The parts of the business:**

- **Formulation business** — The finished-medicine business (tablets, capsules, injectables) sold across the US, Europe, growth markets and ARV; it is the bulk of the company at roughly 86-89% of total revenue, Rs. 7,646 crores in Q4 FY26.
- **API business** — Sells the active pharmaceutical ingredients — the bulk chemicals inside drugs — to other manufacturers and to its own plants; smaller but steady at roughly 11-14% of revenue, about Rs. 4,047 crores for full-year FY26.
- **US formulations (Aurobindo Pharma USA)** — The oral-solid generics business in the United States, described as a large, diversified portfolio that keeps pricing stable; US revenue was $387 million in Q4 FY26 and $1,631 million for the full year, with management aiming at $2 billion 'over the near term'.
- **Injectables and specialty (Eugia Pharma Specialities / AuroMedics)** — A separately-run unit making injectable and specialty products, treated as a higher-value growth engine; global specialty and injectable revenue was $513 million in FY26, growing 12-13% year-on-year excluding gRevlimid.
- **Europe formulations business** — Generic formulations sold across key European markets, called a 'strategic growth engine' and the fastest grower recently at ~27-30% year-on-year; it hit the €1 billion annual revenue milestone in FY26.
- **Growth markets** — Everything outside the US and Europe — Canada, India and other newer geographies — worth Rs. 3,499 crores (about $397 million) in FY26, expanded through launches and bolt-on acquisitions such as Khandelwal Laboratories' non-oncology business.
- **ARV formulation business** — Antiretroviral (HIV) medicines sold largely through tenders and donor programmes; a smaller line at Rs. 1,384 crores (about $157 million) in FY26, up 33% on tender wins and volumes.
- **Biosimilars, vaccines and peptides** — A newer R&D-heavy unit under its own CEO developing biologic copies and complex products, still pre-scale — described as 'progressing well' with clinical trials advancing and framed as a long-term growth driver alongside the core generics business.
- **Pen-G / 6APA / Amoxicillin (antibiotic raw materials, PLI project)** — A large new Indian plant making penicillin and related antibiotic building blocks in-house instead of importing them, ramping toward 10,000+ tonnes a year and expected to lift margins over time, helped by a government minimum-import-price rule.
- **US manufacturing and acquisitions (Dayton, Raleigh, Lannett, Acrotech)** — Owned US plants for oral, transdermal and respiratory products plus acquired businesses; Dayton has just gone commercial and is expected to contribute meaningfully from FY27, with Lannett still pending regulatory approval.
- **China OSD facility** — An oral-solid-dosage plant in China building toward 2 billion units a year, supplying mainly Europe and local approvals; still small and only expected to reach EBITDA break-even around Q4 FY26.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Aurobindo's growth is real but it is the kind of growth you get by selling more units, not by charging more. Sales have compounded at roughly 10% a year with unusually steady year-to-year progress, revenue rising from ₹6,236 crore in the first quarter of FY23 to ₹33,653 crore in FY26, and about ₹20 of every ₹100 of sales still drops through as operating profit with only about a two-point swing in margin from year to year. The growth checks are the strongest part of the whole record (Growth scores 0.67), and they show up in the patterns as genuine share capture: Europe grew from €189 million a quarter to €261 million and delivered the promised €1 billion year while its market grew 0–3%, growth markets rose 25% with Canada added, and China's oral-solids plant is scaling toward 2 billion units. But the same records that credit the volumes deny the price. The 'Pricing, Mix and Volume' check is negative because growth is explicitly volume-led with pricing at best flat and often falling — about 3% US erosion in FY23 and 'pricing pressures' still being cited in FY26 — and that single trait knocks out four patterns at once (Pricing Power, Brand Strength, Cost to Replicate and Friendly Middleman all score NO FIT) while simultaneously creating two of the standing risks: New Entrants is ELEVATED precisely because each new filer on a molecule resets the price, and Good Enough Goods is on WATCH because the products are interchangeable and the buying decision sits with distributors, pharmacy benefit managers and buying consortia rather than with any patient who knows the name. This is why Industry Structure is the worst-scoring area in the whole framework at -0.67, and why Market Share Gainers only rates PARTIAL — Europe compounds ahead of its market, but the US is flat to down once the limited-competition product gRevlimid is stripped out, and the gap has been filled by buying portfolios and companies rather than by winning on price.

The company's answer to having no pricing power is to be cheaper than everyone else, and that answer explains both the heaviest spending in the record and the biggest hole in the balance sheet. Capital expenditure has climbed from $61 million a quarter in FY23 to $341 million in FY26 across penicillin-G, China oral solids, Vizag injectables, the Dayton and Raleigh sites and a biologics contract-manufacturing plant, backed by 60 billion units of capacity, backward integration into its own penicillin-G and 6-APA feedstock, and gross margin improving toward 60%. That is a serious cost-and-scale strategy — enough for the Low Price Plus pattern to register as PARTIAL — and it is also the silver lining cited against the Good Enough Goods and Cyclicality risks, since the capacity is being built from a net cash position of $317 million rather than from borrowing. The trouble is what the same spending has produced so far. The capex check notes that incremental returns have lagged badly, with penicillin-G and China named as the disappointments, and the record shows penicillin-G needed a minimum import price from the Government of India to be viable at all — which is exactly why the Toll Roads pattern is a flat NO FIT and why Government Dependency is the one HIGH RISK on the list, alongside FDA warning letters and an Official Action Indicated classification at Eugia that gate injectable growth, approvals that gate every launch, and a US government shutdown that delayed antitrust clearance on the Lannett deal by 76 days. Meanwhile the cost of carrying all this is severe: roughly 252 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, the single worst-scoring check in the framework, so even the growth that does arrive absorbs capital before it returns any. The Stakeholder Concentration risk sits on WATCH for a related reason — management says no single product dominates, yet FY26 results are reported almost entirely excluding gRevlimid because that one product swung US constant-currency revenue down 18%.

What holds the case together is management and the research effort, and both come with the same caveat — they are credible but they are buying time rather than building a moat. Leadership has been stable and promoter-anchored across all sixteen calls, the balance sheet stayed conservative with gross debt cut, bad news including the penicillin-G teething problems and the Eugia data-integrity findings has been disclosed reasonably plainly, and shareholders saw a first-ever ₹750 crore buyback completed in August 2024 at ₹1,460 alongside a ₹264 crore dividend — which earns Management a 0.5 and puts Corporate Culture at PARTIAL, though the distribution record is still short and thin. Research spending has been held steady at 4.5–5% of revenue, growing from ₹310 crore in the first quarter of FY23 to ₹1,590 crore in FY26, and it does convert — 838 cumulative filings, 668 approvals, nine launches and seven approvals in a single quarter, plus named biosimilar assets and a GLP-1 cartridge line. That is enough for Innovation Dominance to read LIKELY FIT, but the same entry says the work is replication-driven and funded by thin generic margins, and the Technological Innovation risk is ELEVATED for the mirror-image reason: the core business is old-format oral-solid small molecules while treatment shifts toward biologics, peptides and GLP-1 drugs, so the heavy spending is defensive, meant to avoid being left with a legacy product format. Put the three views together and they agree rather than conflict. Business quality scores 54, multibagger fit 33 and risk safety 32, giving a combined 42 out of 100 and a Mixed, two-star rating with resilience described as STRAINED. The company executes well inside an industry that does not let good execution turn into pricing power, and every strength named here — the volumes, the capacity, the research budget — is the same trait that generates the matching risk.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Fortunes are repeatedly set by government action across the whole record: USFDA warning letters/OAI at Eugia gate injectable growth, ANDA and EMA approvals gate every launch, FTC clearance (delayed 76 days by a US government shutdown) gates the Lannett deal, the PLI scheme funded Pen-G, ARV revenue runs on PEPFAR-type tenders, and a one-year Indian minimum-import-price notification is openly described as the catalyst for Pen-G profitability.

*Silver lining: Revenue is spread across US, Europe (€1bn), growth markets and India so no single regulator sets group pricing; the MIP is an upside, not the base case, of an otherwise commercially priced portfolio*
