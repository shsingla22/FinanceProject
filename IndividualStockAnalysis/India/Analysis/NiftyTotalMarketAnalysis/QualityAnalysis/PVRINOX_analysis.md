# PVR Inox (PVRINOX) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

PVR INOX runs movie theatres — multiplex cinemas — in India and Sri Lanka, selling tickets to ordinary moviegoers to watch films on the big screen. It makes money three main ways: ticket sales (average ticket price of about INR280 in FY26), food and beverages sold at the cinema (about INR147 spend per head), and advertising shown on its screens; it also earns from sponsorships and related activity. It shows Hindi, regional-language and Hollywood films, plus re-releases of older titles and non-film content like live-streamed sport (IPL, FIFA World Cup 2026), concerts and comedy shows. As of FY26 it operated close to 1,800 screens and describes itself as running roughly 40% of India's multiplex screens and capturing 31% of the country's box office; FY26 revenue was about INR6,742 crores with a profit of INR386 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Movie exhibition / box office (ticketing)** — The core business of selling cinema tickets — 150 million guests across FY26 at an average ticket price of INR280 — and management calls movies "our mainstay".
- **Food & Beverage (F&B)** — Selling popcorn, meals and drinks inside the cinemas, measured as spend per head (SPH), which reached INR147 in FY26 and which management has pushed for years toward matching the ticket price — a large and deliberately growing share of revenue.
- **Advertising** — Selling on-screen and in-cinema advertising slots to brands; management tracks it as ad revenue per screen and treats it as a meaningful recovery driver alongside admissions.
- **Screen network / property development** — Building and operating the cinema estate itself — 1,747 screens across 356 cinemas in 111 cities as of Oct 2024, growing to roughly 100–120 new screens a year while closing underperformers; increasingly built under "capital-light" and FOCO arrangements (55% of FY26 additions) where partners fund the property.
- **Alternate content / out-of-home entertainment** — Using the same screens for live sport streaming, concerts, live events, stand-up comedy and curated re-releases — a newer push to make the cinemas a general leisure destination rather than only a film venue.
- **PVR Pictures (film distribution)** — A film business headed by its own CEO within the group, referenced in the calls as part of the company's business planning and strategy side; the excerpts give no size figures for it.

### The story in depth

PVR INOX earns a "Decent" 60 out of 100, built from 61 for business quality, 64 for multibagger fit and 52 for risk safety. The one thing that holds the whole story together is the screen network. The quality framework scores "Distribution advantage" at its top mark because roughly 40% of India's multiplex screens and 31% of national box office make the company unavoidable for film producers and the first call for mall developers. That same scale is what lets the two strongest patterns work: "Pricing Power" and "Brand Strength" both come through as strong fits, with average ticket prices up 8% to INR280 and spend per head up 10% to INR147 in FY26 while footfall still grew 10%, and again +8%/+9% in the first quarter of FY27. The framework's "Intangible benefits" check agrees from the accounting side: after direct input costs the company keeps INR93 of every INR100 of sales. Customers also pay before suppliers do, so cash comes back 391 days ahead of when bills are due, which is why "Cost of working capital" scores full marks.

The catch is that the same premium pricing that makes the patterns work is what makes the "Good Enough Goods" risk live. A cheaper alternative is permanently on the table: streaming at home, single screens, and rival multiplexes where the national average ticket is INR100-150 against PVR's INR280. The company visibly manages that gap with discounted Tuesdays, mornings and front seats, which is also why the "Low Price Plus" pattern is only a partial fit rather than a real second engine. Scale cuts both ways elsewhere too. The company reached its dominant position mainly through the PVR-INOX merger, and the framework marks "Merger and acquisition" negatively because that was a transformational deal rather than a small bolt-on, with margins deteriorating afterwards (an FY25 loss of INR152 crores and 72 screen exits) before recovering. The same merger is why "Market Share Gainers" only reaches a likely fit — the step-change was bought, not earned organically — and why "Corporate Culture" is partial: the FY24-FY26 promises on cash flow, debt and screen closures were delivered, but the earlier record, including a July 2022 line that debt would not rise, was weaker.

Underneath everything sits the film slate, which is why resilience reads as STRAINED with two risks at elevated. Earnings swing violently: quarterly EBITDA of INR207 crores against INR447 crores a year earlier, a full-year loss in FY25, then a record FY26 with EBITDA doubling to INR968 crores. That is the "Cyclicality" risk and the "Fashion Risk" of shifting audience taste, and it is also why "Persistence of growth" scores negatively despite roughly 15% average annual sales growth — the growth is lumpy year to year. Management's answer is to make the business less capital-hungry and less dependent on any one film: 55% of FY26's 93 new screens came under capital-light formats with a 138-screen signed pipeline where developers fund the build, which is the "Forward Integrators" pattern, and mid-scale INR100-200 crore films rose from 12% to 20% of collections. Returns are only now becoming respectable — 15% cash return on capital and ROCE of 10.2% in FY26 — and although the company is at net cash of INR80 crores, the "Distribution to shareholders" check scores negatively because there have been no dividends or buybacks in four years and no distribution policy has been formed. Competitive Advantage scores 0.0 across the quality areas, consistent with "Cost to Replicate" being only a partial fit: the barrier is finite prime mall real estate and scale, not something a well-funded rival could never build over a few years.

- ✅ Operating margin moved from about 15% (early years) to 30% (recent years) — the widening-profitability signature of pricing power / lock-in.

*"The name itself sells"*

**What the calls show:** By FY26 management frames the company as a scale consumer brand — ~40% of India's multiplex screens and 31% of box office, preferred partner for mall developers and producers — and explicitly monetises the brand through franchising, while the brand also carries adjacencies (live sport, concerts, alternate content).

> "PVR-INOX stands apart as a well-loved consumer brand with unmatched market leadership and scale" — *management, on an earnings call*

> "16% of our circuit is premium screens like IMAX, 4DX, ICE and ScreenX" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 1 check supporting).

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Fashion Risk. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.9× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 112% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ⚠️ Returns on capital slid from about 13% to 5% — the business is earning less and less on the money invested in it.

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 3 numeric fingerprints reinforce it.

**What the calls show:** Not a commodity or customer-capex cycle, but earnings swing violently with the film slate — FY25 Q2 EBITDA of INR207cr vs INR447cr a year earlier, a FY25 loss of INR152cr, then a record FY26 with EBITDA doubling to INR968cr; management itself frames the business as hits and misses against a heavy fixed rent base. The pivot to capital-light/FOCO growth and screen exits shows they are deliberately reducing the over-investment leg of the cycle.

> "our business is of hits and misses . I think it's best to look at it o n annual basis rather than on quarter -to-quarter basis" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Operations-linked consumer footfall rather than customer capex; capital-light/FOCO expansion, capex intensity down 24%, net debt cut ~90% to net cash; management notes small ticket size means cinema holds up in downturns*

- ⚠️ Year-to-year sales growth swings by ±123 percentage points — boom-and-bust revenue, not a steady compounder.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from -120% to 32% (swinging ±41.7 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 42% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 13% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Fashion Risk — ELEVATED
*"Cool today, uncool tomorrow"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Demand rests entirely on shifting audience taste in films and stars — the 2022-24 stretch saw Hindi cinema underperform while South content and re-releases carried the load, and management explicitly pinned hopes on 'Ranbir Kapoor, Ranveer Singh, Shahrukh Khan, Salman Khan' returning; that reversed sharply with Bollywood up 55% in FY26. The company owns no brand that can go uncool, and the wide portfolio of ~1,500 releases a year spreads the taste risk.

> "some of the big starrer which were completely missin g in this year, your Ranbir Kapoor, your Ranveer Singh, Shahrukh Khan, Salman Khan, all of these guys have been -- have not really had any single release this year" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Repeat-purchase habit across many titles and languages rather than one bet — '1,500 films get released every year... if one movie does well, one doesn't do well, it doesn't matter to us that much'*

- ⚠️ Year-to-year sales growth swings by ±123 percentage points — boom-and-bust revenue, not a steady compounder.
- ✅ Growth is holding up: -24% a year earlier, 88% recently — no structural stall.

### Government Dependency — WATCH
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Cinema economics in India sit inside a policy perimeter — screen openings slipped a full quarter in Q1 FY27 purely on regulatory licences, subscription pricing was stalled by the recurring-payments regulator, electricity relief came from state governments, and analysts routinely ask whether a new state Chief Minister brings 'positive regulatory' change, which management cannot answer. **What the calls show:** Across the four years no structurally different competitor appears in the exhibition calls; the industry is consolidating toward PVR INOX, which now runs ~40% of India's multiplex screens and takes 31% of box office, and management reports competing leisure formats such as IPL show no measurable effect on cinema attendance.

## What to watch

- Whether ATP and SPH can keep rising without losing footfall — if volumes crack, both the "Pricing Power" and "Brand Strength" patterns weaken and the "Good Enough Goods" risk becomes real at the INR280 vs INR100-150 price gap.
- Occupancy trend against the 25.7% seen in Q2 FY25 and the 27%+ pencilled in for FY27 — the test of whether the "Shifting Consumer Preferences" risk from at-home viewing is being contained.
- Delivery on the 138-screen capital-light pipeline (52 FOCO, 86 asset-light) and the ~100 screens a year guided — this is the "Forward Integrators" pattern and the main silver lining offsetting the ELEVATED "Cyclicality" risk.
- Whether ROCE builds beyond FY26's 10.2% and whether a distribution policy finally appears now that the company is at net cash — the two weakest points in the "Capex for growth" and "Distribution to shareholders" checks.
- Broadening of the slate beyond a handful of titles and studios — mid-scale films at 20% of collections need to hold or rise, or the "Fashion Risk" and "Stakeholder Concentration" risks reassert and earnings stay as lumpy as the "Persistence of growth" check flags.
