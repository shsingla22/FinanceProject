# Latent View (LATENTVIEW) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

LatentView Analytics is an India-headquartered, Indian-listed (BSE/NSE) data-analytics services firm that sells consulting and hands-on technical work to large corporate clients — helping them set their analytics strategy, build data platforms and pipelines, and create models, dashboards and, increasingly, generative-AI and agentic solutions. Its customers are mostly big US companies (the US was over 90% of revenue in FY23, with Europe and India added later as smaller geographies), concentrated in technology, banking/financial services, consumer packaged goods and retail, and manufacturing. It makes money by billing clients for people-delivered projects and statements of work — signed account by account, largely staffed from lower-cost delivery teams in India with client-facing sales and consulting staff onsite — so revenue grows by adding new logos and expanding into more stakeholders and workstreams inside existing accounts. Management guided to roughly $119–120 million of revenue in FY26, about 19–20% growth, with EBITDA margins historically around the high-20s percent.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Consulting** — Advises clients on their analytics strategy, roadmap and how to structure their in-house analytics teams (e.g. helping a fintech's marketing organisation from the CMO down); described as the front end of the relationship rather than a separately sized segment.
- **Data engineering** — Builds the underlying data platforms and pipelines that pull information from many sources into one place — for example a 360-degree patient data platform built for the International Myeloma Foundation; called out as one of the core service lines alongside consulting.
- **Data science / BI and AI work** — Model building, business intelligence and AI delivery; management said about 28% of FY26 revenue was 'primary AI' (generative or agentic solutions the client can see and use directly) plus another 21% where AI sits 'under the hood', so roughly half of all revenue now involves AI in some form.
- **Agentic / generative AI solutions** — Automated workflows built on tools like OpenAI Codex, Anthropic's Claude Code and Google Gemini for payments and invoice reconciliation, market-intelligence gathering, automated campaigns, fraud and counterfeit detection for marketplaces, and manufacturing warranty claims; the fastest-growing part of the work and one of the two stated pillars of growth.
- **Technology vertical** — Selling analytics services to technology companies; explicitly the company's largest industry vertical, though it went through sluggish quarters before returning to growth in Q3 FY26 on year-end projects and a price increase at its second-largest account.
- **BFSI / financial services practice** — Analytics work for banking and financial-services clients; the fastest-growing vertical, lifting its share of total revenue by almost 4 percentage points during FY26, driven by a couple of accounts won about a year earlier.
- **CPG and retail practice (including Decision Point)** — Serves consumer packaged goods, beverage, apparel and retail clients with solutions such as revenue growth management (RGM), supply chain and on-shelf availability; boosted by the FY24 acquisition of Decision Point, which management said could take the CPG practice to roughly 20% of company revenue within two years, though the segment was flattish in Q3 FY26.
- **ConnectedView (supply chain solution)** — A packaged supply-chain analytics offering the company markets to clients and prospects; described in FY23 as an early-stage value proposition with about half a dozen live conversations rather than meaningful revenue.
- **Marketing analytics centre of excellence** — A horizontal practice launched in FY24 focused on full-funnel growth marketing and B2B revenue generation, built on years of existing marketing-analytics work and increasingly using gen-AI use cases.
- **Partnership ecosystem** — Go-to-market alliances that bring in leads and joint solutions — IBM Data Services and Neo4j (FY23), NVIDIA's generative-AI tech stack (FY24), and above all Databricks, which management named as the second pillar of growth alongside AI, plus an emerging Anthropic partnership.
- **Geographies** — The US is the dominant market (90%+ of revenue in FY23) with Europe and India built out from FY23 onwards as smaller, later-stage growth markets; delivery is largely offshore from India.
- **Acquisitions and investments** — Inorganic expansion is an explicit part of the strategy — Decision Point was bought in FY24, an investment was made in Healtheon (FY26), and management said it is evaluating further acquisitions of firms with stronger Databricks professional-services credentials.

### The story in depth

LatentView is, at its core, a fast-growing but project-based analytics services company, and almost every strength and weakness in these records traces back to that one fact. The business sits in a genuinely expanding end market — data analytics moving into generative and agentic AI — which scores as one of the two strongest checks in the framework, and the company has been taking share within it: revenue has roughly doubled (2.2x in dollars) since listing, with twelve consecutive quarters of sequential growth and BFSI up more than 80% in FY26. Management is the standout: the same CEO and CFO have fronted all seventeen earnings calls with a consistent multi-year agenda, and they own bad news plainly — the missed 25-30% FY24 growth ambition that landed at 18.9%, a pipeline they themselves called weaker than they wanted, the Europe pullback, the 40-person role rationalisation, and shrinkage at a large account. That candour is why the Corporate Culture pattern is the only outright strong fit in the multibagger screen, and why the Management area of the quality framework scores at the top of its range. The balance sheet backs it up — net cash, one small self-funded acquisition, and continued investment through the FY24 downturn rather than retrenchment.

The trouble is that the same project-based model that lets the company grow fast also denies it the traits that make growth durable. Revenue is won deal by deal on statements of work, with no subscription, licence or maintenance stream, no deferred revenue and no attach rates — which is why the Recurring Revenue pattern is a clear no-fit, and why the Toll Roads and Cost to Replicate patterns fail too: the assets here are people, partner certifications and client relationships, all of which a well-funded entrant could rebuild. That single trait then reappears on the risk side as elevated cyclicality, because analytics budgets are discretionary and clients openly delay or deprioritise the work, exactly as the FY23-FY24 calls describe. Pricing tells the same two-sided story. There is real evidence of pricing power — better realisation on renegotiated contracts and an explicit price increase in the second-largest account — but procurement layers have been entering deals since as far back as August 2022, closures slip, and volumes shrink, so the pattern only earns a partial verdict and the same facts drive the Good Enough Goods risk. The quality framework registers this directly: Competitive Advantage and Industry Structure are the two negative areas, and the framework flags no image- or taste-based pricing power, with margins parked in a 21-24% band.

Concentration and technology work the same double-edged way. Results are still moved by single accounts and a single geography — the technology vertical was about 70% of revenue in Q1 FY25 and the US about 94%, and Q4 FY26 opened with a gap down when one large technology client consolidated vendors — yet the diversification away from that (technology down to roughly 55%, rest-of-world up from 6% to 15%, BFSI growing strongly, plus the Decision Point acquisition lifting CPG/retail) is precisely what earns the partial Market Share Gainers verdict. Likewise, the AI platform shift is both the engine and the threat: solution build-out has genuinely converted into revenue, with 28% of FY26 revenue involving client-visible AI and another 21% under the hood, and partnerships with NVIDIA, Databricks, Microsoft and Anthropic increasingly source and co-fund deals — the basis of the partial Friendly Middleman and Innovation Dominance fits. But those same model labs are the new entrants management addressed defensively in May 2026, reframing them as validation that a services layer is still needed. One clear blemish sits apart from all this: the company has held over Rs.1,100 crores of cash beyond IPO proceeds with no buyback or dividend policy articulated on any call, only vague inorganic intentions. Add lumpy year-to-year growth despite a high average, and the arithmetic lands where it does — 52 for business quality, 40 for pattern fit, 46 for risk safety, a combined 47 out of 100, a Mixed two-star verdict on a business rated Sound for resilience.

Reading it as one story: this is a well-run, honestly managed company riding a real secular wave with plenty of room to grow at roughly $120m of revenue, but without the structural protections — recurring revenue, switching costs, replication barriers — that turn fast growth into compounding. The management and end-market strengths are what keep the rating from being poor; the absence of a moat and the discretionary, concentrated nature of the revenue are what keep it from being good.

## What to watch

- Whether the technology-account shrinkage and vendor consolidation behind the Q4 FY26 gap down spreads to other large clients — the core of the elevated Stakeholder Concentration risk.
- Any move from project work toward a repeatable, contracted revenue stream, which is the single change that would flip the Recurring Revenue no-fit and soften the elevated Cyclicality risk.
- Whether the price increase in the second-largest account repeats elsewhere or procurement pressure wins — this decides if Pricing Power moves from partial to real and eases the Good Enough Goods risk.
- A stated buyback or dividend policy for the Rs.1,100+ crores of surplus cash, which is the sole reason the Distribution to Shareholders check scores negative.
- Whether the Anthropic, Databricks and NVIDIA partnerships keep sourcing deals or the model labs move directly into services — the fork between the Friendly Middleman pattern and the elevated New Entrants risk.
