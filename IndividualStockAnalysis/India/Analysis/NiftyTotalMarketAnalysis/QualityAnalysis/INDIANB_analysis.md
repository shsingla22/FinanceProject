# Indian Bank (INDIANB) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

Indian Bank is an Indian commercial bank, run out of a corporate office in Chennai and listed on the NSE and BSE, that takes deposits from the public and lends the money back out. On the deposit side it gathers current and savings accounts (CASA, about 40% of deposits) plus fixed/term deposits; on the lending side it makes loans to individuals, farmers, small businesses and large companies, with total advances of roughly Rs 6.85 lakh crore as of Q1 FY27. It earns mainly the spread between what it pays depositors (cost of deposits) and what it charges borrowers (yield on advances) — the resulting net interest income was about Rs 26,915 crore in FY26 — topped up by fee income, foreign-exchange income, sale of priority-sector lending certificates, recoveries of previously written-off bad loans, and profits on its bond/treasury portfolio. It operates through a branch network and increasingly through digital channels (about 90% of transactions are digital), is mostly domestic but has some overseas/global business, and management steers the bank against public guidance on deposit growth, loan growth, margins, bad loans and recoveries.

**The parts of the business:**

- **RAM — Retail, Agri and MSME** — The bank's core lending engine to individuals, farmers and small businesses, which management repeatedly calls out as its growth driver at about 61% of the loan book in 2022 rising to 66% by Q1 FY27, growing 14-15% a year.
- **Retail loans** — Home loans, car/auto loans, personal loans and jewel (gold-backed) loans to individual customers; the fastest-growing piece within RAM at roughly 18-19% growth in FY26 and Q1 FY27.
- **Jewel / gold loans** — Loans against gold jewellery, made to both retail and farm customers, which management calls a 'strength area' — it grew about 28% to Rs 1.27 trillion by FY26 and makes up around 78% of the farm credit book.
- **Agriculture lending** — Crop/farm credit, investment credit, allied agri, farm infrastructure and self-help-group lending to rural borrowers; growing in the high teens in most years, though it slowed to about 10% in Q1 FY27 on jewel-loan system issues.
- **MSME lending** — Loans to micro, small and medium businesses — roughly 6 lakh loans and Rs 48,000 crore sanctioned in FY26, growing 16-17% — and historically the segment where the bank sees the most repayment stress; it also carries government-guaranteed ECLGS credit of about Rs 11,000 crore eligible, Rs 5,000 crore disbursed.
- **Corporate lending** — Large-company loans, which shrank or barely grew in some years (down 2% in FY23, up 3% in FY25) but picked up to about 9-11% growth in FY26 and Q1 FY27, with Rs 1.31 trillion sanctioned in FY26 and NBFC exposure that is 99% rated A or better.
- **Deposits — CASA and term** — The bank's funding base: low-cost current and savings accounts, which management calls its main challenge and holds at roughly 40% of deposits, plus higher-cost term and bulk deposits it says it raises cautiously; total deposits grew 12-13% in the latest years.
- **Treasury / investments** — A large bond and securities portfolio (about Rs 1.85 lakh crore in 2022, split across HTM, AFS and FVTPL) that earns interest — yield of about 7.15% in FY25 — and generates trading profits or mark-to-market losses depending on interest rates; management flags it as a swing factor in earnings.
- **Non-interest income (fees, forex, PSLC, recoveries)** — Fee income from customers, foreign exchange, penal charges, sale of priority-sector lending certificates (Rs 627 crore earned in one year, amortised over four quarters) and cash recovered from bad debts already written off — recoveries alone were Rs 6,651 crore in FY26.
- **Digital banking** — Mobile, internet and UPI banking, debit and credit cards, POS terminals and QR codes, plus digital lending 'journeys' — 90% of transactions are now digital, with 175 lakh mobile banking users and Rs 81,250 crore of digital business in one year.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## The verdict: Weak — 33 out of 100 ★☆☆☆☆

In one breath: the quality framework finds an average business today (50/100); it strongly fits 1 of the 11 patterns long-term winners share; the risk review found 3 high risks (Cyclicality, Government Dependency…). Weighing those together gives 33 out of 100 — weak.

**The exact arithmetic:** Overall = 45% × 50 (business quality) + 30% × 15 (multibagger fit) + 25% × 24 (risk safety) = 33 out of 100 → Weak (1 star).

- **Business quality (50/100):** The 34-check quality framework scored the business -0.01 on its −2 (poor) to +2 (excellent) scale, with 82% of checks backed by evidence; mapped onto 0–100 that is 50 points.
- **Multibagger fit (15/100):** the foundation test did not pass (+0); 1 pattern fit strongly (Corporate Culture) (+15) → 15 of 100.
- **Risk safety (24/100):** started from a clean 100; 3 high risks (Cyclicality, Government Dependency, Good Enough Goods) (−60); 4 worth watching (−16) → 24 of 100.

### The story in depth

Indian Bank comes out of the three reviews with a combined score of 33 out of 100, a Weak (1-star) rating, built from 50 for business quality, 15 for multibagger pattern fit and 24 for risk safety. The one thing that scores well everywhere is the people running it. Candor and Disciplined stewards are the only checks scoring +2, Management and Capital Allocation are the two strongest quality areas at about 0.67 each, and Corporate Culture is the single STRONG FIT among eleven patterns — the same evidence in all three places: guidance set and then beaten across 17 calls, bad loans guided to roughly 7% in Aug'22 from 8.13% and actually delivered at 1.86% by Q1FY27, FY26 recoveries of Rs.6,651 crore against Rs.5,500-6,500 crore promised, an admission of the one parameter missed, a refusal to quote unverified expected-loss numbers, and voluntary extra provisioning (Rs.310 crore buffer, Rs.1,000 crore floating provision).

The trouble is that the same discipline that earns those marks is also the clearest proof the bank has nothing to price with. Walking away from Rs.6,000 crore of thinly priced loans in a quarter and staying out of bulk deposits at 7.7-8.0% reads as prudence under Disciplined stewards, but the identical facts make Pricing Power a NO FIT, sit behind the -1 on Pricing, Mix and Volume (margin down 17 basis points in FY26, fee growth from volume not rate), behind the -1 on Intangible benefits and Barriers to entry, and behind the HIGH RISK verdict on Good Enough Goods, where home-loan rates are stuck at 7.15-7.20% and 'no bank is taking the lead'. Competitive Advantage scores a flat 0.0 and Industry Structure -0.5 for the same reason. The clean-up of bad loans works the same double way: falling bad loans and a 98% provision cover support the PARTIAL scores on Market Share Gainers and Low Price Plus, yet Cyclicality is rated HIGH RISK precisely because a 0.31% credit cost means today's profit sits at a kind point in the credit cycle. Return on Capital scores -1.0 because the roughly 19-20% return on equity comes off a roughly 1.3% return on assets — that is borrowed money doing the work, not the assets themselves.

Where the bank spends money is sensible but does not build anything a rival could not copy. Growth capex (300 new branches over three years, mid-corporate centres, gold-loan branches, an overseas book up 27%) is internally funded and earns +1s, and advances growing 12-14% with jewel loans up about 28% to Rs.1.27 trillion give the Market Share Gainers pattern a partial tick — but it is network extension, and no market-share target is stated. The digital build is the same story from a different angle: digital business volumes rising from Rs.81,250 crore in FY24 to Rs.2.72 lakh crore in FY26 with 10-plus AI platforms earns +1 on Research and development and softens the Technological Innovation risk to WATCH, yet because it is bought through 160+ fintech partners, Innovation Dominance is only PARTIAL and Cost to Replicate is a NO FIT. Two constraints sit outside management's hands entirely: Government Dependency is HIGH RISK, with policy setting yields, draft liquidity norms costing 4-5% and the final expected-loss rules carrying a Rs.3,000-3,500 crore transition hit; and low-cost deposits have slipped to about 39.7% against a 40% target for behavioural reasons, which is both the cost-culture gap the Low Price Plus pattern flags and the Shifting Consumer Preferences risk. Resilience is recorded as UNKNOWN. In short: a well-run bank selling a commodity in a policy-set industry — good custodianship, no moat.

*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**What the calls show:** Earnings across the 17 calls track the credit and rate cycle: GNPA fell from 8.13% (Q1FY23, with a Rs22,000cr COVID-restructured book, SREI/Future/sugar slippages) to 1.86% by Q1FY27, with credit cost down to 0.31% for FY26 — i.e. current profits sit at a benign point management itself names, while treasury income swings sharply with bond yields (Rs605cr to Rs105cr in FY23; FY27 plan assumes only Rs600-700cr) and NIM compressed from 3.53% to 3.10-3.25% guidance. > "Since last five years, we have been going through a very good phase, I will say benign asset quality phase." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Granular flow-type retail/agri/MSME book (RAM 66% of loans, jewel loans Rs1.32 trillion) rather than a single commodity or customer-capex exposure; recoveries have exceeded slippages consistently*

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** As a PSU bank, pricing, provisioning and capital are set by policy: RBI repo/EBLR transmission drives yields, draft LCR norms cut LCR by 4-5%, the final ECL guidelines impose a Rs3,000-3,500cr transition hit, and business is routed through government schemes (ECLGS Rs11,000cr eligible/Rs5,000cr disbursed, PSLC amortisation, SHG, priority-sector agri gold loans). Government accounts appear directly in SMA/slippage disclosures and an analyst flagged the low free float from government holding.

> "We expect the ECL transition impact to be around Rs.3,000-3,500 crore." — *management, on an earnings call*

## What to watch

- Credit cost drifting up from FY26's 0.31% or bad loans rising off 1.86% — the Cyclicality HIGH RISK says today's profit rests on a benign point in the cycle, and a turn there removes the main support under the Market Share Gainers and Low Price Plus partial fits.
- The size and timing of the Rs.3,000-3,500 crore expected-loss transition hit and the 4-5% liquidity-ratio cut named under Government Dependency (HIGH RISK) — these land regardless of how well the bank is run.
- Low-cost current and savings deposits versus the 40% target (about 39.7% now) — the cost gap management itself admits missing, which is both the Low Price Plus shortfall and the Shifting Consumer Preferences risk; a recovery here would be the first sign the funding franchise is defensible.
- Any evidence of the bank leading on price rather than walking away from it — the Rs.6,000 crore of loans shed and the 7.15-7.20% home-loan stalemate are what make Pricing Power a NO FIT and Good Enough Goods a HIGH RISK; leadership on rate would change both.
- Whether net interest margin stops compressing (down 17 basis points in FY26, around 3.2%) with return on assets near 1.3% — the -1 on Asset turn and the -1.0 Return on Capital area mean the roughly 19-20% return on equity currently depends on leverage, not on the earning power of the assets.
- Whether management continues to keep guidance and volunteer unflattering detail — the Candor and Disciplined stewards +2 scores and the lone STRONG FIT on Corporate Culture are the only things holding the score as high as 33; a missed guidance run or a leadership change would remove them.
