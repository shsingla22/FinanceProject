# Eicher Motors Ltd. (EICHERMOT) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: AUTOMOBILE*

Eicher Motors Limited (EICHERMOT) is an India-listed vehicle maker whose main business is Royal Enfield, which designs and sells mid-size motorcycles — 350cc, 450cc and 650cc models such as the Classic, Meteor, Hunter, Himalayan and Guerrilla — to retail riders in India and in over 70 countries abroad, and which the calls describe as the oldest motorcycle manufacturer in continuous production, celebrating 125 years in FY26. It makes money mainly by manufacturing and selling these motorcycles through dealers (plus spare parts, apparel and accessories), and it dominates its niche, holding roughly 85%-89% of India's middleweight (above-250cc) segment; in FY26 it sold 12.27 lakh motorcycles and reported consolidated revenue of INR 23,408 crores, EBITDA of INR 5,785 crores and profit after tax of INR 5,515 crores. Separately, it owns half of VE Commercial Vehicles (VECV), a 50-50 joint venture with Sweden's Volvo Group that builds and sells Eicher and Volvo trucks and buses in India and for export; EML does not book VECV's sales as its own revenue but takes its share of VECV's profit (about INR 183 crores in one quarter), and management says EML and VECV combined crossed INR 50,000 crores of revenue in FY26. Production is based in India — Royal Enfield's plants include Cheyyar in Tamil Nadu, now being expanded from 1.4 million to 2 million units a year, with a new greenfield plant planned in Andhra Pradesh — while sales reach India, Brazil (its largest and fastest-growing overseas market), Latin America, Europe, the UK, Thailand, Nepal and beyond.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Royal Enfield (motorcycles)** — The core business and by far the biggest profit engine: it makes and sells mid-size 350cc/450cc/650cc motorcycles in India and internationally, selling 12.27 lakh units in FY26 (1.1 million domestic, 120,634 export) with an 88.9% exit share of India's mid-sized segment.
- **Royal Enfield international business** — The overseas arm of the motorcycle business, selling through distributors and exclusive stores in 70+ countries with 3,000+ service touch points; exports grew 20% in FY26 to about 120,000 units, with Brazil (up ~71%), Argentina, Thailand, Nepal and Europe called out as key markets.
- **Allied businesses (apparel, accessories, lifestyle)** — Riding gear, clothing and lifestyle products sold alongside the bikes — apparel alone grew 21% in FY26 — plus branded collaborations such as the Vallon Moto Aviators eyewear range; a smaller but steadily growing add-on to motorcycle sales.
- **Flying Flea (electric)** — Royal Enfield's new 'City Plus' electric mobility brand, whose first product, the Flying Flea C6, launched in Bangalore in April 2026 with a deliberate city-by-city rollout; still tiny and at the start-up stage, but management frames it as the company's entry into electric.
- **VE Commercial Vehicles (VECV) — 50:50 JV with Volvo Group** — An 18-year-old joint venture making and selling Eicher and Volvo trucks and buses in India; it crossed 100,000 units sold for the first time in FY26, and EML reports only its share of VECV's profit (e.g. INR 175 crores in Q1 FY25, INR 183 crores in Q3 FY26) rather than VECV's revenue, which ran around INR 5,000 crores a quarter at roughly 7.7% EBITDA margin.
- **VECV — heavy-duty trucks** — Large trucks sold under the Eicher brand, running around 4,700 units in a first quarter with roughly 8.4% market share — the segment where VECV is a challenger rather than a leader.
- **VECV — light and medium-duty trucks** — Smaller haulage trucks, about 7,800-8,300 units in a first quarter and a commanding ~34% market share, making this VECV's strongest position; a small commercial vehicle programme was also under customer trials.
- **VECV — bus division** — Buses including Eicher electric city buses (the first delivered to Chandigarh) and premium Volvo coaches such as the 9600 platform; volumes of roughly 5,200 units in a first quarter, a record for the division at that time.
- **VECV — exports** — Truck and bus shipments outside India, a small but fast-growing line at about 1,192 units in Q1 FY25, up 57.5% year on year.
- **VE Connected Systems** — A joint venture VECV formed with iTriangle Infotech to build connected-vehicle and digital telematics services for Eicher truck customers; an early-stage services add-on, not yet a meaningful earnings contributor.
- **Proposed vehicle financing JV with Volvo (Volvo Financial Services India)** — Announced in May 2026: EML intends to invest up to INR 750 crores for a 50% stake in a financing, leasing and financial-services JV with Volvo that already has INR 1,806 crores of assets under management, acting as the captive lender to Eicher, Volvo and Royal Enfield customers in India.

### The story in depth

Eicher Motors comes out of all three reviews as a genuinely strong business, rated 79 out of 100 (four stars), built on 45% business quality (79), 30% multibagger pattern fit (79) and 25% risk safety (80), with overall resilience judged sound. The clearest strength is what the customer gets and what the customer pays for: the customer-benefits area scores a perfect 2.0, and after direct input costs the company keeps ₹44 of every ₹100 of sales — money customers hand over willingly for a name with 125 years of continuous production, ranked the world's third strongest automobile brand and first in the dealer satisfaction survey. Money is handled with the same care: capital allocation scores 1.67, with a ₹958 crore brownfield expansion at Cheyyar lifting capacity from 1.4 million to 2 million units and a new greenfield plant in Andhra Pradesh, both funded from the company's own cash at roughly 26% operating margins, helped by the unusual fact that cash comes back from customers 16 days before suppliers need to be paid. Management scores 1.5 on clean internal succession and a decade-plus of sticking to one narrow plan. The scores are noticeably thinner elsewhere — return on capital and industry structure at 0.67 and growth at 0.83 — so the quality here is concentrated in the brand and the way the business is run rather than spread evenly across every dimension.

The multibagger screen picks up the same traits under different names. Corporate culture and cost to replicate are the two strongest fits, and both trace back to the identical assets: 125 years of production, a riding community of 40,000-plus at Motoverse and One Ride across 66 countries, over 3,000 service and dealer touchpoints, and a management team that made promises in 2022 and then publicly reconciled them to a 12-quarter record of 14% volume, 18% revenue, 20% gross profit and 22% operating profit compound growth. Brand strength, innovation dominance and market share gains are the next tier, and again the underlying facts overlap with the quality checks — the same yearly launch cadence that earns the research-and-development check (Hunter 350, the Sherpa 450 platform, Guerrilla, Bear 650, the Flying Flea electric bike) is what the innovation pattern is scoring, and the same premium positioning shows up as share moving from 36% of the above-125cc market to an 88.9% exit share in mid-size, alongside the truck and bus joint venture lifting light and medium duty share from 27.7% to 34.5%. Four patterns only partly fit, and one of them is worth pausing on: pricing power is rated partial not because customers resist the price, but because management deliberately chose in 2022 to chase volume and absolute profit rather than price, and the recent margin gain came from operating leverage rather than raising prices. That same choice is what the culture pattern praises. Two patterns do not fit at all — there is no prescribing professional steering buyers, and a motorcycle is a large discretionary purchase rather than a small unavoidable input, which is precisely why demand has to be earned afresh every year.

The risk review turns several of these strengths around and looks at their shadow side. Five channels sit on watch. Fashion risk is the mirror image of the brand and advertising strengths — demand rests on the bike staying desirable, credited to buzz, community events, cultural collaborations and search interest, which is a softer foundation than a contract; the offsetting comfort is a wide model range rather than one big bet, plus repeat spending on spares. Technological innovation is the shadow of the heritage itself: a franchise built on retro petrol motorcycles now faces a live electric transition in both businesses, with the Flying Flea platform launched in Bangalore and electric city buses on the truck side, and the answer so far is the same steady launch cadence that the innovation pattern rewards. New entrants — Harley-Davidson and Triumph in the core middleweight segment — pressured the category and made management visibly defensive, and the response was new formats rather than discounting, which is why the good-enough-goods risk stays low. Cyclicality attaches mainly to the truck and bus joint venture, openly described in cycle language and tied to government and fleet spending, and it is sharpened by the fact that the company is committing growth capital right now — the capex strength and the cyclicality risk are the same decision seen from two sides, softened by parts and accessories revenue growing 14% to 32% and by capacity added in steps as demand appears. Government dependency runs through goods and services tax changes, US tariffs swinging between roughly 42% and 18% on an important export market, and bus tender timing, which touches the international expansion the quality framework credits. The three low-rated risks are all places where the trend runs toward the company: buyers are millions of individuals across 70-plus countries with no single customer that matters, premiumisation is described as the only growing part of the Indian motorcycle market, and the premium is holding rather than eroding.

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT

## What to watch

- New entrants risk: whether the 88.9% mid-size exit share holds against Harley-Davidson and Triumph without resorting to discounting, which would undercut the ₹44-per-₹100 margin behind the customer-benefits score.

- Pricing power (partial fit) and return on capital (0.67): whether margins keep improving on operating leverage and cost work, or the volume-first rebalance leaves returns capped.
- Government dependency risk: how the US tariff swing between roughly 42% and 18% and future tax changes affect the export build-out that the geographic expansion check rewards.
