# ASK Automotive (ASKAUTOLTD) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

ASK Automotive makes and sells auto parts in India, overwhelmingly for two-wheelers (motorcycles and scooters) — its results track two-wheeler production volumes reported by SIAM, and management repeatedly benchmarks growth against that industry. It earns money by supplying components to vehicle makers (OEMs, described as "our prestigious OEMs", including in the fast-growing EV two-wheeler space) and by selling the same kinds of parts as replacement products through a large dealer network in India's independent aftermarket, where a GST cut from 28% to 18% is helping it take share from grey-market and counterfeit sellers. Its three named product segments are Advanced Braking Systems, Aluminium Lightweighting Precision Solutions and Safety Control Cables; consolidated income was about Rs 3,005 crore in FY24, and by FY26 EBITDA was Rs 551 crore at a 13.1% margin with EPS of Rs 15.08. Manufacturing is entirely India-based across multiple plants (Manesar/Haryana head office, plus a large new plant at Karoli in Rajasthan and one in Bangalore serving southern OEMs), with a modest export business of roughly Rs 141-147 crore a year.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Advanced Braking System** — Braking parts such as brake shoes and brake pads for two-wheelers, where management says it holds the market leadership position (roughly 10-12% share in brake pads specifically) and is expanding capacity toward 32 crore brake shoes and pads; revenue grew 7% in FY24, 16% in FY25 and 17% in FY26.
- **Aluminium Lightweighting Precision Solutions** — Aluminium castings/precision parts that make vehicles lighter and help with thermal management, sold to Indian and global OEMs; this is the fastest-growing of the three segments (up 29% in FY24, 28% in FY25, 30% in FY26) and its profitability swings with aluminium alloy prices, which are passed through to customers.
- **Safety Control Cable** — Control cables used in vehicles; the smallest of the three named segments by the tone of management commentary, growing 36% in FY24, 14% in FY25 and 14% in FY26.
- **Wheel Assembly business (exited)** — A low-margin assembly business the company deliberately shrank after asking the customer for two years to move it elsewhere — it cost about Rs 300 crore of FY26 revenue but added roughly 80 basis points to margins, and from 1 April 2026 this revenue is nil.
- **Independent aftermarket (replacement parts)** — Selling parts to end users through ASK's established dealer network rather than to vehicle makers; it grew 24.7% in FY26 and management sees it gaining further share from grey-market operators and duplicators after the GST rate cut.
- **EV segment** — Supplying components for electric two-wheelers; revenue was Rs 120 crore in FY24 (up 133%), and management says its share grows in line with an EV industry that is still under 5% of total two-wheeler volumes.
- **Exports** — Overseas sales of the same components, at Rs 147 crore in FY24 and FY25 and Rs 141 crore in FY26 — a small slice that has stalled on tariffs, geopolitical tension and supply-chain and logistics costs, with a Ford order pending.
- **ASK Fras-le joint venture (commercial vehicles)** — A 49%-owned JV covering the commercial-vehicle business, which turned around from a Rs 6 crore loss share in FY23 to a Rs 16.5 crore profit share in FY24.
- **AISIN joint venture (passenger-car aftermarket)** — A JV signed in April 2024 with Japan's AISIN, a top-10 global tier-one supplier, in which ASK holds 51%, to market and sell passenger-car components through ASK's independent aftermarket dealer network; the product range was unveiled at Bharat Mobility Global Expo 2025 and launched in April 2025.
- **HPDC alloy wheels (new initiative)** — A planned two-wheeler alloy-wheel line using high-pressure die casting, with technical tie-ups with LIOHO of Taiwan and Kyushu Yanagawa Seiki of Japan; capacity is built and the product was still under testing as of the FY25 call, positioned as a first-mover advantage versus India's usual gravity die-cast wheels.
- **Captive solar power** — Not a revenue line but a cost initiative: a 9.9 MW solar plant at Sirsa, Haryana (about Rs 48 crore, fully operational since April 2025) plus a second 11.55 MW plant in Bikaner, Rajasthan due in Q2 FY27, both for the company's own factory consumption.

### The story in depth

ASK Automotive earns its Strong rating (70 out of 100) mainly on two things: how it is run and what it earns on the money it puts to work. Management scores highest of all quality areas (1.17), and return on capital next (1.33). The record behind those scores is concrete — operating margin up from 9.6% to 13.1%, return on capital employed up from 23.6% to 26.9%, debt down from 0.42x to 0.38x equity and around one year of profit, all while spending heavily on new plants (Rs 490cr at Karoli, Rs 500cr in FY26, Rs 400cr planned for FY27) paid for largely out of its own cash rather than borrowing. Every rupee of assets throws off Rs 1.63 of sales, and only about 18 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. Management also tells it straight — stripping out pass-through effects to show real growth, owning up to self-inflicted losses and missed export targets — which is why the Corporate Culture pattern is a strong fit: a promise of 100 basis points of margin improvement made in May 2024 came in at 193 basis points. The Market Share Gainers pattern is the other strong fit, with ten straight quarters of growing faster than two-wheeler industry production and FY26 revenue up 20.1% against an industry up 11.8%.

The uncomfortable part is that the same position that produces those returns also caps them. ASK sells parts to a handful of Indian two-wheeler makers, and those customers negotiate hard. Management says plainly it has taken no price increases; aluminium cost is passed through month by month, and even that slipped in the fourth quarter of FY26 at a cost of Rs 5cr. Wage inflation is passed on rather than priced for. So both the Pricing Power and Toll Roads patterns fail outright, and the two weakest quality areas are competitive advantage and industry structure (both 0.5). The margin gains came from a better sales mix and bigger scale, not from charging more — which is also why the Low Price Plus pattern is only a likely fit: the cost discipline is real (captive solar, scale at the new plants, roughly two rupees of sales per rupee of new plant) but it is discipline the customers demand, not a strategy ASK chose. That same customer power shows up directly as a risk: one relationship carried around Rs 300cr of revenue, close to a tenth of FY25 sales, and its unwinding dominated three consecutive quarterly discussions — though in that case ASK asked to be released from the low-margin work and gained 80 basis points of margin by walking away, which is the same independent, long-term streak that had it building capacity a year ahead of demand and persisting through repeated testing delays on new alloy wheels.

The second growth engine, the independent aftermarket, is the clearest case of one trait driving both a pattern and a risk. Aftermarket sales grew 24.7% in FY26 by winning share back from grey-market operators and copycats after the GST cut removed a ten-point price handicap — that is the Share donators strength and part of the market-share story. But it is the same fact that makes Good Enough Goods the one elevated risk: the products are brake shoes and pads where the mechanic or dealer picks the part, buyers barely know the brand, and cheap substitutes are always available; before the tax change ASK was structurally uncompetitive there. It also explains why Brand Strength and Friendly Middleman rate only partial — the ASK name is worth copying, but there is no advertising spend, no premium pricing and no trade training programme behind it. Policy giveth here, which is why government dependency sits on watch even with no government customers: emissions rules crushed volumes in FY23 and the GST cut lifted them in FY26. Elsewhere the risk picture is calm and the overall resilience reads sound — the shift from petrol to electric two-wheelers is neutral to positive, since ASK already supplies about 80% of the organised electric two-wheeler market with more aluminium content per vehicle, demand is functional and repeat-purchase rather than fashion-driven, and new electric entrants arrive as customers. The honest caveats are that the technology behind the newer products is licensed in from partners in Taiwan and Japan rather than invented, and that die casting itself is replicable — the barriers are the multi-year safety validation cycles and 18 plants sited next to customers, not the process.

*"Winning customers from rivals"*

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** Promises made in old calls are delivered in later ones — the May-2024 pledge of +100bps margin became +193bps in FY25 and guidance was beaten outright; the balance sheet stayed conservative (D/E 0.42x→0.38x, debt ≈1x EBITDA, CRISIL upgrade to AA), the family leadership is long-tenured with an orderly Rathee succession, and bad news is disclosed promptly and precisely (Rs.300 Cr wheel-assembly revenue loss flagged in advance, exports missed target, a self-admitted Rs.5 Cr pricing loss, and 'I don't want to give a wrong data to you' when he didn't know).

> "There was a conscious loss by us of Rs. 5 Cr because every day we cannot increase the prices" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Low Price Plus — LIKELY FIT
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and the only computable check supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** Real cost culture with itemised actions — captive solar (9.9MW Sirsa live, 11.55MW Bikaner coming), economies of scale from Karoli/Bangalore ramp-ups, 2:1 asset turns and margin up from 9.6% to 13.1% — but this is OEM-mandated cost discipline, not a low-price-plus-quality consumer proposition, and scale is regional rather than industry-dominating.

> "Improvement in margins... driven by better economies of scale, improved volumes, better product mix... and continued focus on cost optimization" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹34 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 1.6× a year with 23% returns on capital — thin margins made up on volume, the low-price-winner profile.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 20% to 26% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** In the independent aftermarket ASK competes directly against 'grey market operators and duplicators' — cheap substitutes for brake shoes and pads where the mechanic/dealer picks the part and end-customer brand awareness is low; ASK was structurally uncompetitive against them until a tax change closed the price gap, which is exactly the good-enough mechanism. The exited wheel assembly line was itself commoditized, 'very low margin' work, and brake pad share is only 10-12%.

> "a lot of grey market, we are giving a tough fight to the grey market operators, which are prevalent in the country" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: GST 2.0 removed the 10-point price handicap and ASK is now taking share back; market leadership and OEM qualification in advanced braking; deliberate exit from low-value-added work and shift toward machined/value-added aluminium and licensed HPDC technology.*

- ⚠️ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs fell from about 36% to 33% — pricing is being competed away at the gross level.
- ✅ Operating margin (13%) remains near its historical peak (13%) — no collapse in the premium.
- ✅ Operating margin held (about 13% then 12%) — no drift down under competition.

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Revenue tracks two-wheeler production volumes (an operations/flow linkage, not customer capex), but the calls repeatedly show demand swinging with rural income, monsoon, OBD-2/GST policy and the industry only regaining its FY19 peak in FY26; aluminium alloy prices are a direct commodity input that management says distorts reported revenue and EBITDA margin each quarter, and capex has run Rs.285-500cr a year ahead of demand.

> "The phenomenal increase in aluminium alloy prices in particular have affected our industry." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/consumable demand — components consumed in OEM production plus a fast-growing independent aftermarket (+24.7% in FY26); aluminium is a month-on-month pass-through so absolute EBITDA is protected; countercyclical habit of building plants 'one year in advance'.*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 2 of the last 7 years with swings of ±15 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.

## What to watch

- Aftermarket share gains reversing — the +24.7% FY26 growth rests on the GST cut closing the price gap to grey-market copies; if that advantage fades, both the Share donators strength and the elevated Good Enough Goods risk turn against the story.
- The ten-quarter streak of beating two-wheeler industry production breaking, which is the entire evidence base for the Market Share Gainers strong fit and for the Cyclicality watch, given demand swings with rural income and monsoon and the industry only just regained its FY19 peak.
- New alloy wheel revenue missing the guided Rs 90-100cr in FY27 and Rs 220cr in FY28 — the programme has already seen repeated testing delays, and it is the main proof point behind the partial Innovation Dominance fit.
- Return on capital stalling below 26.9% or new plants failing to deliver the stated two-rupees-of-sales-per-rupee-of-assets, with Rs 400cr more capex planned for FY27; the headline cash return on capital is only 15%, one of the weaker individual checks.
- Another large customer relationship shrinking the way the Rs 300cr wheel assembly business did, or the aluminium pass-through lagging again as it did for Rs 5cr in Q4 FY26 — both go to the Stakeholder Concentration watch and the absent Pricing Power.
