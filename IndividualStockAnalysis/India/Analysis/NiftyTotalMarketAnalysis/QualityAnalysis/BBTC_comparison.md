### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 2 worsened, 2 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Standalone earnings are dominated by agri-commodity plantations (tea, coffee) where output swings with weather and profit swings with realised price, and by auto components tied to the automobile cycle; both calls explain results almost entirely through crop volumes and price per kilo. FY23 tea crop fell 4.34 lakh kg on 'excessive rain in the main cropping months' while coffee realisation jumped from INR186 to INR280/kg, and Q1 FY23 swung to an operating loss of INR15.29 crores. (The long view had said: The standalone business is dominated by agri-commodities where management explains results purely through per-kilo price realisations and weather-driven crop swings — tea production fell 41.72 to 37.38 lakh kg on excessive rain, coffee realisation jumped from INR186 to INR280 a kilo, and the FY22 coffee crop was skipped entirely due to 'uncertain market conditions and disproportionate high fruit prices'; the associate GoAir added acute aviation cyclicality culminating in insolvency. Across both AGMs revenue and operating results swing violently (standalone operating loss of INR15.29 cr in Q1FY23; consolidated PAT of +808 cr flipping to -533 cr).)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The single largest value event of the window was supplier concentration at associate Go Airlines: sole engine supplier Pratt & Whitney failed to deliver, grounding ~50% of the fleet, triggering lessor repossessions, NCLT insolvency and an INR1,865.65 crores impairment that turned a INR808 crores consolidated profit into a INR533 crores loss and produced an audit qualification. Separately, BBTC's consolidated economics are almost wholly the Britannia stake (INR16,303 crores consolidated sales vs INR334 crores standalone revenue). (The long view had said: The single largest value event in the period was a supplier-concentration failure at the associate: GoAir's dependence on one engine maker grounded half the fleet and forced BBTC subsidiaries to book INR1,865.65 crores of impairment. Separately, shareholders openly treat BBTC as a proxy for one asset — its Britannia stake ('we are a good holding company for Britannia, which we get returns'), so both earnings and market value rest on a very narrow base.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Technological Innovation (NO SIGNAL); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL); Fashion Risk (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: New Entrants (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

*Not comparable: Government Dependency (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 8 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
