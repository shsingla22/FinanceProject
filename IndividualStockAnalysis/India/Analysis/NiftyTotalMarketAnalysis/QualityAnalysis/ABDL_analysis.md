# Allied Blenders (ABDL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Allied Blenders and Distillers (ABD) makes and sells alcoholic spirits in India — mainly whisky, plus brandy, rum, vodka and gin — under what the industry calls IMFL (Indian-made foreign liquor), with 17 brands including its flagship Officer's Choice and its fast-growing ICONiQ White. Because liquor in India is regulated state by state, ABD produces locally and sells locally: at listing it ran one distillery in Telangana and 32 bottling units across the country, supplying roughly 79,000 retail outlets plus bars and restaurants, giving over 90% distribution width. It earns money by selling cases of spirits to state-government wholesalers or private wholesalers depending on the market, to the defence forces (CSD and paramilitary), and through exports — it calls itself India's largest IMFL exporter, present in 39 countries as of Q1 FY27, a model management describes as asset-light, higher-margin and less working-capital-hungry than the domestic business. Scale is roughly 31.7 million cases and Rs. 7,675 crores of total income in FY24 on the pre-listing basis, and Rs. 3,949 crores of consolidated income from operations with Rs. 568 crores EBITDA in FY26, with profitability improving as the mix shifts to pricier brands and as in-house bottle and spirit production replaces bought-in inputs.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Mass Premium (and others) — led by Officer's Choice** — The company's original, cheapest and highest-volume whisky/brandy business, built around Officer's Choice, which management calls India's leading Mass Premium whisky and its top exported brand; it earns gross margins of over 40%, is described as the main cash-flow generator, and now accounts for a little over half of volumes but a shrinking share of value as premium brands grow.
- **Prestige & Above (P&A)** — The mid-to-upper mainstream tier — Officer's Choice Blue and ICONiQ White in Prestige, Sterling Reserve B7 and Srishti in semi-premium — which is the company's declared growth engine: its share of volume rose from about 25% in FY18 to 37% in FY24, 47.2% in FY26 and 48.2% in Q1 FY27, contributing 59.3% of value in that quarter.
- **ICONiQ White** — A single Prestige whisky launched in September 2022 that has become the company's standout brand — 10.7 million cases in FY26 and 3.1 million cases in Q1 FY27 (over 1 million a month, up 33.8% year-on-year) — repeatedly cited as the world's fastest-growing millionaire whisky brand and sold in 10 international markets.
- **ABD Maestro — Super-Premium to Luxury** — A newer, deliberately asset-light 'build, buy and partner' platform that in its first year (FY26) assembled 10 brands across whisky, gin, vodka and rum (including Arthaus and Zoya Gin); it is small in volume but strategically important, now in over 5,500 premium outlets, six international markets and four travel-retail/duty-free locations.
- **Non-whisky categories (brandy, rum, vodka, gin)** — A modest complement to a portfolio that was about 97% whisky at FY24, including entry-level Officer's Choice brandy, the premium southern-India brandy Kyron, opportunistic rum brands in specific states, CLASS 21 vodka and the Zoya luxury gin launched in January 2024.
- **International business / exports** — Sales of ABD brands into overseas markets, expanded from 14 countries at IPO to 30 by late 2025 and 39 by Q1 FY27, described as asset-light with higher profitability and better working-capital efficiency than domestic sales, with the GCC as the base and Africa targeted at 1 million cases by FY28.
- **Manufacturing and backward integration** — The plant network (33 facilities at IPO — one Telangana distillery plus 32 bottling units, and an R&D/blending centre at Aurangabad) plus a capex programme to make its own inputs: a Rs. 115 crore PET bottle plant in Rangapur commissioned in September 2025 (600 million bottles a year, already EBITDA-accretive), a malt distillery due in H1 FY27, and ENA distillation capacity in Maharashtra, together expected to add roughly 300 basis points to EBITDA margin by FY28 and another 100 by FY29.
- **Defence and institutional channel** — Supply to the armed forces through CSD and paramilitary canteens, described as one of the channels supporting ICONiQ White's scale alongside domestic retail and exports.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Allied Blenders is the country's #3 spirits maker, and almost everything good in these records traces back to one asset: reach. The company holds one of only four pan-India licences alongside Diageo, Pernod and Radico, runs 32 state bottling units placed to serve each state efficiently, sells through about 79,000 outlets at over 90% width of distribution, fields roughly 500 feet-on-street, and has added 5,500 premium touchpoints for its luxury range. That reach is what lets it serve government wholesale, private trade, defence and export channels at once, and it is what turns new products into real volume: ICONiQ White went from 2.3 million cases in FY24 to 10.7 million in FY26, ten ABD Maestro brands launched in FY26, a 34-year single malt arrived, and Zoya gin's flavour variants are now 30% of that brand's volume. The multibagger screen calls Innovation Dominance a STRONG FIT for exactly this reason, and rates Market Share Gainers a likely fit on the evidence: premium-and-above salience climbed from 39.7% to 48.2% of volumes, Officer's Choice gained share even as the mass-premium segment shrank, and exports went from 14 countries to 39, reaching Rs 235 crore at better-than-domestic profitability. Management behaviour supports the picture — growth is entirely organic, capex sits inside pre-stated debt limits (net debt to EBITDA 1.7x against a below-2x promise), assets were commissioned on the dates guided, and bad news gets quantified rather than buried, including a Rs 24 crore supply-chain hit with a full bridge and a Rs 400 crore Telangana overdue disclosed on request.

The awkward part is that the same facts that create the strengths also create the biggest risks. Because liquor is a state subject with state-run wholesale, the distribution network that is hardest to replicate is also the network most exposed to policy: state committees set prices, which is why the screen downgrades Pricing Power to only a LIKELY FIT — realisation per case does rise (up 5.1% in FY24, 3.8% in Q2 FY26) and gross margin reached 48.2% in Q4 FY26, but that comes from mix and input costs rather than the company's own ability to raise prices, and the Telangana price increase described as the single biggest margin lever has been merely 'awaited' across calls. Government Dependency is rated HIGH RISK for this reason. Concentration compounds it: Telangana is simultaneously the largest market at roughly 10 of 32 million cases in FY24, about 65% of PET consumption, the site of the sole distillery and the new PET plant — and the customer whose non-payment stalled growth in the second half of FY24. That is why Stakeholder Concentration is also HIGH RISK. The innovation engine carries the same double edge: ICONiQ White is both the proof of Innovation Dominance and, at 10.7 million cases, the single brand growth now leans on, while three of the four millionaire brands (Officer's Choice Blue, Sterling Reserve B7 and B10) have lost consumer salience and need brand resets and repackaging. That is precisely why Brand Strength scores only PARTIAL and Fashion Risk sits on WATCH, and why the eroding mass-premium base — still over half of volumes, facing cheaper local brands, with management now 'driving growth at value price points' — makes Good Enough Goods an ELEVATED risk. Maharashtra's new state-created MML category, priced aggressively and blamed for a roughly 20% drop in IMFL volumes in that state, shows how quickly a policy change can act like a new competitor; the response so far has been lobbying rather than a competitive answer, which is why Cost to Replicate also lands at PARTIAL.

What is missing from the story is money coming back out. The quality framework scores Return on Capital at 0.0 and Capital Allocation at 0.5, and the single worst check is the working capital cycle: about 154 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. The business model itself offers no help here — the screen finds NO FIT on Recurring Revenue (outright case sales into state wholesale, no subscriptions, renewals or deferred revenue to fund the business), NO FIT on Toll Roads (a branded end-product that must petition governments for price rises, not a critical input priced without negotiation), and NO FIT on Forward Integrators, since the capex runs the other way into PET bottling, extra neutral alcohol and malt distilling. Backward integration is genuinely value-creating — a Rs 115 crore in-house PET plant expected to add over Rs 30 crore to gross margin, plus market-bottle reuse and value engineering, which is what earns the full marks on capex-for-growth and half-marks on Low Price Plus — but it consumes cash today and pays back over time, on top of a cycle that already ties up 154 days. That combination is why the overall resilience reading is STRAINED and why the arithmetic lands where it does: 68 out of 100 on business quality, 41 on multibagger fit and 30 on risk safety, weighting out to 50 — a Decent, three-star company. The honest summary is a genuinely strong distribution and innovation machine, sitting in a market growing around 9% a year with premium volumes growing one-and-a-half to two times that, operating in an industry structure it does not control (Industry Structure scores just 0.33) and not yet converting its growth into returns on the capital employed.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, Stakeholder Concentration, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.7× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ⚠️ Only about -11% of recent operating profit became cash (was 163%) — profits are getting stuck in paper, an early stress signal.
- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 11% to 18% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Every call is dominated by state policy: liquor is a state subject with state-run wholesale, prices set by state committees (Telangana price increase is the single biggest margin lever), Maharashtra's MML policy, Andhra tender conditions, Karnataka slab changes, Telangana retail licence lottery disrupting Q2 FY26 volumes, and ~Rs.400 crores still overdue from the Telangana government after receivables blew out from 60 to 150 days in FY24. Margin guidance for FY27-28 explicitly rests on a government price hike and the UK FTA.

> "Overdue from Telangana government is approximate 400 crores" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Pan-India spread across 33 states, growing asset-light exports (14 to 39 countries) and CSD/travel-retail channels dilute any single state's policy grip*

### Stakeholder Concentration — HIGH RISK
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 1 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Telangana is both the largest market (~10 of 32 million cases in FY24, ~65% of PET consumption, sole distillery and new PET plant sited there) and the customer whose non-payment stalled H2 FY24 growth; on the product side ICONiQ White (10.7m cases FY26, 3.1m in Q1 FY27) now supplies essentially all growth while the other three millionaire brands decline. Concentration sits on both the customer/state side and the single-brand side.

> "largely due to non-availability of working capital largely on account of Telangana, which is a large market where receivables went from 60 days to over 150 days" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: 79,000-outlet distribution, 32 bottling units, export expansion to 39 countries and new ABD Maestro/CSD/travel-retail channels broaden the base*

- ⚠️ Customers now take about 162 days to pay, up from 126 — lengthening collection terms are how weak bargaining power against concentrated customers shows up in the numbers.

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED

**What the calls show:** The mass-premium end, still over half of volumes, faces cheaper local brands and management is now explicitly 'driving growth at value price points'; in a state-controlled shop channel the consumer's choice is heavily shaped by shelf availability and price slabs rather than brand pull.

> "driving growth at value price points in the whisky category to cater the consumer demand" — *management, on an earnings call*

## What to watch

- Telangana price increase and the Rs 400 crore overdue — the single biggest margin lever named under Pricing Power and the core of the HIGH RISK Government Dependency and Stakeholder Concentration findings; payment plus a granted hike would lift both.
- The 154-day cash cycle and the zero score on Return on Capital — if backward integration (PET, malt, ENA) starts shortening the cycle and lifting returns, the Capital Allocation and Return on Capital scores, and the STRAINED resilience reading, all move together.
- Officer's Choice Blue, Sterling Reserve B7 and B10 brand resets — three of four millionaire brands have lost salience; a successful reappraisal would turn Brand Strength from PARTIAL toward a fit and take pressure off Fashion Risk.
- Concentration in ICONiQ White (10.7 million cases in FY26) — whether the ten ABD Maestro launches and the single malt broaden the growth base, or whether Innovation Dominance keeps resting on one trending brand.
- Maharashtra's MML category and rising regional brands — the New Entrants risk is only on WATCH, but the current answer is policy lobbying rather than a competitive response; a second state copying the MML model would test the Cost to Replicate barrier directly.
