**Checks that improved:**

- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Weak → Good. Now: Tenacity is demonstrated by closing the Ghitorni land dispute after years of litigation and grinding out 30,000 Amrapali units to near-completion, though as a CPSE with a government-appointed board its independence is structurally limited. | The long view had said: Leadership turned over twice in the window (Gupta to Mahadevaswamy) and targets are handed down by the ministry rather than self-set, while the flagship promise to finish Amrapali 'on or before 31st March 2025' slipped to June 2026.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: The customer base is almost entirely corporate/government and the benefit offered matches it well — reliability, statutory comfort and nomination lock-in rather than brand intangibles. | The long view had said: The customer base is entirely institutional/government and is served with reliability as promised, but there is no lock-in or TCO premium — the client dictates fee, funding and timing.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: Customers — courts, ministries and homebuyers — buy NBCC's certainty of delivery rather than its price, evidenced by the Supreme Court's endorsement of its capability and the DPE 98.5% excellent rating. | The long view had said: Customers and courts select NBCC for certainty of completion on complex distressed projects rather than on price, which is the clearest genuine customer benefit in the record.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Good. Now: Pan-India project-management and contractor-mobilisation reach — running INR34,000cr of live projects across states plus subsidiary HSCL — is a delivery network private peers would struggle to replicate, though it rests on tendered-out execution rather than owned capability. | The long view had said: Reach is broad — pan-India via HSCC/HSCL/NSL plus Maldives, Mauritius and Africa — but it is not a service network rivals cannot replicate, and reach has repeatedly failed to convert to execution.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: NBCC's growth is literally fed by failed private developers — Amrapali, Supertech and similar stalled builders whose portfolios are transferred to it by courts, an unusually explicit supply of donated share. | The long view had said: Failed private developers are a continuous source of transferred work — Amrapali (38,000 units) and now Supertech (~50,000 units) both landed with NBCC after the incumbents collapsed.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: NBCC is asset-light and spends little on plant, but it has committed real growth capital to land/development assets — settling and paying for the 21.23-acre Ghitorni parcel with ~INR8,500cr revenue and INR4,000-5,000cr PAT potential, plus Gurugram 37D — implying very high incremental returns on the capital deployed. | The long view had said: As an asset-light PMC there is essentially no plant capex; the only growth investment is a small opportunistic land book (39 acres/INR652cr, plus the long-litigated 21-acre Ghitorni parcel) whose high claimed returns remain unrealised after years of delay.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Prudent leverage is explicit — NBCC itself takes no debt, clients arrange HUDCO funding — and growth is fully organic with no M&A, though 60% of the order book sits in redevelopment projects that cannot start pending funding/approvals, which is a bidding-discipline weakness. | The long view had said: Balance sheet discipline is real — no debt at NBCC level, clients carry the borrowing, and no acquisitions — but this is partly a statutory constraint, and it sits alongside scattered diversification talk into highways, railways, metro, renewables and Dubai real estate.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Genuine domestic spread into new states (RIICO Rajasthan, Naveen Nagpur, Meerut, Ghaziabad, J&K, Jharkhand) but the overseas leg is MoU-stage only and the one executed foreign job is winding down, dragging EPC revenue 16% year-on-year. | The long view had said: Expansion is announced far faster than it is executed — Dubai subsidiary (2024), Africa/Arabian countries, Australia and UAE MoUs produced no disclosed revenue, and even domestic out-of-Delhi wins (Kerala, MAHAPREIT, J&K) remain unstarted years later.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Clear evidence of share capture in the stressed-realty and government-redevelopment niche — order book more than doubled to INR1.04-1.28 lakh crore and the Supreme Court handed NBCC the ~50,000-unit Supertech portfolio — though NBCC is already the default agency in this space, limiting incremental runway. | The long view had said: Order book compounded from INR56,000cr consolidated (Aug 2022) to INR1,27,000-1,28,000cr (FY26) with new mandates spanning DDA, MAHAPREIT, J&K, RIICO, Mumbai Port Trust and court-awarded Supertech, showing continuing capture of new client pools rather than saturation.

*Unchanged: Cost of working capital; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Intangible benefits; Structural end-market growth; Barriers to entry and rationality; Technology advantage; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Candor; Mini-monopolies; Security by obscurity; Out of limelight; Halo effect (caution); Asset turn; Profit margin; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Convenience benefits; Return on capital (headline).*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 0 weakened, 9 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The model is structurally thin-margin and asset-light — 6%-6.5% EBITDA guidance on a INR1.27 lakh crore order book executed through outsourced contractors with no balance-sheet leverage taken by NBCC — but the calls contain no cost-obsession culture, itemised savings or deliberate price-for-volume strategy, so the low margin looks like a fee-model artefact rather than a replicable cost advantage. (The long view had said: Margins are structurally thin (EBITDA 3.5%–6.5%) and the model is asset-light, but no call discusses a cost culture, itemised savings, or passing cost advantage to price — the evidence is silent on the mechanism the pattern requires.)
- **Corporate Culture**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management does disclose bad news openly — the INR95 crore exceptional provision for the Kerala environment-clearance issue, and candid admission that INR40,000 crore of order book is stuck on MAHAPREIT funding and J&K approvals — but guidance has slipped repeatedly inside these four calls (FY26 top line guided INR15,000-16,000 crore in May, cut to INR14,000-15,000 crore in August, with analysts modelling INR12,500-13,000 crore by November), and the chairman/MD history shows rotating government appointees rather than long-tenured owner-operators. (The long view had said: Bad news is disclosed promptly (INR110 crore Gurgaon 37D provision, Kochi EC provision) and the balance sheet is debt-free with large cash, but the promise-versus-delivery record across the years fails: Amrapali was to 'finish' in FY24 (Aug 2022), then all 38,000 units 'on or before 31st March 2025' (May 2024), yet was still running in FY26, and the INR12,500–13,000 crore revenue target guided for FY25 was still the run-rate in FY26 — alongside CMD turnover from P.K. Gupta to K.P. Mahadevaswamy.)

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 1 unchanged; 5 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Revenue is entirely a function of clients' construction capex plus NBCC's own real estate sales, and both swung visibly within the year — EPC revenue fell ~16% year-on-year as the Maldives project wound down, real estate revenue and profit dropped sharply in Q1, and Q2 EBITDA margin slipped on lower real estate and Amrapali marketing fees; management's FY28 profit guidance leans on two property monetisations (Ghitorni, Gurugram 37D). (The long view had said: Every rupee of revenue is linked to someone else's capex programme (government construction, redevelopment, real estate monetisation) with no flow/consumable base, and margins swing visibly with lumpy real-estate sale timing — EBITDA fell in Q2 and Q3 FY26 on lower real estate and Amrapali marketing fees, a GRAP construction ban dented Q3 FY26 profit, and a INR110 crore expected loss provision was booked on the Gurgaon 37D land in Q1 FY23; EBITDA margins have run thin at ~3.5-6.5% throughout.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Results and the order book hinge on a handful of named mandates — management concedes INR40,000 crores of the INR1,28,000 crores book is just MAHAPREIT and J&K and both are stalled awaiting client funding/approval, while top-line growth rests on Amrapali, the GPRA colonies, Ghitorni and now Supertech; the ultimate counterparty across nearly all of it is the same one (Government of India/state agencies and the courts). (The long view had said: Results are repeatedly driven by a handful of named relationships — management itself states that 30-40% of turnover comes from just Amrapali and the 7 GPRA colonies, and two clients (MAHAPREIT and J&K) account for ~INR40,000 crores of the INR1,27,000 crore order book that has produced no revenue at all; MoHUA is simultaneously client, funder (seed money) and debtor on INR219-481 crores of pending seed money/interest.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Good Enough Goods (QUANT FLAG); Technological Innovation (NO SIGNAL); New Entrants (NO SIGNAL); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL); Fashion Risk (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
