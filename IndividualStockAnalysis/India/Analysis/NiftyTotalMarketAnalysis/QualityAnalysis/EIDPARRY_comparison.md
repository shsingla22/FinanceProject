**Checks that improved:**

- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: There is little halo risk to strip out because results are visibly driven by monsoon, FRP, MSP and release quotas rather than management skill, but equally no independent outperformance is demonstrated. | The long view had said: Outcomes track cane weather, FRP, release quotas and OMC pricing far more than management action — recovery jumping 7.78% to 11.19% on 'good, diurnal weather' — so results cannot be read as evidence of skill, and the one big discretionary bet (the refinery) destroyed Rs.600 crore.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Good → Weak. Now: The Parry's consumer franchise holds no monopoly position in the customer's mind — its sweetener volumes are dictated by government release quota and total CPG revenue nearly halved. | The long view had said: The Parry brand holds a genuine mind-share position in southern branded sweeteners with a 55% share and management sees little competition there, backed by owned brown-sugar and jaggery manufacturing.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Weak. Now: The reliability EID Parry demonstrates is toward suppliers, not customers — FRP paid on time to growers — while its industrial sugar customers buy purely on price, as the refinery's Rs.293 crore loss and closure show. | The long view had said: Some reliability positioning via in-house manufacture and vertical integration on dals for quality control, but customers are buying a regulated commodity on price, not certainty.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Weak. Now: The company sits on the wrong side of the cycle — global balances moved from deficit to surplus, white sugar fell from $500 to $420/t, and exports have been banned to September 2026, with no identified upturn. | The long view had said: The cycle is currently adverse — global surplus of ~3 MMT, white sugar down from $500 to $420/t, raws collapsing and exports banned to Sept 2026 — which is a low base, but the identified upturn (E30 blending) is a policy hope rather than a visible turn.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: Sugar in India is the opposite of an ignored niche — it is a politically salient, heavily regulated sector under constant policymaker and analyst scrutiny. | The long view had said: An unglamorous commodity niche, but hardly ignored — a listed Murugappa flagship covered by DAM Capital with quarterly analyst calls and multiple institutional investors on the line.
- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Ethanol blending is a genuine secular end-market, but cane-based feedstock is structurally losing it — grain now takes over 70% of awarded tenders and diversion guidance was cut from 3.4 to 3.0 MMT. | The long view had said: Ethanol blending is a genuine secular policy expansion — 10% blending in 2022, 20% target met, and BIS intent for E30 now signalled — which structurally grows the distillery end-market even if pricing lags.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: Whatever regional captive-ness cane command areas confer is eroding — planting fell in TN and AP forcing outside molasses purchases, and there are no customer switching costs in a commodity sold at administered prices. | The long view had said: Sugar mills enjoy a de facto regional cane catchment (crushing is state-bound in TN, Karnataka and AP), but there are no switching costs — molasses must be bought in, ethanol goes to OMC tenders, and CPG customers are free-choice retail.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Weak. Now: Distribution intimacy was disrupted rather than leveraged this year — the consumer business was put through a channel restructuring that cut revenue sharply for three consecutive quarters. | The long view had said: A real direct retail reach of ~1-1.2 lakh outlets with 70,000 billing quarterly, but it shrank from 1.5 lakh and is being rebuilt mid-restructuring, so accessibility is not yet a demonstrated advantage.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Weak. Now: Rather than an unmatched reach, the distribution network was the problem being fixed — a deliberate channel recalibration that halved Q4 consumer revenue. | The long view had said: South-India retail reach plus an owned cane catchment gives more distribution than a typical sugar miller, but it is regional, currently contracting, and no match for national FMCG rivals as the company moves into snacking and culinary.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: No weak competitor is ceding ground to EID Parry; the company is itself the one exiting capacity via the refinery closure and shrinking consumer volumes. | The long view had said: Weak/co-operative mills and unbranded jaggery exist as theoretical donors and peers are described as subscale in TN, but across four years there is no evidence of EID actually capturing their volume — its own TN/AP throughput shrank.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 3 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: LIKELY FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: Management references cost management in sugar and biofuel but the calls give no itemised savings, no structural cost advantage versus peers, and no low-price-plus-quality model; cane cost is externally mandated via FRP rather than managed down. (The long view had said: There is a genuine and repeated cost/efficiency drive — refinery energy-efficiency projects cutting cost to ~$41/MT (Feb 2026), better Q3 FY26 EBITDA 'driven by efficiency and cost work', and 'hunker down and run for cost and efficiency' (Jun 2026) — plus penetrative pricing on brown sugar. But it does not translate into the high asset turns and strong returns the pattern requires: the sugar segment still lost money in Q3 FY26 and the refinery was shut.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No R&D spend, pipeline, or named new-product launches with revenue impact are disclosed in any of the four FY26 calls. (The long view had said: There are named developments each year — Flomentum and the Valensa prostate/derma launches, Green Grow soil-less media from bagasse, jaggery and premium-white variants targeted at 30%+ gross margin — but R&D spend is never disclosed, gross margins are commodity-level, and Nutra has been under 'strategic review' from FY25 through FY26, so innovation is neither profitable nor scaled.)

Why now: No market-share figures are cited and the FY26 direction is contraction: domestic sugar sales volumes fell in Q1–Q3, CPG declined every quarter culminating in -48% in Q4, and the Parry Sugars refinery was shut with a Rs.293 crore Q4 loss. (The long view had said: Management asserts a 55% share of the southern sweetener segment (Feb 2026), but this is a stated position rather than a demonstrated series of gains, and the adjacent staples push reversed — CPG turnover fell from Rs.236cr to Rs.143cr in Q3 and Rs.195cr to Rs.115cr in Q4 FY26 — while the refinery was closed outright.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 15 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
