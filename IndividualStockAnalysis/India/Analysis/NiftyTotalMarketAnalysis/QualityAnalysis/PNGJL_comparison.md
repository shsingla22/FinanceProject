### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 8 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: The FY26 model is explicitly own-store plus franchise plus direct e-commerce: store count went 55 to 78 with a COCO/FOCO split disclosed each quarter, franchisee revenue grew 83% to INR1,292 crores and e-commerce 105% to INR529 crores, with third-party franchisees funding part of the rollout. (The long view had said: The model is explicitly forward-integrated and has scaled on plan: 39 stores at Sep-2024 to 78 across 36 cities by Mar-2026 in a COCO plus FOCO mix, with franchisee revenue up 83% to INR1,292 crores and e-commerce up 105% to INR529 crores in FY26; management repeatedly frames franchising as third-party-funded, asset-light growth.)

**That weakened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: LIKELY FIT → NO FIT. Why now: Q4 FY26 margin dilution came from heavy making-charge discounting and a swing into thin-spread gold bars/coins, i.e. price-led promotion that compressed returns rather than a structural low-cost culture; no itemised cost savings or no-frills operating philosophy is described on any of the four calls. (The long view had said: There is a genuine asset-efficiency and cost culture — medium-sized stores with 40-45% lower capex/opex than peers, made-to-order to cut inventory, best-in-class revenue per square foot and ROCE of 30.5% in FY26 on ~4% PAT margins — but low price is not the stated proposition; discounting appears as one-off festival offers rather than an everyday-low-price model.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Growth is clearly ahead of the category — FY26 revenue +40% to INR10,739 crores with 43% same-store growth, 25 net new stores and entry into MP, UP and Bihar — but management never quotes an actual market-share number or compares itself with named competitors, and much of the value growth reflects a ~70% rise in gold prices. (The long view had said: Growth is persistent and largely organic-plus-network — 40% FY26 revenue growth to INR10,739 crores, 43% full-year SSSG, expansion from Maharashtra/Goa into MP, UP and Bihar with local mix adaptation toward studded/fancy — but management gives no explicit share numbers and declined to engage when asked why Q3 growth of 35.6% trailed Titan/Kalyan at 42-43%.)

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Every one of the four calls opens by framing demand and results against the gold price (up 35% year-on-year in Q1, 50% in Q2, 70% in Q3), and reported margins swing with the metal: a Rs 25 crore hedging loss in Q1 FY26, unhedged gains of Rs 7.4cr / Rs 45cr / Rs 20cr across Q4 FY25–Q4 FY26, and a 230bps Q4 gross-margin dilution as commodity-like gold bars and coins jumped from 28% to 40% of the mix. Revenue is a price-times-volume commodity aggregate, so a gold price reversal would hit both the top line and the 2,000kg inventory position. (The long view had said: Revenue and margins are driven by the gold price cycle rather than by any customer's capex — gold rose ~35% year-on-year in Q1 FY26 and >70% year-on-year in Q3 FY26, and management repeatedly frames demand as volume-versus-value trade-offs, while hedging swings (Rs 25 cr hedging loss in Q1 FY26; unhedged gains of Rs 7.4 cr, Rs 45 cr and Rs 20 cr across Q4 FY25/Q3 FY26/Q4 FY26) visibly moved reported margins. Underlying demand is consumer, festival- and wedding-led (a flow, repeat-purchase base) rather than a capex cycle, and hedging has been raised from 55-57% to 67% with a target of 75-80%, which bounds the exposure.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Government Dependency (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
