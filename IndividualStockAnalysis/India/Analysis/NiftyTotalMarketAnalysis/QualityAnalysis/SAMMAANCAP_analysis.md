# Sammaan Capital (SAMMAANCAP) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Sammaan Capital (formerly Indiabulls Housing Finance) is an India-based non-bank lender — since July 2024 an RBI-registered NBFC-Investment and Credit Company, classified in the upper layer — that mainly makes mortgage-backed loans to retail borrowers: home loans and loans against property for prime and for mid-market/low-income "affordable" customers, sold through a nationwide branch network and an increasingly digital origination and underwriting platform. It earns money on the interest spread between what it lends at and what it borrows at, plus fees and gains from selling loans down, and it deliberately runs an "asset-light" model in which loans are co-lent with or sold to banks and financial institutions rather than all held on its own balance sheet. Alongside retail lending it runs an AIF (alternative investment fund) platform that lends wholesale to real-estate developers in India's top cities, and it is separately collecting down an older "legacy" wholesale book originated up to FY21. It operates only in India (offices in Gurugram and New Delhi, listed on BSE and NSE), and as of the May 2026 call its new promoter is Abu Dhabi's International Holding Company, which invested about USD 1 billion.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Retail mortgage lending (home loans and LAP, prime and affordable)** — The core business — four products: prime home loans around Rs. 30 lakh and LAP around Rs. 75 lakh, plus affordable home loans around Rs. 15 lakh and affordable LAP around Rs. 25 lakh — and management calls it the company's central focus, with growth AUM of roughly Rs. 44,000 crore as of December 2025 and stable asset quality (gross NPA 1.2%, net NPA 0.7%).
- **Asset-light / co-lending and sell-down model** — Instead of holding all loans itself, the company originates loans and shares or sells them to banks and financial institutions through co-lending, direct assignment and pass-through certificates — management describes roughly Rs. 1.03 lakh crore of loans sold down over time (about Rs. 85,000 crore already repaid to partners, ~Rs. 17,000 crore outstanding, 90-day-past-due just 0.54%) and calls this platform a key moat and the continuing strategy.
- **AIF platform (wholesale / developer lending)** — SEBI-approved alternative investment funds through which the company lends larger tickets (originally around Rs. 200 crore per loan) to real-estate developers; by February 2026 it had lent roughly Rs. 6,200 crore across the top eight cities and is described as the vehicle for all wholesale lending going forward.
- **Legacy book run-down** — The older wholesale/corporate loans originated in FY21 and earlier, which the company is not growing but collecting and recovering — targeted to shrink to a single-digit percentage of AUM by FY27, with about Rs. 5,000 crore of net collections in FY26 and further recoveries expected via insolvency (IBC) proceedings.
- **Sammaan Finserve (subsidiary)** — A lending subsidiary that had been running the affordable-housing business; its lending and distribution are being merged into Sammaan Capital so all mortgage products sit in one company, while Sammaan Finserve is kept as the shell to house other financial services the group may add later under IHC's guidance.
- **Fee-based businesses (planned)** — Management has said it identified a couple of fee-earning businesses to sit alongside the lending franchise in a new corporate structure, but the calls give no size or revenue detail — it is an intention rather than an established segment.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Sammaan Capital scores 39 out of 100 — a Mixed, two-star verdict built from 34 on business quality (weighted 64%) and 48 on risk safety (weighted 36%). The quality work is negative almost everywhere it matters: Industry Structure at -1.33, Capital Allocation and Return on Capital both at -1.0, Management at -0.5, with Competitive Advantage sitting at exactly zero. The multibagger screen tells the same story from the other side — of the patterns tested, six are outright NO FIT (Recurring Revenue, Low Price Plus, Pricing Power, Brand Strength, Market Share Gainers, Cost to Replicate) and only Corporate Culture reaches a PARTIAL. Nothing in the record suggests the company has found a way to charge more than the next lender or hold onto customers who could easily leave; resilience is recorded as UNKNOWN. What the risk review adds is that the same features producing the low quality score are also the things that could hurt: two channels are HIGH RISK (Cyclicality, Government Dependency), three are on WATCH (Stakeholder Concentration, New Entrants, Good Enough Goods), and only Technological Innovation is LOW.

The clearest thread running through all three analyses is that this is a commodity lending product financed at a cost the company does not control. Over 99% of advances are floating rate, and pricing has been a straight pass-through of RBI moves (+140bps retail and +160bps wholesale in the Aug 2022 call), with the May 2026 grid showing yields set by segment competition — 8.5-10% on prime, 11-13.5% on affordable. That single fact drives the Pricing Power NO FIT, the Good Enough Goods WATCH, the -1 on Intangible benefits (spreads run off a 3.75% NIM, 10.8% book yield against 8.1% funding), and the -2 on Oligopoly, since Indian mortgage lending is fragmented across banks, HFCs and NBFCs and borrowers shop on rate. Management's own concession that it cannot profitably hold 8-10% prime loans on its balance sheet is the hinge: it explains the shift to originating loans and selling them onward, and it explains the New Entrants WATCH, because the structurally cheaper-funded banks are simultaneously the competition and the buyers. The asset-light sell-down arrangement is genuinely the company's best asset — a Rs.1.03 lakh crore sold pool running 0.54% 90+ DPD across 24 bank relationships — but the pattern screen judges that track record rebuildable by any well-capitalised lender (Cost to Replicate NO FIT), and the risk review counts the same 24 relationships as the Stakeholder Concentration exposure. One trait, three different verdicts, all pointing the same way: the model works, but it belongs to the counterparties as much as to Sammaan.

The second thread is that survival has been paid for with shareholders' money and with the company's own history. Assets under management have fallen for four straight years, from over Rs.73,000 crore in Q1FY23 to Rs.53,160 crore by FY26, against an FY27 target of Rs.1 trillion in retail AUM that was never approached — hence the -2 on Gain in market share and the Market Share Gainers NO FIT. Capital has flowed the wrong way for existing holders: rights issue call money, private placements and a $1bn preferential allotment plus warrants that take IHC to 43.5%, all at depressed valuations and with no buybacks, which is the -2 on Distribution to shareholders and the reason IHC now appears in the Stakeholder Concentration risk as the single supplier of capital, ratings uplift and strategy. Meanwhile the Indiabulls name was rebranded to Sammaan in 2024 and later sold back to the ex-promoter to put distance between the company and a seven-year PIL — a defensive spend that scores -1 on Advertisement and promotion, kills the Brand Strength pattern, and produces the -2 on Security by obscurity given the CBI/ED/SEBI/RBI affidavits and the Supreme Court SLP. Regulators sit on top of all of it (a six-month RBI review before the fresh NBFC-ICC certificate, LCR and scale-based rules, and new co-lending regulations that widen the field of competitors), which is why Government Dependency is HIGH RISK with no silver lining recorded. The offsetting evidence is real and sits entirely in the Corporate Culture PARTIAL: a long-tenured team under Banga since the Indiabulls era, roughly Rs.1.3 lakh crore of net deleveraging since 2018, pre-funded FCCB and ECB repayments, and full disclosure of the PIL — a management that has been honest and has cleaned up, but that has also repeatedly missed what it promised, with Tier 3/4 branch expansion replaced by consolidation and deferred again to after the capital raise closes.
