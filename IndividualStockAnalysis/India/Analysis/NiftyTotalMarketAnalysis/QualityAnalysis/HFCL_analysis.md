# Himachal Fut Com Ltd. (HFCL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: TELECOM*

HFCL Limited is an Indian manufacturer of telecom and networking hardware, headquartered in New Delhi with its registered works at Solan, Himachal Pradesh. Its core business is making optical fibre and optical fibre cable, plus the connectivity products that go with them, and selling these to telecom operators (private telcos and BSNL), enterprises like banks, hotels and schools, government-funded network programmes such as BharatNet, and increasingly to hyperscale data-centre customers overseas. Alongside cable it designs and sells its own telecom equipment (backhaul radios, 5G fixed-wireless devices, routers, switches, Wi-Fi access points) and a growing range of defence electronics, and it also earns fees from turnkey network-building (EPC) work billed against project milestones. It makes money by manufacturing and shipping products at a margin, and it has shifted heavily toward exports — over 70% of cable output was exported by FY26, with exports at 41.36% of revenue versus 12.23% the prior year — selling into 30-plus countries; the order book stood at about Rs 26,665 crore in Q1 FY27, roughly five times FY26 revenue.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Optical Fibre and Optical Fibre Cable (Optical Connectivity)** — The traditional core: HFCL draws optical fibre and manufactures it into cables, including very high fibre-count cables of up to 6,912 fibres designed in-house, with capacity being expanded from 28 to 34 million fibre-km of fibre and 34 to 43 million fibre-km of cable, and it won a single long-term global supply contract worth about USD 1.1 billion (Rs 10,159 crore) — management calls it the largest such contract ever secured by an Indian telecom company.
- **Data Centre Interconnect / connectivity solutions** — A fast-growing newer line making pre-connectorised cable assemblies and high-density fibre termination products for AI and hyperscale data centres, produced partly through subsidiary HTL Limited, with capacity being raised five-fold and expected revenue of more than Rs 700 crore in its first year of production (FY27) and multiples of that later.
- **Telecom and Networking Products (Telecom Equipment segment)** — HFCL's own-designed equipment — unlicensed-band backhaul radios (where it says it holds over 70% of the Indian market and has shipped 500,000+ units globally), 5G fixed wireless access customer devices, IP/MPLS routers, switches and Wi-Fi access points — sold to telcos and enterprises; management guided this segment from Rs 143 crore in FY24 to roughly Rs 2,000 crore in FY25, with Rs 401 crore booked in the first quarter alone.
- **Defence and Aerospace** — Indigenously developed defence electronics — electronic fuzes for artillery ammunition, thermal weapon sights and their titanium cores, ground and coastal surveillance radars, high-capacity radio relay, with multimode hand grenades and trans-horizon communications in the pipeline — now being consolidated under subsidiary HFCL Advance Systems along with an acquired aerospace business that brings an export order book of about Rs 1,930 crore; management sizes the domestic market for its current products at over USD 35 billion over six to seven years.
- **EPC / turnkey network services and Digital Infrastructure** — Project work laying and building networks (for example under BharatNet and highway projects), billed on completed milestones so revenue can slip when the customer's site infrastructure isn't ready; management names EPC as one of the segments driving FY26's record results, though it is described as more working-capital hungry than product sales, which is why HFCL has deliberately tilted the mix toward products (product revenue rose to 59% of sales in Q1 FY23 from 43% in FY22).
- **Preform manufacturing (backward integration, under construction)** — A new greenfield plant of 300 tonnes a year costing about Rs 580 crore to make the glass preforms from which optical fibre is drawn — not a selling business itself but described by management as a key margin-expansion lever and supply-chain safeguard for the fibre business.
- **R&D and technology development** — In-house design centres near Bengaluru, Gurgaon, Hyderabad and the United States, plus engineering tie-ups with firms such as Wipro and Capgemini, that create the telecom, cable and defence products the company sells — presented across the calls as the engine behind the shift from commodity cable to higher-margin technology-led products.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

The clearest strengths in this record all point the same way: HFCL has been winning ground in a market that is genuinely growing. Structural end-market growth scores +2 — AI and hyperscale data-centre fibre demand (100–150 million fibre-km of incremental global demand), FTTH/5G, BharatNet and Indian defence indigenisation are described as real secular expansion rather than a cyclical bounce. Geographical expansion also scores +2: exports went from 4.54% of revenue in FY21 to 41.36% in FY26, with more than 70% of cable production exported, helped by the European Commission exempting only HFCL from anti-dumping duty and by a landmark USD 1.1bn multi-year global contract. Market share gain scores +1 (roughly 50% of the domestic OFC market, over 70% of India's unlicensed-band radio market, more than 500,000 radios shipped), and the multibagger screen's single strongest verdict, 'Market Share Gainers' at LIKELY FIT, rests on exactly these same numbers. Alongside this, capex is judged growth-oriented rather than maintenance (fibre 28→34 mn fkm, OFC 34→43 mn fkm, ₹580cr preform, ₹215cr data-centre connectivity, ₹640cr in FY27 and ₹615cr in FY28), and the mix is improving — product revenue rose from 27% of the total in FY21 to 62% in FY26 and 85% in Q1FY27.

The problem is that the very facts producing those strengths also produce the biggest risks. The USD 1.1bn (₹10,159cr) hyperscaler contract is the headline proof of share gain and export success, but it is also roughly half of the ₹21,200cr order book — which is why Stakeholder Concentration is rated HIGH RISK, with management itself calling reduced single-customer dependence an unfinished job. The secular end-markets that score +2 are largely policy-driven — BharatNet Phase-III at around ₹60,000cr of USOF funding, PLI incentives, BSNL orders, spectrum auctions, the European Commission ruling, and a defence build-out whose customer is the state — so the same demand story sits underneath the HIGH RISK rating for Government Dependency, softened only by government order-book exposure falling from 51% in FY21 to 37% in FY26. And the core product that delivers the volume is a commodity: standard 288/432-count cable competes on price, which is why 'Pricing Power' is a NO FIT (Q1FY23 margins fell on pre-committed prices during input inflation, FY24-25 saw an OFC demand and price slowdown, and Q4FY25 EBITDA was actually negative at -2.79%), why 'Good Enough Goods' is ELEVATED, and why Cyclicality is HIGH RISK with fibre realisations swinging from ₹260-270 per fibre-km to ₹425-450. 'Toll Roads' fails for the same reason — fibre is an openly negotiated, per-fibre-kilometre line of customer capex, not a small unnoticed input.

Where all this lands is in the money. Growth, competitive advantage and customer benefits score positively (Growth +1.17, Competitive Advantage +1.0, Customer Benefits +0.75), but the capital side is negative: Return on Capital -0.33, Capital Allocation -0.2, Industry Structure -0.17, Management 0.0. Two checks score -2 outright. Return on capital is -6% — every ₹100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings generated negative ₹6 of operating cash this year — and about 210 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. Shareholder distribution scores -1 because capital is flowing the other way: no buybacks or stated policy, a ₹555cr promoter warrant issuance, and the preform plant funded through 'debt and equity', meaning dilution. So the growth capex that earns a +1 is being funded while the business is not yet generating cash on the capital already deployed. The multibagger screen reflects the same ambivalence — seven patterns are PARTIAL or NO FIT, with 'Innovation Dominance', 'Cost to Replicate' and 'Low Price Plus' all held to PARTIAL because the evidence is real but narrow (UBRs converted into genuine dominance; R&D spend is never disclosed; the core OFC business is described as capital-heavy but replicable). The combined rating is 45% × 58 on business quality, 30% × 18 on multibagger fit and 25% × 0 on risk safety, giving 32 out of 100 — Weak, one star — with resilience marked STRESSED. In plain terms: a real share gainer in a real growth market, on a balance sheet and a margin structure that cannot currently absorb the swings that same market delivers.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Government Dependency, Stakeholder Concentration, Technological Innovation, Good Enough Goods, New Entrants, Shifting Consumer Preferences, Fashion Risk. The balance sheet and cash engine shows multiple financial stress signals — 2 of the 3 stress checks are flagged — multiple warning lights at once.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 2 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is tied to telecom operator and hyperscaler capex programmes and to swinging fibre/OFC realisations — fibre went from Rs.260-270/fkm to Rs.425-450/fkm in FY23, a global OFC 'temporary slowdown' hit FY24-Q1FY25, and by Q4FY25 EBITDA was actually negative (-2.79%, PAT -Rs.83 cr) before rebounding to 23.25% EBITDA in Q1FY27 — while capacity is being expanded into the upswing (fibre 28→34 mn fkm, cable 34→43 mn fkm, Rs.580 cr preform, Rs.640 cr FY27 capex).

> "Global optical fibre cable market is going through a relatively slowdown phase... large inventory built with operators and delay in major government funded programs" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Growing consumable-like O&M/EPC annuity and a claimed multi-year AI/data-centre demand cycle with order book at 5x revenue*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 4 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 9% and 15% (±2.1 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 10% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 4% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.
