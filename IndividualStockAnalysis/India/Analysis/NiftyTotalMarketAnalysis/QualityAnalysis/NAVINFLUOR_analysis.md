# Navin Fluorine International Ltd. (NAVINFLUOR) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CHEMICALS*

Navin Fluorine International is an Indian chemicals maker that specialises in fluorine-based chemistry — it takes fluorine and hydrogen fluoride and turns them into refrigerant gases, inorganic fluorides, and complex fluorine-containing molecules. It sells business-to-business: to global refrigeration and chemical majors (Honeywell, Chemours are named), to large global agrochemical companies, and to big pharma and biotech firms, both in India and for export (the calls describe both domestic and international sales, with the mix varying by segment). It makes money in two ways management repeatedly describes as "product and service play" — selling its own manufactured products, and being paid to develop and manufacture molecules to a customer's specification under long-term contracts and dedicated plants. Manufacturing is in Gujarat and nearby India — Dahej and Surat are the plants named, plus an R&D centre at Surat — and the company grew revenue to about INR 3,314 crore in FY26 (up 41%) with EBITDA margins around 32.6%.

**The parts of the business:**

- **High Performance Products (HPP)** — Refrigerant gases (R22, R32, HFO) and inorganic fluorides plus anhydrous hydrofluoric acid (AHF), sold on volume and price — the calls call out low-global-warming-potential refrigerants and a big new 40,000 tonne AHF plant at Dahej; it was the largest of the three at roughly INR 393 crore in the March 2026 quarter.
- **Specialty Chemicals** — Makes specific fluorine-containing molecules and intermediates, largely for global agrochemical customers (and some patented agro products for Japan), often in dedicated or multi-purpose plants under long-term contracts; roughly INR 360 crore in the March 2026 quarter and described as its highest-ever levels, though it slumped badly in FY25 when agro customers cut inventory.
- **CDMO (Contract Development and Manufacturing Organization)** — A paid-for-service business developing and manufacturing drug molecules for big pharma and biotech across oncology, respiratory, cardiovascular, neurology and animal health, run out of regulated cGMP plants (cGMP-3, cGMP-4); the smallest of the three at about INR 186 crore in the March 2026 quarter, and management repeatedly warns it is lumpy and shouldn't be judged quarter to quarter.
- **Advanced / Performance Materials (early stage)** — An R&D-stage effort, not yet a reported revenue segment, co-developing materials with global chemical majors for semiconductors and the electronic chemicals value chain.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Navin Fluorine comes out of the three reviews as a genuinely good business that is priced into a difficult industry. The quality framework scores its Competitive Advantage a full 2.0 — a deep fluorination capability stack running from simple chemistry through hydrogen fluoride to complex molecules and now high-purity electronic gases, evidenced by stabilising a licensed plant in four weeks. What it sells to customers is not a cheap molecule but supply certainty: entirely corporate buyers — top-five global agro innovators, big pharma, Honeywell, Chemours — pay to keep a validated non-China fluorochemicals partner and bind pricing in long-term contracts. Management has funded this the hard way, with entirely organic growth, no acquisitions, counter-cyclical spending through the agrochemical downturn, and borrowings cut from 0.38x to effectively nil while running a large building programme (Rs 540cr agro-specialty, Rs 450cr AHF, Rs 288cr cGMP-4, R32 doublings, the Chemours plant). The end markets themselves are expanding — the shift to low-global-warming refrigerants, crop-protection demand, pharmaceutical outsourcing, and an emerging semiconductor chemicals chain. The combined rating lands at 64 out of 100, or 'Decent': business quality scores 76, but multibagger fit only 56 and risk safety only 54, and that gap is the whole story.

The patterns the screen does find are the same traits the quality checks reward, and in several cases they are also the source of the biggest risks. Innovation Dominance is the one clear STRONG FIT — the Surat research and pilot-plant upgrade converting into named new molecules every year, a Rs 40-50cr agro molecule, a Rs 20-30cr Japanese patented product, roughly 13 new molecules in FY26 — and that same engine is what the Technological Innovation risk (WATCH) says the company needs, because the refrigerant portfolio sits on a moving ladder where R22 is already a phase-out legacy, R32 is today's earner but itself transitional, and value is migrating to next-generation low-warming products. Market Share Gainers is a LIKELY FIT on the strength of winning places in global majors' supply chains and growing 44% in the first nine months of FY26 while peers described a weak agrochemical cycle — but the way that share is won, through dedicated single-customer assets like the greenfield plant built solely for Honeywell, sole-site status for Chemours, and the Fermion/Bayer molecule, is exactly why Stakeholder Concentration is rated ELEVATED. The Kigali refrigerant quota cuts both ways too: it is the barrier behind the Cost to Replicate pattern, since capacity can be built but quota cannot, and it is simultaneously the reason Government Dependency is on watch, because a regulator decides who may produce and sell. Pricing Power is only a LIKELY FIT for the same reason the Profit margin check scores just 1 — pricing was reaffirmed in FY23 and drove roughly 30% of the 990 basis point margin expansion in FY26, but FY24-25 went the other way.

The weak spots cluster in the same place. Industry Structure scores lowest at 0.5, Return on Capital 0.67, and the two headline financial checks each score only 1: operating profit is Rs 33 on every Rs 100 of sales but has swung about 5.2 points a year, and the business turns Rs 100 of capital into Rs 17 of operating cash. Cyclicality is the single HIGH RISK, and it explains those scores — specialty revenue fell 30% year-on-year in one quarter of FY25 and operating margin dropped from about 26% to 15% before rebounding, which is the same volatility the Profit margin check flags as costs outside management's control. New Entrants and Good Enough Goods are both on watch and both point at the same pressure: aggressive Chinese generic capacity resetting agrochemical intermediate prices, and an analyst-cited wave of more than 120,000 tonnes of new Indian R32 capacity by CY27 that management answers by pointing to quota rather than to cost. Four patterns come back PARTIAL because the business simply does not work that way — there are no licence fees, renewals or customer advances, no prescribers or trade middlemen, no brand economics — and two are outright NO FIT, since integration runs backward to secure raw material rather than forward to the end customer, and the company explicitly refuses to chase generic pricing. What is genuinely stable is the demand base: Fashion Risk and Shifting Consumer Preferences are both rated LOW, because this is functional, repeat industrial purchasing under validated, contracted supply. Resilience is judged SOUND — the company should get through the cycle; the open question is whether the moat converts into steadier returns on the far side of it.

## What to watch

- Margin steadiness through the next agrochemical cycle — the Cyclicality risk (HIGH) and the Profit margin check (score 1, swings of about 5.2 points a year) both improve only if the FY26 recovery holds rather than reversing as it did in FY24-25.
