# Brainbees Solut. (FIRSTCRY) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Brainbees Solutions, which trades under the brand FirstCry, is an Indian retailer of products for mothers, babies and kids — diapers, baby gear, nursery items, fashion, toys and similar — sold mainly to young parents. It reaches them through several channels at once: its own online platform, a chain of 1,000-plus (now about 1,200) branded stores, and distribution of its own in-house 'home brands' into ordinary general-trade retail shops; it also manufactures part of what it sells and runs its own delivery network. It earns money by selling merchandise at a margin (gross margin was about 37.8% in India in Q1 FY26), so profit comes from growing order volumes and widening that margin rather than from fees. Beyond India it runs the same model in the UAE and Saudi Arabia, owns a house-of-brands business called GlobalBees, and operates an asset-light preschool chain; consolidated FY26 revenue was ₹8,547 crore, up 12%, with adjusted EBITDA of ₹486 crore.

**The parts of the business:**

- **India multi-channel** — The original and by far largest segment (started 2010) — the FirstCry online store, 1,000+ (about 1,200) offline stores including company-owned COCO stores, and a newer distribution arm that pushes FirstCry's home brands into general-trade shops; FY26 revenue grew 9% with adjusted EBITDA of about ₹505 crore, and management says it stayed profit-and-cash-flow positive all year.
- **International (UAE and KSA)** — The same retail playbook run in the United Arab Emirates and Saudi Arabia, roughly ₹207 crore of revenue in Q1 FY26; it is still loss-making but management is deliberately shrinking the losses (adjusted EBITDA loss down 35% in FY26) rather than chasing growth against discounting by large horizontal e-commerce rivals.
- **GlobalBees** — A separate company incubated in May 2021 that owns and runs a portfolio of consumer brands in home improvement and utilities, home appliances, health and personal care, and active/lifestyle and accessories; revenue was ₹426.5 crore in Q1 FY26 (up 31%), all growth organic since the last acquisition in September 2022, with FY26 adjusted EBITDA of roughly ₹92 crore after corporate costs, while weaker non-core brands are being pruned.
- **Others (preschool)** — The smallest segment — an asset-light preschool business where FirstCry does not own the physical assets; FY26 revenue grew 11% and its adjusted EBITDA improved 21% over FY25.
- **RocketBees (initiative within India multi-channel)** — FirstCry's own asset-light delivery network, where the company owns the technology and dedicated third-party regional couriers ship its orders, built after customer-experience problems with outside logistics firms; it went from 13 cities to 62 cities during FY26 and handled over 40% of online delivery volumes by the March 2026 quarter, improving delivery times about 20%.
- **FirstCry Qwik (initiative within India multi-channel)** — A fast-delivery service promising under three hours across the full product range, fulfilled from company-owned stores, warehouses, stockists and dark stores; started December 2025 and already in 5 cities, where it accounts for over 20% of online orders in those catchments, with management expecting roughly 10% of all online business in FY27.

### The story in depth

FirstCry is best understood as a company that owns its own path to the customer. The strongest single finding across all three reviews is the "Forward Integrators" pattern, rated a strong fit: more than 1,000 stores heading toward roughly 1,200 with 90-100 company-owned additions a year, its own online platform, home brands pushed into general trade, and an in-house delivery arm (RocketBees) that reached over 40% of online volumes by the fourth quarter of FY26. Management scores well (0.8 on a scale where 1 is best) and the culture review credits a founder-led, stable team that admitted openly it was "late" on logistics, then delivered: India growth improved in each quarter of FY26 (7.5%, 7.9%, 8.9%, 11.4%), RocketBees beat its own city and volume targets, and there have been no acquisitions since September 2022 after the GlobalBees roll-up required several quarters of cleaning up low-growth, loss-making acquired brands. Customer benefits score positively (0.75), and the fashion-risk channel is rated low because demand is need-based and age-driven rather than trend-driven, with 11 million customers transacting each year replenishing the base.

The trouble is that the same build-it-yourself trait that creates the pattern also explains most of the risks. The company is spending to own stores, brands and last-mile delivery in an industry the framework scores as having low entry barriers and irrational pricing, with competitive advantage rated at zero and industry structure at -0.5. "Cost to Replicate" is only a partial fit for exactly this reason: the 15-year asset base is expensive and slow for a newcomer to rebuild, yet the calls themselves describe well-funded horizontal players and quick-commerce entrants already inside the market. Two risks are rated elevated. New entrants pressured India growth and cost about 140 basis points of gross margin over two consecutive quarters, concentrated in diapering, which is also the "good enough goods" exposure: a commoditised category where cheaper, promotional competition bites hardest. The defensive response was to build FirstCry Qwik for three-hour delivery off the store and dark-store network, which is forward integration again, this time as damage control rather than offence. The second elevated risk, cyclicality, shows up in management repeatedly attributing growth misses to a consumption slowdown, weather, geopolitical conflict, and in the fourth quarter of FY26 crude-linked input costs and a weaker rupee. Three further risks sit on watch: technology and format change (customers now expect delivery in hours), shifting channel preferences, and supplier concentration, where consolidation among third-party logistics providers degraded deliveries and depressed India growth for multiple quarters.

The financial record is where the story turns cautious, and it lines up with the competitive picture rather than contradicting it. Pricing power is a clear no-fit: gross margins fell on competitive intensity and the strategic answer was to cut prices, not raise them, with the "width to depth" programme deliberately converting cost savings into lower shelf prices. Growth is therefore coming from more customers and more orders (both up around 10%) rather than from price, so market-share gain is judged only a likely fit and the underlying check is scored negative, because 8-11% growth in India alongside admitted losses in diapering to horizontals and quick commerce looks more like giving ground than taking it, and management never quotes a share number. Profitability is thin and unstable: about 3 rupees of operating profit per 100 rupees of sales, margins swinging by roughly 61 points a year, and a 6% cash return on the capital employed, which is why return on capital scores the worst of any area at -1.0. Growth capex is real but deliberately asset-light, and incremental returns on it are still unproven. The combined rating is 48 out of 100 (49 on business quality, 33 on multibagger fit, 64 on risk safety), a Mixed two-star verdict, with resilience judged sound. In plain terms: a genuinely differentiated, well-run distribution machine in a category with structural demand, operating in an industry that does not currently let it earn a good return on what it has built.

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 2 numeric fingerprints reinforce it.

**What the calls show:** Across FY26 calls management repeatedly attributes growth misses to a broad-based consumption slowdown, weather (unusually soft summer, early monsoon), geopolitical conflict and, in Q4 FY26, crude-linked raw material prices and rupee depreciation hitting manufacturing gross margin — but demand is replenishment-led (diapering, baby care) rather than tied to any customer's capex cycle, and margins moved in a narrow band while EBITDA kept compounding.

> "rupee depreciation as well as crude-linked raw material prices, which has gone up... these are very transitional in nature. These will be passed on to the customers" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/consumable, operations-linked demand base (repeat baby-care purchases), and consol adjusted EBITDA still grew 24-25% y-o-y through the slowdown with free cash flow positive FY26*
