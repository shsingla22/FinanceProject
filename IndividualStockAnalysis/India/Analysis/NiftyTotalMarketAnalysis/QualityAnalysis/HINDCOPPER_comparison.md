### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: NO FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: No consumer or B2B brand discussion, A&P spend, or premium-pricing evidence appears in the one call in the window. (The long view had said: The product is an undifferentiated concentrate sold on a price formula; no A&P, heritage positioning or consumer franchise appears anywhere in the call.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: NO FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: No R&D spend, pipeline or named product innovation is disclosed; the only technology reference is a bought-in concentrator plant, which is capex rather than an innovation engine. (The long view had said: No R&D spend, pipeline or proprietary technology; the company imports European winders because they are not made in India and is importing operating know-how from Codelco rather than generating it.)

**That weakened:**

- **Cost to Replicate**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The genuinely hard-to-replicate asset is the mining lease bank itself — three states, leases to 2040-41, with forest and environmental clearances that took decades and defeated prior managements; but the barrier protects access to a commodity, not economics, since realisation and cost are both set outside the company. (The long view had said: The barrier is concrete and locational: HCL holds essentially all of India's producing copper ore leases across three states with tenure to 2040-42, and the binding constraint — central plus state forest and environmental clearance — took multiple management generations to obtain, with private licence holders still unable to start.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Management itself frames costs as structurally high and geology-constrained — $5,000/t at Malanjkhand, ~$6,000/t at Khetri and Jharkhand, with an admitted inverted (top-heavy) employee structure; there is no itemised cost-culture or scale-driven savings narrative. (The long view had said: Management cites $5,000/t at Malanjkhand as 'very competitive' and expects an inverted age-heavy manpower structure to optimise cost as seniors retire, but there is no itemised cost culture, and Khetri/Jharkhand at ~$6,000/t plus sub-scale volumes (4mt vs Codelco's 200mt) undercut any scale-based cost advantage.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT); Market Share Gainers (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
