# Vikram Solar (VIKRAMSOLR) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Vikram Solar makes solar panels (solar PV modules) in India and sells them to whoever is building solar power projects — large utility-scale developers and public-sector companies, commercial and industrial buyers, and rooftop/retail customers reached through a distributor and dealer network. It earns money by manufacturing and shipping modules against a large order book on what management calls a cost-plus basis, so rising input costs are largely passed through to customers in supply contracts. Sales are overwhelmingly domestic — it has shipped to 39 export markets since inception and has a pan-India presence across 19 states and two union territories with 98 distributors, 375+ dealers and 76+ system integrators — and FY26 revenue was about INR 4,800 crores on 3.3 gigawatts of module sales. The company is now spending heavily to build the rest of the supply chain itself (cells, then wafers and ingots) and to add battery storage, moving from selling a product to selling 'solar plus storage' power solutions.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Solar PV module manufacturing** — The core and effectively the entire current business — making N-type TOPCon solar panels at plants in Falta (West Bengal) and the new 5 GW Vallam (Tamil Nadu) facility, taking installed capacity to 9.5 GW, with a further 6 GW at Gangaikondan lifting it to 15.5 GW.
- **Utility-scale segment (domestic)** — Selling modules in bulk to large public and private solar project developers — the biggest demand driver, with named wins such as 336 MW from L&T, 326 MW from GIPCL and 250 MW from Bondada Group, and an order book of 10.96 GW as of June 2025.
- **Commercial & industrial (C&I) segment** — Modules sold to businesses installing solar to cut power bills and meet decarbonisation targets; management calls it a growing part of demand alongside utility-scale, but gives no separate revenue figure.
- **Retail / rooftop / distribution segment** — Modules sold to smaller and rooftop customers through an exclusive network of authorised distributors, dealers and system integrators, supported by government schemes like PM Surya Ghar and PM-KUSUM.
- **Exports** — Shipments to overseas customers — historically 39 markets including the U.S. — but currently a small share, as almost all shipments are domestic; management says improving costs should make Europe and MENA more competitive over time.
- **Solar cell manufacturing (backward integration, under construction)** — A planned 12 GW TOPCon cell plant at Gangaikondan — the second stage of integration, with first cell output targeted around end-December 2026/January 2027 and full ramp-up by Q2 FY28; today cells are bought in, increasingly from Southeast Asia rather than China.
- **Wafer and ingot manufacturing (planned)** — The third and final integration stage — a 6 GW wafer-ingot facility scaling to 12 GW by FY30, aimed at replacing material India currently imports almost entirely from China; still being evaluated for commercial viability.
- **Battery energy storage (BESS)** — A new growth area management calls the next frontier, targeting 15 GWh of capacity by FY30 so the company can sell solar plus storage as dispatchable power rather than just panels; nothing operating yet.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*
