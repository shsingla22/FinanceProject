### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 5. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 3 weakened, 6 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Explicit, repeated share-gain framing with numbers: 13.1% domestic volume growth in Q1 FY27 against a slower industry, and a multi-quarter table of UltraTech volume/EBITDA per ton versus 'rest of the industry'; the gains come with rising per-ton EBITDA rather than bought volume. (The long view had said: Share gain is quantified call after call — capacity from ~65 mt (2016) to 200 mt (2026) reached a year early, plus a quarter-by-quarter volume comparison against the rest of the industry, and it is explicitly gained profitably (EBITDA/t consistently above industry), not bought with price.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Q4 FY26 showed ~2.5% grey cement price improvement plus premiumisation and converting B/C-brand buyers to a price premium, but Q2–Q3 FY26 prices were subdued post-GST and management admits cost escalations of the past two quarters are still not fully passed through — pricing power is real but not consistent through the year. (The long view had said: UltraTech consistently realizes above the industry through premiumisation, trade mix and brand premium, and by FY26 converted acquired B/C-grade brands to price-premium UltraTech volumes; but the record also includes genuine price deflation years (Q2 FY25 EBITDA/t at a 4–5 year low, putty prices 'taken a beating'), so pricing power is cyclical rather than unconditional.)

**That weakened:**

- **Cost to Replicate**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The calls articulate concrete replication barriers — 200mt in a single country (unique outside China), full clinker/limestone backing, 1.8GW captive green power, 400+ RMC plants and pan-India logistics built over a decade — but cement remains a regional commodity where well-funded rivals are adding capacity, so the barrier is capital-and-time, not absolute. (The long view had said: Management articulates concrete, non-replicable barriers: secured limestone behind every committed tonne of expansion, 76 operating facilities with ~2,000 warehouses and 150,000 channel partners, and the arithmetic that greenfield capacity costs $110–120/t and takes 7 years — a well-funded copycat cannot rebuild a 200 mt footprint or the reserve base in a few years.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: LIKELY FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: No R&D spend, pipeline, named new products or share-of-industry-R&D disclosure appears in any of the four calls; premiumisation and blended cement mix are discussed as marketing/mix choices, not as innovation output. (The long view had said: There is steady product-mix innovation — value-added products above 18% of sales, white-cement-based liquid primer, putty capacity, GreenPro/EPD-certified green portfolio, and now cables & wires — but no R&D spend is ever disclosed and no claim to a share of industry R&D is made.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Management explicitly frames the buying decision as sitting with the end customer (the individual home builder) rather than a prescribing professional, and the channel discussion is about own retail stores and dealers, not trade training or installer recommendation. (The long view had said: Sales run through a vast trade channel — ~150,000 channel partners, 5,000+ dedicated UBS outlets, dedicated transporters — but management explicitly frames the decision-maker as the end customer (the individual home builder), and no trade training programmes or professional prescriber lock-in are described.)

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 2 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Cement is a commodity whose realisations swing with pricing cycles and input costs: prices 'remained subdued post GST change' with softening through Sept–Nov '25, then improved ~2.5% in Q4 and 'constructive' in Q1 FY27, while a West Asia oil shock pushed coal costs 'to the roof' and hit EBITDA/ton via bags, freight and FX. Demand is explicitly tied to infrastructure and real-estate capex cycles, and the company is in a very heavy expansion phase (200mt reached, +37mt committed to 242.5mt by FY28) — classic expansionary over-investment risk, though so far funded from accruals. (The long view had said: Across the 17 calls results swing with monsoons, elections, fuel prices and cement price cycles — Q2FY23 saw prices sliding and costs spiking, Q2FY25 EBITDA/ton hit a 4-5 year low on ~68% utilisation, and FY26-27 results were hit by the West Asia crude shock; management itself calls the industry cyclical and demand is levered to infra/housing capex programmes while ~INR8,000-10,000cr/year of capacity capex is committed regardless.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: NO SIGNAL → LOW. Why now: The demand base is functional and expanding rather than eroding — urbanisation from 35% to ~39% by 2030, PMAY housing, redevelopment and commercial real estate — and the only preference movement described runs in the company's favour: trade mix, blended cement and premium portfolio contribution all rising, with B/C brand buyers converted to a premium A-category brand. (The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and the only computed fingerprint is clean.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
