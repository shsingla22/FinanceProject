# H.E.G. Ltd. (HEG) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: INDUSTRIAL MANUFACTURING*

HEG Limited makes graphite electrodes — the large consumable carbon rods that electric arc furnaces need to melt scrap and make steel — from a single plant near Bhopal, India, with an installed capacity of 100,000 tonnes a year, which management says is by far the world's largest electrode plant at one location (the next two biggest are around 70,000 tonnes). Its customers are steel mills running electric arc furnaces, in both ultra-high-power (UHP) and non-UHP grades; management says about two-thirds of output is exported to 25–30 countries, and it does not sell electrodes directly to China. It makes money on the spread between electrode selling prices and its main raw material, needle coke, plus power and fuel costs, so profits swing with global steel output, electrode prices and needle coke prices — FY26 revenue was about Rs. 2,569 crores with EBITDA of Rs. 497 crores at more than 90% capacity utilisation. The company has no long-term debt, holds a treasury portfolio (about Rs. 792 crores at March 2026) plus an equity stake in GrafTech, and is building capacity out to 115,000 tonnes by early 2028 on the view that global decarbonisation is shifting steelmaking toward electric arc furnaces.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Graphite electrodes** — The core and by far the dominant business — manufacturing UHP and non-UHP graphite electrodes at the 100,000-tonne Bhopal plant for electric arc furnace steel mills worldwide; it accounts for essentially all of the roughly Rs. 2,500-crore annual revenue discussed on the calls.
- **HEG Greentech (later referred to as HEG Advanced Materials)** — A separate green-energy and materials arm holding hydro power assets, some wind assets, a battery-storage business and a planned graphite anode project for batteries; management put its consolidated EBITDA at roughly Rs. 125 crores in late 2024, small next to the electrode business, and it is being separated out and listed via a court-approved scheme of arrangement (NCLT order awaited as of July 2026).
- **Treasury and investments** — A debt-free balance sheet with a treasury book of roughly Rs. 800–1,150 crores over the period, earning interest and mark-to-market gains or losses, plus a deliberate long-term equity investment in GrafTech; these swing 'other income' and quarterly reported profit sharply — a Rs. 189-crore quarterly loss in Q4 FY26 came mainly from fair-value and currency effects on that foreign investment, not from operations.
- **Capacity expansion to 115,000 tonnes** — An ongoing electrode capacity addition of 15,000 tonnes targeted for early 2028, following the completed 80,000-to-100,000-tonne expansion, aimed at capturing demand from over 100 million tonnes of new electric arc furnace steel capacity management expects globally by 2030.

### The story in depth

H.E.G. is, on the evidence here, a genuinely good operator in a difficult business. It runs the world's largest single-location graphite electrode plant at 100,000 tons against the next two at about 70,000 tons, and it kept that plant above 90% utilisation for four straight quarters while foreign competitors sat at 50-55% and Indian ones at 60-65%. Volumes grew 20% in FY26 even as global crude steel output fell about 2%, and it is the only producer expanding — from 80,000 to 100,000 and on to 115,000 tons — at a time when, in management's words, 'there is nobody who has even announced anything as yet.' The management score is the strongest of any quality area (+0.67): the promoter family stayed long-term debt-free right through the 2022-26 downturn with roughly Rs 800-1,150 crore of treasury, delivered the 100,000-ton expansion as promised, and tracked the demerger call by call. That balance sheet is why the resilience read is SOUND despite everything below. Yet not one of the eleven multibagger patterns scored a clean fit — the best of them (Toll Roads, Low Price Plus, Cost to Replicate, Market Share Gainers, Corporate Culture) all came back PARTIAL, and the multibagger component of the rating is therefore zero.

The reason the patterns stall short is that the same traits that create them also create the risks. Being the biggest, lowest-cost single site is what supports Low Price Plus and Cost to Replicate — but it is also why every Rs 1 of assets produces only Rs 0.42 of sales, why the last expansion cost Rs 1,200 crore for just 20,000 tons of extra capacity, and why the business returned only about Rs 4 of operating cash for every Rs 100 of capital employed this year. Add roughly 323 days of cash tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and Return on Capital comes out as the single worst area at -1.0. Similarly, the three-to-four player oligopoly makes electrodes look like a toll road — under 1% of the steel cost yet indispensable to running an electric arc furnace — and it is why the New Entrants risk is LOW. But that same oligopoly is where HEG sits as a follower, not a leader: after competitors announced hikes of $600-$1,200 and $930 in July 2026, management said 'we will follow suit' from October. Hence Pricing Power is a flat NO FIT, and Good Enough Goods is an ELEVATED risk, because announced hikes have repeatedly failed to stick when demand was weak and the company's own defence is being cheapest rather than being preferred. The bright spots in the outlook carry their own shadow the same way: the strong structural demand score (+2, about 100 million tons of new electric arc furnace capacity outside China by 2030, worth roughly 200,000 tons of extra electrode demand) rests entirely on carbon rules, CBAM, EU tariff quotas and Section 232 — which is precisely why Government Dependency is rated HIGH RISK, with US countervailing and anti-dumping proceedings able to restrict HEG's own exports. And the cyclical bottoming signal (+2) is a positive reading of the same cycle that makes Cyclicality a HIGH RISK, having already swung the company from a +75% revenue quarter in Q1 FY23 to order push-backs and margins compressed to about 17% in FY25.

Capital allocation is where the operating strength does not yet convert into shareholder returns, and it sits at -0.2. Sustained growth spending scores positively — the Rs 1,200 crore expansion is complete and stabilised, a further 15,000-ton line is targeted for early 2028, and there is a Rs 1,750 crore anode plant — but that anode project's own stated payback is 9-10 years, and the research effort behind it, while real (an 18-month pilot testing 28 raw-material combinations, now sampling with LG, Panasonic and CATL), is a single new product rather than a repeatable innovation engine, which is why Innovation Dominance is only PARTIAL. The other main use of capital is a large equity stake in competitor GrafTech, which is neither a bolt-on nor operationally complementary and whose market-value swings drove a Rs 189 crore quarterly loss in Q4 FY26. Meanwhile long-run sales growth has averaged about 7% a year and is lumpy from year to year, so persistence of growth scores -2. The arithmetic that follows is unforgiving: 45% of a business-quality score of 49, plus 30% of a multibagger fit of zero, plus 25% of a risk-safety score of 46, gives 34 out of 100 — a Weak, one-star verdict. The honest read is a well-run, financially conservative company holding a defensible position in a small oligopoly, whose product is essential but whose price it does not set, and whose returns on the capital it must keep sinking into that position have so far been poor. Survival is not the question here; earning a decent return through the cycle is.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 2 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across all 17 calls results swing with the global steel cycle and the electrode/needle-coke spread: Q1FY23 was a peak (revenue +75% year-on-year, prices up >50% in four quarters) followed by order push-backs, then FY25 margin compression to ~17% with utilisation cut to 80%, then FY27 recovery to 29% EBITDA margins; management itself concedes the industry is cyclical.

> "Cyclical volatility is intrinsic to this industry, and interim fluctuations in no way alter our outlook" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Electrodes are a consumable used in the customer's steel operations (not customer capex), plus long-term debt-free balance sheet with ~INR 800-1,150 crore treasury and counter-cyclical expansion (80k to 100k tons through the downturn, now 115k by 2028) while 'nobody... has even announced anything as yet'*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 5 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from -4% to 71% (swinging ±22.8 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 10% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 20%).
