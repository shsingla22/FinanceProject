**Checks that improved:**

- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹44 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹44 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±12.0 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Alumina spot has collapsed to ~$305–310 from ~$580 on Middle East curtailments and new Indonesian/Indian supply — plausibly near a cyclical trough — while metal LME is firm at ~$2,700 with China capped at 45 Mt. | The long view had said: Alumina is deep in a down-cycle ($310-320 spot) but management sees continued oversupply from Indonesian refineries with no identified upturn, while aluminium LME is already near highs — so entry is not near a clear cyclical bottom.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: Long-dated plans exist (smelter by 2030, refinery ramp to FY28) but this is a 51.28% Government-owned Navratna CPSE with rotating board appointees, so independence is structurally limited and tenacity is untested on the delayed projects. | The long view had said: Long-horizon ambitions exist but execution slips repeatedly — refinery commissioning moved from Sept 2025 to June 2026 with ramp-up guidance cut from 5-6 lakh tons to 2 lakh tons, and the smelter zero-date drifted from May 2026 to a 2030-31 completion.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: Customers face essentially zero switching costs; roughly three of four monthly shipments are spot and buyers are re-tendered each month rather than locked in. | The long view had said: Buyers face zero switching costs — alumina and metal are re-tendered constantly and customers shifted away when the Middle East disrupted, with pricing set entirely by external benchmarks.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Poor → Weak. Now: No proprietary technology edge is claimed; the advantage is conventional process efficiency (caustic soda, CP coke, furnace oil consumption) on standard Bayer/smelting technology, with rare-earth extraction technology being outsourced to NML. | The long view had said: Technology is bought in, not owned — external consultants prepare DPRs and a technology licensor is being appointed for the new smelter, with no proprietary multi-product edge.
- **Distribution to shareholders** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: Consistent dividend distribution alongside record profits (final dividend of Rs.2.50/share recommended), though no buyback or valuation-linked distribution discipline is evidenced. | The long view had said: Regular dividends are paid (₹2.50 final recommended) and governance is praised for dividend policy, but there is no buyback discipline or valuation-linked distribution framework — payout is a PSU convention.
- **Candor** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Management openly quantifies adverse price impacts, admits the refinery slipped from September 2025 to June 2026, corrects an analyst's mis-recollection of prior guidance, and concedes some techno-economic parameters were poor. | The long view had said: Disclosure is unusually granular — cost bridges, caustic/CP coke specific consumption, one-off provisions quantified — and management openly concedes delays and capex overruns rather than deflecting.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: No mindshare or aftermarket monopoly — output is undifferentiated alumina and cast metal sold via monthly spot tenders at transparent LME-linked prices. | The long view had said: Output is an undifferentiated commodity sold via spot tenders at LME-linked prices with no aftermarket or mind-share position.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Active new entry into refining and smelting plus spot price collapse to $305 indicates weak entry barriers and price-taking rather than rational pricing; the only barrier cited is bauxite resource access disrupted in Guinea and Ghana. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±12.0 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Capital intensity and mine leases are real barriers, but mines are now openly auctioned with no PSU preference and fresh global capacity keeps entering, so entry is constrained but not closed.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Neutral. Now: Only R&D signal is an early-stage MoU with NML Jamshedpur for a pilot to extract rare earths from red mud, still 1.5–2 years from a methodology, with no track record of R&D-driven products. | The long view had said: R&D is limited to outsourced MoUs (NML Jamshedpur, BARC, a Chennai party) for red-mud critical minerals and gallium with no commercial output after years of effort.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: A Director (HR) is on every call and employees are credited for results, and the new Director (Commercial) was inducted internally-styled, but no systematic talent-development or succession programme is described. | The long view had said: Talent is discussed mainly as a cost line — savings from high-paid superannuations replaced by lower-scale recruits — with no evidence of a systematic leadership pipeline, and top leadership changes by government appointment (CMD changed from Patra to Singh).
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: A dedicated Vizag port facility with silo storage enabling four to five shipments a month, plus a deliberate push into direct domestic smelter relationships, gives real accessibility advantage. | The long view had said: A dedicated Vizag port facility and a deliberate push into domestic direct sales give reasonable accessibility, but there is no differentiated sales-force intimacy beyond tenders.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: Global alumina is fragmented and swinging into surplus with new Indonesian and Indian capacity, and buyers clearly hold the whip hand — spot prices fell to ~$305 when Middle East smelters curtailed. | The long view had said: Indian primary aluminium is a three-player structure (NALCO, Hindalco, Vedanta) but the global commodity price and new Indonesian/Indian capacity strip any collective pricing power from producers.
- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Management frames aluminium demand as tracking India's 6.5–6.7% GDP growth and the Viksit Bharat 2047 build-out, with global aluminium in slight deficit and Chinese supply capped — a genuinely expanding end market. | The long view had said: End market is in secular expansion — ~9% Indian aluminium consumption growth, Ministry of Mines vision document demand projections, and EV/solar/electrical drivers on a 6.5-7% GDP base.

Now: Purely organic, no M&A, backward integration into captive coal, and continued counter-cyclical expansion capex while alumina prices halved; no leverage stress mentioned. | The long view had said: Zero-debt balance sheet funding a multi-thousand-crore expansion entirely from internal accruals, purely organic growth, no acquisitions, and investment continued through the alumina price downturn.

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
