# OneSource Speci. (ONESOURCE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

OneSource Specialty Pharma (formerly Stelis Biopharma, carved out of Strides and listed on the BSE and NSE in January 2025) is a contract development and manufacturing organisation — it does not mainly sell its own branded medicines, it develops and manufactures drugs for other pharmaceutical companies under their labels. Management calls it India's first specialty pharma CDMO and a "one-stop shop": from sites in and around Bengaluru it makes drug-device combinations (injector pens and cartridges, notably generic semaglutide and other GLP-1s), biologics, sterile injectables and soft gelatin capsules, so a customer can buy several of these from one supplier. It earns money in two stages — MSA fees for pre-approval development and readiness work, and CSA revenue from commercial supply once the customer's product is approved (steady state is expected to be roughly 80% commercial, 20% development) — plus take-or-pay and reservation fees, and in some cases a profit share with the partner (for example with NATCO). Customers are global and Indian generic and biotech firms (60-plus logos, over 20 in GLP-1 alone, including Dr. Reddy's, NATCO and Formycon), supplying India, Canada, the US, MENA and other emerging markets; quarterly revenue has run from about INR393 crores in Q3 FY25 to INR4,490 million in Q1 FY27, with EBITDA margins in the mid-30s to about 40%, and guidance of US$400 million of organic revenue at ~40% margins by FY28.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Drug Device Combinations (DDC)** — Fills and assembles injectable pens and cartridges — above all generic semaglutide and other GLP-1s — for around 20 global generic customers; it is the company's main growth engine and current earnings driver, described as fully sold out with new customer onboarding paused, running a US$100 million capex to add cartridge lines toward more than 200 million pen units of capacity by end-CY2026, and it supplied the first three generic semaglutide approvals in the US and Canada plus over 40% of India's generic pen market by June 2026.
- **Biologics** — Develops and manufactures biologic drug substance (both mammalian and microbial) and biosimilars for innovators, biosimilar firms and animal health companies; still small ("nascent") but flagged as the second growth pillar, with 4,000 litres installed capacity said to be filling up and needing expansion, an RFP funnel roughly 4x larger than a year earlier, and new partners added in Europe, the US and with Formycon.
- **Sterile Injectables (Steriscience plant)** — The 20-year-old, FDA-approved flagship injectables site making sterile injectable products on a "scarcity play" focus (niche, hard-to-make products); part of the base business and currently going through a major retrofit and capacity expansion due to complete in FY27.
- **Soft Gelatin Capsules** — Long-standing softgel development and manufacturing operation, historically making Strides' own IP-led products and now being opened up to outside CDMO customers; capacity was raised from 800 million to 2.4 billion capsules against roughly INR550 crores of revenue, management expects the added capacity to fill over the next 12–15 months and has begun planning a greenfield site.
- **MSA and CSA revenue streams** — Not a separate factory but how management splits the money: MSA is pre-approval development and readiness income (which dominated FY25), CSA is commercial supply income after a customer's product is approved, with FY26 described as the cusp year and steady state expected at about 80% CSA / 20% MSA.
- **Steriscience injectables and Brooks (carbapenems) — deferred acquisition** — A related-party scheme to fold in the family/TPG-owned Steriscience injectables business and Brooks (51%-owned, makes carbapenems), which would have lifted the FY28 target from US$400 million to US$500 million; it was approved by SEBI and the exchanges but deferred in May 2026 over valuation concerns from smaller shareholders, so it is not part of the company today.

### The story in depth

OneSource Speci. is a contract manufacturer that has built a genuinely scarce position in one narrow, fast-growing corner of pharmaceuticals: filling generic semaglutide into pens and cartridges. The quality checks score it at the top of the scale for market-share gains (customers holding two-thirds of India's generic semaglutide market by value, roughly 40% of Indian generic pens made at its site, and all three Canadian generic semaglutide approvals), for a near-monopoly in that fill-finish step, and for switching costs, because a customer's regulatory filing names the specific manufacturing site and cannot easily be moved once approved. The multibagger screen agrees, calling Market Share Gainers a strong fit and Pricing Power and Innovation Dominance likely fits, with the scarcity showing up as sold-out drug-device capacity, a halt on onboarding new customers, take-or-pay contracts and capacity reservation fees. The risk review reaches the same conclusion from the other side: New Entrants, Good Enough Goods, Fashion Risk and Shifting Consumer Preferences are all rated Low, because long equipment lead times, sterile isolator capability, a rare FDA-approved penicillin plant and a record of 49 audits in FY26 are hard to copy, and because demand is repeat prescriptions for chronic therapy rather than a taste-driven fad. Overall the combined rating is 55 out of 100, a Decent three stars, with resilience judged Sound.

The catch is that the very things that create the moat also create the strain, and this is the central tension in the file. Being the named, approved, hard-to-replace site requires owning a lot of plant: every rupee of assets produces only about 17 paise of sales, roughly 230 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and this year every 100 rupees of shareholders' funds and borrowings generated 0 rupees of operating cash - a 0% cash return on capital. The Return on Capital area scores negative overall (-1.33) and Capital Allocation sits at zero. Management is spending into this rather than pulling back: a $100mn growth programme is expanding pen capacity roughly five-fold, from 40mn to 220mn units, pulled forward a year, with fully committed back-to-back lines and a 50%-plus return target, while FY26 revenue of INR14,216mn was flat-to-down and net debt rose. That capex is scored as a strength because it buys growth rather than merely maintaining what exists, and it is the same spending that keeps the Cost to Replicate and Toll Roads barriers standing - but until the new lines convert into cash, the asset-heaviness, the long cash cycle and the zero cash return are all one problem, not three.

The second tension is concentration. The share gains that make the Market Share Gainers pattern a strong fit come overwhelmingly from one molecule and a small set of large customers, which is precisely why Stakeholder Concentration is rated Elevated: a single named customer, Dr. Reddy's, has driven the story twice - the Novo litigation naming both parties in November 2025 and an announced supply disruption in July 2026. Because revenue is only recognised when regulators and courts allow a customer's product to launch, Government Dependency is the one High Risk in the file; management itself told investors in November 2025 that the following two quarters were 'ambiguity' because outcomes rested on court decisions and partner approvals. That gating is also the explanation for the lumpiness the quality checks penalise: average sales growth of about 132% a year, but wildly uneven, with EBITDA margin moving from 36% to 28% to a loss-making quarter and then rising five-fold in the next one, all while full-year FY26 revenue fell 2%. The offsetting evidence is real and is scored as such - candour rates a full 2 for pre-warning investors, declining to guide on things outside management's control, disclosing the customer disruption openly, and deferring the related-party Steriscience/Brookes scheme in May 2026 despite having SEBI approval, because minority holders questioned the valuation. Launches are also being spread across Canada, India, Brazil, Saudi Arabia and MENA so no single regulator gates the whole book, and the customer base is broadening to 20-plus GLP-1 customers and 12 customers spanning multiple modalities. The honest reading is a business with a strong, defensible niche riding real tailwinds - GLP-1 demand, outsourcing by innovators moving away from Chinese suppliers, simpler biosimilar pathways - whose economics have not yet arrived, and whose timing is in other people's hands.

## What to watch

- Cash return on capital: the Return on Capital check is at 0% today; the test is whether the $100mn pen expansion (Capex for growth, scored +2) starts converting into operating cash as the 40mn-to-220mn unit capacity fills, or whether net debt keeps rising against flat revenue.
