# Newgen Software (NEWGEN) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Newgen Software is an India-headquartered enterprise software company that sells a single core software platform, NewgenONE, which bundles document/content management, business process automation, customer communication, low-code app development, analytics and (more recently) AI and 'agentic AI' capabilities into one system that big organisations use to run paperwork-heavy processes end to end. Its customers are large, regulated, process-intensive institutions — above all banks and financial services firms, plus insurers and healthcare, and some government bodies and large corporates — who buy ready-made 'journey' solutions built on the platform such as loan origination, digital account opening, trade finance, supply chain finance and insurance policy administration. It makes money three ways: upfront software licences, recurring cloud/SaaS and subscription fees, and implementation/services plus annual support and maintenance; recurring 'annuity' revenue was INR968 crores, or 62% of FY26 revenue of INR1,574 crores, with subscription revenue up 24% and SaaS up 36%. It sells globally across four regions — EMEA, India, the USA and APAC (roughly INR114/96/92/56 crores respectively in Q1 FY27) — mostly through its own direct sales force (about 80%) with the rest via partners and system integrators, and employs around 4,300 people.

**The parts of the business:**

- **NewgenONE platform (low-code digital transformation platform)** — The company's flagship product that unifies content, process, communication, low-code, analytics and AI into one 'orchestration layer', now with embedded AI agents, governance and auditability — it is the foundation on which essentially all other offerings and revenue sit.
- **Banking and financial services vertical** — The largest customer segment by far, contributing about INR225 crores in Q1 FY27 (growing 5%), through solutions like retail and corporate loan origination, digital account opening, trade finance, supply chain finance and business financing sold to banks worldwide.
- **Insurance and healthcare vertical** — A newer but fast-growing focus area — about INR79 crores in Q1 FY27, growing 58% — where the company has been building out its team and products, notably core insurance policy administration and policy binding systems sold in mature markets.
- **Cloud, SaaS and subscription revenue** — The recurring, pay-over-time way of buying the software, which management treats as the key structural shift in the business — INR525 crores in FY26 (up 24%), with the SaaS piece up 36% for the year and 44% in Q4, improving revenue visibility and smoothing the old licence-driven seasonality.
- **Licence revenue** — The traditional upfront software sale that historically made Q4 heavy and Q1 lean; still material but shrinking in mix, and weak licence sales were cited as the reason India's revenue was muted in FY26.
- **Implementation and services revenue** — The work of deploying and configuring the software at each customer, which typically follows licence wins with a lag (large Q4 FY24 licences drove an implementation uptick in Q1 FY25) and which was weak in Q1 FY27 due to slow project starts, especially in EMEA.
- **Annuity revenue (support, maintenance and subscriptions combined)** — Management's headline measure of recurring income — INR968 crores or 62% of FY26 revenue, up from 56% in FY25, and about INR254 crores growing 14% in Q1 FY27 — used to show the business is becoming more predictable.
- **Geographies: EMEA, India, USA, APAC** — Revenue is split across four regions with India and EMEA historically the largest contributors and the US the fastest-growing recently (up 17% in FY26 and 27% in Q1 FY27), while EMEA and India have faced delayed large-deal closures from geopolitical uncertainty.
- **LumYn** — A Gen-AI powered 'hyper personalization' and growth-intelligence product launched in FY25 specifically for banks, aimed at improving customer engagement and profitability; described as early-stage in terms of revenue contribution.
- **Partner and alliance channel** — Sales through system integrators and technology partners (examples cited include Coforge, Anabatic Digital and Finastra), which management says has stayed steady at roughly 20% of revenue versus 80% direct.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Newgen's core appeal is a software business where almost nothing is spent on direct inputs — nearly all of every ₹100 of sales is left over for people, marketing and profit — and where 26 of every ₹100 of sales currently comes through as operating profit. Two of the eleven patterns are rated a strong fit, and both rest on that same economic base. The first is recurring revenue: management frames the whole business around annuity, maintenance and subscription income, which has risen from 56% to 62% of revenue, with subscription up 24% and the cloud-based portion up 36-44%. The second is sustained innovation: research and development has run at 8.5% to 11% of revenue for four straight years alongside 22-26% on sales and marketing, producing named launches every year and 67 patent filings. The high gross margin is what pays for both at once. Sales have compounded at about 16% a year with unusual year-to-year consistency, and the company invests in its people systematically — campus and lateral hiring, an internal CEO succession, and two consecutive Great Place to Work certifications.

The share-gain story is credible but not yet proven in the way a stronger verdict would need. Customers billing more than ₹5 crore rose from 87 to 101 in a single year, 47 new customers were added in FY26, and US revenue grew 17% in FY26 and 27% in the first quarter of FY27 — though off a small base, and the US was promised as an acceleration engine back in 2022 and has been slow to deliver. Management offers no share figures against named competitors, so that pattern sits at 'likely' rather than confirmed. Several other patterns land only partially, and each one is partial for a reason worth noting. The India-based cost structure lets Newgen spend 20-26% of revenue on selling versus 40-45% for US peers and still make money at roughly $100m of scale — a genuine advantage, but not the classic low-price model. Partners such as system integrators bring in a steady 20% of revenue, yet management admits it has little control over those deals, while 80% of revenue comes from selling directly. Reputation assets are real — Gartner inclusion, a commissioned return-on-investment study, a CRISIL A1 rating — without evidence of true brand pricing power, which is why the pricing-power pattern shows up only as a numbers-based hint that the management commentary does not back up. Founder-led continuity and candid disclosure of bad news support the culture pattern, but a repeatedly missed 20% growth ambition holds it back.

The risks are not separate from the strengths — in most cases they are the same trait viewed from the other side. Cyclicality is rated elevated precisely because the lumpy upfront licence sales that powered 30-40% growth for two years then stalled, cutting growth from 25% in the first quarter of FY25 to 6% for FY26; the push into recurring revenue that makes the annuity pattern a strong fit is management's own answer to that swing, and 62% annuity revenue is exactly the cushion. Technology change is also elevated, and it too mirrors a strength: the AI platform shift that drives the innovation pattern is the same uncertainty causing customers to defer large purchases while they re-decide whether the spend is still worth it, which feeds directly into the 'cheaper alternative may be good enough' concern. The migration from perpetual licences to cloud subscriptions is a customer-preference shift that management has been managing for years, and it mechanically reduced reported revenue in FY26 even as it improved revenue quality. Public-sector exposure across Indian state-owned banks, a UAE tax system and APAC government work explains part of the muted India performance, offset by genuine spread across regions and private-sector verticals. Two housekeeping weaknesses stand out against an otherwise sound picture: roughly 164 days of cash is tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and operating margins have swung about five percentage points a year, suggesting some key costs sit outside management's hands. Capital allocation and return on capital are the weakest of the seven quality areas, while customer benefit and management score highest. Overall the framework puts business quality at 65, pattern fit at 66 and risk safety at 68, for a combined 66 out of 100 — a 'Strong' four-star rating with resilience judged sound.

### Innovation Dominance — STRONG FIT
*"Out-inventing the competition"*

### Market Share Gainers — LIKELY FIT
