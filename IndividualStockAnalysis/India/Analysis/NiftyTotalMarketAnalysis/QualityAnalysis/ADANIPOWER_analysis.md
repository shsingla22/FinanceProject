# Adani Power Ltd. (ADANIPOWER) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: ENERGY*

Adani Power Limited is an Indian electricity generator that runs a fleet of large coal-fired (thermal) power plants and sells the power they produce. Its main customers are state electricity distribution companies (DISCOMs) across India, plus Bangladesh, which buys the output of the Godda plant; the rest of its output is sold into the merchant and short-term markets at market prices. It earns money two ways: under long-term power purchase agreements (PPAs) it collects fixed capacity charges simply for keeping plants available, with fuel costs passed through to the buyer, and it collects energy charges on the units it actually dispatches; on untied capacity it sells electricity at prevailing market rates. It operates about 18.15 GW of installed capacity (as of December 2025) at plants including Mundra, Udupi, Raipur, Raigarh, Mahan, Godda, Korba, Dahanu, Tuticorin and Butibori, and is building toward 45 GW, with revenue of roughly INR 17,936 crore and profit after tax of INR 4,867 crore in the quarter ended June 2026.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Power sales under long-term PPAs** — Supplying electricity to state DISCOMs under contracts that pay a fixed capacity charge for plant availability while fuel is a pass-through; this is the core of the business — more than 90% of the 18.15 GW operating fleet is tied up under PPAs, and in Q1 FY27 PPA sales were 25 of 29 billion units at a realization of INR 5.93 per unit.
- **Merchant and short-term market sales** — Selling power from untied (uncontracted) capacity into the day-ahead, short-term and bilateral markets at floating prices, which boosts profit when demand and tariffs are high — 4 billion units in Q1 FY27 at INR 7.04 per unit, though management is deliberately shrinking this share by signing more PPAs to reduce price volatility.
- **Godda power plant (cross-border supply to Bangladesh)** — A 1,600 MW plant commissioned in FY24 that exports power to Bangladesh under contract; management notes payments from Bangladesh have been received on time.
- **Capacity expansion / project development** — Building new thermal units — a 23.7 GW expansion program with Mahan Phase-II, Raipur Phase-II, Raigarh Phase-II, Korba Phase-II, Mirzapur and a 3,200 MW greenfield project in Assam — with all 24 GW of main plant equipment already ordered, 56% of upcoming capacity tied up under long-term PPAs, and commissioning phased from FY27; management says these new PPAs carry higher capacity charges than legacy ones.
- **Acquisitions of existing power assets** — Buying and turning around distressed or shut-down plants — Mahan (FY22), Moxie Power (formerly Coastal Energen/Tuticorin), Korba (formerly Lanco Amarkantak), Dahanu, the Vidarbha/Butibori plant (acquired July 2025 and restarted under a 5-year Maharashtra DISCOM PPA), and in FY27 the Jaiprakash Associates insolvency assets including the 180 MW Churk plant plus minority stakes in Jaiprakash Power Ventures and Prayagraj Power Generation.
- **Fuel security / captive coal mining** — Acquiring coal blocks near its plants to control fuel supply — for example Stratatech Mineral Resources (the Dhirauli mine next to the Mahan plant), expected to supply 5 million tonnes of coal for Mahan's untied capacity; fuel is the single largest cost line, at INR 9,513 crore in Q1 FY27.
- **Regulatory claims / prior period income** — One-off recoveries of past dues from DISCOMs — domestic coal shortfall compensation, carrying cost and late payment surcharges — which were very large in FY24 (INR 9,227 crore in nine months) but have shrunk since as the major disputes were settled (INR 1,353 crore in 9M FY26).
- **New areas: international hydro and nuclear** — An early-stage diversification beyond coal, mentioned in July 2026 as international hydropower projects and preparation for future nuclear power opportunities; no revenue or size is given in the calls.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, Cyclicality, Stakeholder Concentration. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.8× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 95% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 8% to 24% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Fortunes are set almost entirely by state and central policy: revenues come from state DISCOM PPAs at regulator-approved tariffs, a large share of past profit was prior-period regulatory claims for domestic coal shortfall (INR 9,227 crore in 9M FY24, INR 2,420 crore in 9M FY25), the addressable bid pipeline exists only because states received coal under SHAKTI B(iv), the Rajasthan regulator blocked a 3,200 MW PPA on resource-adequacy grounds, fuel linkage for the Assam project depends on SHAKTI, and the nuclear plan cannot even be ordered until the government issues rules. Bangladesh adds sovereign counterparty exposure.

> "the regulator felt based on the submissions given that the full 3,200 megawatt PPA may not be required together, based on the resource adequacy plans" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Across FY24-FY27 the calls show classic cycle behaviour: merchant realisation fell from INR 6.86/unit (Q3FY24) to INR 4.54 and INR 4.37, PLF swung with weather (68.6% → 63.9% → 62.6% → 78%), and 9M FY26 revenue and EBITDA both declined year-on-year before Q1FY27 rebounded 27%/22% on a record heatwave — while the company commits over INR 2 lakh crore of capex for 24 GW at the top of a demand upcycle. Exposure is bounded because electricity is a flow/operations-linked product and management has deliberately shrunk merchant exposure from 20% to 10% of capacity, targeting 3-4%.

> "Merchant realization in terms of tariff for this quarter, is INR 4.37/kWh, as against that, of same period last year, it was INR 4.56/kWh. And for this year 9 months, INR 5.44/kWh, and it was INR 6.16/kWh" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow-product, operations-linked demand (baseload electricity, not customer capex); >90% of the 18.15 GW fleet tied under long-term/medium-term PPAs with two-part tariffs where EBITDA comes from availability-based capacity charges and fuel is a pass-through; open capacity cut from 20% to 10% with a 3-4% target*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 3 of the last 11 years with swings of ±18 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 21% and 38% (±6.2 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 43% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 8% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Stakeholder Concentration — ELEVATED
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but the only computed fingerprint does not confirm it — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** The Godda plant sells 100% of output to a single foreign sovereign buyer, BPDB, and contributed INR 1,092 crore of the INR 4,636 crore Q3FY26 continuing EBITDA (~24%); analysts probe the Bangladesh PPA and its receivables in essentially every call, with USD 400 million still outstanding as of June 2026. Supplier concentration is also visible in the single BTG order for the entire 24 GW expansion.

> "Specifically at the end of June, our receivables level is near about USD400 million. We are expecting that every month we will also get near about USD100 million on an average from BPDB." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Broadening customer base across many state DISCOMs (Maharashtra, Haryana, Karnataka, Assam, Uttarakhand, Rajasthan bids) and >90% of the fleet contracted across multiple counterparties; Bangladesh payments now running at ~USD 100mn/month, above monthly billing, reducing receivables*

- ✅ Customers pay in about 83 days (was 124) — no sign of weakening bargaining power in collection terms.

### Technological Innovation — WATCH
*"Out-invented by the industry"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Renewables are repeatedly discussed defensively as displacing thermal — in Feb 2025 management explained solar-hour tariffs force plants down to technical minimum, in Feb 2026 they attributed weaker thermal demand and a sharp fall in day-ahead prices to higher renewable generation, and an analyst directly asked about the 'structural impact on thermal dispatch and merchant pricing'; the entire 42 GW asset base is coal-fired infrastructure. The economic damage is contained because new PPAs pay capacity charges on availability rather than dispatch, and management is adding nuclear and hydro optionality.

> "Higher renewable generation also impacted thermal demand. Together, these factors led to lower merchant market price. The average market clearing price in the day ahead market declined sharply year-on-year." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Two-part tariffs insulate EBITDA from dispatch loss ('EBITDA from plant availability while fuel charges are a pass-through'); ultra-supercritical technology on new plants; diversification into international hydro and a stated 10 GW nuclear ambition by 2035*

- ✅ Growth is holding up: 6% a year earlier, 16% recently — no structural stall.
- ✅ Operating margin (37%) remains near its historical peak (38%) — no collapse in the premium.

### New Entrants — WATCH
*"A new player changes the rules"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Solar developers with a ~INR 2.50/unit cost base are a structurally different competitor that has reset intraday merchant economics, forcing thermal units to technical minimum during solar hours, and private nuclear entry is being opened up. Management responds by building its own new-format exposure rather than only defending coal.

### Shifting Consumer Preferences — WATCH
*"The customer is changing"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Societal and investor attitudes have migrated against coal, and management repeatedly frames thermal defensively against the renewable focus while devoting large parts of every opening statement to ESG scores and ratings improvements. The underlying demand base has not eroded — peak demand hit a record 271 GW in May 2026 and thermal was 'once again the mainstay' — so the shift is a positioning and cost-of-capital issue rather than volume loss so far.

> "While there is a strong focus on renewable energy, it is crucial to acknowledge that thermal power generation remains an integral part of India's energy mix. It serves as the cornerstone for supplying base load power" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Portfolio pivot underway toward the new preference — ultra-supercritical plants, S&P CSA score improved 48→68 (top 15% of peers), and diversification into hydro and nuclear*

- ✅ Growth is holding up: 6% a year earlier, 16% recently — no structural stall.

### Good Enough Goods — WATCH
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Power is an undifferentiated commodity procured by DISCOMs through lowest-tariff reverse bidding, and cheaper alternatives — solar at ~INR 2.50/unit intraday, and rival IPPs bidding for the same SHAKTI-linked state tenders — directly cap realisations; there is no brand premium to defend. The offset is that APL competes as the structurally low-cost producer rather than as the premium product.

> "our operations remain resilient due to our fuel logistics cost advantages, long-term tie-ups and competitive merit order position in most PPAs" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Cost leadership rather than a defended premium — locational advantage, captive coal (Dhirauli mine), advance BTG ordering and in-house execution give 'a structural cost advantage'; favourable merit-order position keeps plants dispatched*

- ⬜ No input-cost data.
- ✅ Operating margin (37%) remains near its historical peak (38%) — no collapse in the premium.
- ✅ Operating margin held (about 29% then 37%) — no drift down under competition.
