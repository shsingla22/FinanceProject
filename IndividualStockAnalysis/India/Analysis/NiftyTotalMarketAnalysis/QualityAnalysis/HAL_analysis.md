# Hindustan Aeronautics Ltd. (HAL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: INDUSTRIAL MANUFACTURING*

Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL) is an Indian state-owned ("CPSE", granted Maharatna status in October 2024) aerospace and defence manufacturer that designs, builds, repairs and overhauls military aircraft, helicopters and aero engines. Its main customers are India's armed forces — the Indian Air Force, Army, Navy and Coast Guard — with smaller amounts from ISRO (space hardware), civil operators such as Pawan Hans, foreign buyers like Guyana, and global engine makers like Safran. It makes money two ways: selling newly manufactured platforms and engines (₹9,227 crores of FY26 revenue) and, more heavily, repair-and-overhaul (ROH) work on aircraft already in service (₹20,524 crores of FY26 revenue), all executed against long-dated government contracts, with customers funding much of the working capital upfront. It operates entirely from plants in India — Bangalore/Bengaluru, Nashik, Tumakuru and other locations — with about 24,000 employees, FY26 revenue of ₹33,050 crores, profit before tax of ₹12,112 crores, roughly 30% operating EBITDA margin, and an order book of ₹2,54,538 crores that management says keeps its lines busy to 2032-33.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Manufacturing** — Building and delivering new aircraft, helicopters and engines; ₹9,227 crores of FY26 revenue (up from ₹7,957 crores), the smaller of the two revenue lines but the one management expects to grow as LCA Mark-1A and HTT-40 deliveries begin.
- **ROH (Repair and Overhaul)** — Servicing, repairing and overhauling platforms already flying with the customer; at ₹20,524 crores it was the majority of FY26 revenue, brought in ₹26,539 crores of fresh orders in FY26, and management is investing capex in new ROH facilities and expects roughly ₹20,000 crores more of such orders.
- **Fighter aircraft (LCA Tejas / Sukhoi-30MKI)** — The flagship programme — indigenous LCA Tejas Mark-1A plus Su-30MKI supply and upgrades; the 97 LCA Mark-1A order was booked in FY26, a third production line at Nashik took capacity to 8 aircraft a year, and deliveries have been repeatedly delayed by GE engine supply.
- **Helicopters** — Advanced Light Helicopter (ALH Dhruv and Dhruv NG), Light Combat Helicopter (LCH Prachand), Light Utility Helicopter (LUH) and the future Indian Multi Role Helicopter (IMRH); the 156 LCH Prachand contract alone was worth ₹62,777 crores, and helicopter deliveries were a main revenue prop in FY26.
- **Trainer and transport aircraft** — The HTT-40 basic trainer and the Dornier / Hindustan-228 light transport aircraft, sold to the Air Force, Navy and Coast Guard and also exported (two aircraft to Guyana), with a 40-Dornier upgrade contract still awaited.
- **Aero engines** — Manufacture and supply of AL-31FP engines for the Sukhoi-30 (a 240-engine, ₹25,500 crore contract) and RD-33 engines, plus the indigenously certified Shakti civil engine and planned GE F414 and IMRH engine manufacture; engine deliveries repeatedly rescued revenue when aircraft programmes slipped.
- **Design and Development / R&D** — In-house design of indigenous platforms (LCA Mark 2, AMCA, IMRH, naval utility helicopter), funded partly by customers (₹3,180 crores of D&D orders in FY25) and partly internally — R&D spend was ₹2,794 crores, about 8.4% of FY26 revenue, with 15% of profit set aside annually as an R&D corpus.
- **Exports** — Sales to foreign customers, still very small at ₹501 crores in FY26 versus ₹400 crores the year before, but described as an area management wants to pursue aggressively as capacity frees up.
- **Space / ISRO work** — Supplying space-worthy hardware to ISRO — Gaganyaan crew module fairing, propellant tanks and machining for the LVM3 heavy rocket — and a technology-transfer licence signed in September 2025 to manufacture the SSLV small launch vehicle; presented as diversification rather than a large revenue line.
- **Civil aerospace and global supply chain** — A newer diversification push: an agreement with Safran to produce rotating parts for LEAP engines used on the Airbus A320neo and Boeing 737 MAX, an MoU with Russia's UAC to build the SJ-100 civil commuter aircraft in India, civil-certified Dhruv helicopters for operators like Pawan Hans, and an IAI tie-up to convert passenger jets into tanker aircraft.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

**Industry Structure — Good (+0.83)** · 6 of 6 checks had evidence

- **Mini-monopolies — Excellent** *(from the conference calls)*: HAL holds a near-absolute monopoly in the Indian military aircraft aftermarket — repair and overhaul is ~68% of revenue (Rs 20,524 crore in FY26) on a fleet only it can service, and every new platform it manufactures compounds that captive annuity.

- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs — Excellent** *(from the conference calls)*: Switching costs are effectively absolute — the Indian armed forces cannot re-source overhaul of Sukhoi, ALH, LCA or RD-33/AL-31FP engines elsewhere, and platform ownership plus the Positive Indigenisation List entrenches HAL as the sole national supplier for its platforms.

  > "ERV clause is there in each and every contract, and even in the repair and overhaul contracts" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly) — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: HAL faces no domestic rival in integrated military aircraft manufacture and treats private firms as suppliers (L&T, VEM, Alpha Tocol, Tata TASL, PTC) rather than competitors, but its single end customer — the Government of India — holds enormous negotiating power over pricing and order timing.
  > "the wings will come from L&T, the centre fuselage will come from VEM, the rear fuselage will come from Alpha Tocol" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Share donators — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: The identifiable share donors are foreign OEMs being displaced through indigenisation — 30 of 34 platforms delivered were indigenous, AL-31FP indigenous content at 57% and rising via MIDHANI and PTC — though capture of that share has been slow.
  > "almost out of 34 new platforms which has been manufactured and delivered, 30 are indigenously designed and developed" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Security by obscurity — Poor** *(from the conference calls)*: HAL is the opposite of obscure — a Maharatna PSU with a market cap discussed on calls hosted successively by ICICI, Elara and Ambit, covered by JP Morgan, Nomura, UBS, Goldman Sachs and Citi, with management noting investor anxiety moving the stock.
  > "in the market, it is causing a fair bit of anxiety among the investor base" — *management, on an earnings call*

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Innovation Dominance — STRONG FIT
*"Out-inventing the competition"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence and the numbers agree (1 of 2 checks computable supporting).

**What the calls show:** R&D is disclosed, sustained and rising — 6-7% of revenue in FY23 to INR2,794 cr / 8.4% in FY26, plus 15% of PAT ring-fenced as an R&D corpus and self-funded programmes (IMRH Rs. 4,000 cr, UHM Rs. 2,000 cr) — with named platforms landing each year (HTT-40, LUH, LCH, ALH Dhruv NG, LCA Mk2, CATS Warrior) and 2,842 cumulative IPR filings; the caveat is that development-to-delivery conversion keeps slipping.

> "HAL has filed 223 IPR applications" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹56 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ❌ High retained margin (56% of sales) alongside 7% yearly growth — the flywheel is incomplete.

### Cost to Replicate — STRONG FIT
*"Could a newcomer copy this?"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** A well-funded copycat cannot replicate design authority over sovereign combat platforms, CEMILAC/DGCA type certification, a 60-year installed base that generates the ROH annuity, or the Positive Indigenisation List that mandates HAL platforms; even private partners (L&T, Tata, VEM, Alpha Tocol) sit as sub-tier suppliers inside HAL's integration role.

> "HAL received Restricted Military Type Certification (RMTC) from CEMILAC" — *management, on an earnings call*

> "funded upfront by our customers" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Pricing Power — LIKELY FIT

**What the calls show:** Conditional/contractual rather than brand pricing power: escalation and exchange-rate-variation clauses are embedded in every manufacturing and ROH contract, and the FY24 Change Order 3 repricing added Rs. 1,502 cr — but prices are set by negotiation with a single monopsony customer, not raised at will.

> "ERV clause is there in each and every contract, and even in the repair and overhaul contracts." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹56 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Fortunes are set almost entirely by government policy and process: orders originate from MoD AoN/CCS approvals, demand is manufactured by the Positive Indigenization List and Atmanirbhar policy, pricing moves via negotiated change orders and ERV/escalation clauses, and even a Rs 10,079cr tax dispute was settled by the state and reimbursed by the customer. The management record reinforces it — CMD turnover is by government appointment (Ananthakrishnan, then D.K. Sunil in 2025, then Ravi Kota in May 2026).

> "All the major platforms of HAL, namely, our LCA, Dornier 228, HTT-40, LCH, LUH are part of the positive list notified by MOD ensuring assured order" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Attempted diversification — exports, civil MRO/passenger-to-freight, SSLV licence from ISRO, Safran LEAP rotating parts, SJ-100 MoU — but exports were only Rs 501cr of Rs 33,050cr revenue in FY26, so the mitigation is immaterial so far*
