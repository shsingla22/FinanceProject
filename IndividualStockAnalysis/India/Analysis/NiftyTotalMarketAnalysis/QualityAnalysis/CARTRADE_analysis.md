# Cartrade Tech (CARTRADE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

CarTrade Tech runs India's largest online marketplaces for vehicles — the websites and apps CarWale, BikeWale, CarTrade and OLX — where ordinary consumers research and buy new and used cars, two-wheelers and (on OLX) other used goods. It makes money mainly from car manufacturers (OEMs) and car dealers who pay to advertise and get customer leads on its sites, from fees on vehicles sold through its auction platform, and from classified listing and newer paid seller/buyer products on OLX such as Elite Buyer and Verification. Alongside the websites it runs a physical network — over 350 locations including Shriram Automall auction yards and abSure used-car outlets — where banks, financiers, fleet owners and individuals sell used vehicles by auction, at a run rate of about 1.9 million vehicles auctioned a year. The business is entirely India-based, debt free, with roughly INR1,244 crores of cash, about 85 million monthly users (95% of whom arrive without paid marketing), FY26 revenue growing 22% and profit after tax of about INR243 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Consumer Group (CarWale, BikeWale, CarTrade, abSure)** — The auto classifieds and media business where people research and shop for new and used cars and bikes, earning advertising and lead fees from manufacturers and dealers; it is the largest of the three businesses, growing revenue about 30% in FY26 at a 43% EBITDA margin, and includes the abSure used-car retail outlets.
- **Remarketing (Shriram Automall)** — The used-vehicle auction business, run through physical auto malls plus online bidding, selling repossessed vehicles for financiers and increasingly retail/other seller supply — roughly 1.9 million vehicles auctioned a year, with FY26 revenue up 22% and margins crossing 28-30% after a flat patch in FY24.
- **OLX India (Sobek Auto)** — The general online classifieds platform acquired in August 2023 where users buy and sell used items — about half automotive and half non-automotive — running at roughly INR61.7 crores of revenue a quarter with a 37% EBITDA margin, and now being monetised further through Elite Buyer, Verification and AI matchmaking/pricing tools.

### The story in depth

CarTrade's whole story starts from one asset: brand recall that people arrive with on their own. Unpaid, organic traffic went from 84% of visits in FY23 to 95% by FY26, monthly users from 31 million in 2022 to 85 million by Q3FY26, and the company keeps showing Google Trends slides where CarWale, BikeWale and OLX sit head and shoulders above rivals. That single trait explains most of the high scores at once. Advertising and promotion spend is deliberately kept near zero, yet the brand keeps compounding. Owning all three properties gives a top-of-mind position in Indian auto classifieds (mini-monopolies, score 2), and because two million monthly used-vehicle buyers and sellers plus nearly all Indian dealers pull each other onto the platform, the network effect feeds itself at almost no customer acquisition cost (score 2). The same fact is what makes Brand Strength a strong fit in the multibagger screen, and it underpins two more patterns: being the cheapest channel a dealer can buy (Low Price Plus) and the barrier a copycat would have to pay for in marketing money (Cost to Replicate). With essentially no direct input costs, nearly every rupee of sales is available for people, marketing and profit, which is how management turned that traffic into group margins rising from 9% to 33%, an EBITDA growth rate of roughly 98% a year, and a full turnaround of the loss-making OLX India business with costs held flat, margins to 37% and profit up 77%.



The uncomfortable part is that the same free-traffic engine is the biggest single risk. A business where 95% of visits arrive unpaid sits directly in the path of AI-driven search and agentic discovery, which is why Technological Innovation is on watch — and management's own answers by FY26 are dominated by AI agents for matchmaking, pricing and condition, plus Super Dost delivered over WhatsApp. That defensive push is exactly what earns the Innovation Dominance pattern a 'likely' rather than a 'strong' verdict: products get named every quarter, but research spend is never disclosed and the promised revenue keeps slipping, with Q3FY26 pushing monetisation out again. Related is the Good Enough Goods watch: a listing is not a complicated product, free or cheaper substitutes exist, and management defends its ground on cost-effectiveness rather than on being irreplaceable. Cyclicality is the one elevated risk, and it connects straight to the weakest quality check — sales have compounded around 42% a year but lumpily, scoring -2 on persistence, because revenue rides on manufacturer and dealer advertising budgets and vehicle volumes, with the remarketing arm going flat for several quarters. The 70% manufacturer / 30% dealer revenue mix means a handful of carmakers' digital budgets drive the largest and fastest-growing segment (Stakeholder Concentration, watch), and that same concentration is what makes the Market Share Gainers pattern a strong fit, since consumer-group revenue outgrew the industry 20% against 8% in FY24 and 32% over nine months of FY26 against a cyclical market. Competition stays live: a rival laid off 600 staff in 2022, and CarTrade pursued and then shelved a CarDekho acquisition (New Entrants, watch).



Management and culture are the steadying force, and here the record is unusually consistent. The same leaders appear across all 17 calls, they set out a margin-and-leverage plan in 2022 and delivered against it for twelve straight quarters without outside pressure, and the balance sheet has stayed debt-free — which is why Corporate Culture is a strong fit and the independence check scores 2. But that same conservatism produces the clear soft spot. Cash has grown from roughly ₹750 crores to ₹1,244 crores, about ₹300 crores of it in the last year alone, with no dividend, no buyback and no stated policy anywhere in the calls — the distribution check scores -1, capital allocation is one of the two weakest areas at 0.33, and return on capital scores zero. Money is simply piling up rather than being turned into returns. Several patterns land only as partial for a shared reason: management does not disclose the numbers that would confirm them — no renewal or attach rates behind the subscription revenue, no explicit price increases behind the ARPU story, no itemised cost discipline behind the low-cost claim, and the abSure used-car retail rollout that anchored forward integration in 2022 simply disappears from the 2024–2026 calls. The end-market itself is a genuine tailwind — the permanent shift of auto marketing budgets from offline to digital plus growing used-vehicle transactions (score 2) — and the three lowest risks (government dependency, shifting consumer preferences, fashion) are all benign because the platform is asset-light, unregulated in its pricing, and serves functional repeat demand. That mix produces the overall rating: 67 on business quality, 71 on multibagger fit, 74 on risk safety, blending to 70 out of 100, a Strong four-star verdict with sound resilience.
