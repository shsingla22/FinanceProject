**Checks that improved:**

- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Good. Now: Growth is deliberately mix-led — shedding Rs 38,000-40,000 crore of high-cost bulk deposits, churning Rs 30,000 crore of low-yield corporate loans into higher-yielding RAM assets, which lifted NIM from 2.67% to 2.76%. | The long view had said: Growth is mix-churn (bulk deposits into CASA/RTD, low-yield corporate exits) with no pricing power — yields fall with repo resets and NIM has compressed from 2.91% to 2.70%.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: No monopoly-like position; a mid-sized PSU bank in a commoditised market that must price-compete for deposits and admits carrying corporate loans below benchmark yields. | The long view had said: Banking products here are commodities with no mind-monopoly — management itself notes MSME customers rotate banks purely for rate arbitrage.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Explicit attention to workforce quality and staff experience — 32,000 of 74,000 staff professionally qualified, Project MUSKAAN aimed at staff ease-of-doing-business, and a new 1,200-person relationship vertical staffed internally. | The long view had said: There is systematic skill-mapping and HR transformation (dedicated MSME vertical staffed with credit-skilled officers, verticalization, new ecosystem-banking cadre), but top-leadership succession is a government appointment, not an internal pipeline.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: Benefit type is correctly matched by segment — RAM/retail raised to 57.49% for granular CASA and cross-sell, while corporates are locked in via cash-management integration and stickiness. | The long view had said: The mix is served with undifferentiated products — retail gets rate-driven CASA campaigns rather than intangibles, while corporate lock-in rests on transaction integration that is only now being built.
- **Distribution to shareholders** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: A coherent, rising dividend policy with a stated payout ratio (Rs 5/share, ~20.6% payout, up from Rs 4.75), though no buybacks and capital is being retained for growth. | The long view had said: Dividend policy is coherent and rising (INR 5/share, ~20.6% payout, up from 4.75) but the bank issues equity rather than buying it back, and did QIP-type raises at depressed valuations to cut government stake.
- **Network effects** (Competitive Advantage): Weak → Neutral. Now: The ecosystem-banking vertical and corporate integrations create modest anchor-and-supplier pull-through, but nothing self-reinforcing at scale is evidenced. | The long view had said: No self-reinforcing two-sided network; growth in NACH registrations is transaction volume, not a network that strengthens with scale, and PSLC trading is an anonymous exchange.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: A coherent five-pillar plan with an honestly stated 9-12 month payback horizon, but this is a government-owned bank with a newly arrived MD and repeated churn in EDs and CFO (Prabhu to Jain) that limits independence and continuity. | The long view had said: As a government-majority PSU the bank has externally appointed, short-tenure leadership (MD changed from Manimekhalai to Asheesh Pandey mid-period) and capital decisions driven by the mandate to cut government stake rather than by independent long-term strategy.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: The bank sits at a favourable point in the credit cycle — credit cost collapsed from 109 bps to 22 bps, slippages from 2.40% to 0.91%, with 125 bps of repo cuts and CRR easing supporting the upturn. | The long view had said: The credit-cycle upturn has already been harvested — GNPA fell from 10.22% (Jun-22) to 2.82% (Mar-26) and credit cost normalised — so the bank sits late in, not near the bottom of, the asset-quality cycle.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Genuine switching-cost evidence in the form of very high account stickiness and rising corporate cash-management integrations and NACH mandates. | The long view had said: Some switching-cost capture is claimed via CASA stickiness and corporate integration/NACH mandates, but it is asserted rather than demonstrated and coexists with heavy deposit churn.

**Checks that regressed:**

Now: Communication is operationally focused but the MD devotes meaningful airtime to award-listing and self-congratulation across calls. | The long view had said: Management is low-profile and technocratic, with calls dominated by ratios, provisioning and process detail rather than personality, though the latest MD does foreground awards.

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 2 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Unknown on the long view, Unknown on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Earnings visibly swing with the rate and credit cycle: 125bps of repo cuts plus a 100bps CRR cut compressed NIM from 2.91% (Dec-24) to 2.76% (Dec-25), while credit cost fell from 109bps in Q2FY25 to 22bps in Q2FY26 and slippages from 2.40% to 0.91% — the current benign phase flatters profitability, and management is already parking a Rs.700 crore general provision 'as and when good time is there' against future eventualities. (The long view had said: Earnings across the 17 calls are dominated by the credit and rate cycle rather than by stable flow income: gross NPA fell from 13.60% (Jun 2021) to 10.22% (Jun 2022) to 2.82% (Mar 2026) with quarterly profit rising from Rs.1,558cr to over Rs.5,300cr largely on credit-cost normalisation, write-offs of Rs.6,648cr in a single quarter, one-off standard-asset provisions of Rs.1,296cr for 'anticipated distress', and treasury income collapsing from Rs.1,186cr to Rs.220cr when rates hardened; management also ties corporate loan growth to 'once the private capex come into full force', and NIM compressed 2.91%→2.70% on 125bps of repo cuts.)
- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Across all four calls technology appears only as incremental, value-adding investment — AI and robotic process automation, two DC and two DR sites, Project MUSKAAN, ecosystem-banking vertical, NACH registrations up from 12-13k to 18k per day — with IBA Tech Awards and CIBIL/cybersecurity recognition; no analyst or management discussion of a disruptive platform threat or legacy-format risk. (The long view had said: Technology enters the calls mainly as fraud and operational risk rather than as a competing platform — 70-80% of reported frauds are digital, and the bank is retrofitting AI-based transaction monitoring, velocity checks and a 24/7 cyber SOC; structural tech/ops changes (Project Muskaan, ecosystem banking vertical, NACH registrations up from 12-13k to 18k/day) read as incremental modernisation of a legacy branch estate, not a format reset, and no analyst raises fintech or platform disruption.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Management is actively deconcentrating both sides of the balance sheet — shedding Rs.38,000-40,000 crore of high-cost bulk deposits in favour of Rs.1,10,000 crore of CASA plus retail term deposits, and steering the mix toward RAM (55%→57.49%, targeting 58%) away from lumpy corporate exposure; no single customer, supplier or channel relationship is cited as driving results. (The long view had said: Quarterly results are repeatedly swung by single large corporate names — Future Retail provisioning in 2022, the Sterling Biotech settlement contributing Rs.658cr of the Mar-2026 recovery line, NCLT/NARCL resolution timing on ~Rs.10,000cr of accounts, and an NBFC book of Rs.1.2 lakh crore; against that the funding and asset base is genuinely broad.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Unknown on the long view (none of the stress checks could be computed.) vs Unknown on the one-year view (none of the stress checks could be computed.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
