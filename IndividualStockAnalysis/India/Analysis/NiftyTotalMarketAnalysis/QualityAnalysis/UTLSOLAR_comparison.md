**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Mixed picture: ALMM/DCR rules and cell manufacturing create real regulatory and capital barriers, but downstream panel and system assembly faces oversupply and easy entry with no evidence of pricing rationality. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Low entry barriers with heavy capacity addition and module oversupply industry-wide, and management openly welcomes all comers rather than pointing to any structural deterrent.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Purely organic expansion with no M&A, capex delivered under budget, and leverage cut sharply as IPO proceeds repaid debt (net debt/equity 0.85 to 0.25) while still funding growth. | The long view had said: Entirely organic growth with no M&A, deleveraging post-IPO to 0.25x net debt/equity and capex delivered under budget, tempered by a short listed track record and a rising inventory/working-capital build.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Occupies an under-covered niche — off-grid/hybrid backup systems for Tier 2/3 households — that management says is routinely overlooked while capital chases pure-play panel makers and developers. | The long view had said: The off-grid backup niche in towns and villages is genuinely under-served by subsidy-focused on-grid players, but the solar sector overall is a magnet for capital and the company has just IPO'd and signed a celebrity ambassador.
- **Candor** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Consistently candid on bad news: the GST-driven revenue decline, delayed battery and inverter lines with named causes, weak subsidy-segment sales, and the Bawal fire with capacity and insurance detail all disclosed unprompted. | The long view had said: Generally candid: they admitted the GST-cut sales were permanently lost, disclosed BIS seizures, the Bawal fire and the Ratlam inverter/battery line delays, and refused to quantify cell-line savings before ramp-up — though they withhold segment-wise revenue splits.
- **Advertisement and promotion** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: A&P is real and brand-building (Surya Kumar Yadav signed as ambassador on top of existing word-of-mouth brand equity in a >90% B2C mix), but no spend figures or efficiency evidence are disclosed to judge it like capex. | The long view had said: A&P is limited to a newly signed cricketer brand ambassador with no spend disclosure or track record of durable equity, while management itself attributes demand to word-of-mouth and service rather than advertising.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Some mind-share and service-based lock-in via the UTL/Fujiyama brands and a service engineer network management calls hard to duplicate, but this is brand strength rather than a monopoly-like position. | The long view had said: Brand trust with ~1 million customers in specific rural/Tier 2-3 pockets, but no monopoly-like position and the aftermarket/service annuity is deliberately handed to dealers rather than captured.

And from the calls: Asset intensity is rising, not falling: total assets more than doubled to Rs 23,436mn against FY26 revenue of Rs 26,545mn (~1.1x turn) as the company pushes into capital-heavy cell, panel and battery manufacturing, with working capital days up from 71 to 83. | The long view had said: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.68 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: Asset turn is deteriorating as the model becomes capital-heavier — total assets more than doubled to Rs 23,436mn against 72% revenue growth, ROCE fell from ~40% to ~25% on lower asset turnover, and working capital days rose from 71 to 83 — offset by a genuine distribution/service moat.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: No regional exclusivity or captive aftermarket lock-in is evidenced — customers buy hardware from a wide dealer network with low switching costs, and after-sales support is a service promise rather than a captive revenue stream. | The long view had said: System-level lock-in exists because customers buy inverter, battery and panel as one matched package from the same brand, creating modest switching costs reinforced by the local dealer relationship.

Now: Commentary is operational and undramatic, but the company simultaneously leans on celebrity marketing, and one year post-IPO gives too little evidence to credit a low-profile culture either way. | The long view had said: Promoter-managers speak plainly and operationally, decline to give bold guidance and avoid self-promotion, though the company has begun buying visibility through a celebrity endorsement post-listing.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 2 weakened, 7 unchanged.

*Foundation test: UNKNOWN on the long view, UNKNOWN on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Forward Integrators**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: A manufacturer moving to the customer is the clearest thread across all three calls: exclusive Shoppe franchise outlets added every quarter (20 in Q3, 30 in Q4) inside a channel base that grew from ~8,200 to over 8,900 by March 2026, with franchisees funding last-mile presence and controlling the customer experience. (The long view had said: The exclusive Shoppe format is a genuine move toward the customer — 20 added in Q3 and 30 in Q4 within an 8,900-strong partner network — but no own-store share of sales, franchise royalty economics or D2C revenue is disclosed, and online is described as immaterial.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The three calls describe after-sales support and field service engineers but never quantify AMC/service revenue, attach rates, renewals or deferred revenue; working capital is positive and inventory-heavy (83 days NWC in FY26), so there is no evidence either way on a recurring, customer-funded stream. (The long view had said: Management explicitly disclaims recurring service/AMC income, handing annual maintenance economics to dealers instead, and working capital is inventory-heavy (83 days in FY26, inventory 108 days) rather than customer-funded.)

**That weakened:**

- **Friendly Middleman**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Sales run through distributors, dealers and exclusive Shoppe franchisees who advise the household on system sizing, installation and financing, and installer feedback loops back into R&D — but the end customer still pays and chooses, and no trade-training programme or professional certification is described in any of the three calls. (The long view had said: Across all three calls sales run through distributors/dealers/Shoppe franchisees who also do installation, supported by 600+ field service engineers who train the trade, and margin on AMC is deliberately left to the channel to keep partners loyal.)
- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Within the 12-month window the promise-to-delivery record holds: the Dadri 1GW cell line promised 'within one month' in December was commissioned by the February call, under budget; delays to the Ratlam battery and inverter lines and the Bawal fire were disclosed with cause and mitigation. Only three post-IPO calls exist, so long-run cultural durability cannot be judged. (The long view had said: Promises made on the Dec call were delivered by Feb (Dadri 1GW cell line commissioned in six months, under budget), and bad news is disclosed promptly and specifically — the Bawal fire, BIS seizures on 10-15 SKUs, and the Ratlam battery/inverter delays with reasons — alongside a conservative 0.25 net debt/equity and founder-led continuity.)

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (LIKELY FIT); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Technological Innovation**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Within one year the company commissioned a 1GW Mono PERC cell line and then announced a 1,200MW TOPCon line, and its battery capex slipped because lithium cell formats moved from 100AH to 300AH mid-execution — evidence that its freshly built asset base can become legacy quickly; a 1.3GW lead-acid battery plant sits on the older chemistry entirely. (The long view had said: The company spent ~Rs 300 crore on a 1 GW Mono-PERC cell line while, as an analyst pressed in Feb 2026, 'everyone has shifted to TOPCon' — and by May 2026 management announced a further Rs 350 crore 1.2 GW TOPCon line, confirming the just-commissioned PERC asset is on the wrong side of the transition; separately the 1.3 GW Bawal lead-acid battery plant sits in a chemistry the company itself says the market is leaving, and the Ratlam battery line was delayed to re-spec from 100 AH to 300 AH lithium cells.)
- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Management repeatedly insists growth is not subsidy-driven, yet the GST cut from 12% to 5% froze the market for roughly a month and dented Q2 sequentially, and by Q4 the company was explicitly leaning into PM Surya Ghar volumes, building DCR/TOPCon cell capacity to qualify under the ALMM 2 framework and citing ~25GW of subsidy-scheme opportunity — policy now shapes both pricing and the capacity roadmap. (The long view had said: Policy repeatedly moved the P&L and the capex plan across all three calls: a GST cut from 12% to 5% froze roughly a month of sales and drove the Q2 sequential revenue decline; DCR/ALMM-2 mandates force a Rs 350 crore TOPCon investment as 'mandatory'; Q4 volume growth is credited to PM Surya Ghar participation; and BIS seizures, wafer/polysilicon duty changes and the 300 GW national target dominate the demand narrative.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
