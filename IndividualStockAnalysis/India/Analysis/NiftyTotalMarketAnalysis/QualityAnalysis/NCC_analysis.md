# NCC Ltd. (NCC) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSTRUCTION*

NCC Limited (formerly Nagarjuna Construction Company) is an India-based construction and engineering contractor, headquartered in Hyderabad and listed on the NSE and BSE, that builds infrastructure for others under fixed contracts — buildings, roads and transportation links, water and sewage systems, power transmission and distribution networks, railways, irrigation works, and contract mining. Its customers are mainly government bodies and state agencies (calls cite Andhra Pradesh capital-city works, Bihar state projects, the Jal Jeevan Mission water programme, and a Mumbai municipal wastewater treatment plant), plus some private clients, so its workload tracks government infrastructure budgets. It makes money by winning tenders into an order book, executing the work over several years and billing clients as milestones are certified — revenue of roughly INR 21,000 crore consolidated in FY26 at an EBITDA margin of about 8-9% and net profit margin of 2-4%, meaning it is a high-volume, thin-margin business where cash collection, mobilisation advances and working capital matter as much as winning work. Operations are all-India (management repeatedly refers to 'orders from all over India'), run through the parent company plus subsidiaries, with an order book of INR 83,004 crore at March 2026 — about 4x annual billing, the company's highest ever.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Buildings** — Construction of building structures (housing, institutional and commercial projects); historically the single biggest vertical at 57% of the order book in 2022, still the largest at roughly 31% by December 2025 though much reduced in share as other segments grew.
- **Transportation (Roads)** — Road and highway construction and related transport infrastructure; called 'Roads' in the earlier calls and the second-largest vertical at about 22% of the order book by December 2025, helped by government emphasis on expanding the road network.
- **Electrical / Electrical T&D** — Electrical works and power transmission and distribution projects; a smaller line in 2022 that had grown to roughly 18% of the order book by December 2025.
- **Mining** — Contract mining work for clients; about 13% of the order book at December 2025, up from a minor 'Mines' line in 2022.
- **Water & Environment (later grouped as Water & Railway)** — Water supply, sewage and wastewater treatment projects, including work under the Jal Jeevan Mission and a INR 3,833 crore wastewater treatment plant at Malad, Mumbai that also carried a INR 1,854 crore operations-and-maintenance component; about 18% of the order book in 2022 and roughly 10% (combined with Railways) by December 2025.
- **Railways** — Railway infrastructure work, reported together with Water at about 10% of the December 2025 order book; the company bought a tunnel boring machine (about INR 320 crore, still under capital work-in-progress at March 2026) indicating tunnelling-type projects.
- **Irrigation and others** — Irrigation and miscellaneous works, the smallest vertical at roughly 7% of the order book at December 2025.
- **Subsidiaries / investments (incl. NCC Vizag Urban)** — A set of subsidiary companies that add revenue beyond the parent (consolidated FY26 turnover of INR 20,944 crore versus standalone INR 17,669 crore, and INR 6,823 crore of the order book sat in subsidiaries as of December 2025); it also held a land/urban development venture, NCC Vizag Urban, holding about 98 acres in Vizag, which the company sold and has been collecting proceeds from in instalments since FY22.

### The story in depth

NCC is one of India's oldest infrastructure contractors, and on the surface the top line of the story looks strong: the order book more than doubled from INR40,616 crore in August 2022 to a record INR83,004 crore in March 2026, spread across seven verticals — Buildings ~31%, Transportation 22%, Electrical T&D 18%, Mining 13%, Water & Railway 10%, Irrigation and others 7% — with FY26 order inflows of INR31,884 crore and roughly four years of work in hand. The quality framework finds two things NCC genuinely does well. First, and rarest, the company gets paid by customers about 283 days before it has to pay its own suppliers, so the business is largely funded by other people's money rather than its own capital. Second, operating profit has held near INR9 on every INR100 of sales and has moved only about a point and a half in either direction each year, which says day-to-day cost control is real. Almost everything else in the 34-check framework scores badly, and the combined rating lands at 27 out of 100 — Weak, one star — built from 37 on business quality, zero on multibagger fit, and 40 on risk safety.

The reason the multibagger screen returns a zero is the same single fact that shapes the quality scores: every rupee of work is won by being the lowest bidder on a separately tendered, fixed-price contract. That structure means seven of the eleven patterns are outright non-fits. There is no pricing power — management could not hold even its own 9.5-10% profit band and ended FY26 at 8.44% standalone, down from 9.51% in the first quarter of FY23, while flagging 'pricing pressure in the whole economy'. There is no recurring revenue beyond small operations-and-maintenance tails such as the INR1,854 crore Malad contract. There is nothing a well-funded newcomer could not rebuild, because the work is tendered, the margins are thin, and the cash depends entirely on when clients choose to pay. The four patterns rated partial are all weakened by the same thing: the heritage and prequalification standing are real, and the growing order book does suggest NCC keeps winning bids, but neither converts into a better price, and no share figures against named rivals are given. This shows up directly in the quality scores — industry structure is the worst area at -1.33, and competitive advantage sits at -1.0, with zero marks on monopoly position, network effects and switching costs, since clients can and do simply cancel awarded work, as the Andhra Pradesh government did in 2019.

The risk review then shows how NCC's two strengths and its risks are the same coin. Being funded by customer cash is a strength only for as long as government customers pay: at March 2026 the Uttar Pradesh Jal Jeevan Mission programme alone carried INR3,015 crore of receivables and unbilled work, Andhra Pradesh has left roughly INR5,000 crore of orders sitting restartable, and the whole franchise depends on Union and state budget lines such as INR15,000 crore for Amaravati and INR22,000 crore for Bihar — which is why government dependency and cyclicality are both rated high risk. That payment behaviour is what turns steady margins into a negative return: for every INR100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings in the business, NCC generated minus INR4 of operating cash this year. The record order book is genuine multi-year visibility, but it is also the source of the swing — revenue rose 56% in one quarter of FY23 and 23% in the first quarter of FY25, then fell 9% in FY26 with guidance formally withdrawn. Against that, the company spent INR912 crore of capital expenditure in FY26 against roughly INR250 crore budgeted the prior year, including a INR320 crore tunnel boring machine still sitting unused in capital work-in-progress, in a year when revenue was falling. Two points sit on the credit side: management discusses bad news openly, including the Jal Jeevan receivables, the Andhra blockages and a INR21.3 crore Oman write-down, and the risks that are absent matter too — no technology disruption, no new-entrant threat and no consumer-preference shift appear anywhere in sixteen calls. The overall resilience reading is strained: this is a competent, honest contractor with no structural advantage, whose results are set mainly by government budgets and government payment timing rather than by anything it controls.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** NCC's revenue is entirely a function of clients' capex programmes (infra construction), and the 16-call arc shows exactly the swing the channel describes: +56% revenue in Q1FY23 and +23% in Q1FY25 during the upcycle, then a 9% standalone revenue decline in FY26 with EBITDA falling from a guided 9.5–10% to 8.44%, guidance formally withdrawn for FY26 and again for FY27. Debt and working capital swing with the cycle (net debt INR706cr at Mar-25 to INR2,830cr at Dec-25), and management repeatedly attributes swings to elections, commodity prices and client payment ability rather than anything it controls.

> "For the full year, the turnover stands at INR 17,669 crores as against INR 19,393 crores of the previous year, a decline by 9%." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Record INR83,004cr order book at ~4x book-to-bill spread across seven verticals gives multi-year visibility, and the company kept bidding through the downturn ('we will continue to bid depending on the risk/reward equation').*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 5 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 8% and 12% (±1.5 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 2% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 5%).

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Practically the entire franchise is government-funded: order flow depends on Union/state budget allocations (INR15,000cr for Amaravati, INR22,000cr for Bihar), a change of government in AP cancelled or abandoned roughly INR10,000cr of orders in 2019 and left payments stranded for four years, and FY26's revenue collapse traces to Jal Jeevan Mission payments simply not arriving until a central budget provision of INR17,000cr was created. Management explicitly slows work around elections and says its own execution is hostage to client payments and permits.

> "JJM as you know, there is -- this information is already available in the public domain that payments have not been coming." — *management, on an earnings call*
