# NLC India Ltd. (NLCINDIA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: ENERGY*

NLC India Limited is a 'Navratna' Government of India enterprise that digs up fuel from its own mines and burns most of it in its own power stations to sell electricity. It mines lignite (a soft brown coal) and, more recently, regular coal, selling some of that coal to outside buyers such as NTPC and through e-auctions, while the rest feeds its thermal plants; it also runs a large solar and wind portfolio. Its electricity is sold mainly to state government distribution companies under long-term power purchase agreements (TANGEDCO in Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Puducherry, Uttar Pradesh, Assam, Odisha's GRIDCO), and because it is a regulated utility, much of its earnings come from an allowed return on its 'regulated equity' with tariffs approved by CERC — the calls report regulated equity of Rs 6,593 crore split between mines (Rs 2,831 crore) and thermal (Rs 3,762 crore). It is headquartered in Chennai with its main operations at Neyveli in Tamil Nadu, plus projects in Odisha (Talabira) and Uttar Pradesh (Ghatampur); H1 FY23 standalone revenue was Rs 5,847 crore and consolidated revenue Rs 7,352 crore, with consolidated profit after tax of Rs 986 crore. (Note: the second excerpt, dated Feb 2026, contains only the exchange cover letter and a web link — no call content — so everything here comes from the Nov 2022 call.)

**The parts of the business:**

- **Mining (lignite)** — Open-cast lignite mines that mostly feed the company's own power stations at an internal transfer price of Rs 1,950 per tonne; H1 FY23 lignite production was 124.42 lakh tonnes, and management says output is regularly hurt by the monsoon and by land acquisition problems.
- **Mining (coal)** — A fast-growing coal mining business that jumped from 18.76 lakh tonnes to 49.75 lakh tonnes in H1 FY23 and is sold outside the group — roughly 1 MMT swapped with NTPC, 1.25 MMT to subsidiary NTPL, 2.575 MMT to NTPC under an MOU and 1.61 MMT via e-auction — earning EBIT of Rs 252 crore in the half year; the mining segment overall made Rs 819 crore of profit in H1 FY23 versus Rs 377 crore a year earlier, making it the main driver of the profit jump.
- **Thermal power generation** — Lignite- and coal-fired stations that generated 13,105 million units standalone in H1 FY23 at an average PLF of 74.66% against an all-India average of 64.49%; the key financial issue management tracks here is 'under-recovery' of costs, which improved to Rs 190 crore in H1 FY23 from Rs 243 crore, with the new NNTPS plant now running at almost nil under-recovery.
- **Renewables (solar and wind)** — 1,421 MW of commissioned capacity — 51 MW wind and the rest solar — built with a total investment of Rs 6,594 crore of which Rs 1,675 crore is the company's own equity; as of the Nov 2022 call there were no solar projects under construction.
- **NTPL (subsidiary/JV)** — A separate power company outside the standalone accounts with regulated equity of Rs 1,490 crore against total equity of Rs 2,200 crore; it buys coal from NLC and had declared a 5% interim dividend not yet received at the time of the call.
- **NUPPL / Ghatampur project (Uttar Pradesh)** — An under-construction thermal project where the company was tying up buyers — 75% of the power to Uttar Pradesh and a proposed 25% (495 MW) to Assam — and had just awarded the Rs 980 crore GA4 package to speed up completion.
- **Talabira thermal power project (Odisha)** — A planned 3 x 800 MW coal plant estimated at Rs 19,422 crore, with 2,000 MW of its 2,400 MW already tied up via PPAs (TANGEDCO 1,500 MW, Kerala 400 MW, Puducherry 100 MW) and the main EPC contract still being evaluated between L&T and BHEL — described as the company's biggest project in the pipeline.
- **Neyveli lignite expansion (1,320 MW)** — A second expansion using proven lignite technology where land is already in hand and TANGEDCO has consented to buy the full output; the tender was floated on 1 October 2022 with award targeted within FY23.
- **Lignite gasification / lignite-to-methanol** — A new business line approved by the board at a project cost of Rs 4,394 crore that converts lignite into syngas and then methanol, with the first EPC package tendered in October 2022 and an MOU with BHEL to develop indigenous technology; management frames it as a large future opportunity given the government's 100 million tonne gasification target for 2030.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

**Capital Allocation — Neutral (+0.20)** · 5 of 6 checks had evidence

- **Capex for growth (not maintenance) — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: Growth capex is large and rising — ₹2,920 cr in FY23 stepping to ₹8,244 cr in FY24 and a ₹71,000 cr+ eight-year plan for new mines, thermal, gasification and renewables — but returns are regulated (fixed lignite price of ₹1,950/t, CERC-set tariffs) and execution slips on land acquisition and PPAs, so incremental ROIC is capped rather than high.
  > "we have set our target for the next -- up to 2030 total CapEx during the next eight years is going to be INR 7 2,000 crore plus" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Cost of working capital — Neutral** *(from the financial statements)*: About 70 days of cash stays stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers — a long cycle that ties up capital.
- **Research and development — Neutral** *(from the conference calls)*: The only R&D-type effort is an MOU with BHEL to develop indigenous lignite gasification technology, still at pilot stage with no track record of R&D translating into products or customers.
  > "With BHEL, we have signed an MOU for development of indigenous technology... initially we will have a pilot project" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Merger and acquisition — Neutral** *(from the conference calls)*: Growth is organic/JV-based (NTPL, NUPPL, Pachwara SPV) with no bolt-on acquisitions; the one auction bid for commercial coal mines was lost on price, which is neither a positive nor a negative M&A record.
  > "commercial coal mining already we have applied and of course, because of high rate, we could not be successful bidder" — *management, on an earnings call*

  > "we will definitely like to keep a balance between servicing of the shareholders with the respectful dividend and also will see to it that our growth plans are not adversely impacted" — *management, on an earnings call*
