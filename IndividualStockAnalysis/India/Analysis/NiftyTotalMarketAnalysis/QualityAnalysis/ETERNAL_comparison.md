### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 2 weakened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: The four calls discuss order volumes, dark-store throughput, MTUs and take rates, but never license fees, subscriptions, AMC/attach rates, renewal rates or deferred revenue as a funding source. Working capital is discussed only as a ROCE input (18 days NWC), not as customer prefunding. (The long view had said: Zomato Gold is a paid subscription that management says covers ~50-55% of food-delivery GOV (Aug 2024 call), and it demonstrably reduces customer delivery charges — but there is no discussion of attach rates, renewals, deferred revenue or customer advances as a funding source, and the core business is transactional commission/take-rate revenue, not licence or AMC streams.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is evidence of pull rather than paid-for demand — sustained marketing at very low customer-acquisition cost as competitors pull back, and older cohorts spending 3x versus three years ago driven by frequency — but no heritage story, no premium pricing, and no adjacency stretch under the brand is discussed. (The long view had said: The Zomato app's traffic is used deliberately to launch new brands (Blinkit, District) at low customer-acquisition cost, and by Q4FY26 competitors' pullback let them acquire at 'extremely low cost' — evidence of brand-driven distribution leverage and adjacency stretch, but no heritage story, no premium pricing from brand, and A&P is acquisition spend rather than brand building.)

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management owns a past strategic mistake publicly (discount-led entry in the south failed), delivers what it guided (3,000 stores on track, quick-commerce breakeven), refuses to publish numbers it cannot defend, and anchors decisions on ROCE — but it also withdrew the 100% FY27 growth guidance given a quarter earlier, and the CEO seat moved to Albinder Dhindsa during the window. (The long view had said: Promises made on early calls were delivered — the Aug 2022 Zomato adjusted-EBITDA breakeven target and the cut in the Blinkit investment budget from $400m to $320m, then the 3,000-store guidance reaffirmed in May 2026 ('we remain firmly on track'); management owns failures publicly (the southern-market discounting approach that 'didn't work'), declines to give metrics it cannot defend, and refuses growth it deems unhealthy.)
- **Pricing Power**: LIKELY FIT → NO FIT. Why now: Across all four calls the direction is price deflation, not pricing power: delivery charges cut in some markets in Q3FY26, forced price-matching and falling NAOV for two consecutive quarters into Q1FY27, with management stating they do not expect NAOV to grow from here. (The long view had said: In food delivery they have raised platform fees consistently while MTUs and order growth accelerated (May 2026, Jul 2026) and take rates have risen since Aug 2022 without volume loss; but Blinkit shows the opposite — flat-to-falling NAOV, price matching forced by competitors, and management saying they do not expect NAOV to grow.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Quick commerce is a largely undifferentiated basket of third-party goods where rivals attack with grocery and delivery-fee subsidies, and management concedes it is forced to price match and to keep reinvesting profits into lower prices to reach more price-sensitive cohorts — the customer sees limited reason to pay a premium beyond service quality. (The long view had said: Blinkit largely sells undifferentiated FMCG, staples and groceries where competitors attack purely on price, and management concedes it must price match and keep reinvesting margin into lower prices to reach price-sensitive cohorts; take-rate gains have repeatedly failed to flow fully to contribution margin.)
- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No analyst or management discussion across the last four calls of a platform shift, AI threat, or the company's format becoming legacy; the innovation discussed is incremental and value-adding — automation capex per store and larger store formats that management says are lifting the long-term margin guidance from 5-6% to 6%. (The long view had said: The company operates in a format-driven industry where the business model itself has been reset once already (Zomato pivoting via the Blinkit quick-commerce acquisition, then District and Bistro), and analysts probe new competitor models such as Swiggy's Toing; management admits it does not yet understand what problem that model solves.)

**That worsened:**

- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Competition dominates all four calls: management calls the market 'irrational', admits share loss on order growth, cut delivery charges in some markets in response, withdrew the 100% FY27 growth guidance because of competitive intensity, and describes Q1FY27 as the peak of competition to date with more players and everybody more aggressive; a differently-structured entrant format (Swiggy's Toing) was also raised by analysts. (The long view had said: Quick commerce has drawn multiple well-capitalised entrants running subsidy-led acquisition; by Q1FY27 management calls it the peak of competitive intensity, FY27 growth guidance was cut from 100% to 70-80%, and the company concedes it is forced to price match while channelling platform-fee revenue into targeted discounts in food delivery.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Stakeholder Concentration (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
