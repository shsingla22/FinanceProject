# Karnataka Bank (KTKBANK) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Karnataka Bank is an Indian commercial bank, headquartered in Mangaluru on the Karnataka coast and founded in 1924, that takes deposits from the public and lends the money out at a higher rate — the gap between the two (net interest margin, around 2.9%-3.1% recently) is its main source of earnings, supplemented by fees and other income. It serves ordinary savers, farmers, small businesses and mid-sized companies through a branch network of over 900 branches, calling itself 'Your Family Bank, Across India'; it is strongest in Karnataka but also present in cities like Mumbai, Delhi, Hyderabad and Chennai, and increasingly originates and underwrites loans digitally. As of March 2026 it held about INR 1,08,779 crores of deposits and about INR 83,340 crores of loans, for total business of roughly INR 1,92,118 crores. Management's stated strategy is to grow the higher-yielding retail, agriculture and MSME book, shrink low-yielding corporate exposure, and fund itself more cheaply with current and savings accounts instead of costly bulk deposits.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Retail, Agri and MSME (RAM)** — Loans to individuals, farmers and small businesses — housing, gold, vehicle and MSME loans are the named growth drivers — and management calls this 'the core driver of our growth strategy', at INR 51,197 crores of the INR 83,340 crores loan book as of March 2026, or roughly 60%.
- **Corporate and mid-corporate advances** — Lending to mid-sized and large companies, including loans taken on opportunistically when they paid better than parking money in treasury; the bank is deliberately rationalising this book, cutting low-yielding exposure while growing mid-corporate (up ~13% in Q4 FY26) with better-quality, better-yielding names.
- **Deposits / liabilities (CASA, retail term, bulk)** — The funding side of the bank — current and savings accounts (CASA) at 33.61% of deposits, or INR 36,559 crores, plus retail term deposits under INR 3 crores at INR 67,648 crores — with an explicit push to raise CASA and cut high-cost bulk deposits (down to 4.2% of total deposits) to keep funding costs low.
- **Branch banking and retail network** — The physical distribution arm of over 900 branches, run as its own vertical alongside a dedicated retail advances vertical, and supported by retail credit hubs (expanded from 3 towards 8) that process loans up to about INR 2.5 crores.
- **Treasury / investments** — The bank's portfolio of government and other securities, which generates investment income and trading profit and is the alternative use of funds against corporate lending; RBI's investment accounting change in FY25 routed some gains to an 'AFS Reserve' rather than through profit.
- **Government and transaction business** — Collection of taxes, GST and customs duty and, via integration with the Karnataka government's Khajane-II treasury system, government challan receipts — pursued mainly as a way to bring in low-cost balances rather than as a large standalone earner.
- **Third-party products and fee income** — Sale of bundled and partner products such as insurance top-ups on the KBL WISE senior-citizen deposit, overseen by a Head of Liabilities Sales and Third-Party Products; contributes to 'other income', which was INR 1,155.50 crores in FY23.
- **Digital origination and technology** — In-house digital lending and platform build-out, described as underwriting about 85% of originated loans digitally as of 2023, with the technology budget raised roughly 80% that year and new products and platforms still being rolled out.
- **Recovery and asset quality management** — Dedicated credit monitoring, recovery and provisioning effort to clean up bad loans — gross NPAs were brought down to 2.78% and net NPAs to 0.98% by March 2026, with provision coverage built back up through accelerated provisioning.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Karnataka Bank is a century-old regional lender that scores well on the things that depend on character rather than economics. The framework credits it for candor: the FY26 call opened with a line-by-line comparison of guidance versus actual results that owned the misses, including advances of Rs.83,340 crore against Rs.84,000-85,000 crore guided and a cost-to-income ratio of 56.34% against 55-56% promised. Management is out of the limelight, a Mangaluru-based team of career bankers with no celebrity positioning. The end-market is genuinely expanding, with Indian credit and the retail/agriculture/MSME segment growing, and the bank appears to have passed the worst of the rate cycle: margin bottomed at 2.92% in the third quarter of FY26 and recovered to 3.07% in the fourth as deposits repriced downward. On the risk side the franchise looks solid where it is granular, with deposits spread across 940 branches, bulk deposits cut to 4.2% of the total, low-cost current and savings balances rebuilt to 33.61%, and no exposure to trend-driven demand at all, which is why fashion risk is rated low.

Almost none of that converts into an advantage a competitor cannot copy. Of the eleven multibagger patterns, three are only partial fits and six are outright misses, and the misses all trace back to the same fact: the products are undifferentiated and the price is set by someone else. The 1924 heritage, the centenary, the 'Your Family Bank' tagline and the acknowledged goodwill in Karnataka earn a partial mark on brand, but management itself says the brand buys neither a price premium nor customer lock-in. That is exactly why pricing power fails outright, with yield on advances sliding from 9.95% to 9.52% to 8.71% while the bank granted rate concessions to keep borrowers who were being quoted lower elsewhere, and it is the same evidence that makes 'good enough goods' a high risk in the risk review. The low-cost-operator route is closed too: cost of deposits rose from 4.62% to between 5.38% and 5.58%, and cost-to-income worsened from 47.14% to roughly 60% before partly recovering to 56.34%, despite repeated rationalisation promises. The market-share ambition to beat industry growth held only briefly, at 19.75% annual advance growth in one quarter, then reversed into negative advance growth between March and September 2025, with total business rising only from Rs.1.76 lakh crore to Rs.1.92 lakh crore. Nothing in the calls explains why a funded newcomer could not replicate the position, and the bank's own account of losing customers on price is the strongest evidence that one could.

Several traits do double duty, which is the clearest way to read the record. Roughly 80% of advances are tied to the RBI's external benchmark: that is the single mechanism behind the high-risk government-dependency rating, behind the cyclicality watch as margin swung from 3.87% to 2.72%, and simultaneously behind the one positive cycle call, because the same linkage that forced margin down is now letting deposits reprice behind it. Likewise, the shift to sourcing through builder tie-ups, business facilitators and loan service providers earns a partial mark as a middleman network while doubling as the bank's stated defence against new-format competitors, though it comes without training programmes or reputation-based lock-in. Management is the clearest split of all: the honesty that earns a positive check sits alongside three chief executives across the period, from an interim holder to the first outside hire who then exited to the current incumbent, meaning repeated strategy resets instead of one plan pursued patiently. Capital allocation shows the same tension, with a modest dividend paid while Rs.1,500 crore of equity was raised at depressed valuations, diluting rather than buying back cheaply. Underlying scores are worst where it matters most, with return on capital at the floor and industry structure deeply negative in a fragmented market of public banks, large private banks, small finance banks and non-bank lenders where the customer holds the bargaining power. The combined verdict is Mixed at 42 out of 100, two stars, from 43 on business quality and 40 on risk safety, and long-term resilience is recorded as unknown.
