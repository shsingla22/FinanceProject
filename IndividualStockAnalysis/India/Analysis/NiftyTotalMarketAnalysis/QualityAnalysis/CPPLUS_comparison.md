### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 31 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 2 improved, 4 regressed, 25 unchanged. 2 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them). 1 was newly assessable in the recent view.

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Allocation | Good (+1.17) | Good (+1.17) | +0.00 |
| Return on Capital | Neutral (-0.33) | Weak (-0.67) | -0.33 |
| Growth | Good (+1.00) | Good (+0.83) | -0.17 |
| Management | Good (+1.17) | Good (+0.83) | -0.33 |
| Industry Structure | Good (+0.67) | Good (+1.00) | +0.33 |
| Customer Benefits | Good (+1.25) | Good (+1.25) | +0.00 |
| Competitive Advantage | Excellent (+1.50) | Good (+0.67) | -0.83 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±3.5 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: STQC certification, localization depth and scale manufacturing are hard entry barriers, and pricing has been rational — an industry-wide 6-8% hike was passed through rather than discounted away, with stable promoter ownership plus Dixon retaining 6.6%. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.8 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: STQC/BIS certification, semiconductor allocation and manufacturing scale form real barriers and the leader is setting orderly monthly price rises rather than discounting, but ~30 brands did clear certification and the company itself launched Eyra explicitly to fight on price at the bottom.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: The market is consolidating post-STQC toward a handful of certified Indian and MNC brands, and component shortages are squeezing smaller players further, but the channel still runs through 1,000+ distributors with some negotiating power. | The long view had said: The structure is a dominant leader plus a fragmented fringe of ~30 brands each roughly a tenth its size, not a stable oligopoly, and management itself cannot name a credible number two.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Growth was explicitly volume- and share-led through H1 with no price rises, but mix (analog to IP, own-brand to 86-87%) is a stated margin driver and a 6-8% price hike was passed through in January 2026 with more planned. | The long view had said: FY27 guidance explicitly splits growth into 25-30% units and a 20-25% rise in per-unit realisation from staggered price hikes plus the HD-to-IP mix shift, and Q4 gross margin expanded ~900bps on that mix.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Management communicates plainly and operationally, but the company is freshly listed, guidance-forward and runs deliberately high-profile celebrity and sponsorship campaigns, so it is not a low-limelight profile. | The long view had said: The brand is loudly promoted but the promoters themselves are low-key operators who only began public disclosure with the Aug 2025 maiden call and speak in operational detail rather than self-promotion.
- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Management disclosed the Q1 STQC supply-chain disruption, the Rs7.7cr one-time labour-code provision, and openly tempered expectations on lens localization economics, while setting and then beating explicit guidance. | The long view had said: Across four calls they repeatedly disclosed unflattering specifics — low-cost inventory now exhausted, profit growth will lag revenue, price-pass-through lag, a INR7.7 crore labour-code provision — and cut their own market-growth assumption from 20%+ to 15% rather than defending it.
- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Good → Neutral. Now: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.06 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: The business is deliberately becoming more asset-heavy through backward integration (housing/moulding, lens assembly, cable JV, further land acquisition), offset by strong operating leverage and a brand/distribution moat — no explicit turnover disclosure to judge either way. | The long view had said: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.06 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: FY26 revenue of INR4,220 crores against a capex plan of only INR200-300 crores and a single Kadapa plant implies high asset turn, protected by brand and distribution, though outsourcing of HD analog to EMS is what keeps it light rather than a structural asset-light model.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 5. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 8 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: R&D is expanding on a named cadence across all four calls — Noida plus new Ahmedabad, a wholly-owned Taiwan R&D subsidiary, an upcoming Bangalore centre, DSIR-certified labs — funding co-design of India's first vision SoC with L&T Semiconductor (9m cameras over three years), a Qualcomm edge-AI platform in trials, and a product mix shift to IP at 73-75%; the caveat is that absolute R&D spend is never disclosed. (The long view had said: R&D footprint expands every call (Noida, Ahmedabad, Taiwan subsidiary, planned Bangalore, DSIR-certified labs) with named pipeline items — Qualcomm edge-AI platform, multi-SoC architecture, AI analytics — but R&D spend is never quantified or shown as a share of industry, and gross margin gains are attributed to mix, localization and price, not innovation rents.)
- **Toll Roads**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: There is genuine standards/certification anchoring — STQC cyber-security certification from April 2025 became the gating requirement and CP PLUS's largest certified portfolio drove share gains — but a CCTV system is a discrete capital purchase, not a sub-1% critical input, and management openly negotiates price increases with the market rather than describing customers who pay without question. (The long view had said: The product is a discretionary capex line for the buyer, not a sub-1% critical input, and the calls repeatedly discuss demand elasticity to price — management models customers trading down from entry IP to analog as ASPs rise, the opposite of toll-road indifference; the STQC standard is a government mandate CP PLUS complies with, not a standard it owns.)

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Management moved from 'we haven't taken any price rises' in Q2 FY26 to a 6-8% hike announced in January 2026 already passed through, with further hikes flagged for FY27 — and volume did not suffer: Q4 FY26 revenue grew 45.5% with EBITDA margin up 800bps to 18%, so cost inflation in DDR/flash/sensors was more than recovered. (The long view had said: Management took a 6-8% hike in January 2026, then moved to staged monthly single-digit hikes into FY27 with ASP guided up 20-25%, explicitly saying input cost is passed on without margin damage; the IP/premium mix shift adds pricing-for-value on top, in a category the analysts themselves call historically deflationary.)

*Unchanged: Friendly Middleman (STRONG FIT); Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 0 worsened, 7 unchanged. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **New Entrants**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Over this window the structural entrant threat ran in the company's favour — STQC certification raised the entry bar and pushed Chinese brands out, and rather than defending, CP PLUS built its own new-format brands (EYRA for partners previously serving Chinese brands, NEXIVUE for the unorganised interiors market). No discounting or margin investment was framed as competitive response; the company raised prices 6-8% in January 2026 instead. (The long view had said: The competitive set is being actively reconstituted rather than settled: ~30 certified brands including a dozen global names (~10% share) and 18 domestic brands are scaling into the vacuum left by the Chinese, and CP PLUS's own two new brands were launched partly because otherwise depth-distribution partners 'would have probably given some ray of light to small private other Indian brands'. The displaced Chinese incumbents remain a re-entry risk if certification rules soften.)

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Cyclicality (WATCH); Technological Innovation (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
