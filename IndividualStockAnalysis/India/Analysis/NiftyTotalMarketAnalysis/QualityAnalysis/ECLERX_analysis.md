# eClerx Services Ltd. (ECLERX) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: IT*

eClerx is an India-headquartered outsourcing firm that runs specialised back-office, data and customer-support work for large Western companies — banks and asset managers, telecom/cable and media firms, high-tech, manufacturing & distribution, retail and fashion/luxury brands. It sells ongoing operational services (trade and client life-cycle processing, KYC and regulatory remediation, customer care and field-technician support, product-content and digital-shelf work, data operations and market intelligence, and finance & accounting), increasingly bundled with its own analytics and automation platforms such as Roboworx CogniFlows, GenAI360 and Market360. It earns money mainly per person deployed and per seat used — hence the constant talk of headcount (~17,700 in FY25), utilisation, bench, seat counts and attrition — plus some transaction-volume-based BPaaS contracts, consulting/staff-augmentation billing and platform deals; revenue is billed largely in US dollars while most costs sit in rupees. Work is delivered mostly offshore from Indian centres (Mumbai, Pune, Chandigarh) with a growing nearshore/onshore footprint (Cairo, Manila, Fayetteville) and roughly a fifth of revenue done onshore at client sites; FY26 operating revenue was USD 469 million, up ~18%, with EBITDA margins in the mid-to-high 20s and the top 10 clients at 59% of revenue.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Financial Markets / BFSI** — Runs trade life-cycle and client life-cycle operations for banks and capital-markets firms — onboarding, KYC, financial-crime compliance, regulatory remediation projects and change/BAU staffing onshore and offshore — historically one of the three core businesses and the main growth driver in FY25, though it had a softer Q4 FY26 as some consulting engagements wound down.
- **Customer Operations / CMT (Communications, Media & Telecom)** — Provides customer care, inbound sales and field-technician operations support for telecom, cable and media clients; a large, steady business that grew over 7% sequentially in Q4 FY26 and is expanding into new delivery centres in Cairo, Manila and Fayetteville, with management watching proposed US rules on offshore call restrictions.
- **Digital (including Fashion & Luxury / CLX)** — Handles digital commerce and marketing content work for consumer brands — product content, creative/digital-shelf and campaign operations, backed by the Market360 tool and a Gold-tier Adobe partnership; it had a sluggish FY26 as the fashion and luxury industry weakened, with management expecting a return to growth in H1 FY27.
- **Analytics and Automation** — The cross-cutting higher-value layer sold across all verticals — data analytics, DataOps, market intelligence, automation tooling and AI platforms (GenAI360, Roboworx CogniFlows, agentic KYC and data-sourcing tools); it grew from about USD 17 million a quarter in FY25 to roughly a USD 90 million annual book by FY26 and is the area getting the most investment.
- **Emerging business / Finance & Accounting (Personiv)** — Outsourced finance and accounting plus order-management services, largely under the Personiv brand acquired earlier and aimed at small and mid-sized businesses in the US; described as growing strongly for three straight quarters in FY26 and a key contributor to the faster growth in non-top-10 clients.
- **Hi-Tech and M&D (Manufacturing & Distribution)** — Serves technology and industrial/distribution clients with transformation, automation and customer-satisfaction programmes, DataOps and market intelligence; both grew strongly through FY26 and hosted the company's first agentic-AI deployment wins, though M&D and retail softened in Q4 FY26.
- **BPaaS (business platform-as-a-service)** — Contracts where eClerx is paid on transaction volumes rather than headcount, sitting mainly inside the client life-cycle work — smaller and more volatile, having been called out as soft in Q1 FY25 on seasonal volume dips.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

eClerx earns a "Decent" 58 out of 100 — 45% weight on business quality (67), 30% on multibagger fit (56), 25% on risk safety (44) — and the three views agree on why. What the company does well is convert a small asset base into cash: every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.11 of sales, every ₹100 of capital produced ₹30 of operating cash this year, free cash flow reached INR 7,560 million (up 41%) with operating cash flow at 75–90% of EBITDA. That cash is returned in a coherent, repeated way — a INR 3,850 million buyback in July 2024, another completed in January 2026 that extinguished 625,000 shares, bonus issues in 2022 and 2026, plus a token dividend. Management scores highest of any area alongside return on capital: the founder pair (Mundhra and Malik, with PD Mundhra as Chairman in FY24-25), an unbroken CFO tenure across all 16 calls, and a strategy set out in FY24 that was tracked quarter after quarter through two weak years and then delivered. That is exactly why Corporate Culture is the one pattern rated a strong fit.

The patterns and the risks are two readings of the same business model, and the model is people-based offshore delivery. About 80% of revenue is tied to full-time-equivalent staffing, which is what makes the business asset-light and cash-generative — and also what caps it. Growth comes from adding heads, not from charging more: not a single price increase is discussed across four years, wage rises squeeze first-quarter margins every year, onshore work carries lower margins, so Pricing Power is rated no fit and the competitive advantage area scores zero. The same headcount base is what Agentic AI is aimed at, which is why technology risk is elevated — clients are pushing automation into precisely the processes eClerx staffs. Management's answer is its own product line (GenAI360, Roboworx CogniFlows, Market360, Compliance Manager), an analytics and automation book that grew from roughly $17 million a quarter in FY25 to a $90 million annual book in FY26, 3,000-plus employees trained on Agentic AI, and a first large-scale Agentic AI win in the fourth quarter of FY26. So the innovation effort is real and revenue-bearing, but it reads as a defence of the existing book rather than an independent moat — hence a partial rather than a strong fit on Innovation Dominance and Cost to Replicate. The same duality shows in low-cost delivery: new centres in Cairo, Manila, Peru and Mohali are growth-directed capex behind an 18% revenue year with rising margins, yet they also concentrate the business in offshore locations that policy can reach — SEZ rules already forced a 50%-in-office reconfiguration in FY23, and management is now monitoring a proposed rule on offshore call restrictions.

The clearest link runs between the strongest pattern and the biggest risk. eClerx is a sub-$500 million player winning share in a large market — 17.9% revenue growth in USD in FY26 against a flat industry — and the share it wins is coming disproportionately from outside its largest accounts, with top-10 concentration easing from 63–64% to 59% and rising counts of $1 million, $3 million and $5 million clients. That broadening is the single most useful thing happening, because customer concentration is the one high risk in the review: the top ten have supplied 59–64% of revenue for four straight years, and one European luxury client's distress showed up directly in reported numbers. But the way share is won also feeds the "good enough" risk — incremental and onshore work is admitted to carry lower margins, 15–20% of the book rolls off every year, and the EBITDA band has settled at 24–28% against 28–32% guided back in FY23, with operating margins swinging about ±4.8 points a year. The CLX Europe acquisition that built the Fashion & Luxury vertical is a similar trade: a complementary bolt-on that adds reach but has been a persistent drag through a sluggish year. Growth of roughly 14% a year sits in the band the framework prizes but is lumpy. Overall resilience is judged sound — long Fortune 500 relationships, work embedded in non-discretionary client operations like know-your-customer and trade settlement, and no signs of new entrants resetting industry economics — but the record supports a good, well-run, cash-returning operator gaining share, not a business with a durable moat.

### Brand Strength — LIKELY FIT
