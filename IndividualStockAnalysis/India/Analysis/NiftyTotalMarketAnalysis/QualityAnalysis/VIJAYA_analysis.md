# Vijaya Diagnost. (VIJAYA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Vijaya Diagnostic Centre runs a chain of diagnostic testing centres in India, where walk-in patients get blood tests and other lab work (pathology) plus scans such as MRI, CT, PET-CT and X-ray (radiology) under one roof. Almost all the money comes straight from individual consumers rather than from hospitals or corporate tie-ups: B2C was 95% of revenue in Q1 FY23 and about 92% in FY26, which management says is better priced and stickier than B2B work. It earns per test and per visit — roughly INR 488 per test and INR 1,808 per patient footfall in Q4 FY26 — so revenue grows by adding centres and pushing more volume through them. It is anchored in Hyderabad (about 67-71% of revenue) with the rest of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, and newer markets in Pune and West Bengal; the network grew from 81 centres at listing to 162 by FY26, with FY26 revenue of INR 814 crores, EBITDA margin 41.4% and profit of INR 173 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Radiology** — Imaging and scans done on advanced equipment (3.0 Tesla MRI, 160-slice and dual-source cardiac CT, PET-CT, gamma camera) at its larger hub centres; steady at roughly 36-38% of revenue and described as the higher-margin half of the business.
- **Pathology** — Routine and specialised lab testing of samples, the larger share of revenue at roughly 62-64%, and the piece most affected by seasonality — a good fever/infection season lifts growth noticeably.
- **Wellness (preventive health check-up packages)** — Bundled retail and corporate health check-up packages that mix pathology and radiology roughly 50:50; it fell to about 6% of revenue during COVID, recovered to 9.6% in Q1 FY23 and 13.4% by Q1 FY25, and has been growing around 30% a year recently.
- **B2C business** — Direct walk-in customers, the core of the model at 92-95% of revenue, which management says is less price-sensitive and carries better margins than the small B2B (hospital/institutional) remainder.
- **Hub-and-spoke centre network** — Large hub centres (often ~10,000 sq ft with a full lab and advanced imaging) supported by smaller spokes and mini-spokes that feed them; hubs in core markets now break even in about 2-3 quarters and new-geography hubs within a year, and FY27 plans call for 4-5 new hubs and 10-12 spokes on a capex of INR 140-150 crores.
- **Home markets: Hyderabad and rest of AP/Telangana** — The original 40-year-old base and still the bulk of the business — Hyderabad alone 67-71% of revenue with the rest of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana adding 18-20% — and still growing around 15-20% a year from existing capacity.
- **PH Diagnostics, Pune** — An acquired diagnostic business in Pune that contributes roughly 6-7% of revenue and grew 8-16% year-on-year in FY26, mainly by opening new centres.
- **West Bengal / Kolkata** — The newest expansion geography, built out to 7 hubs by Q3 FY26 and contributing about 3-4% of revenue, with several new hubs hitting breakeven in three quarters.
- **Medinova (subsidiary)** — A listed subsidiary running a centre in south Kolkata, merged into the parent from April 2024 to cut compliance and operating costs, with no material effect on consolidated numbers.
- **Planned: genomic testing and automated Panjagutta lab** — Announced for FY27 — a fully automated lab with a track system in Panjagutta, Hyderabad to speed up turnaround, plus advanced genomic testing as a new specialised offering.

### The story in depth

Vijaya Diagnostics comes out of all three reviews as a high-quality, self-funded regional franchise that earns an Outstanding rating of 84 out of 100 — 80 on business quality, 86 on multibagger fit and 88 on risk safety. The core of the story is a 40-year Hyderabad brand selling directly to consumers, with 92-95% of revenue coming straight from individuals rather than intermediaries. That single trait is what ties the record together. It shows up in the quality framework as unusually high gross margins (₹88 of every ₹100 of sales left after direct input costs), a 41% operating margin, and working capital where cash arrives 117 days before suppliers must be paid, so customers effectively finance the business. It shows up again as the STRONG FIT on Brand Strength, where 40 years of recall is cited as the defence against discounting entrants and the reason the name travelled successfully into Kolkata, Pune and Bangalore. And it shows up on the risk side as the silver lining on three separate low-rated risks: with an atomised base of roughly 0.75 million individual footfalls a quarter, there is no named customer, distributor, licensor, government tender or referral gatekeeper whose loss could break the model.

The growth engine and the capital discipline reinforce one another rather than competing. The company went from 2 states to 6 and from 81 to 162 centres in five years, funding it entirely from internal accruals while holding net surplus cash of about ₹260 crore and no debt, with growth capex of ₹159-169 crore in FY26 and ₹140-150 crore guided for FY27. New hubs in Kolkata, Krishnanagar, Barasat and Bangalore broke even in three quarters against guidance of 12-14 months, and Khammam and Nandyal in two — so expansion adds profit quickly instead of diluting it, which is why group margin kept expanding to 41.4% through the build-out. That delivery record is exactly what earns the STRONG FIT on Corporate Culture: promises made on early calls — the hub pipeline, the 39-40% margin guidance, the breakeven timelines — were met or beaten later. Market Share Gainers is likewise a STRONG FIT, supported by a 17% revenue CAGR since listing against 15% guidance and a mature Hyderabad cluster still compounding 15-20% off existing capacity. Management is pacing hub additions to the bandwidth it actually has, applying valuation discipline to the 8-15 assets it has been offered, and has doubled the workforce to 3,500 while building out a full senior bench and second-line leadership in the new states. The end market helps too: preventive and wellness testing is compounding at around 30% and has risen from 6% to 13.4% of revenue, and the same shift that could have been a threat is instead scored as a LOW risk because the company pivoted into it early.

The honest tension is that the growth is volume- and mix-led, not price-led — and that one fact drives both a failed pattern and the three risks flagged WATCH. Pricing Power is a NO FIT: acquired PH revenue was flat because, in management's own words, no price increase was taken; wellness package prices were held unchanged through competitive intensity; and May 2026 guidance points to only 1-1.5% realisation growth. Low Price Plus and Cost to Replicate land only at PARTIAL for the same reason — the company positions itself as high-end technology at market-level prices, but without a cost-obsession culture, and management's answer to new entrants is confidence rather than a demonstrated structural barrier. That maps directly onto the risks: New Entrants is a WATCH because online aggregators ran deep-discount pricing from FY23 and, by Q3 FY26, large organised pathology chains were moving into radiology; Good Enough Goods is a WATCH because routine pathology is standardised work a cheaper challenger can copy, and the company already competes at market rates rather than above them. The mitigant, notably, is the same asset that creates the third WATCH — Technological Innovation. Heavy radiology equipment at ₹12-19 crore per hub (PET-CT, 3T MRI, 160-slice CT) is what pathology-only chains cannot match and what protects half the revenue mix, yet it is also fixed, capital-hungry infrastructure carrying recurring replacement capex that a platform shift in imaging or AI reads would be expensive to absorb. Friendly Middleman is a clean NO FIT because the company deliberately avoids doctor-referral arrangements, and the weaker quality scores sit in Industry Structure (0.5), Capital Allocation, Growth and Return on Capital (1.0 each) — but with resilience judged SOUND and discounting so far absorbed without matching it, margins expanding rather than eroding, the balance of evidence stays firmly on the strong side.
