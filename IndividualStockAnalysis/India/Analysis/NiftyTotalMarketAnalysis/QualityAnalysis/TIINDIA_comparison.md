**Checks that improved:**

- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: Exports are a modest ~15% of TI-level revenue and no geographic expansion programme was described in the last four calls. | The long view had said: Exports have been stuck at ~15% of sales for years, the US is shut out by Section 232's 50% duty, Europe is weak, and management explicitly concedes the export lever failed.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Post-GST demand picked up strongly through the festive period, but by Q4 management flagged commodity and fuel inflation and OEM caution — mid-cycle with visible cost pressure rather than a clear bottom. | The long view had said: Post-GST-cut auto demand inflected upward through FY26 with double-digit engineering volume growth, and the depressed railway/MFPD business plus a Rs.1,000cr seven-year contract represents a trough yet to turn, though commodity and fuel inflation now loom.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Contractual pass-through implies rational pricing in the legacy businesses, but the EV segments the company is investing behind face explicit new competition and near-zero gross margins. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.8 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Approval-based barriers protect the legacy tube and chain business, but the new segments show the opposite — seven to eight entrants in heavy electric trucks within two years and rising three-wheeler competitive intensity.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: Heavy growth capex has gone into TI Clean Mobility (capacity now 'built out') and the 3xper CDMO plant, but incremental returns are absent — TICMPL is still pre-EBITDA-breakeven and 3xper slipped ~18 months — even as the core standalone business earns 44% ROIC. | The long view had said: Sustained growth capex (Nasik CRSS, Phaltan, large-diameter tube line, ~Rs.300-350cr core capex for FY27) with capacity kept 2-3 years ahead and core ROIC of 44-49%, though a parallel Rs.500-750cr of parent capital is flowing into loss-making EV.
- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Weak. Now: The TI Medical acquisition platform is admitted to be stuck for lack of targets, with no evidence of post-deal margin improvement in any bolt-on. | The long view had said: Recent deals are genuinely small and complementary (Medicura IV-cannula asset purchase, taking over the body-in-white supplier's facility) and cheaply bought, but the acquiree was a non-operating asset rather than an already-strong business, and management concedes the target pool is thin.

*Unchanged: Research and development; Structural end-market growth; Disciplined stewards; Out of limelight; Gain in market share; Candor; Mini-monopolies; People matter (talent development); Customer types (retail vs corporate); Independent, long-term and tenacious; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Assurance benefits; Intangible benefits; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Return on capital (headline); Distribution to shareholders; Asset turn; Halo effect (caution); Pricing, Mix and Volume; Cost of working capital; Technology advantage.*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 9 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
