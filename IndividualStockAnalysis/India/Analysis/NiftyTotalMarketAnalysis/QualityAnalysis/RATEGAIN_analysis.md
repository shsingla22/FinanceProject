# RateGain (RATEGAIN) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

RateGain is an Indian-listed technology company that sells software and data services to the travel and hospitality industry — hotels and hotel chains, online travel agents (OTAs), airlines, car rental firms, cruise lines and tourism boards (destination management organizations) — to help them attract guests, price better and sell more directly. Its products cover the whole traveller journey: digital and AI-led marketing to acquire guests, data feeds and pricing/competitive intelligence to set rates, and distribution tools (channel manager, booking engine, AI voice agent) to capture bookings, increasingly bundled into one integrated 'UNO' platform. It earns money mainly through recurring subscriptions and usage-based contracts (management said 97-99% of revenue is recurring and about 75% subscription, with gross revenue retention of 90% and net retention of 114%), and it is moving more towards performance-linked pricing where customers pay based on the revenue lift delivered. It operates globally with the US as its largest market (62% of revenue in FY22) followed by Europe, plus growing teams in APAC, the Middle East and Latin America; revenue grew from INR 366.6 crore in FY22 to a stated FY26 run rate of about INR 2,700 crore, helped by acquisitions including Sojern, and headcount is now over 1,300.

**The parts of the business:**

- **MarTech** — Digital and AI-driven marketing for travel brands — audience/travel-intent data, ad campaigns and booking measurement — described as the largest growth engine, growing about 12% year-on-year in Q2 FY26, and now the dominant provider to destination management organizations at roughly a $90 million business after combining ADARA and Sojern.
- **ADARA (within MarTech)** — Data-driven marketing and measurement that helps brands target high-intent travellers and prove return on ad spend; called out as a steady performer with healthy renewals from large enterprise clients.
- **Sojern (acquired, within MarTech)** — A global AI-led travel marketing business bought for roughly INR 1,090 crore that added scale in hotel/property marketing; it runs on a calendar-year cycle, carried about 14-14.4% EBITDA margin at acquisition, and management executed about $12 million of annualized cost savings within 100 days.
- **Demand Booster (within MarTech)** — Search-and-social marketing that drives direct bookings for hotels trying to reduce dependence on OTAs; gaining traction in Europe and APAC and being merged with Sojern's hospitality marketing into a single offering.
- **SoHo (formerly BCV, within MarTech)** — Social media marketing and engagement for hotels; the only acquisition that had been loss-making, it turned profitable in Q3 FY26 on a large marquee account win and cost alignment.
- **AI Concierge (from Sojern)** — An AI guest-service tool that automates up to 80% of guest queries and, per management, lifts hotels' ancillary revenue by up to 300%; being folded into the UNO platform.
- **DaaS (Data-as-a-Service)** — Sells pricing, rate-parity and competitive intelligence data feeds embedded into customers' workflows; grew 10% overall and 17.5% organically in Q2 FY26, and is the company's second main engine.
- **OTA (within DaaS)** — Data services to online travel agencies — described as the largest revenue driver within DaaS — growing through upsell to existing enterprise accounts and higher volumes.
- **Navigator (hotels, within DaaS)** — Rate and demand intelligence for hotel revenue managers, upgraded from dashboards to AI-assisted pricing and market-share recommendations, and distributed partly through partnerships such as with revenue-management provider FLYR.
- **AIR / AirGain (within DaaS)** — Fare parity and competitive pricing intelligence for airlines and, more recently, cruise lines; customers named include Singapore Airlines, Air Astana and Sunrise Airways.
- **CAR (within DaaS)** — Pricing intelligence for car rental companies, including the Rev-AI Clarity pricing tool; management says it serves all the leading car rental firms and won new tech-forward operators in Europe.
- **Distribution** — Connectivity and booking plumbing — the UNO channel manager, booking engine (now integrated with conversational AI systems like ChatGPT so travellers can search and book live rates) and enterprise connectivity for large hotel chains and OTAs — with UNO having its best-ever quarter for new wins in Q2 FY26.
- **UNO VIVA (within Distribution)** — An AI voice agent that answers calls and converts them into direct bookings, pitched at hotels facing labour shortages in Europe and the US; won a CII award for best AI solution.

### The story in depth

RateGain earns a "Decent" rating of 58 out of 100 — 59 for business quality, 56 for fit with the multibagger patterns and 58 for risk safety — with overall resilience judged sound. The foundation is genuinely good. The end market is in secular expansion (score of 2): travel is digitising, online bookings are rising, AI-led discovery is spreading and labour shortages push hotels and airlines toward software. The company has executed against that opening with a well-run multi-region push (also a score of 2) — over 100 countries, five new markets added in Q2 FY26, LATAM up 52%, APAC and the Middle East roughly doubling, with local go-to-market teams on the ground. And the revenue it wins is sticky: the Recurring Revenue pattern is a strong fit, with 97-99% of revenue recurring, 75% subscription, 90% gross retention and 114% net revenue retention. Innovation Dominance is the second strong fit — a named new product lands every year, from Rev AI, Demand AI and Content AI in FY22 through UNO, VIVA voice AI, MCP booking-engine integration, Navigator AI and AI Concierge by FY26 — funded out of roughly 75% gross margins. On the scorecard this shows up as decent marks for customer benefits (0.75) and competitive advantage (0.67).

The single thread running through most of the weaknesses is that growth has been bought more than earned, and the same trait that creates a pattern also creates a risk. Share gains are real — 23 of the top 30 hotel chains and 25 of the top 30 online travel agents, plus dominance in destination marketing after buying Sojern — but the Market Share Gainers pattern only rates a likely fit because that dominance came from combining the number one and number two players rather than out-competing them. Buying the closest competitor is exactly why the New Entrants risk sits at merely "watch", and it is also why capital allocation scores just 0.25 and the cash return on capital is negative on the checklist: every ₹100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings in the business generated only ₹8 of operating cash this year. The same acquisitions built the roughly $90 million destination-marketing business whose customers are largely publicly funded tourism boards — the Government Dependency risk. Meanwhile organic growth ran at only 4-6% in FY26 against 30%-plus booking growth, which is the reason the innovation cadence and the research and development effort are marked as yielding muted returns, why Pricing Power stops at likely rather than strong (no example in four years of raising prices above inflation without losing volume), and why management scores lowest of all the areas at 0.17 — the FY22 promise of "north of 30%" organic growth was ultimately met inorganically. About 94 days of cash also sits tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers.

The risk review lands on three elevated items, and each one is the shadow of a strength. Stakeholder concentration is elevated because results keep turning on single named relationships — one large online travel agent sunsetting dragged the whole distribution business down for several quarters, and one Demand Booster client's exit is blamed for the weak organic growth; that same departure (roughly 18 months before the client returned) is the evidence behind the "good enough goods" watch flag, where cheaper generic tools and customers building in-house are live alternatives. Cyclicality is elevated precisely because of the move management is proud of: performance-linked, pay-for-outcome pricing ties revenue to customers' booking volumes, which is what makes the Pricing Power pattern volume-led rather than price-led and leaves the company levered to travel activity — the pandemic produced two years of losses before the FY22 rebound. Technological innovation is elevated because the industry is one of repeated disruption, with analysts pressing on AI platform threats, chains in-housing their distribution software, and travellers shifting discovery to conversational AI. The offsetting cushion in every case is the same one: 97-99% recurring revenue, 114% net revenue retention and functional business-to-business demand bought on contract, which is why fashion risk and shifting consumer preferences both rate low and why resilience overall is sound. The honest read is a company with a real position in a growing market, an admirable retention profile and a credible product engine, held back by capital that has been spent faster than it has earned its way back and by growth that still depends on purchases and on a handful of large customers.
