# MOIL (MOIL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

MOIL (Manganese Ore India Limited) is a more-than-120-year-old, government-controlled Indian mining company, based in Nagpur and run under the Ministry of Steel, that digs manganese ore out of 10 mines (7 underground, 3 opencast) in Maharashtra and Madhya Pradesh and sells it mainly to India's steel and ferroalloy industry, where manganese is used to remove impurities and harden steel (about 30 kg per tonne of crude steel). It is the country's largest manganese ore producer, accounting for roughly 45–52% of India's output, producing about 1.8 million tonnes in FY25 and earning total income of about INR 1,696 crore that year; earnings swing with steel demand and ore prices, which management says are set by the market and not by MOIL. Alongside raw ore it runs two smaller downstream plants that convert ore into higher-value products — a ferro manganese alloy plant and India's only electrolytic manganese dioxide (EMD) plant. Operations are almost entirely domestic, the company is debt-free with cash to fund its own growth, pays regular dividends, and is spending roughly INR 320 crore a year on capex plus exploration and new shafts to lift output toward 3.5 million tonnes by 2030.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Manganese ore mining (the core activity)** — Mining and selling manganese ore from 10 leased mines in Maharashtra and Madhya Pradesh — about 70% of volume comes from underground mines — and this is overwhelmingly the main business, at roughly 1.8 million tonnes produced and about 1.59 million tonnes sold in FY25.
- **Ferro manganese / ferroalloy plant** — A ferroalloy plant set up in 1998 at the Balaghat mine with capacity of 12,000 tonnes a year, which turns ore into the manganese alloy that steelmakers use — described by management as one of two small 'other segments' beside the main ore business.
- **EMD (Electrolytic Manganese Dioxide) plant** — India's only EMD plant, set up in 1991 at the Dongri Buzurg mine with capacity of about 1,500 tonnes a year, making a chemical-grade manganese product for dry-cell and alkaline batteries, pharmaceuticals and chemicals as an import substitute — small in size but strategically unique.
- **Exploration and new projects (brownfield, greenfield, JVs)** — A growth arm rather than a revenue line: heavy exploratory drilling (about 1,07,000 metres in FY25, adding 16.07 million tonnes of resources), five shaft-sinking projects worth roughly INR 664 crore, and tie-ups outside its home states — a joint venture being formed with Gujarat's GMDC (MOIL to hold 51%) plus exploration MoUs in Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh.
- **Captive wind power** — MOIL says it was the first central public-sector company in India to install windmills, and around 43% of its energy use now comes from renewable sources — power for its own operations rather than a business it sells to outsiders.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*
