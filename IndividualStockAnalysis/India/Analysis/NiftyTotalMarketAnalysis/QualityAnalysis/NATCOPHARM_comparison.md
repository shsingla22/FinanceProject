### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is a branded domestic franchise being extended with new launches (Risdiplam, semaglutide) under the NATCO name, but at ~Rs 105 crores a quarter it is a small slice of a Rs 1,400 crore revenue base and no heritage, A&P spend or premium-pricing evidence appears on the calls. (The long view had said: There is real brand intent — TV campaigns and farmer reach in agro, own-brand semaglutide, and an acquisition strategy explicitly targeting 'established brand business' including Adcock in South Africa — but no heritage premium or sustained A&P-driven share/price evidence in the core business, and the agro brand build has underdelivered since FY23.)
- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Family-led management discloses bad news early and repeatedly (guided the Revlimid decline down quarter after quarter, admitted 'we don't have anything in '27'), delivered what it promised within the window (crop health moved from a Rs 3.2 crore loss to EBITDA positive, semaglutide filed and launched roughly on the March timeline), gave explicit FY27 guidance, and runs a net-cash balance sheet of ~Rs 2,400 crores with dividends each quarter. (The long view had said: Long-tenured family leadership, conservative guidance by habit, a net-cash balance sheet (INR2,400 crores, no leverage) and unusually candid disclosure of bad news — the Kothur warning letter with its remediation plan, the agro provisions, the missed INR1,400 crore PAT target; but delivery against promises is mixed: agro was promised as a INR250-300 crore branded business 'in two, three years' from FY25 and remains immaterial, and the domestic acquisition flagged 'this financial year' in Aug 2022 took until Adcock in 2025.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Share is being gained in emerging markets and agri (first generic Carfilzomib in Brazil, Middle East, Canada post warning-letter lift, CTPR combinations, semaglutide vial uptake) while share is being lost in the core US portfolio, where management admits it is unsure of holding any lenalidomide share. (The long view had said: Share gains exist but are contractual or bought rather than persistently earned — Revlimid rose from single digits to one-third of the market purely under the Teva agreement and then went to zero, while incremental scale in South Africa came from acquiring 35% of Adcock; management is explicit that organic share gain is not available.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Cost to Replicate (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 3 worsened, 3 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: NATCO has no fill-finish capacity of its own for its flagship GLP-1 and depends entirely on a single contract manufacturer, OneSource; separately, a growing share of FY27 profit is routed through one associate stake (Adcock, ~INR35-40cr PAT per quarter). Customer/distributor concentration is not discussed on any of the four calls. (The long view had said: Results were dominated for years by one molecule sold through one front-end partner (Teva controlled Revlimid pricing and market share entirely), with the same pattern repeating on semaglutide (Mylan as partner taking the larger share, Stelis and OneSource as sole contract manufacturers, Eris/Glenmark as India partners); analysts question single-relationship economics on nearly every call.)

**That worsened:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The products are undifferentiated molecules where the buyer chooses on price: Q1 flagged outright 'pricing pressure in the U.S. product portfolio', and the FY27 growth driver — the semaglutide vial — is deliberately aimed at the cheapest end of the market at a fraction of pen realisation, with no brand pull to defend it. (The long view had said: NATCO's economics are repeatedly reset by cheaper equivalent generics: pomalidomide 'exclusivity' shared with 4-5 players, India semaglutide described as a 'crowded 10, 15 generic market', and NATCO itself winning on a vial priced at a quarter of the pen — end-prescriber/patient brand awareness is irrelevant, price decides.)
- **New Entrants**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Every call in the window describes competitor entry resetting the economics of NATCO's key molecules — Revlimid went from consistent contribution in Q1 to 'zero Revlimid' by Q3, and the FY26 pomalidomide launch was crowded on day one with 4-5 exclusivity holders, so a 'namesake' exclusivity produced no bump. (The long view had said: Every profitable window is compressed by waves of new generic entrants — Revlimid 'multiple players' from launch, pomalidomide launched with multiple first-wave exclusivities, and the Indian semaglutide market expected to have 10-15 generics plus out-licensed brands; margin is defended by price disruption rather than position.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: NO SIGNAL → LOW. Why now: Demand is prescription-driven and needs-based (oncology, diabetes, SMA), and where consumer preference has moved — toward GLP-1 obesity and diabetes therapy — NATCO has pivoted into it rather than being eroded by it, filing for both Ozempic and Wegovy equivalents and launching in the first wave. (The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and the only computed fingerprint is clean.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
