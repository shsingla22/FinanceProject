# NBCC (India) Ltd. (NBCC) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSTRUCTION*

NBCC (India) Limited is a government-of-India-owned company (a "Navratna" public sector enterprise) that gets buildings and townships built for government clients — ministries, state governments, state agencies, universities, hospitals and public sector companies — mostly in India, plus some overseas work in the Maldives, Mauritius, Seychelles, Africa and the UAE/Dubai. It does not lay bricks itself: it acts as project manager, awarding tenders to outside contractors and supervising them, so management says its own execution risk sits mainly in how fast it can award and push work along. It earns money three ways: a fee (cost-plus) on government projects it manages under the "PMC" model; profit from redeveloping government land where it builds housing/offices for the client for free and pays for it by selling the surplus commercial and residential space (the "self-sustainable" model); and marketing/completion fees plus sales proceeds from reviving stalled private housing projects handed to it by the courts. Scale as stated on the calls: consolidated order book of about INR 1,27,000 crores, consolidated revenue of about INR 8,329 crores in the first nine months of FY26 and a full-year profit expectation of INR 800–900 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Project Management Consultancy (PMC)** — Fee-based management of government-funded construction — smart cities, IITs/IIMs, central universities, defence and factory buildings, grain storage — where the client's budget pays for the work; management says this is about 40% of the standalone order book and executes in roughly three years.
- **Redevelopment / "self-sustainable" projects** — NBCC rebuilds old government land parcels and colonies (7 GPRA in Delhi — Nauroji Nagar, Netaji Nagar, Sarojini Nagar — plus Kerala State Housing Board, MAHAPREIT, RIICO, Naveen Nagpur) and funds them out of selling commercial/residential space rather than a budget; described as about 60% of the order book (roughly INR 67,000 crores), slower to start because approvals and client loans are needed, and contributed INR 1,500 crores of top line in 9M FY26.
- **Real estate sales / land monetization** — Selling the commercial and residential space NBCC creates — World Trade Centre New Delhi (INR 6,480 crores sold in FY24), the Gurgaon Sector-37D project, and the 21.23-acre Ghitorni land in South Delhi with claimed revenue potential of about INR 8,500 crores — described as the high-margin part that swings quarterly EBITDA, with management guiding to INR 5,000–6,000 crores of profit from Ghitorni plus 37D in FY27-28.
- **Stalled/stressed housing project revival (Amrapali, Supertech)** — Court-appointed completion of collapsed private builders' housing — Amrapali (about 38,000 units, 30,000 completed, contributing INR 1,640 crores of top line in 9M FY26, with a Phase 2 worth INR 17,000 crores of sales) and Supertech (about 50,000 units, INR 16,000 crores of receivables) — a major growth driver and, management argues, proof of its ability to handle problem projects.
- **Subsidiaries (HSCC and HSCL)** — Group companies that win their own construction work — hospitals (including one in Mauritius), sports academies and institutes like IISER Kolkata — and account for the gap between standalone and consolidated numbers, roughly INR 15,000 crores of the consolidated order book and about INR 900–1,000 crores of quarterly consolidated income.
- **Overseas business** — Building work outside India — social housing in the Maldives, projects in Mauritius, a consular building in Seychelles, plus MoUs with an Australian developer and a UAE developer and a planned Dubai real estate subsidiary — small relative to the domestic book but repeatedly flagged as a focus area.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

NBCC's single biggest strength and its single biggest weakness are the same thing: the work is handed to it by the state. Three of the highest quality scores rest on that footing — the framework gives full marks for barriers to entry (Navratna CPSE status and nomination-based awards keep new entrants out, and profit margins have barely moved, within plus or minus 1.6 points, which is what you see when nobody new is fighting for the work), full marks for mini-monopolies (it has become the default national answer for stalled housing and government colony redevelopment, a position the judiciary itself has validated), and full marks for gaining share, with the order book compounding from about INR56,000 crore in August 2022 to INR1,27,000-1,28,000 crore in FY26 on new mandates from DDA, MAHAPREIT, J&K, RIICO, Mumbai Port Trust and the court-awarded Supertech job. The multibagger screen sees the same asset and calls it real but partial: under Cost to Replicate it names statutory and judicial standing as the government's designated rescuer of stalled projects, something no well-funded private builder can buy, with the Supreme Court barring any court from interfering — but it also notes the position is conferred rather than built. Brand Strength and Market Share Gainers land in the same place: genuine institutional trust, but government and judicial trust rather than a consumer franchise, with no advertising spend, no premium pricing and no market-share figures ever cited.

That is exactly why the risk review puts Government Dependency at HIGH RISK: the entire revenue base is government — ministry seed money and interest receivables, PSU land from BHEL, SAIL, HPCL and ONGC, state governments including Kerala Housing Board, J&K, RIICO and MAHAPREIT, and DDA/MCD/PWD colonies. The quality framework docks the company for the flip side of the same fact, marking down the halo effect because the headline results owe more to conferred mandates — court-appointed Amrapali and Supertech work, ministry-allotted GPRA colonies — than to demonstrated operating skill, and marking down independence because targets are handed down by the ministry rather than set by the company, with leadership turning over twice in the window. Concentration compounds it: management itself says 30-40 percent of turnover comes from just Amrapali and the seven GPRA colonies, and two clients, MAHAPREIT and J&K, account for roughly INR40,000 crore of the INR1,27,000 crore order book — an ELEVATED Stakeholder Concentration flag. And the order book is not a promise: roughly INR3,000 crore of works were simply deleted in Q1FY23, which is part of why the Recurring Revenue pattern is a clear NO FIT — every rupee is one-off project execution, with no renewals, licences or installed base.

The economics on top of that position are thin and largely fixed, which is what sinks the pattern screen to a fit score of 10 out of 100. After direct input costs the company keeps only about INR10 of every INR100 of sales, and the framework's harshest mark, minus two on intangible benefits, says plainly that there is no image- or taste-driven pricing power. Pricing Power is a NO FIT because the fee is administered and unchanged across the whole period — 8 percent project management plus a 1 percent marketing fee in FY24, still exactly 1 percent in November 2025 — so growth is volume and execution led, and margin moves only with project mix and the timing of real-estate sales. That same mechanism drives the ELEVATED Cyclicality verdict, with EBITDA falling in both Q2 and Q3 FY26 on mix. The one route out is Forward Integrators, rated PARTIAL: NBCC has moved into owning and selling real estate at Nauroji Nagar/WTC, Ghitorni, Jaipur, Coimbatore and through a Dubai subsidiary, where margins run 20-25 percent against roughly 8 percent on fee work — but own-development revenue is still tiny, and the framework separately notes those self-directed items are the weaker part of the record. Against all this the balance sheet is a genuine cushion: debt-free with large cash, customers effectively fund the business with cash coming back 18 days before suppliers need paying, capital allocation scores best of the seven quality areas at 1.0, and resilience reads SOUND. Delivery, though, is where the story keeps failing — Corporate Culture is a NO FIT despite prompt disclosure of bad news such as the INR110 crore Gurgaon 37D provision and the Kochi provision, because Amrapali was to finish in FY24, then on or before 31 March 2025, and slipped again. The combined rating of 42 out of 100, or Mixed, is the honest arithmetic of a company with a protected position, a fortress balance sheet, weak economics and a promise-versus-delivery gap.
