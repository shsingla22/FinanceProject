# Rainbow Child. (RAINBOW) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Rainbow Children's Medicare Limited (brand: Rainbow Children's Hospital) runs a chain of private hospitals in India that specialise in treating children and in caring for women through pregnancy and childbirth. It earns money by charging patients (and their insurers/employers) for outpatient consultations, inpatient admissions, intensive care, deliveries, fertility treatment and complex surgeries such as heart operations and organ transplants — so revenue tracks bed occupancy, patient volumes and average revenue per occupied bed. It follows a "hub and spoke" model: large hub hospitals do advanced tertiary/quaternary work, while smaller spoke hospitals in the same city handle emergencies, outpatients, obstetrics and neonatal ICU care; doctors are employed full-time rather than working as visiting consultants. From 14 hospitals and about 1,500 beds in 6 cities in FY23, it had grown to roughly 2,000+ beds by FY24 and added nearly 500 beds in FY26, operating in Bengaluru, Chennai, Delhi, Guwahati, Hyderabad, Rajahmundry, Vijayawada, Visakhapatnam and Warangal, with new hospitals under construction in Coimbatore, Gurugram, Pune and Indore.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Rainbow Children's Hospital (pediatric services)** — The core business — newborn and pediatric intensive care, pediatric multi-specialty care, and quaternary care including cardiac surgery and liver, kidney and bone-marrow transplants (about 1,000+ cardiac procedures and 75 transplants in FY24); management describes this as the main driver of revenue and of the group's industry-leading margins.
- **Birthright by Rainbow (perinatal / women's care)** — Integrated pregnancy and women's services — normal and complex obstetrics, multi-specialty fetal care and gynaecology — which feeds the children's hospitals with deliveries and is tracked as a separate volume metric alongside outpatient footfalls and inpatient admissions.
- **Fertility / IVF centres** — IVF services offered inside the hospitals as an extension of Birthright, expanded from 3 centres in FY23 to about 11 by FY24, with further centres such as the Mahadevapura IVF centre in Bangalore added later; the Kondapur fertility centre in Hyderabad is one of the group's three JCI-accredited facilities.
- **Hub hospitals** — Large flagship facilities (Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Chennai, plus planned regional hubs in Coimbatore, Gurugram Sector 44 and Pune) that provide the full range of advanced pediatric specialties and carry the transplant and cardiac programmes.
- **Spoke hospitals and outpatient clinics** — Smaller units of roughly 55–100 beds spread across a city (e.g. Hennur and Electronic City in Bengaluru, OMR in Chennai, Financial District in Hyderabad) offering emergency pediatrics, obstetrics, level-3 NICU and large outpatient volumes, plus standalone outpatient clinics; these are the main vehicle for adding capacity.
- **Mature vs new hospitals** — Management splits the network into mature units (occupancy around 59% in Q4 FY24) and recently opened units (around 32%), because new beds dilute group occupancy — roughly 45–48% in recent quarters — until they ramp up, which is the stated focus after the FY25–FY26 capacity build.
- **Acquired and managed hospitals** — A smaller part of the group — beds managed at Delhi, and acquired hospitals at Warangal and Guwahati that management says have been integrated and are scaling in line with expectations, with more acquisitions being considered.
- **Academic training programmes** — One of the largest private-sector academic programmes in India, offering postgraduate DNB residential training and fellowships; presented as a way to build the doctor pipeline and brand rather than as a meaningful revenue line.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*
