# GSFC (GSFC) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

GSFC (Gujarat State Fertilizers & Chemicals Limited) is a Gujarat, India-based manufacturer that makes and sells crop fertilizers and industrial chemicals, run out of two plant complexes — Fertilizernagar in Vadodara and Sikka near Jamnagar — and listed on the BSE and NSE with the Gujarat state government associated with it (a shareholder on one call called it "the Navaratna of the State Government of Gujarat"). On the fertilizer side it produces urea, DAP, Ammonium Sulphate (AS), Ammonium Phosphate Sulphate (APS) and other NPK/water-soluble grades, sells them through a dealer-retailer chain to farmers at a government-set MRP, and collects a large part of its revenue as subsidy paid by the Government of India when the farmer actually buys at the point-of-sale terminal; it also imports and trades DAP and urea to supplement its own output. On the industrial side it makes and sells chemicals such as Caprolactam, Nylon-6, Melamine, HX Crystal, technical-grade urea, methanol, ammonia and sulphuric acid to domestic and export industrial customers. Profits swing with the gap between selling prices/subsidy rates and bought-in input costs — phosphoric acid, sulphur, sulphuric acid, ammonia, natural gas and benzene — and in FY26 standalone sales were about Rs. 10,827 crore with profit after tax of about Rs. 652 crore, on a balance sheet management describes as having no long-term debt.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Fertilizer segment** — The much larger of the two businesses — roughly Rs. 2,000-2,300 crore of sales in a single quarter versus about Rs. 600 crore for industrial products — it manufactures and sells urea, DAP, Ammonium Sulphate, Ammonium Phosphate Sulphate and other P&K/water-soluble grades to farmers, with FY26 volumes of 22.31 lakh metric tons sold and 17.59 lakh metric tons produced, and margins management pegs at only about 5%-6% EBITDA (roughly Rs. 2,500 per ton in an earlier quarter).
- **Industrial Products (IP) segment / chemicals** — The smaller, non-farm arm selling Caprolactam, Nylon-6, Melamine, HX Crystal, technical-grade urea, methanol and traded ammonia into domestic and export markets, contributing about Rs. 596 crore of quarterly sales and Rs. 200 crore of annual profit in FY26, with earnings driven largely by the Caprolactam-Benzene price spread (quoted at $620-$1,000 per metric ton in various quarters, down to $495 in Q3 FY26).
- **Trading / imported fertilizers** — Alongside its own factories, GSFC buys in and resells DAP and urea — for example finalizing import contracts of about 1.25 lakh metric tons for one quarter — to meet government pressure to supply more DAP; volumes here move up and down with policy and are tracked separately from manufactured volumes.
- **Government subsidy receipts** — Not a product line but the mechanism behind most fertilizer revenue: the Government of India pays subsidy per ton (NBS rates for P&K, plus urea subsidy) when the farmer buys, so changes in subsidy rates swing reported revenue and profit sharply, and management repeatedly reports how current the subsidy collections are — for instance, dues received up to the third/fourth week of April 2026 as of the May 2026 call.
- **Capex / captive input projects** — Ongoing investments — the Sulphuric Acid-V plant commissioned 7 January 2026, Urea-II revamp, the HX Crystal plant started October 2024 at 20 tons per day, and cheaper solar power sourced from GIPCL — aimed at making its own key inputs and cutting cost, with over Rs. 670 crore of projects capitalized in FY26.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

GSFC comes out of all three reviews as a well-run but structurally weak business, and the combined rating of 24 out of 100 (Weak, one star) is arithmetically driven by the middle term: 45% of 41 on business quality, 30% of zero on multibagger fit, and 25% of 24 on risk safety. The zero is not an oversight — of the eleven patterns tested, eight are outright NO FIT and the other three (Low Price Plus, Market Share Gainers, Corporate Culture) only half-fit. What the company does genuinely well is operational and financial housekeeping: it has consistently carried zero long-term debt, funded every expansion from its own cash, and kept building through weak spreads. FY26 delivered real volume — fertilizer volumes up 12% to 22.31 lakh MT, the highest production in five years, with record APS output. But underneath that, the company keeps only ₹32 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs, runs a blended EBITDA margin of 5-6%, and this year generated ₹1 of operating cash for every ₹100 of capital employed. Good behaviour is not producing good returns.

The reason is that the same single trait — selling an undifferentiated, government-priced commodity — is simultaneously the source of the one pattern that partly fits and the source of most of the risk. Because there is no brand, no network effect (scored -2, a plain dealer-to-farmer chain with nothing self-reinforcing in five years of calls) and no ability to set price, the only lever left is cost, which is exactly why Low Price Plus half-fits: captive Sulphuric Acid-V commissioned in January 2026 worth about ₹100 crore a year including steam, captive ammonia on subsidised gas, a solar tie-up with GIPCL, the Urea-II energy revamp, and long-term sulphur and acid contracts. That is genuine, itemised work. But the same absence of pricing power is what the risk review calls Good Enough Goods (ELEVATED) — discounts have to be given and withdrawn to move product at season start and in lean Q4 — and it is why Pricing Power, Brand Strength and Toll Roads are all NO FIT. The volume win reads the same way: it is real, but realisations are set by New Delhi through NBS rates and MRP, and the mix shift from DAP to APS was made to escape losses rather than to raise price. So the Gain in Market Share strength and the Government Dependency HIGH RISK are two descriptions of one fact — the state sets the economics, and this year the settings were favourable.

The cyclical picture works the same way. The Capro-Benzene spread bottoming near $495/MT in Q3 FY26 and recovering past $800/MT by May 2026 as ASEAN capacity rationalised is scored as a strength (emerging from a trough) and as Cyclicality HIGH RISK — the same swing, read forward instead of backward. On the other side of the ledger, input costs moved violently against the company in FY26 (phosphoric acid +34%, sulphur +130%, sulphuric acid +91%), with no ability to pass them on. Being India's sole producer of melamine, caprolactam and HX Crystal sounds like a moat but is graded Cost to Replicate NO FIT, because the defence being sought is a request to the Government of India for a minimum support price or import duties against Chinese and ASEAN product — which is also why New Entrants is ELEVATED. Governance is mixed rather than bad: disclosure improved after shareholders pushed in July 2022, but the company has had four chairmen in five years as a state PSU, and capital allocation is the clearest self-inflicted weakness — payout cut from 21% to 11% while EPS more than doubled, no buybacks contemplated, over ₹2,000 crore sitting idle in deposits, and the one inorganic bet (the 2013 Karnalyte potash stake in Canada) still non-operational nearly a decade on and still asking for money. That idle cash is the direct link between the debt-free strength and the 1% return on capital. Overall resilience is STRAINED: the balance sheet can absorb a bad cycle, but nothing in the business model turns a good cycle into a durable advantage.
