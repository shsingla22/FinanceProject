**That weakened:**

Why now: The whole model is company-owned points of care — a hub-and-spoke network of 269 facilities across 155 cities with 148 greenfield adds since FY23, giving full control of patient experience and pricing — but there is no franchising, royalty or third-party-funded expansion; growth is entirely balance-sheet funded. (The long view had said: Every one of the 269 facilities is company-owned/operated with no franchising, giving full control of pricing and experience, and optical/pharmacy retail attached to the clinics is a stable ~21% of revenue — but this is a services network rather than a manufacturer integrating forward, and no D2C/e-commerce or franchise royalty stream exists.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Claims of market leadership in Tamil Nadu and Telangana are repeated each call and growth is broad-based (South +22.6%, West +19%, North +19.7% in FY26) with new-market entry into Delhi-NCR and 19 African facilities, but no explicit share percentages or comparisons against named competitors are ever given. (The long view had said: Consistent 21-23% revenue growth with 148 greenfield adds since FY23, 26 new cities in FY26, and Africa operations implies share gain, and management claims leadership in Tamil Nadu/Telangana — but across all seven calls no explicit market-share percentage or comparison versus named competitors is ever given.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: QUANT SIGNAL → NO FIT. Why now: Demand is described as coming directly from patients drawn by brand recall and proximity, not from independent prescribers or referrers who choose on the patient's behalf; the doctors are the company's own employed clinicians (1,000+ on network) rather than a third-party trade channel. (The long view had said: The excerpts are silent on any external prescriber/optometrist referral channel; doctors are in-house employees and training (50 doctors in Q3 FY26, 140 in FY26) is internal capability building, not trade-channel investment.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The calls contain no cost-culture language, itemised savings or no-frills positioning; the only cost discussion is one-off merger compliance synergies (auditors, GST leakage, filing costs), which is not a scale cost advantage. (The long view had said: The asset-light, leased, hub-and-spoke format (primary clinics at INR30-35 lakh capex, 220 of 221 facilities leased) delivers high capital turns and margins held at ~28-29% through 52-60 openings a year, but there is no cost-obsession culture, itemised savings or low-price positioning in any call.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
