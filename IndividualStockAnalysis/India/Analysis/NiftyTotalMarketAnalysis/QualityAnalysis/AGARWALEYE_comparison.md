**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Entry requires clinical talent, costly equipment and accreditation, and pricing appears rational since margins expanded to 28.9% with no mention of discounting, but the barrier is moderate and the company itself is the main new entrant in most markets. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±3.6 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing behaviour is rational with no discount war and steady 2-3% annual increases, and clinical talent plus cluster build-out is a real barrier, but the modest price increase also signals limited pricing latitude against low-cost local competition.
- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: The only deal in the window is a simple, complementary intra-group absorption of Dr. Agarwal's Eye Hospital that simplifies structure and removes duplicate audit/GST/filing costs, though it is a related-party consolidation at a 15% premium rather than an external bolt-on and no post-deal OPM uplift has yet been quantified. | The long view had said: Past bolt-ons in Punjab, Maharashtra and Gujarat were small and complementary with deferred consideration winding down, but the AEHL related-party merger drew repeated, unanswered minority objections on the swap ratio, offsetting the good record.
- **Network effects** (Competitive Advantage): Weak → Neutral. Now: The spoke-to-hub referral structure concentrates surgical volume internally but is an operating model, not a self-reinforcing two-sided network that gets more valuable to each user as it scales. | The long view had said: There is no two-sided network that strengthens with scale; the hub-and-spoke referral flow is an internal operating design, and patient demand is generated independently at each catchment.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: The target markets are explicitly underserved Tier-2/3 geographies held by unorganised single-clinic practitioners, and 62-63% of the network sits in non-Tier-1 cities where that share is being taken. | The long view had said: The expansion thesis is built on entering underserved Tier 2/3 micro-markets served by weaker standalone clinics, which management targets systematically, though competitor weakness is implied rather than explicitly evidenced.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Good → Neutral. Now: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹0.53 of sales — a moderate asset intensity. And from the calls: The hub-and-spoke model is lighter at the spoke level, but the business is equipment- and property-intensive with continuous purchases of Femto/CATALYS/VisuMax/LUMERA systems, and no asset-turn or ROCE figure is given to support an asset-light claim. | The long view had said: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹0.53 of sales — a moderate asset intensity. And from the calls: The model is explicitly asset-light — 220 of 221 facilities leased, Rs.30-35 lakh capex for a primary centre and Rs.10-12cr for a tertiary generating Rs.15-16cr revenue by year five — combined with a brand and clinical-talent moat.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Results are strong but the assessment is not fully halo-proof: growth is flattered by a fast-expanding base, and the high-end cataract mix is quoted as 43.5% of procedures for 9M yet 26.3% for the full year, an inconsistency that went unchallenged. | The long view had said: Headline growth is flattered by IPO/PE-funded centre additions and deleveraging, but skill is separable in the underlying data — 14% same-store growth on pre-FY22 facilities and margins held at ~28.5% through 52 openings.

*Unchanged: People matter (talent development); Technology advantage; Structural end-market growth; Cyclical market growth; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Research and development; Convenience benefits; Gain in market share; Disciplined stewards; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Distribution advantage; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Out of limelight; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Independent, long-term and tenacious; Cost of working capital; Profit margin; Assurance benefits; Mini-monopolies; Candor; Geographical expansion; Return on capital (headline); Intangible benefits; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Security by obscurity.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 6 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 6. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Across the window management set guidance and then claimed delivery against it, honoured the long-pending merger promise with named independent valuers and fairness opinions, and disclosed soft spots candidly (Tamil Nadu monsoon, festive-quarter impact in the West) rather than burying them; leadership is family-owned and stable with promoters at ~30.9% post-merger. **That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Growth is well above sector rates — FY26 revenue +21.6% to INR 2,080 crores, daily walk-ins up 25%, 148 greenfield facilities since FY23, plus new-market entry into Delhi-NCR and 19 African facilities — but management only asserts leadership qualitatively and never cites a share percentage or names a competitor. (The long view had said: Consistent 20%+ revenue growth with 148 greenfield additions since FY23, one new facility a week in FY26, and claimed leadership in Tamil Nadu and Telangana plus 19 African facilities point to share gain, but management never quantifies market share or benchmarks against named competitors — and gains are funded by IPO/equity capital rather than purely organic outperformance, a point minority shareholders raised on the merger call.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No pricing positioning, unit economics per procedure, or cost-culture disclosure appears in the FY26 calls; the only cost commentary is one-off merger synergies (dual auditors, GST cross-charge leakage, filing costs), which is too narrow to judge a low-price-plus model. (The long view had said: The hub-and-spoke, asset-light, leased-format model (220 of 221 facilities leased; primary clinics at INR30-35 lakh capex) delivers accessible eye care at high asset turns and rising margins (28.9% FY26 with 60 openings a year), but the calls show no explicit cost-obsession culture, itemised savings, or deliberate price-cutting to buy volume.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (LIKELY FIT); Forward Integrators (LIKELY FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
