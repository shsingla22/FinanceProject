# Greaves Cotton (GREAVESCOT) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Greaves Cotton is an old Indian engineering company (it dates itself to 1859, incorporated 1922, registered in Maharashtra and headquartered in Mumbai) that designs and manufactures engines, generator sets, vehicle components and electric vehicles, and then makes money again servicing them. Its biggest traditional business is small diesel engines that go into three-wheeler auto-rickshaws sold to vehicle makers (OEMs), plus engines for non-automotive uses like gensets, marine, firefighting, defence, construction and agriculture; alongside this it sells control cables and other engineered components (Excel Controlinkage), spare parts and servicing through a franchise retail/workshop network, and builds its own electric scooters (Ampere brand) and electric three-wheelers (Eltra) through subsidiary Greaves Electric Mobility. So it earns revenue partly B2B from OEMs and industrial customers, and partly B2C from consumers buying electric two- and three-wheelers, spare parts and service. It is mainly an India business with a pan-India dealer and service network, and a growing export share — roughly 9% of revenue in FY25 rising to about 13% in FY26 — going to Europe, the US and the Middle East; consolidated quarterly revenue has run in the region of Rs 640-660 crore, and management has grouped everything under three segments called Energy Solutions, Mobility Solutions and Industrial Solutions.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Energy Solutions** — Generator sets (gensets) and the engines behind them, plus the spares, service and annual maintenance contracts sold afterwards — management calls India's genset market a 10-12% annual growth market, and this segment grew about 20-21% year-on-year in FY26 with the aftermarket/spares part growing even faster at 35-40%; it recently launched a 650 kVA genset built on an in-house engine and won a single Rs 35 crore institutional order.
- **Mobility Solutions** — The automotive side — diesel and other fuel engines for three-wheelers sold to OEMs, exports of Euro V+ engines to Europe (including to Ligier), the Excel Controlinkage components business making control cables and assemblies, and the aftermarket retail spares business; it grew about 15-16% in FY26 with the automotive piece up 48% year-on-year in the March quarter, and management describes it as deliberately 'fuel-agnostic', noting diesel still holds 18-20% of the three-wheeler market.
- **Greaves Retail / Greaves Care (aftermarket retail, part of Mobility Solutions)** — Sells multi-brand spare parts and runs franchisee-owned Greaves Care service workshops (163 outlets as of mid-2024) for three-wheelers, small commercial vehicles and EVs — roughly Rs 148 crore of revenue in a quarter, described as asset-light, cash-generating and high-return, though by FY26 the company exited non-core lines like two-wheeler parts and construction equipment parts to focus on the core.
- **Industrial Solutions** — Special-purpose engines for demanding applications such as firefighting, marine, defence and agriculture — the smallest and slowest-growing of the three segments (about 3% growth over nine months of FY26, 15% in the March quarter), but with wins like a direct defence supply order, FM/UL-certified engines for a European customer, and over 300 engines supplied to Assam Inland Waterways.
- **Greaves Electric Mobility (GEMPL)** — The separately-run electric vehicle arm making Ampere electric scooters (roughly Rs 40,000 to Rs 1,10,000 price points, about 3.4% market share and a top-five position in e-two-wheelers in mid-2024) and Eltra electric three-wheelers for passengers and cargo; it was scaled up with a committed investment of up to about $220 million from partner Abdul Latif Jameel, and its products depend partly on government subsidy schemes.
- **Greaves Finance** — Mentioned by management as a post-purchase/financing arm alongside Greaves Retail, helping customers fund vehicle purchases; the calls give no size for it.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Greaves Cotton runs a business that is unusually light on its feet. Every rupee of assets produces ₹1.20 of sales, customers pay in cash six days before suppliers need to be paid, and the balance sheet has stayed net-cash or near-zero-debt for four straight years. Management has behaved consistently with that: no large acquisitions, deliberate exits from low-return segments, and the electric vehicle arm's funding pushed out to an external IPO rather than loaded onto the parent. It is also genuinely growing into growing markets — gensets guided at 10-12% annual growth over five years, data-centre power, and India's shift to electric vehicles — with exports up from about 9% of revenue in FY25 to 13% in FY26 and a Rs 500-700 crore multi-year earmark for new technology, products and capacity. On paper this is the profile of a well-run, self-funding compounder, and the capital allocation score (0.8) is the strongest thing in the whole record.

The problem is that none of it is yet turning into a return. For every ₹100 of capital in the business, the company generated ₹2 of operating cash this year — a 2% cash return, the single worst check in the framework and the reason return on capital scores negative overall. The reason sits directly inside the growth story. Share gains in electric two-wheelers are real and sequential (3.4% in Q1 FY25 to 3.6% to 4.4% for FY26, 5% in Q3 FY26, 21.5% in Bihar, eastern region from 5.7% to 8%), but the Market Share Gainers pattern itself notes those gains have been bought by a business running at roughly -20% EBITDA and needing a Rs 1,000 crore IPO to keep going. The same trait that reads as a strength in one place is the cost driver in another: the aggressive pricing that wins share is why both Pricing Power and Low Price Plus come back as NO FIT — price moves across 2022-2026 are commodity pass-through or outright cuts, never value capture — and why the Pricing, Mix and Volume check scores negative, with growth coming from volume and cost-cutting rather than better realisation. Every one of the eleven multibagger patterns lands at PARTIAL or NO FIT; nothing scores a clean fit, which is why the pattern component contributes only 10 out of 100.

The risk review explains why that gap may not close on its own, and it is the same story again from a third angle. The core profit engine is diesel single-cylinder engines for three-wheelers, and that end market is migrating to CNG and electric — management admits it mis-timed the transition and now describes the whole strategy as 'fuel agnostic'. So the legacy business is being cannibalised by the new one, while the new one depends on government subsidy schemes that have already produced eligibility 'blips', and competes against venture-funded direct-to-consumer entrants in a market management itself calls price-led and crowded. That is three HIGH RISK channels — Technological Innovation, Government Dependency, New Entrants — reinforcing each other, and the Cost to Replicate pattern confirms there is no barrier a funded newcomer could not rebuild. The genuinely defensible pieces are small and unquantified: the Excel Controlinkage bolt-on at 28-30% margins, the aftermarket and spares stream growing 35-40%, the Upahar mechanic loyalty app and 163 Greaves Care franchise outlets. These are exactly the assets that hold up the Toll Roads, Recurring Revenue and Friendly Middleman patterns at PARTIAL, and they are also the silver linings inside the Good Enough Goods and Cyclicality risks. The combined verdict — 29 out of 100, Weak, resilience STRESSED — is not a judgement that the company is badly run; it is that a well-managed, cash-safe, asset-light business is currently earning 2% on its capital while paying for a share-buying campaign in a market where it has no pricing power.

The honest way to hold this name is as a turnaround that has not yet turned. The management scorecard (0.33) reflects a record that cuts both ways — net cash throughout, prompt conservative disclosure of the Rs 16 crore ePowertrain impairment, an ICSI governance award — set against delivery and stability problems the Corporate Culture pattern flags directly. Fashion risk is LOW because demand is functional and commercial, so this is not a business that can disappear; it is one that has to prove the small profitable pieces can outgrow the large loss-making one before the capital return justifies the price.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Technological Innovation, Government Dependency, New Entrants, Cyclicality, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows multiple financial stress signals — 2 of the 3 stress checks are flagged — multiple warning lights at once.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.3× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ⚠️ Only about -31% of recent operating profit became cash (was 90%) — profits are getting stuck in paper, an early stress signal.
- ⚠️ Returns on capital slid from about 24% to 6% — the business is earning less and less on the money invested in it.

### Technological Innovation — HIGH RISK
*"Out-invented by the industry"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 2 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** The core profit engine is diesel/ICE single-cylinder engines for three-wheelers whose end market is structurally migrating to CNG and electric — management now frames the entire strategy as 'fuel agnostic' and admits the transition is only 'more gradual than we expected', which is a timing argument, not a protection. The FY26 write-off of ePowertrain assets shows the cost of betting on the wrong transition technology, while the EV arm remains loss-making after four years of investment.

> "We also continue to see a structural shift in the fuel mix... we see that diesel will still hold an 18% to 20% market share" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Incremental, value-adding renewal cadence — CPCB-IV+ gensets, in-house 650 kVA engine, Euro V+ exports, rare-earth-free motors — and Excel's controls that are 'relevant irrespective of the technology that keeps changing'*

- ✅ Growth is holding up: -2% a year earlier, 18% recently — no structural stall.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has collapsed from a peak of 15% to 7% — the Nobel-Biocare-style fingerprint of a premium being competed away.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** EV volumes and pricing depend directly on MHI subsidy schemes (FAME/EMPS), with an admitted period of 'blips' requiring management to work with the government to resolve eligibility, and analysts questioning profitability given 'lack of support from the PLI and government related scheme'. On the ICE side, genset demand is being 'steered' by CPCB-IV+ emission regulation, GST 2.0 is cited as an auto-demand driver, and a Labour Code provision hit Q3 FY26 profits; defence and institutional orders add further policy-linked revenue.

> "MHI has communicated that our two-wheeler electric vehicles under Ampere brand are eligible to be tested by the relevant testing agencies for EMPS registration" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Diversification away from policy-driven revenue: exports up from ~9% to 13-14% of revenue, private OEM/industrial/marine/firefighting demand, and Eltra pricing cut through BOM efficiency rather than subsidy reliance*

### New Entrants — HIGH RISK
*"A new player changes the rules"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 2 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** The EV two-wheeler market has been reset by structurally different, venture-funded, direct-to-consumer entrants; analysts pressed management on this from the very first call, and by FY26 management describes the market as price-led and crowded while Greaves holds only ~4-5% share as the sixth player. Aggressive repricing of Eltra City and heavy brand/dealer investment are explicitly competitive responses.

> "a market increasingly crowded with price-led competition" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Building its own new-format position early ('we got into this early as early mover'), LFP safety differentiation, Build-for-Bharat regional strongholds (21.5% share in Bihar), and financing tie-ups that widen access*

- ✅ Growth is holding up: -2% a year earlier, 18% recently — no structural stall.

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** The CFO explicitly concedes seasonality/cyclicality in the standalone engine business, and commodity swings bookend the period — raw-material inflation and price hikes in FY23, then an input-cost spike again in Q4 FY26; genset demand is tied to infrastructure and industrial capex cycles. ## What to watch

- Return on capital (headline) — the 2% cash return on capital is the binding constraint; a move toward the standalone segments' stated 30%+ ROCE at the group level is what would re-rate this, and nothing else substitutes for it.
- Market Share Gainers pattern — watch whether electric two-wheeler share keeps rising past 5% while EBITDA losses narrow from the roughly -20% level; share gains that still cost the same amount per point would confirm the pattern is bought, not earned.
- Pricing Power / Pricing, Mix and Volume — any evidence of margin gains coming from better realisation rather than cost optimisation or commodity pass-through would be the first real sign of a moat forming.
- Government Dependency (HIGH RISK) — further subsidy 'blips' under FAME/EMPS, or continued absence of PLI support, would hit EV volumes and pricing at exactly the point the business can least absorb it.
- Toll Roads and Recurring Revenue patterns — management putting actual numbers behind Excel Controlinkage and the aftermarket/AMC stream, which are currently PARTIAL only because they are unquantified, would show whether the defensible core is large enough to matter.
