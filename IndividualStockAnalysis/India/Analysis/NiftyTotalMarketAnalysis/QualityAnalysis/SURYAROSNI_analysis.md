# Surya Roshni (SURYAROSNI) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Surya Roshni is an Indian manufacturer with two very different businesses under one roof: it makes steel pipes and strips, and it makes lighting products and household electrical goods sold under the 'Surya' brand. The steel side rolls and welds pipe — ordinary ERW pipe plus higher-priced 'value-added' items like API and spiral coated pipe for oil-and-gas lines, galvanized pipe for water schemes, and hollow/structural sections for construction and fabrication — selling to government infrastructure projects, industrial buyers and, increasingly, export markets. The lighting side sells LED bulbs, battens and downlighters through a retail dealer/distributor network, project-based 'professional' lighting for airports, railways, tunnels, stadiums and facades, plus appliances such as fans, water pumps and wires and cables. It earns money on manufacturing spread — management tracks steel profitability as EBITDA per tonne and pushes product mix toward value-added items — from plants at Bahadurgarh, Gwalior, Anjar and Hindupur in India; FY26 consolidated revenue was about Rs 7,540 crore with Rs 541 crore EBITDA and Rs 286 crore profit, and the company describes itself as zero-debt with a Rs 337 crore cash surplus.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Steel Pipes and Strips** — Manufactures and sells steel pipe and cold-rolled strip — ERW, API/spiral coated line pipe for oil and gas, galvanized pipe, and hollow section/structural pipe — and is by far the larger business at Rs 5,731 crore of FY26 revenue (roughly three-quarters of the company) on 9.04 lakh tonnes of volume, with value-added products at 43% of volume and an order book above Rs 1,000 crore.
- **Lighting and Consumer Durables** — Sells branded lighting and household electrical goods, contributing Rs 1,809 crore of FY26 revenue (up 7%) with Rs 156 crore EBITDA at roughly 8.8% margins; management targets 22%-25% annual value growth over the medium term.
- **Consumer Lighting (within Lighting segment)** — LED bulbs, battens and downlighters sold through retailers and distributors — described as the volume backbone of the lighting segment, growing strongly on volumes though periodically hit by price erosion (about 9% in Q1 FY25).
- **Professional Lighting (within Lighting segment)** — Project-based lighting for infrastructure and industry — airports, railways, tunnels, stadiums, facades, street and solar lighting — worth Rs 473 crore in FY26, about 26% of lighting revenue, with a Rs 160 crore order book at year end.
- **Appliances / Consumer Durables (within Lighting segment)** — Fans and other home appliances, plus a new 'Surya Water Pumps' monoblock residential pump line launched in July 2024 aimed at a roughly Rs 1,000 crore Indian market driven by the government's Har Ghar Jal water scheme; appliance volumes grew 15% and fans 43% in Q1 FY25.
- **Wire and Cable (within Lighting segment)** — A newly built end-to-end manufactured product line that did Rs 38 crore in FY26, with a Rs 260 crore target for FY27 and a three-year guidance of Rs 500-600 crore.
- **Exports (within Steel)** — Overseas pipe sales, 1.36 lakh tonnes in FY26 with expansion into the UK for section pipe and order visibility in North America and Europe; targeted to cross 2.5 lakh tonnes next year, though Q4 FY26 exports fell to zero because of the Middle East crisis.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Surya Roshni is a financially careful company operating in a difficult patch of its own industry, and the two facts explain most of what the records show. The strongest marks in the quality framework are about stewardship rather than business power: four straight years of paying down borrowings to the point of zero total debt, Rs 337 crore of net cash, a roughly Rs 500 crore expansion of steel capacity from 12 to 19 lakh tons paid for entirely out of the company's own earnings, no acquisitions, and a stated policy of splitting profits one-third to shareholders, one-third to growth and one-third to capex, with dividends of Rs 5.00 in FY26 and a buyback under consideration. That same conservatism is what the multibagger screen picks up as a partial fit on Corporate Culture, and it is also the single biggest offset in the risk review: the balance sheet is what allows the company to keep investing counter-cyclically while steel prices sit at multi-year lows. The rating of 47 out of 100 — 53 for business quality, 33 for pattern fit, 52 for risk safety — reflects a company judged sound and well run but not, on this evidence, structurally advantaged.

The weak spots cluster in one place: the company does not appear to control its own prices. The quality framework marks pricing, mix and volume negative on 8-9% steel price erosion and 29% lighting price erosion, with the value-added share of volume slipping to 43% in FY26 from 46% in FY25 against a stated 70% target. The screen reaches the same conclusion from a different direction, rejecting both Pricing Power and Toll Roads outright, because pipes are a large, price-sensitive item bought through tenders where realisations move with HR coil, and rejecting Cost to Replicate because, in management's own words, everybody buys the same HR coil and pipe mills cost Rs 75-250 crore each to build. The risk review then turns the same fact into its two most severe findings. Cyclicality is HIGH RISK precisely because about three-quarters of revenue is steel pipes whose margins management narrates through the HR coil price falling from Rs 75,000 to Rs 55,000 a ton, with a Rs 12 crore stock loss in Q3 FY26 and a Rs 40-45 crore gain expected in Q4. Good Enough Goods is a WATCH for the same reason at the product end: plain ERW pipe earns Rs 4,500-5,000 a ton against Rs 10,000-plus for value-added, so the entire margin defence depends on moving up-mix — the very mix that went backwards this year. Market share is contested rather than demonstrated: FY26 steel volumes grew only 3% to 9.04 lakh tons, peers posted visibly stronger quarters, and management asserts it has peer data without ever giving share numbers.

The second pairing of strength and risk runs through the customer base. Steel pipe demand is anchored in government programmes — Jal Jeevan/Har Ghar Jal, Amrut, city gas distribution and ONGC/Oil India tenders — which makes Government Dependency a HIGH RISK, with misses attributed to election timing, a 35% fall in API volumes, and only Rs 3,000 crore released against a Rs 55,000 crore Jal Jeevan budget. Yet the framework reads that same trough as an identifiable set of reversals ahead, and the response to it is what earns the better scores elsewhere: exports built into the UK, North America (65,000 tons booked, a roughly Rs 1,000 crore order book at Rs 9,000-10,000 a ton of EBITDA), the Middle East and Africa, targeting exports rising to 2.5 lakh tons, plus new categories from R&D — water pumps, wire and cable at Rs 38 crore heading for a Rs 260 crore FY27 target, smart and solar street lights, and a first-in-India ERW approval as a substitute for seamless casing. Those launches are also why the screen grades Innovation Dominance, Brand Strength and Friendly Middleman as partial rather than absent, and why Stakeholder Concentration, Shifting Consumer Preferences and Fashion Risk all come out LOW — the business is spread across two divisions, retail and project channels, and several utility categories with no single decisive customer or supplier. What the story lacks is self-reinforcement: Network effects scores the worst mark in the framework because nothing here is two-sided, growth comes from adding capacity and distribution headcount, and sales growth of about 9% a year has been lumpy. Overall resilience is judged SOUND; the upside case rests on cyclical recovery and diversification working, not on a moat.

## What to watch
