**Checks that improved:**

- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Consistently organic growth with no M&A, deliberate restraint on finely-priced refinance and bulk deposits, strong capital at CRAR 16.3%, and counter-cyclical floating provisions of Rs 400 crore and Rs 1,500 crore funded from a one-off tax refund rather than booked as profit. | The long view had said: Consistently organic and prudent — deliberately shed bulk deposits and low-yield assets to protect margins, kept CET1 at 13-13.9%, and used an IT refund to create Rs 1,500cr of floating provisions rather than book the profit.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: Deposit cost is falling (4.91% to 4.66%) which should support NIM off its trough, but credit cost at 0.29% and SMA at 0.07% are cyclical bests with far more room to worsen than improve, so the bank sits mid-to-late cycle, not near the bottom. | The long view had said: The bank sits near the top of the credit cycle, not the bottom — credit cost at 0.29%, GNPA 1.89% and SMA 0.07% are cycle-best levels that management itself buffers against with floating provisions, implying normalisation ahead.
- **Distribution to shareholders** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: A clear dividend of Rs 8.5 per share was declared off record FY26 profit of Rs 20,021 crore with capital adequacy still at 15.8%, indicating a coherent payout policy, though no buyback or explicit distribution framework was articulated. | The long view had said: A dividend of Rs 8.5/share was declared for FY26 and growth has been self-funded from accruals without equity raising, but there is no buyback and no articulated distribution policy or valuation discipline.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: Nothing in the calls suggests a monopoly-like position; the bank competes for the same RAM and corporate assets as every other large lender and describes itself only relative to 'peer banks' and 'top quartile' metrics. | The long view had said: Commercial banking products here are undifferentiated commodities — the bank competes on price for both fine-priced corporate assets and deposits, with no monopoly position in any customer's mind.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: A 38-39% CASA ratio and 14.5% current-account growth imply some transactional stickiness and switching friction, but no regional monopoly or captive franchise is claimed or evident. | The long view had said: Switching costs are weak — deposits are openly contested and management repeatedly cites liability-side pressure and sticky market pricing it cannot control.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: BoB is a Rs 30-lakh-crore, heavily analyst-covered listed PSU bank whose provisional numbers are pre-covered by media and which courts international recognition — the opposite of an ignored niche. | The long view had said: This is one of India's largest banks with a Rs 30 lakh crore balance sheet, holding dedicated media and analyst meets each quarter with heavy sell-side coverage — the opposite of under-the-radar.
- **Candor** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Management flagged the prior-year one-off that inflated the base, disclosed the NMC settlement fully with both reported and adjusted profit, gave credit cost both with and without the floating provision, and openly admitted ROA fell below its own guidance while deferring the full-year number — offset slightly by heavy 'best in N quarters' framing. | The long view had said: Guidance is explicit and shortfalls are acknowledged, but candor is uneven — the bank declined to quantify the RBI investment-norm impact when peers did, and on NMC it reversed a stated intent to litigate while withholding the rationale as confidential.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Sustained 14-17% advance growth well above system growth implies share is being taken from someone, but no weak competitor or specific pool of donated share is identified in the calls. | The long view had said: Weaker PSU peers are ceding ground — the bank's CASA and overall growth run ahead of peer banks and well ahead of system credit growth in FY26 and Q1FY27.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Tone is largely operational and data-led, but the MD repeatedly frames results as 'one of the best in last 8 quarters', 'highest ever' and highlights an external award, which is mild self-promotion rather than genuine low-profile behaviour. | The long view had said: Management is career-banker, substance-first — MD and EDs present detailed operating metrics with no personality-driven narrative across all four calls.

Now: A dedicated retail liability franchise and IT/platform vertical exist and deposit gathering is clearly effective, but the calls give no detail on branch reach, digital capability or sales-force intimacy to judge accessibility as an advantage. | The long view had said: Accessibility is a genuine strength — the bob World mobile platform reached 38% active penetration of non-FI customers within a year and directly unlocked cross-sell of personal loans into the existing base.

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 8 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
