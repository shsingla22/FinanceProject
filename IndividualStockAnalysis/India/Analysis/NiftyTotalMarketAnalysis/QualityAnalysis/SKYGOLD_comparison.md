**Checks that improved:**

- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: Results should be discounted for a large exogenous tailwind — record gold prices inflate rupee revenue and push consumers toward the lightweight/low-carat product it happens to specialise in — though the 163bps gross margin expansion and mix shift are attributable to management action rather than price. | The long view had said: Results are flattered by a 60-70% gold price run, a formalisation tailwind and an advanced-gold accounting model that records only job-work fees, so skill is hard to separate from favourable conditions.
- **Network effects** (Competitive Advantage): Weak → Neutral. Now: No self-reinforcing two-sided network is evident; the design-bank app and retailer co-creation loop create feedback on what sells but do not strengthen with scale in the way a network would. | The long view had said: No self-reinforcing two-sided network exists; the design bank and B2B app are useful tools but confer no value that grows with the number of participants.
- **Distribution to shareholders** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: A coherent and unusually disciplined policy was announced — promoters move to zero salary from FY27 and take reward only via dividends paid strictly out of operating cash flow — though no buybacks and no distributions have actually been executed yet in the window. | The long view had said: A coherent policy is being articulated — zero promoter salary from FY27 with dividends paid strictly out of operating cash flow — but there is no distribution track record and operating cash flow was still negative INR45 crores in FY26.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: The calls lean promotional — repeated upward guidance revisions, listing of awards, and quotable aphorisms — but management is not personality-driven and gives airtime to the CFO and sales head rather than itself. | The long view had said: Management is markedly promotional — extended award recitations, media interviews and repeated guidance headlines dominate call scripts rather than substance-only communication.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Switching costs are modest but not absent — co-creation workflows, an embedded design-bank app and custom cell create stickiness, yet large retailers routinely multi-source and no aftermarket captivity exists. | The long view had said: Switching costs are low in casted jewellery manufacturing and retailers deliberately multi-source, with the top five clients at 70% of revenue giving buyers, not Sky Gold, the leverage.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Mix is genuinely doing work — advanced gold rose 4%→7%, 18Kt 4%→7%, studded doubled, lifting gross margin 163bps year-on-year — but headline growth is still substantially volume plus gold-price inflation rather than pricing power. | The long view had said: Growth is explicitly mix-led — value-added products went from 4-5% to over 50% of revenue and gross margin expanded 245bps to 8.45%, with management withdrawing volume guidance in favour of value.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: Accessibility and customer intimacy are real strengths — a one-stop-shop range across 25+ categories, a dedicated CRM team, a digital ordering app already taking 7-8% of orders, and a new dealership network reaching smaller retailers. | The long view had said: Deep customer intimacy is built into the model — co-creation with retailers, one of India's biggest custom cells, a CRM team, an ordering app taking 7-8% of orders, and a strengthened sales force under a dedicated President of Sales.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Weak. Now: The company is positioned near a cyclical top, not a bottom — record gold prices, a Prime Ministerial directive to curb gold imports, and sector-wide demand softness are the stated current conditions. | The long view had said: The gold cycle is currently at a difficult point — prices up 60-70% and a policy push to curb imports causing sector demand softness — so the company is nearer a headwind than a clearly identified cyclical upturn.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Good → Neutral. Now: Technology is modern but not proprietary or deep — 3D design, laser cutting and stamping are available to any well-capitalised manufacturer; management itself ranks technology behind creativity as a differentiator. | The long view had said: There is a genuine process-technology edge — 3D printing, laser and hollow stamping, direct casting plus ERP digitisation cutting gold loss from 1.5% to 0.5% — but it is equipment-based and replicable by well-capitalised rivals.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: There is evidence of an internal pipeline and deliberate team building — the group finance controller was promoted to CFO, the merchandising team doubled to ~150, and senior sales and independent-director hires were made from the industry. | The long view had said: Systematic talent build-out is evident — merchandising/design team doubled to ~150, senior hires from Titan and industry (Akash, Thiru, Siddharth), ESOPs for 5+ year employees, and an internal promotion to CEO.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 0 weakened, 8 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Within this window promises made were visibly delivered — a global audit firm promised in Feb 2026 and MSKA/BDO appointed by May 2026, zero promoter salary from FY27 with dividend-only compensation from operating cash flows — alongside candid disclosure of the GML programme slipping and an explicit choice to cap growth at 30-35% to protect cash flow; leadership is a stable long-tenured promoter family. (The long view had said: Recent governance and delivery evidence is genuine — the Feb 2026 promise of a global auditor was kept by May 2026 (MSKA/BDO), promoters move to zero salary and dividend-only compensation from FY27, ESOPs for 5+ year employees, a professional CFO and an outside CEO, working capital cut from 71 to 59 days as promised — but the earlier years were guidance-heavy and cash-consumptive (negative INR272 crores OCF), and the promoter-family concentration and repeatedly revised targets mean the long-term track record is still short.)
- **Forward Integrators**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Movement toward the customer is real but modest for a B2B firm: own Dubai B2B sales office (Sky Souk), a Kerala office, a new dealership network to reach smaller retailers, and a direct ordering app already carrying 7-8% of orders — but there are no own stores, franchising or D2C revenue. (The long view had said: Management repeatedly and explicitly rejects forward integration into retail, staying pure B2B manufacturing; the 2023 US D2C venture (Vesteria) disappeared from the narrative, and later 'downstream' moves are a dealer/distributor network and a Dubai Souk sales office, i.e. wholesale reach, not owned stores or franchising.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The articulated barriers — a doubled ~150-person merchandising/design bank with high retailer strike rates, approved-vendor status across most large Indian chains, and scale capital in a working-capital-heavy trade — are real but rebuildable by a well-funded entrant over a few years; nothing time-, data- or location-based is cited that a copycat could not replicate. (The long view had said: Nothing described would stop a well-funded newcomer: the barriers cited are capital, machines, designers and client relationships, all rebuildable in a few years — as Sky Gold itself demonstrated by scaling roughly sevenfold since FY23 in an industry management calls fragmented and still organising, while conceding it remains 'such a tiny player' at 8% gross margins.)

*Unchanged: Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Low Price Plus (LIKELY FIT); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 1 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
