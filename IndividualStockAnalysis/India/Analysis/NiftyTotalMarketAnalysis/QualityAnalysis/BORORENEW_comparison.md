**Checks that improved:**

- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Weak → Good. Now: Management is now organically focused (brownfield expansion at the existing site, equity-funded rather than levered, no new M&A) and explicitly refuses to expand faster than it can staff, though the equity raises and the German misadventure temper the score. | The long view had said: Stewardship was poor through the cycle — a big overseas acquisition that had to be written off, debt that required a Rs 450 cr rights issue at the trough, and a shelved SG4 — though the current expansion is fully funded from internal accruals with no near-term equity raise.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Weak → Neutral. Now: The only technology evidence is incremental process efficiency and a quality reputation among Indian peers; no R&D intensity, patents or multi-product technical edge is described. | The long view had said: No technology differentiation is claimed anywhere in five years of calls — the stated expertise is simply 'glass production', and competitive positioning rests on duties, fuel flexibility and cost per unit.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: A 600 TPD greenfield-scale brownfield expansion (+60% capacity) is fully funded, on schedule for Q4 FY27, with demand already visible and management guiding to a corresponding EBITDA rise on 33-35% margins. | The long view had said: Sustained growth capex — SG3 (550 TPD, ~Rs 688 cr) commissioned 2022 and a fully-funded 600 TPD expansion due Q4 FY27 expected to lift sales 60% at 33% EBITDA — but the FY23 promise of SG4 1,100 TPD plus Rs 1,100 cr equity was shelved in the downturn and replaced by a rights issue to repay debt.
- **Candor** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Disclosure is unusually forthcoming — the German write-off was walked through step by step, the Q4 number was voluntarily flagged as inflated by INR32.07 crores of prior-quarter dispatches, and the PAT-vs-tax-shield confusion was patiently corrected rather than deflected. | The long view had said: Communication is consistently candid — flagging in FY23 that 50% margins were 'abnormally high' and unsustainable, disclosing losses and the full German write-off mechanics unprompted, and patiently correcting an analyst's misreading of the PAT tax shield.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: There is a quality-leader perception among Indian buyers, but solar glass is a specification-driven commodity with no aftermarket or services lock-in and 70% of consumption still imported, so no monopoly-in-the-mind exists. | The long view had said: There is no monopoly in the customer's mind — the product is priced off the landed Chinese import price with only a 3-5% premium, and imports still hold ~70% of consumption.
- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: After direct input costs, the company keeps ₹76 of every ₹100 of sales — pricing power customers are happy to pay for. And from the calls: There is demonstrable ability to pass through cost — customers accepted a fuel surcharge without margin loss — and margins are high, but the pricing power derives from import duties and scarcity rather than from brand image or taste. | The long view had said: After direct input costs, the company keeps ₹76 of every ₹100 of sales — pricing power customers are happy to pay for. And from the calls: The Borosil name carries genuine consumer recognition that management is now trying to monetise in rooftop, but in the core B2B glass business pricing is set by import parity and duties rather than image, and gross margin collapsed to 5.8% EBITDA when duties were absent.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Good. Now: The company has an established domestic customer network sufficient to pre-sell a 60% capacity increase and an underused Borosil-branded distribution channel it is now activating for rooftop, though this is relationship reach rather than an unmatchable service network. | The long view had said: Distribution is direct B2B to a handful of large module makers with no service network advantage; the Borosil group's consumer distribution is only now being borrowed for the nascent rooftop division.

Now: This is the opposite of an ignored niche — it is a policy-spotlighted sector with brokerage-hosted calls, heavy analyst attendance and capital flooding in to build competing capacity. | The long view had said: The opposite of obscure — solar is the most policy-favoured, capital-attracting sector in India, with ~33 GW of new solar glass capacity from multiple companies being commissioned by March 2027 and Reliance among the entrants.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: An analyst volunteered that 'Borosil offers the best quality among Indian players' and management is stretching the name into a new rooftop solutions division using existing brand and distribution — but there is no A&P spend, heritage story or premium-price-plus-share evidence in these four calls. (The long view had said: Management explicitly leans on the Borosil name to launch a rooftop-solutions division in FY26-27 (sourcing modules from third parties, sold under Borosil), which is a genuine adjacency stretch — but the core solar-glass business shows no brand premium beyond import parity and the rooftop revenue is tiny (Rs 1.3 crores in Q1 FY27 against a Rs 36 crore target).)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Realisations rose from INR113.44/mm (FY25) to INR146.7 (FY26) and INR160.30 in Q1FY27 with volumes up 8-15%, and customers accepted a fuel surcharge from March 2026 without margin loss — but management explicitly attributes the rise to antidumping/CVD duties on Chinese, Vietnamese and Malaysian imports, so this is policy-conferred rather than intrinsic pricing power. (The long view had said: Realisations are set by Chinese import parity plus a 3-5% premium, and the record includes severe deflation (Rs 128/mm in Q1FY24 to Rs 99.6 by Q4FY24); the FY26 recovery to Rs 150-160 came from government anti-dumping/basic customs duty, and management itself says the price is now capped.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Domestic volumes grew only 8% for FY26 and 8% in Q1FY27 while imports still hold ~70% of a 62GW-equivalent market; the 600 TPD expansion (+60% sales, commissioning Q4 FY27) is a bid for import-substitution share but the gain is prospective, and exports fell from 7.9% to 2.8% of turnover as EU/Turkey/US markets were lost. (The long view had said: Volume growth is real (capacity 450 to 1,000 TPD, going to 1,600 TPD by Mar 2027, with top 10 customers at 65-68% of volume), but imports still hold ~70% of Indian consumption and four rival domestic plants added 1,300 TPD in a single year — share gain is import substitution driven by duties, not competitive displacement.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (QUANT SIGNAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Cost to Replicate (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 2 worsened, 3 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Domestic solar glass capacity is set to rise from 2,600 tpd to ~7,700 tpd by March 2027, a structurally different set of entrants — mostly module makers building captive glass lines, i.e. customers integrating backward — and the chairman describes a surge in participants poaching skilled people, which is why he is holding back on further expansion announcements despite acknowledged demand. (The long view had said: Structurally different competitors dominate: Chinese (and China-owned Vietnamese/Malaysian/Indonesian) producers supply ~70% of Indian consumption at prices management itself calls unviable and subsidised, and domestic capacity is set to triple from 2,600 to 7,700 TPD by March 2027 with new entrants including Reliance. The company's answer has been duty protection rather than a competitive moat.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Solar glass is a near-commodity input where imports still hold ~70% of Indian consumption on price; the company's realisation premium exists behind anti-dumping and countervailing duties rather than product differentiation, and the same cheaper-substitute dynamic destroyed the European operations when customers demanded unremunerative prices from Interfloat. Competitors' new domestic capacity is largely captive, i.e. buyers producing their own 'good enough' glass in-house. (The long view had said: The product commands almost no brand premium: management admits it simply prices 3-5% above the Chinese landed cost, so the cheaper import is a genuine good-enough substitute and the entire realisation gain from Rs.105 to Rs.160/mm came from duties raising the substitute's price, not from differentiation. Module makers buying on cost control the choice, and glass is not a complex, niche-protected product — the Nobel Biocare pattern.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Technological Innovation (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 8 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
