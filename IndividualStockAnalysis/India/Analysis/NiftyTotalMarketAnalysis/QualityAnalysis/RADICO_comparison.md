## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 30 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 1 improved, 7 regressed, 22 unchanged. 4 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them).

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Allocation | Good (+0.67) | Neutral (+0.33) | -0.33 |
| Return on Capital | Good (+1.00) | Good (+1.33) | +0.33 |
| Growth | Good (+1.00) | Good (+0.67) | -0.33 |
| Management | Good (+1.17) | Good (+1.00) | -0.17 |
| Industry Structure | Good (+1.00) | Neutral (+0.33) | -0.67 |
| Customer Benefits | Good (+1.00) | Good (+1.00) | +0.00 |
| Competitive Advantage | Neutral (+0.00) | Neutral (+0.00) | +0.00 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Good. Now: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.21 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: Revenue crossed Rs 6,000 crores and EBITDA Rs 1,000 crores off an asset base needing only maintenance capex, with management citing better asset utilisation lifting ROE/ROCE, protected by brand and licensing barriers. | The long view had said: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.21 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: The business is structurally asset-heavy — captive distilleries, biomass power and multi-year malt maturation inventory — so despite a genuine brand moat, asset turn is only average and return ratios are improving off a low base rather than being intrinsically high.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Weak. Now: Management explicitly states capex is now mainly maintenance/efficiency rather than growth, with the growth story funded by existing capacity and deleveraging instead of fresh expansion spend. | The long view had said: Large greenfield growth capex (Rampur Dual-Feed, Sitapur) was funded and completed through FY23-FY25 and is now visibly earning — margins and return ratios inflected while capex reverted to maintenance-only, indicating decent incremental returns but no fresh growth-capex cycle ahead.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Weak. Now: There are no customer switching costs and the state governments — not the company — control route to market and pricing, as shown by policy changes in Andhra, Maharashtra and Karnataka swinging volumes materially. | The long view had said: State-licensed excise structures create quasi-regional strongholds (UP home state, Andhra, Telangana, Rajasthan) that are hard for outsiders to replicate, though consumer switching costs are essentially nil.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: Magic Moments occupies a near-category-defining position in Indian vodka at 8.6m cases and Rampur owns the Indian single malt prestige niche, giving mind-space monopolies in narrow categories. | The long view had said: Radico owns monopoly-like mindshare in defined niches — over 50% of India's luxury gin, the leading vodka franchise at 8.6m cases, and the highest UP country-liquor share.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Well-executed state-by-state rollout (After Dark to 18 states, Morpheus to 10, Kashmyr to 7) and early international validation via Rampur on Air India, but exports faced trade headwinds so international expansion is unproven. | The long view had said: Well-executed expansion on two axes — state-by-state rollout of luxury brands (10 to 20 states) and international premium placement (largest IMFL exporter, duty-free, Air India first/business class, Paris Olympics) — showing the brands travel.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Communication is competent but distinctly promotional in tone ('defining phase of acceleration', repeated 'highest-ever' framing) and leans on celebrity validation events, though no personal cult of the promoters is evident. | The long view had said: The same promoter-managers have run the business quietly for the whole period with brand-led rather than personality-led publicity; the Olympics and celebrity-tasting associations are marketing of products, not of executives.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: The company is benefiting from an already-favourable input cost cycle (benign ENA/grain prices contributed 225bps of gross margin expansion) rather than sitting near a cyclical bottom, with West Asia flagged as a possible reversal risk. | The long view had said: The company passed through the grain/ENA and glass inflation trough of FY24-FY25 (335bps gross-margin hit) into a benign input cycle now driving 450bps expansion, i.e. it is past the bottom with the upturn already visible but partly priced.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.1 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Licensing, distillery assets and state approvals keep new entrants scarce, and behaviour is rational — Radico is premium-pricing new launches rather than discounting, with a stable promoter ownership structure. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.1 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Licensing and distillery barriers keep new entrants out of the premium segment, and pricing behaviour is explicitly rational with management refusing to discount for volume; promoter ownership has been stable throughout.

*Unchanged: Profit margin; Distribution advantage; Disciplined stewards; Merger and acquisition; Candor; Convenience benefits; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Structural end-market growth; Cost of working capital; Advertisement and promotion; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Return on capital (headline); Technology advantage; Share donators; Intangible benefits; Gain in market share; Security by obscurity; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Research and development; Distribution to shareholders; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Halo effect (caution).*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 7 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Across FY26 P&A realisations rose 2.1% (Q2) and 2.8% (Q3) year-on-year while volumes grew 22-28%, and gross margin climbed from 43% in Q1 to 48% in Q4 with EBITDA margin up 565bps in Q4; premium launches were deliberately priced above competitors without losing traction, though part of the margin gain is admittedly benign ENA/grain costs. (The long view had said: Price increases are real but small and granted by state governments rather than taken at will — 3% realisation impact in Q1FY23, only 150-170bps guided for FY25, ~60bps of the FY27 margin bridge — and gross margin actually fell from 47.1% (Q1FY23) to 41.5% (Q1FY25) under grain inflation before recovering to 48% in Q4FY26 mainly on mix and softer inputs, so the lift is premiumisation-led more than pricing-led.)

**That weakened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Every call names new launches with measurable traction — Morpheus Super Premium, The Spirit of Kashmyr, Rampur 1943 Virasat, Jamun SpicyMint, 8PM Black repack — and management calls the approach 'innovation-led', but this is brand/flavour innovation with no disclosed R&D spend or share-of-industry-R&D evidence. (The long view had said: Named launches every single year with disclosed revenue impact — Trigun and Royal Ranthambore (FY23), Rampur Asava, Sangam World Malt and Jaisalmer Gold (FY25), Virasat and Spirit of Kashmyr (FY26) — funded by rising gross margin and 6-8% A&SP; the innovation is incremental and repeatable rather than a single bet, though no R&D spend is ever quantified.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Demand is a consumer flow product (repeat-purchase spirits, not customer capex), but the margin line is visibly levered to agri-commodity inputs: gross margin moved from 41-43% in FY26 H1 to 46.9% and then 48%, with management attributing 225bps of the Q3 expansion to raw material alone, and flagging West Asia supply-chain/input-cost risk in Q4. (The long view had said: Demand is a repeat-purchase consumption product rather than customer capex, but gross margin swung materially with agri/packaging commodity cycles across the 16 calls — 47.1% (Q1FY23) to 41.5% (Q1FY25) on grain inflation, then back to 48% (Q4FY26) on benign inputs — and the FY23-25 period carried a large Rampur Dual-Feed/Sitapur greenfield capex build that pushed net debt up before deleveraging.)
- **Fashion Risk**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Growth is credited to specific brands riding taste — After Dark Blue aimed at 'a younger, contemporary audience' (+60%, 3.1m cases), flavour launches driving Magic Moments, limited-edition packs and a Jim Murray tasting moment for Virasat — so a portion of the acceleration rests on trend-following consumer taste that can move. (The long view had said: An increasing share of value sits in taste-driven luxury and flavour-led launches whose demand rests on remaining fashionable — Jaisalmer, Rampur, Virasat, Kashmyr, D'YAVOL tequila, flavour extensions, celebrity and event tie-ins (Saregama music series, Paris Olympics India House) — and the record already shows a flagship going cold: 8PM Premium Black stalled and needed a packaging relaunch before recovering.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
