# NATCO Pharma Ltd. (NATCOPHARM) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: PHARMA*

Natco Pharma is an Indian drug maker that sells generic (copycat) versions of patented medicines, mostly complex ones like cancer drugs, plus the bulk chemical ingredients (APIs) that go into them. Its biggest money-spinner is exporting to the United States, where it typically partners with a larger company (Teva on generic Revlimid/lenalidomide, Mylan on generic semaglutide) and earns both a manufacturing margin and a share of the partner's profits; it also sells through its own subsidiaries in Canada, Brazil and the Middle East, and runs a smaller branded business inside India. On top of pharma it has a crop-protection (agrochemical) arm and a 35.75% stake in South Africa's Adcock Ingram. Management describes the model as lumpy and cyclical — it bets on patent challenges and first-to-file exclusivities, so a won 'jackpot' like Revlimid inflates earnings for a few years (FY25 revenue INR4,784 crores, profit INR1,883 crores) and then falls away (FY26 revenue INR4,376 crores, profit INR1,418 crores, with FY27 guided to roughly INR3,400-3,500 crores revenue and INR700-750 crores profit).

**The parts of the business:**

- **Formulation exports (including profit share and foreign subsidiaries)** — Finished generic medicines sold outside India — the dominant business by far, at about INR1,210 crores of the INR1,411 crores total revenue in Q1 FY25 — driven for several years by generic Revlimid in the US under a profit-sharing deal with Teva, and increasingly by the company's own subsidiaries in Canada, Brazil and the Middle East as Revlimid dropped to zero by late FY26.
- **US partnered/exclusivity products** — The high-stakes part of exports: first-to-file and exclusivity plays on hard-to-copy drugs — lenalidomide (Revlimid), glatiramer (Copaxone), pomalidomide, and semaglutide (generic Ozempic/Wegovy, filed with Mylan and made by Stelis/OneSource) — where management admits earnings swing hugely depending on whether an exclusivity is live, with nothing big expected in FY27 and more coming from FY28.
- **Domestic formulations (India pharma)** — Branded generics sold within India, described repeatedly as 'stable' at roughly INR100 crores a quarter and growing only 8-10%, in a market management calls extremely competitive where growth has to be bought through acquisitions; India launches of generic semaglutide are being added here.
- **API (active pharmaceutical ingredients)** — Sale of the bulk drug substances that go into finished medicines — the smallest pharma line, about INR39 crores in the Q1 FY25 quarter.
- **Crop Health Sciences (agrochemicals)** — A newer generic crop-protection business led by its own EVP, built around off-patent products (it fought FMC in court over a process patent before launching); it has stayed small and disappointing — about INR15 crores net in Q1 FY25 after discounts and returns provisions — and by FY26 the company had begun the process of demerging it.
- **Adcock Ingram Holdings (South Africa) associate stake** — A 35.75% shareholding acquired in late 2025 in a South African pharma company that itself earns roughly INR2,464 crores of revenue per half-year; Natco books its share of the profit as associate income, expected to add about INR35-40 crores of profit per quarter after amortisation.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

NATCO Pharma comes out of the three reviews with a combined score of 45 out of 100, a Mixed (2-star) rating built from 45 on business quality, 41 on multibagger fit and 48 on risk safety, with underlying resilience judged SOUND. The one clear strength is research: the company spends a sustained 7-9% of sales on complex generics and has consistently turned that into saleable assets — Revlimid, sole first-to-file status on semaglutide for weight loss, the first generic erdafitinib approval, and a pipeline of exclusivity filings mapped out to 2035. That is the single check scored positively alongside an 18% cash return on capital, and it is also what earns the only two positive pattern reads, Innovation Dominance and Market Share Gainers, both graded LIKELY FIT rather than a clean fit. The balance sheet supports it: no borrowings and roughly INR2,400 crores of cash, plus a management team the culture review credits with long tenure, habitually conservative guidance and unusually frank disclosure of bad news, including the Kothur warning letter and the agro provisions.

The problem is that the same engine driving the strength also produces the biggest risk, and the records say so directly. Because earnings come from time-limited exclusivities rather than repeating sales, Recurring Revenue is scored NO FIT — management itself calls the business cyclical and describes 'jackpots' — and Cyclicality is the only HIGH RISK channel, evidenced by a quarter where revenue rose 115% and profit quadrupled on Revlimid, then FY25 profit of INR1,883 crores falling to INR1,418 crores in FY26 and guided down to INR700-750 crores for FY27 with 'zero Revlimid' quarters. The Innovation Dominance verdict is qualified for exactly this reason: the R&D is regulatory and complex-generic work whose payoffs are lumpy, which also shows up in the quality checks as growth that averages about 16% a year but arrives unevenly. Market Share Gainers is qualified the same way — Revlimid share went from single digits to a third of the market purely under the Teva agreement and then to zero, and the added scale in South Africa was bought through a 35.75% stake in Adcock Ingram rather than won. That reliance on partners is scored separately as ELEVATED stakeholder concentration, since Teva controlled Revlimid pricing and share outright and the pattern repeats with Mylan on semaglutide, and it is why Forward Integrators only rates PARTIAL despite owned front-ends in Brazil, Canada and India.

Underneath both sits the industry itself, and here the strengths run out. End markets are fragmented commodity generics with a dozen or more entrants and buyers holding the negotiating power — the weakest possible score on industry structure — which is why Toll Roads, Pricing Power and Cost to Replicate are all NO FIT and margins swing by nearly 10 points. NATCO's response is deliberate price destruction: the India semaglutide vial priced at under INR1,000 against INR3,000-4,000 pens. That single move is simultaneously the best evidence for the Low Price Plus pattern, the reason pricing and mix score at the bottom of the quality framework, and part of why Good Enough Goods and New Entrants are both flagged as risks, with 10-15 generic semaglutide players expected in India and pomalidomide launched alongside 4-5 other exclusivity holders. Capital allocation carries the other weak scores: about 343 days of cash tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, roughly INR165 crores sunk into an agro facility that stalled, no owned capacity for GLP-1 with fill-finish outsourced, and INR2,400 crores sitting idle against falling revenue — while management hunts further 'very large' deals and the domestic acquisition promised since 2022 has not landed. The diversification those deals are meant to fund is also the named silver lining for both the cyclicality and concentration risks, so the acquisition strategy is at once the main criticism of capital allocation and the main proposed fix for the biggest risk. One further link matters: fortunes hinge on regulators and courts rather than customers, and the USFDA warning letter at Kothur froze new approvals and forced filings to be relocated — meaning the Government Dependency risk bites directly on the R&D pipeline that is the company's one real strength.

*"Out-inventing the competition"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and every one of the 2 computable checks supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** R&D is disclosed, guided (7-9% of sales in FY27) and deliberately sustained through the post-Revlimid earnings decline, with a named Para IV/FTF pipeline running 2026-2035 and NCE seed bets like eGenesis; but this is regulatory/complex-generic R&D producing lumpy one-offs, not the regular, profitable incremental innovation the pattern describes.

> "In terms of like R&D expenditure next year, I think we expect to spend about 7% to 9%… we're still continuing our strategy of doing complex generics" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ High retained margin (83% of sales) alongside 16% yearly growth — the profitable-innovation flywheel: margins fund the spending that drives the growth.

### Market Share Gainers — LIKELY FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and the only computable check supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** Share gains exist but are contractual or bought rather than persistently earned — Revlimid rose from single digits to one-third of the market purely under the Teva agreement and then went to zero, while incremental scale in South Africa came from acquiring 35% of Adcock; management is explicit that organic share gain is not available.

> "in a competitive generic environment like ours, it's very difficult to grow organically. You have to grow inorganic" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Sales compounding at 16% while margins held or improved (28% → 43%) — growth that was WON, not bought with price cuts.
