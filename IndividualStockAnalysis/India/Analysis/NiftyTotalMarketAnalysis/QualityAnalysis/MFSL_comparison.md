### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 6. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 4 strengthened, 1 weakened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Friendly Middleman**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Sales run overwhelmingly through intermediaries who recommend the product — banca partners (Axis Bank plus 51 new partners added in 9M FY26) and an agency force grown from ~61,000 in FY22 to ~1.42 lakh, with activation/productivity of new advisers cited as the growth driver rather than end-customer advertising. (The long view had said: Virtually all sales run through intermediaries — bank counter staff and agents — not end-customer pull; management manages 'counter share' at partner banks (Axis 70%, Yes 58%) and grew the adviser force from ~61,000 in FY22 to ~1.42 lakh, with adviser reward redesign, mSpace/Sales Navigator adoption and Sales Genie training tools as the channel lock-in.)
- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Deliberate shift toward owning the customer relationship: proprietary channels at 3-year 39% CAGR and 52% growth in Q3 FY26, online at 68% 3-year CAGR, 150 new branch units in three years, an in-house app with 4 lakh installs in 100 days, and a GIFT City office for the NRI segment. (The long view had said: A deliberate multi-year shift toward owning the customer relationship: proprietary channels grew 32% (Q1FY23), 28% (FY24) and 52% (Q3FY26), with a 3-year CAGR of 39% and online at 68% CAGR, 150 new branch units added, own e-commerce site plus a customer app (6 lakh downloads, 3 lakh MAU) — proprietary now ~46% of business versus a historically banca-dominated mix.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Explicit, repeated share numbers rising — private market share 10.1% (+83bps) in H1 FY26 and 9.8% (+53bps) in 9M FY26, rank 4 for FY25 and rank 3 in H2/Q4, highest retail protection share in H1 FY26 — and the share is not bought cheaply, with persistency at all-time highs and a 99.7% claims paid ratio. (The long view had said: Share gains are quantified and repeated across the whole period — +61 bps in FY24 (10.4% Q4 exit vs 8.6% in Q1), +83 bps to 10.1% in H1FY26, +53 bps in 9M FY26 — consistently growing ~2x private sector and 2-3x industry, and not obviously bought at the expense of quality given persistency and grievance metrics improved simultaneously.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Brand metrics improved measurably after the Max Life → Axis Max Life rebrand (consideration 78→83, NPS 52→58, rank 3 in brand search) and share is rising, but the calls show no premium pricing and the rebrand itself replaced rather than leveraged heritage. (The long view had said: Consistent-delivery trust markers recur across the years — #1 claims paid ratio three years running (99.34% FY22, 99.51% FY23), NPS rising 49→58, #1 13-month persistency, 'Best BFSI Brands' and 'Most Trusted Brands' recognition — but the heritage was deliberately re-anchored to the bank partner (Max Life → Axis Max Life, maxlifeinsurance.com → axismaxlife.com), and premium pricing is not demonstrated.)

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The last 12 months show the opposite of price increases: the full GST benefit was passed through to customers, FY25 margins fell 250bps to 24% on mix, and Q3 FY26 launched a savings plan with zero premium allocation charges — margin defence comes from product mix and riders, not price. (The long view had said: VNB margin rose from 19.7% (Q1FY23) to 26.5% (FY24) and guidance of 24-25% was held through the GST shock, but the lift comes from product mix and cost actions rather than price increases — management repeatedly passes benefits back to customers (interest-rate gains in non-par, and the full GST cut), which is closer to price-for-value than pricing power.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (STRONG FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 5 unchanged. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The Q3FY26 call notes the FDI limit was raised from 74% to 100% expressly to 'attract additional capital into the sector', and banca open architecture already lets rival insurers into partner branches — both lower the barrier for structurally new competitors, though no discounting or defensive margin investment is described. (The long view had said: Structurally different distributors are reshaping economics: web aggregators/brokers already take 60% of online business, open architecture let rivals onto Axis counters, and an analyst raised a bank chairman publicly targeting a shift from a 'high-cost' to a 'medium-cost' insurance industry — a direct challenge to the commission-led model that MFSL answers with distributor renegotiation.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The margin story shows the commoditisation pressure: mix drift into low-margin ULIP cut FY25 margins 250bps, riders and protection had to be layered on to defend them, and the intermediary — bank branch or agent — controls customer choice, with GST cost pressure met through 'distributor renegotiations' rather than pricing power. (The long view had said: Term and non-par savings are comparison-shopped commodities where the intermediary controls the choice: analysts asked whether non-par pricing 'is getting too aggressive', management admits passing rate benefits through to customer IRRs to keep the proposition competitive, and 60% of online sales run through aggregators where price is the visible axis; the GST episode forced explicit distributor commission renegotiation.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Axis Bank is simultaneously the dominant bancassurance counterparty, a shareholder and now part of the brand identity after the rebrand to Axis Max Life; management singles it out by name for January's 29% growth, and the whole banca book faces 'headwinds of open architecture' — a single relationship whose terms materially drive results. (The long view had said: Axis Bank has dominated the franchise throughout: 'majority of our sales actually comes from the bank', counter share disclosed at 70%, open-architecture erosion depressing Banca growth to 8% in Q1FY23, and analysts questioning the relationship on essentially every call; Axis then took a 19.02%/20% stake with two board nominees, and the partnership channel still carried 54% of business in H1FY26 while Axis itself grew only 7%.)

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Cyclicality (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
