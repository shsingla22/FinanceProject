**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Entry requires scarce port land allotments, cryogenic and jetty infrastructure and billion-dollar capex; no new entrants or price competition are mentioned across the year, and ownership is stable with Aegis Logistics, Royal Vopak and now ITOCHU committing capital. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±4.0 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Barriers are high — port land allotment, cryogenic construction skill, 18-24 month gas terminal build times and tightening environmental law — and pricing is rational with realisations rising rather than discounting, though management acknowledges growing domestic and global interest in the segment.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Port-level assets are effectively regional monopolies with hard switching costs — dedicated pipeline hookups (KGPL, JLPL), VLGC-enabled jetties, rail gantries and 15-year take-or-pay contracts physically tie customers to a specific terminal. | The long view had said: Port-level positions are effectively regional monopolies protected by scarce allotted land, fixed pipeline hookups and multi-modal gantries that make customer relocation costly once a terminal is plumbed into the grid.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: Purely corporate customer base and the benefit match is correct — reliability and total-cost evacuation efficiency sold via long-dated lock-ins (HPCL to 2038, 15-year Hindustan Zinc and conglomerate take-or-pay) rather than any retail-style intangible. | The long view had said: Customers are exclusively corporates and the offer is correctly matched — TCO savings, reliability and physical lock-in via pipeline hookups plus 15-year take-or-pay — though management deliberately prefers open-source spot terminalling over contractual lock-in.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Candor** (Management): Good → Weak. Now: The KGPL pipeline timeline slipped across all three calls (later FY26 → Q4 FY26 → H1/Q2 FY27) with no acknowledgement of the delay, and management routinely declines segment disclosure, once dismissing a volume question as 'not relevant'. | The long view had said: Candid on unflattering detail — openly guided that Q3 would stay subdued, explained the ~20% parent construction margin flowing through OCI, and flagged the Hormuz volume hit — but repeatedly declines routine disclosures such as segment EBITDA and contract mix.
- **Network effects** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Weak. Now: What management calls a 'network' is a physical multi-port footprint, not a self-reinforcing two-sided network — additional customers do not make the platform more valuable to other customers. | The long view had said: No two-sided or self-reinforcing network exists — the pan-India multi-port footprint is a scale and reach advantage, not a network effect that compounds with each additional user.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Calls are factual and operationally focused with no personality cult, but a freshly listed company repeatedly headlining a $5bn capex roadmap and 'most comprehensive energy infrastructure platform' is not low-profile. | The long view had said: Management consistently refuses to give throughput or profit guidance and answers in operational rather than promotional terms, though a recent high-profile IPO with wide sell-side coverage limits how far out of the limelight they are.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 6 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 3 weakened, 4 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management asserts leadership ('India's largest independent owner and operator... based on storage capacity') and has grown liquid capacity 3.75x and LPG static capacity 4.5x since the Nov-2021 JV, adding Mangalore, Pipavav and — via the 75% HALPG acquisition — an East Coast entry. But no explicit market-share percentages or share-versus-named-competitor numbers are given in these calls, so the gain is inferred from capacity, not disclosed. (The long view had said: Explicit and repeated share-gain trajectory: India's largest third-party LPG/liquid tank storage operator, liquid capacity up 3.75x and LPG 4.5x since the Nov-2021 JV, six ports going to seven then a stated 12 by 2030, plus three bolt-on acquisitions and the HALPG buy opening the East Coast — and gains are made with rising margins and net profit up 52% in FY26, not bought with profitability.)
- **Brand Strength**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The moat framing is B2B reputation, not consumer brand: Royal Vopak's 400-year global operating record plus Aegis Logistics' domestic relationships are repeatedly cited as delivering safety and reliability standards, and that credential has attracted partners (ITOCHU, L&T, HPCL). But there is no A&P spend, no premium pricing evidence and no brand stretch into adjacencies. (The long view had said: The 'brand' is B2B credibility from Royal Vopak's 400-year heritage and AAA ESG/safety standards plus Aegis Logistics' domestic relationships, which management says wins business from multinationals — but there is no A&P spend, consumer franchise, premium pricing or brand-stretch evidence.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Growth in the last four quarters is explicitly attributed to capacity additions, volumes and product mix — never to price increases — and FY26 gas terminalling revenue rose only 8.6% while gas throughput rose ~18% (3.3mt to 3.9mt), implying realisation per tonne fell; management also flagged gas EBITDA per tonne at 'just a shade under 1100' as new cryogenic tanks ramp. (The long view had said: Liquid realisation rose from ~INR2,250 to INR2,500 and toward a INR3,000/CBM benchmark, but management attributes this to product mix and new terminals rather than price increases; on the gas side realisation actually fell from ~INR1,500 (FY25) to ~INR1,175-1,200 as new terminals came on, so no demonstrated ability to price above cost inflation.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (LIKELY FIT); Friendly Middleman (QUANT SIGNAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Toll Roads (STRONG FIT); Cost to Replicate (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL).*

*Not comparable: Innovation Dominance (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 6 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **New Entrants**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No competitor, discounting or margin-defence discussion appears in any of the three calls; instead management points to structural entry barriers — scarce allotted port land, cryogenic capital intensity and the Aegis–Royal Vopak parentage — while continuing to take price-insensitive long-term contracts. (The long view had said: An analyst directly flagged 'increased interest in chemical gas storage from both domestic as well as global players' and asked what the differentiator is, and management named competitors hooked into the same Kandla pipelines (IOC, Adani Mundra); entry is real but bounded by scarce allotted port land, 18-24 month cryogenic build times and capital intensity.)

Why now: No discussion across the three calls of defending tariffs against cheaper substitutes or of customers trading down; the service is protected by scale and specialised assets (cryogenic LPG tanks, VLGC-capable jetties, pipeline/rail/road connectivity) that a low-cost challenger cannot assemble on already-allotted scarce port land, and customers are large sophisticated counterparties contracting directly. (The long view had said: Terminaling is largely a tariff service where sophisticated buyers (oil marketing companies, traders) can switch on price — gas realisations already drifted to ~INR1,200/ton and management was pressed on why realisations fell — and the defence offered is essentially service quality (Vopak safety/ESG standards, cheapest in-house construction) rather than a structurally protected premium.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
