# Shree Cement Ltd. (SHREECEM) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CEMENT & CEMENT PRODUCTS*

Shree Cement Limited makes and sells grey cement — the powder mixed with sand, stone and water to make concrete — plus clinker, the semi-finished input that goes into cement. Its buyers are individual house-builders and small contractors reached through dealers (the "trade" channel, about 65%-80% of sales depending on the year) under the Bangur brand, along with bulk/institutional buyers such as infrastructure and real-estate projects; management ties demand to government spending on roads, railways and housing schemes. It earns money by selling tonnes of cement at a realisation of roughly INR 4,500-4,750 per tonne and keeping cost per tonne low, mainly through cheap fuel buying (pet coke and coal), waste-derived alternative fuels, and self-generated power — it runs about 1,000 MW of its own solar, wind, thermal and waste-heat plants, with green power at about 61% of electricity used. It operates plants across India (Rajasthan, West Bengal, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Chhattisgarh, Uttar Pradesh, Jharkhand, Uttarakhand, Haryana and more) with installed India capacity of 69.3 million tonnes as of March 2026, calling itself India's third-largest cement group, plus a cement business in the UAE and a new blending/packing venture in Mauritius.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Cement (and clinker) – India** — The core business: manufacturing and selling grey cement and clinker across Indian states, about 36.4 million tonnes sold in FY26 with roughly INR 4,222 crores of operating EBITDA, so it is effectively the whole company.
- **Power / captive and green energy** — Own power generation — solar, wind, thermal and waste-heat recovery totalling over 1,000 MW installed (about 666.5 MW of it green) — used mainly to run the cement plants and cut costs, with a small amount of power revenue that the company does not separately disclose.
- **Ready-Mix Concrete (RMC) – "Bangur Concrete"** — A recently started business (about 2-3 years old) selling factory-mixed concrete from city plants; still tiny at INR 71 crores of revenue in the December 2025 quarter from 19 plants, but growing fast to 26-36 plants by FY27 with a target of about 45 plants, and it consumes the company's own cement (45% captive) while extending geographic reach.
- **Union Cement (UAE) – overseas operations** — A separately-run cement company in the UAE that sold 45.65 lakh tonnes in FY26 with revenue of AED 870 million (up 39%), is adding a 2.5 million tonne cement mill by September 2026, and was hit short-term by Middle East conflict.
- **Capacity expansion projects** — A continuous pipeline of new integrated plants and grinding units — Purulia, Nawalgarh, Guntur, Jaitaran, Kodla, Baloda Bazar, Etah and a planned Meghalaya plant — driving the stated goal of 80 million tonnes of capacity, with capex such as roughly INR 500 crores for RMC in FY26.
- **Mauritius subsidiary** — A newly incorporated wholly owned subsidiary set up to establish cement blending, storage and packing facilities in Mauritius; no size given, described only as a new expansion step.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Shree Cement comes out of this work as a genuinely well-run, financially conservative business inside a hard industry, which is why the combined rating lands at 55 out of 100, or Decent — 56 for business quality, 64 for how well it fits the multibagger patterns, and only 42 for risk safety, with overall resilience judged Strained. The strongest and most consistent thread is cost and balance-sheet discipline: the lowest fuel cost in the industry at INR1.56 per kcal against a peer group at INR1.80, 61% green electricity, 1 GW of captive power, no borrowing for fifteen years, capex paid for out of the company's own cash, and about INR6,400 crores of net cash. That single trait does double duty. It is what earns the Low Price Plus pattern a Likely Fit and helps carry the Corporate Culture pattern to the only Strong Fit in the screen — management sets a target on one call and is measured against it on the next, having promised 2-3% volume growth and delivered 2.2%. The same trait is also the named silver lining under two of the elevated risks, Cyclicality and New Entrants: being the cheapest producer with no debt is how a company survives a downturn it cannot control. Growth and Management both score 0.83 on the quality areas, the two highest marks in the framework, and sales have compounded at roughly 12% a year, right inside the 10-15% band the framework prizes, albeit lumpily.

The awkward part of the story is that the very same set of facts that produces the strengths also produces the risks, and in one case the strengths and the risks are two readings of one management decision. Shree's cement sells at a discount to UltraTech — INR30 a bag, now halved to INR15 — which is exactly the Good Enough Goods risk: a product buyers do not strongly distinguish, where the dealer or mason often picks. Management's answer was to deliberately stop chasing volume from October 2024 and push price instead. That worked on its own terms: FY26 realization rose 3.6% to INR4,732 and profit per tonne reached INR1,161 on only 2.2% volume growth, earning a positive mark on the Pricing, Mix and Volume check, and the heavier advertising behind the new brand identity, the premium Bangur MAGNA product and the 'Solid Ghar Sirf Bangur' campaign earns a positive mark too and lifts Brand Strength to a Likely Fit. But the identical decision is why the Market Share Gainers pattern was cut to Partial — the company had been taking share convincingly through FY23-FY25, with 8% volume growth in one quarter while three named rivals shrank, and then handed that back on purpose. It is also why Pricing Power is scored a flat No Fit: realization actually fell from INR4,854 to INR4,469 over the period, management says plainly that price is set by demand and will not forecast it, and the company had to give up two quarters of volume simply to narrow a discount. Trading volume for price is a reasonable choice, but the record reads it as a company managing a weak position rather than exercising a strong one, and the Competitive Advantage and Capital Allocation areas both score zero for that reason — capacity is added freely by rivals, and capex was trimmed going into the downturn rather than pressed while assets were cheap.

Where the money comes from is the third connection worth naming. Indian cement demand grows at roughly 1 to 1.1 times GDP off a long infrastructure and housing build-out, which earns a positive mark for structural end-market growth, and the company is positioned after a trough of weak pricing and mid-50s utilisation with demand guided at 7-8%, earning another for an identifiable upturn ahead. But that same government-driven demand is the highest-rated risk in the review: management ties quarterly demand to the Union Budget, tender flow and state housing and road schemes, and the plants themselves depend on state-granted limestone leases and approvals — the good news and the dependency are the same pipeline, which is why Industry Structure is the only negative quality area at -0.33. The partial offsets are real but small: overseas assets in the UAE, where volumes rose 18% and revenue 39% since Shree took ownership, a new Mauritius subsidiary, and a ready-mix concrete business built from nothing to 19 plants with 36 entering FY27 and a 50-55 target. That last one is the Forward Integrators pattern, held at Partial because revenue is still only about INR71 crores a quarter, and it doubles as the company's answer to the channel shift showing in its own numbers, with trade sales falling from 78-80% to 65%. Set against all this is the framework's worst single mark: roughly 227 days of cash tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, scored -2. A company sitting on INR6,400 crores of net cash can carry that, but it means a genuinely good 15% cash return on capital is being earned with a lot of money parked in the working cycle. The overall picture is a high-quality operator with a fortress balance sheet, real cost advantage and honest management, competing in a commodity market where it does not set the price and cannot control the demand.

The rating hinges on whether the price-led strategy proves to be a durable repositioning or a temporary trade of growth for margin, and the four elevated or high risks — Government Dependency, Cyclicality, New Entrants and Good Enough Goods — all point at the same place: what happens to volume and price together when the guided upturn actually arrives.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, Cyclicality, New Entrants, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.1× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Management explicitly ties quarterly demand to the Union Budget cycle, tender flow and government housing/road schemes, and the business rests on state-granted limestone leases and 'statutory approvals' for new sites; in Aug'24 they attributed weak Q2/Q3 demand purely to the budget not yet having received Presidential assent, and in Feb'26 credited the Q4 pickup to central agencies having to exhaust the budget by 31st March.

> "The central budget has still not been passed... So demand in this quarter is going to remain weak." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Some diversification away from Indian policy-driven demand via Union Cement (UAE) and the new Mauritius subsidiary, plus a growing RMC/private-construction channel*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Cement is a classic capacity/price cycle business and the calls show it repeatedly: EBITDA/ton swung from INR1,260 (Dec'21) to INR881 (Dec'22) to INR950 (Q1FY25) to INR1,125 (Q4FY26), realizations fell 6% Y-o-Y in Q1FY25, utilisation ranged from 56% to 76%, and management admits costs are hostage to international pet coke/coal prices while ~INR2,900-3,500 crores/year of capex was committed into a market whose demand later disappointed (80mt by FY29 now openly at risk of deferral).

> "Pricing is a function of demand. If the demand does not revive, the pricing power will not be there with the industry." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Countercyclical strength: zero net debt (INR6,400 crores net cash), 15 years without borrowing, capex funded from internal accruals, lowest fuel cost in industry (INR1.56/kcal) and 61% green power; demand is operations/construction flow rather than a single customer's capex programme*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 2 of the last 10 years with swings of ±19 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 17% and 30% (±4.2 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.

## What to watch

- Whether the remaining INR15/bag discount to UltraTech keeps narrowing — the Brand Strength pattern and the Good Enough Goods risk both rest on it.
