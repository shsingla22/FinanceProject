# Shriram Finance (SHRIRAMFIN) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Shriram Finance is an Indian non-bank lender (formerly Shriram Transport Finance, which merged with group company Shriram City Union Finance in 2022 to form the current company) that lends money to buyers of vehicles and to small businesses, mainly through a large branch network across India. Its core business, which management says it has been in for nearly 50 years, is financing used commercial vehicles — trucks and other goods vehicles — for operators who typically cannot get bank loans easily, with new-vehicle loans plus rural and MSME (small business) lending added on. It makes money on the spread between the interest it charges borrowers and what it pays to raise funds — it borrows from banks, bond markets, foreign-currency bonds and overseas lenders, and sells down loans through securitisation and assignment; in Q1 FY27 it earned net interest income of INR8,056 crores at a net interest margin of 9.04%, versus a cost of liabilities of about 8.56%. It operates only in India, has grown assets under management from about INR1.31 lakh crore in mid-2022 to INR3.14 lakh crore by June 2026, and in April 2026 sold a 20% stake to Japan's MUFG Bank for about INR39,618 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Used vehicle (commercial vehicle) financing** — Loans against second-hand trucks and commercial vehicles — the dominant business: in Q1 FY23 used-vehicle disbursements were INR15,755 crores out of INR16,670 crores of total disbursement, i.e. almost the entire loan book at the time.
- **New vehicle financing** — Loans for brand-new vehicles, historically small (INR785 crores in Q1 FY23) but deliberately being grown, mostly by financing existing customers who are upgrading from an older vehicle.
- **Rural and MSME lending** — Lending to small businesses and rural borrowers, added through the merger with Shriram City Union Finance; management repeatedly ties it to farm output, monsoon and government spending on agriculture and jobs, and expects 'maximum traction' there, though the excerpts give no separate size.
- **Shriram Housing Finance (subsidiary, since divested)** — A home-loan subsidiary with AUM of INR14,397 crores in June 2024 (up 51% year-on-year) and quarterly profit of about INR48 crores; the board approved selling the entire stake to a Warburg Pincus affiliate in May 2024, after which it was treated as a discontinued operation.
- **Borrowing and treasury (liabilities)** — The funding side that determines profitability: bank loans, bonds, dollar bonds, a USD 250 million line from the US Development Finance Corporation, and securitisation/assignment deals — total liabilities were about INR2.33 lakh crore in June 2026, with liquidity kept for roughly six months of repayments.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** The book is anchored to commercial-vehicle, tractor and construction-equipment demand, and management opens every call with OEM unit cycles, monsoon and fuel/oil price commentary — CE sales fell 16% in Q4 FY26, tractors swing with rainfall, and FY27 guidance was hedged on a 90%-of-normal monsoon and West Asia oil risk. Bounded because the core asset is used-vehicle lending against operators' running cash flows on fixed-rate contracted EMIs rather than new-capex financing, and AUM/NIM have compounded steadily (8.1–9.0% NIM) through the 2022–2026 span.

> "the peak sale on commercial vehicle was 1 million units being sold and from 1 million, it came down to 5,50,000 during the 2021" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/operations-linked used-vehicle finance with fixed contracted EMIs ("asset side is completely fixed"), fuel-cost pass-through by operators to shippers, and diversification into MSME, gold, two-wheeler and personal loans*
