### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 0 weakened, 8 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Two decades of regional reputation and 8.7 lakh patients served in FY26 give a genuine trust story ('the Park brand'), and NABH/NABL accreditation across 15 hospitals and 8 labs is the consistency proof — but there is no A&P spend, no premium pricing (the brand is explicitly positioned on affordability) and no adjacency stretch discussed. (The long view had said: There is genuine regional standing — largest private chain in Haryana and the Tri-City, second-largest in North India, 8.7 lakh patients served, brand cited as a magnet for hiring clinicians — but no A&P spend, no heritage/provenance story, and by design no premium pricing, which is half of what brand strength should show up as.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Share gain is visible in both position and flow: largest private chain in Haryana and the Tri-City and second-largest in North India, with capacity +20% in one year (3,000 to 3,610 beds) yet occupancy still rising 244bps to 64.1% and footfall up 24%, plus entry into UP via Agra and an IBC acquisition in Narela — and it is not bought with balance-sheet or credit laxity, since term debt fell from INR450 crore to INR28 crore and debtor days improved. (The long view had said: Capacity grew 20% in FY26 (3,000 to 3,610 beds, 16 hospitals) with IPD +18%, OPD +22% and occupancy up 244bps, and the share gain is not bought — margins expanded (Q4 EBITDA 28%, +268bps) and debtor days fell 161 to 129; but no explicit market-share percentages versus named competitors, and only two calls of listed record to judge persistence.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (LIKELY FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: There is no single named customer, but payor concentration is real: ~80% of revenue sits with government schemes whose rates and claim settlement move together, producing a ~INR200 crore receivables provision and 8-9% disallowed claims. The bloc is at least split across CGHS, ECHS, railways, paramilitary, state governments and electricity boards, and the patient base is broad (8.7 lakh patients in FY26), which bounds the exposure. (The long view had said: A single payer relationship — the central government scheme apparatus — drives the large majority of revenue, its rate list is the main pricing lever discussed on both calls, and analysts repeatedly probed the receivable cycle and disallowed claims arising from that one counterparty (8-9% of claims disallowed, INR200 crores of provisions). Patient and doctor bases are broad, but payer concentration is the binding stakeholder risk.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: The company occupies the good-enough price point itself rather than defending a premium — it deliberately caps ARPOB inside the affordable band and builds ~INR34-35 lakh per-bed capex 250-350 bed units for quick break-even — while simultaneously moving mix toward hard-to-replicate high-acuity work (56.9% of revenue from high-end specialties, robotic transplants, PTCA), which is not easily undercut by a cheaper substitute. (The long view had said: Park is deliberately the low-price proposition (ARPOB ~INR28,000, capex/bed ~INR34-35 lakhs, management refusing to push ARPOB up), so it has no premium to defend — but its price is set by a third-party tariff rather than brand, and an analyst pressed on cost inflation of ~8% against ARPOB growth of 5-7%, which is the classic squeeze on a commodity-priced service.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Cyclicality (LOW); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 2 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 2 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
