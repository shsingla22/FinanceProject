**Checks that improved:**

Now: VBL is a heavily covered large cap — calls are led by Jefferies and Nuvama analysts — and its category has attracted exactly the kind of new capital this test wants absent. | The long view had said: Far from under-the-radar — the calls are attended by Jefferies, CLSA, Kotak, JP Morgan, IIFL, Nuvama and Emkay, and the category is attracting large new capital from Reliance.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Africa offers clear donators — South Africa growth came from taking general-trade reach, and Twizza plus Crickley Dairy were bought outright — but in India the weak-competitor dynamic is running the other way. | The long view had said: Weak franchisees and low-share incumbents were a real donor pool historically (territories acquired from PepsiCo, sub-2% PepsiCo share in South Africa), but in India the current narrative is market expansion against strengthening rivals rather than taking share from weak players.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Good → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹23 is operating profit. **Checks that regressed:**

- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: The former Coke/Pepsi duopoly is being broken open — analysts probe competition every quarter and management concedes discounting was industry-wide in CY2025, so the structure is destabilising rather than a stable oligopoly. | The long view had said: Indian CSD is a concentrated few-player market but stability has weakened with Reliance's Campa entering and industry-wide discounting, and the end customer (retail trade) can switch.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Real runway remains (low per-capita consumption, under-penetrated semi-urban/rural) and Q1 CY2026 India volumes grew 14.4% with international +21.4%, but the share gain is being bought with pack upsizing and selective lower price points rather than won outright. | The long view had said: Long runway from a low base — underpenetrated Indian territories acquired from PepsiCo and 1.8% PepsiCo share in South Africa — with a demonstrated capture record (Zimbabwe) and 300,000-500,000 new outlets added annually.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: The customer base is retail, where intangible/brand pull should dominate, yet the levers actually pulled this year were price points, pack upsizing and distribution reach rather than image-led pricing power. | The long view had said: The retail-consumer benefit set is correctly matched on availability and price points, and the retailer relationship is locked in through company-owned coolers, but the intangible/brand half of the retail equation is owned by the franchisor.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Weak. Now: Nothing suggests customers pay for certainty; the recent evidence points the other way, with the category being fought over on a Rs 10 affordability price point and consumers onboarded via cheaper entry packs. | The long view had said: There is some reliability-based value for retailers — six months of international inventory cover and no stock-outs in peak season — but the consumer buys on impulse and price, not on assurance, and water is explicitly a commodity.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Poor. Now: Growth is almost entirely volume-led with negative domestic pricing — CY2025 net realization per case rose just 0.5%, India realization fell 1.5% in Q1 CY2026 on pack upsizing and lower price points, and the only realization uplift came from African currency movement. | The long view had said: Growth is overwhelmingly volume-led with negative pricing/mix in India — realisation fell 4% in Q4 CY2025 and 1.5% in Q1 CY2026 on pack upsizing and Rs. 10 price points — and full-year CY2025 realisation rose just 0.5%.

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 3 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: VBL is structurally a franchise bottler — essentially the whole beverage portfolio and now the snacks extension (Cheetos in Morocco, Zimbabwe) exist under PepsiCo licence, so one licensor relationship drives results across every territory discussed in all four calls. (The long view had said: VBL is structurally a single-licensor business: essentially the entire portfolio (Pepsi, Sting, Tropicana, Gatorade, Cheetos) and every territory right comes from PepsiCo, which grants franchise territories, awards bottler recognition, and must approve operational decisions — the Safilo-style licensor dependence, repeated in every call from 2022 to 2026.)
- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: A structurally different low-price entrant has reset the India price ladder at Rs. 10, and management has moved from 'we are prepared if necessary' in Oct 2025 to actually launching selective price points and upsizing packs by Q1 CY2026 — India realisation per case fell 1.5% and the Q4 revenue-volume gap of ~4% was explicitly competition-driven. (The long view had said: Reliance's Campa Cola is a structurally different, deep-pocketed entrant attacking on price; from May 2024 onward management is repeatedly asked about it, and by CY2025-26 the response is visible in the P&L — India realization per case fell ~4% in Q4 CY2025 and 1.5% in Q1 CY2026 through pack upsizing and selective Rs.10 launches, with industry-wide discounting after capacity additions.)
- **Government Dependency**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Pricing and cost economics are materially touched by policy — the GST 2.0 rate cut affected roughly a quarter of the India portfolio and the benefit was passed through in full, the new labour codes added ~Rs. 14 crore of cost, and expansion steps in South Africa (land parcel, Twizza, Crickley Dairy) hinge on regulatory clearance — but no revenue is set by a regulator or dependent on subsidies/PSU tenders. (The long view had said: Revenue is consumer-driven with no regulator-set pricing, tenders, subsidies or fixed-infrastructure dependence anywhere in the calls; the only policy item raised in four years is the FY2025 labour-code cost of ~Rs. 14 crore, absorbed within operating performance.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Technological Innovation (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
