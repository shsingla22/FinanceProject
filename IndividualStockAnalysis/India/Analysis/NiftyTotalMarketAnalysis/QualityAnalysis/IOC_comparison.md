**Checks that improved:**

- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Poor → Weak. Now: Growth is volume- and macro-led, not price-led — record throughput and sales volumes with earnings swinging on crack spreads, inventory gains/losses and a Rs.14,486 crore government compensation for LPG under-recoveries rather than on pricing power. | The long view had said: Growth is purely volume- and crack-spread-driven with no pricing power: LPG was sold at a Rs.100/cylinder loss requiring a Rs.14,486cr government bailout, and management declined to commit to market-linked daily pricing even after crude spiked 30%.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: IOC holds an infrastructure position rivals cannot replicate — over half the nation's pipeline network and 42,818 retail outlets — giving quasi-monopoly supply status in many geographies, though the product itself is an administered-price commodity. | The long view had said: A few genuine niche monopolies exist (sole SAF-from-UCO certification, only hydrogen dispensing company, first CBG brand) but the core fuel business holds no monopoly position in the customer's mind, as motorists switch pumps freely.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Project Sprint explicitly names leadership-pipeline development as one of its six strategic pillars, indicating a systematic intent, though no metrics or outcomes are given. | The long view had said: Leadership development is named as one of six Project Sprint pillars, but the five-year history shows six Managing Directors and three Chairmen rotating through FY21-FY22 under government appointment, which is the opposite of a self-built internal pipeline.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: The cyclical trough is already behind — normalized GRM rose $4.53 (FY25) → $5.39 → $6.91 → $8.91 and PAT went Rs.12,962 cr to Rs.36,802 cr — so the company sits mid-to-late cycle with crude now spiked ~30% to $83/bbl on the US-Iran conflict, not near a bottom. | The long view had said: Rather than sitting near a cycle bottom, IOC has just printed a peak year (PAT Rs.36,802cr vs Rs.12,962cr, normalized GRM $6.60→$8.91) into a $83/bbl crude spike, 11% rupee depreciation and Hormuz disruption that management concedes will hit Q1 FY27.
- **Network effects** (Competitive Advantage): Poor → Weak. Now: Nothing in the business model is self-reinforcing in a two-sided sense — scale advantages come from physical assets and reach, not from users making the service more valuable to other users. | The long view had said: Nothing in the business is self-reinforcing with scale — growth comes from physically building outlets, pipelines and refineries, each requiring incremental capital, with no two-sided dynamic anywhere in the five calls.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Credible but early international extension: first-ever LNG export by road to Nepal with cryogenic infrastructure at Simara, and an oil discovery in Abu Dhabi Block 1 via the 50:50 Urja Bharat JV; it is infrastructure reach, not a premium brand travelling. | The long view had said: Geographic moves are token relative to the base — first LNG-by-road export to Nepal and a 50:50 Abu Dhabi exploration JV — with essentially all growth capex directed at domestic refining, pipelines and retail.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Growth is organic with no big M&A and S&P assigned a BBB rating citing disciplined capital management, but a very heavy multi-year capex programme funded through a year of depressed earnings and dependence on government compensation tempers the stewardship case. | The long view had said: Leverage was cut from 0.77 to 0.54 gross (0.32 net) with borrowings down Rs.23,798cr while capex was held through the FY25 earnings collapse, and Project Sprint delivered a quantified Rs.2,200cr saving with no large M&A.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: The finance team presents in a dry, functional, non-promotional style, but the company itself is a highly visible national champion whose plants are inaugurated by the Prime Minister. | The long view had said: The calls are run by finance executives delivering operational detail without promotional framing, and even during a record year management avoided forward-looking claims, though the PSU setting brings PM inaugurations and nationalistic sign-offs.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 0 weakened, 8 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: FY26 domestic sales volume grew ~4.8% against industry petroleum consumption growth of 2%, explicitly attributed to market share gain, alongside lube share gains of 9% among PSUs and 5% versus industry in H1 FY26 — and the share is being won on network build, not on discounting or credit. (The long view had said: Share gain is explicitly claimed and repeated: FY26 domestic volume growth of ~4.8% against industry consumption growth of 2% attributed to market share, lube share up 9% among PSUs and 5% among industry in H1 FY26, plus record highway outlet additions 'leading to positive market share'. It is real but modest, within a three-PSU administered oligopoly rather than against genuine competition, and share numbers are given as growth rates rather than absolute levels tracked over time.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: The physical asset base is genuinely un-replicable in any reasonable timeframe: over 20,000 km of cross-country pipelines representing more than half the national network, 42,818 retail outlets on finite sites, and ~98 MMTPA of refining — a well-funded newcomer could not rebuild this in years, though the returns from it remain policy-capped rather than shareholder-captured. (The long view had said: The physical barrier is genuinely un-replicable — over 18,500 km of pipeline representing more than half of India's entire network, 42,818 retail sites on finite locations, and Rs 72,000 crore of refinery expansion — no well-funded newcomer rebuilds that in a few years. But the test is whether the barrier produces durable excess returns, and here it does not: earnings are hostage to crude, crack spreads and administered LPG prices, and the calls themselves record the market refusing to capitalise the asset base (Rs 2 lakh crore market cap on Rs 5.5 lakh crore of assets).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 8 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
