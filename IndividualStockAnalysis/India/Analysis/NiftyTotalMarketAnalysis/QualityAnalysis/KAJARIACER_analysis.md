# Kajaria Ceramics Ltd. (KAJARIACER) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSTRUCTION*

Kajaria Ceramics makes and sells ceramic and vitrified tiles in India — floor and wall tiles in sizes from small ceramic tiles up to large glazed vitrified tiles and four-foot-by-eight-foot slabs — under its own brand. It earns money by manufacturing tiles at its own plants (North at Gailpur, South at Srikalahasti and near Hyderabad, and units in Morbi, Gujarat, plus joint ventures there and a new plant in Nepal), topping that up with tiles bought in from outside makers, and selling almost entirely through a network of dealers who display the product in their showrooms; management repeatedly stresses that 'what is visible is sold.' Demand comes from Indian home building and renovation, real-estate projects and government housing/infrastructure schemes, with particularly good traction in tier-II and smaller cities rather than the metros. Alongside tiles it sells bathroom fittings, tile adhesives and, until it was shut in FY26, plywood; revenue runs at roughly Rs 1,100–1,400 crore a quarter, and natural gas/fuel cost is the single biggest swing factor on its margins.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Tiles** — The core business — ceramic floor and wall tiles, glazed vitrified tiles (GVT) and large slabs, made at company plants and joint ventures plus outsourced supply, accounting for the overwhelming majority of revenue (about Rs 1,212 crore of Rs 1,373 crore consolidated revenue in Q4 FY26) and volumes of roughly 23–27 million square metres per quarter.
- **Bathware** — Bathroom products (sanitaryware and fittings) sold under the same brand and dealer network — a small but steadily growing side business, moving from about Rs 71 crore a quarter in FY23 to Rs 117 crore in Q4 FY26.
- **Adhesives** — Tile adhesives and related fixing materials, run as its own division under a dedicated head — the fastest-growing part off a tiny base, from about Rs 15 crore in Q1 FY25 to Rs 44 crore in Q4 FY26.
- **Plywood** — A small plywood business that grew from roughly Rs 5 crore to Rs 20 crore a quarter in FY23 and then stagnated around Rs 18 crore, and which the company closed down during FY26 — management cites 'absence of Ply sales due to closure of that division' as a drag on Q3 FY26 revenue.
- **Joint ventures and acquired units in Morbi** — Stakes in and acquisitions of tile plants in the Morbi cluster in Gujarat (such as the Keronite unit, 6 million square metres of GVT) plus buyouts like South Asian Ceramics near Hyderabad, used to add cheap capacity and cut delivery time to nearby markets; profits from JVs are reported separately from the main operating profit.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

**Why this verdict:** the calls contradict the pattern — that grounded reading overrides any supportive numbers (1 of 2 checks had pointed this way).

**What the calls show:** The record shows price deflation, the pattern's disqualifier: own-manufactured realisation fell ~9% from Rs.401/sqm in Q3FY23 to Rs.365/sqm in Q3FY26, discounts were given in Q3FY26 to clear SKUs, and the FY26 12-17% hikes were pure gas-cost pass-through, not value capture.

> "prices of tiles are not increasing in the market with so many players there... it has fallen from 401 square meter in Q3 of FY '23 to right now at 365 square meters" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹57 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ❌ Operating margin moved from about 18% (early years) to 16% (recent years) — margins are ERODING, the opposite of pricing power.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Good Enough Goods, Cyclicality. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.1× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 80% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ✅ Returns on capital moderated from about 33% to 20% — lower, but still well clear of the deep slide this check looks for.

### Good Enough Goods — HIGH RISK
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Own-manufactured tile realization fell ~9% from Rs.401/sqm (Q3FY23) to Rs.365/sqm (Q3FY26), and management concedes it cannot price up against the Morbi cluster's cheaper equivalent product; in Q3FY26 it gave discounts to liquidate SKUs. The choice is intermediated by dealers and increasingly by architects/interior designers, exactly the Nobel Biocare structure where the intermediary captures the premium.

> "prices of tiles are not increasing in the market with so many players there. But the only way forward is that we have to sell more of value addition" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Brand equity has periodically allowed price increases (2% in May-2022, 12–17% in Mar-2026), the display-driven channel creates switching friction ('what is visible is sold'), and the mix shift into GVT/slabs/Continua+ moves the portfolio away from commodity ceramic.*

- ⚠️ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs fell from about 68% to 60% — pricing is being competed away at the gross level.
- ✅ Operating margin (18%) remains near its historical peak (20%) — no collapse in the premium.
- ✅ Operating margin held (about 18% then 16%) — no drift down under competition.

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across all 17 calls the P&L swings with construction demand and with gas, a commodity input management repeatedly refuses to forecast: gas moved Rs.34→Rs.69 (FY23), back to ~Rs.37 (FY25), then to Rs.84 landed in Morbi by Apr-2026, and EBITDA margin ranged from 15.2% (Q1FY23) to 10.0% (Q4FY25) to 19.2% (Q4FY26) with volumes flat for most of FY26. Management withdrew margin and volume guidance twice ('please do not talk about this subject', and again in May-2026), which is itself an admission that the cycle, not the franchise, sets near-term outcomes.

> "the gas cost which has gone up from Rs.34 in August 23rd to Rs.69 as of today" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Tiles are a small line item in a building's cost ('tile is a very small segment in the entire real estate project'), demand is replacement/renovation as well as new build, and the company invested counter-cyclically (Keronite, Nepal, South Asian Ceramics) while Morbi shut down.*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 11 years with swings of ±10 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 14% and 20% (±2.0 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 6% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 12%).

### Government Dependency — WATCH
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Government does not set Kajaria's prices, but it sets its single largest cost — gas allocation and tariffs run through GAIL/GSPC and an APM-linked regime, with Delhi literally rationing supply in Q4FY26 — and demand tailwinds are repeatedly credited to policy (Rs.10 lakh crore urban housing, Bihar/Andhra spending). US anti-dumping duty on Indian tiles is another policy variable, and the Kajaria 2.0 plan adds a dedicated government-tender team.

> "the Government of India made a restriction that you will get for the first 2 days 80% and 65% and it's a varied price" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Revenue is retail/dealer-led rather than tender-led, exports are negligible for Kajaria so ADD is not a direct hit, and fuel diversification (biofuel ~15% overall, ~30% at North plants) reduces dependence on administered gas.*

### Stakeholder Concentration — WATCH
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** No customer concentration — ~1,700 dealers, 380 exclusive showrooms, and no region above 35% of sales — but supply is concentrated: roughly 27–28% of material is outsourced from the Morbi cluster, and when Morbi shut in Mar-2026 management flagged 'outsourcing is still a little problem' and could not say how much material it would receive.

> "today as Kajaria, we are outsourcing to the extent of almost 27%, 28% of the materials from Morbi" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Broad, deliberately widened dealer base and multi-locational owned plants (North, South, Morbi JVs, Nepal) that ran at full capacity and cleared 1.5 msm of stock while the cluster was down.*

- ✅ Customers pay in about 48 days (was 42) — no sign of weakening bargaining power in collection terms.

### New Entrants — WATCH
*"A new player changes the rules"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** The recurring competitive theme is not a new format but the same low-cost Morbi cluster (500–600 plants) whose export/domestic swings set industry pricing; Kajaria answers with branding spend (~Rs.100 crore p.a.), showroom expansion and discounting rather than a structural moat. Exposure is bounded because these are long-standing, not structurally new, competitors and formalisation is working in Kajaria's favour.

> "Out of 500, 600 plants, which people say in the investing community... there are only about 70, 80, 90 plants which are running" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: GST-driven formalisation pushing small-town dealers toward branded players, and the Mar-2026 gas shock forcing Morbi to raise prices 35–40% — 'a very, very positive scenario for the so-called organized players who have multi-locational plants'.*

- ✅ Operating margin held (about 18% then 16%) — no drift down under competition.
- ✅ Growth is holding up: 4% a year earlier, 12% recently — no structural stall.

### Technological Innovation — LOW
*"Out-invented by the industry"*

**Why this verdict:** the judge affirmatively found the risk absent or mitigated in the calls.

**What the calls show:** Format change in tiles is incremental and the company has consistently been an adopter rather than a victim: Continua+ from Sacmi for slabs in 2022, disposal of two 26-year-old ceramic lines in 2024, and conversion of a 9.1 msm Gailpur ceramic line to GVT in FY26. No analyst on these calls raises a disruption threat to the tile format itself.

> "we have converted 1 unit with a capacity of 9.1 million square meters from ceramic floor tiles to glazed vitrified tiles" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Steady small-scale, value-adding innovation cadence and willingness to retire/retool legacy lines rather than defend them.*

- ✅ Growth is holding up: 4% a year earlier, 12% recently — no structural stall.
- ✅ Operating margin (18%) remains near its historical peak (20%) — no collapse in the premium.

### Shifting Consumer Preferences — LOW

**What the calls show:** The only preference migration visible is within-category and favourable — buyers trading from ceramic to GVT and large-format slabs — and Kajaria has repositioned capacity and its dealer displays accordingly rather than losing a demand base. Demand drivers cited are tier-2/3 penetration and housing, not eroding habits.
