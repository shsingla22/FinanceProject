- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: There is a sales-and-service network that creates some aftermarket stickiness in pumps and solar, but no evidence of switching costs or regional exclusivity — lighting is openly price-eroding. | The long view had said: Consumer electrical products carry negligible switching costs and no captive aftermarket — customers move on price and the company must repeatedly take pricing action and fight erosion to hold position.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Mix is genuinely improving (BLDC fans, premium gas stoves, designer lighting) and pricing actions were led by Crompton, but pricing was largely a reactive pass-through of commodity inflation on a thin 3% volume base. | The long view had said: Management explicitly describes fan growth as volume-led, and the repeated 7-8% price actions are cost pass-through to defend margins rather than pricing power, though premiumisation (BLDC, panels, Rhion) is now adding mix.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing discipline exists in fans, but industry-wide price erosion persists in lighting and the newly entered wires and solar markets are crowded, so entry barriers are only partial. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.8 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing rationality is unreliable — management repeatedly hopes competitors will follow its price increases rather than discount, and lighting has suffered structural price erosion, indicating low barriers.

Now: Management gestated new categories internally for several quarters before launch and is running multi-year programs (GTM revamp over 18–20 months, 2035 emission targets) rather than chasing quarters. | The long view had said: Multi-year projects were seen through with tenacity — the lighting turnaround over three years including Baddi/Baroda restructuring and VRS, solar pumps scaled from ₹20 crore to well beyond ₹200 crore, and wires/solar gestated internally for quarters before launch.

Now: The narrative (World #1, fastest-growing kitchen business, share gains everywhere) runs well ahead of the delivered numbers — ~7% year-on-year growth for most of the year, single-digit margins and a write-down — so results should not be read as proof of skill. | The long view had said: Much of the reported strength traces to exogenous drivers — intense summers, COVID base effects, GST-driven demand and price pass-throughs — rather than proven skill, so the record supports neither a strong positive nor negative once results are separated from luck.

Now: Commentary is consistently self-promotional — trumpeting the Euromonitor 'World's #1' certification and claiming industry leadership on pricing — rather than low-key and substance-first. | The long view had said: Communication is largely operational and business-focused, but the CEO's showmanship on calls and the promotion of ESG index rankings temper the low-profile assessment.

- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Expansion is domestic roll-out only — wires launched in select markets to go pan-India, plus a rural acceleration program — with no international expansion in the window. | The long view had said: Expansion is intra-India and reasonably well executed — Butterfly pushed beyond the south, wires launched first in South India then rolled out nationally, solar rooftop scaled in Andhra/Telangana — but there is no international or premium-brand-travels story.

Now: Management owns the Butterfly impairment and its ROCE impact and admits margin pressure, but withholds solar order-book and revenue detail and leans on unquantified 'we believe we gained share' claims. | The long view had said: Management owns problems plainly — supply glitches that cost sales, loss-making kitchen incubation, the Butterfly impairment and lighting's historic profitability issues — while declining to disclose order books or guidance.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 9 unchanged.

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Share gains are claimed in all four calls and several are specific: solar pumps from near-zero to 6-8% national share in ~two years while growing 100%+ year-on-year, #2 water heater brand in general trade, world #1 in ceiling fans, and lighting's best growth in six years — gained alongside margin expansion, not bought with it. (The long view had said: Sales run through dealers/distributors and Crompton repeatedly cites 'deep dealer relationships' and channel-partner selection as its right to win in new categories like wires and solar rooftop; but there is no evidence of trade training programmes or prescriber-led specification, and the demand model is explicitly consumer-pull (IPL campaigns, #PicturePerfectFan, rising A&P).)

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 3 worsened, 3 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Lighting faces persistent industry-wide price erosion that management can only offset through mix, and rising commodity costs are being met by qualifying alternate raw materials while defending warranties — the classic squeeze between a branded premium and cheaper substitutes in largely undifferentiated mass categories. (The long view had said: The core products are non-complex, mass-market commodities where cheaper alternatives constantly attack: pumps lost growth for years because Crompton's prices ran 20-25% ahead of unorganised rivals, lighting suffers chronic price erosion, and management repeatedly frames results around whether the industry will follow its price increases — 'I am hoping that competition, rather than playing the pricing game, will now also start to see...'. Entering wires on an outsourced, brand-and-distribution-led model puts it into another commoditised, dealer-influenced category.)

**That worsened:**

- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: The two businesses management points to as the growth engine are policy-anchored: solar pumps won on a Rs.101 Cr Maharashtra Energy Development Agency order and solar rooftop revenue that is currently entirely from state tenders (Telangana, Andhra), with management saying solar could become the second-largest business within 1-2 years; the core fans category was also reshaped by the BEE 2.0 regulatory transition. (The long view had said: A growing share of new growth engines is policy-anchored: solar pumps executed against state schemes and solar rooftop revenue coming 'currently... from the state tender orders', with a ~Rs.500 Cr rooftop order book from Andhra/Telangana government programmes; GST changes on pumps and BEE norms have also moved economics. Offsetting this, management has deliberately cut government exposure in lighting B2B.)
- **Technological Innovation**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The BEE 2.0 star-rating transition effective 1 Jan 2026 forced a technology shift across the core ceiling-fan category, requiring liquidation of legacy one-star inventory, and the BLDC migration is resetting the fan product platform; Crompton has so far responded with its own indigenous Nucleus BLDC platform growing 30%+. (The long view had said: Innovation in fans/lighting is incremental and regulation-paced rather than platform-shifting; the BEE 2.0 star-rating transition and the induction-to-BLDC motor shift were navigated with 18 months of prior engineering work and no disruption, and management runs a steady small-scale launch cadence (41 new products in one quarter, patents in cooling and kitchen).)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The newest growth vector rests on a handful of very large single counterparties — a Rs.445 Cr landmark rooftop order plus a Rs.52 Cr first order, and a Rs.101 Cr MEDA pump order — and the wires launch is entirely on outsourced third-party manufacturing; the legacy business, by contrast, sells through a broad pan-India dealer network. (The long view had said: No single customer, licensor or supplier is named as driving results across the 16 calls; sales run through a broadening multi-channel base (general trade, e-commerce, modern retail, rural, EBOs, kitchen dealers) with alternate channels rising from 9% to 17% of sales and e-commerce sustaining Rs.100 Cr+ per quarter.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
