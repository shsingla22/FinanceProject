# A B Real Estate (ABREL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Aditya Birla Real Estate Limited (ABREL), formerly Century Textiles and Industries, is an Indian company that today mainly builds and sells homes — apartments in premium, luxury and ultra-luxury residential projects sold under the "Birla Estates" brand to individual home buyers. It buys or ties up land (and, more recently, takes on redevelopment of existing housing societies), gets approvals such as RERA, launches projects in phases, and books "presales" as buyers commit; cash comes in as "collections" staged against construction progress, with revenue recognised in the accounts mainly on delivery. It operates in four main Indian markets — Mumbai/MMR (Worli, Thane, Khar, Boisar), the Delhi-NCR region (Gurugram), Bengaluru and Pune — and in FY26 it reported presales of INR 8,136 crores, collections of INR 3,341 crores and 5.5 million sq ft sold. In the earlier calls (2022) the same company was a three-part group that also ran a large pulp and paper business and a textiles business; over the period covered these manufacturing arms fall away (textiles is treated as discontinued by FY25 and the later calls discuss real estate only), and the company is renamed to reflect the pure real-estate identity.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Real Estate (Birla Estates)** — Develops and sells residential apartments — increasingly premium and luxury — in Mumbai/MMR, NCR, Bengaluru and Pune, with named projects like Birla Niyaara (Worli), Birla Trimaya/Evara and Alokya (Bengaluru), Birla Arika/Navya/Pravaah (Gurugram), Birla Taranya (Thane), Birla Vanya (Kalyan) and Birla Punya/Evam (Pune); it starts as the smallest reported segment (only INR 33 crores of revenue in Q1 FY23) and becomes the entire company, delivering INR 8,136 crores of presales in FY26.
- **Business development / land pipeline** — The engine behind the above — buying land parcels and signing project tie-ups measured in "GDV" (gross development value, the expected sales value); management cited a INR 54,000 crore GDV pipeline in mid-2024, added roughly INR 25,000 crores of new projects in one year, and guided to INR 10,000–15,000 crores of new additions in FY26.
- **Society redevelopment** — A newer route to land in Mumbai, where the company redevelops existing housing-society buildings; its first such project, in Khar, carries about INR 1,700 crores of GDV, with more society discussions described as in advanced stages.
- **Pulp & Paper** — In the earlier years this was the biggest revenue contributor — manufacturing writing and printing paper, tissue, paperboard and cup stock for domestic customers, government tenders and exports (INR 857 crores of sales in Q1 FY23 and still INR 786 crores in Q1 FY25) — but it is absent from the later, real-estate-only calls.
- **Textiles** — The legacy business making apparel fabrics and bed linen, the latter sold mainly to the US market; it was the smallest of the three at INR 268 crores of sales in Q1 FY23 with near-zero EBITDA, and by FY25 the company reports figures for "continuing operations" only, with textiles no longer discussed.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 3 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** The business has been reshaped around residential real estate — an inherently cyclical, capex-heavy asset class — after the earlier paper/textile cycle already showed the pattern (paper EBITDA margin 15.6% in Q1FY23 on 'multiple price increases' for coal/pulp costs, collapsing to 8.3% by Q1FY25 as NSR fell 14% Y-o-Y). Management explicitly bets on the upcycle and is scaling land buying into it (INR25,000 crore of BD signed in FY25, INR60,000 crore pipeline being pursued), which is the classic expansionary over-investment setup; presales are lumpy and launch-driven (Q4 FY26 +69% Q-o-Q) and management now refuses to give guidance at all.

> "we are looking at a very positive and strong real estate cycle for the next few years" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: IRR-first discipline over margin ('even if we get caught in the wrong cycle, we are not left with too much unsold inventory'), asset-light JDAs with small upfront payments (Barmalt INR5,300 crore GDV for ~INR145 crore), phased launches and holding back the last Trimaya/Arika towers as a hedge*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 6 of the last 9 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from -90% to 24% (swinging ±33.7 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 52% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 4% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK

**What the calls show:** Across all four years, revenue timing is set by government bodies rather than by the company: Navya RERA delays (2022), RR Nagar and Walkeshwar launches postponed for RERA (2024), Tower C Niyaara pushed out of FY26 by a Supreme Court land case plus pending MoEF/RERA, the Thane launch delayed by an NGT ban, the ITC paper-divestment cash awaiting MoEF and lease transfer, and Talegaon land unusable until PMRDA zoning conversion; the legacy paper business was likewise levered to PSU tenders, with Q1FY25 volumes down 8% Q-o-Q on 'deferment of government tenders' during the general election.
