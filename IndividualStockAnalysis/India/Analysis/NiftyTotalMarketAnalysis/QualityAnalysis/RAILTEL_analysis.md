# Railtel Corpn. (RAILTEL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

RailTel Corporation of India is a government-owned (Government of India undertaking) telecom and IT-projects company based in New Delhi and operating across India, listed on the NSE and BSE. It runs a fibre-optic network laid along the Indian Railways system and earns recurring money by selling telecom connectivity — such as leased lines to institutional customers like DRDO (billed annually or quarterly) — while adding revenue as it takes over new railway sections; this telecom side was about Rs 274 crore of Rs 377 crore consolidated Q1 FY23 revenue. The second half is a 'project' business where it bids for and executes large government/railway tenders on a contract basis, such as the roughly Rs 411 crore video surveillance systems (VSS) order at major railway stations under the Nirbhaya fund, contributing about Rs 103 crore in the quarter. It works off a tender-driven order book of over Rs 5,800 crore (Rs 662 crore of new orders won in Q1 FY23), and is also moving into longer-gestation infrastructure like data centres and telecom towers.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Telecom business** — Sells connectivity and leased-line services over its fibre network — including pan-India leased lines for customers such as DRDO — and is the larger, more recurring half at roughly Rs 274 crore of Rs 377 crore of Q1 FY23 revenue, though management says margins are squeezed by continuous price pressure and rising fibre and equipment maintenance costs.
- **Project business** — Executes contracted turnkey projects won through tenders, mainly for Indian Railways and government bodies, such as the ~Rs 411 crore station video surveillance (VSS) project under the Nirbhaya fund; it was about Rs 103 crore in Q1 FY23 and management guided to roughly Rs 800–900 crore for the full year, with margins under stress at the time.
- **Data centres and telecom towers** — New, longer-gestation infrastructure ventures the company had tendered for; management said these would take about 18 months to start contributing, with revenue expected only from the second half of FY2024.

### The story in depth

RailTel earns a Mixed rating of 35 out of 100 (two stars), built from 47 for business quality, 25 for multibagger fit and 26 for risk safety, with underlying resilience judged SOUND. The genuine strengths are few but real. The company sits in end markets that are expanding on their own — enterprise connectivity, data centres, private 5G networks, rail video surveillance and rural broadband — which supports a Rs.5,800 Crore order book and 23% revenue growth, and it turned every Rs.100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings into Rs.21 of operating cash. Management is disciplined in the narrow sense: growth is strictly organic, capex is a modest Rs.150 Crores, there are no acquisitions, and the plan for towers and data centres is to bring in private investment rather than pile on debt. But that same modesty is why the capex check scores negatively — Rs.150 Crores for a full year against Rs.377 Crores of revenue in a single quarter looks like keeping the lights on more than funding the future, and the named growth projects are long-gestation with no revenue arriving before H2 FY2024. Meanwhile roughly 195 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, which quietly drains much of what the 21% cash return appears to deliver.

The one asset that genuinely cannot be copied is the exclusive right of way along the Indian Railways network — around 1,000 towers across 6,000-plus stations that a well-funded newcomer simply cannot rebuild. That single fact is why the Cost to Replicate pattern scores a partial fit, and it is also the stated cushion against the Good Enough Goods risk, since pure resellers have no equivalent. The problem is that the same asset is the source of the two highest risks in the review. Being a Government of India undertaking under the Ministry of Railways means revenue is dominated by government-linked work — the Nirbhaya-fund video surveillance tender at roughly Rs.411 Crores, RDN and COD tenders, Kavach specified by the Ministry's own centre of excellence — and Indian Railways is simultaneously the parent, the landlord of the fibre route and the source of the biggest project orders. Government Dependency and Stakeholder Concentration are both flagged HIGH RISK for exactly the reason the moat exists. The partial offsets run the same way: around 20,000 km of network built away from the railway track, and the RailWire retail broadband base of about 4.69 lakh subscribers, are cited as diversification against both risks, and RailWire is also what earns the partial score on Forward Integrators — a real step toward owning the end customer, with disclosed subscribers, roughly Rs.560 ARPU and a self-launched OTT and IPTV bundle. Recurring Revenue is likewise only partial: the leased-line and telecom annuity billed in advance is real, and Rs.274 Crores of the Rs.377 Crores quarterly revenue is operations-linked recurring flow rather than customer capex — which is precisely why Cyclicality is only a WATCH — but management never discusses renewal rates, attach rates or deferred revenue.

The remaining eight multibagger patterns are outright misses, and each one has a matching mark against the business or a matching risk. Bandwidth is a commodity with falling prices: management itself names price deflation as a structural feature and blames it for soft telecom revenue, so Pricing Power is a direct contradiction rather than mere silence, the pricing and mix check scores its worst mark, intangible or image-driven pricing power scores negatively, and Good Enough Goods is ELEVATED with telecom margins compressed toward 19% against 28-29% historically. Growth that does come is volume and order-book led, and one defence job was executed at a 4% margin against project EBIT guidance of 8.37-8.5% and a historic 8-9%. On innovation, the company chose not to develop or bid on Kavach, preferring to wait for an OEM partner whose product has matured — that stance sinks the Innovation Dominance pattern, scores against the research and development check, and feeds the ELEVATED Technological Innovation risk, alongside 5G timing being outside its control and BSNL's indigenous effort. In retail broadband, new operators arriving with bundled models forced the subscriber target down from 10 lakh to 7-8 lakh off a 4.69 lakh base, which simultaneously kills the Market Share Gainers pattern, scores against the market-share check and drives the ELEVATED New Entrants risk. Finally, four chairmen across FY2021-FY2023 plus a CFO exit rules out the Corporate Culture pattern and scores against the independent, long-term stewardship check — the same rotation that sits behind targets being abandoned rather than pursued. Friendly Middleman, Toll Roads, Low Price Plus and Brand Strength find no support in the record, and two risks — Shifting Consumer Preferences and Fashion Risk — show no signal at all.

## What to watch

- Working capital: whether the ~195-day gap between paying suppliers and collecting from customers shortens — it is the worst-scoring check and it consumes most of the 21% cash return on capital.
- Capex follow-through: whether spending rises above the Rs.150 Crore guide and the data centre and tower projects start producing revenue from H2 FY2024 — the capex check is negative today purely on size versus Rs.377 Crores of quarterly revenue.
- Margin repair: whether project EBIT climbs back from 3.94% toward the guided 8.37-8.5% and telecom recovers from ~19% toward the historic 28-29% — falling short again confirms both the Pricing Power miss and the Good Enough Goods risk.
- RailWire subscribers: whether the base moves up from 4.69 lakh toward the cut 7-8 lakh target at around Rs.560 ARPU — this is the single test of both the Forward Integrators partial fit and the ELEVATED New Entrants risk.
- Railways concentration: whether the ~20,000 km of off-track network and non-railway enterprise revenue grow enough to loosen the twin HIGH RISK ratings on Government Dependency and Stakeholder Concentration, given the same relationship supplies the un-replicable right of way.
- Leadership stability: whether the current chairman and a settled CFO stay in place after four chairmen across FY2021-FY2023 — the churn behind both the Corporate Culture miss and the negative long-term stewardship check.
