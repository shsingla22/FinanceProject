Now: ALMM List 2 confers a regulatory ring-fence around domestic cell supply that functions like a partial monopoly today, but it is shared with every listed peer and creates no customer switching costs. | The long view had said: No switching costs — customers are IPPs and EPCs procuring on price and ALMM compliance; the only lock-in is regulatory eligibility, which competitors can also obtain.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 8 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is a genuine three-decade heritage story (solar water heaters to integrated PV) and repeated 'customer trust/quality' framing across the Jul 2026 and Apr 2026 calls, but no A&P spend, no premium pricing and no adjacency launches are evidenced — the brand claim is asserted, not quantified. (The long view had said: There is a genuine three-decade quality record used as a B2B credential — warranty claim rate <0.008% over three years and a claim to sit 'at the top of the evaluation matrix' for developers — but no A&P spend, no premium pricing from the name, and no adjacency stretch; realisations track the commodity.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The barriers cited are capital, time and regulation — INR 5,500 crore for the 6 GW integrated facility, ALMM List 2 inclusion and tight domestic TOPCon cell supply — which deter small entrants but are exactly what a well-funded copycat can buy, and management concedes competition will increase and technology will evolve. (The long view had said: The articulated barriers — Class 6 cleanrooms, 14-step TOPCon vs 8-step PERC, ALMM listing — are capital and permitting hurdles that Emmvee itself cleared in about two years and is replicating for INR5,500cr, in an industry already carrying 82GW of module capacity against 26GW of domestic production. The advantage rests on government DCR/ALMM policy rather than anything a funded entrant could not rebuild.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Volume and order book expand monotonically across the window — order book 5 GW (Dec 2025) to 9.3, 9.4 and 9.9 GW (Jul 2026), module capacity 7.8 to 10.3 GW, module production 1,482 MW to 2,999 MW in FY26 — but management never cites a market share number or names a competitor, so the gain is inferred from absolute growth in an expanding, policy-driven market. (The long view had said: Volumes and order book grew persistently (module production 1,482MW to 2,999MW; order book 4.9GW to 9.4GW to 9.9GW; top-10 average order size 121MW to 221MW), which implies share gain in a fragmented industry where only 26GW of 82GW capacity actually produced — but management never gives an explicit market-share number or names competitors, and exports were zero in FY26.)

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 2 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The entire asset base has just been re-tooled once already — 'a 100% transition to TOPCon' plus a new G12R format — and the 6 GW Devanahalli plant doubles down on TOPCon, so a shift to the next cell architecture would strand freshly built infrastructure; PV cell technology turns over on a short cycle and the company's answer is to be an early adopter rather than to be insulated. (The long view had said: Management's own answer on Monoperc-to-TOPCon migration is a textbook description of installed-base obsolescence: 8 steps to 14 steps, double the equipment, Class 8 to Class 6 cleanrooms, 1.6x power, 1.5x water — 'practically a very challenging task,' with Chinese players building new lines rather than converting. MNRE lifting the efficiency floor to 21%/21.5% strands 'over 30% of capacities.' Emmvee is on the right side of this specific transition, but the same mechanism (next: HJT/perovskite, G12R format shifts, ALMM List 3 upstream rules) can reset its own INR5,500 crore TOPCon investment.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Modules are a price-per-watt commodity bought by EPCs and IPPs on tender, with no end-customer brand pull — management's own history of module prices collapsing from EUR 3/watt to 15 cents illustrates how quickly cheaper equivalents reset economics, and the CEO deflects percentage-margin questions toward absolute EBITDA per watt peak, which is the classic tell of a price-taking product. (The long view had said: A solar module is a specification-defined commodity: buyers procure to watt-peak, efficiency class and ALMM listing, and management concedes realisation of only 15-16 cents/watt in non-DCR with prices in permanent decline — Nobel Biocare's pattern of a not-very-complex product where challengers undercut. Management defends the premium on warranty-claim rates and reliability rather than any structural moat, and today's 35% margin rests substantially on the DCR/ALMM price wedge ('the DCR non-DCR mix... healthier on the DCR side, which is reflected in our increased margin') rather than on customer willingness to pay up for the brand.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Not comparable: Shifting Consumer Preferences (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
