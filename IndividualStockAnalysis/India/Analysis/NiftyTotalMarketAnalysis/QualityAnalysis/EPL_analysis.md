# EPL (EPL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

EPL Limited makes flexible packaging — principally squeezable tubes — that consumer-goods companies fill with their products, selling to leading global oral care brands (toothpaste) and beauty, cosmetics and personal care brands. It earns money by manufacturing and selling these tubes in volume from 21 factories across 11 countries, with roughly half its business under contractual arrangements that pass raw-material cost changes (mainly polymers and aluminium) through to customers, and it uses price increases to protect margins when input costs rise. It runs as four geographic regions — AMESA (India, Middle East, South Asia, Africa), EAP (East Asia Pacific, including China), Europe and the Americas — and reported FY26 revenue growth of 13% with EBITDA margins above 20%. Sustainability is central to how it competes: 'sustainable' recyclable tube formats reached 38% of sales in FY26 and have helped it win wallet share from customers, and it holds an EcoVadis Platinum rating. In March 2026 it agreed to merge with Indovida, a rigid-packaging company owned by Indorama Ventures, to create a roughly $1 billion (INR 8,300 crore) emerging-markets consumer packaging group in which Indorama becomes the promoter.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Oral Care** — Tubes for toothpaste and similar oral products — historically EPL's biggest category and still a core one, growing about 10% in Q4 FY26, though by FY26 management says Beauty & Cosmetics had become larger than Oral Care in most key markets.
- **Personal Care and Beyond (including Beauty & Cosmetics)** — Tubes and newer formats such as Neo-seam for cosmetics, beauty and personal care brands; this is the company's declared growth priority, reaching 53% of the mix in FY26 with Beauty & Cosmetics growing about 30% for the year.
- **AMESA region** — Asia Middle East South Asia and Africa, centred on the Indian business, which management reports separately as 'India standalone'; grew 10.4% in Q4 FY26 with India standalone up 11.5%.
- **EAP region** — East Asia Pacific, including China, where COVID lockdowns badly hurt sales in FY23; it recovered strongly and grew 25% in Q4 FY26, and it now includes a new plant in Thailand serving that country's beauty and cosmetics market.
- **Europe region** — European manufacturing and sales, which had the weakest margins (single digits) in FY23 due to energy and wage inflation but recovered to mid-teens margins by FY25 and grew 15.5% in Q4 FY26.
- **Americas region** — North and South America, including a new Brazil plant commercialised around FY24 that has added multinational and local customers; regional margins went from single digits to high teens, and the region grew 24.1% in Q4 FY26.
- **Sustainable tubes** — Recyclable tube formats (including the Platina line) that customers are switching to on their own sustainability commitments; the mix rose from 21% of volume in FY24 to 29% in Q1 FY25 and 38% of sales in FY26, and management treats it as a source of competitive advantage and wallet-share gains.
- **Indovida merger (announced March 2026, pending)** — A share-swap merger with Indorama Ventures' rigid-packaging arm — INR 3,800+ crore revenue, 21.3% EBITDA margin, 19 plants in nine countries, mostly Southeast Asia and Africa — that would roughly double EPL to INR 8,300 crore revenue and add rigid packaging alongside EPL's flexible tubes, with $35-50 million of identified synergies.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

EPL is a contract manufacturer of tubes for large consumer-goods companies, and the quality work says it runs that business well. Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹20 is operating profit, and that margin has wobbled by only about ±1.4 points a year — steady enough that the framework reads it as evidence of cost control and of an industry where few new competitors show up and pricing stays rational. Capital earns a 19% cash return. Sales have compounded at about 7% a year with very consistent year-to-year growth. Management scores well on candor: it called Europe's margins 'not acceptable' in 2022, admitted the India Beauty & Cosmetics miss in 2024, and refused to promise timing it could not back — while also delivering what it did promise, holding EBITDA margins above 20% for seven straight quarters, adding 500 basis points over 15 quarters, and cutting debt to 0.52x. Against that, two areas score poorly: there is no self-reinforcing network in this model (a clear negative), and roughly 97 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. The overall rating is 58 out of 100 — 'Decent', three stars — built as 45% × 67 on business quality, 30% × 18 on multibagger fit, and 25% × 88 on risk safety. Resilience is judged sound.

The pattern screen is where the story gets more sober, and its verdicts trace back to the same traits. Only one of eleven patterns is a likely fit: Market Share Gainers. That one is well supported — wallet-share wins named repeatedly and by geography from FY25 onward, two new customers each in Europe, India and the Americas, growth off a roughly 35% base, Beauty & Cosmetics up 30% and now larger than Oral Care in most key markets, Brazil taken from announcement in 2022 to multi-customer outperformance, Thailand entered in nine months, and the Indovida merger opening Southeast Asia and Africa. The engine behind it is sustainability: the sustainable-tube mix went from 21% in FY24 to 29% to 38% by Q4FY26, backed by EcoVadis Platinum and SBTi firsts, and is directly credited with the oral-care wallet-share gains. Seven patterns land as only partial and three as outright no fit, and the reason is structural rather than incidental. EPL sells business-to-business into brand owners; the brands, advertising and consumer loyalty belong to its customers, so Brand Strength does not apply, no prescriber or trade professional sits in between so Friendly Middleman does not apply, and the strategy of raising prices and premiumising is the opposite of Low Price Plus. Pricing Power and Toll Roads stop at partial for one shared reason: around 50% of revenue runs on contractual cost pass-through, which is cost recovery rather than the ability to price above cost inflation — management has openly conceded negative price/mix and 'price corrections' when commodity costs eased. Cost to Replicate is partial because the real barriers that do exist — 21 plants in 11 countries sited next to customers, decades-long supplier relationships, multi-year qualification and certification — are barriers made of capital, which money can eventually buy. Innovation Dominance is partial only because R&D spend is never disclosed, so the innovation cannot be sized.

The risk review is the most reassuring of the three, and its watch items are the flip side of the strengths rather than separate problems. Three of eight channels are flagged 'watch' and none is severe. Cyclicality is on watch because polymer, aluminium and energy cost cycles swing margins — EBITDA fell to roughly 15% in FY23 with single-digit Europe margins before recovering above 20% by FY26 — but the same pass-through contracts that cap pricing power on the pattern screen are what soften this risk, and demand is consumable rather than tied to customers' capital spending. Stakeholder concentration is on watch for exactly the trait that makes the one strong pattern work: growth is repeatedly explained as wallet-share gain with a few key customers, the core oral-care market has only four global buyers, and the Brazil plant was underwritten by a single anchor customer's commitment. The offset is the same diversification the pattern screen credits — Personal Care & Beyond now 53% of the mix, Beauty & Cosmetics larger than Oral Care in most key markets, and Brazil since adding more customers. 'Good enough goods' is on watch because the tube is a component the FMCG customer picks, with no end-consumer awareness, and price corrections have been given back — the same absence of consumer brand that kills three patterns. What pushes back is the assurance customers demonstrably pay for: supply security during the Middle East crisis, long supplier partnerships, and ESG compliance that won wallet share ahead of price. Technological innovation, shifting consumer preferences and fashion risk are all low, because the one format shift the industry is undergoing — recyclable, non-aluminium tubes — has EPL as the beneficiary rather than the victim. New entrants shows no signal at all; when an analyst probed competitive intensity in Q1FY23, management pointed to input costs, COVID absenteeism and operational issues instead. In short: a well-run, honest, steadily profitable supplier that is genuinely taking share, held back from a higher rating not by danger but by the ceiling that comes with being a capital-heavy, un-branded, pass-through-priced supplier to a handful of very large buyers.
