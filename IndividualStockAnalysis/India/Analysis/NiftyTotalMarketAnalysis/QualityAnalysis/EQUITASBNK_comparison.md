**Checks that improved:**

Now: The record Q4 PAT is largely cyclical and seasonal rather than proof of skill, and management themselves separate the two by flagging the normalisation, which keeps the assessment honest. | The long view had said: The FY26 recovery is materially attributable to industry-wide factors — MFIN guardrails and a falling rate cycle repricing deposits — as much as to management skill, so results should not be read as pure execution alpha.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Good. Now: Two decades of branch-level field presence with high-vintage staff and dedicated collection teams gives a service-intensive reach that produced a 99.4% X-bucket collection efficiency recovery — hard for non-branch rivals to replicate. | The long view had said: Distribution is decent and multi-channel (branches, RMs, business correspondents, digital, sound box/QR/POS for current accounts) but is a work-in-progress rather than reach rivals cannot match.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Weak → Neutral. Now: Technology investment is real and functioning (CRM, super app, borrower app, selfie loan app) but is table-stakes digital banking infrastructure rather than a defensible multi-product edge. | The long view had said: Technology is bought-in and catch-up in nature (Microsoft Dynamics CRM, new LOS, standard apps) rather than a proprietary multi-product edge, and it has weighed on cost-to-income without a visible customer-benefit moat.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: The bank is emerging from the bottom of the microfinance credit cycle with a clearly identifiable upturn — credit cost fell from 2.74% to 1.11%, net slippages from 3.78% to 0.79%, and NIM inflected upward after years of decline. | The long view had said: The bank is visibly coming off the bottom of the microfinance credit cycle, with credit cost falling from 2.74% to 1.11%, net slippage at a 10-quarter low and NIM inflecting up for the first time in years.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: The customer base is almost entirely retail/micro-enterprise where the bank correctly leads with access and convenience, but it lacks the intangible pricing power that retail franchises need and has no corporate switching-cost lock-in either. | The long view had said: Benefit types are deliberately matched to customer tiers — segmented retail propositions (ARTHA/Elite Lite/Elite, family banking) on liabilities and cash-flow-based informal lending on assets.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: This is a listed, heavily analyst-covered bank in the most scrutinised segment of Indian financials, with a large dedicated IR function on the call — the opposite of an ignored niche. | The long view had said: The informal-segment niche is genuinely unglamorous, but the bank is listed, widely covered by analysts on every call and openly targeting a universal bank licence.

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
