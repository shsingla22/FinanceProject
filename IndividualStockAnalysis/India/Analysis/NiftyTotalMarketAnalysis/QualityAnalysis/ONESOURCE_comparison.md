### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 5. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 0 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: The asset is B2B reputation — an exemplary inspection record and status as a 'trusted partner' for global biologics — not consumer heritage, A&P spend or distributor leverage, and no premium pricing is claimed from it. (The long view had said: A contract manufacturer with no consumer-facing brand; heritage, A&P spend and distributor leverage are simply not discussed on any of the calls.)
- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Management warned candidly and early that Q3/Q4 prints would understate the business, refused to guide on events outside its control, then delivered the promised sequential recovery (Q4 revenue ~$48m, +47% quarter-on-quarter; Q1 FY27 revenue +37% year-on-year) while reiterating the FY28 target unchanged — and deferred the Steriscience/Brooks related-party scheme despite having the votes, citing minority shareholder discomfort on valuation. (The long view had said: Genuine governance signals — Arun pre-warned investors in Nov-25 that reported numbers would understate the business, and the related-party Steriscience/Brookes scheme was deferred in May-26 despite SEBI approval because minority holders questioned valuation; against that, FY26 revenue fell 2% and EBITDA 35% versus the 25-30% CAGR promised at listing, and the FY28 $400m target has been reiterated rather than re-based.)

Why now: Management frames scale-up of batch sizes explicitly as a cost-competitiveness lever for partners in a price-sensitive generic market, but there is no evidence of an itemised cost culture or of low price paired with high asset turns. (The long view had said: There is no cost-culture or no-frills narrative anywhere across the seven calls; management positions explicitly on scarcity, compliance and capacity rather than price, and targets 40% EBITDA margins.)

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 3 worsened, 3 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Results swung violently within the 12-month window purely on launch/approval timing — Q3 FY26 revenue deferred with explicit 'negative operating leverage', then Q4 FY26 revenue up 47% quarter-on-quarter — while the company committed to INR700cr plus a US$100m capex programme ahead of the demand it is underwriting; the offset is that the end demand is chronic, repeat-supply commercial product, not customer capex equipment. (The long view had said: Results swing violently on launch/approval timing rather than a commodity cycle: EBITDA margin went 36% (Q3FY25) to 28% (Q2FY26) to a loss-making Q3FY26, then Q4FY26 EBITDA rose 5x sequentially (+1,550bps) while full-year FY26 revenue fell 2% and EBITDA fell 35%; meanwhile the company is committing $100m to a 5x capacity build (40m to 220m units) with net debt up INR4,326m and working capital elevated — the classic expansion-ahead-of-demand pattern.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: A single named partner drove the downside twice inside 12 months — Dr. Reddy's, described as 'one of our anchor partners', was the subject of Novo litigation, its deferred Canadian approval forced OneSource's Q3 FY26 revenue deferral, and its supply disruption was flagged again in Jul 2026; management is repeatedly questioned about this one relationship. (The long view had said: A single named customer, Dr. Reddy's, has twice driven the narrative — the Novo litigation against DRL and OneSource in Nov 2025, then DRL's announced supply disruption in Jul 2026 — and analysts questioned both; revenue is also heavily concentrated in one molecule (semaglutide). The exposure is demonstrably bounded: OneSource holds all three Canadian generic approvals, ~40% of India's generic pen market, 20+ GLP-1 customers, and pulled other customers' demand forward to fill DRL's gap without a supply impact.)
- **New Entrants**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Management concedes severe competitive intensity in the one market that has actually opened at scale for its customers and explicitly de-prioritises it, and its cost position depends on persuading partners to scale up batch sizes because current runs are 'not very commercially viable' — a sign that competing CDMO capacity will set price as more supply arrives. (The long view had said: The calls affirmatively describe structural entry barriers rather than a defensive response to new-format competitors: long-lead isolator equipment, one of few FDA-approved penicillin plants globally, top-five soft gel capacity (with European incumbents impaired), and 49 successful regulatory/customer audits in FY26 including surprise FDA inspections. No discounting or margin investment is framed as a competitive response anywhere in the seven calls.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: As a contract manufacturer its output is a service its partners can re-source, and management is openly working to cut cost per unit so partners can compete on price — the value it captures is squeezed from both ends, with the end patient having no awareness of who made the pen. (The long view had said: Management never defends a price premium against cheaper substitutes; instead it describes a scarcity-based niche where customers explicitly buy reliability over price — dedicated penicillin manufacturing, FDA-shortage products, isolator-based GLP-1 fill, and first-to-file work on tirzepatide — and profit-share rather than pure conversion-fee economics in regulated markets.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (the only stress check that could be computed is clean.) vs Sound on the one-year view (the only stress check that could be computed is clean.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
