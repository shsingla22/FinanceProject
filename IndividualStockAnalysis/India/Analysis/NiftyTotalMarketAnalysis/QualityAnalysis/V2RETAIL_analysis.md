# V2 Retail (V2RETAIL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

V2 Retail runs a chain of value-fashion apparel stores in India, selling trendy clothing at low prices to budget-conscious shoppers. Management describes the aim as 'democratizing fashion' — offering good quality, fresh, on-trend apparel at affordable prices, largely at full price rather than through discounting (full-price sales were about 89-92% of the total). It makes money purely from selling merchandise through its own physical stores, with most of what it sells being its own private-label brands (about 85% of business, and rising toward 100%), which it designs in-house and sources from vendors. It operates only in India, concentrated in smaller-town and rural markets plus Tier-II and Tier-III cities — clusters like Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Odisha and Jharkhand, expanding into Punjab, Bengal, Rajasthan and the South — growing from 127 stores in mid-2024 to over 350 stores and about 3.5 million square feet by mid-2026, with revenue of Rs. 3,067 crores in FY26.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Value-fashion retail stores** — The entire business: physical apparel stores across India that sell clothing at value prices, with 325 stores at end-FY26 (over 350 today) generating all of the company's Rs. 3,067 crore FY26 revenue; management said in June 2026 that it is 'now focusing on retail business only.'
- **Private label / own-design merchandise** — Its own in-house brands and designs rather than outside brands — about 85% of sales are private label (up from 40% in FY23 and targeted at 100%), with own-designed product around 35-40% in FY25 and targeted at 60% by summer 2026 and 80% by 2027; management says this drives freshness, less discounting and customer loyalty, though the price benefit is passed to customers rather than kept as extra margin.
- **In-house manufacturing (discontinued)** — The company previously ran its own manufacturing operations alongside retail, but stated in the Q4 FY26 call that it has moved away from in-house manufacturing to focus on retail only.
- **Warehousing and supply chain** — A network of zonal warehouses that stocks the stores — each roughly 1 lakh square feet costing about Rs. 20 crores of capex and able to serve 70-80 stores — described as a support function whose cost (about Rs. 55-60 per square foot along with head office) spreads over a growing store base to give operating leverage.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Every check, area by area

**Capital Allocation — Neutral (-0.17)** · 6 of 6 checks had evidence

- **Capex for growth (not maintenance) — Excellent** *(from the conference calls)*: Almost all capex is growth capex — 74 stores in FY25, 136 in FY26 and 170-200 guided for FY27 at ~Rs. 2.6-2.8 cr per store — funded largely by internal accruals while ROE rose from 10.7% (FY24) to 26% (FY26), indicating high incremental returns.
  > "During FY '26, we added 136 stores... ROE continues to improve and now stands at 26% compared to 23.2% in FY '25 and around 10.7% in FY '24" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Research and development — Neutral** *(from the conference calls)*: No R&D line exists, but there is an analogous and improving product-design capability — own-design merchandise rising from 35-40% toward a targeted 60% by summer 2026 and 80% by 2027 — with no disclosed efficiency metrics to judge it.
  > "Our own design products contributed about 35% to 40% this year and we target it to reach around 60% by the summer of 2026 and 80% by 2027" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Merger and acquisition — Neutral** *(from the conference calls)*: Growth is entirely organic with no acquisitions in the period; the only corporate action is an internal simplification — shutting three manufacturing units and merging the subsidiary into the standalone retail entity.
  > "because now, we have shut down three manufacturing units... we are going to merge both the entities now" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Cost of working capital — Weak** *(from the financial statements)*: About 116 days of cash stays stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers — a long cycle that ties up capital.
- **Advertisement and promotion — Weak** *(from the conference calls)*: A&P is deliberately kept below 1% of sales with most 'marketing' being customer gifts/discounts booked in COGS, so brand equity is built by price-value rather than by durable advertising investment.
  > "So, it's less than 1% of total sales... Because we have understood that the product is the best brand ambassad[or]" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Distribution to shareholders — Weak** *(from the conference calls)*: There is no dividend or buyback policy discussed; capital flowed the other way with a Rs. 400 crore QIP raised at peak valuations after a 60%+ growth run, and management flags further debt or equity for expansion.
  > "The company successfully raised approximately Rs. 400 crores through a QIP with marquee investors" — *management, on an earnings call*

**Return on Capital — Neutral (-0.33)** · 3 of 3 checks had evidence

- **Asset turn — Good** *(from the numbers and the calls together)*: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.27 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: Asset turn is genuinely high and improving — leased stores, sales rising to Rs. 1,200 psf/month in mature stores, inventory down to ~90 days and ROE at 26% — but the moat protecting it is thin given competition at nearly every location.
  > "our older stores this year have already touched Rs. 1,200 per square feet of sales" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Profit margin — Neutral** *(from the financial statements)*: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹15 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±17.1 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Return on capital (headline) — Poor** *(from the financial statements)*: For every ₹100 of capital in the business (shareholders' funds + borrowings), it generated ₹-5 of operating cash this year — a -5% cash return on capital.

**Growth — Good (+0.67)** · 6 of 6 checks had evidence

- **Gain in market share — Excellent** *(from the conference calls)*: With only 325-350 stores against a fragmented unorganized base and much larger rivals, V2 has enormous runway and is demonstrably capturing share, growing 63% in FY26 versus a far slower market.
  > "The 60% year-on-year revenue growth for the 4th Quarter significantly outpaces the broader market" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Geographical expansion — Excellent** *(from the conference calls)*: Disciplined, data-led entry into seven new states in FY26 (Punjab, Rajasthan, Bengal, AP, Karnataka, Gujarat, Maharashtra) using a 4-5 store pilot before scaling, with new-state stores performing at par with the new-store average and negligible added logistics cost.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

**Overall:** the company partly passes the foundation every multibagger shares (steady cash + high returns on capital + growth), 1 of 3 testable checks passing, and fits 3 of the 11 patterns: Corporate Culture, Low Price Plus, Market Share Gainers.

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** The 2024–2026 record shows promises kept and bad news owned: guidance of 40–50% growth was beaten twice, the 100-store FY26 plan was exceeded (136 added), old inventory came down 23% → 7% → 5% exactly as promised, the audit qualification was resolved via physical PPE verification and a Rs 5–5.8 crore write-off, pre-IndAS numbers are volunteered for transparency, and management openly cites the Vishal Mega Mart failure as its own learning. Offsets: chairman churn, a small pledge, and a visible key-man dependence on Akash Agarwal.

> "one of the biggest learnings was not chasing growth blindly by compromising on profitability... we have learned from those mistakes and implemented a lot of checks and balances" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Low Price Plus — LIKELY FIT
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern, but the numbers push back (0 of 1 check supporting) — held one step below a full confirmation.

**What the calls show:** The entire model is low price plus quality and variety, with gross margin deliberately capped at 28–29% and benefits passed to the customer while returns come from asset turns (PSF rising Rs 854 → Rs 1,017 → Rs 1,200; breakeven at Rs 500 PSF) and tight cost lines (rent Rs 52/sqft, total cost Rs 190/sqft, marketing <1% of sales). ROE improved 10.7% (FY24) → 23.2% (FY25) → 26% (FY26) on this formula, repeated consistently across FY25–FY26 calls.

> "our gross margin strategy is by design. We want to pass on most of the benefits to the consumer... all the margin expansion should happen from operating leverage and from higher sales per square feet" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹30 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 1.3× a year — not the high-turnover, low-margin machine this pattern needs.

### Market Share Gainers — LIKELY FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

> "we won't absorb it in gross margins because 3%-4% increase is not significant and if our average selling price is Rs. 300 that translates to about Rs. 10-Rs. 12 additional MRP" — *management, on an earnings call*
