# Uno Minda (UNOMINDA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Uno Minda (formerly Minda Industries) is an Indian auto-parts maker that designs and manufactures components sold to vehicle makers (OEMs) — switches, lamps, alloy wheels, horns, seats, sensors and controllers — for two-wheelers, passenger cars, three-wheelers and commercial vehicles, including electric models. It earns money mainly by supplying these parts in bulk to Indian and global OEMs under long-term programs, plus a growing export stream (switches, lamps and seating go to markets like the US and Europe), and it also books a share of profit from joint ventures with partners such as Denso Ten, Roki, Kosei and TG. Manufacturing is concentrated in India (Bawal, Manesar, Gujarat, Khed, Kharkhoda in Haryana) with overseas operations in Europe (a Spanish horn business, Clarton Horn, and a German lighting/innovation unit) and a plant in Indonesia. Consolidated quarterly revenue grew from about INR 2,555 crores in the June 2022 quarter to about INR 5,336 crores in the March 2026 quarter, with EBITDA margins run at roughly 11%.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Switching Systems (switches)** — Makes the switches on vehicles — handlebar switches for two-wheelers plus newer car switches for sunroofs, cruise control, power windows, pedals and vehicle stability — and is the single largest line, about 25-29% of consolidated revenue (INR 730 crores in Q1 FY23, INR 958 crores in Q1 FY25), with two-wheeler switch exports a fast-growing part.
- **Lighting** — Supplies head lamps, tail lamps and blinkers for both two-wheelers and cars, including full lighting sets for EV models and niche premium solutions from the German operation such as the 'Roof Star Liner' and illuminated logos; roughly 23-24% of revenue (INR 582 crores in Q1 FY23 rising to INR 894 crores in Q1 FY25), with new four-wheeler lighting plants in Gujarat, Khed City and Indonesia.
- **Casting (alloy wheels and aluminium die casting)** — Makes aluminium alloy wheels for two-wheelers and cars plus aluminium die-cast parts, and is about 19-20% of revenue (INR 484 crores in Q1 FY23, INR 750 crores in Q1 FY25 of which alloy wheels were INR 504 crores); it is the most capacity-hungry line, with repeated expansions at Bawal and a new 120,000-wheels-a-month greenfield plant at Kharkhoda.
- **Acoustics (horns)** — Makes vehicle horns in India and, through European subsidiary Clarton Horn, in Europe; a smaller line at about 7% of revenue (INR 172 crores in Q1 FY23), with the European arm squeezed at times by high energy costs and weak volumes.
- **Seating** — Supplies seats and seating systems, aimed heavily at the commercial vehicle segment and also exported to the US; about 7% of revenue (INR 271 crores in Q1 FY25) and the slowest-growing line, tied to CV production cycles.
- **Sensors and controllers** — Newer electronics-linked products called out by management as a strong-performing growth area alongside lighting, alloy wheels and switches, particularly relevant to electric vehicles; the calls do not give a separate revenue figure for it.
- **Joint ventures and associates** — Partnerships with names like Denso Ten, Roki, Kosei Minda Aluminum, TRMN and TG that add products and technology; they show up as share of profit rather than sales, contributing INR 37 crores of profit in Q1 FY25 versus INR 30 crores a year earlier.

### The story in depth

Uno Minda comes across as a well-run car and two-wheeler parts maker that has been growing faster than the vehicle market it sells into. The multibagger screen calls "Market Share Gainers" a strong fit, and the numbers behind it are specific: 59% growth against 37% for the industry in Q1 FY23, revenue growth of 18-20% against an already strong FY26 industry, and two-wheeler switch growth of 26-30% as it won a bigger share of each customer's business. That growth has been paid for with real new capacity — alloy wheels, lighting, switches and EV powertrain plants at Bawal, Supa, Khed, Kharkhoda and Indonesia — which is why the quality framework scores capex for growth and structural end-market growth 2 out of 2 and gives Growth an 0.83 area score. The second strong pattern, "Corporate Culture", is really the reason to trust the first: the same Chairman and Group CFO presented all 16 calls, the promised 11% (give or take half a point) margin was actually held from FY23 through FY26, and the promised plants actually got commissioned. Out of every 100 rupees of sales, 11 rupees is operating profit, and every 100 rupees of capital in the business threw off 18 rupees of operating cash.

The interesting part is that several of the traits producing those strengths are the very same traits producing the risks. Growth in content per vehicle — LED lighting, sunroofs up fivefold, alloy wheels, airbags, the AVAS mandate — is what makes the end-market check score full marks, but the risk review's "Shifting Consumer Preferences" entry shows the same lever running backwards: by May 2026 customers had moved toward entry-level models and some programmes reverted to steel wheels, which is exactly what softened alloy wheels. Similarly, the heavy capital intensity that helps the "Cost to Replicate" barrier is also what makes the cyclicality risk uncomfortable, because the 764 crore alloy wheel plant and 550 crore EV powertrain plant are being committed at a record industry peak of 34.7 million vehicles. And the web of joint ventures and technology licences with Aisin, Inovance, Denso Ten, TG, ROKI and others is counted as a barrier under "Cost to Replicate" and as the source of new products under "Innovation Dominance", yet it is also why the technology risk and the stakeholder concentration risk are both flagged: the company buys in the important new technology rather than owning it, and 74 crore of JV profit in Q3 FY26 sat with a handful of partners, while results are repeatedly credited to unnamed single customers.

What holds the overall grade to 64 out of 100, or Decent, is the thin middle of the story: the company is good at growing but has limited ability to charge more. Competitive Advantage scores zero and Industry Structure 0.33, and the screen finds no fit at all on "Pricing Power" or "Recurring Revenue" — price changes are cost pass-throughs negotiated with the customer, operating margin went from 10.4% in Q1 FY23 to 11.1-11.3% in FY26 and essentially went nowhere, and 93% of revenue is one-off component sales to vehicle makers with the aftermarket only 7-9%. That flatness is described positively in one place (steady margins, costs under control, rational pricing with few new entrants) and negatively in another (the "Good Enough Goods" risk, where sophisticated buyers control the specification and can trade down, as they did on wheels). Six of the eight risk channels sit at WATCH, only fashion risk is low, and on new entrants the report is careful to say the evidence is silent rather than reassuring. The risk review still concludes the business is SOUND, and fairly so — the products are functional, fitted for the life of a vehicle model, spread across switches, lighting, castings, seating and acoustics, and mostly indifferent to whether the vehicle runs on petrol or a battery. But the earnings come from volume, capacity and richer kit per vehicle, not from pricing, so the story only works while vehicle production and content per vehicle keep climbing.
