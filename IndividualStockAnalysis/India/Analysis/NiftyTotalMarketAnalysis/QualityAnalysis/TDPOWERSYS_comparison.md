### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 4 weakened, 7 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Order inflow grew 51% in FY26 (exports +76%) and pending orders 'more than doubled in the past 24 months', far above the 10-12% market growth management repeatedly cites for steam turbines, implying wallet-share gains with global OEMs — but management explicitly refuses market-wise or customer-wise breakups and gives no share numbers versus named competitors. (The long view had said: Persistent, quantified share gain over the whole period: order inflow rose from a record INR2.97bn in Q1FY25 to INR6.66bn in Q4FY26 (+61% year-on-year), FY26 inflow INR22.38bn vs INR14.78bn (+51%), export inflow +76%, pending generator/motor orders 'more than doubled in the past 24 months', exports 79% of inflow — and it was not bought with profitability, since EBITDA rose 17.46%→18.14% and they walked away from the loss-making railway price war.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The four calls contain no cost-culture language, no itemised savings and no no-frills positioning; capex talk is about automation, lean manufacturing and added capacity rather than a replicable cost advantage passed to price. (The long view had said: The core proposition is Indian cost base plus quality for global OEMs, with repeated operating-leverage language — Aug 2022 'increasing our top line without having a proportional increase in the employee cost', continued automation/lean and debottlenecking capex of INR50cr+INR50cr, and Q4FY26 employee expenses flat while output rose to INR590cr. But there is no itemised cost-savings culture or explicit price-led share strategy; margins rose rather than being passed to price.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No heritage story, A&P spend, distributor leverage or brand-adjacency launches appear anywhere in the FY26 calls — the customer set is industrial OEMs, and the evidence is simply silent on brand. (The long view had said: The asset is an installed-base track record rather than a consumer brand — 5,400+ generators in 100 countries in Aug 2022 rising to 6,419 by Aug 2024, with export orders explicitly 'providing great references for us to build our business for the future' and a new marquee US gas-turbine customer in FY26. No heritage story, A&P spend, or distribution leverage is ever discussed.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No R&D spend, pipeline or named new-product revenue is disclosed in any of the four calls; the forward-looking investment discussed is manufacturing capability (rotor machining, 2-pole generators, motors) rather than an innovation engine funded by high gross margins. (The long view had said: Capability extension is regular and disclosed — in-house rotor manufacture (training in Germany, Aug 2022), hazardous-location and synchronous motors, 2-pole generator investment for FY28, and machines up to 200MW for SMR/combined-cycle — but R&D spend is never quantified, no share-of-industry-R&D claim is made, and several development bets (synchronous motors, third gas-engine OEM) failed to convert.)

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The growth engine is a small set of unnamed European/US 'prime mover partners' — engine and turbine OEMs who package TDPS generators — and management builds guidance directly off their forecasts while explicitly refusing to disclose customer- or market-wise splits, which is where the real dependency would show. Railways similarly hinges on the Alstom India order plus three single-customer qualification programmes. (The long view had said: TDPS sells almost entirely through a handful of prime-mover OEMs — management says there are only a limited number of engine/turbine makers — and the risk has already bitten twice: the railway business was tied to a single OEM ('we are currently focused on this one OEM') which then lost the loco and Vande Bharat tenders, stalling that vertical for years, and an attempt at a third gas-engine OEM failed because the customer stayed with its incumbent supplier; today's growth and the new capex plan rest on forecasts handed down by two engine customers and a couple of turbine customers.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: TDPS sells generators that go into customers' new power-generation capacity — gas engine/gas turbine gensets for AI data centres, captive coal/biomass plants, hydro projects — so revenue is a direct function of third-party capex cycles, and the last four calls describe an unmistakable peak: order inflow +51% for FY26, quarterly inflow hitting record INR 6.66bn, guidance raised three times (INR 15bn → 18bn → 22bn → 24bn), and a third plant commissioned with a further heavy rotor/machining capex announced in May 2026 for machines up to 200MW. Aftermarket/spares are negligible (INR 0.2bn of a INR 19.7bn book), so there is almost no flow-product ballast if the buildout pauses; the guidance and capacity plans are being set off forecasts management itself admits could be only '80% correct'. (The long view had said: TDPS sells generators that go into customers' capital projects — captive power plants, waste-heat recovery, hydro schemes, and now data-centre gensets — so revenue is a direct function of others' capex; management itself frames the domestic large-generator market as reviving 'due to CAPEX taking place in the metal sector' and conditions it on the cycle continuing, while the FY24–FY26 boom (order inflow +51% in FY26, sales from ~INR 840cr to ~INR 1,737cr, third plant plus a fresh large-generator capex round) is textbook expansionary over-investment at a visible peak, compounded by copper/special-steel input swings.)
- **Fashion Risk**: NO SIGNAL → LOW. Why now: Demand is functional industrial capital equipment ordered against multi-year power projects, not taste-driven; there is no trending-product or celebrity-moment framing. The nearest analogue is the AI data-centre theme, and management pre-empts it by pointing to non-data-centre drivers and calling the demand real. (The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and all 2 computed fingerprints are clean.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
