## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 29 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 10 improved, 1 regressed, 18 unchanged. 2 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them). 2 were newly assessable in the recent view.

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |

| Return on Capital | Weak (-1.00) | Neutral (-0.33) | +0.67 |
| Growth | Good (+0.83) | Good (+0.83) | +0.00 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Good. Now: IDL Explosives (INR107 crores, 100%) is genuinely complementary — it lets Apollo make the MIGM warhead in-house and brought TNT/RDX licences — but a further ADIPL acquisition is already queued and the deals are not small relative to the company, so the bolt-on discipline is unproven. | The long view had said: IDL Explosives was a INR107cr 100% buy of a weak, seven-plant business needing a 'complete overhaul' with outside consultants and it dilutes consolidated PAT margins, and management is queuing three more deals — the opposite of small bolt-ons of already-strong assets.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Barriers are high and formal — lifetime DPIIT licence, TNT/RDX industrial licence, DRDO qualification and multi-year trials — but the Indian defence space is attracting many new entrants and lead integrators, which management itself acknowledges. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.1 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Barriers are genuinely high (DPIIT lifetime licence, TNT/RDX industrial licence, program qualification), but the government is actively inviting deep-pocketed new entrants such as Adani, Bharat Forge and Economic Explosives into the same value chain.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Good → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹24 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹24 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±2.1 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Clear runway plus demonstrated capture — order book grew from INR735 crores (Jun-25) to INR1,432 crores (Mar-26), and management claims 63% subsystem participation across DRDO missile programs while moving up to full weapon-system/OEM status. | The long view had said: Clear share capture in indigenous DRDO programs (claimed 63% of missile subsystems, participation in every indigenous missile programme) with genuine runway in land systems, explosives and exports where presence has been 'historically limited'.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Defence qualification creates near-absolute switching costs — once designed into a missile program and formalised via ToT/DCPP agreements with DRDO, the subsystem cannot practically be re-sourced. | The long view had said: Once qualified into a program's approved supply chain and appointed a DcPP partner, replacement requires re-qualification and re-trials — a real switching cost, evidenced by integrators returning to approved suppliers.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: 41 years building indigenous core technology rather than licence-assembly, deliberately choosing the harder route to platform ownership and publishing a 10-year Vision 2036 with concrete pillars. | The long view had said: A 40-year owner-led record of self-funded indigenous technology development, now formalised into a Vision 2036 to become a global OEM, with insistence on owning 100% indigenous technology rather than licensed integration.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: The entire proposition is certified reliability for mission-critical weapons — customers buy through years of lab tests, user trials and DRDO validation, where failure cost dwarfs price. | The long view had said: The core customer proposition is qualified reliability in mission-critical weapon subsystems — 40 years of trials, testing and program qualification — where trust and approval, not price, determine selection.
- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Poor → Weak. Now: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹0.38 of sales — a fairly asset-heavy business. And from the calls: The business is structurally asset- and working-capital-heavy: a 359-day working capital cycle even after improving from 626 days, with heavy ongoing greenfield capex — moat is real but asset turn is not. | The long view had said: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹0.38 of sales — a fairly asset-heavy business. And from the calls: Structurally asset- and working-capital-heavy: a 359-day working capital cycle (improved from 626 days but still extreme), receivables of ~INR360cr equal to two quarters of sales, rising inventory and heavy plant investment.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: Growth capex is large, on-schedule and multi-phase (Unit 3 Phase 1 complete, Phase 2 civil started, greenfield Telangana), and incremental returns are visibly improving with ROCE up from 10% in FY21 to 18% in FY26 alongside 61% FY26 revenue growth. | The long view had said: Large, clearly growth-oriented capex — Unit 2, Unit 3 phases (INR150cr with INR110cr SBI term loan), plus 2.5 and 5.6 additional acres taking capacity to ~8x and eventually 12-13x — but returns are only just improving (ROCE 10%→18%, ROE 11%) and it is substantially debt-funded, so incremental ROIC is not yet proven high.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: Mixed: a credit rating upgraded two notches and organic guidance deliberately excluding acquisitions, offset by back-to-back acquisitions and equity dilution funding the expansion. | The long view had said: Stewardship is expansionary rather than prudent: serial acquisitions (IDL plus three more in due diligence), INR75cr and INR110cr term loans, repeated equity raises and a promoter pledge only now being unwound.

- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Growth is overwhelmingly volume/order-execution led, though margin expansion (EBITDA 22%→28% H1, PAT margin to 16% standalone) does come from a better product mix as systems move into serial production rather than from pricing. | The long view had said: Margin expansion (EBITDA 19%→24%, PAT 5%→12%) is attributed to mix shifting from low-margin development work into serial production rather than to price, though the topline itself is order-execution volume.

*Unchanged: Halo effect (caution); Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Mini-monopolies; Cyclical market growth; Technology advantage; People matter (talent development); Distribution to shareholders; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Structural end-market growth; Intangible benefits; Geographical expansion; Candor; Cost of working capital; Research and development; Share donators; Return on capital (headline); Out of limelight; Security by obscurity.*

**Newly assessable in the recent view:**

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 5 weakened, 4 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Founder-led with stable leadership (MD Karunakar Reddy and CFO Sudarshan through FY21-FY26), guidance repeated across all four calls (45-50% CAGR, 15% standalone PAT target) and beaten (61% growth, 16% PAT), ROCE explicitly tracked (10%→18%), heavy organic reinvestment in R&D/capex and an explicit transparency commitment — though repeated 'on track, no delay' language on MIGM/mine orders that kept slipping is a mild optimism flag. (The long view had said: Promises made in earlier calls were largely delivered — the 45-50% growth guidance repeated since Feb 2025 was beaten (61% in FY26), the 15% standalone PAT target set in Feb 2026 came in at 16%, ROCE is measured and disclosed (10%→18%), leadership is long-tenured family/promoter-led, and May 2026 openly commits to reporting setbacks; against that, promoter pledge was still ~35% in Nov 2025 despite a zero-by-FY26 promise, and the Nov 2025 pledge to cut receivables 'significantly' by Q4 was met in May 2026 with a deflection that rising inventory and receivables are 'nothing to be looked down about'.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: A newcomer with money still could not quickly replicate 40+ years of DRDO design-in across 80-90 missile programmes, DCPP partner status, ToT agreements, an explosives industrial licence for TNT/RDX and a lifetime DPIIT licence spanning torpedoes, mines, missiles and munitions — these are multi-year qualification and regulatory barriers, not purchasable assets. (The long view had said: There is a concrete replication barrier — 41 years of DRDO programme qualification, approved-supply-chain and DcPP production-partner status, ToTs, and a lifetime DPIIT licence covering torpedoes, mines, missiles and loitering munition, none of which money alone buys quickly; but management itself concedes deep-pocketed entrants (Adani, Bharat Forge, Economic Explosives) are being handed DRDO ToTs and that Apollo 'caught up a little bit late' versus lead integrators, so the barrier protects the subsystem franchise more than the OEM ambition.)

**That weakened:**

- **Toll Roads**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management claims Apollo supplies 63% of subsystems across 80-90 DRDO missile programmes — small-value electronics critical to the platform, locked in by qualification, DCPP partner status and ToT agreements — but the calls never state the cost share or that customers pay without negotiating. (The long view had said: Apollo supplies small-value but mission-critical subsystems (LRUs, seekers, fuzes, INS) that are a tiny share of a missile's cost yet cannot be swapped once qualified; the qualification/certification anchoring is repeated across all six calls — participation in every indigenous DRDO missile programme (Feb 2025), 63% of subsystems across 80-90 programmes (Feb 2026), DcPP production partner status and a lifetime DPIIT licence (May 2026).)
- **Forward Integrators**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Apollo is moving up toward the end customer — subsystem maker to full weapon-system/platform OEM, plus its first direct export order and buying IDL Explosives to own the warhead — but this is vertical/platform integration, not own stores, franchising or D2C, and the OEM position is not yet achieved. (The long view had said: Forward integration is the explicit, repeatedly delivered strategy: Unit 3 (IPiDS) built for weapon integration (Feb 2025), the IDL Explosives acquisition described as both backward and forward integration (Nov 2025), and the stated shift from tier-2 subsystem supplier to full weapon-system OEM selling directly to MoD/Navy and export customers (May 2026) — though it is vertical integration toward the end customer, not the retail/franchise variant.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Revenue grew 61% in FY26 to INR904 crores with the order book rising from INR735 crores (Jun-25) to INR1,432 crores (Mar-26) and a claimed 63% subsystem presence across 80-90 programmes, but no verified market-share series versus named competitors is given and much of the gain reflects a rising defence budget rather than share capture. (The long view had said: Share gain is claimed and quantified with increasing specificity across the years — 'increasing our market share in indigenous DRDO programs' (Feb 2025) to '63% of subsystems' across 80-90 programmes (Feb 2026) — and is corroborated by delivery: revenue up 61% to INR904 crores in FY26 on a 30% five-year CAGR, plus first export order and entry into land systems; the caveat is that growth has been funded by dilution, term loans and a stretched working capital cycle rather than internal accruals.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Apollo is designed in during DRDO's development phase and is then ordered by lead integrators BEL/BDL while the MoD pays — a specifier-vs-payer split with reputation-based selection — but the calls show no professional-training or channel-relationship programme, and defence procurement is competitively priced rather than price-insensitive. (The long view had said: There is a genuine expert-gatekeeper structure — DRDO develops and qualifies, lead integrators (BDL, Adani, Bharat Forge) take ToT and must buy from the approved supply chain, while MoD/Navy pays — but management never describes training programmes or relationship investment in a professional channel, and the 'recommender' is a state agency rather than a trade professional.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No call discusses price increases, premiumisation or elasticity; margin expansion is consistently attributed to operating leverage, product mix and cost efficiency rather than pricing. (The long view had said: Price increases, elasticity, premiumisation or waiting lists are never discussed; margin expansion (EBITDA 19%→24% FY21-FY26) is attributed entirely to product mix shifting from development to serial production, not to pricing.)

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
