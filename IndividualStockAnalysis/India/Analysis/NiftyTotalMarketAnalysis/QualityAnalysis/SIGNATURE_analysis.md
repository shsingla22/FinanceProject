# SignatureGlobal (SIGNATURE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Signature Global is an Indian residential real-estate developer that buys land, builds housing projects on it, and sells the flats and floors to individual home buyers. Its focus is 'aspirational value homes' — originally affordable housing, and increasingly mid-income and premium homes — sold mainly to end-users such as working professionals and families rather than investors. It makes money by pre-selling units (booking sales before and during construction) and collecting instalments from buyers as construction progresses; management tracks the business through pre-sales value, price realisation per square foot, collections, and the surplus cash left after construction, taxes and selling costs. It operates almost entirely in one geography — Gurugram and nearby Delhi-NCR micro-markets such as the Dwarka Expressway, the Southern Peripheral Road (Sector 71, 37D, 84), Sohna and Manesar — describing itself as a product- and geography-focused platform.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Mid-income housing** — Apartments and low-rise floors priced for middle-income buyers, which management repeatedly calls the core of the strategy — it drove the bulk of sales from FY24 onward, pushed average realisation from about INR9,800/sq ft in H1 FY24 to over INR15,000/sq ft by FY26, and is credited for the higher gross margins (40% in Q3 FY26).
- **Affordable housing** — The company's original segment of lower-priced homes (eight of these projects are IGBC Gold-rated); still referenced as part of the 'value homes' identity, but the calls show sales shifting decisively toward mid-income and premium product, with average ticket sizes rising to roughly INR2.5 crore (FY25) and INR3.5–3.8 crore (FY26).
- **Premium / group housing and township projects** — Larger group-housing launches such as Titanium SPR, Twin Towers DXP, Cloverdale and Sarvam inside Gurugram, plus township projects outside Gurgaon like Daxin Vistas (Sohna) and City of Colors (Manesar); Gurgaon-heavy mix in FY26 is what management says lifted realisations ~20% year on year.
- **Land acquisition and business development** — Buying land parcels outright, converting joint-development (JDA/collaboration) arrangements into owned land, and paying for approvals — funded from operating surplus (e.g. INR5.7 billion of the INR12 billion surplus in 9M FY25) and adding developable area such as ~3 mn sq ft in Sector 37D, 2.7 mn sq ft in Sector 71 and 2.3 mn sq ft in Sohna.
- **Construction and project delivery** — Actually building and handing over the projects — about 6 mn sq ft delivered by late 2023 rising to roughly 13.5 mn sq ft completed by early 2025, with a further ~11 mn sq ft ongoing; construction absorbs the largest slice of collections (about 47% including taxes in H1 FY26).
- **Financing and balance-sheet management** — Raising and repaying debt to fund the land-and-build cycle — surplus cash is split between land, net-debt reduction and debt servicing, and in FY26 the company raised INR8.75 billion (about $100 million) of non-convertible debentures placed with the IFC (World Bank arm) for ESG-aligned mid-income housing and debt reduction.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

SignatureGlobal's story starts with price. Average realisation climbed from about ₹4,000 per square foot in FY21 to ₹7,425 in FY23, ₹9,800 in H1FY24, ₹11,700 in FY24, ₹12,700 in 9MFY25 and ₹15,200 in 9MFY26 — up 21% in the latest year — while presales compounded at roughly 60% a year over three years to ₹128bn on a trailing basis. That combination of rising prices and rising sales is why the multibagger screen scores Pricing Power as its one STRONG FIT, and why Market Share Gainers rates a LIKELY FIT: the company describes itself as one of the few large creators of new supply in its Gurugram micro-markets, with an 8 million square foot launch pipeline and dominant supply in Sector 71, in a fragmented NCR market that leaves room to keep taking share. The quality framework backs the same picture from a different angle: top marks for gaining market share, for growth being led by price and mix rather than by simply selling more units (units sold actually fell from around 3,500), and for the delivery certainty the company sells on — 16 million square feet already handed over, tier-1 contractors such as Ahluwalia, Capacit'e and ACC, a Bain engagement on on-time completion, and ISO, IGBC and EDGE certification. Management also earns full marks for candour, volunteering guidance misses, weather-hit completions, an unsold-inventory breakdown and doubts about order-book quality, and for stewardship: net debt held around ₹10bn for two to three years through that 60% sales growth, land funded out of operating surplus, and no large acquisitions.

The trouble is that the very traits driving the patterns also drive the biggest risks. The price escalation that makes Pricing Power a strong fit is the same escalation the risk review reads as Cyclicality — rated HIGH RISK — because this is build-to-sell development concentrated in a single boom market, and management itself has called the 2023-25 phase a euphoria. Government Dependency is the other HIGH RISK: the company's origins are in policy-driven affordable housing, and its own opening remarks anchor on Union Budget allocations (PMAY ₹54,832cr, SWAMIH 2.0), RBI rate cuts, RERA, TOD and FAR entitlements and land licences. The partial offset — moving from price-capped affordable housing to market-priced mid-income and premium product — is the same mix shift that produced the higher realisations, so the pivot that improved pricing also increased exposure to a cyclical, non-subsidised buyer. Meanwhile the moat looks thinner than the growth does. Brand Strength, Low Price Plus, Corporate Culture and Cost to Replicate all come out only PARTIAL, and Friendly Middleman and Forward Integrators are PARTIAL mainly because the calls never develop a channel story at all — brokerage appears only as a cost line. The risk review flags Good Enough Goods as a WATCH for a directly related reason: mid-income housing is not a complex product, and management defends its position by staying price-competitive — launching Cloverdale and Sarvam at what it calls fairly competitive pricing and shrinking unit sizes to protect ticket size — rather than by leaning on pricing power. New Entrants is also a WATCH, with NCR launches more than doubling year-on-year in the December 2025 quarter and Gurugram accounting for nearly half. The finite prime land — roughly 92 contiguous owned acres in Sector 71 plus a 21 million square foot approved-stage land bank — is the one concrete barrier, but land, approvals and contractors are things others can also assemble. That is why Competitive Advantage scores negative (-0.33) even as Customer Benefits scores +1.5.

Underneath the growth, the economics are weak and that is what caps the rating. Return on Capital is the worst area at -1.67: every ₹100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings generated just ₹3 of operating cash this year, every ₹100 of sales produced ₹-2 of operating profit, and margins have swung by around 36 points a year, pointing to key costs outside management's control. Asset turn is low at ₹0.15 of sales per ₹1 of assets — the deliberate choice to churn land into launches quickly instead of land-banking helps, but the model stays capital-heavy. Growth is fast at roughly 49% a year on average yet lumpy, so Persistence of Growth scores -2, which is the same volatility the cyclicality risk describes, seen through the income statement. There is also no annuity to cushion the swings: Recurring Revenue is a NO FIT because revenue is booked on project completion with customer advances arising only from construction-linked payment plans, and Toll Roads is a NO FIT because at an average ticket of ₹3.5 crore a home is the buyer's single largest outlay, not a trivial input — which is exactly why management keeps re-engineering unit sizes down. What genuinely cushions the business is balance-sheet conservatism rather than earnings power: construction-linked rather than back-ended payment plans, roughly 35-40% operating surplus on collections, fast land-to-launch churn and near-zero net debt, alongside genuinely low-rated risks in technology disruption, stakeholder concentration, shifting consumer preferences and fashion. Netting it out, the framework scores business quality 52, multibagger fit 41 and risk safety 44, weighted to 47 out of 100 — a Mixed, two-star verdict with resilience described as STRAINED. In plain terms: a real share-gainer with a credible delivery brand and an honest, prudent management team, riding a price cycle it does not control, on capital returns that have not yet shown up.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 3 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Build-to-sell residential development in a single boom market: realization rose from ~INR4,000/sqft (FY21) to INR9,800 (H1FY24) to INR15,200 (9M FY26), presales CAGR ~60% over three years, and management itself calls the 2023-25 phase a euphoria that has since normalised to 50-60% sell-through in six months; GP margins have swung (26.7% FY22, 36% H1FY24, 27% 9M FY25, 31-40% 9M FY26) and the company is simultaneously committing to ~42mn sqft of land-stage/recently launched inventory and single-shot construction of 8mn sqft of launches at the top of a price cycle.

> "there was kind of a euphoria in the market. And it was very inordinate for something like real estate that on the date of launch, there were multiple subscriptions happening" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Construction-linked payment plans rather than back-ended ones, ~35-40% operating surplus on collections, fast land-to-launch churn, and near-zero net debt (INR10bn against INR650bn+ GDV) which cushions a downturn*

- ⚠️ Year-to-year sales growth swings by ±405 percentage points — boom-and-bust revenue, not a steady compounder.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from -99% to 2% (swinging ±36.3 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 65% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 3% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Fortunes are repeatedly framed in terms of government action - the company's origins in policy-driven affordable housing, opening remarks anchored on Union Budget allocations (PMAY INR54,832cr, SWAMIH 2.0), RBI repo cuts, RERA, TOD/FAR entitlements, land licensing and approvals spend (INR0.7bn in 9M FY26), while the Delhi growth option is explicitly contingent on a new policy framework and NCR pollution bans directly cut completions and customer footfall in Q3 FY26.

> "with today, BJP government both in center and at the state level, the policy framework should enable development within Delhi" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate shift from price-capped affordable housing to market-priced mid-income and premium product, which removes regulator-set pricing from the bulk of revenue*
