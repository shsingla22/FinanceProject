# Adani Enterp. (ADANIENT) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Adani Enterprises (AEL) is an Indian holding-and-incubator company: it builds capital-heavy infrastructure and resource businesses from scratch, scales them, and then spins them off as separate listed companies — management says this playbook already produced Adani Ports, Adani Transmission, Adani Green Energy, Adani Total Gas and Adani Wilmar, at a 36% compound annual return to shareholders. Its money today comes from three kinds of activity: selling physical services and products (running airports and toll/annuity roads, contract-mining coal and iron ore for mine owners, manufacturing solar modules and wind turbines, refining copper), trading imported coal and other raw materials to Indian industrial and public-sector buyers, and mining its own coal in Australia and India. Customers are mostly other businesses and government bodies — power producers, cement and steel makers, state mining companies, airlines and highway authorities — plus airport passengers and retail buyers of solar equipment. In FY26 the group reported total income of about INR 1,02,943 crores and EBITDA of INR 16,464 crores, with roughly 80% of EBITDA now coming from core infrastructure and services, and management says the next step is 'value unlock' by demerging the big units.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Airports (Adani Airport Holdings)** — Operates 8 Indian airports — earning regulated 'aeronautical' fees on a regulatory asset base at a 12%–14% allowed return plus faster-growing non-aero income from retail, cargo, ground handling and advertising; it handled 95.3 million passengers and INR 5,394 crores of EBITDA in FY26 (about a third of group EBITDA), carries ~23% of India's passenger traffic and 29% of air cargo, and just opened the greenfield Navi Mumbai airport.
- **Adani New Industries Limited (ANIL) — green hydrogen ecosystem** — Manufactures and sells solar modules, cells, ingots and wafers plus wind turbines, and is building toward green hydrogen and downstream products like ammonia, urea and methanol; module sales run over 1 GW a quarter, Adani Solar is called a world top-10 solar manufacturer with 15+ GW cumulative shipments, and the division grew from INR 8,741 crores revenue / INR 2,296 crores EBITDA in FY24 with capacity now expanding about 2.5x.
- **Integrated Resource Management (IRM)** — Buys and resells coal and other natural resources to a mix of retail and public-sector industrial customers, including through its own online trading portal; management calls it India's number-one player, and in FY26 it moved 44.6 million tonnes for INR 29,112 crores of revenue and INR 2,767 crores of EBITDA — the largest revenue line but a thin-margin one.
- **Mining Services (MDO — Mine Developer and Operator)** — Gets paid a per-tonne fee to develop and run mines owned by others, handling everything from permits and land acquisition to digging, washing and transporting the coal or iron ore; it holds 18 MDO contracts with 145 million tonnes of peak annual capacity but only 7 are operational (about a third of the potential), delivering 49.4 million tonnes, INR 4,536 crores revenue and INR 1,986 crores EBITDA in FY26.
- **Commercial Mining** — Mines coal it owns itself — chiefly the Carmichael mine in Australia (11.2 million tonnes produced and shipped in FY24) — plus domestic coal and bauxite blocks still in development; earnings here swing with weather and currency, with a rain event and a non-cash forex mark-to-market knocking roughly INR 900 crores off FY26.
- **Roads (Adani Road Transport)** — Builds and operates highways under BOT/HAM concessions, earning tolls or government annuities; the portfolio reached 9–10 projects and is headlined by the newly completed Ganga Expressway, an ~INR 18,000 crores toll-risk asset with a 27-year concession that management expects to add meaningfully to revenue and EBITDA.
- **Metals / Primary industry incubation (Kutch Copper, PVC)** — A greenfield 0.5 million tonne copper refinery at Mundra that shipped its first output in March 2024, alongside a planned PVC plant; still ramping, and grouped with Navi Mumbai airport and Ganga Expressway as the three assets expected to add over INR 3,000 crores of EBITDA once stabilised.
- **Data centres (AdaniConneX)** — A full-service data centre business, described as a smaller incubating line — around INR 5,000 crores of the FY25 capex plan — that recently signed a 358 MW hyperscale order with roughly a 40-month build timeline.
- **Aerospace, Technology & Defence** — A newly forming business with announced tie-ups with Embraer for regional civilian transport aircraft and Leonardo for a helicopter ecosystem in India; management declined to give revenue or investment figures and said it will only start reporting it as a separate segment from the second half of FY27.
- **Adani Capability Center (GCC)** — An in-house global capability centre set up within AEL to provide shared corporate and technology services, including artificial intelligence work and a shift to what management calls an agentic workforce model; no financial figures were given in the calls.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Every check, area by area

**Capital Allocation — Good (+0.60)** · 5 of 6 checks had evidence

- **Cost of working capital — Excellent** *(from the financial statements)*: Cash comes back 13 days BEFORE suppliers need to be paid — customers effectively fund the business (negative working capital, a rare strength).
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance) — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: Capex is overwhelmingly growth-oriented and enormous (₹85,000cr FY23-24, ₹80,000cr FY25, ~₹40,000cr FY27) and has demonstrably converted into operating assets (Navi Mumbai, Ganga Expressway, Kutch Copper), but returns are regulated/moderate (12-14% RoA on airport RAB) rather than high incremental ROIC.
  > "we are looking at a CapEx of about INR 80,000 crores in FY25, out of which major part of CapEx will go in ANIL and Airports" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Merger and acquisition — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: M&A over the period is limited to small complementary bolt-ons around the airport platform (ground handling, airport media) rather than large transformative deals, though no post-deal margin improvement is quantified.
  > "Adani Airports acquired 100% stake in AGHPort Aviation Services... full operational control" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Research and development — Neutral** *(from the conference calls)*: Product development exists (own-blade wind prototype certified by WindGuard, first Indian ingot/wafer line, in-house electrolyzer testing) but no R&D spend, intensity or efficiency is ever disclosed and the solar/wind products remain commodity with compressing margins.
  > "We have received the provisional type certification from WindGuard Germany for prototype 2 using our own ANIL's blades" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Distribution to shareholders — Weak** *(from the conference calls)*: There is no buyback or dividend policy discussed at all across five years; the capital flow is consistently the opposite — repeated dilution via the ₹7,700cr IHC placement and the ₹24,930cr rights issue plus NCDs.
  > "we completed the rights issue raising INR24,930 crores" — *management, on an earnings call*

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

**Overall:** the company partly passes the foundation every multibagger shares (steady cash + high returns on capital + growth), 1 of 3 testable checks passing, and fits 1 of the 11 patterns: Cost to Replicate.

### Cost to Replicate — STRONG FIT
*"Could a newcomer copy this?"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** The infra core passes the replication test on finite-location and concession grounds that money alone cannot undo: 8 airports including the only second airport for Mumbai, 27-year expressway concessions, 18 long-term MDO agreements with 145 MMTPA of peak capacity, and regulated asset bases — a well-funded newcomer cannot recreate these because the assets and concessions are singular and already awarded.

> "We have completed India's largest greenfield Ganga Expressway project in a record time of less than 3.5 years. It's a long-term asset with a concession period of 27 years." — *management, on an earnings call*

### Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) — PARTIAL
*"Sells once, earns forever"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (2 of 3 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Long-dated contracted annuity streams do recur — 18 MDO service agreements, 27-year road concessions, data-centre order book billed as capacity comes online — but across all 15 calls there is no attach rate, renewal rate, AMC share or deferred-revenue-as-funding discussion, and the business is capital-hungry (INR 40,000-80,000 crore annual capex) rather than customer-funded.

> "we have a portfolio of 18 MDO service agreements with total peak capacity of 145 MMTPA" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Customers effectively fund the business — cash arrives 13 days before suppliers are paid.
- ❌ Capital spending is about 33% of sales — growth is capital-hungry.
- ✅ Operating cash was positive in 12 of 12 years — the reliable cash engine recurring-revenue businesses show.

### Toll Roads — PARTIAL
*"Small cost, can't skip it"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 2 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Airports and roads are literal toll-type assets with regulator-anchored returns that customers cannot negotiate away, and management repeatedly frames them as regulatory asset bases with an assured 12-14% return; but this is concession/regulatory protection, not the niche 'magic ingredient' supplier economics, and the largest EBITDA line (IRM coal trading) is a price-taking commodity business.

> "It's a regulatory asset base provisionally in the high teens and the expected rate of return on assets of this type is in the range of 12% to 14%" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Operating margin 14%, swings of ±5.0 points, median return on capital 8% — good but not the near-monopoly profile of a toll road.
- ❌ Operating margin of 14% — too thin for a protected franchise.

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 1 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** The entire ANIL thesis is scale-plus-vertical-integration cost leadership — 'cheapest electron' to 'cheapest green hydrogen', polysilicon-to-module integration for minimal scale economics, top-10 global solar on vertical integration and high utilisation — but there is no itemised cost culture on the calls and returns are being carried by regulated infra, not by high asset turns.

> "produce the world's least expensive electrons which will drive our ability to produce the world's least expensive green hydrogen" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹46 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 0.4× a year — not the high-turnover, low-margin machine this pattern needs.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Most of the EBITDA is either priced by a regulator or awarded by government: airports earn a regulated 12-14% return on a regulatory asset base with tariff revisions driving FY26 EBITDA up 55%; roads are HAM/BOT concessions (Ganga Expressway, 27-year concession); mining is MDO service contracts on government-allotted coal blocks plus commercial-mine auctions; solar leans on PLI/ALMM-type domestic content rules and airport growth depends on the government's privatisation pipeline.

> "Airport is a regulatory asset, so there is no question of any losses... take the regulatory asset base... the weighted average rate of return is expected to be around 12% to 14%" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Diversification across many policy regimes (aviation, roads, mining, metals, data centres) and a stated stance that green hydrogen economics stand alone: 'We are not doing this business for PLI scheme'; growing non-aero revenue (+31%) is commercially, not tariff, determined.*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across all 15 calls the earnings base swings with commodity and capex cycles: IRM coal trading EBITDA moved from INR5,173cr (FY24) to INR2,767cr (FY26) and management concedes structural volatility; commercial mining took a weather/mark-to-market hit; copper economics are framed off global copper prices; and solar/wind sales are equipment sold into customers' renewable capex programmes.

> "there is an inherent , it's a profitable business by itself, but there is an inherent variation, fluctuation in that business by the very nature" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Growing share of flow-type, operations-linked EBITDA — airports (regulated tariffs, 95.3mn pax), long-term MDO service contracts and toll roads now ~80% of EBITDA, which management says will shrink 'the variability itself as a percentage'; heavy countercyclical capex (~INR40,000cr/yr) continues through downturns.*
