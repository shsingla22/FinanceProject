# Adani Enterp. (ADANIENT) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Adani Enterprises (AEL) is an Indian holding-and-incubator company: it builds large infrastructure and resource businesses from scratch, scales them up, and then spins them off as separate listed companies — management says this playbook already produced Adani Ports, Adani Transmission, Adani Green Energy, Adani Total Gas and Adani Wilmar, and is described as 'build, stabilize, scale and unlock'. Its money today comes from two kinds of activity: older 'established' businesses that sell services and commodities — running coal and iron-ore mines under contract for their owners, and importing/trading coal and other natural resources for Indian industrial, retail and public-sector customers — and newer 'incubating' infrastructure it owns and operates, mainly airports, toll/annuity roads, solar-and-wind equipment manufacturing, a copper smelter and data centres. Almost all operations are in India (Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha, Maharashtra, Gujarat/Mundra, plus 8 airports), with one coal mine in Australia (Carmichael). For FY26 the whole company reported total income of about INR 1,02,943 crores and EBITDA of about INR 16,464 crores, with roughly 80% of EBITDA now coming from core infrastructure and services.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Airports (Adani Airport Holdings)** — Operates 8 Indian airports — including Mumbai and the new greenfield Navi Mumbai airport opened in December 2025 — earning regulated 'aero' charges plus retail, cargo, ground-handling and advertising income; it is now the single biggest earner, with FY26 income of INR 13,081 crores, EBITDA of INR 5,394 crores, 95.3 million passengers, and about 23% of India's passenger traffic and 29% of its air cargo.
- **Adani New Industries Ltd (ANIL) / green hydrogen ecosystem** — Makes and sells solar modules (plus its own ingots, wafers and cells) and wind turbines, and is building toward green hydrogen and downstream products like ammonia and methanol; module sales run over 1 GW a quarter, Adani Solar is called a top-10 global solar manufacturer with 15+ GW cumulative shipments, and the announced ambition was USD 50 billion of investment over a decade with TotalEnergies taking a 25% stake.
- **Mining services (MDO — Mine Developer and Operator)** — Develops and runs coal and iron-ore mines on contract for their owners — handling approvals, land, infrastructure, mining, washing and transport to the customer's plant — with 18 contracts covering 145 million tonnes of peak annual capacity, of which only 7 are operating (about 50 MMT), producing FY26 revenue of INR 4,536 crores and EBITDA of INR 1,986 crores.
- **Integrated Resource Management (IRM)** — Buys and sells coal and other natural resources to a mix of retail and public-sector Indian customers, including through an online trading portal; management calls it India's number one player, with FY26 volume of 44.6 million tonnes, revenue of INR 29,112 crores and EBITDA of INR 2,767 crores.
- **Commercial mining** — Mines coal it owns itself, principally the Carmichael mine in Australia (11.2 million tonnes in FY24), plus domestic coal and bauxite blocks still in the project stage; FY26 earnings here fell on heavy Australian rain and a non-cash currency mark-to-market of roughly INR 600 crores.
- **Roads (Adani Road Transport)** — Builds and operates highway and expressway concessions under BOT/HAM contracts, now 9 operational projects plus the completed 1,000-plus-crore-scale Ganga Expressway — an asset of roughly INR 18,000 crores on a 27-year concession where AEL takes traffic risk.
- **Metals / primary industries (Kutch Copper, PVC)** — A new 0.5 million tonne greenfield copper refinery at Mundra that shipped its first output in March 2024, alongside a planned PVC project — both still early and yet to contribute much profit.
- **Data centres (AdaniConneX)** — Builds and runs full-service data centres, an incubating business with an INR 5,000 crore capex plan flagged for FY25 and a new 358 MW hyperscale order signed in Q4 FY26 with about a 40-month build timeline.
- **Aerospace, Technology & Defence** — A newly forming business with announced tie-ups on regional civilian transport aircraft (Embraer) and helicopters (Leonardo); management declined to give revenue or investment figures and said it will only report it as a proper segment from the second half of FY27.
- **Adani Capability Center (GCC)** — An in-house global capability centre set up within AEL for shared corporate/technology work, including artificial intelligence and a shift to an 'agentic workforce' model; no financial figures were given.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Adani Enterprises scores 44 out of 100 — a Mixed, two-star rating built from 51 on business quality, 33 on multibagger fit and 46 on risk safety — and the reason the three pieces land where they do is that one decision drives all of them. The company has deliberately turned itself into an infrastructure owner: management set out in 2019 to move the profit mix to 80% from core infrastructure (airports, roads, copper) and got there from 12%, funded by roughly ₹85,000 crore of growth capital in FY23-24, ₹80,000 crore in FY25 and about ₹40,000 crore planned for FY27. That spending is the source of the single strongest thing in the whole record — the 'Cost to Replicate' test is the only pattern rated a strong fit, because eight airport concessions, a new second Mumbai airport whose slots are already fully taken, a 27-year expressway and 18 long-term mine-operating agreements simply cannot be rebuilt by a rival. It is also the source of the two worst marks: assets produce only ₹0.39 of sales for every ₹1 invested, and every ₹100 of shareholder money and borrowings generated just ₹1 of operating cash this year. The asset base that makes the business hard to copy is the same asset base that makes it hard to earn on, which is why capital allocation scores well (0.8) while return on capital is deeply negative (-1.33).

The same doubling-back shows up in where the money comes from. Prices do rise, but they rise because a regulator revises airport tariffs and sets a 12-14% return on the regulated asset base — that mechanism alone drove a 55% jump in airport profits in FY26. So the pricing-power and toll-road patterns are recorded as regulated anchoring rather than genuine customer loyalty, and the very predictability that makes them attractive is filed on the risk side as the highest-rated danger in the review: Government Dependency, marked HIGH RISK, because airport economics, road concessions and mining contracts are all set by government bodies. The manufacturing half of the company shows the mirror image. Solar modules, copper cathode and traded coal are goods buyers pick purely on price, which is why 'Good Enough Goods' and 'New Entrants' both sit on watch — post-subsidy overcapacity means rivals cannot fill their own lines, and the same glut that lets Adani earn tolling fees on others' idle capacity is what pushed solar realisations down and margins from over 30% to under 25%. Coal trading profits fell from ₹5,173 crore in FY24 to ₹2,767 crore in FY26, which is the concrete evidence behind the ELEVATED cyclicality rating. The market-share numbers are real — about 23% of India's passengers, 29% of air cargo, the only Indian name in the global top ten in solar, domestic module sales up 96% in FY26 — but the screen notes plainly that these gains are bought with enormous capital rather than won on economics.

What is genuinely good sits outside the capital question and is not disputed anywhere in the records: customers pay 13 days before suppliers need paying, so buyers effectively fund the business; nearly every end market — air travel, roads, solar, copper for electrification, data centres, hydrogen — is in long-run expansion, confirmed by the volumes discussed on the calls; leadership has been stable across all fifteen calls and flagged bad news early, including the copper ramp-up delay a quarter ahead. Against that, the management area scores -0.6 and industry structure only 0.17, and two quality checks penalise the company for being loudly public — global rankings, superlatives and marquee foreign partners are the opposite of an overlooked business, and the record treats that visibility as a weakness rather than an asset. Innovation is real but unproven as an advantage: home-grown 5.2 MW and 3.3 MW turbines with German certification, India's first ingot and wafer line, electrolyser trials — yet no research spending is ever disclosed, so the innovation pattern stays partial while the technology risk stays on watch, since the electrolyser choice was still open in 2022 and wafer rules change again in FY29. Sales growth has averaged only about 4% a year and is uneven. The overall resilience reading is STRAINED, and that is the honest summary: a business with assets nobody can rebuild, funded at a pace its current cash returns do not support, earning through channels it does not control.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

**Overall:** the company partly passes the foundation every multibagger shares (steady cash + high returns on capital + growth), 1 of 3 testable checks passing, and fits 2 of the 11 patterns: Cost to Replicate, Pricing Power.

### Cost to Replicate — STRONG FIT
*"Could a newcomer copy this?"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** The infra core passes a concrete cost-to-replicate test: eight airport concessions including a greenfield second Mumbai airport with slots already fully taken, a regulated asset base, a 27-year expressway concession, and 18 long-term MDO agreements over specific mine blocks — finite locations and licences a funded copycat cannot rebuild; the manufacturing and trading arms do not pass, but they are now the minority of EBITDA.

> "Already the slots are fully taken up by the airlines at Navi Mumbai Airport" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Pricing Power — LIKELY FIT
*"Raises prices, keeps the customers"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and every one of the 2 computable checks supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** Price increases come from regulated airport tariff revisions and RAB determinations rather than brand-based pricing power, and the manufacturing side shows the opposite — solar realisations fell and the India-only mix 'compresses the margin', with module EBITDA margin guided down from 30%+ to 25-30% and lower again by FY26.

> "it compresses the margin, but that gain we will make from productivity" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹46 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ✅ Operating margin moved from about 10% (early years) to 14% (recent years) — the widening-profitability signature of pricing power / lock-in.

### Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) — PARTIAL
*"Sells once, earns forever"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (2 of 3 checks supporting).
