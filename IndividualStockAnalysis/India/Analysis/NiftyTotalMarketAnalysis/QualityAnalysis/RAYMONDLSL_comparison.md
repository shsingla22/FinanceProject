Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.4 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing discipline held through a ~25% wool cost spike with no mention of discount wars, but apparel/garmenting entry barriers are inherently low. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±3.1 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Low entry barriers in apparel/ethnics with multiple new industrial-house entrants and imported competition, plus routine end-of-season discounting.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Neutral. Now: Capex is described as calibrated and modest — debottlenecking/automation plus asset-light franchise stores — with no evidence of high, rising growth capex or measured incremental ROIC. | The long view had said: Capex is modest and majority maintenance, with management explicitly disavowing major growth investment.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Excellent → Good. Now: A nationwide multi-format retail plus dealer network that rivals struggle to match is the clearest structural advantage, with the trade channel outgrowing the industry, though store rationalisation shows the footprint is not uniformly productive. | The long view had said: The TRS network across 600+ towns, layered with EBO, MBO, LFS and online, is a reach management credibly claims no rival possesses, and it combines retail with stitching service.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Weak → Poor. Now: The opposite of obscure — a centenary-celebrating iconic listed brand advertising through cricket and cinema and covered by multiple brokerages. | The long view had said: A century-old iconic listed brand with broad analyst coverage and mass-media presence is the opposite of an ignored niche.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: The retail side is correctly served with brand intangibles, but the corporate/B2B side (garmenting, shirting) shows no switching-cost lock-in and had to concede duty-paid terms to retain clients. | The long view had said: Benefit mix is well matched — image/trust intangibles for retail consumers, and design/innovation-led supply for B2B fabric buyers driving repeat demand.

Now: Quality/craftsmanship claims are made but there is no evidence customers buy Raymond primarily for certainty or that price is secondary to trust. | The long view had said: The proposition is explicitly trust and authenticity rather than price, particularly for high-stakes wedding purchases.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 1 weakened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: UNKNOWN on the long view, UNKNOWN on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Q3 FY26 disclosed an explicit ASP rise of ~INR26/metre alongside volume growth, and management said margins held despite a ~25% jump in wool prices, citing pricing discipline plus 'price inelastic' wedding/occasion demand; Q4 branded textile revenue +14% with segment EBITDA +126% on mix and ASP. (The long view had said: FY26 shows real evidence — ASP up ~INR26/metre with volume growth, margins held through a 25% wool price rise, and 'price inelastic' wedding demand — but FY25 was the opposite (revenue -6.2%, branded textile EBITDA margin down from 22.2% to 18.9%), and management is now designing cheaper products for Tier-3/4 buyers who find Raymond costly.)
- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Raymond is a manufacturer selling through its own 1,675-store network plus EBOs/MBOs/online, and Q3 FY26 described a deliberate asset-light, franchise-led, capital-partnered format for Tier-2/3 expansion alongside active closure of unproductive stores; Q4 reported double-digit like-for-like growth in retail channels. (The long view had said: The company manufactures fabric and owns the route to the customer — 1,592 stores (Sep-24) rising to 1,675 (Jun-25) across 600 towns, asset-light franchised expansion, EBO/MBO/LFS/online, and a unique TRS format where fabric purchase and stitching cross-sell in-house; FY27 plans 100 gross EBO additions.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The four calls contain no discussion of licence fees, AMC/service revenue, renewals, attach rates, or customer advances funding the business; revenue is described purely as fabric/apparel product sales. (The long view had said: Across all seven calls the model is one-time sale of fabric, apparel and garment exports — no licence fees, AMC, subscriptions, attach rates or renewals are ever discussed, and working capital is positive and inventory-heavy (90 days in Jun-25, 77 days in Mar-26) rather than customer-funded.)

**That weakened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The stated strategy across all four FY26 calls is the opposite — premiumisation, super-premium suiting lines (Super Luxe super 200s, Regio Italia) and rising ASPs, with cost control framed as margin protection rather than a scale-driven low-price culture. (The long view had said: There is genuine cost discipline (opex +1.2% on income +15% in Q4 FY26, factory utilisation lifted from 80-85% to above 90-95%, loss-making stores closed) and a value entry play in Sleepz at INR500-999, but the stated strategy is premiumisation, not low price plus scale.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Management explicitly names casualisation of dress codes as a structural, not cyclical, shift against its core suiting/formal-fabric franchise, and is reformulating the portfolio (premium casuals, fusion wear, Smart Ethnix, linen) in response — real erosion of the historic demand base, but the pivot is under way and premium/occasion volumes are still growing. (The long view had said: Management itself names the erosion of its core demand base: casualization of the dress code is called a structural trend, and by FY27 casualization is one of the two strategic pillars, pushing branded textiles toward comfort-first fabrics and knitwear and apparel toward polos, chinos, t-shirts and denim — a direct migration away from the worsted-suiting franchise the company is built on. This runs alongside a channel shift to online/LFS and repeated repositioning toward premiumisation and ethnic/fusion occasion wear.)
- **New Entrants**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Across four calls management never discusses a new-format competitor defensively, and the affirmative evidence points the other way: post-GST formalisation is shifting volume toward organised branded players, with Raymond's trade channel outgrowing the industry by a wide margin. Increased ad spend is framed as brand building, not as a discount response. (The long view had said: Ethnic wear drew a wave of well-capitalised entrants (an analyst names Tasva and 'many, many industrial houses'), and Raymond's own Ethnix build-out stalled — 18 of 140 stores closed in one quarter, high-single-digit growth against an industry management calls 'lacklustre', and a four-year payback horizon of which two years are gone. Imported Italian fabric had taken MBO counter space in suiting. These are same-format competitors rather than a structurally different discounter or quick-commerce entrant.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The calls show the premium proposition being paid for rather than defended: average selling price rose about Rs.26 per metre on premiumisation, and branded textile EBITDA margin expanded to 20% in Q2 and held through a roughly 25% rise in wool prices — evidence of end-consumer brand pull in a 100-year-old brand rather than an intermediary quietly substituting a cheaper equivalent. (The long view had said: Roughly half the ethnic market is unbranded, management concedes smaller-town consumers prefer 'a local boutique product and not a branded product' and perceive Raymond as too costly, and the response has been to add cheaper entry points (Aldano Super 90, Urban Flare Super 100, sub-INR1,000 Sleepz) — defending against good-enough alternatives on price. The TRS/tailoring and MBO channels also put an intermediary between the brand and the buyer. Against this, the premium end is showing genuine pricing power.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Fashion Risk (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (the only stress check that could be computed is clean.) vs Sound on the one-year view (the only stress check that could be computed is clean.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
