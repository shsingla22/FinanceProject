# Sai Life (SAILIFE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Sai Life Sciences is an Indian contract research, development and manufacturing organisation (CRDMO) — it does not own or sell its own medicines, but is hired by global drug companies to do their chemistry work for them. It helps customers take a new molecule all the way from early laboratory discovery, through development and clinical-trial supplies, to full commercial manufacture of the active drug ingredient (API), charging for research staff time (FTE contracts) and for the material it makes and ships. Its customers are pharmaceutical and biotech innovators in the US, UK, EU and Japan — 300+ active customers including 19 of the top 25 global pharma companies, with large pharma accounting for 49% of FY26 revenue. Operations are centred in India (R&D in Hyderabad, large-scale manufacturing at Bidar) with customer-facing science centres in Manchester, UK and Cambridge, USA; FY26 revenue grew 29% with EBITDA margins around 28-30%.

**The parts of the business:**

- **CDMO (Development and Manufacturing)** — Develops the manufacturing process for a customer's drug molecule and then makes it at scale — clinical batches through to commercial API supply — and is the larger half of the company at roughly 64-65% of revenue, growing 33% in FY26, with 34 commercial molecules, 11 in Phase III/pre-registration and about 155 in earlier development stages, and over 90% of its revenue from large pharma.
- **CRO (Discovery Research)** — Provides early-stage laboratory research — discovery chemistry, biology, DMPK and preclinical assays — usually sold as dedicated scientist (FTE) contracts, and accounts for roughly 35-36% of revenue, growing 24% in FY26, with its customer mix split about 48% pharma and 52% biotech.
- **Development / dedicated R&D FTE teams** — A fast-growing model where global customers move their process-development and R&D staffing to Sai's Indian labs under long-duration dedicated-team contracts; management says it lifted revenue meaningfully this year and, more importantly, feeds a downstream pipeline of later-stage manufacturing work.
- **Veterinary / Animal Health API** — A newly launched dedicated facility making active ingredients for animal medicines — described as a strategic entry into a new segment with three of the top five global animal health companies already signed, with Phase I targeted for completion by March 2027, so it is early-stage and not yet a material revenue contributor.
- **New modalities and technology platforms** — Capability build-outs rather than separate reporting segments — peptides, ADCs (linker chemistry and bioconjugation), oligonucleotides, flow chemistry and high-potency (OEB) labs, plus AI tools like retrosynthesis — funded by heavy capex (INR 633 crore in FY26, INR 1,100-1,300 crore planned for FY27) and intended to win future large-pharma programmes.

### The story in depth

Sai Life is a contract research and manufacturing partner to global drug companies, and the single thread running through all three reviews is that customers buy certainty from it rather than cheapness. Its regulatory record is unblemished, 35 customer audits produced zero critical observations, and management says contracts are won on a decade or more of trust rather than price. That trust is converting into measurable share gain in a market where India is only about 5% of the global total: revenue from large pharma rose from 28% in FY22 to 49% in FY26, relationships now cover 19 of the top 25 global drug makers, commercial molecules went from 31 to 34 with 7 added to late-stage or commercial work in FY26, and FY26 revenue grew 29%. The screen rates Market Share Gainers and Corporate Culture as strong fits, the latter because February 2025 promises on margins, growth and debt repayment were met or beaten by FY26 without guidance being opportunistically raised, and because management volunteers bad news, admitting it is smaller than the largest global players and cannot see customer destocking coming.

The same traits that create the good patterns also create most of the risks. Deep dependence on large pharma is what drives the share-gain story, and it is also the concentration risk: the top 10 customers were 54% of FY26 revenue, the top 5 were 37%, the top 1 was 12%, and over 90% of manufacturing revenue comes from large pharma, with two of three new commercial molecules relying on Sai being the primary supplier. The regulatory record and 15 to 18 month validation cycles give real switching costs, which is why the New Entrants risk is only a watch item, yet those same qualification barriers apply mainly to development and commercial supply, not to plain manufacturing, where management concedes customers may have multiple options. Most tellingly, pricing is open-book with large pharma and management anchors its own margin ceiling at 28-30% to what Chinese rivals earn at far larger scale, which is why Pricing Power scores no fit and the Good Enough Goods risk stands: the premium is capped by choice, not defended.

The financial picture is solid but not yet exceptional, which is what holds the overall rating to 63 out of 100, or Decent, built from 69 on business quality, 48 on multibagger fit and 72 on risk safety. Out of every 100 rupees of sales, 29 is operating profit, but margins have swung about 5 points a year, and the business earns 18 rupees of operating cash for every 100 rupees of capital employed. Working capital is a genuine strength: cash comes back 24 days before suppliers must be paid, so customers effectively fund the business. Capital spending has gone from roughly 200 crore two years ago to 633 crore in FY26 with 1,100 to 1,300 crore guided for FY27, taking Bidar capacity from 700KL to 1,150KL, with a quarter of next year's spend going into new capabilities and AI. That build is demand-backed and lands into a biotech funding cycle turning up, with year-to-date funding 52% higher and April 2026 at 10.6 billion dollars, but it is also the main thing that could dilute returns if the lumpiness management keeps warning about arrives first. Overall resilience is judged sound.
