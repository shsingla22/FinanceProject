Now: A deep, multi-domain technology edge backed by industry-leading R&D intensity (>5% of revenue vs <1% for most peers), a dedicated technology-moat presentation, separate CTO and CTO-AI roles, and external validation from Microsoft. | The long view had said: A genuine multi-domain technology edge (middleware, powertrain, cockpit, connected, AI triage) backed by >5% revenue R&D intensity and a dedicated CTO-AI, tempered by the middleware/autonomous shortfall and AI-led cannibalisation of its own scope.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Good → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹19 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±1.4 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹19 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±3.0 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Emerging aftermarket-style position in after-sales diagnostics and a claimed one-of-a-kind software benchmarking capability via Caresoft, which is closest to a mini-monopoly in the customer's mind. | The long view had said: Growing after-sales and reusable platform positions hint at owned niches, but KPIT is one of several engineering partners rather than holding a monopoly-like position in the OEM's mind.

Now: The improvement is mix-led rather than volume-led — fixed-price revenue rose from 59% to 66%, solutions revenue more than doubled to 18%, and per-person revenue rose, sustaining 21% EBITDA despite flat volumes. | The long view had said: Mix is improving (fixed-price at 66% vs 59%, higher per-person revenue) but pricing is negative — the company concedes rate/cost reductions and offshores work, which depresses topline even as margins hold.

Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.4 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. Why now: Strong markers: co-founder-led with very long tenure (Anup Sable 31 years, Patil/Tikekar co-founders), net cash of ~INR 9–10.5bn through the downturn with dividends, sustained technology investment when growth was weak, and unusually candid disclosure of bad news (the $65m walk-down, a 500-person reduction, Qorix/N-Dream losses, Tier-1 drop). Against that, the Q1 FY26 promise that 'H2 will be higher than H1' with growth momentum was not delivered — Q3 organic growth was still negative. (The long view had said: Co-founder leadership (Patil, Pandit, Tikekar) with 31-year insiders like Anup Sable promoted to COO; guidance raised and then beaten in FY23, and bad news disclosed promptly and specifically in FY26 — Honda's cancelled platform programmes, self-cannibalisation, and middleware/AD shortfall — alongside a net-cash balance sheet (INR 9bn), dividends, and sustained long-term technology capex through the downturn.)

Why now: the only computable check points this way but the calls offer no evidence — numbers alone are only ever a screening hint, never a confirmed fit. (The long view had said: KPIT has clear B2B reputational standing — CES reception of the KPIT+Technica story, Microsoft naming it a frontier partner, Eclipse/Covesa membership and 'thought leadership' framing — but there is no heritage narrative, A&P spend or brand-led pricing premium in the calls.)

(The long view had said: There is a genuine cost-delivery advantage — rising offshoring, fixed-price mix up from 59% to 66%, productivity gains and 'cheaper, better, faster' solutions — sustaining ~20.8% EBITDA described as best-in-industry, but management frames this as margin engineering rather than a scale-driven low-price culture with itemised savings.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (QUANT SIGNAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

**That eased:**

(The long view had said: The industry is mid-platform-shift (SDV, e-architecture, AD/ADAS, AI) and management admits AI is cannibalising its own services base while middleware/autonomous-driving bets (Qorix) underperformed; the FY26 call lists 'AI-led transformation led to near-term cannibalization' and 'lower than expected growth in middleware and autonomous driving' as headwinds, and concedes AD/ADAS platform value may accrue to a few platform owners, not KPIT.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
