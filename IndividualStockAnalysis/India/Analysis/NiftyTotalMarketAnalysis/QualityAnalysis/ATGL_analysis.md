# Adani Total Gas (ATGL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Adani Total Gas Limited (ATGL) is an Indian city gas distribution (CGD) company: it buys natural gas and pipes or dispenses it to end users in the cities and districts it has been licensed to serve. It sells CNG to vehicles through its own network of filling stations (707 stations as of June 2026, plus more via its joint venture) and piped natural gas (PNG) to household kitchens (about 11.4 lakh homes) and to industrial and commercial customers (about 10,400). It makes money on the spread between what it pays for gas — a mix of government-allocated domestic APM/New Well gas, long-term imported contracts and spot LNG purchases — and the calibrated retail price it charges consumers, so margins swing with gas sourcing costs; FY2025-26 revenue was INR6,415 crores with EBITDA of INR1,225 crores. It operates across 53 geographical areas covering roughly 14% of India's population and 125 districts, of which 34 are served directly and 19 through a 50-50 joint venture with IndianOil.

**The parts of the business:**

- **CNG (compressed natural gas for transport)** — Sells CNG as a cheaper vehicle fuel through company-run and dealer-run (CODO/DODO) filling stations — the biggest and fastest-growing part, roughly two-thirds of gas volumes and growing 17-19% a year on the calls.
- **PNG - Domestic (piped natural gas to homes)** — Pipes gas directly into household kitchens via steel and MDPE pipeline networks, with about 11.4 lakh connections and roughly 137,000 added in FY2025-26; volumes grow more slowly (4-6%) than CNG.
- **PNG - Industrial and Commercial** — Supplies piped gas as a fuel to factories and commercial establishments (about 10,400 customers directly); management describes this as the segment most exposed to gas supply shortfalls and price swings.
- **Pipeline and station infrastructure** — Builds and owns the physical network — 15,987-inch km of steel pipeline plus over 8,300 km of MDPE last-mile pipe — which management treats as the backbone that drives all future volume growth.
- **E-mobility / EV charging (ATEL subsidiary)** — Operates a nationwide EV charging network of 5,306 charge points across 26 states and 226 cities (58 MW installed), targeting 10,000 points; small in revenue terms but growing 100% year-on-year.
- **Biomass / Compressed Bio-Gas (ATBL)** — Produces and sells CBG from its Barsana plant along with organic manure by-products (FOM/PROM); described on the calls as an emerging, early-stage business.
- **LNG for long-haul mobility (LTM)** — Sets up LNG fuelling stations for long-distance trucks and buses, starting with a first station at Tirupur, Tamil Nadu — the newest and smallest of the businesses mentioned.
- **IndianOil-Adani Gas Private Limited (IOAGPL) - 50:50 JV** — A joint venture with IndianOil that runs the CGD business in 19 additional geographical areas, adding roughly 460 CNG stations and lifting the combined network to about 1,167 stations and 13.74 lakh household customers.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Adani Total Gas is, at its core, a licensed pipe-and-station network, and that single fact explains most of what the three reviews found. The quality framework gives its highest marks to just two things: the physical distribution asset (28,600 inch-km of steel pipeline, 10,500 km of MDPE, 1,167 CNG stations and city gate stations across 34 gas areas) and the end market it sits in, where natural gas is meant to go from 6.5% to 15% of India's energy mix by 2030 and CNG vehicles grew 43% nationally. The multibagger screen's one clear hit, Cost to Replicate, is the same asset seen from another angle — regulator-granted exclusive territories plus buried infrastructure that a well-funded newcomer simply cannot rebuild. And the risk review's lowest-rated danger, New Entrants, is that same moat a third time: across eleven calls the only competitor mentioned is minor overlap at the edges of neighbouring areas. One strength is therefore doing three jobs at once, which is worth noting when judging how much of the story rests on it. The screen's other favourable-looking signal, Innovation Dominance, comes only from the numbers with nothing in the calls to back it, so it should be treated as a screening flag rather than a finding.

The growth side of the story and the biggest weakness are also the same trait wearing two faces. The company is winning volume steadily — CNG up 61%, 19% and 18% across recent years, the footprint expanding from 33 gas areas in 95 districts to 53 across 125 districts covering 14% of the population, with newer areas already supplying 32% of volume, and an installed base of piped-gas homes rising from 600,000 in 2022 to 11.41 lakh by mid-2026. But it wins that volume by staying visibly cheaper than petrol, diesel and LPG, and that is precisely why it cannot raise prices. The quality framework's worst score, on pricing and mix, records margins falling from roughly 25% to 15% because management repeatedly 'calibrates' the pass-through rather than recovering its gas costs. The screen reaches the same conclusion from the other direction, marking Pricing Power, Toll Roads and Friendly Middleman as outright non-fits — customers are price-sensitive end users, not intermediaries or captive buyers of a trivial input, and management itself warns that further price rises cause 'demand destruction'. The risk review labels this Good Enough Goods, elevated: the product is an undifferentiated molecule whose whole appeal is the discount. Market Share Gainers, Brand Strength, Low Price Plus and Forward Integrators all land as partial fits for the same reason — the effort is real and visible (branded stations up from 65 to 140, an owned last-mile network, an EV charging build-out to 5,306 points) but none of it converts into a price premium.

What turns that squeeze from a nuisance into the main risk is who sets the input cost. Government Dependency is the only high-rated risk, and it is upstream of nearly everything else: allocated gas moved from 63% to 51% to 37% and back to 51%, a pooled-gas mechanism arrived and was then abruptly withdrawn, and roughly 40% of supply is administratively allocated with 15% bought at spot prices. That is also what makes Stakeholder Concentration a watch item on the supply side even though the customer base is genuinely spread across 1.14 million homes, 10,422 industrial users and 707 stations. Cyclicality is judged manageable because demand keeps growing every quarter, and the framework reads the current position as a visible trough with an identifiable recovery as US and Qatari supply arrives. Against that, the company keeps spending heavily on growth — stations from 349 to 707, pipeline from 13,082 to 15,987 inch-km — while deflecting questions on returns from new spending and conceding that some areas earn only 8-10%, against a headline 16% cash return on capital. Management scores zero of the quality areas, and the culture read notes that promises made in 2022 were delivered but bad news is soft-pedalled in prepared remarks. The combined verdict — 53 out of 100, Decent, with resilience judged sound — fairly reflects a business with a near-unassailable position in its territories whose economics are set by someone else.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Cost to Replicate — STRONG FIT
*"Could a newcomer copy this?"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** The advantage is a regulator-granted, geography-exclusive licence plus buried infrastructure that cannot be rebuilt by a funded entrant: 53 GAs across 125 districts, 15,987 inch-km of own steel pipeline (28,600 with the IOAGPL JV), 8,300+ km MDPE and 707 CNG stations laid down over a decade of supply-driven capex.

> "this infrastructure, when we took the licenses, we decided to take the first step. We let it make it supply driven rather than only the demand driven" — *management, on an earnings call*

*"Out-inventing the competition"*

## What to watch

- Whether allocated gas as a share of supply holds or is cut again — the Government Dependency risk is the single input that sets margins, and the last withdrawal of pooled gas showed how fast policy can reverse.
- Whether operating margin recovers from roughly 15% back toward the old 25% as the trough passes — this is the direct test of the Pricing, Mix and Volume check and of the cyclical-upturn call.
- Whether the CNG discount to petrol, diesel and LPG stays wide enough to keep converting customers — if it narrows, the Good Enough Goods risk and the Market Share Gainers pattern both break at once.
- Whether management starts disclosing incremental returns on new gas areas rather than deflecting, given the admission that some earn only 8-10% against a 16% headline cash return — the weakest point in the Capital Allocation and Management scores.
- Whether the EV charging, CBG and LNG-trucking build-out grows into a real second earnings stream or stays a hedge — it is the stated answer to the elevated Technological Innovation risk and currently rests on point counts, not economics.
