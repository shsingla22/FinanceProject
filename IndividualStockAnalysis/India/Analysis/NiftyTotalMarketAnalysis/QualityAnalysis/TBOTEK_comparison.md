### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 3 weakened, 5 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Buyer and agent counts rise persistently — monthly transacting buyers +5% to over 29,500, international monthly active agents from ~8,600-9,000 to ~11,000 — with growth sustained through the India-Pakistan/Iran-Israel disruptions and Classic bought to enter North America, but management never gives explicit market-share percentages or comparisons to named competitors. (The long view had said: Share gain is quantified and repeated across years: 35% hotel growth versus listed peers HBX and Webbeds in FY25, wallet share taken from competitors in UAE, 15 new markets added, and Q4 FY26 growth attributed mainly to share gain — with profitability maintained, not bought.)
- **Pricing Power**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: GP consistently grows faster than GTV (Q2 FY26: GTV +12%, GP +19%) helped by 'slight improvement in retention' on hotels and mix shift to hotels/ancillary, but no call cites explicit price increases taken without volume loss, and blended take rate is noisy post-Classic. (The long view had said: Take rate rose 4.77%→5.25%→6.04% (organic, Q3 FY26), but management attributes this to hotel mix shift rather than price increases, admits airline monetisation 'has been a struggle', and says of Classic 'we are not looking at optimizing margins per se'.)
- **Forward Integrators**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The model is deliberately the opposite: no own stores, franchising or D2C is mentioned in any call, and management's stated strategy is to route more volume through third-party travel advisors rather than toward the end traveller. (The long view had said: Not a manufacturer with own stores, but TBO has moved toward the end demand channel — a DMC business contracting Mecca/Medina supply and the Classic Vacations acquisition giving it a direct North American luxury demand channel it is now cross-selling into.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Friendly Middleman (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged; 4 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 10 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
