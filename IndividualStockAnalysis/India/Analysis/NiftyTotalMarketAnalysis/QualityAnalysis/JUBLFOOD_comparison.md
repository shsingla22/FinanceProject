### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 5. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Domino's shows brand pull — share gains confirmed by an independent Nielsen panel, plans to 'invest more in brand building', and multi-brand stretch (Popeyes, Dunkin, Hong's) — but the flagship brands are licensed rather than owned heritage, and pricing premium is absent; the new brands are currently a profit drag. (The long view had said: Real brand salience — 'It Happens Only with Pizza' refresh lifting brand scores, ~2/3 share of the pizza category, loyalty stickiness, and stretch into Popeyes/COFFY/Hong's — but the core brand is licensed from Domino's rather than owned heritage, and brand strength has not converted into premium pricing (see the price freeze and MOV cut).)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The calls describe loyalty membership (40mn+), app MAUs and a new ad-monetisation platform, but there is no discussion of licence fees, AMC/service contracts, renewal rates or customer advances funding the business, so the pattern's core mechanics are simply not evidenced. (The long view had said: Across all 16 calls the revenue model is purely transactional QSR order revenue — no licence fees, AMC, subscription or deferred-revenue funding is ever discussed; the closest analogue, Cheesy Rewards loyalty (25mn members, >50% of business), is repeat purchase, not contracted recurring revenue.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Only 'calibrated' price increases on select products (Volcano Pizza) plus premium-mix launches lifted gross margin ~80bps over six months, but this sits against 2.5 years of no hikes, free delivery and a deliberate MOV cut that reduced average order value — value-led deflation coexists with narrow pockets of pricing. (The long view had said: The multi-year trajectory is the opposite of pricing power: two-year price freeze while cheese hit 10-year highs (Nov 2022, Aug 2024), free delivery and a forced cut of minimum order value from Rs.149 to Rs.99 to match aggregators, and in May 2026 only ~120bps of pricing against ~120bps of energy inflation alone plus labour and commodity headwinds; the recent 'calibrated' increases and premium launches are a partial offset, not demonstrated pricing power.)

**That weakened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: A regular, named, margin-accretive product cadence is clearly evidenced every quarter (Big Big Pizza, Chicken Burst, Sourdough, Cheese Lava Pull Apart, Flavor Burst burgers, Cheezilla) alongside AI/tech investment, but there is no disclosed R&D spend or share-of-industry-R&D — this is menu and tech innovation, not R&D-led dominance. (The long view had said: Every call names new products with stated commercial impact — East India regional range and no-onion-no-garlic (2022), Cheese Volcano, Rs.99 Lunch Thali, Chillrz (2024), Big Big Pizza, Sourdough and Cheese Lava Pull Apart (2026) explicitly called gross-margin accretive — alongside sustained tech/AI and micro-services investment funded by ~75% gross margins; no formal R&D line is disclosed, which is the only gap.)
- **Forward Integrators**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: JFL is a store operator moving customers onto its own digital assets — 15mn MAU app, growing share of own-channel orders, own delivery fleet and 20-minute delivery, plus monetising that traffic with advertising — but it is a franchisee of others' brands rather than a manufacturer integrating forward, and it funds its own store growth rather than franchising it out. (The long view had said: The whole model is forward-integrated manufacturing: own commissaries feeding ~2,530 own stores in India, own-app D2C with 76% delivery mix and own-asset share of delivery pushed to record highs, its own delivery fleet, plus an asset-light franchise engine in Turkey where 88% of stores are franchisee-run and JFL earns revenue serving them.)

*Unchanged: Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Low Price Plus (LIKELY FIT); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Stressed on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Demand is operations-linked repeat food consumption, not customer capex: across all four calls JFL posted eight consecutive quarters of positive Domino's India LFL and 13-20% consolidated revenue growth, and it kept expanding (~230-250 stores a year, 114 added in Q3 alone) explicitly while calling the demand environment challenging — countercyclical investment rather than cycle-chasing. (The long view had said: Demand is flow/repeat consumption rather than customer capex, but the four years of calls are dominated by input-cost cycles that swing margins hard — cheese at 10-year highs in FY23 (EBITDA 24.3% down 170bps), EBITDA troughing at ~19.3% in FY25, recovering to 20.5% in Q3FY26, then a fresh energy/LPG/wage inflation shock in Q4FY26 worth 100-120bps on energy alone with management unable to guide beyond 'a quarter, 2 quarters, 3 quarters, anybody's guess'.)
- **Fashion Risk**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Growth rests on a functional, high-frequency repeat-purchase base (positive LFL for eight straight quarters, 40mn loyalty members) rather than a single trending product; the many launches over the window — Big Big Pizza, Chicken Burst, Sourdough, Cheese Lava Pull Apart, Flavor Burst burgers — form a rolling portfolio of small bets, none of which carries the P&L alone. (The long view had said: Growth leans on a rolling sequence of trending launches (Volcano, Big Big Pizza, Sourdough, Cheese Lava Pull Apart, Chillrz, Bubble Tea) and an analyst directly raised category fatigue — 'how do you fight the lethargy that may come with the category of pizza itself?' — with management conceding pizza frequency is only about 3 occasions a year.)

**That worsened:**

- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The May 2026 call is dominated by a structurally different competitive set — an analyst notes 'there is competition that has changed' — and management's response is textbook margin investment: free delivery (now two years in the base), and cutting minimum order value from Rs.149 to Rs.99 purely to match competitors, knowingly dragging average ticket size and margins. (The long view had said: Aggregator/quick-commerce platforms have repeatedly reset the economics and JFL has responded with margin investment: two years of free delivery from FY25, eight straight quarters of no price increase, and in FY26 cutting minimum order value from Rs.149 to Rs.99 explicitly to match Swiggy/Zomato — with management conceding average ticket size fell as a result and Q4FY26 LFL collapsing to 0.2%.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Government Dependency (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Stressed on the long view (2 of the 3 stress checks are flagged — multiple warning lights at once.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
