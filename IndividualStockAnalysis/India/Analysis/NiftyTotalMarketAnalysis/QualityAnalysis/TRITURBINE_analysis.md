# Triveni Turbine Ltd. (TRITURBINE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: INDUSTRIAL MANUFACTURING*

Triveni Turbine Limited makes and sells steam turbines — machines that turn steam into mechanical drive and electricity — which management describes as "heat and power solutions" for industrial and renewable-based applications, historically in the 0 MW to 100 MW range with R&D now extending the product range beyond 120 MW. Its customers are factories and power producers that need steam and captive power as part of their process: distilleries and sugar, process cogeneration users such as pharmaceuticals, food processing and pulp & paper, plus steel, cement, chemicals, waste heat recovery, municipal solid waste, independent power producers, and oil & gas (API) customers; it also supplies a large "strategic" utility order to NTPC. It earns money two ways — one-off sales of new turbines (the Product segment) and a recurring, higher-margin Aftermarket business of spares, servicing and refurbishment, including refurbishing equipment originally built by other OEMs. It manufactures in Bengaluru (Peenya and Sompura), is headquartered in Noida, and sells both in India and abroad, with exports growing from about a third of sales in FY23 to 58% of revenue in FY26; overseas presence includes TSE Engineering in the southern African (SADC) region and a facility in Houston, Texas, with export orders coming from Europe, Turkey and Southeast Asia. Full-year FY26 revenue was ₹21.81 billion with EBITDA of ₹5.27 billion (24.2% margin) and a closing order book of ₹20.54 billion.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Product segment (steam turbines)** — Design and manufacture of new steam turbines from 0 MW up to 100 MW-plus (R&D now taking the range past 120 MW) for process industries, renewable/cogeneration projects and power producers; it is the larger of the two segments — for example ₹3.7 billion of the ₹4.63 billion quarterly revenue in Q1 FY25 — and management says growth is increasingly coming from the bigger-megawatt machines where its market share is rising.
- **Aftermarket (spares, service and refurbishment)** — Ongoing supply of spare parts, servicing, O&M support and refurbishment over the life of installed turbines — including machines made by other manufacturers — which management repeatedly calls a high-growth, higher-margin segment with a short order-to-billing cycle; it contributed ₹1.5 billion of order booking in Q1 FY25 and 50% of total order booking in Q4 FY26 after growing 121% year-on-year, and quarters where aftermarket dispatches fall are explicitly blamed for weaker company margins.
- **API turbines (oil & gas)** — Turbines built to API standards for upstream and downstream oil and gas customers in India and internationally; management calls it a distinct order-booking driver and notes that very large expected API orders slipping in FY26 hurt that year's bookings and revenue.
- **International operations and expansion** — Overseas selling and service capacity, including the TSE Engineering acquisition serving the southern African (SADC) region — which won a large-turbine services contract worth over ₹1 billion — and a newly commissioned facility in Houston, Texas, meant to bring the company closer to customers for spares and third-party service work; exports reached 58% of FY26 revenue and 69% of Q4 FY26 order booking.
- **New products and technologies** — Newer lines the company is commercialising — industrial heat pumps (enquiry book "well in excess of 100" with a first order received), MVRs, entry into the geothermal products segment described as a key FY26 milestone, other rotating equipment including gas applications, and longer-term R&D in supercritical and transcritical carbon dioxide; these are still small today but management presents them as future growth areas.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Triveni Turbine scores 84 out of 100 and rates Outstanding, and the three separate reviews keep pointing at the same handful of traits. It sells industrial steam turbines into a market management itself describes as a two-player market at home and a small group of players internationally, with fragmented industrial buyers who have little bargaining leverage. That structure shows up in the quality framework as a full-marks score for barriers to entry, in the pattern screen as a strong fit for Cost to Replicate, and again in the risk review as a low New Entrants risk — one fact, three places. It is built on a 25-year installed base of machines that keeps feeding spares, service and refurbishment work, now about 34% of turnover; that same installed base is what makes the Recurring Revenue pattern a strong fit. Operating profit runs at roughly Rs 21 on every Rs 100 of sales and has barely moved, around plus or minus 1.6 points a year, which the framework reads as cost control and the pattern screen reads as the funding source for a steady stream of R&D-led products — carbon-dioxide-based turbines flagged back in 2022 that turned into commercial orders by 2026, a range extended past 120 MW, heat pumps, MVR compressors, geothermal and organic Rankine cycle machines. Management is stable across the whole 2022-2026 span, discloses bad news without being asked, runs with net cash of Rs 10.37 billion and negative working capital, and has made no large acquisitions in five years — which is why Management is the strongest quality area at 1.67 and Corporate Culture is a strong pattern fit.

The interesting part is where the same traits cut both ways. Demand is tied to other companies' plant-building — steel, cement, oil and gas, paper — and management openly frames the moment as an investment cycle for energy while reminding listeners of the 2007 and 2011 ordering peaks. That makes Cyclicality the one high risk in the review, and it is the direct reason the risk-safety score of 76 is the weakest of the three inputs into the 84. The defence against it is the very aftermarket annuity that drives the Recurring Revenue pattern: service work follows machines already running, not new capex decisions, and it made up half of the fourth-quarter FY26 order intake. The second defence is geography — exports went from 33% of sales in early FY23 to 58-60% by FY26, with record export order booking, a commissioned Houston facility and full ownership of the South African service business. That expansion earns full marks in the quality framework and is what makes Forward Integrators a partial fit, but it is also the source of two of the acknowledged sore points: losses at the US subsidiary, and US tariff and duty changes that visibly froze order finalisation for several quarters until duties came back to 18%, which is why Government Dependency sits on watch. Pricing shows the same double edge: profit before tax has been held above 20% and margins have widened, yet management deliberately declines to push prices, choosing to absorb input costs and take customers along. That restraint is exactly why the New Entrants and Good Enough Goods risks are judged low — margins rose rather than being defended — and equally why Pricing Power only rates a partial fit.

The soft spots are narrow but real, and they sit next to the strengths rather than contradicting them. Return on Capital is the lowest quality area at 0.67 and Capital Allocation the next lowest at 1.0, in a company that is organic, net cash and holding Rs 10.37 billion in investments and cash — the same conservatism that makes it a disciplined steward leaves a large pile of capital sitting in the business. Market Share Gainers stays only a partial fit for a measurement reason rather than a business one: share gains are claimed repeatedly and plausibly — high domestic share, rising share in turbines above 30 MW, export order booking up 174% in the fourth quarter of FY26, entry into the US and Sub-Saharan Africa — but almost no actual percentages are tracked over time in the calls, so the claim cannot be checked. Brand Strength is a likely rather than strong fit because the reputation is engineering credibility, quality systems customers regard well and an all-time-high customer satisfaction score, not a consumer-style brand. Toll Roads and Friendly Middleman are partial, and Low Price Plus does not fit at all — a turbine is a large capital purchase for the buyer, not a small consumable. Set against that, management's own candour is the best signal available: it has flagged muted domestic bookings, a 26% decline in order booking, the US subsidiary losses and lengthening receivable days from 49 days without prompting, and warned against reading too much into strong quarters. The overall resilience reads as sound, and the story hangs together as a structurally protected niche manufacturer riding genuinely expanding end markets — energy transition, waste heat recovery, geothermal and now data-centre power, with the enquiry book roughly doubling to about 18 GW over FY26 — whose main vulnerability is the capital-spending cycle of its customers rather than competition.

## What to watch

- Aftermarket's share of turnover and order booking — the ~34% of sales and 50% of Q4 FY26 intake is both the Recurring Revenue pattern and the main cushion against the high Cyclicality risk; if that share slips, both weaken at once.
- Domestic order booking and the enquiry pipeline — the 26% order booking decline and muted domestic bookings management disclosed are the first place a turn in the customer capex cycle would appear, ahead of the roughly 18 GW enquiry book.
- US subsidiary losses and tariff policy — the Houston build-out drives the Geographical expansion score and the Forward Integrators fit, but is also the live Government Dependency watch item after duties froze order finalisation for several quarters.
- Deployment of the Rs 10.37 billion net cash — Return on Capital at 0.67 and Capital Allocation at 1.0 are the weakest quality areas; either productive deployment or continued idle cash would move the business-quality score materially.
- Receivable days beyond the disclosed 49-day starting point, and whether payment terms stay firm — management's refusal to loosen terms to chase dispatches is a core plank of the Disciplined stewards check and the Corporate Culture pattern.
