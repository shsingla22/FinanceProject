**Checks that improved:**

- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: A professional non-family bench (CEO, CFO, Head of Strategy, Treasury/IR) runs the calls and management explicitly prioritises building a durable franchisee-partner base over store count, but no formal internal talent pipeline is described. | The long view had said: Essentially no discussion of systematic talent development or a leadership pipeline beyond generic praise for the team, despite a professional CEO/CFO layer being in place alongside the promoter.
- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Good → Excellent. Now: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.73 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: The FOCO model pushes inventory and store capex onto franchisees (43% of revenue by June 2025) and the return profile has improved, but the business is still gold-inventory heavy with stock turns of only 2–2.5x. | The long view had said: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.73 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: Jewellery retail is inherently inventory-heavy with stock turns of only ~2-2.5x, though the shift to franchisee-funded (FOCO) stores and gold metal loans moves the model progressively asset-lighter.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Neutral → Weak. Now: Headline results (PAT +89% for FY26) are heavily flattered by a near-100% rise in gold prices and a sector-wide boom, making it hard to separate managerial skill from the commodity tailwind. | The long view had said: Results must be discounted for a large exogenous tailwind — a near-100% rise in gold prices and a sector-wide exceptional year — though genuine skill shows in SSSG outperformance versus listed peers and share taken from stumbling competitors.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Non-South India is accelerating faster than the home South market and the first UK store plus US pilots are open, but Middle East revenue growth is modest (INR1,074cr vs INR784cr) and overseas remains unproven. | The long view had said: A methodical, well-executed multi-year geographic push — franchise pilots in 2022 scaled to nationwide non-South expansion, plus Middle East, and first UK/US showrooms — with the non-South revenue share proving the execution.
- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Management quantifies the procurement pilot's benefit precisely, discloses Candere's losses quarter by quarter and explains the deliberate pause in debt repayment, though the Q4 gross-margin reversal was answered somewhat evasively. | The long view had said: Management repeatedly owns misses and quantifies bad news without prompting — the missed PBT target from overspending on marketing, the INR120-130 crore custom-duty inventory hit, and a budgeting error on advance tax — and declines to guide where uncertain.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: Gold jewellery is a trust purchase and the brand's assurance is evidenced by strong franchisee demand and 30%+ SSSG despite volatile prices, though the calls never discuss purity/certification as an explicit differentiator. | The long view had said: The proposition is certainty of purity and fair dealing — management even declines to sell lab-grown diamonds to avoid a price crash betraying customer trust, and welcomes tighter hallmarking enforcement.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Barriers are low — a store is essentially working capital plus a franchisee's cheque — and Kalyan itself is adding 150 stores a year alongside peers, indicating capacity addition rather than restrained industry structure. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.8 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Inventory intensity is a real capital barrier and Kalyan avoids discount wars and refuses to chase lab-grown pricing, but heavy competitive activity on installment and exchange schemes plus new LGD entrants signal only moderate rationality.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: Jewellery buying carries essentially no switching costs and no captive aftermarket; customers can and do compare across chains and local jewellers on every purchase, with nothing in the calls indicating lock-in. | The long view had said: Only modest lock-in exists through gold savings schemes at a stable 18-20% of revenue and old-gold exchange, but customers face no real switching cost in jewellery retail.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Growth is largely gold-price pass-through with volumes being deliberately trimmed, and mix gains (18kt, silver/platinum ~2-2.5% of revenue, lightweight Candere) are small; margin improvement came from procurement terms (0.2-0.3%), not pricing power. | The long view had said: Mix is genuinely improving (rising studded share, 18/14/9-karat introduction, higher-margin non-South mix) and gold-price inflation lifts realisation, but much of the growth is volume from ~130-150 new stores a year rather than pricing power.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Calls are business-focused and led by an executive director plus professional CEO/CFO with no self-promotion, but this is a heavily advertised consumer brand with a highly visible promoter family, so it is not an under-the-radar management. | The long view had said: Management refuses to engage with share-price commentary and redirects to execution, and calls are substance-heavy, though a promoter-family brand built on heavy celebrity advertising is not truly low-profile.

*Unchanged: Advertisement and promotion; Intangible benefits; Cost of working capital; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Customer types (retail vs corporate); Mini-monopolies; Security by obscurity; Convenience benefits; Cyclical market growth; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Disciplined stewards; Structural end-market growth; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Distribution advantage; Profit margin; Distribution to shareholders; Return on capital (headline); Gain in market share; Share donators.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: This is the dominant structural story of the year: capital-light FOCO franchising now at 43% of revenue with 160+ showrooms added over three years, third-party franchisee capital funding growth, 129 launches in FY26 with 150 planned, plus the Candere omnichannel/D2C build (110 stores, revenue +160%, PAT positive from H2 FY26) and overseas own-store pilots. (The long view had said: The clearest pattern in the record: from the first franchise pilot in Aurangabad (Aug 2022) to franchisee-funded capex (Aug 2024, '0.25% margin growth'), 129 showrooms opened in FY26, 150 planned for FY27, Candere D2C-to-omnichannel, and an announced Middle East FOCO conversion drive with Arab investors — third parties fund the store growth.)
- **Low Price Plus**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is a genuine and quantified cost/capital-efficiency culture — the lean-credit procurement pilot worth 0.2–0.3% of gross margin, the capital-light FOCO model, working-capital and debt reduction — but nothing in the four calls about passing savings to customers as lower price or competing on price at all. (The long view had said: the calls are silent and no computable check supports the pattern.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Value growth held up remarkably through a near-doubling of gold prices (Q4 FY26 revenue +66%, SSSG 30%+), which shows demand resilience to a rising ticket, but management attributes margin gains to procurement and product/region mix rather than its own price increases, and it trims volumes/shifts to 18kt to protect affordability. (The long view had said: Realisations track the gold price rather than management pricing decisions; across FY25-FY26 the response to high gold prices was to trim inventory volume, push 18/14/9-karat lower-value product, and run exchange offers and promotions, and in Aug 2024 they raised ad spend rather than price to hold share, missing the PBT target that quarter.)

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Brand investment is visible and works — the Candere campaign drove a 75%+ rise in footfall and conversions, and the name is being stretched into new formats and geographies (UK showroom, US/UK pilots) — but the excerpts carry no heritage/provenance story, A&P quantum, or evidence of premium pricing from the brand. (The long view had said: Sustained ~1.8-2% of sales on advertising through every call, brand trust cited as the reason they will not sell unstable-priced lab-grown diamonds (Aug 2024), and the name is stretched into adjacencies — Candere omnichannel (110 stores, PAT-positive FY26), a new regional brand, and UK/US pilots — while non-South share rose from ~30% (2022) to 49% (2024).)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No R&D spend, pipeline, named product launches with revenue impact, or share-of-industry-R&D discussion appears in any of the four calls; new formats are retail-format launches, not disclosed innovation economics. (The long view had said: There is regular product/format renewal — fresh festive collections, a push into 18/14/9-karat studded lines, the Candere digital-first platform and a new regional brand — but no R&D disclosure or industry-R&D-share argument; innovation here is merchandising, not a funded innovation engine.)

*Unchanged: Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Across four calls no revenue, pricing or tender is policy-determined — demand is festive/wedding retail; the only government touchpoint is a one-off Rs 41.5 crore Labour Code cost provision in Q3 FY26, a cost item rather than a revenue dependency. (The long view had said: No government revenue, but policy repeatedly moves the P&L: the July 2024 customs duty cut caused an INR 120-130 crore inventory hit (and a demand fillip), BIS hallmarking rules were discussed as reshaping the competitive field, a Labour Code change forced a Rs 41.5 crore exceptional provision in Q3 FY26, and gold import channels are governed by IGST notifications and the GIFT City bullion exchange.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Three of the four calls open by flagging gold-price volatility, and FY26's 43% revenue growth came against a period an analyst described as gold rising 'almost near 100%' — i.e. much of the growth is metal price, not volume, with management explicitly trimming tonnage in own and FOCO stores and raising the 18-karat mix to protect affordability; the Q4 call ends with an analyst asking whether growth can be sustained on such an exceptional base. (The long view had said: Revenue and margins are visibly levered to the gold price cycle — management repeatedly frames quarters around 'extreme volatility in gold prices', a customs-duty-driven price fall cost INR 120-130 crore of inventory value in FY25, and by Feb 2026 gold was up ~100% forcing inventory tonnage to be trimmed and karatage re-mixed. But demand is consumption/wedding-led rather than tied to any customer's capex, and the company kept expanding (129 showrooms in FY26) through the volatility.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Technological Innovation (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: New Entrants (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
