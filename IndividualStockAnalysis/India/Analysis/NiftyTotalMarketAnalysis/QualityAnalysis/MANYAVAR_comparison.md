### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 3 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: LIKELY FIT → NO FIT. Why now: In the current window pricing power failed the test: when GST rose from 12% to 18% on 90% of products, management chose not to raise MRP and absorbed it, compressing gross margin from 66.9% (Q1) to 65.0% (Q4), while simultaneously introducing lower/'more competitive' price points and value-for-money positioning amid only 2.7% FY26 SSG. (The long view had said: Growth in ASP has come from merchandising mix rather than explicit price increases (Aug 2022: ASP growth just 1.4% with management saying they don't take direct price increases), and by Feb–May 2026 they absorbed the 12%→18% GST rather than raise MRP, taking gross margin from 68.8% to 65% — evidence of conditional, not robust, pricing power.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls the revenue model is purely one-off product sales — 'sale of our customers' (retail) versus primary dispatches to stores — with no licence fees, AMC, subscription, renewal or deferred-revenue discussion anywhere. (The long view had said: Across all 16 calls there is no discussion of licence fees, AMC/service contracts, subscriptions, attach rates or renewal revenue; franchisee security deposits are mentioned only as a receivables offset, not as a recurring revenue stream.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The company runs a premium, high-margin model (65-67% gross margin, 44% EBITDA) with heavy marketing spend, the opposite of a scale-driven low-price, high-asset-turn culture; there is no cost-obsession or itemised-savings language. (The long view had said: The 'superior quality and value for money' mantra, the asset-light franchise model and ~98% ROCE fit the high-return side, but the economics are the opposite of low-price-plus — 65-69% gross margins and 44-51% EBITDA margins, with no itemised cost-culture disclosure.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Forward Integrators (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Demand swings hard with the wedding-date calendar and discretionary consumer sentiment rather than any commodity or customer-capex cycle: Q1 FY26 rebounded 23% purely on a better wedding calendar, Q3 was hit by fewer December dates and no January dates, and management has flagged weak mid-premium sentiment through all four calls. Bounded because it is occasion-driven consumer demand, not a price cycle, and margins stayed in a narrow 65-67% gross / 43-46% EBITDA band all year. (The long view had said: Demand is discretionary and calendar-bound rather than commodity- or capex-driven, but the swings are large: Q1 FY25 'behaved like a non-wedding quarter' with sales collapsing, and Q3 FY26 was wrecked by 'no weddings in January at all... vis-a-vis last year, there were 11 wedding dates', driving EBITDA margin from 51% (Q1 FY23) to ~44% (FY26). Revenue is tied to weddings/festivals in consumers' lives, not to any customer's capacity expansion, so this is occasion-timing volatility layered on a discretionary consumption cycle, not a pure commodity cycle.)
- **Fashion Risk**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Growth narrative leans heavily on celebrity and trend moments — the Karan Johar 'Manyavar Shaadi Show', the Rashmika Mandanna / Vijay Deverakonda 'Made for Each Other' campaign with 1 billion+ views, Rinku Singh — and marketing spend is the lever management cites when asked what is driving performance, which means brand heat must be continually repurchased. Tempered by the fact that the underlying purchase occasion (weddings) is functional and recurring rather than taste-dependent. (The long view had said: Management repeatedly credits growth and brand salience to celebrity moments — Alia Bhatt for Mohey, the Virat-Anushka campaign, and the Vijay Deverakonda-Rashmika 'Made for Each Other' campaign with 1bn+ views described as carrying the brand 'for the next 5 to 6 years' — and concedes merchandise mix churns faster than two years. Taste-led salience that needs periodic celebrity refresh is a real, if bounded, dependency.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Technological Innovation (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
