# SBFC Finance (SBFC) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

SBFC Finance is an Indian non-bank lender (an NBFC listed on the NSE and BSE, headquartered in Mumbai) that makes small secured loans to micro and small business owners — the self-employed shopkeepers, restaurateurs and traders in India's smaller towns who otherwise borrow at very high rates. Almost the entire book (99%) is backed by collateral, either the borrower's property or their gold, with typical MSME ticket sizes in the sub-₹20 lakh range. It earns money the classic lending way: it borrows wholesale (mainly bank loans, plus market borrowings) at around 8.4% and lends at around 17.9%, pocketing the spread of roughly 9.4% after paying for branches and loan losses; quarterly profit was ₹130 crores in Q1 FY27 on an AUM of ₹11,922 crores. Distribution is a physical branch network — 256 branches as of June 2026, present in only about one-fourth of the districts of the states it operates in, which management frames as its main runway for growth.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Secured MSME loans ("ME" / small business loans against property)** — The core business — secured loans to small business owners in small towns, mostly against their property; MSME AUM was ₹9,271 crores of the ₹11,922 crore total (roughly 78%) in June 2026, with quarterly disbursements of about ₹809 crores, and management calls it the focus category and estimates the addressable market at about ₹4 lakh crores.
- **Loan against gold** — Lending against customers' gold jewellery at yields of roughly 18%–21%, which management guides to keep at about 20% of the book; it stood at ₹2,631 crores (21% of AUM) in June 2026 and was the main driver of a step-up in overall portfolio yield in FY25.
- **Co-origination with banks** — Loans originated jointly with banks rather than fully on SBFC's own balance sheet, run at a guided ~20% of disbursals and book; a regulatory circular effective April 2026 barring banks from taking collateral on MSME loans under ₹20 lakh temporarily cut this mix to 10%, which management expects to return to the normal run rate.
- **Branch network and distribution** — The physical delivery arm that sources and collects the loans — 251 branches at end-FY26 after adding 46 that year, 256 by June 2026, with a target of stabilising around 275; management treats cutting operating cost as a share of AUM (down 161 basis points since listing, to 4.29%) as one of its three core jobs alongside cost of funds and credit cost.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*
