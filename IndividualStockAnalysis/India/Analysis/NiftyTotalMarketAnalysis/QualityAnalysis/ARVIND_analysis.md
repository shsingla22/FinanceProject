## The verdict: Decent — 52 out of 100 ★★★☆☆

In one breath: the quality framework finds a good business today (67/100); it strongly fits 1 of the 11 patterns long-term winners share; the risk review found 1 high risk (Government Dependency). Weighing those together gives 52 out of 100 — decent.

**The exact arithmetic:** Overall = 45% × 67 (business quality) + 30% × 25 (multibagger fit) + 25% × 58 (risk safety) = 52 out of 100 → Decent (3 stars).

- **Business quality (67/100):** The 34-check quality framework scored the business +0.67 on its −2 (poor) to +2 (excellent) scale, with 91% of checks backed by evidence; mapped onto 0–100 that is 67 points.
- **Multibagger fit (25/100):** the foundation test partly passed (+10); 1 pattern fit strongly (Corporate Culture) (+15) → 25 of 100.
- **Risk safety (58/100):** started from a clean 100; 1 high risk (Government Dependency) (−20); 1 elevated (−10); 3 worth watching (−12) → 58 of 100.

### The story in depth

Arvind comes out of these three reviews as a well-run, honestly managed company that is buying and building its way toward better businesses than the one it started with — which is why the combined rating lands at 52 out of 100, or "Decent" (three stars): 45% weight on business quality scored 67, 30% on multibagger fit scored only 25, and 25% on risk safety scored 58. Management is the clear standout, the highest-scoring quality area at 1.17, and Candor is the single highest-scoring check in the whole framework. Management repeatedly talks down flattering numbers — warning against reading too much into the 32% Advanced Materials quarter, disclosing that chunky one-off defence orders inflated it, quantifying the tariff discounts it is absorbing, and calling the Bangladesh disruption a near-term negative. That same honesty shows up in the Corporate Culture pattern, the only STRONG FIT of the eleven patterns tested, supported by long-tenured family leadership and a record of delivering against stated guidance. The financial base underneath is steady rather than spectacular: about ₹11 of operating profit per ₹100 of sales with only about ±1.1 points of yearly variation, and ₹15 of operating cash per ₹100 of capital employed, with return on capital improving 150 basis points to 16%.

The uncomfortable connection is that the very candor that scores highest is also what documents the weakest part of the business. Management itself says fabric is replicable — "huge amount of capacity available in the country and in the world" — and that brands can buy fabric from many suppliers. That single admission is why Cost to Replicate only partially fits (the largest segment fails the test outright), why Pricing Power is a flat NO FIT with the evidence pointing the other way (roughly ₹25 crores a quarter of tariff discounts absorbed, realisations flat excluding cotton, brands running reverse online auctions to find the cheapest supplier), and why Good Enough Goods sits at ELEVATED risk. Competitive Advantage and Industry Structure both score just 0.5. The company's answer to this is to move capital away from fabric and toward garmenting and Advanced Materials — ₹348 crores of growth capex in nine months with about ₹400 crores a year planned — and to buy the Dalco-GFT nonwovens business in the US at 7.75x EBITDA on a $136mn enterprise value. Dalco is the one place where genuinely strong economics appear: 40% pre-deal return on capital, 17% margins, sole-sourced spec-in automotive parts with 5-6 year platform lives that customers do not reopen. But that is exactly why Toll Roads only partially fits — those toll-road economics belong to a business bought in May 2026, not to Arvind's own base. The bolt-on that most improves the story is also the one that has been owned the shortest.

The same pattern of one trait cutting both ways runs through the growth story and the risks. Nearly every forward driver is a policy driver: the pending UK and EU free-trade agreements granting duty-free access, PM MITRA parks and labour-law reform, defence orders in Advanced Materials, and a signed US infrastructure bill pushing geotextile demand beyond available supply. Those same items are what earn credit under Cyclical market growth, Structural end-market growth and Geographical expansion, and they are also the entire basis of the one HIGH RISK verdict, Government Dependency. Likewise, the deliberate reduction of Bangladesh exposure is scored as a strength under Geographical expansion while Bangladesh still shows up as a WATCH-level Stakeholder Concentration risk, alongside Dalco's own narrow base of roughly 75 customers reached through six Tier-1 automotive suppliers. Denim volumes up 16% at full capacity support the Market Share Gainers case, but denim is a taste-driven category and carries the Fashion Risk WATCH. And the Disciplined stewards check, built on a record of low long-term debt and stable net debt plus a stated two-year paydown plan, is explicitly tempered by the fact that this large acquisition was debt-funded and breaks the organic-only pattern. Overall resilience reads SOUND, and eight of eleven patterns land at PARTIAL — real direction of travel, not yet proof. That mix of honest management, improving returns and a still-commoditised core is what produces a Decent rating rather than a better or worse one.

- **Merger and acquisition — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: Dalco-GFT is a genuinely complementary bolt-on in an adjacent needle-punch nonwoven platform, already strong (17% margin, 40% pre-deal ROC) and bought at 7.75x EBITDA with management rolled over, but at $136mn EV funded entirely by fresh debt it is not small, and OPM uplift is aspirational rather than demonstrated.
  > "We have acquired 61% stake in the company at a valuation of 7.75x of calendar year 2025 reported EBITDA" — *management, on an earnings call*

- **Research and development — Neutral** *(from the conference calls)*: Innovation is referenced as a capex filter and as a platform capability, but no R&D spend, intensity or product-vitality metrics are disclosed to judge efficiency.
  > "We are only investing in innovation-based or differentiation-based capex" — *management, on an earnings call*

**Return on Capital — Good (+0.67)** · 3 of 3 checks had evidence

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** Family-led (Lalbhai) with long-tenured leadership (Jayesh Shah as Group CFO across the five-year record), repeated delivery against stated guidance (AMD's 18-20% aspiration, garment capacity build to 55-60 million pieces, AA rating one notch above parent), candour about dependencies (admitting Bangladesh disruption hurts Arvind short term, tariff discounts quantified) and explicit conservatism on leverage with a plan to wind down acquisition debt.

> "we are not off is to not have long-term debt on our books... our aim would be to almost wind this down over the next couple of years" — *management, on an earnings call*

*"Small cost, can't skip it"*

> "almost 85% or maybe slightly higher than that even as sole sourced by the customer from only the company. So it's a deep relationship." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Operating margin 11%, swings of ±1.1 points, median return on capital 13% — good but not the near-monopoly profile of a toll road.
- ❌ Operating margin of 11% — too thin for a protected franchise.

*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

> "Higher volumes and timely management action on costs helped us partially offset the tariff impact." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹52 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 1.1× a year — not the high-turnover, low-margin machine this pattern needs.
