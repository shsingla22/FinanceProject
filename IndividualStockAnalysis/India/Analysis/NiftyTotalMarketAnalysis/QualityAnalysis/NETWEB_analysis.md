# Netweb Technol. (NETWEB) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Netweb designs, builds and sells high-end computing hardware — supercomputers (HPC), private-cloud/hyperconverged systems and AI GPU servers — which it manufactures in-house in Faridabad, India, and describes itself as India's largest OEM and only full-stack domestic provider in high-end computing. Its customers are large Indian enterprises and government/public-sector bodies (public-sector banks buying private cloud, oil & gas firms running HPC, national AI-compute projects), plus high-frequency trading firms like Graviton, NK Securities, Tower Research and Societe Generale. It makes money by winning tenders and large orders and then billing on delivery of the systems, with gross margins management guided at roughly 27% and EBITDA margins running around 12-14%; revenue is lumpy because a single big order can land in one quarter. It sells mainly in India under the government's Make-in-India and India AI Mission push, with exports a small slice (about 7.9% of revenue in the first nine months of FY25), and it grew revenue 90% in FY26 to about Rs 21,836 million with PAT of Rs 2,058 million.

**The parts of the business:**

- **AI systems** — Designs and manufactures AI GPU servers, including systems built on NVIDIA's Blackwell GB200 platform under an OEM partnership; it is now the biggest and fastest-growing part, at 43.4% of FY26 revenue (64% in Q3 FY26 alone) after growing 459.6% in FY26, up from just 14.7% of revenue in the first nine months of FY25.
- **High-Performance Computing (HPC) / supercomputing** — Supercomputers sold to research institutions, oil & gas explorers and trading firms that use them to develop and test algorithms; one of the three 'core pillars', described as growing steadily at over 20% year-to-date in FY26 though its share has been diluted by the AI ramp.
- **Private Cloud and HCI (hyperconverged infrastructure)** — On-premise private-cloud and hyperconverged data-centre systems, sold heavily into public-sector banks and other large enterprises via tenders; the third core pillar, also cited as growing over 20% year-to-date in FY26 on a booming data-centre market.
- **Strategic (national-scale) orders** — Very large government-linked contracts to build India's sovereign AI compute infrastructure, tracked separately from the regular 'organic' order book — one such order worth Rs 4,504 million was executed in Q3 FY26, with a strategic order book of Rs 17,336 million still outstanding at that point versus an organic order book of Rs 5,258 million.
- **Exports / international business** — Sales outside India, a small but deliberately growing slice that management put at about 7.9% of revenue for the first nine months of FY25.
- **New/emerging products (switches, 5G ORAN, own manufacturing line)** — Early-stage additions flagged in FY24 — Netweb-branded network switches, 5G ORAN equipment, an Intel licence for next-gen high-end computing systems, ARM/RISC-based low-power servers, and its own surface-mount (SMT) manufacturing line — described as future opportunities rather than meaningful revenue at the time.

### The story in depth

Netweb comes out as a genuinely good business attached to a genuinely lumpy revenue stream, which is why the combined rating lands at 51 out of 100 — 'Decent', three stars — from 65 on business quality, 41 on multibagger fit and 38 on risk safety, with overall resilience judged sound. The quality work is where it looks best: revenue grew 90% to Rs 21,836 million in FY26 on essentially no new plant, with gross fixed asset turnover of 33.2x, ROCE of 37.5%, ROE of 32.9%, zero net debt and Rs 833 million of net free cash. Growth was entirely organic, with no acquisitions and capex kept routine. Behind that sits sustained in-house R&D that has kept turning into new product lines — an Intel next-generation architecture licence, ARM and RISC systems, its own switches, 5G ORAN, high-performance storage and NVIDIA Blackwell GB200-based AI systems — which lifted the AI segment from a mid-teens share of revenue to 43.4%. The end market is expanding on its own account too: sovereign AI compute, the India AI Mission, and a national data-centre build-out planned to go from about 1.5 GW to 9 GW by 2030. On the pattern screen, the same evidence produces the strongest result of the whole exercise — Corporate Culture is a strong fit because the management team promised things in early calls and then delivered them: guidance of 30-35% growth was beaten (60% in FY25, 90% in FY26), exports promised in FY24 reached roughly 9% by FY25, margin guidance held for three years, and a strategic order guided for the fourth quarter arrived in the third.

The awkward part is that the single trait doing most of the good work is also the single largest risk. Netweb's share gains are explicitly helped by government policy — DGFT server import restrictions, cyber-security concerns and Make-in-India preference — which is what makes foreign OEMs 'structurally disadvantaged donors of share' and gives Netweb a clear pool to take from. That is scored as a strength on two separate quality checks and underpins the Market Share Gainers pattern. It is also, word for word, the reason Government Dependency is rated high risk: government was around 53-55% of revenue at listing, the import restrictions were framed by management itself as the growth opening back in August 2023, and by FY26 the bulk of the order book sits on the government side. The concentration risk is the same story at the customer level. One strategic order of Rs 4,504 million produced 64% of third-quarter FY26 revenue, and Rs 17,336 million of order book — more than three times the organic book of Rs 5,258 million — belongs to that same counterparty programme. The growth that makes the pattern screen look good is therefore the growth that makes the risk review look bad. Cyclicality, rated elevated, is the arithmetic consequence: this is infrastructure bought in capex programmes, not consumed month to month, so the record swings from -13% in one quarter of FY24 to +141% year-on-year and +165% quarter-on-quarter in the third quarter of FY26.

The second connected thread is pricing. Management deliberately runs modest margins — 13-14% EBITDA guidance held unchanged from FY24 to FY26 despite 90% revenue growth, gross margins flat near 27%, and 200 basis points of lower pre-tax margin accepted on the big strategic order — and has explicitly declined to raise prices when it could, on the view that the customer would not feel good about it. Combined with very high asset turns, that gives a partial fit to the Low Price Plus pattern, and it is plainly part of why share is being won. The same behaviour is why Pricing Power scores no fit at all, and why Good Enough Goods sits on watch, since a large share of orders is won through lowest-bidder government tendering. Four patterns fail outright, and they fail for one shared reason: this is one-off hardware sold directly to end payers. Recurring Revenue is no fit — software and services was guided at only 2-4% of revenue and no renewal, attach-rate or deferred-revenue discussion appears in twelve calls. Friendly Middleman is no fit because there is no channel professional between Netweb and the buyer. Toll Roads is no fit because these are large capital purchases — single orders of Rs 450 crore — priced in competitive tenders, not small unnegotiated inputs. That structure also shows in the working capital: about 121 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, the one clearly negative check, and it explains why the headline cash return on capital is 17% even with a 37.5% ROCE. What is real and defensible is the competitive advantage area, which scores full marks — full-stack in-house design that no other domestic player offers, direct Intel and NVIDIA relationships giving priority allocation of scarce components — but that too rests on silicon Netweb does not own, so every platform generation forces a redesign, which is why technological innovation sits on watch. Capital allocation is the weakest scored area at 0.25.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
