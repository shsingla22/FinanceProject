# Zydus Lifesci. (ZYDUSLIFE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Zydus Lifesciences is an India-based pharmaceutical company (listed in Mumbai as ZYDUSLIFE) that makes and sells medicines — mostly copies of off-patent drugs (generics) plus its own branded and newly invented drugs — along with consumer health products and, more recently, medical devices. It earns money by manufacturing pills, injectables, biologic drugs and active ingredients at its own plants in India (Ahmedabad, Jarod/Vadodara and elsewhere) and selling them to US and Canadian generic-drug buyers, to doctors and chemists in India through a branded salesforce, and to distributors across emerging markets and Europe; consumer brands are sold to shoppers in shops and online. Its two biggest revenue pools are North America and India, with international markets, consumer wellness and medical devices adding the rest; total consolidated revenue was ₹271.5 billion in the year to March 2026, up 17%, with an EBITDA margin of 31.2%. It also spends heavily on R&D to invent new drugs of its own (such as Saroglitazar) and to treat very rare diseases through its US subsidiary Sentynl, and has bought several businesses (LiqMeds, Comfort Click, Amplitude Surgical) to widen what it sells.

**The parts of the business:**

- **North America formulations (US and Canada)** — Sells generic copies of prescription drugs plus a growing 'specialty' and rare-disease range in the US and Canada, filing and launching dozens of products a year; it is the single largest business — revenue crossed US$1 billion in FY24 and it was ₹29.5 billion in the March 2026 quarter, with management saying Zydus ranks among the top 3 US generic players.
- **India branded formulations** — Sells prescription medicines under Zydus brand names to Indian doctors and pharmacies across cardiology, respiratory, dermatology, pain, gynaecology and the super-specialty areas of oncology and nephrology (where it holds leadership positions); it has grown 14-15% a year recently, faster than the market, with long-term/'chronic' medicines now 46.3% of the mix.
- **Consumer Wellness** — Everyday health and personal-care products sold in shops and online — Indian brands like Glucon-D, Nycil and Everyuth, protein snacks (Ritebite Max Protein), and since 2025 the UK-based Comfort Click/WeightWorld vitamins-and-supplements business selling across the UK, EU and US; revenue was ₹14.6 billion in the March 2026 quarter, up 61%.
- **International markets formulations (emerging markets and Europe)** — Sells generic and niche medicines outside India and North America — across emerging markets plus retail pharmacy operations in France, Spain and the UK; smaller but fast-growing, at ₹8 billion in the March 2026 quarter, up 45% year-on-year.
- **Medical Devices (MedTech)** — Newly built third business vertical selling surgical and medical hardware, anchored by the acquired French orthopaedic implant maker Amplitude Surgical plus a move into kidney and heart devices; still small at about ₹3.3 billion a quarter.
- **Innovation / NCE research and specialty pipeline** — In-house invention of brand-new drug molecules — Saroglitazar (sold in India as Lipaglyn and heading for a US filing), Desidustat and ZYIL1 — plus 505(b)(2) specialty products; not a separate revenue line but management presents it as the main long-term growth engine.
- **Biologics and biosimilars** — Develops and makes complex protein-based drugs and cheaper copies of them, including the world-first Nivolumab biosimilar (Tishtha) and an Aflibercept biosimilar in India, licensed Pembrolizumab and Ranibizumab for the US, and a recently acquired US biologics plant used both for its own pipeline and contract supply to partners such as Agenus.
- **Sentynl Therapeutics (US rare disease)** — Wholly owned US subsidiary that markets treatments for very rare children's genetic disorders — Nulibry for MoCD Type A and Zycubo (copper histidinate) for Menkes disease — now with three marketed rare-disease products; small in revenue but described as a high-value niche.
- **API business** — Makes the active chemical ingredients that go into finished medicines, from its own USFDA-inspected Ahmedabad plant; mentioned mainly in connection with regulatory inspections rather than as a large separate revenue line.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Zydus Lifesciences comes across as a well-run, honestly managed business whose strongest scores sit in the places that are hardest to fake. The Candor check scores full marks because guidance has been specific, deliberately conservative and then repeatedly beaten — the August 2022 promise of 20%-plus operating margin for FY23 was met, FY24 came in at 27.5% and FY26 at 31.2% — and the Corporate Culture pattern is rated a strong fit for exactly that reason: it is the same track record of keeping promises, seen from a different angle. Underneath the promises is real operating substance. The company keeps ₹31 of operating profit out of every ₹100 of sales with margins that have wobbled only about ±3.8 points a year, and keeps ₹73 of every ₹100 after direct input costs. Sales have compounded at roughly 11% a year with unusually little year-to-year variation, which the framework treats as the ideal steady band. Research spending has been held at a disciplined 7.5-8% of revenue for years and has visibly turned into named products — Desidustat approved in China, Saroglitazar heading to a US filing, a world-first Nivolumab biosimilar, Aflibercept — which is why Innovation Dominance is also marked a strong fit. Management, competitive advantage and growth all score at the top of the quality areas.

The growth patterns and the risks are, in several places, the same fact told twice. Share gain is the clearest strength: India branded formulations have beaten the wider Indian pharma market quarter after quarter at 14-15% growth with chronic therapies rising from 41.2% to 46.3% of the book, and the US base business gained volume share while peers declined — so both the Gain in Market Share check and the Market Share Gainers pattern score well. Geographic expansion is equally credible, with UK operations started in FY24, a France and Spain retail build-out, WeightWorld entering Poland, Finland and Portugal, and international formulations growing 38-45% a year. But the engine underneath all of this is US generics at roughly 40-47% of revenue, and that is precisely where the risk review concentrates. The scale that makes the cost culture work — spend-base analysis worth 50-70 basis points a year, top-three US generic standing — sits in a market the review calls structurally 'good enough', where the intermediary picks the product and price erosion never stops. That is why Toll Roads is a clear no-fit and Pricing Power only a likely fit: pricing strength is real in pockets such as consumer wellness, where gross margin expanded 377 basis points on calibrated price increases, but not in the largest segment. The same trait shows up again in the New Entrants risk: FY27 margin guidance has already been cut to 24%-plus on expected Revlimid and Mirabegron competition, and guidance is routinely framed around assumed competition in Asacol.

The defences against that erosion are genuine but still small relative to the base, and this is where the story turns into a race. The Sentynl rare-disease portfolio gives literal single-product monopolies — Zycubo the only approved Menkes therapy, Nulibry the only MoCD Type A therapy, Zokinvy the only Progeria therapy — and these, with the decade-long independently funded Saroglitazar programme and a network of USFDA-cleared plants, are what the Cost to Replicate pattern rates as partially in place. Heritage consumer brands such as Glucon-D, Nycil and Everyuth lead their categories with skin and haircare up 39.7% in the fourth quarter of FY26, and the Comfort Click and WeightWorld acquisitions push the company toward owning its own digital shelf. The question is whether these legs grow faster than the generics base erodes, because the risk review flags concentration in a handful of named products — Asacol, Revlimid now running off, and Mirabegron with exclusivity riding on a jury trial — rather than in named customers. Two structural weaknesses reinforce the caution: about 212 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and the framework scores capital allocation, return on capital and industry structure as the three weakest areas. Regulatory dependence is the other overhang, with recurring inspection observations at Moraiya, Jarod and the Ahmedabad site and around four launches flagged as at risk in May 2024. Against that, cyclicality, shifting consumer preferences and fashion risk are all rated low because demand is prescription-led and repeat. The combined verdict — 79 out of 100, Strong, with resilience sound — reads as a high-quality, honestly run compounder whose main uncertainty is not management or execution but the arithmetic of replacing an eroding generics base fast enough.
