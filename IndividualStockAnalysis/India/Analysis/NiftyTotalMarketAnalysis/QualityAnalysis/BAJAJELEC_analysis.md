# Bajaj Electricals (BAJAJELEC) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Bajaj Electricals is an India-based, Mumbai-headquartered maker and seller of branded electrical goods for homes, sold under the Bajaj name (plus Morphy Richards) and, on the lighting side, to institutional and outdoor buyers as well. It earns money by manufacturing/sourcing and selling household products — fans, kitchen and small appliances such as mixers and induction cooktops, geysers, room heaters and summer cooling products — and lighting products including ceiling and outdoor lights, mainly by shipping stock to a deep network of distributors and dealers ('primary sales') who then sell through to retailers and consumers ('secondaries'), so quarterly results swing with channel inventory, the festive season (Diwali) and the summer season. It was historically also an EPC (engineering-procurement-construction) contractor, but that business was demerged in September 2023 into a separate listed entity, Bajel Projects Limited, leaving Bajaj Electricals a pure consumer company with two verticals: Consumer Products and Lighting Solutions. The company says it has been debt-free since March 2022, runs on negative working capital, and ended FY26 with about INR 934 crore of cash; it recently began extending the lighting business into adjacent electrical categories — switchgear, solar solutions and wires.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Consumer Products** — Fans, kitchen and small domestic appliances (mixers, induction cooktops), water heaters/geysers, room heaters and summer cooling products sold through distributors and retail; it is the larger of the two verticals and the main revenue driver, but also the volatile one — it grew ~58% year-on-year in Q1 FY23 and about 7.4% on a 3-year CAGR then, while in Q3 FY26 revenue fell 25% and EBIT went negative as management deliberately cut shipments to clear elevated channel inventory.
- **Lighting Solutions (earlier called the Illumination business)** — Lighting products including ceiling and outdoor lights, plus newly added adjacent categories — switchgear (entered Q2 FY26), solar solutions (Q3 FY26) and wires (launched Q4 FY26); management describes it as the consistent performer, growing 9% in Q3 FY26 and 16% in Q4 FY26, with annual revenue up 9.5% in FY26 and a record annual EBIT margin of about 8.5% (versus roughly 2% EBIT in the year-ago quarter), and it is run separately under its own COO, Rajesh Naik.
- **EPC (demerged — no longer part of the company)** — An engineering, procurement and construction project business (power transmission/infrastructure-type contracting) that had a large pending order book and contributed to over INR 2,000 crore of debt five years earlier; it was brought back to breakeven around FY23 and then demerged in September 2023 into a separate company, Bajel Projects Limited, so it no longer forms part of Bajaj Electricals' results.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Demand is consumption/flow-linked (fans, geysers, mixers, lighting) rather than tied to any customer's capex, but the calls show repeated, large swings driven by the commodity cycle and by weather-dependent summer products — management tracked an 18-month input-cost cycle in FY23, and by FY26 coolers fell 38-40% and CP EBIT went from 6-7% to negative on operating deleverage, with 3-10% price hikes chasing war-related inflation.

> "coolers are down by almost 38% to 40% versus our sales last year" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/consumption products used in households, not customer capex; EPC (project-cycle) business demerged in Sept 2023 leaving a pure consumer company; negative working capital and INR 934 crore cash allow continued brand and innovation investment through the downturn*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 5 of the last 10 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 3% and 8% (±1.3 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
