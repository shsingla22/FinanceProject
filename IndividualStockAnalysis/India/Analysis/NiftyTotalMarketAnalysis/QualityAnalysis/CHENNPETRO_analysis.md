# Chennai Petroleum Corporation Ltd. (CHENNPETRO) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: ENERGY*

Chennai Petroleum Corporation Limited (CPCL) is a government-owned oil refiner based at Manali, Chennai, and a group company of Indian Oil Corporation (IOCL). It buys crude oil — roughly 55%-60% under long-term term contracts priced off suppliers' monthly Official Selling Price (Basrah heavy/medium from Iraq, Arab grades from Saudi Aramco), the rest bought spot as opportunity cargoes, including Russian crude in FY23 — runs it through its three crude units, and sells the resulting fuels: diesel (HSD), petrol (MS), LPG, ATF, naphtha and a range of speciality products. It makes money on the gross refining margin, the per-barrel spread between what the crude costs and what the products fetch, which management tracks against the Singapore benchmark ($9.2/bbl vs $5.83 in FY26); profit therefore depends on running the plant hard (throughput was 11.71 MMT, or 112% of installed capacity, in FY26) and on keeping fuel-and-loss and energy use low. Products go mainly into the Indian market, with export used as an option to optimise realisations rather than a compulsion, and a large greenfield refinery is being built through a joint venture at Nagapattinam.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Refinery operations / crude throughput (the Manali refinery)** — The core business — three crude units processing about 10.5-11.7 million tonnes of crude a year at 99%-113% of rated capacity, producing petrol, diesel, LPG and other fuels; this is essentially the whole of the company's revenue and profit.
- **Gross Refining Margin (GRM) and crude sourcing** — How the money is actually made: management picks the crude mix (52%-66% high-sulphur depending on the year, term vs spot balanced for economics) and sells the product slate to earn a per-barrel margin that has consistently beaten the Singapore benchmark over the past five to six years.
- **Value-added and niche products** — Speciality streams sold alongside bulk fuels — food-grade and pharma-grade hexane, MTO, lean butane, LS naphtha, NATO-grade diesel, with pentane and textile-grade MTO in trial runs; volumes are small relative to fuels but management flags them repeatedly as record-setting and as the route into new, higher-margin markets.
- **Energy efficiency / fuel-and-loss reduction** — An in-house programme to cut the share of crude burned as refinery fuel (fuel and loss down from 9.71% to 7.73% over the period, Energy Intensity Index down to 84) partly by substituting purchased RLNG gas; management says this contributed a significant part of profits and is a main reason its margin beats the benchmark.
- **Cauvery Basin Refinery (CBR) joint venture, Nagapattinam** — A new 9 million-tonne greenfield refinery with about 6% polypropylene integration, built as a separate JV with Indian Oil at a revised cost of Rs. 36,354 crore and 25:75 equity — CPCL puts in equity (around Rs. 2,570 crore, spread over years) but no capex of its own, so it is an investment rather than an operating business today.
- **Growth and capex projects (Group II/III LOBS, retail outlets)** — Standalone spending is mostly maintenance capex of Rs. 200-250 crore a year (total capex Rs. 856 crore in FY26), on top of which the approved Group II/III lube base oil project is now under execution and about 300 retail outlet licences have been taken with commissioning expected from FY27.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Every check, area by area

**Capital Allocation — Neutral (+0.20)** · 5 of 6 checks had evidence

- **Cost of working capital — Good** *(from the financial statements)*: About 29 days of cash stays stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers — a short, healthy cycle.
- **Distribution to shareholders — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: Dividends are consistently paid and scale with profits (Rs 5/share FY25 rising to a record Rs 62/share including a first-ever interim in FY26), but the policy is discretionary and there are no buybacks.
  > "in addition to the interim dividend of INR8 per equity share of INR10 each, the Board has recommended a final dividend of INR54 per share, taking the total dividend for the year to INR62 per share" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance) — Neutral** *(from the conference calls)*: Capex is dominated by maintenance (Rs 200-300 cr/yr) with growth confined to the Rs 1,600 cr LOBS and Rs 400 cr retail outlet projects plus a 25% stake in a Rs 36,354 cr JV refinery whose incremental ROIC is unproven.
  > "LOBS project, Group 2, Group 3 LOBS project is INR1,600 crores. The retail outlet project is INR400 crores... Besides, every year, you can assume another INR500 crores is our normal capex" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Research and development — Neutral** *(from the conference calls)*: No formal R&D line, but continuous process/product development yields new value-added grades (pharma-grade hexane, SAF trial, pentane, textile-grade MTO) — incremental refinery optimisation rather than genuine R&D.
  > "we have launched trial runs of 2 new products. One is pentane, the other is textile grade MTO" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Merger and acquisition — Weak** *(from the conference calls)*: No bolt-ons; instead a single very large greenfield JV commitment (Cauvery Basin, Rs 36,354 cr, cost already revised up from Rs 31,500 cr) that is the opposite of small, simple and complementary.
  > "The revised capital cost is at Rs. 36,354 crores with a 25:75 equity holding between CPCL and Indian oil respectively" — *management, on an earnings call*
- *Not assessed (the evidence was silent — never guessed): Advertisement and promotion.*

**Return on Capital — Good (+0.67)** · 3 of 3 checks had evidence

- **Return on capital (headline) — Good** *(from the financial statements)*: For every ₹100 of capital in the business (shareholders' funds + borrowings), it generated ₹23 of operating cash this year — a 23% cash return on capital.

**Growth — Neutral (-0.33)** · 6 of 6 checks had evidence

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Low Price Plus — LIKELY FIT
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and the only computable check supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** Genuine, itemised and sustained cost obsession — opex $1.7/bbl, fuel & loss driven 9.06% (FY23) to 8.51% (FY25) to 7.73% (FY26), MBN 74.2 to 69.8, EII 89.2 to 84, first-quartile Solomon, and 108-112% capacity utilisation to spread fixed cost — but CPCL cannot set price at all, so the 'low price plus' consumer proposition is absent; it is cost leadership within a price-taking commodity.

> "CPCL achieved the best fuel and loss of 7.73%, best MBN of 69.8% and best EII of 84%" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹12 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 3.2× a year with 20% returns on capital — thin margins made up on volume, the low-price-winner profile.

> "we have launched trial runs of 2 new products. One is pentane, the other is textile grade MTO" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Market Share Gainers — NO FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls contradict the pattern — that grounded reading overrides any supportive numbers.

**What the calls show:** Capacity is fixed at 10.5 MMT with management stating expansion at Manali is not feasible, so volume growth comes only from running above nameplate; no market-share figures versus named competitors are given, and new HPCL/IOCL capacity is expected to add supply.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Government Dependency, Stakeholder Concentration. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.2× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 68% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 19% to 25% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** A pure commodity refiner whose earnings are set by crude-product spreads: FY23 was a 'lifetime best' on robust cracks, FY25 GRM collapsed to $4.22/bbl from $8.64 as HSD cracks fell from $13-15 to sub-$10 (dividend cut accordingly), then FY26 GRM rebounded to $9.2 with Q4 at $13.75 on geopolitics — management repeatedly refuses to guide on cracks and books inventory gains/losses (Rs 680 cr loss FY23, Rs 125 cr gain Q4FY25).

> "But the broad gross cracks I will be deriving from the market only." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/operations-linked demand (transport fuels, LPG), consistent >100% utilisation, structurally premium GRM vs Singapore, and net debt-equity down to 0.09 gives balance-sheet room through the trough*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 5 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between -6% and 9% (±4.3 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 3% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 5%).

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** A Government of India enterprise and IOCL group company whose fortunes are repeatedly moved by policy: ~Rs 4,000 cr windfall tax in FY23, Russian crude subject to the EU/India price cap, export duties in FY26 that analysts argue turn netbacks negative, RTP-based domestic realisations, Schedule-A/Navaratna reclassification, land acquired through government agencies, and the Rs 36,354 cr Cauvery refinery awaiting CCEA approval.

> "Full year, it would be in the range of approximately Rs. 4,000+ crores on a gross basis." — *management, on an earnings call*

### Stakeholder Concentration — ELEVATED
