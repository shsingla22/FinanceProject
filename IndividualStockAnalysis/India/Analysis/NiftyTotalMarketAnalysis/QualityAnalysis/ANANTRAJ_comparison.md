### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 0 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Across the four calls promises are made and then verifiably delivered: net-debt-free by Dec '24 was guided in Apr '24 (INR 290 cr), tracked in Oct '24 (INR 95 cr) and by Nov '25 net debt had been under INR 50 cr for five consecutive quarters; FY25 guidance of INR 1,800-1,900 cr topline / INR 370-390 cr PAT was beaten at INR 2,100 cr / INR 425 cr; 28MW was promised and physically showcased. Bad news is owned directly — the MD conceded the data-centre delay rather than deflecting — and promoters funded warrants 18 months early. (The long view had said: Several promises were kept: net-debt-free by December 2024 was guided in FY24 and delivered (net debt INR95 crore by Sep-24, then five straight quarters under INR50 crore), FY25 guidance of INR1,800-1,900 crore/INR370-390 crore PAT was beaten at INR2,100 crore/INR425 crore, promoters converted warrants early, and leadership is stable family management. Against that, the 300MW-in-four-years target from the FY24 call has quietly become 117MW by FY28 and the 28MW promised for December 2024 only showcased in August 2025, with no acknowledgement of the slippage.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: New offerings do arrive on a named schedule with revenue impact — co-location, then Ashok Cloud launched 7 Oct 2024, with a stated path from IaaS to PaaS and SaaS — but there is no disclosed R&D spend, no pipeline metrics and no claim to a share of industry R&D; the 'innovation' is service-line extension built on OEM technology. (The long view had said: No R&D spend is disclosed in any of the five calls; the cloud move was handed to them by a client with 'handholding', built on an Orange/OEM technical tie-up, with management admitting a lack of in-house expertise — the opposite of R&D-led dominance.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The land bank is the one genuinely hard-to-replicate asset — Sector 63A alone holds ~INR 10,000 cr of fully-paid, already-owned monetisable inventory plus ~100 acres in Delhi NCR carried on the balance sheet, which is a finite-location barrier; the data centre and cloud businesses, by contrast, are capital-and-OEM replicable and management itself frames the constraint as equipment lead times, not any proprietary edge. (The long view had said: Management's own articulation of the barrier — ready land, pre-certified buildings — is explicitly a three-to-four-year head start, exactly what a funded copycat can rebuild, and they concede pricing will move when competition arrives; the land bank is valuable but not un-replicable.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 1 unchanged; 4 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Both legs of the business run through government gatekeeping: every real estate launch is paced by RERA approvals, LOIs and Haryana group-housing licences discussed on every call, the Tirupati affordable-housing project sits on land allotted by a government development authority, and the cloud venture is built with PSU partner TCIL and positioned as a 'sovereign Cloud'. Pricing is not regulator-set, which bounds the risk. (The long view had said: The entire data centre demand thesis is stated as a government policy creation — data localisation law, a pending Data Protection Act, a proposed DC tax benefit, and a 'sovereign cloud' positioning — while the real estate arm depends on state licences, RERA and land approvals, and the historical roots are government construction contracts and government-allotted low-cost housing land.)
- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The growth story is now a data centre and cloud platform, an industry with fast-moving standards (AI/GPU density, cooling, hyperscaler platforms) where a purpose-built installed base can become legacy; management concedes limited in-house expertise in the cloud layer, having taken it on because a partner offered it. Exposure is bounded because the underlying asset is power-and-shell infrastructure and the innovation cadence described is incremental (colocation to IaaS to PaaS/SaaS). (The long view had said: A real-estate family firm is moving up a fast-moving technology stack — colocation to IaaS to promised PaaS/SaaS — explicitly targeting competition with global hyperscalers, on infrastructure specced years ago and with admittedly thin in-house expertise ('we do not have a lot of expertise in this segment'); a shift in workload density or AI-era cooling/GPU requirements would strand purpose-built assets.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Through the window a single land bank (Sector 63A, Gurugram) is repeatedly described as the dominant revenue contributor, and the entire cloud extension originated from and depends on one partner, TCIL; on the supply side management flagged dependence on imported data centre equipment with lengthening lead times and said it was hunting for alternative suppliers. The calls do not disclose data centre tenant mix, so anchor-tenant concentration is unverified. (The long view had said: Colocation clients are 75% government as of Nov 2025, and the same handful of state-linked counterparties — RailTel, TCIL, CAC — have been named as the client-acquisition channel since the May 2023 call, with TCIL also being the partner that originated the cloud business; land/project exposure is likewise concentrated in one Gurgaon micro-market (Sector 63A).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (ELEVATED); Good Enough Goods (ELEVATED); Fashion Risk (QUANT FLAG); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 11 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
