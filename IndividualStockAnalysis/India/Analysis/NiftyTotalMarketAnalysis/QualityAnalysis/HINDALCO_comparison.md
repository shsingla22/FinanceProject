### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 5 weakened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Novelis cites higher product pricing as an EBITDA driver in Q2 FY26 across North America and Europe and is passing tariff costs through to customers, but the underlying realisation remains LME- and scrap-spread-driven rather than management-set, and a $54 million net tariff hit still could not be recovered in-quarter. (The long view had said: Hindalco is structurally a price-taker: it hedges LME exposure quarter after quarter (30% at $2,579/t in Nov 2024, 64% at $2,807/t in Feb 2026), realisations move with global surplus/deficit, and copper conversion margins were deflated to record-low negative TC/RCs — the opposite of pricing power, only partly offset by downstream premiumisation.)

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management discloses bad news promptly and specifically — three fatalities reported in the FY26 year-end call, the Oswego plant fire and its leverage consequence quantified — and Satish Pai has led through FY22–FY26 with the cost programme's raised run-rate delivered as promised; but the CFO seat turned over in FY25 and net leverage guided toward 4x on negative free cash flow tempers the conservative-balance-sheet claim. (The long view had said: Stable leadership (Satish Pai MD throughout), bad news disclosed promptly and unflinchingly — fatalities named each year, the Sierre flood's $25m hit, the Oswego fires quantified and PAT shown both reported and adjusted — and promises made in earlier calls were delivered on schedule (Guthrie ramping FY25, Aditya FRP commissioned, Bay Minette on track), with cost-savings targets raised rather than missed and leverage held to a stated ~2x through a heavy capex cycle.)
- **Low Price Plus**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: There is a live, itemised cost programme — Novelis' $300 million three-year efficiency plan with organisation redesign and footprint rationalisation, and the exit run-rate raised above $125 million within Q2 FY26 — plus scale integration, but the calls show no low-price-plus-quality customer proposition and returns are currently pressured by tariffs and leverage. (The long view had said: The consistent thread across all years is cost position, not price: Utkal 'among the lowest cost producers of alumina in the world' (Apr 2023), first-decile global cost curve and 41%→45%→48% upstream EBITDA margins 'best in the global industry' (Nov 2024 through May 2026), captive Chakla/Meenakshi/Bandha coal mines to cut costs further, and Novelis' itemised structural savings run-rate raised $75m→$125m→$150m→$200m against a $350-400m FY28 goal.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The four calls in the last-12-months window discuss shipments, LME/scrap prices, tariffs and capex only; there is no mention of attach rates, service/AMC contracts, renewals, installed base or customer advances as a funding source. (The long view had said: Nothing in fifteen calls touches attach rates, AMC/service revenue, renewals, installed-base density or customer advances as a funding source; revenue is metal shipments priced off LME.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: R&D spend, named new products or pipeline economics are not disclosed anywhere in the four calls; capex commentary is capacity (Bay Minette, Logan de-bottlenecking) rather than innovation-led. (The long view had said: New value-added products are named and tracked to commissioning each year — battery foil, battery enclosures, AC coated fins, India's first 25 Kt Inner Grooved Tube plant, 50 Kt e-waste/copper recycling, plus Novelis' high-recycled-content alloys — but R&D spend is never disclosed or quantified as a share of industry R&D, and gross margins are commodity-driven rather than innovation-funded.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: In the last-12-months window volumes went backwards where demand was favourable: Q2 FY26 total shipments were only flat at 941kt and North America fell 7% on Logan ramp-up limits, footprint rationalisation and the Oswego fire; no share figures versus named competitors are given. (The long view had said: Real leadership share is disclosed (Novelis ~40% global can-sheet ex-China, ~30% North America, #1 can and auto position, 35% Europe FRP) and Bay Minette is 'nearly fully contracted through end of decade', but the later calls show shipments falling 3-4% year-on-year and domestic copper producer share dropping from 73% to 63%, so persistent share gain is not demonstrated.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The consumer/industrial preference shifts described in the calls run in Hindalco's favour rather than against it — sustainability-linked demand, recyclability, a ~30-37% surge in Chinese NEV production and rising solar installation all pull aluminium demand up, and management points to record festive-season consumption in India rather than eroding habits. (The long view had said: A large slice of Novelis volume rests on beverage consumption habits, which visibly wobbled — the FY23 Investor Day devoted slides to lower promotional activity, price elasticity and 'beer was down the most', with recovery framed as hopeful rather than certain. Offsetting this, the dominant preference shifts (sustainability, recyclability, EV lightweighting) run in aluminium's favour and management has pivoted the portfolio toward them.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: All four FY26 calls frame the business around LME price direction, global supply-demand balances and regional surpluses/deficits — the classic commodity cycle — while Hindalco/Novelis simultaneously run heavy expansionary capex ($1.9–2.2bn at Novelis alone, Bay Minette greenfield) into that cycle, with EBITDA/ton swinging on tariffs, scrap spreads and metal price lag. Novelis' YTD adjusted free cash outflow of $499m and net leverage rising toward ~4x show the capex-into-cycle mechanism at work. (The long view had said: Hindalco is a pure commodity producer whose management frames every quarter around LME prices, global surplus/deficit balances and hedging positions; realised profitability has swung enormously across the period — Novelis adjusted EBITDA/t fell to $327 in Q3FY23 and recovered toward $543 by Q4FY26, while India upstream EBITDA/t went from $1,349 (Q2FY25) to $1,756 (Q4FY26), with EBITDA up 79% and 100% year-on-year in single quarters. The company is simultaneously running a heavy expansionary capex cycle (~$4-5bn declared, FY26 capex INR 31,619 crores, up 47%) funded partly with new debt into a price upswing, the classic over-investment-at-the-peak pattern.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Good Enough Goods (ELEVATED); New Entrants (WATCH); Fashion Risk (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 9 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
