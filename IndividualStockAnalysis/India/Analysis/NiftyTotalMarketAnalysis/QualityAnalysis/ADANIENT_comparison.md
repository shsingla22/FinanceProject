**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Infrastructure concessions and a 500 KTPA smelter carry enormous capital/licensing barriers, but solar shows the opposite — global turbulence and price-driven margin compression. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±5.0 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Capital intensity, concessions and regulated tariffs create very high entry barriers in airports and roads with stable promoter ownership, partially offset by irrational competition in solar modules where realisations fell.
- **Candor** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Management gives specific, unflattering explanations when pressed (rain at Carmichael, INR600 crore non-cash FX mark-to-market, solar margin compression), though it defers repeatedly on defence and the Google contract economics. | The long view had said: Mixed: they do flag bad news (Kutch Copper ramp delay, Carmichael weather, solar margin compression) but also deflect direct questions on defence investment and data-centre capex, and reframe a weak quarter as an 'accounting artifact' with a self-selected run-rate number.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: MDO business runs at only ~34% of contracted peak capacity (50 of 145 MMTPA) giving a long contracted runway, and airports already hold 23% of India passenger traffic with Navi Mumbai adding more. | The long view had said: Genuine runway and evidence of capture in MDO (only 34% of contracted peak capacity utilised), airports (23% of India passengers, 29% cargo) and solar (top-10 globally), though IRM is already #1 with little headroom.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Good. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹14 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹14 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±5.0 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: Explicit multi-year deep investment phase with large completed growth assets (Navi Mumbai airport, 500 KTPA Kutch Copper, Ganga Expressway, 6 GW cell/module line, PVC 1 MMTPA) funded by a ~INR25,000 crore rights issue, though incremental ROIC is regulated/modest (12-14% on airport RAB). | The long view had said: Capex is overwhelmingly growth-oriented and enormous (₹85,000cr FY23-24, ₹80,000cr FY25, ~₹40,000cr FY27) and has demonstrably converted into operating assets (Navi Mumbai, Ganga Expressway, Kutch Copper), but returns are regulated/moderate (12-14% RoA on airport RAB) rather than high incremental ROIC.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Poor → Weak. Now: Commentary leans on trophy claims and marquee-name partnerships (world top 10 solar, Google, Embraer, Leonardo, agentic AI) rather than low-key substance. | The long view had said: Management commentary is built on awards, superlatives and world-scale announcements (world's largest green H2 ecosystem, world's top-10 solar, Wind power Monthly bronze), the opposite of low-profile substance-over-celebrity.

Now: Airports growth is genuinely price/mix-led (aero yield +20%, non-aero per pax +34% on just 2% passenger growth), but solar explicitly suffers realization/margin compression, so the picture is mixed. | The long view had said: Airports growth is genuinely price/mix-led (tariff revisions plus non-aero up 31%), but the rest of the group grows on volume and tonnage with explicit margin compression in solar and eroded MDO per-tonne EBITDA.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 0 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: No heritage narrative, A&P spend, distributor leverage or consumer-brand adjacency discussion appears in any of the last four calls. (The long view had said: The Adani name is stretched across every adjacency (Adani Solar, Adani Airports, Adani New Industries, Adani Roads, Adani One app) and third-party recognition is cited as validation, but there is no heritage story, no A&P spend, no premium pricing and no consumer market-share-plus-price evidence across the 15 calls.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: New product introductions are named (3.0/3.3 MW WTG models shipping, top-10 global solar manufacturer on 15 GW cumulative shipments), but R&D spend is never disclosed and the newer technology moves rely on licensed foreign partners (Embraer, Leonardo, AIONOS) rather than in-house innovation dominance. (The long view had said: There is a steady cadence of named product/capability firsts with revenue attached — India's first 2 GW monocrystalline ingot-and-wafer line, the 5.2 MW and now 3.3 MW turbines with WindGuard type certification — but R&D spend is never disclosed or discussed, gross margins are commodity-level, and technology (electrolysers) is being sourced via partnerships rather than in-house dominance.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Airports show genuine realisation gains — aero yield per passenger up 20% and non-aero per passenger up 34% in H1 FY26, FY26 airport EBITDA up 55% on tariff revisions — but this is regulator-granted rather than brand pricing power, and solar realisations are explicitly deflating while IRM trading margins swung on price volatility. (The long view had said: The evidence runs the other way: solar realisation and margins have deflated (30%+ to sub-25% in FY24, further compression in FY26 as mix shifts to domestic), IRM EBITDA is explicitly described as inherently fluctuating and fell ~11% YoY, and the one source of price increases — airport tariff revisions — is set by a regulator, not by brand or scarcity.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Cost to Replicate (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 9 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
