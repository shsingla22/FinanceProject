### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Concession-based infrastructure has very high entry barriers and regulated (rational) pricing, offset by solar where global turbulence and India-only selling are visibly compressing realisations. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±5.0 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Capital intensity and concession rights create real barriers in airports/roads/mining, but solar shows the opposite — realisation slippage and margin compression indicate price competition rather than pricing rationality.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Poor → Weak. Now: The group is anything but low-profile — calls foreground marquee partnerships (Google, Embraer, Leonardo), world-ranking claims and 'largest in India' framing rather than understatement. | The long view had said: Management is emphatically not low-profile — calls lead with global rankings, superlatives and marquee foreign partnerships, and the group is among the most publicised in India.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: The main profit engine grew on price and mix, not volume: airport passengers rose only 2% in H1 while aero yield/pax rose 20% and non-aero/pax 34%, with FY26 EBITDA up 55% on tariff revisions and non-aero. | The long view had said: Growth is volume- and asset-addition-led with acknowledged price compression in solar; the one genuine mix/pricing win is airports, where tariff revisions and faster non-aero growth (31%) lifted EBITDA 55%.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Good. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹14 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹14 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±5.0 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Candor** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Weak results are explained specifically and without spin — the Australian rain event, a INR600 crore non-cash FX mark-to-market, and admitted solar margin compression — though Defence and the Google contract economics are deferred rather than disclosed. | The long view had said: Candid on adverse specifics — Kutch Copper delay, solar margin compression, the ₹600 cr non-cash FX loss, one-off ₹220 cr lease income, separating the ₹9,215 cr exceptional gain — but repeatedly defers or declines disclosure on defence, data centre capex and copper EBITDA, and once instructed an analyst how to frame questions.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: The core assets are contractual regional monopolies with very long lock-ins — 27-year Ganga Expressway concession, road/water LOAs of 15-29 years, and 18 MDO agreements where the mine owner cannot readily switch operator. | The long view had said: Structurally regional-monopoly assets with high switching costs — 8 airports where slots are already fully taken, 18 long-term MDO contracts where the customer cannot easily re-tender a developed mine.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: MDO growth implies work taken from less capable operators and the top-10 solar ranking implies weaker domestic peers, but no competitor weakness is actually discussed in these calls. | The long view had said: Explicit evidence of weaker competitors donating share — AEL sells 4.9 GW of modules against 4 GW of own capacity by tolling from rivals who cannot fill their own lines.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Neutral → Weak. Now: Geographic footprint narrowed rather than expanded in this window — solar retreated to selling essentially only in India with an explicit margin cost, and no new overseas markets were opened. | The long view had said: Geographic footprint is essentially India plus the legacy Carmichael mine, and module exports have been deliberately wound down to domestic-only, so expansion is scale-within-India rather than credible international brand travel.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: Some in-house product development (new 3.0/3.3 MW WTG models shipped, vertically integrated solar), but no R&D spend, pipeline or efficiency disclosure, and the newest technology comes via partners (Embraer, Leonardo, AIONOS, Google) rather than own R&D. | The long view had said: Real product development evidence — indigenous 5.2 MW and 3.3 MW turbines with own blades and German type certification, India's first ingot/wafer plant, electrolyzer trials — but no R&D spend disclosure or efficiency metric, and the green hydrogen product itself is still not commercial.

**That worsened:**

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
