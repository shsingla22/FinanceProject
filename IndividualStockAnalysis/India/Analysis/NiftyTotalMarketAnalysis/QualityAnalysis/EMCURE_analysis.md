# Emcure Pharma (EMCURE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Emcure Pharmaceuticals is a Pune-based Indian drugmaker that makes and sells prescription medicines — branded generics, specialty products and biologics/biosimilars — in therapy areas like women's health and anemia, cardiac, diabetes, anti-infectives, vitamins, CNS, nephrology, oncology and, more recently, dermatology and consumer wellness. In India it earns money by having a field force of over 5,000 medical representatives promote branded products to doctors (specialists account for about 70% of its prescriptions), and it supplements its own R&D pipeline by in-licensing or distributing other companies' brands, such as Sanofi's cardiac and oral diabetes portfolios, Novo Nordisk's semaglutide (Poviztra) and Roche's nephrology/transplant products. Outside India it sells generics and complex injectables through businesses it has bought and built in Canada (Marcan), Europe/UK (Tillomed, Manx) and emerging markets, including HIV/ARV medicines. FY26 revenue was about INR 9,204 crore (over $1 billion), split roughly INR 4,027 crore domestic and INR 5,177 crore international; the US business was demerged before listing, so US exposure is under 3% of sales.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Domestic / India business** — Branded prescription medicines sold to Indian doctors and patients across women's health, cardiac, diabetes, anti-infectives, vitamins, CNS, nephrology and oncology — roughly INR 4,027 crore in FY26 (about 44% of revenue), the part management calls closest to its heart, with Emcure ranked 12th largest pharma company in India and 4th largest in its covered market.
- **In-licensed / partnered India portfolio** — Brands owned by multinationals that Emcure markets and distributes in India — Sanofi's cardiac portfolio and oral diabetes brands Amaryl and Cetapin, Novo Nordisk's patented rDNA semaglutide sold as Poviztra, and Roche nephrology and transplant medicines from April 2026 — a growing profit driver that management says proves its commercial reach, though the Sanofi diabetes deal initially weighed on margins.
- **International – Europe** — Generic and specialty medicines sold across Europe and the UK, built from the 2014 Tillomed acquisition and scaled about tenfold in a decade, with the recent Manx bolt-on in the UK and the Amphotericin B launch adding growth; revenue was INR 403 crore in Q1 FY26, growing 12.8% then and 29.6% by Q3 FY26.
- **International – Canada** — A top-10 Canadian generics player built through the 2015 Marcan acquisition plus a second company for Quebec and the Cutimed derma buy, growing on market share gains and new launches; revenue was INR 342 crore in Q1 FY26, up 16.4%.
- **International – Emerging markets / Rest of world** — Sells products developed in India, Europe and Canada into emerging markets, partly through its own front-end teams and partly via partners and distributors, split between HIV antiretrovirals (ARV) and a widening non-ARV range; the fastest-growing piece at INR 360 crore in Q1 FY26, up 42% year-on-year.
- **R&D, biologics and biosimilars** — The in-house engine management credits for its differentiated products — chirality-based cardiac drugs, the Orofer anemia franchise, seven marketed biotherapeutics with two awaiting approval, plus a pipeline in complex injectables, biosimilars (including ophthalmic Bevacizumab), new delivery routes and antibody-drug conjugates, and a global voluntary licence for Gilead's lenacapavir.
- **Zuventus** — An Indian subsidiary whose minority stake Emcure bought out in FY26 for full consolidation; its portfolio and team reorganisation caused attrition that made Q4 FY26 domestic growth softer, which management says was fixed by April.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*
