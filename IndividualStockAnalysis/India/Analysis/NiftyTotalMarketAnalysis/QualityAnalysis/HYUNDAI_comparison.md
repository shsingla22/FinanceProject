## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

**Checks that improved:**

- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Good. Now: A deep, still-expanding sales and service footprint reaching into rural white spaces is a genuine reach advantage in a market where after-sales service matters, though the clear leader in Indian PV distribution is a rival. | The long view had said: Distribution is expanding methodically into rural white spaces and delivering record penetration, but it is a competitive dealer network that rivals — notably the far larger market leader — can and do match.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: A 2030 roadmap of 26 products, a first Investor Day and a 30-year India build-out show long-term tenacity, but independence is limited — HMIL is a controlled subsidiary whose strategy is set within the parent's global plan. | The long view had said: Thirty years of operations, Creta holding the No.1 mid-SUV position every year since 2015, a published 2030 roadmap of 26 products, and the independence to abstain from a sector-wide price war evidence long-term tenacity.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: An internal promotion of the COO to MD & CEO, a settled three-year union wage agreement and stated talent-nurturing at the HTWO centre point to a functioning pipeline and stable labour relations. | The long view had said: Top Employer recognition, a three-year union wage settlement described as an industry benchmark, and a demonstrated internal pipeline — COO Tarun Garg elevated to MD & CEO by FY26 — show systematic talent development.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. Now: Clearly growth (not maintenance) capex — a third plant at Talegaon commissioned in FY26 after 15 years and a record FY27 capex plan — but the excerpts flag near-term margin dilution from the new capacity and give no incremental-ROIC evidence. | The long view had said: Sustained, rising growth capex — Pune plant acquired and commissioned, phased expansion to 320k units and >1.1mn total by 2030, with FY27 capex guided at a record INR 7,500 crores.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Neutral → Weak. Now: Much of the recent improvement traces to exogenous factors — GST 2.0 rate cuts and parent-allocated export volumes — rather than demonstrable operating skill, since domestic volumes actually declined for the first half of FY26. | The long view had said: Genuine skill is visible in localization (78%→82%), mix and export execution, but much of the good news came from exogenous factors — GST 2.0, parent product/technology and export tailwinds — while the Q4 margin drop to 10.4% shows results are not purely management-driven.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Growth in FY26 was substantially mix- and price-led — record 71% SUV penetration, premiumisation via ADAS/sunroof, and calibrated price increases — on essentially flat overall volumes (+1.7%). | The long view had said: With volumes flat-to-down, growth and margins came almost entirely from mix and price — SUV penetration to a record 71%, CNG 13%→18%, rising sunroof/ADAS trims, and repeated calibrated price increases while refusing the industry price war.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Purely organic growth — no M&A, capacity funded internally, margins held in the guided band while investing counter-cyclically through the H1 downturn — though the Pune build was a large, lumpy commitment. | The long view had said: Growth is fully organic and counter-cyclical — commissioning the Pune plant and committing record capex during the industry's worst demand phase — while holding discounting discipline and refusing to chase volume.
- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Management gives explicit, checkable guidance and reports against it (exports beat 7-8% guidance; margins delivered inside the 11-14% band) and openly flags weak domestic demand and near-term Pune cost drag rather than glossing over them. | The long view had said: Management gives quantified margin bridges (bps by driver), discloses discount levels, separates one-offs, and openly admits bad news such as market-share loss and persisting commodity headwinds.

*Unchanged: Customer types (retail vs corporate); Technology advantage; Geographical expansion; Structural end-market growth; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Barriers to entry and rationality; Research and development; Asset turn; Profit margin; Mini-monopolies; Share donators; Assurance benefits; Security by obscurity; Gain in market share; Out of limelight; Network effects; Cost of working capital; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Intangible benefits; Convenience benefits; Advertisement and promotion; Return on capital (headline); Cyclical market growth.*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Across all four calls demand is described in macro-cycle terms — 'prolonged softness' through FY25/H1 FY26, then a sharp policy-driven surge in H2 FY26 — while margins are explicitly moved by commodity costs and by the cost of the new Pune plant; the classic expansion-at-the-peak pattern is visible in the record INR 7,500 crore FY27 capex and a third plant commissioned just as the demand wave arrived. ## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
