# IRCON International Ltd. (IRCON) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSTRUCTION*

IRCON International Limited is an Indian government-owned (Navratna, awarded October 2023) engineering and construction company that builds infrastructure — mainly railway lines, roads and highways, and bridges — working as an EPC contractor that takes a project from 'concept to commissioning', doing design, material procurement and construction largely in-house with a large in-house workforce. Its customers are overwhelmingly government bodies, above all the Ministry of Railways and Indian Railways, plus road/highway authorities and Coal India and its subsidiaries; roughly 45-55% of its order book comes on 'nomination' (directly awarded, mostly by railways) and the rest through competitive bidding. It earns money by billing progress on construction contracts over typical four-to-five-year build cycles (revenue is lumpy, with monsoon-hit Q1/Q2 and 60-70% of the year's work landing in Q3/Q4), and separately by owning long-term PPP/concession assets through subsidiaries and JVs that earn toll or track-usage income. About 90% of the order book is inside India with roughly 8-10% international (e.g. the Khulna-Mongla project in Bangladesh); FY26 revenue was Rs.9,502 crore with PAT of Rs.592 crore, against an order book of Rs.24,984 crore, about 2x annual revenue.

**The parts of the business:**

- **EPC / construction (the core company)** — The main business: bidding for and executing railway, highway, bridge and related infrastructure contracts as a full engineering-procurement-construction contractor, and this is where essentially all of the roughly Rs.9,500-11,000 crore of annual revenue and the Rs.24,984 crore order book sit.
- **Railways** — Management calls railways its core domain expertise and the main growth driver — including work already executed on the Western Dedicated Freight Corridor and hopes of bidding for the newly announced Dhankuni-Surat freight corridor once tenders are floated; in FY26 it submitted 107 bids worth about Rs.48,000 crore and won roughly Rs.5,000 crore of orders.
- **Roads and highways** — The second leg of the construction business, done both as plain EPC work and as PPP projects, and described alongside railways as one of the two engines expected to drive growth.
- **Road and highway PPP subsidiaries (SPVs)** — Of IRCON's 11 subsidiaries, about nine are special-purpose vehicles that own and operate long-term road/highway concession assets rather than just build them — the company invests equity into them and they were a drag on consolidated margins in FY26 while still under construction.
- **Coal connectivity joint ventures** — Around five to seven JVs that build and then operate, under concession agreements, 'last-mile' railway lines linking Coal India's mines to the main rail network; only CERL Phase 1 is operational and it is currently loss-making, with break-even expected in roughly 18-20 months once nearby mines and a spur line come on stream.
- **Ircon-Soma Tollway Limited (highway JV)** — A toll-road joint venture that has been a steady profit contributor to the consolidated results for several years, though its concession period ends in 2026-27.
- **IISL (consultancy subsidiary)** — A subsidiary that does project management consultancy and some early-stage consultancy work; IRCON deliberately keeps pure standalone consultancy out of the parent company to avoid a conflict of interest with its EPC role, so this is a small, supporting piece.
- **Renewable power subsidiary** — One subsidiary in renewable power, mentioned consistently across the calls as part of the group but never sized or discussed as a meaningful earnings contributor.
- **International business** — Overseas construction projects such as Khulna-Mongla in Bangladesh — only about 4% of turnover and 8-10% of the order book, but management says margins are better there, helped in FY26 by roughly Rs.20 crore of foreign-exchange gains from rupee depreciation.
- **IRSDC/IRFDC (railway station development JV)** — A station-development joint venture now being wound up — a liquidator is being appointed and IRCON expects to recover a significant part of its investment from around early 2026.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

IRCON scores 19 out of 100 and lands at a "Weak" (1 star) rating, with the combined picture described as stressed. The arithmetic behind that is 45% weight on business quality (35), 30% on multibagger fit (10), and 25% on risk safety (0) — and the zero on risk safety is the part that does most of the damage. Of the eight risk channels reviewed, four come back high risk (cyclicality, government dependency, new entrants, and 'good enough' competition), one is elevated (customer concentration), one is low (technology change), and two show no signal at all. On the quality side, the weakest areas are industry structure (-1.17), customer benefits (-1.0) and return on capital (-1.0), while management is the only area scoring positive (+0.33) and capital allocation sits at zero. On the eleven-pattern multibagger screen, not one pattern is a clear fit: three are partial (brand strength, forward integration, corporate culture) and the remaining eight do not apply.

The single fact that explains most of the record is the end of nomination (negotiated) work by the Ministry of Railways. Under that system IRCON earned margins of 8-10%; once it went away, the company's core market opened to a flood of smaller private bidders quoting at or below cost estimate. This one change shows up simultaneously as a failed pattern and as a high risk. It is why 'Market Share Gainers' fails — the order book fell from Rs.42,066 crore in June 2022 to Rs.23,865-24,984 crore by FY26, and FY26 revenue fell to Rs.9,502 crore from Rs.11,131 crore, against a February 2024 promise to double turnover in four to five years. It is why 'Cost to Replicate' fails — the stated barriers of credentials, in-house design and procurement, and Navratna status did not stop new entrants. And it is why 'Pricing Power' and 'Low Price Plus' both fail: management admitted by November 2025 that it had abandoned margin discipline to defend share, with PAT guidance drifting from 7-7.5% in August 2022 to 6.1-6.3%, standalone core EBITDA guided to just 4.0-4.2%, and losses booked on two projects in FY26. The quality checks record the same thing from a different angle — market share scores -2, pricing power scores -2, industry structure scores -2 because the customer (Railways/NHAI) holds all the negotiating power, and after direct input costs the company keeps only Rs.12 of every Rs.100 of sales. The Navratna upgrade of October 2023 and the sole-CPSE credentials in high-speed rail and dedicated freight corridor work are real, which is why 'Brand Strength' rates partial rather than absent — but that standing buys the right to bid, not a better price.

Two other threads tie the strengths back to the weaknesses. First, the move downstream into asset ownership — 11 subsidiaries in road, highway and renewable SPVs plus 7 coal-connectivity joint ventures — earns a partial score on 'Forward Integrators' because IRCON also captures the construction work on its own projects. But that same capital-hungry ownership is what drags the asset turn check to -1 (every Rs.1 of assets produces only Rs.0.43 of sales) and sits behind the headline return on capital of -2: for every Rs.100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings, the business produced negative Rs.5 of operating cash this year. The framework specifically notes an otherwise asset-light construction core being weighed down by capital locked in long-gestation PPP and coal projects while revenue fell. Second, management. Candour scores +2 — Ragini Advani has fronted every call from 2022 to 2026 and volunteered the bad news with specifics: order intake of only Rs.500 crore by mid-FY24, booked project losses, the missed Rs.10,000-11,000 crore revenue guidance, share loss, and continuing losses at CERL. That honesty is the reason management is the only positive quality area and why 'Corporate Culture' rates partial, but it is candour about deterioration, and the February 2024 framing of the decline as a 'temporary phenomena' did not hold. The bright spots that remain are narrow: a working capital cycle of roughly two days between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, average sales growth of about 10% a year, price-variation clauses in all contracts that pass commodity inflation through, an order book still around twice annual revenue, and credentials in complex work — dedicated freight corridor, high-speed rail, tunnelling, Kavach signalling — that smaller entrants cannot bid for.

## What to watch

- Order book direction — the 'Market Share Gainers' pattern and the cyclicality risk both hinge on whether the Rs.23,865-24,984 crore book stops falling and rebuilds toward the Rs.42,066 crore of June 2022, or keeps eroding.
- Margin floor — whether standalone core EBITDA holds at the guided 4.0-4.2% and PAT stops drifting below 6.1-6.3%, which is the direct test of the 'Pricing Power' and 'Good Enough Goods' risk of price-led L1 tendering.
- Cash return on capital — the return on capital check is at -5% cash return; watch whether operating cash turns positive as capital tied up in the PPP, road and coal SPVs behind the 'Forward Integrators' pattern starts to unwind or generate income.
- Further booked project losses — two were taken in FY26; more would confirm that aggressive bidding to defend share is destroying value rather than buying time, the core concern under the 'New Entrants' risk.
