# Anand Rathi Wea. (ANANDRATHI) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Anand Rathi Wealth is an India-listed, non-bank wealth management firm that helps rich families (high net worth and ultra-HNI individuals, mainly those with roughly Rs 5 crore to Rs 50 crore to invest) put their money to work in financial products — chiefly equity mutual funds, plus structured products/market-linked debentures and some debt funds. It does not manufacture most of these products; it advises and distributes them through a large in-house team of relationship managers (360 as of mid-2024, with team leaders and unit heads who also manage clients), and earns commissions and trail fees on the assets it brings in and keeps — mutual fund distribution alone brought in Rs 494 crores of the Rs 1,198 crores total revenue in FY26. Client assets under management crossed Rs 1 lakh crore in April 2026 and stood at Rs 1,06,300 crores by June 2026, across about 13,941 client families, producing FY26 revenue of Rs 1,198 crores and profit after tax of Rs 386 crores at roughly 32% margins and ~47% return on equity. Operations are India-based (Mumbai head office, listed on BSE and NSE), with a UK operation just started as of the July 2026 call.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Private Wealth / Wealth Management (flagship business)** — The core business: relationship managers advise HNI and ultra-HNI families and place their long-term money into equity mutual funds, structured products and some debt, earning distribution and trail commissions — this is the overwhelming bulk of AUM (about Rs 1,06,300 crores) , clients (13,941 families) and revenue, and management repeatedly calls it 'our flagship business'.
- **Platinum / Gold client segments (within Private Wealth)** — A segmentation of the flagship client base launched about two and a half years before July 2026, with 'Platinum' (the larger accounts) growing from roughly 40-50 clients at launch to about 230 by Q1 FY27, and management targeting 400-500 within two years, mostly by growing existing clients up rather than poaching newly-rich prospects.
- **Digital Wealth (DWM), a B2B2C business** — A technology-led offering for the 'mass affluent' segment — wealth solutions delivered by a mix of people plus technology through partners — which is small: Rs 2,526 crores of AUM and 7,320 clients as of June 2026, and only about Rs 7 crores of revenue in a quarter of FY25.
- **Omni Financial Advisor (OFA), a SaaS platform** — A software subscription platform sold to independent mutual fund distributors so they can service their own retail clients; about 6,890 subscribers with Rs 1.66 lakh crores of assets sitting on the platform as of June 2026, but tiny in revenue terms (roughly Rs 1.84 crores in a quarter of FY25).
- **Structured products / market-linked debentures (MLDs) distribution** — The firm places MLDs with its wealth clients, sourced both from group NBFC Anand Rathi Global Finance and from third parties (third-party yields being slightly better) — in Q1 FY23 about Rs 703 crores came from the group NBFC and Rs 390 crores-plus from third parties; structured products were about 24% of AUM as of mid-2024.
- **Minority stake in Anand Rathi Global Finance Limited (ARGFL), the group NBFC** — Not an operating business but a roughly 8% shareholding in the unlisted group lending company, in which the firm subscribed about Rs 40 crores in an April 2026 rights issue (Rs 500 a share) just to keep its stake; revaluation of this stake shows up as fair-value gains in other income.
- **UK operations** — Newly started as of the July 2026 call, with management saying business from there will start contributing 'very soon' — no revenue or size disclosed yet.

### The story in depth

Anand Rathi Wealth earns a 'Decent' 64 out of 100, and the reason is that it does a narrow set of things unusually well rather than owning a wide moat. The business is asset-light and highly profitable: every ₹100 of capital in the business threw off ₹26 of operating cash this year, return on equity has held at 42-47% for four years, and profit margins run above 30%. Scale has come almost entirely from doing more of the same thing — assets under management went from ₹32,142 crore in June 2022 to ₹1,06,300 crore in June 2026, client families from 7,400 to 13,941, and relationship managers from 271 to over 400. That is why two of the three strongest patterns are Market Share Gainers and Recurring Revenue: the firm books an upfront fee when a structured product is issued (₹1,875-1,895 crore of gross primary issuance a quarter by FY26) and then collects a trail fee that keeps compounding on the installed asset base. The third strong pattern, Corporate Culture, is the same story told through management behaviour — FY26 profit of ₹386 crore against ₹375 crore guided, 18-19 straight quarters of 20%-plus profit growth, and an open admission that the ₹1 lakh crore asset target was missed at year-end. The candour check and the disciplined-stewards check are scoring the same behaviour the pattern screen is scoring.

The traits that make the model attractive are, almost item for item, the traits that create the risks. The firm charges roughly 1.09% of client assets a year, and it says so out loud to clients — that transparency is exactly what earns the top marks on assurance benefits and helps explain near-zero client attrition of 0.09-0.54%. But a visible 1% is also why the Toll Roads pattern is a clear no-fit (this is a noticeable share of the client's return, not an invisible levy) and why 'good enough goods' sits on watch: direct plans and low-cost passive funds are a structurally cheaper substitute for the same 1%. Management leans into this rather than hedging — it declined a ₹2,000 crore direct-plan mandate and holds 100% regular-plan assets while the industry drifts the other way — which is simultaneously the independence the quality framework rewards and the concentration the risk review flags. Similarly, the fee itself sits inside a pool that SEBI sets, so pricing power is a no-fit (yields flat at ~1.09%, with a further 2-4 basis point squeeze acknowledged for FY27 on about 40% of trail revenue) and government dependency stays on watch — all growth has to come from volume, never from price. Cyclicality is the one elevated risk, because revenue tracks market-linked assets: FY26 net flows grew just 7%, and the structured-product economics need rates of 7-8% and volatility of 16-17 to work.

The defences are real but they are people-based rather than structural, which is why the moat-type patterns land on 'partial' and the two weakest quality areas are competitive advantage (0.33) and industry structure (0.17). What is genuinely hard to copy is time and credibility, not capital: 78% of the 45 relationship managers added in FY26 were trained in-house from colleges, leadership tenure runs 18-19 years, and books of ₹500-1,500 crore take decades to build. That home-grown pipeline is why Cost to Replicate and Friendly Middleman score partial and why the New Entrants risk stays merely on watch — capital-backed rivals do poach (three managers left for a competitor paying 'obscene salaries'), but 80-90% of client assets stay put when they go, and ₹685 crore of ₹758 crore was retained in the disclosed case. Two structural exposures remain. Structured-product supply is concentrated in the group's own non-bank lender, with ₹1,875 crore of group issuance against ₹312 crore third-party in Q1FY27 — the same in-house sourcing that powers the upfront half of the recurring-revenue engine. And the firm has Board consent to apply for an asset-management licence, moving from distribution toward manufacturing, which is the opposite direction from the forward-integrator pattern. Overall resilience is judged sound, and fashion risk is low because the money is intergenerational and the two core product designs have each been run about 1,500 times over 10-12 years without any new launches. This is a well-run, honestly managed compounder operating in a genuinely expanding market, not a business with a structural lock on its customers.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) — STRONG FIT
*"Sells once, earns forever"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence and the numbers agree (2 of 3 computable checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** The model is exactly upfront-plus-annuity: upfront income on structured products/MLDs at issuance (Rs 1,875-1,895 crore gross primary issuance per quarter by FY26) layered on trail commissions that compound with the installed AUM base (Rs 32,142 crore in Jul 2022 to Rs 1,06,300 crore by Jun 2026), with management explicitly steering to a 50:50 trail/upfront mix and trail growing 70% YoY in Q1 FY25. Density economics are visible — AUM per RM rising from ~Rs 100 crore at listing to Rs 230 crore-plus, client attrition of 0.09-0.54% of AUM, and no capital consumed to grow the trail book.

> "Trail revenues was Rs 89 crores and grew by about 70% year -on-year" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Only about 12 days of cash is tied up in the trade cycle.
- ✅ Capital spending is about 1% of sales — growth needs little capital.
- ❌ Operating cash was positive in 7 of 8 years — not fully consistent.

### Market Share Gainers — STRONG FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence and the numbers agree (1 of 1 computable checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Persistent, quantified share gain over the full window: AUM from Rs 32,142 crore (Jun 2022) to Rs 1,06,300 crore (Jun 2026), client families from 7,400 to 13,941, RMs from 271 to 400-plus, with an explicit 3-4% equity MF market-share ambition and UK operations started in FY27. Crucially the share is not bought — no lending or underwriting risk is taken, ROE is 46.7% and PAT margin expanded from 29.8% to 32.2% while AUM tripled.

> "I am also told that we will get to 3% - 4% of equity mutual fund market share in India" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Sales compounding at 24% while margins held or improved (32% → 45%) — growth that was WON, not bought with price cuts.

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** The 'under commit, over deliver' discipline is documented against outcomes — FY26 PAT of Rs 386 crore against Rs 375 crore guided, 18-19 consecutive quarters of 20%+ PAT growth with the standard deviation of growth disclosed, the Rs 1 lakh crore AUM guidance publicly flagged as missed on 30 March before being met in April, and a slide of 'what we learned from our mistakes'. The offsets to watch are the related-party concentration in ARGFL structured products and the Rs 54.6-96 crore fair-value gains on that unlisted stake flowing through P&L.

> "under commit, over deliver" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Friendly Middleman — PARTIAL
*"The expert sells it for you"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (1 of 2 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** The relationship manager is the expert who selects the product while the AMC/issuer pays the commission out of TER, so the end client never negotiates the fee — and lock-in is built through in-house professional training (78% of FY26 RM additions were college hires trained internally, every leader including the joint CEO carries clients). But ARW is itself the intermediary rather than a manufacturer selling through a friendly trade channel, so the pattern fits only by analogy; the OFA SaaS platform serving 6,890 distributors is the closest true channel play.

> "78% are people whom we trained from colleges and made them RMs" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Operating margin of 47% but swinging ±6.9 points a year — too volatile for a protected franchise.
- ✅ Operating margin moved from about 32% (early years) to 45% (recent years) — the widening-profitability signature of pricing power / lock-in.

### Brand Strength — PARTIAL
*"The name itself sells"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 1 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Trust and transparency are the stated franchise (client attrition 0.1-0.54% of AUM, 100% regular-plan assets while the industry shifts to direct, listing status cited as a credibility flywheel), but there is no heritage narrative, no meaningful A&P spend, and growth comes from referrals and RM relationships rather than brand-led pull; adjacency stretch (AMC licence, UK) is only just beginning.

## What to watch

- The FY27 TER reset — a 2-4 basis point squeeze on about 40% of trail revenue (Government Dependency risk). A larger-than-guided cut would hit the one price the firm cannot negotiate.
- Net flow growth versus market-driven asset growth — FY26 net flows grew only 7% (Cyclicality, rated elevated). If asset growth becomes mark-to-market rather than new money, the Market Share Gainers pattern weakens.
- Group sourcing concentration — ₹1,875 crore group issuance versus ₹312 crore third-party in Q1FY27 (Stakeholder Concentration). Watch whether third-party sourcing genuinely scales, and what the AMC licence application changes.
- Relationship-manager retention — currently zero regret attrition, 78% home-grown additions, 80-90% asset retention when managers do leave (New Entrants risk; Cost to Replicate pattern). A break here removes the main replication barrier.
- Any erosion in the 100% regular-plan, ~1.09% fee position (Good Enough Goods risk; Pricing Power no-fit). Client migration toward direct plans or passives would attack the core economics directly.
