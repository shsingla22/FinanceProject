**Checks that improved:**

- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Bankability and 25-year performance warranties create real stickiness — over 70% repeat business from the top 10 customers — but there is no captive aftermarket or contractual switching cost preventing a developer from rebidding the next project. | The long view had said: There is no captive aftermarket or switching-cost lock-in — contracts are cost-plus with price-variation clauses and customers can re-tender each project.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Weak → Neutral. Now: The company is a competent fast follower — it adopted TOPCon after it became the dominant standard in China and India and is only exploring back-contact and tandem — so technology depth is real but not a differentiating multi-product edge. | The long view had said: Technology is bought, not owned — TOPCon is the industry standard, cell equipment is ordered from Chinese suppliers, and silver-reduction routes are described as what peers like Jinko and AIKO have proven.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Poor → Weak. Now: Growth is overwhelmingly volume-led (YTD 2.3GW volume already 23% above the prior full year, record ~1GW quarterly production) in a market where module prices structurally deflate and cost shocks are passed through rather than priced for, with mix (N-type) the only positive lever. | The long view had said: Growth is purely volume-led on collapsing prices — non-DCR realization fell to INR14-14.5/Wp with a 30-40% Q3 drop — under an explicitly cost-plus pass-through model that surrenders pricing power.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Good. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹19 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±3.5 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹19 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±5.2 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: Exports are effectively paused — almost all shipments are domestic and US orders sit under policy uncertainty — while Europe/MENA remains only a stated intent to start conversations rather than executed expansion. | The long view had said: Exports have gone backwards — zero exports in Q1FY26, US orders stalled on policy, and Europe/MENA remains aspirational talk rather than executed expansion.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: The customer base is corporate/utility and the value proposition is correctly matched to it — LCOE, bankability and megawatt-hours delivered over decades rather than retail-style intangibles. | The long view had said: The customer base is correctly corporate (IPPs 69%, C&I 13%) and served on reliability/TCO, but the required switching-cost lock-in is absent since contracts reprice with cell costs and are re-tendered.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Regulation (ALMM-II cells, the wafer roadmap) is actively narrowing the field toward an oligopoly, but that consolidation is management's forward assumption rather than today's structure, and large utility developers retain strong negotiating power. | The long view had said: The industry is fragmented with 145GW of ALMM-listed capacity against far smaller demand, and large IPP/utility buyers (69% of the order book) clearly hold the negotiating power that drove realizations down 30-40%.
- **Candor** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: Disclosure on policy mechanics is specific and includes unfavourable items such as the DGTR anti-dumping recommendation and the ALMM-II exemption maths, but the tone is uniformly celebratory and no mistake or miss is owned in any of the four calls. | The long view had said: Disclosure is reactive — the 17.5GW module target was quietly cut to 15.5GW and only explained when an analyst noticed, and the CFO deflected repeated cost and margin questions as 'forward looking' or 'nuts and bolts'.

Now: Customer intimacy is named as one of the sector's barriers and the company is broadening into C&I, rooftop and distribution channels, but no distinctive accessibility or sales-force advantage is evidenced in these calls. | The long view had said: A genuine pan-India reach — 98 distributors, 375+ dealers and 76 system integrators across 19 states — now converts into 13% of the order book from distribution.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls management discusses module volumes, order book and capacity build-out only; there is no mention of AMC/service attach rates, renewals, installed-base monetisation or customer advances as a funding source. (The long view had said: Revenue is one-time capital-goods module sales — LC-backed shipments against an order book — with no licence fees, AMC, subscription or service attach discussed across any of the four calls; the only 'recurring' element cited is repeat ordering by customers, not a recurring revenue stream, and working capital is funded by payables/credit rather than customer advances.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management articulates a real barrier set — capital access, technology, execution and the ALMM cell/wafer mandates that force integration, with only 78 of 145 GW of ALMM-compliant supply able to exceed 22% efficiency — but the moat is largely policy-conferred and capital-based, which a well-funded domestic entrant can replicate within the same three-year build cycle Vikram itself is using. (The long view had said: Nothing described would stop a well-funded newcomer: the company itself built a 5GW greenfield plant from ideation to commissioning in under nine months with off-the-shelf Chinese equipment, and its own thesis admits capacity is being replicated industry-wide toward an 80–100GW shake-out — the barrier is access to capital and ALMM policy, both of which peers already have.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Volumes and orders are clearly rising (9M FY26 sales of 2.3 GW already 23% above all of FY25; record 1.9 GW Q4 order booking; capacity from 4.5 GW to 9.5 GW), but no explicit market-share percentage or comparison against named competitors is given, and India's market itself grew ~87% in FY26, so absolute growth may not equal share gain. (The long view had said: Volumes grew 76% to 3.3GW in FY26 with capacity going 4.5GW→9.5GW→15.5GW and cumulative deployment crossing 10GW, but no explicit market-share percentage or comparison versus named domestic competitors is ever given, and India's own market roughly doubled (45GW added in FY26), so absolute growth does not demonstrate persistent share gain.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The ALMM/BCD-created profit pool has triggered a wave of headline module capacity announcements across India, and management is defensive about it — arguing the sector will shake out to 80-100GW held by 'a small group of serious full-stack players'. Chinese state-supported export pricing is a second structurally different competitor whose economics management welcomes seeing moderated. (The long view had said: A wave of new domestic integrated manufacturers has reset pricing — 145GW of ALMM-compliant module supply against ~45GW of annual installs, non-DCR realisation down 30-40% in a single quarter, and management concedes the ecosystem 'scaling' is what brought 'more competitive pricing dynamics'; its own defence is that the industry will shake out to 80-100GW held by 'only a few', which is an admission that entry economics are now the binding issue.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Management itself frames the business as capital goods sold into customers' capacity expansion — revenue rides utility-scale developer capex and auction-driven build-out, and the company is simultaneously scaling capacity from 4.5GW to 17.5GW plus 12GW cells and 15GWh BESS, the classic expansionary over-investment setup. Q4 FY26 records (1GW production, INR1,450cr revenue, 20.3% 9M EBITDA margin vs 154% jump year-on-year) come off a year India added 45GW against a 24GW target — near-certainly elevated versus mid-cycle. (The long view had said: Management explicitly places itself in the capital-goods segment selling into customers' capacity expansion, and the P&L swings accordingly — EBITDA margin moved 8% (Q3FY25) → 22% (Q1FY26) → 18.5% (Q3FY26) → 16% (Q4FY26), with non-DCR realisations falling from ~INR18/Wp to INR14-14.5/Wp — while the company commits INR6,000cr+ (later ~INR5,400cr cell, INR3,700cr wafer, plus BESS) of capex into the upswing, the classic over-investment-at-the-peak pattern.)
- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The product platform has already been forced through PERC→TOPCon and now N-type, with management flagging HJT, Back-Contact and Tandem cells as the next wave — and MNRE is administratively obsoleting installed technology by raising the minimum efficiency threshold from 20% to 21.5%, with only 78GW of the 145GW ALMM-compliant supply able to exceed 22%. Every transition demands fresh capex on lines that are still being commissioned. (The long view had said: The industry has run through multi-crystalline → Mono PERC → N-type TOPCon in under a decade, with HJT, back-contact and tandem already discussed, and MNRE is legislating legacy technology out (efficiency floor rising 20% → 21.5%); management itself concedes that mis-timing equipment vintage is paid for over the asset's whole life just as it sinks ~INR9,000cr into TOPCon cell and wafer fabs.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
