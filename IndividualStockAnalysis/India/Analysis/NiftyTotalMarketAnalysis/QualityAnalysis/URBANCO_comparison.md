### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

**Checks that improved:**

- **Advertisement and promotion** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Good. Now: Brand marketing is explicitly funding new user acquisition and appears productive — 1.2 million new customers in Q1 FY27 and 83% of NTV from retained users — though InstaHelp acquisition spend is subsidy-heavy rather than brand-building. | The long view had said: A&P is a mix of brand marketing and heavy transactional discounting in InstaHelp (discounts running near the level of NTV), which buys volume rather than durable brand equity.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: This is the opposite of an ignored niche — a headline IPO, heavy sell-side coverage from Kotak and Morgan Stanley, and a category attracting well-funded competitors. | The long view had said: The opposite of obscure: a headline IPO covered by Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, Kotak, JM and BofA, with competitors raising capital specifically to attack the niche.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Neutral. Now: The business is asset-light and never discloses growth capex — growth spend is opex-like (density subsidies, consumer acquisition, training), with strong incremental returns in the core but unproven returns on the ₹132 crore/quarter InstaHelp investment. | The long view had said: Growth investment is large and rising but is subsidy/opex rather than capex, and management admits much of it is 'artificially subsidising orders' with no demonstrated incremental ROIC (InstaHelp loss ₹44cr → ₹119cr → ₹132cr per quarter).
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: The overwhelming majority of the market is informal, unorganized providers with no capital, brand or quality control — an unusually large pool of share donators. | The long view had said: A vast unorganized offline supply base is the donor pool — professionals earning ₹15,000-17,000 offline can be pulled onto the platform — but in InstaHelp the competitors are well-funded rather than weak.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Poor → Weak. Now: Growth is overwhelmingly volume-led — orders up 79% year-on-year to 13.2 million — and management explicitly passes density gains back to customers as lower prices rather than taking price. | The long view had said: Growth is explicitly volume- and user-led with falling AOVs and subsidised pricing — orders up 79% y/y versus NTV up 42% — the opposite of pricing/mix-led growth.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: UAE and Singapore grew 75% in FY26 and turned adjusted-EBITDA positive, the KSA JV is growing with margin, and the India footprint spans 47 cities — expansion that is both fast and now self-funding. | The long view had said: UAE/Singapore grew 75-76% and turned adjusted-EBITDA positive with the KSA JV improving, but management explicitly rules out new markets and concedes prior failures in the US and Australia.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: Profit margins swing by ±8.8 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: The InstaHelp category shows subsidy-led competition rather than pricing rationality, with density subsidies and consumer acquisition driving a ₹132 crore quarterly loss to keep rivals at bay. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±47.3 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: New well-capitalised entrants and an active discount war in the fastest-growing segment, with management openly willing to price irrationally, indicate weak entry barriers and poor pricing rationality there even if the core is benign.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Headline growth is genuinely impressive but the consolidated business is still loss-making and only four quarters of listed disclosure exist, so results cannot yet be cleanly separated from a capital-funded land grab. | The long view had said: Management itself resists extrapolating the strong results, refusing to declare a structural growth shift until several more quarters confirm it, which supports separating skill from favourable outcomes.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Management deliberately sacrificed reported profit to build InstaHelp and Native while committing to a Q3 FY28 breakeven and FY31 ₹1,000 crore target, and has delivered on its own near-term margin promises so far. | The long view had said: Twelve years of patient category building, an explicit reinvestment year in FY26 that still beat its own margin promise (FY25 3.3% → FY26 4.1% of NTV) and four straight quarters of accelerating core growth show long-term vision executed independently.

*Unchanged: Intangible benefits; Out of limelight; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Convenience benefits; Return on capital (headline); Profit margin; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Technology advantage; People matter (talent development); Disciplined stewards; Gain in market share; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Asset turn; Research and development; Distribution advantage; Cost of working capital; Assurance benefits; Network effects; Structural end-market growth; Mini-monopolies; Candor.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Only Native shows a genuine product-upfront-then-recurring stream: water purifiers/door locks sold upfront with filter replacements and app-linked servicing behind them, with ~75% of first-cycle cohorts renewing through UC (Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 calls). No attach-rate, AMC-share, deferred-revenue or customer-advance funding discussion, and it is a small slice (Native NTV ₹119 cr of ₹1,465 cr consolidated in Q1 FY27). (The long view had said: Native sells water purifiers and smart locks upfront and then earns recurring filter-replacement and service revenue off the installed base, with 75% of first-cycle cohorts renewing through UC (May 2026 and Aug 2026 calls); but there is no discussion of deferred revenue, advance billing or negative working capital, and it is confined to one small segment.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Consistent trust signals (4.8/5 average rating, 83% of FY26 NTV from retained users) and clear stretch of one name into adjacencies — Native, Revamp, InstaHelp, UC Instant — plus deliberate brand-marketing spend; but no heritage story, no premium pricing evidence, and the brand is barely a decade old. (The long view had said: Trust and consistency are the stated core asset (4.8/5 ratings, 'in businesses of trust, it's a winner-take-all') and the name has been stretched into Native, Revamp and InstaHelp with brand-marketing spend behind new user acquisition; but there is no heritage story and no evidence of premium pricing — the opposite in InstaHelp.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Four straight quarters of accelerating core India growth (10% → 19% → 21% → 26% → 29%) in a market management calls under-1%-penetrated, plus international scaling (UAE/Singapore NTV +75% in FY26, KSA JV), implies share capture — but no explicit share percentages or named-competitor comparisons are given, and InstaHelp leadership is being bought with heavy losses. (The long view had said: Core India NTV growth accelerated four straight quarters (10%→19%→21%→26%→29%) with margins simultaneously expanding, management claims 'the lion's share' of the core market and 'clear market leadership' in InstaHelp, and international grew 75-84% with a localised playbook; the caveat is that InstaHelp share is being bought with ₹132cr/quarter of subsidy, which management openly calls artificial.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The service professional is supply that UC trains and controls, not an independent expert who prescribes the product to a paying end customer; demand comes from the app, brand marketing and word of mouth, with the consumer choosing UC directly (Nov 2025 and Jul 2026 calls). (The long view had said: The service professional is the face of the transaction and UC invests heavily in selection, onboarding, training and SOPs to make that expert the differentiator, but the customer still chooses and pays UC directly rather than being steered by an independent professional.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 1 worsened, 6 unchanged; 1 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

Why now: InstaHelp — a quick-turnaround daily housekeeping format — is absorbing escalating losses (Rs.44cr Q2 → Rs.61cr Q3 → Rs.119cr Q4 → Rs.132cr Q1 FY27) explicitly for consumer acquisition and 'network density subsidies', and management has repeatedly refused to disclose more citing competitive dynamics, which is the classic signature of margin investment as a defensive response to structurally different new-format entrants. (The long view had said: InstaHelp — now the company's largest investment — is a contested new-format category where funded entrants have reset economics: quarterly adjusted EBITDA losses grew from ₹44cr (Nov 2025) to ₹119cr (May 2026) to ₹132cr (Aug 2026), entirely on acquisition and density subsidies, and by Q1 FY27 management admits all three players are subsidising repeat orders rather than expanding the market, with breakeven pushed to FY31.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
