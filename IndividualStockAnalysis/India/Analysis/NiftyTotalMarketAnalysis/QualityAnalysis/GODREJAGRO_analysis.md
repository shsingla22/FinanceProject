# Godrej Agrovet (GODREJAGRO) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Godrej Agrovet is an Indian agriculture and food company that sells things farmers buy and things food buyers want: cattle, poultry, aqua and other animal feed; crop protection chemicals like herbicides, pesticides and plant growth regulators; crude palm oil and palm kernel oil pressed from oil palm fruit bunches; packaged milk and value-added dairy such as curd, buttermilk, ghee and milk drinks; and chicken and frozen foods under brands like Real Good Chicken and Yummiez. It makes money mainly by buying farm commodities (maize, soymeal, fish meal, raw milk, palm fruit) or making chemicals, then selling the processed product at a spread, so profits swing with input costs, monsoon timing and commodity prices as much as with volumes. It operates across India, with a plantation and mill footprint in the northeast (Mizoram, Assam, Tripura, Manipur), an export and contract-manufacturing chemicals arm (Astec LifeSciences), and a feed joint venture in Bangladesh called ACI Godrej. In FY26 consolidated revenue crossed INR 10,000 crore, at INR 10,233 crore, with profit before tax excluding one-offs of INR 569 crore.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Animal Feed / Animal Nutrition** — Makes and sells cattle, poultry and aqua feed to farmers, earning a margin per tonne over grain and protein-meal costs; the calls treat it as the largest and most closely watched business, quoting volume growth and EBIT per tonne (for example INR 2,258/MT in Q1 FY25) every quarter, with Q4 FY26 volumes up 15% and cattle feed up 24%.
- **Vegetable Oil / Oil Palm** — Buys fresh fruit bunches from oil palm growers and crushes them into crude palm oil and palm kernel oil, so earnings depend on palm oil prices, fruit arrivals and the oil extraction ratio; management calls FY26 a landmark year with record area expansion and an all-time-high extraction ratio, and it swings hard with prices — segment results grew 2.6x in Q1 FY23 when palm oil peaked.
- **Crop Protection / Crop Care (standalone)** — Sells branded agrochemicals to Indian farmers through a dealer channel — in-house herbicides, pesticides and plant growth regulators plus co-marketed products — and is a high-margin but seasonal business (segment margins of 45% in Q1 FY25) whose sales hinge on monsoon timing, channel credit discipline and distributor inventory.
- **Astec LifeSciences** — A listed chemicals subsidiary with two halves: 'enterprise' products (mainly triazole fungicide molecules sold in India and exported, hit hard by low prices and Chinese dumping) and a growing CDMO/CMO business making molecules to order for global agrochemical customers; it lost money for several years and only reached EBITDA break-even in FY26, with CDMO now driving its recovery.
- **Dairy / Creamline Dairy (CDPL)** — Buys raw milk from farmers and sells packaged milk plus value-added products such as curd, buttermilk, ghee and milk drinks, mainly in south India; profitability turns on the 'milk spread' between procurement cost and selling price, and management keeps pushing value-added products, which rose from about 36% of sales in FY24 to around 40% in FY26.
- **Poultry & processed foods / Godrej Foods Limited** — Sells live birds to traders plus branded chicken (Real Good Chicken) and frozen ready-to-cook foods (Yummiez); the company has deliberately shrunk the volatile live-bird trade in favour of branded sales, which were over 80% of this business's revenue in FY26.
- **ACI Godrej (Bangladesh joint venture)** — A jointly owned animal feed business in Bangladesh selling cattle, poultry and aqua feed, reported separately as a growth add-on rather than a core segment — revenue grew 42% in Q1 FY23 but fell 13% in Q1 FY25 amid Bangladesh's political and economic turmoil.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Godrej Agrovet earns a Decent rating of 58 out of 100 — 64 for business quality, 48 for multibagger fit, 58 for risk safety — and the record's clearest strength is what it does with money. Every ₹100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings threw off ₹36 of operating cash this year, every ₹1 of assets produced ₹1.66 of sales, and only about 10 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. Return on Capital scores a perfect 1.0 and Capital Allocation 0.8, and the spending is aimed at growth rather than upkeep: record oil palm nursery and acreage expansion, Astec's second herbicide plant, dairy value-added lines and the Barabanki fish feed plant, with the discipline to defer Astec's expansion when it made sense. Sales have compounded at about 11% a year, lumpy year to year but inside the steady band the framework rewards. Worth noting the record's own tension: the same check that calls the business asset-light also observes it is capital-heavy in plantations and feed plants, so the high turns reflect how hard the assets are worked rather than a genuinely light footprint.

The Management area scores 0.83 and Corporate Culture is the single STRONG FIT among the eleven patterns — and the reason is candor. Management admits on the calls that it 'goofed up big time' on crop protection processes, that being caught out by the GM soymeal import policy was 'a good learning', and that visibility on the enterprise agrochemical business is simply absent. That honesty is a real asset, but it is worth seeing what it is honest about: each admission is also the evidence behind a risk. The soymeal surprise that wiped out feed profits in Q1 FY23 is the core of Government Dependency, the only HIGH RISK on the list, which also runs through edible-oil import duties setting palm realisations, Andhra price controls, and the fact that oil palm acreage is allocated through the government's NMEO-OP mission. That last point cuts both ways: the state-allocated acreage, captive Mizoram mill, nursery capacity and three-to-five-year gestation are exactly what makes oil palm hard for a funded newcomer to copy, so the strongest barrier in the portfolio is one the government granted and could alter. Pricing Power, Toll Roads and Friendly Middleman are all NO FIT, Industry Structure is the only negative quality area at -0.17, and Cyclicality is ELEVATED — prices are set by commodity swings and policy, not by the company, and management says plainly it often cannot pass input costs on.

The growth story sits between those two poles and mostly earns PARTIAL verdicts rather than clear fits. Feed volumes grew 11% in Q1 FY23 and 15% in Q4 FY26, described as significantly ahead of the industry, with runway in under-penetrated East and Central India — but management concedes share tracking in India is informal, which is why Market Share Gainers stops at PARTIAL. R&D produced the new Type-1 high-yield feed products behind that volume growth and margin expansion, and Astec's CDMO pipeline is growing 60-70% a year, yet R&D spend is never disclosed, so Innovation Dominance also stops short. Branded salience above 80% in poultry foods and value-added dairy rising from 28% to 40% show the company moving toward the consumer — shrinking live bird in favour of Yummiez, buying out Tyson's stake in an already-understood joint venture to control branding — and this is the same movement that makes Shifting Consumer Preferences a LOW risk: the portfolio is on the winning side of where demand is going. Fashion Risk and Technological Innovation are LOW too, since demand is functional and repeat-purchase and innovation is incremental. The unresolved area is Astec, where cheap Chinese triazole supply reset the generic business (New Entrants and Good Enough Goods, both WATCH) and the CDMO pivot that answers it concentrates growth in a handful of innovator orders whose deferral alone compresses group margins (Stakeholder Concentration, WATCH). Overall resilience is judged SOUND: five segments mean no single policy lever or commodity cycle controls the whole company, and the segments partly offset each other.

## What to watch
