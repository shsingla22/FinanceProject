### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 9 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Cost to Replicate**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Replication barriers are real but capital-based rather than un-copyable: 4.2 mtpa integrated melt capacity, a 1.1 mtpa HRAP and 0.17 mtpa CRAP line, 60,400 trained fabricators and top-5%-in-steel S&P CSA (78/100) ESG standing. Against that, the calls show low-cost imports from China/Vietnam/Indonesia repeatedly eroding the advantage and management relying on government QCOs and DGTR action for protection — a moat that needs regulation to hold is not a durable one. (The long view had said: The advantage is integrated capacity and scale that Chinese and Indonesian mills demonstrably already replicate and undercut — imports have run at 30-50% share for the whole period, and management's defence rests on the government granting QCOs and anti-dumping duty rather than on anything a funded entrant could not rebuild.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → NO FIT. Why now: Management describes holding share, not gaining it, in both Q3 and Q4 FY26, and gives no share percentages or comparisons against named competitors; exports are being de-prioritised in favour of the domestic market rather than expanded. (The long view had said: Volumes compounded (highest-ever quarterly sales in Q1FY25, +11% in Q3FY26, FY26 deliveries 2.57mt, +8%) with a 3.5mt FY29 target, and margins were protected rather than bought — in 2022 they deliberately took a 5-10% volume cut rather than chase share — but management describes 'retaining' share against 30%+ imports and exports shrinking to 8%, not sustained gain versus named rivals.)

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 2 worsened, 6 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Structurally lower-cost subsidised imports from China, Vietnam and Indonesia are described defensively in all four calls as entering 'at a large scale', and management's answer is explicitly a pricing and agility response ('competitive pricing', 'cost competitiveness') plus lobbying DGTR for trade protection — the economics of the domestic market are being reset by entrants JSL cannot match on cost. (The long view had said: Structurally cheaper Chinese and Indonesian imports have reset domestic economics throughout the period — imports rose to ~50% share in Q1FY23 and remain 'steadily at 30% plus' in FY26 — and management responds defensively by vacating low-margin segments and by lobbying for anti-dumping duties rather than competing on price.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: This is the sharpest structural risk in the calls: cheap substandard imports compete directly on price with JSL's quality positioning, the intermediary (fabricator/retailer) controls grade choice, and management concedes end-customer awareness is weak — the Jindal Saathi seal, QR-code loyalty programme and Ranveer Singh campaign exist precisely to address 'counterfeiting and quality opacity'. Stainless flat product is not a niche, complex good with scale protection, so the premium is hard to defend. (The long view had said: This is the central and recurring threat: cheap 'inferior'/'substandard' imported stainless is a good-enough substitute that fabricators and MSME intermediaries buy on price, and the end-consumer cannot tell — which is precisely why the company launched QR-code seals, the Jindal Saathi programmes and the Infinity campaign against 'counterfeiting and quality opacity'. Management admits it cannot fully pass through cost increases because importers' costs have not risen.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (LOW); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 1 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
