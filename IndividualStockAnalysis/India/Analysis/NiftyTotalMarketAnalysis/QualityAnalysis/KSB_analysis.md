# KSB (KSB) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

KSB Limited makes and sells pumps and valves in India — everything from small household and farm pumps to large engineered pumps for power plants, refineries and water utilities, plus gate/globe/check and ball valves and (through associate MIL Controls in Kochi) control valves. Its customers are industrial and infrastructure buyers such as Thermax, BHEL, L&T, NTPC, HPCL/BPCL, NPCIL, ISGEC, Veolia, Wabag and municipal corporations, reached both directly and through a network of 800-1,100+ authorised dealers, 14 branches, four zonal offices and 350+ service centres. It earns money by selling equipment against orders (a mix of standard catalogue pumps and project-specific engineered pumps) and by an ongoing after-sales stream of spare parts, repairs, field service and pump replacement. Established in 1960 and headquartered in Pune, it operates six manufacturing plants plus its own foundries across Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu and Kerala; it is part of the German KSB group, sells mainly in India (with Bangladesh, Nepal and Bhutan served as neighbouring markets) and exports about 17% of order intake, with CY2025 revenue of about ₹26,957 million and profit before tax of about ₹352 crore.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Standard pumps (Water Pump Division, Sinnar)** — Makes standard and domestic pumps — submersible, openwell and mini-monobloc, dewatering — for irrigation, farmhouses, drinking water and building use; management calls this the largest and fastest-growing slice, roughly 48% of 2025 order intake.
- **Engineered pumps (Industrial Pump Division, Pimpri and Energy Pump Division, Shirwal)** — Builds project-specific pumps such as high-pressure multistage, API 610 process, FGD and boiler recirculation pumps for power, oil and gas, petrochemical and process plants; about 19% of 2025 order intake and the source of the large project orders.
- **Valves Division (Coimbatore)** — Manufactures gate, globe, check and ball valves used across energy, industry, water and wastewater, including for export to China after a regulatory audit; about 18% of 2025 order intake, described as steady, with associate MIL Controls in Kochi adding control valves.
- **SupremeServ (aftermarket)** — The after-sales unit at Chinchwad selling spare parts, repairs, field service, modernisation and replacement pumps, plus a training academy and central warehouse; about 15% of 2025 order intake and a recurring revenue stream on the installed base.
- **Nuclear business** — Supplies primary coolant pumps, motors, spares and mechanical seals to NPCIL — one Kaiga 5 & 6 order alone was about ₹5,000 million; a long-gestation business held separately in reporting, with roughly ₹12,816 million of orders on hand at December 2025, about half the total order book.
- **Solar pumps business** — Sells solar pumping products, now including an in-house-made solar controller, largely to state government buyers like Dakshin Gujarat, Maharashtra Energy and Haryana Renewable; small but the fastest-growing line at a stated 112% order intake CAGR.
- **Water and wastewater** — Non-clog submersible and end-suction pumps, vertical turbine pumps and valves for municipal and industrial sewage, effluent, stormwater and drainage; the biggest market area at 29% of sales, growing at a stated 30% CAGR with breakthrough orders from Kolkata, Amritsar and Navi Mumbai.
- **Building services / firefighting** — Hydro-pneumatic systems, FM/UL-certified firefighting pumps and HVAC pumps for commercial buildings, airports, metros, hospitals and data centres; about 8% of sales but called a sunrise segment growing at 68% CAGR.
- **Foundry (Vambori) and KSB Tech** — Supporting units — cast-iron and stainless steel foundries producing castings mainly for KSB's own pumps and valves, and KSB Tech in Pune, a captive engineering and IT services centre serving the worldwide KSB group.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Innovation Dominance — STRONG FIT
*"Out-inventing the competition"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence and the numbers agree (2 of 2 checks computable supporting).

**What the calls show:** Every call names new products with revenue impact — Aqua Series (2022), mechanical seals, solar pumps and in-house solar controller, Sewatec/Vertical Turbine, MFMO, UL/FM Etanorm, IE5 motors, Etaseco train pumps (~400 sold), green hydrogen and marine pumps — supported by KSB Tech, the group's Pune R&D/engineering centre, and management explicitly attributes the majority of recent growth to these launches while flagging that development costs sit in current margins.

> "a major part has come through the new products, be it solar, be it water, wastewater, firefighting" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹46 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ✅ High retained margin (46% of sales) alongside 12% yearly growth — the profitable-innovation flywheel: margins fund the spending that drives the growth.

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** Rajeev Jain has been MD and Gaurav Swarup Chairman across the whole period, with a stable long-tenured senior team; the company runs net cash (~₹283–300 crore) with ROCE ~23–24% and rising dividends, and management discloses bad news promptly — the 2022 cyberattack, repeated nuclear delays, the Q4 top-line shortfall, the labour one-time hit, and a candid admission of discomfort about the recurring nuclear question. > "In every meeting, I little bit am apprehensive how I will answer this question." — *management, on an earnings call*

### Cost to Replicate — STRONG FIT
*"Could a newcomer copy this?"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** The barriers are concrete and time-based: NPCIL primary-coolant-pump qualification took from 2018 to a first 500-hour witnessed test in 2026, ISO 19443 is held by no other Indian pump maker, UL/FM listing and marine type-testing are described as long approval processes, and these sit on top of captive foundries, seven plants, 350+ service centres and 800+ dealers built since 1960 plus German group technology. > "the first pump has to go through a rigorous testing of 500 hours" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Across all four calls fortunes hinge on state actors — NPCIL nuclear orders (₹12,816mn of a ₹25,848mn order book, delayed for years by government site and test-bed readiness), government thermal capacity targets, PSU customers (BHEL, NTPC, IOCL, HPCL, BPCL), municipal corporations, ethanol-blending policy and the KUSUM solar subsidy scheme; a policy or scheme change directly resets whole growth legs.

> "solar we'll have to see after what comes up in the KUSUM 2.0 scheme" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate diversification — six market areas and 21 submarkets, 16% CAGR in non-nuclear order intake presented explicitly to show independence, exports up to 17% of order intake, and private-sector growth in data centres, building services and marine*

### Stakeholder Concentration — HIGH RISK

**What the calls show:** A single counterparty, NPCIL, accounts for roughly half the order book and its readiness — not KSB's — determines revenue recognition; the Mar 2026 top-line shortfall was attributed entirely to nuclear slippage, and analysts have questioned the NPCIL relationship and its ordering cadence in every call since Aug 2022 ('after four-five years we are getting the second order').
