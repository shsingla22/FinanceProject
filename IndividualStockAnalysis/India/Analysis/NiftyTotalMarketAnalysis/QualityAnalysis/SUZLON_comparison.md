**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Weak → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: ALMM listing, domestic manufacturing requirements and certification create genuine regulatory entry barriers, but pricing rationality is unproven — WTG margin fell to 13.7% in Q3 and no pricing discipline commentary is offered. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±17.6 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Domestic manufacturing scale and certification create some barrier, but Chinese peers are introducing cheaper 5 MW platforms and central procurement runs on reverse auctions with tariffs around INR3.67/kWh, limiting pricing rationality.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹18 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹18 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±17.6 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Organic-only growth, no material M&A, a move from debt-laden to INR2,384 crores net cash with net worth of INR9,464 crores, and land seed capital deployed 'without materially impacting working capital'. | The long view had said: Leverage discipline has been exemplary over the period — debt cut from INR13,000 crores to a net cash position of INR2,384 crores with organic focus and no large M&A — though the deleveraging was forced by past distress and funded partly by shareholder dilution.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: Suzlon is anything but under the radar — a heavily covered large-cap with crowded analyst calls, global sustainability rankings and a high-visibility sector attracting capital and new entrants. | The long view had said: Suzlon is the opposite of under-the-radar — a heavily covered listed turnaround story with global sustainability accolades, multiple sell-side analysts on every call, and high-profile executive hires.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: ALMM compliance narrows the Indian wind OEM field to a handful of qualified players, but the end customers are large PSUs, utilities and C&I buyers (66% of order book) who bid competitively and retain real negotiating power. | The long view had said: The Indian wind OEM market is concentrated but customers are large IPPs, PSUs and C&I buyers with clear negotiating power, as shown by realisation varying materially by contract and reverse auctions setting tariffs.
- **Candor** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Management opened Q4 with an explicit promise-versus-delivery scorecard, pre-empted the CFO resignation with a full explanation rather than waiting to be asked, and answered pointed questions on receivables ageing and stuck projects directly. | The long view had said: Management openly reconciles promises against delivery, quantifies adverse margin drivers rather than glossing over them, and flags non-recurring items such as the one-off Q4 FY24 OMS billing and the deferred tax credit in PAT.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Credible early-stage export push — Spain launch of the Blue Sky platform, a dedicated President Europe hire, and EU/US trade-deal positioning — but it is a re-entry with no export revenue delivered yet in the window. | The long view had said: Europe re-entry is credible in intent — a Spain product launch, a President Europe hire, EU FTA tailwind — but this follows an earlier retreat from international markets and management concedes revenue will only flow in FY28.

Now: The wind cycle has clearly turned up from a decade-long trough with FY26 installations the highest since 2017 and a stated multi-year path to 10-15GW, so the upturn is identifiable but the bottom is already behind. | The long view had said: The company was positioned at the bottom of the wind cycle in 2022 (industry ~2.3 GW, Suzlon loss-making) and the identified upturn materialised to a record 6+ GW with 10-15 GW forecast, a textbook bottom-of-cycle entry.

Now: The end-to-end model — land development, EPC, supply and lifetime O&M across 29 sites in seven states — removes the customer's hardest problem, with the EPC share of the order book rising from 20% to 28% as evidence customers value it. | The long view had said: The end-to-end model is the core differentiator and has deepened over the period — turbine, EPC (20%→28% of order book), O&M, plus a DevCo pre-acquiring land and connectivity so customers get a shovel-ready site.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 0 weakened, 9 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Every one of the four calls reports a growing installed-base service annuity alongside the turbine sale — OMS at 15GW (Q1) rising to 15.7GW (Q4) under management with availability above 95%, plus Renom's third-party fleet AUM crossing 3GW; management also quantified OMS profitability and delivered on a promise to recoup a one-off OMS EBITDA dip, closing FY26 at 40.5% EBITDA margin. (The long view had said: Every call pairs the turbine sale with a growing operations & maintenance annuity tied to installed-base density — 13,000 MW under service in Nov 2022, 14.8 GW in Jul 2024, 15.5 GW in Feb 2026, 15.7 GW in Jun 2026 — with contract renewals, VAP/VAS billing, availability guarantees (>95%) and OMS EBITDA margin of 40.5% in FY26; management even maps when a commissioned MW starts earning O&M ('two to three years is our warranty period... Q1 FY'27 or Q1 FY'28 is when this 70 MW would start earning revenue from an O&M basis'). The one departure from the pattern is funding: the business is receivable-heavy (INR5,745 crores) rather than customer-funded with negative working capital.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Deliveries rose 58% to a record 2,456MW in FY26 against industry installations of ~6.3GW, with management explicitly targeting and tracking ~25% market share, order book at record highs across ten-plus consecutive quarters, and a stated export push via a new Europe president and the Spain launch. (The long view had said: Absolute scale grows fast — deliveries 2,456 MW in FY26 (+58%), order book 3.8 GW (Jun 2024) to 6.4 GW (Dec 2025) — but the disclosed share numbers are flat rather than persistently rising: 33% cumulative installed share in Nov 2022 versus 32% in Jul 2024, with annual share described as 'around 25%, which is what normally we've been maintaining'. Export/Europe expansion is announced but revenue only expected FY28.)

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

**That eased:**

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
