**Checks that improved:**

- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Expansion is domestic-only but deliberate and working — Tier 2/3 penetration via RuPay-on-UPI QR acceptance is explicitly called out as a growth market and a strong spend contributor. | The long view had said: Expansion is domestic-only into Tier 2/3 cities via RuPay-UPI acceptance, credible but incremental and with no international travel of the brand.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: Profit improvement is substantially macro-driven — cost of funds down 71-82 bps on repo cuts and credit-cost normalisation — while core revenue growth decelerated to 3% Y-o-Y, so results should not be read as pure managerial skill. | The long view had said: Strong headline results owe more to the parent's distribution and the industry credit cycle than to distinctive management skill — the same team's ROE fell from 30.8% to ~15% as the cycle turned against them.
- **Network effects** (Competitive Advantage): Weak → Neutral. Now: The company is an issuer riding third-party rails (RuPay/UPI, Visa) rather than owning a two-sided network; scale helps it win merchant partnerships but the network economics accrue to the schemes. | The long view had said: The two-sided payment network belongs to Visa/Mastercard/NPCI-UPI, not to the issuer — SBI Card is a beneficiary of RuPay-UPI acceptance rather than an owner of the network.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: Tenacity is visible in a two-year underwriting/collections repair that delivered as guided, but independence and continuity are structurally weak — the parent bank has rotated chairmen and MDs repeatedly over five years. | The long view had said: Leadership is deputed from and rotates rapidly with the parent bank — three MDs (Amara, Chakravorty, Pande) and four chairmen in five years — which undermines independence and long-horizon ownership.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: The banca channel gives quasi-captive access to the parent bank's customer base (46-47% of sourcing), but credit cards carry negligible switching costs and roughly half of sourcing is open-market competition. | The long view had said: Switching costs are minimal — cardholders stack trade lines from several lenders and attrition must be actively fought through engagement programmes rather than lock-in.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: Investment is in digital/AI capability rather than physical capex — Sprint onboarding, hyper-personalisation and AI/ML risk models — and returns are rising (ROA 3.9%, ROE 16.5% in Q1 FY27), though no capex quantum is disclosed. | The long view had said: Investment is in digital/AI platforms (Sprint, instant issuance on YONO) rather than disclosed growth capex with measurable incremental ROIC, and returns have fallen (ROE 30.8% in Q1FY23 to ~16.5% in Q1FY27).
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: No formal R&D line, but consistent technology development shows output — the revamped mobile app won the CII National AI Award, and AI/ML is cited in underwriting, early-warning models and personalisation. | The long view had said: No R&D function as such; analytics/AI-ML work does yield new products (MILES card, Sprint onboarding, scorecards) but efficiency is not demonstrated in returns.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Neutral → Weak. Now: Beyond a passing reference to AI-based upskilling there is no evidence of a talent system, and the five-year record shows top roles filled by deputation from the parent rather than an internal pipeline. | The long view had said: There is culture investment (Great Place To Work certification, employee upskilling) but the top leadership pipeline is imported from SBI on deputation rather than developed internally.
- **Advertisement and promotion** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: Large festive/offer spend (Khushiyan Unlimited, 1,250 offers across 2,950 cities) plus corporate pass-backs drove FY26 opex up 22% Y-o-Y and cost-to-income to 56.8%, yet Q1 FY27 revenue grew only 3% — spend share gained but the promotional rupee is not clearly building durable equity. | The long view had said: Sustained, award-winning brand and offer-led marketing (Apple offer advertised across airports/TV/print/social, Abby and ET brand awards) that visibly supports spends share gains to 19.5%.

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 6 unchanged. Financial resilience: Unknown on the long view, Unknown on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Technological Innovation**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: The payments layer around the card is being restructured by UPI — management notes UPI is '80% of overall digital transactions' and that payments are becoming 'quicker, lower in ticket price, higher in frequency', and the economics show it: portfolio yield fell 16.5% → 16.3% → 16.0% and receivables grew only ~2-3% Y-o-Y while spends grew 27-33%. Exposure is bounded because SBI Card participates in the shift rather than being displaced by it. (The long view had said: The payments rail itself is shifting under the card format: management reports digital transactions up ~11x from 2021-2025 with UPI at ~80% of volume, and card relevance now depends on riding RuPay-on-UPI rather than plastic; spend-based fee yield already fell from a ~1.6% pre-COVID norm to ~1.4% as low-interchange categories grew, and MDR on the new rail is set outside the company (NPCI discussions from the 2022 call onward).)
- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Fortunes are materially shaped by policy: RBI repo action sets cost of funds (6.4-6.7%, 'stable at current levels until we see another policy rate action'), the RuPay-on-UPI rails that drive incremental spend are NPCI/government-administered, and management flags an evolving regulatory agenda on responsible lending and suitability plus a Labour Code change that cost INR12 crores in one quarter. Distribution also runs through PSU parent SBI's branches (46-47% of sourcing). (The long view had said: Core economics are set by regulators, not the company: RBI master circulars forced changes to card activation, over-limit and inactive-card rules; interchange is regulated (analyst: levers are limited because pricing is regulated); RBI's Nov-2023 risk-weight hike reshaped industry underwriting; and RBI rate policy drives the cost of funds management repeatedly refuses to guide on.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
