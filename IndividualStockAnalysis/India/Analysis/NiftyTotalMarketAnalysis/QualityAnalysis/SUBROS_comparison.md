**Checks that regressed:**

- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. And from the calls: Asset turn is respectable (~INR3,755 cr revenue on a modest base, debt-free) but the business is working-capital heavy with repeated inventory build-ups and no pricing moat protecting returns.

Now: Correctly serves corporate/OEM customers on reliability, localization and TCO, but the lock-in is one-sided — the customer dictates compensation mechanics, so the corporate-benefit match is only partially realised. | The long view had said: Correctly matched to a corporate/OEM customer base — reliability, localization-driven TCO and multi-year design-in lock-in are the value proposition, not brand intangibles.

And from the calls: Technical qualification and OEM approval limit entrants, but the industry's economics are thin (9–10% EBITDA with pass-through pricing) and Subros' own edge rests on licensed technology rather than a structural barrier it owns. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.8 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. Now: The company is past the cycle bottom, not near it — industry grew 18.9% in Q3 and 23.6% in Q4 on GST 2.0 and festive demand, and management already flags FY27 as a pressed year on commodities. | The long view had said: The company is past the cyclical trough rather than near it — FY26 saw industry growth of 23.6% in Q4 and management flags FY27 as a pressed year on commodities/geopolitics.

Now: The e-compressor localization is a long-pursued objective finally delivered, but it was granted through Denso/Toyota Industries and Suzuki rather than won independently, so strategic autonomy is limited. | The long view had said: A decade-long localization program (import content 42%→26%→16%, targeting <10%) executed persistently, plus a multi-year phased e-compressor localization aimed at 70% content — though strategic direction is heavily set by Maruti/Denso.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 0 weakened, 8 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Cost obsession is the single most repeated theme across all four FY26 calls — localization, value engineering, productivity — and it is what delivered PBT growth (15-16%) ahead of revenue on thin 9-10% EBITDA margins; but there is no evidence savings are passed to price to win volume, and returns rest on margin defence rather than a demonstrably un-copyable cost culture. (The long view had said: A genuine, sustained cost culture is evidenced across all 13 calls — import content cut from 42% to 26% to 16% with a <10% target, aggressive localization, value engineering, EBITDA lifted from ~6-7% (FY23) to ~9-10% (FY25-26), and debt-free operations — but this is cost defence inside a pass-through OEM contract rather than low price plus differentiation earning high asset turns and returns.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: There is a real named product pipeline in FY26 — EV SOPs for Maruti and Mahindra, the Y17 Kharkhoda programme, and the Denso/Toyota Industries e-compressor localisation with secured Maruti business for FY28-29 — but no R&D spend is disclosed, gross margins are thin rather than innovation-funded, and the core technology is licensed from Denso against royalty rather than self-generated. (The long view had said: Core technology is in-licensed from Denso/Toyota Industries with royalties of 3-6% of sales; the flagship 'breakthrough' announced in Feb 2026 is localisation of a partner's e-compressor design, not proprietary R&D, and no R&D spend or pipeline is ever disclosed across the 13 calls.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT); Market Share Gainers (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The end consumer has no awareness of the AC supplier and the OEM intermediary controls the choice and the price, which shows up as structurally thin 9-10% EBITDA and management framing the new e-compressor purely around being cheaper: margins are conceded to be only 'comparable to our existing business' while they start with 'bare minimum' assembly investment before backward integration. (The long view had said: Subros is an invisible Tier-1: the OEM, not the end consumer, chooses the AC system, and management repeatedly describes price pressure on new model launches, pass-through arrangements with 'no markup available', and the need 'to be more competitive' on e-compressors — the classic intermediary-controls-choice setup. Margins sit at 9–10% with OEM-dictated indexation rather than pricing power.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The relevant preference shifts — SUVs, premiumisation, hybrid/EV/CNG powertrains — are ones Subros has affirmatively pivoted into rather than been eroded by, with hybrid/electric/CNG thermal products rising from 20% to 25% of revenue over the four calls and growing ~30% in H1FY26; AC content per vehicle is not itself an object of changing taste. (The long view had said: The demand mix is visibly migrating — SUV/premiumisation, then a GST-driven swing back to small cars that caused a Q3FY26 volume mismatch, plus steady migration to CNG/hybrid/EV. The franchise has tracked it rather than been eroded by it, with alternative-fuel vehicles rising from 20% of revenue (FY25) to 25% (FY26).)

**That worsened:**

- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: The single largest growth driver of the year was a regulatory mandate, not market demand — truck AC grew 111% for FY26 and 168% in Q4 purely because AC in N2/N3 cabins became compulsory from 8 June; alongside this, demand recovery is credited to GST 2.0 rate cuts, the railway vertical runs on government tenders (INR52 crore AMC), and INR13.39 crores of other income in Q2 was a Gujarat state investment-promotion incentive. (The long view had said: Two of the three growth verticals are policy-created: the Ministry of Road Transport's mandatory truck AC norm for N2/N3 drove 111% full-year and 168% Q4 growth in truck AC, and railways is entirely government tender-driven (INR52cr AMC order). Demand recovery in FY26 is credited to GST 2.0, and a INR14cr Gujarat plant incentive flowed through other income.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Stakeholder Concentration (HIGH RISK); Cyclicality (WATCH); Technological Innovation (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
