# Fortis Healthcare Ltd. (FORTIS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: HEALTHCARE SERVICES*

Fortis Healthcare runs a chain of private hospitals in India and, through its Agilus Diagnostics arm (earlier branded SRL), a network of pathology labs and sample-collection points. It earns money by treating patients — mostly paid-for hospital admissions and surgeries in specialties like oncology, cardiac sciences, neurosciences, orthopedics, gastroenterology and renal sciences — and by charging for lab tests ordered by consumers directly (B2C) and by doctors, hospitals and other labs (B2B), plus some government contracts. Patients are largely Indian, with a meaningful slice of higher-paying foreign 'medical travel' patients (INR639 crore, about 7.8% of hospital revenue in FY26). Operations are concentrated in India in cluster geographies such as Delhi NCR, Punjab, Maharashtra, Bengaluru and Kolkata; FY26 consolidated revenue was INR9,128 crore with operating EBITDA of INR2,085 crore (22.8% margin) and profit after tax of INR1,064 crore.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Hospital business** — Multi-specialty and tertiary-care hospitals where patients are admitted, operated on and treated — by far the main engine, at INR7,773 crore of FY26 revenue and roughly 85% of both consolidated revenue and consolidated EBITDA, run at about 68% bed occupancy with revenue per occupied bed (ARPOB) of INR2.51 crore a year.
- **Diagnostics business (Agilus Diagnostics, formerly SRL)** — A lab-testing business doing about 9.9 million tests a quarter through roughly 4,370 customer touch points with a balanced 52:48 B2C-to-B2B mix; FY26 gross revenue was INR1,527 crore (net INR1,355 crore), the smaller of the two businesses at roughly 15% of the group, and its earnings swung sharply with COVID testing in FY22-23 before recovering to about 23% margins by FY26.
- **Focus / key specialties (within hospitals)** — Oncology, neurosciences, cardiac sciences, gastroenterology, orthopedics and renal sciences, which management tracks as the growth drivers — they grew 18.9% and contributed 62% of hospital revenue in FY26, helped by higher-value complex work such as robotic surgeries (up 66%) and radiation therapy (up 19%).
- **International patients / medical travel** — Foreign patients travelling to Fortis hospitals for treatment, worth INR639 crore in FY26 (up 18.5%) and about 7.8% of hospital revenue — a small but high-yield slice management calls out every quarter.
- **Digital channels** — Patient bookings and enquiries coming through the company's website, mobile apps and online campaigns, which management says now feed roughly 30% of overall hospital revenue, up from about 23% two years earlier.
- **Preventive and specialized test portfolios (within Agilus)** — Higher-value wellness/health check-up packages and specialized tests rather than routine ones; preventive rose to about 12% of diagnostics revenue and specialized to about 35% by Q3 FY26, and management points to both as the route to better lab margins.
- **Emerging care formats** — Newer, smaller-format offerings outside the main multi-specialty hospitals — such as the 12-bed oncology chemotherapy daycare centre in Delhi and Adayu, a 36-bed dedicated mental-health facility launched in Gurugram in November 2025 — described as pilots or new areas to scale rather than material revenue today.
- **Bed expansion and acquisitions** — Growth comes from adding beds in existing hospitals (brownfield) and buying or leasing hospitals in its cluster cities — roughly 800 beds added in FY26 including People Tree Hospital in Bengaluru (INR430 crore), Shrimann Hospital in Jalandhar and a leased 200-bed Greater Noida facility, with about 1,800 more beds planned over four years and FY26 capex of about INR700 crore.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Fortis earns its 'Decent' 63-out-of-100 rating on the strength of the people running it rather than the strength of the business it sits in. Management scores highest of all seven quality areas (1.33), and the reasons repeat across every record: a turnaround thesis set out in FY23 was delivered almost exactly, with the 20% consolidated margin target met and beaten, hospitals earning above 20% margins rising from 8 to 13 (now 76% of revenue), and hospital operating margin climbing from roughly 15-17% in FY23 to 22.2% in FY26. Bad news is volunteered and quantified rather than buried, which is why candor scores a full +2. The same behaviour is what makes 'Corporate Culture' one of only two STRONG FIT patterns. Growth spending backs the talk: about ₹700 crores of capex in FY26, ~800 beds added and 1,800 more planned over four years, funded comfortably because customers pay 99 days before suppliers do — a rare negative working capital position that lets the company build without stretching its balance sheet.

The traits that create the patterns are, in several cases, the same traits that create the risks. The strongest pattern, 'Friendly Middleman', rests on the referring clinician being the real decision-maker while the patient or insurer pays — and that is exactly why 'Stakeholder Concentration' is flagged WATCH, with analysts probing doctor hiring, attrition and rising revenue-share costs in NCR and Bangalore, and the Gleneagles unit shrinking 4% on clinician attrition. Similarly, the premium tertiary positioning that rules out 'Low Price Plus' and supports partial pricing power (roughly 4% on cash payers, revenue per occupied bed up about 28% over four years) is the same positioning that leaves the company exposed when a payer pushes back: the CGHS/ECHS cancer-drug price cap at 30% of MRP forced Fortis to turn patients away in Punjab with no resolution timeline, which is both the 'Government Dependency' WATCH and the reason 'Toll Roads' is a clear NO FIT. Absorbing weak operators — People Tree, Shrimann, Gleneagles — is a genuine +2 strength, but it also means importing other people's problems.

The ceiling on the score comes from what the company cannot control. Competitive Advantage, Industry Structure and Return on Capital all score zero, and this shows up as six PARTIAL patterns and two NO FITs rather than outright weakness. Barriers are real but time-based, not permanent — metro land, 24-30 month approval-and-build cycles, a settled senior clinician pool — and management itself concedes that well-funded entrants are coming, answering a direct question about asset-light, private-equity-backed rivals with brand and legacy rather than economics ('New Entrants', WATCH). Diagnostics is the visibly weaker leg: tests have been flat at about 9.9 million a quarter from FY23 to FY26 at roughly ₹340 per test, and the one big marketing event of the period — rebranding SRL to Agilus — cost money, took about 300 basis points off margin and caused outright decline in the consumer business, scoring -1 and holding 'Brand Strength' to PARTIAL. Offsetting this, the risk review is genuinely reassuring on the demand side: cyclicality, technology, changing preferences and fashion risk are all LOW, and resilience is SOUND. This is a well-run, honestly managed compounder in a growing market — not a business with a moat that defends itself.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Friendly Middleman — STRONG FIT
*"The expert sells it for you"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence and the numbers agree (1 of 2 checks computable supporting).

**What the calls show:** Across all four calls the referring/practising clinician is the decision-maker while patient or insurer pays: Fortis repeatedly reports onboarding specialists (urology, nephrology, cardiac, neuro), names clinician environment and governance involvement as its differentiator versus new PE-backed entrants, and Agilus courts the medical community via CME programmes, sponsored events and in-clinic merchandise with a 48% B2B mix.

> "there is a lot of involvement of physicians in clinical governance and broad policymaking as well. So that makes them feel included" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Operating margin of 23% but swinging ±7.3 points a year — too volatile for a protected franchise.
- ✅ Operating margin moved from about 4% (early years) to 20% (recent years) — the widening-profitability signature of pricing power / lock-in.

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** Promises made in 2022 were delivered: the sub-20% margin problem hospitals were fixed (8 units above 20% in FY25 to 13 units at 76% of revenue in FY26), the ~1,500-bed brownfield plan is on track, guidance is reaffirmed rather than reset, leverage stayed conservative (0.22x-1.24x net debt/EBITDA), dividends paid four consecutive years, and leadership (Raghuvanshi MD since FY22, Goyal CFO since FY23) is stable; bad news is disclosed plainly — Gleneagles 9-month revenue down 4% with clinician attrition, Agilus rebranding de-growth, CGHS/ECHS revenue loss.

> "Nine months growth is actually negative for the unit we are looking at. It is almost 4% negative. And there are a lot of disturbance. There is clinician attrition" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Pricing Power — LIKELY FIT
*"Raises prices, keeps the customers"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and every one of the 2 computable checks supports the pattern.
