# Granules India Ltd. (GRANULES) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: PHARMA*

Granules India is a Hyderabad-based pharmaceutical manufacturer that makes generic medicines and the chemical ingredients that go into them — it describes itself as integrated "from molecule to the tablet," making the chemistry, converting it into the finished formulation, and supplying it into regulated markets. It sells three linked product forms — active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs), pharmaceutical formulation intermediates (PFIs), and finished dosages (tablets/capsules) — historically built on large-volume molecules like paracetamol and metformin, and increasingly on complex generics, ADHD/controlled-substance products, and oncology. Its customers are mainly other pharma companies, distributors and retailers in the US (its largest market), Europe (about 15% of revenue in FY26) and the rest of the world, and it makes money by manufacturing at scale and low cost, filing regulatory dossiers (ANDAs, DMFs, EU/Canada filings) to win approvals, and then selling volume at a gross margin of roughly 65%. It manufactures both in India (Gagillapur, Vizag, Genome Valley) and in the US (its GPI plant in Virginia and Chantilly R&D site, plus a packaging site), and has recently added a Swiss/India peptide contract-manufacturing arm; Q1 FY27 revenue was about INR 1,477 crore.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Finished Dosages (FD)** — Actual pills and capsules sold to customers — now the core of the company at 74% of FY26 revenue (up from 50% in FY23), and the stated growth engine, with complex generics rising from 39% to 50% of finished dosages in a year.
- **Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients (API)** — The bulk drug chemicals Granules makes and sells to other drug makers as well as feeding its own tablet plants — a legacy volume business (paracetamol, metformin) that has seen price erosion, with a new oncology API facility being built at Vizag.
- **Pharmaceutical Formulation Intermediates (PFI)** — A half-way product between raw chemical and finished tablet supplied to customers who do their own final tableting; grouped with API as the older, lower-growth part of the mix, with volumes and prices declining as the company shifts toward finished dosages.
- **Granules Pharmaceuticals Inc. (GPI) – US manufacturing, CNS/ADHD** — The company's own US manufacturing and R&D operation in Virginia and Chantilly, focused on central-nervous-system and ADHD controlled-substance products; management calls it a key US growth driver, ranked 27th among all US generic companies by sales value in FY26 (from 74th in FY21) and 4th in controlled substances.
- **Granules Consumer Health (GCH)** — The US commercial arm selling over-the-counter private-label products (medicines sold under retailers' own brands), described as gaining traction and contributing to US growth; it also runs a Virginia site that passed inspection with zero observations.
- **Peptide CDMO – Senn Chemicals / Ascelis Peptides** — An acquired Swiss (Zurich) contract development and manufacturing business making peptides for pharma and cosmetics customers, now called the "fourth revenue pillar" — INR 1,593 million of FY26 revenue, EBITDA-positive within three quarters of acquisition, growing over 100% year-on-year, with an India footprint being added at Vizag.
- **Granules Life Sciences (GLS) – Genome Valley** — A new Hyderabad formulation plant that started operations in March 2024 with an initial 2 billion-unit capacity; management says scaling it adds roughly 40% to total formulation capacity.
- **Oncology platform (Vizag)** — API and finished-dosage capability for cancer drugs at the Vizag site, with first-to-file and first-to-launch filings aimed at US, Europe and rest of world; launches were guided to begin from FY26.
- **R&D and technology platforms (biocatalysis / enzymes, continuous manufacturing, CZRO green molecules)** — In-house research at Genome Valley and Chantilly aimed at cheaper, greener chemistry and complex products — R&D spend rose from about INR 32 crore a quarter in FY23 to INR 62 crore in Q1 FY25 — and it is the source of the complex-generic, 505(b)(2) and first-to-file pipeline management says will drive future value.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Granules India comes out of the three reviews as a Strong business, scoring 70 out of 100 (45% x 64 for business quality, 30% x 71 for multibagger fit, 25% x 80 for risk safety), with overall resilience judged SOUND. The single clearest thread running through all three records is that this is a company deliberately walking away from what it used to be. Growth is the strongest quality area (1.33), backed by sales compounding at roughly 14% a year, operating profit of about Rs 22 on every Rs 100 of sales with only mild year-to-year variation, and end-markets that are genuinely expanding (ADHD and CNS, oncology, and a peptide CDMO business management wants to grow fivefold in five years). Two of the eleven multibagger patterns are outright STRONG FITs and they are really the same story told twice: Forward Integrators, because finished dosages went from 50% of sales in FY23 to 76% by Q1FY25 on the back of its own US manufacturing, packaging, a distribution centre and a consumer health arm; and Market Share Gainers, because the company climbed from 74th to 27th among US generic companies in five years and is now 4th in controlled substances. The 34-check framework agrees from a different angle, giving top marks to market share gain, to R&D that visibly converted into output (Rs 32cr per quarter in Q1FY23 to Rs 88cr, about 6% of sales, in Q1FY27; complex generics from 39% to 50% of finished dosages; gross margin from 50% in FY22 to 65.6%), and to the fact that reliability itself is why customers buy, with uninterrupted ADHD supply where rivals run out and 330+ audits without a critical observation.

The uncomfortable part is that several of the same traits that earn credit also create the risks. The cost leadership that makes Low Price Plus a LIKELY FIT rests on backward-integrated chemistry and high asset turns in commodity products, and those very products are why three patterns are flat NO FITs. Toll Roads fails because paracetamol, metformin and PFI are large-volume goods under continuous price erosion rather than small, unnegotiated inputs. Pricing Power fails outright, with US price erosion discussed in nearly every year, European paracetamol collapsing on oversupply, and management confirming as late as April 2026 that it was taking no price increases even as raw material costs rose. Brand Strength fails because the customers own the brands: Granules sells APIs and PFIs into other companies' labels and private-label OTC to chains like Walmart and Target. The risk review reaches the same place from its own side, flagging Good Enough Goods, New Entrants and Cyclicality all as WATCH, and pointing at the same episodes: new Indian PAP producers plus a wave of paracetamol capacity wrecking that market, Chinese DCDA producers cutting prices drastically against the company's flagship import-substitution project, and a European inventory glut that killed sales for several quarters. So the forward integration and the R&D spend are not optional strategy; they are the escape route from a base the risk review says is textbook good-enough territory, and the reason Innovation Dominance is a LIKELY FIT and Cost to Replicate only a PARTIAL one is that the harder-to-copy assets (DEA-quota controlled substances, eight FDA-inspected sites, a sole first-to-file sodium oxybate, molecule-to-tablet integration) are real but still surrounded by a competitive generics market.

The third thread is who is paying for the transition, and who controls it. Capital Allocation is the weakest quality area by a wide margin (0.2). Capex has been sustained and growth-directed (Rs 300cr in FY23, Rs 555cr in FY26, Rs 600cr guided for FY27) into new formulation capacity, oncology API, US manufacturing and packaging, and peptides, which the framework credits, but about 256 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and the shareholder-return record scores negative: a Rs 250cr buyback at Rs 400 per share in FY23 with promoters participating, followed three years later by a dilutive Rs 6,656mn equity infusion in FY26, with no stated dividend or distribution policy. In short, an ambitious build-out is being funded while a lot of working capital is tied up. Control is the other issue: the growth engines everyone is counting on are the ones most exposed to outside parties. Government Dependency is a WATCH precisely because the largest growth engine is DEA-quota-governed, a PLI sanction underwrote the DCDA project, and an FDA warning letter at Gagillapur held back nine ready-to-launch applications; Stakeholder Concentration is a WATCH because PAP availability once hinged on a single Chinese producer restarting and DCDA economics are set by Chinese suppliers, while most filings sat at that one Gagillapur site. Against that, risk safety still scored the highest of the three components at 80, with only one risk rated LOW, two showing no signal, none rated high, and clean EIRs at seven of eight sites. Management gets partial credit rather than full: founder-led since 1991, candid about bad news (the warning letter, FY26 as 'a year of deliberate reset', paracetamol 'in a very bad shape'), and it has delivered on multi-year promises about dosage mix, complex generics and debt reduction, which is what makes the mix-shift story believable in the first place.
