# Gillette India Ltd. (GILLETTE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSUMER GOODS*

Gillette India Limited is an Indian consumer-goods company, part of the P&G group (registered at P&G Plaza in Mumbai, listed on the BSE and NSE), that sells everyday personal-care products in two categories: Grooming (mainly razors, blades and shaving-related products under the Gillette and Venus brands) and Oral Care (manual and electric toothbrushes under Oral-B). It makes money by selling these low-priced, frequently repurchased items to Indian consumers through distributors and retailers — traditional grocery and retail stores plus e-commerce and quick-commerce platforms — across a range of price tiers from mass to premium. Growth comes from launching improved products, advertising heavily to bring new users into the categories, and constantly cutting costs (management cites roughly Rs 40-45 crore of annual productivity savings) to fund that spending and widen margins. The business is India-focused and has grown steadily: sales of Rs 2,633 crore with PAT of Rs 412 crore in the year to June 2024, Rs 2,232 crore of sales and Rs 414 crore PAT in the 9-month transition period to March 2025 (after changing its financial year-end), and Rs 3,100 crore of sales with about Rs 650 crore PAT in FY ended March 2026 — a fifth straight year of both top-line and bottom-line growth.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Grooming** — Men's and women's shaving and grooming products — razors and blades across price tiers (Gillette Guard and the new Guard 3in1 for value-seeking consumers, Mach 3, Fusion 5, and the super-premium Gillette Labs), plus shave preps, styling/shaping tools and electronic grooming; management calls it one of the company's two focus categories and says Gillette is the market leader in India after decades of serving Indian consumers, though the calls give no separate revenue figure.
- **Female Grooming (Venus)** — The Gillette Venus line of razors aimed at women, including a Bikini Sensitive razor for intimate grooming, marketed largely through digital and social media to overcome myths and hesitation about female shaving; presented as a distinct part of the grooming portfolio, but no size is disclosed.
- **Oral Care (Oral-B)** — Toothbrushes and oral-hygiene products split into a Manual Oral Care range (including Criss Cross technology, a new Sensitive Care range with softer bristles that management says is growing double-digit and is one of the fastest-growing segments in the category, and a kids line with Chhota Bheem designs) and a Power Oral Care line of electric toothbrushes (iO3, Vitality Pro Sensitive) pitched at more affordable entry prices; it is the company's second focus category, again with no separate revenue figure given.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Gillette India comes out of the three reviews as an Outstanding business (82 out of 100: 45% x 75 on business quality, 30% x 100 on multibagger fit, 25% x 72 on risk safety), and the reason is the same in all three records — the company owns a position in the customer's head that it does not have to keep buying. It is the acknowledged leader in blades and razors after 115 years, Oral-B is the most dentist-recommended brush, and after direct input costs the company keeps Rs 63 of every Rs 100 of sales. That shows up in the pattern screen as four strong fits at once: Brand Strength, Innovation Dominance, Cost to Replicate and Corporate Culture, the last one earned by delivery rather than talk (Power Oral Care doubling in three years as flagged, five straight years of top- and bottom-line growth, roughly eight consecutive quarters of balanced growth, 30-plus years of unbroken dividends). The financial signature matches: Rs 1.19 of sales from every Rs 1 of assets, cash coming back one day before suppliers have to be paid so customers effectively fund the business, and Rs 64 of operating cash per Rs 100 of capital employed.

The traits that make the numbers good are the same traits that create the two biggest risks, and they should be read together rather than separately. First, the brands, the 115 years of blade technology and the innovation pipeline that earn the Cost to Replicate and Innovation Dominance verdicts are all licensed from parent P&G, with strategy, portfolio and supply mix set globally — that is exactly why Stakeholder Concentration is rated ELEVATED, and why the June 2025 call could only answer questions on P&G's global restructuring with 'market-level plans are yet to be developed'. The moat and the dependence are one object. Second, the razor-and-blades economics behind the Recurring Revenue pattern — handles sold once, cartridges bought forever, 5 million new Guard users in FY25 and 20 million over three years — rest on how often Indian men shave, and the ELEVATED Shifting Consumer Preferences risk is precisely that this frequency is eroding as beards and stubble spread, something analysts raised in all three calls and management conceded is accelerating. The company's answer is to sell into the new habit rather than argue with it: Fusion recast as a beard-styling tool, a new trimmer range, Braun appliances, Venus growth. Similarly, the simple nature of a razor or a toothbrush and the fat gross margin that makes the franchise attractive are what invite the WATCH-rated New Entrants and Good Enough Goods risks — management has already blamed slow oral care growth on entry-tier competitive activity, and defends with a full price ladder from Guard to Labs plus itemised productivity savings of Rs 45 crore, Rs 40 crore and Rs 38 crore in successive years.

The soft spot in the quality work is growth and how capital gets deployed, not how the existing business runs. Return on Capital scores 1.67 and Competitive Advantage 1.5, but Growth scores only 0.33, Management 0.5 and Capital Allocation 0.8, and the one clearly negative check is capex for growth: across all three calls management talks about productivity, supply-chain optimisation and even switching between local manufacturing and imports, but never lays out a capacity or growth investment programme. That asset-light, productivity-funded model is the direct cause of the very high cash return on capital — sales grew 1.5x while profit rose 6-8x over ten years with return on equity up 4-5x — so the same choice that produces the returns is what caps the reinvestment runway. It also explains why the pattern screen stops at LIKELY rather than strong on Pricing Power and Market Share Gainers, marks Low Price Plus only PARTIAL, and rejects Toll Roads and Forward Integrators outright: this is a consumer product sold through trade with no gatekeeper, no B2B lock-in and no owned stores. Consistent with that, resilience is judged SOUND and four of the eight risk channels — Cyclicality, Technological Innovation, Government Dependency and Fashion Risk — come in LOW, because demand is habitual, daily, consumer-funded and spread across two categories and every price tier. The honest read: an exceptionally profitable, well-defended franchise whose main uncertainties are a shrinking core use occasion, a parent that sets the agenda, and no visible plan to put fresh capital to work.
