### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 6 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 5. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 8 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Every call in the window centres on the AMC/service annuity attached to the installed base of water purifiers — bookings billed upfront and amortised over the contract tenure, with a rising mix of multi-year AMCs and a push into the consumable filter aftermarket. Three consecutive quarters of double-digit AMC booking growth in FY26 plus explicit talk of the installed base as a cross-sell/upgrade market. (The long view had said: The model is product-upfront plus an AMC annuity on an 8mn+ installed base, with bookings billed in advance sitting as unamortized revenue on the balance sheet; AMC bookings grew double digits for four straight quarters into FY26 and management deliberately shifted mix to multi-year contracts to lock in lifetime value, alongside a new filters-aftermarket push.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Named new products with attributed revenue impact in every call of the window — 40+ two-year filter-life SKUs, the Smart IoT range, three SmartClean robotics launches, Arctic Blaze — with robotics reaching ~60% of vacuum-cleaner sales and air purifiers growing 2.7x in FY26, funded by ~59% gross margins and backed by a revamped Bangalore R&D centre. Absolute R&D spend is not disclosed, so share-of-industry-R&D cannot be judged. (The long view had said: Named launches recur every year with clear revenue impact (Superio/Blaze/Eden in FY23, under-the-counter and instant-hot in FY25, Arctic Blaze in FY26) and premium innovation demonstrably lifted ASPs, but R&D spend is never disclosed or quantified and there is no claim to a share of industry R&D.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Share gain is claimed in water purifiers in Q3 FY26 and product growth has been in double digits in 6 of 8 quarters, but no share percentages, no named competitors and no international expansion are discussed — and Q3 showed the growth is still macro- and channel-dependent. (The long view had said: Share gains are claimed repeatedly — retail share up in both traditional and modern trade in Q2FY25, water purifier share up sequentially and year-on-year in Q3FY26 — but never quantified with actual share numbers, and management concedes two new aggressive entrants in the category.)

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 5 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: Demand is consumer, operations-linked and partly annuity (AMC bookings, filters, service) rather than tied to any commodity cycle or customer capex; through four quarters of repeatedly described 'challenging' macro the company still grew 11.3% for FY26 and expanded margin for a third straight year, while stepping up growth investment counter-cyclically (A&SP +21% in Q2, +23.3% in Q3, +13.2% for FY26). (The long view had said: Demand is consumer-discretionary rather than commodity- or customer-capex-driven, but the calls repeatedly show swings — 'subdued' demand and -8% revenue in Q4FY23, the unanticipated post-festive slowdown in Q3FY26, and FY27 input-cost/currency shocks forcing a 6-7% price increase; the AMC/filters annuity is an operations-linked flow business that dampens this.)

**That worsened:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: This is the sharpest exposure and it recurs in every call: the replacement-filter and service aftermarket is intermediated by market technicians who choose the part, consumers have low awareness of genuine versus cheap substitutes, and cheaper alternatives have held the position for so long that management concedes reversing it requires changing decades of behaviour. The company is simultaneously defending premium (INR 79,999 Arctic Blaze) and fighting at the bottom with an economy range and 'attractive pricing', while Q1 gross margin fell on 'tactical promotions' — the classic squeeze on a product that is not technically hard to copy. (The long view had said: The filters/service aftermarket is a textbook good-enough setup — an intermediary (market service technicians) controls the choice, consumer brand awareness of filters is low, and gray-market and parallel-network operators hold the volume; management is responding with 'attractive pricing', a simplified assortment, technician engagement programmes and consumer education, and concedes it must change decades of entrenched channel behaviour. The economy portfolio's growth and COGS focus on low-gross-margin lines point the same way.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Channel concentration is the live exposure: a single channel (e-commerce) caused the Q3 growth miss on trade-inventory build, and e-com plus modern trade drove 87% of the debtor increase in Q2; there is also acknowledged import dependence, with inventory deliberately built for 'supply security' and Q4 margin pressure from West Asia-driven input costs and currency. No single named customer or licensor drives results. (The long view had said: No named customer, licensor or supplier drives results; management repeatedly stresses growth broad-based across direct, e-commerce, modern trade and traditional trade and across regions, over an 8mn-plus customer base. The Q3FY26 e-commerce inventory episode shows some channel dependence but it reversed within a quarter.)

*Unchanged: Technological Innovation (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Government Dependency (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
