# Tata Comm (TATACOMM) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Tata Communications sells communications and digital infrastructure services to large businesses rather than to ordinary consumers — it runs a global fibre network backbone and layers cloud, security, collaboration, customer-messaging and internet-of-things platforms on top of it. Customers pay it, largely on recurring contracts, to connect their offices, factories, data centres and cloud providers worldwide, to secure that traffic, to move video for broadcasters, and to run the software platforms that handle their internal and customer communications; management calls this combination being a "commtech" player rather than a traditional telco. It operates globally, serving more than half of the Global Fortune 500, with roughly 60% of revenue coming from international markets and the rest from India, where it says it leads in data-centre-to-data-centre connectivity. Quarterly consolidated revenue ran around INR 4,569 crore in Q4 FY23 and about INR 5,798 crore by Q3 FY25, with EBITDA margins in the roughly 20-24% range and heavy ongoing investment in the newer digital platforms.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Core Connectivity** — The traditional business of carrying enterprise data over its own global fibre and subsea cable network — the largest single revenue line (about INR 2,590 crore of INR 4,903 crore of data revenue in Q3 FY25), growing only low single digits and exposed to structural price declines, though management calls it the foundation everything else sits on.
- **Digital Platforms and Services (DPS) / the "digital fabric"** — The higher-growth software and platform layer sold on top of the network, which grew 15-17% a year and rose from 32% of data revenue in FY23 to more than half by FY26, and now accounts for about 70% of the open sales funnel.
- **Next-gen connectivity ("network fabric")** — Software-defined network products such as IZO Hybrid WAN and IZO Multi Cloud Connect that link customer sites to multiple cloud providers with low latency; management calls it the fastest-growing digital platform, up 24% in FY26, and it wins deals by replacing incumbents' legacy networks.
- **Cloud, Hosting and Security ("cloud and security fabric")** — Private cloud and managed hosting (IZO Private Cloud), managed security services including zero-trust and SASE, and newer AI infrastructure such as GPU-as-a-Service and AI Studio; it grew 38.5% year-on-year in Q4 FY23 and 12.5% in Q3 FY25, led by security.
- **Collaboration and managed CPaaS ("interaction fabric")** — Platforms for how enterprises communicate — internal voice and unified communications (Jamvee) plus customer messaging through the Kaleyra CPaaS business and its Kaleyra.AI product; it grew about 11.7% year-on-year in Q3 FY25 and was a standout in Q4 FY26.
- **Media and Entertainment services** — Moving and processing live video for broadcasters and sports events over its media edge platform — it supported over 20,000 events in FY23 with revenue up 28% — and it bought Switch Enterprises to get into the Americas media market; revenue is lumpy quarter to quarter depending on the event calendar.
- **Incubation / connected solutions ("IoT fabric")** — Small but fast-growing newer bets — internet-of-things connectivity for enterprises and smart cities, CloudSIM software SIMs, and the MOVE platform used by carmakers such as JLR for connected vehicles; it grew 65% year-on-year in Q4 FY23 and 23% in Q3 FY25, with revenue that swings on periodic customer campaigns.
- **Subsidiaries and non-core assets (TCTS, TCPSL, Netfoundry, land)** — A set of separate businesses management has been reviewing, selling or fixing to cut debt and lift margins — it agreed to sell payments arm TCPSL, sought outside capital for Netfoundry, monetised a land parcel, and turned services arm TCTS from a loss into profit.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Tata Communications is, at heart, a trusted infrastructure business, and almost everything good in these records traces back to that. It owns global fibre and subsea cable that delivers sub-three-millisecond latency to the major cloud providers, it is the market leader in India for data-centre-to-data-centre connectivity, and it serves more than half of the Global Fortune 500. Customers rate it extraordinarily highly — a Net Promoter Score of 83 globally and 95 in India — and management is candid, openly flagging margin dilution, an impairment, a loss-making contract exit and a new CEO plainly conceding flat operating profit. Its end markets are genuinely expanding: enterprise cloud migration, network transformation, security, and now AI-related connectivity. On the multibagger screen this shows up as a Likely Fit on Brand Strength and Partial fits on Cost to Replicate, Toll Roads and Market Share Gainers — the backbone is expensive to copy, connectivity is a small but mission-critical line in a customer's budget that nobody wants to touch once installed, and the company does describe displacing incumbents. The rating of 53 out of 100, or Decent, is built as 65 for business quality, 74 for risk safety, and only 18 for multibagger fit.

The reason the multibagger score is so low is that the same asset base that creates the strength also creates the weakness. Being trusted and hard to replicate has not translated into the ability to charge more. Intangible benefits scores negative because there is no image- or taste-driven pricing power, and Pricing Power is a flat No Fit: management itself acknowledges structural price decline in the legacy business and cites its own price competitiveness as a selling point in IZO WAN. Operating margins fell from 24.2% in FY23 to 19.4% in FY26, and the shift toward digital revenue is itself margin-dilutive. Capital allocation is the weakest quality area at negative 0.25. Capex has run at 9-10% of sales and is genuinely aimed at growth rather than upkeep, but the returns on it collapsed — return on capital employed fell from 28.3% in FY23 to 14.9% in FY26 — which is why the growth-capex check scores negative even though the intent was right. The acquisitions tell the same story: Switch and the sizeable Kaleyra messaging purchase were followed by margin erosion and digital losses of roughly INR 400-500 crore a year, and subsidiaries were later unwound. Sales growth has averaged only about 2% a year, though very consistently. So the picture is a company spending heavily and reliably, but earning less on each rupee spent, and the screen's Corporate Culture pattern captures the consequence: bad news gets disclosed promptly, but the 23-25% margin promise was not delivered.

The risk review is where the strengths and weaknesses meet most directly. Only one of the eight channels is Elevated — technological change — and it is elevated for exactly the reason the pricing checks scored badly: the installed base keeps getting reset by platform shifts. Legacy circuits face structural price decline, SMS-based messaging is being displaced by newer channels which caps growth at Kaleyra, and media customers are moving off older delivery. The company has already lived through this once: digital payments eroded ATM transactions, forcing a INR 323 crore impairment in FY23 and the eventual sale of that payments business, which is why Shifting Consumer Preferences is on Watch and why Forward Integrators is a No Fit. The good-enough-goods risk is the same story from the demand side — public internet, broadband and 4G/5G substituting for premium private circuits — offset by the bundled digital fabric and the backbone that raise switching costs. Stakeholder concentration is on Watch precisely because the deliberate strategy is to go deeper with fewer customers, so large deal timing swings the order book even though no single account dominates. Government dependency is on Watch because fixed infrastructure means cables, licences and geopolitics, including cable cuts and collection problems in the SAARC region. Against that, cyclicality and fashion risk are both Low: demand is contractual, operations-linked and functional, not discretionary, and management explicitly kept investing through soft periods. Overall resilience is judged Sound. The honest summary is a durable, well-liked, structurally advantaged network business whose advantage protects volume and retention but not price, and whose heavy reinvestment has so far diluted returns rather than compounded them.
