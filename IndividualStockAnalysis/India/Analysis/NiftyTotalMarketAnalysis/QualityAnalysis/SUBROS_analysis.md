# Subros (SUBROS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Subros makes air-conditioning and thermal-management systems for vehicles in India — compressors, hoses and tubes, engine cooling modules, and complete aircon units. It sells these mainly to vehicle manufacturers (OEMs) rather than to consumers, with Maruti Suzuki and Suzuki Motor Gujarat together accounting for roughly 86% of revenue in the early period covered; other named customers include Mahindra & Mahindra's tractor division and Daimler Trucks. Revenue comes from supplying parts fitted into new cars, trucks and buses as they are built, with raw-material cost increases passed back to customers on a lag (quarterly, moving toward monthly indexation), plus a growing railway business that includes an annual maintenance contract. It manufactures in India — plants at Noida, Karsanpura (Gujarat) and a new greenfield plant at Kharkhoda — and reported FY26 revenue of Rs. 3,755 crores with EBITDA of about Rs. 363 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Passenger vehicle (car) thermal products** — Air-conditioning systems for cars, by far the largest part of the business at roughly 93% of revenue including non-car in the car/non-car split, with a 41%-43% share of India's passenger vehicle aircon market.
- **Commercial vehicle truck AC** — Aircon for trucks, which became a major growth driver after the government made AC cabins mandatory for N2/N3 category trucks from mid-2025 — sales grew 111% in FY26 to Rs. 263 crores, with Rs. 325-350 crores expected the following year, and a 41-51% share of the truck AC market.
- **Commercial vehicle bus AC** — Aircon for buses serving tourism and school segments; a smaller line where the company holds about a 16% share of business.
- **Engine Cooling Module (ECM), hoses and tubes** — Cooling and fluid-carrying components sold alongside the aircon systems; ECM was cited at 15% of revenue in the earliest call and is the focus of the new Kharkhoda plant with 4.5-5 lakh units of capacity.
- **Compressors (fixed displacement and e-compressor)** — The company builds about 1.7 million conventional compressors a year at Noida and is adding 0.5 million units at Karsanpura, plus a new Rs. 175 crore first-phase electric compressor plant there — localized with technology partners Denso and Toyota Industries, with business already won from Maruti Suzuki for models launching in 2027-29.
- **Railways** — An emerging vertical supplying driver cabin and coach air-conditioning to Indian Railways, including a Rs. 52 crore annual maintenance contract for railway AC installations to be executed over three years.
- **Alternative-fuel / EV, hybrid and CNG thermal systems** — Thermal products fitted to hybrid, electric and CNG vehicles — described as a cross-cutting revenue stream that grew from a 20% target in FY23 to 25% of total revenue by FY26.
- **Non-car products** — A smaller residual category — around 7% of revenue — covering applications outside passenger cars, such as aircon developed with Mahindra & Mahindra for tractors.

### The story in depth

Subros runs a genuinely well-managed operation. Every rupee of assets produces about ₹1.76 of sales, roughly ₹9 of every ₹100 of sales is operating profit, and that margin has moved only about 1.8 points in either direction over the years — steady enough that the framework reads it as costs firmly under control and as a sign that few new competitors are crowding in. Sales have compounded at about 11% a year, lumpy year to year but inside the 10-15% band the framework prizes. Management has stayed organic, carries no long-term debt, has done no acquisitions, and waited patiently on the big electric-compressor investment until hybrid and EV volumes justified it; the decade-long push to cut imported content from 42% to 26% to 16%, with a target below 10%, is the kind of unglamorous work that gets done only if someone keeps at it for years. The end markets are widening on their own: more vehicles fitted with air conditioning, the mandatory truck-cabin AC rule that drove 111% truck growth in FY26, railway electrification, and hybrid/EV thermal content now a quarter of revenue. On the framework's own scorecard this shows up as solid marks for return on capital, management and customer benefit.

The trouble is that almost none of this converts into the traits that make a stock a long-term compounder — the multibagger screen scores just 10 out of 100, with four partial fits and seven outright misses. The cost culture is real, and it is the single reason operating margins climbed from roughly 6-7% in FY23 to 9-10% in FY25-26, but it is the *only* profit lever the company has, because prices are set by customer pass-through formulas that carry no markup at all. So the same fact — cost discipline is everything — earns a partial 'Low Price Plus' and simultaneously kills 'Pricing Power' outright: when input, currency or wage costs rise, margins compress until the customer's indexation catches up a quarter (now a month) later. The decades-deep Denso and Suzuki technical and equity relationship, together with about 1.7 million units of compressor capacity and the ₹175 crore electric-compressor localisation awarded through Suzuki and Toyota Industries, creates a barrier that would be genuinely expensive to replicate — and that same dependence is why 'Innovation Dominance' fails, since the core technology is licensed in at royalties of 3-6% of sales and the flagship FY26 breakthrough is localising a partner's design rather than inventing one. Growth is also coming from the market getting bigger, not from Subros taking more of it: its disclosed share is flat to falling in passenger vehicles (40% to 43% back to 41%), trucks (45% to 51% back to 41-42%) and buses (20% to 16% to 11%). Management does what it says — it promised ₹160-175 crore of truck AC business and delivered ₹263 crore — but honesty about a business with no pricing lever does not create one.

The risk review turns those same traits into the main threats, which is why the overall picture is rated Mixed at 41 out of 100 and the company's resilience is described as strained. Customer concentration is the one high risk: the Maruti/Suzuki group was 86% of revenue in late FY23 and still around 80-82% in late 2024, and in the December 2025 quarter ₹846 crore of ₹948 crore came from passenger vehicles — the same relationship that supplies the technology also supplies most of the sales, so the moat and the concentration are one and the same thing. Because the vehicle maker, not the driver, chooses the air conditioning system, the product is judged 'good enough' rather than sought after, which is the flip side of having no consumer brand and explains the constant price pressure on new model launches. The regulation-driven growth cuts both ways too: the truck mandate and the government-tendered railway work are what produced the standout FY26 numbers, and they are also why policy dependency is flagged — two of the three growth verticals exist because a rule or a tender created them. The powertrain shift is a watch item rather than a wound, since air conditioning and condenser content works regardless of what powers the vehicle and hybrid radiators carry 1.6 times the content, but management spent years admitting it did not supply the electric compressor before finally committing. Add the absence of any export or overseas business — management deflected the export question and both new plants are domestic — and demand cyclicality tied to vehicle production, and you have a competent, honest, well-financed company whose fortunes are set almost entirely by one customer, one technology partner and Indian regulation.

## What to watch

- Customer concentration (Stakeholder Concentration, high risk): whether the Maruti/Suzuki share falls meaningfully below the ~80% level as trucks, buses and railways scale — the single biggest swing factor in the verdict.
- Pass-through terms (Pricing Power, no fit): any move from formula-based reimbursement with no markup toward pricing that lets Subros keep some of its own cost savings would change the whole profit story.
- Share of business (Market Share Gainers, no fit): a reversal of the flat-to-declining share in passenger vehicles, trucks and buses would show growth is being won rather than just received from a widening market.
- Electric-compressor payoff (Capex for growth check, and Technological Innovation risk): whether the ₹175 crore Karsanpura plant and the 70% localisation target earn a return above the current ~9% margin base, or add idle capacity.
- Policy-created demand (Government Dependency risk): whether truck AC volumes hold once the initial mandate surge passes, and whether railway tender and maintenance orders repeat beyond the ₹52 crore three-year contract.
