# E.I.D. Parry (India) Ltd. (EIDPARRY) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FERTILISERS & PESTICIDES*

E.I.D. Parry (India) Ltd. is an Indian sugarcane processing company that buys cane from farmers in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and nearby states, crushes it at its own mills (Nellikuppam, Sankili, Haliyal, Pugalur and others), and sells the sugar plus everything else the cane yields. Most of its money comes from selling sugar in bulk to the domestic market (and some exports, both governed by government release quotas and export permits), from alcohol — industrial-grade ENA and ethanol sold largely to oil marketing companies for fuel blending — and from surplus electricity generated by burning bagasse and exported to the grid. Smaller businesses sell branded consumer staples and packaged sugar to retail shoppers, and nutraceutical ingredients (algae-based), which are partly exported to the US. It also ran a separate raw-sugar refinery subsidiary that bought imported/raw sugar and refined it for sale — a large-revenue but loss-making operation the company shut down in FY26. Prices and costs are heavily policy-driven: cane cost is set by the government's FRP, sugar sales are capped by monthly release quotas, and ethanol offtake prices are fixed by the government.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Sugar** — Crushes sugarcane into sugar and sells it domestically and occasionally for export; the core business, running roughly Rs. 390-470 crores of revenue a quarter recently, with cane cost (Rs. 4,000+ per tonne under FRP) and government release quotas the main pressure points.
- **Distillery (part of the Sugar and Biofuel division)** — Turns molasses from the mills (and bought-in molasses) into ENA and ethanol, selling around 400 lakh litres a quarter at roughly Rs. 64-68 per litre for about Rs. 275-290 crores a quarter — the second-largest business, but squeezed because ethanol prices have been flat for about three years.
- **Cogeneration / power** — Burns bagasse left over from crushing to generate electricity, using some in-house and exporting the rest to the grid at roughly Rs. 4-4.6 a unit; small but steady, about Rs. 37-66 crores of revenue a quarter.
- **Consumer Products Group (CPG)** — Sells branded retail packs — packaged sweeteners plus a branded staples range launched around FY25 — with turnover that peaked near Rs. 195-236 crores a quarter and then fell to roughly Rs. 115-143 crores as management deliberately restructured the distribution channel to chase better margins.
- **Nutraceuticals (Nutra)** — Makes nutraceutical/algae-based ingredients sold in India and exported (notably to the US); the smallest business, around Rs. 6-13 crores a quarter standalone and roughly Rs. 50-65 crores at the consolidated level including overseas operations.
- **Refinery — Parry Sugars Refinery India Pvt. Ltd. (PSRIPL)** — A separate SEZ-based subsidiary that refined raw sugar for sale, generating very large revenue (about Rs. 700-1,200 crores a quarter) but persistent losses; operations were closed as of 31 March 2026, with staff settlements done, an SEZ exit underway, and about $78 million of bank loans repaid using an equity infusion from EID Parry.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, Cyclicality. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Essentially every profit driver is set by policy: cane cost by FRP (raised repeatedly, Rs.3,221 to Rs.4,122/MT), sugar volumes by monthly release quotas and export bans, sugar floor price by an MSP the government has refused to revise for years, ethanol by OMC offtake prices frozen ~3 years and by state-wise allocations, and power by exchange/PPA tariffs and tribunal orders. Management explicitly says only policy can move the needle.

> "We will really need to look towards the policymakers for some respite on upward revision of MSP and perhaps ethanol price as well... to move the needle, we'll need support from policy on that front" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate diversification into branded CPG (sweeteners, staples, planned ethnic snacking/culinary convenience) and Nutra exports, which are not policy-priced*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Every call is framed around the global sugar balance (surplus/deficit), raw sugar and white premium prices, and cane availability; results swing violently — Q1 FY23 PAT +Rs.13cr vs prior-year loss, Q1 FY25 standalone loss of Rs.78.6cr, refinery PBT swinging from -Rs.96cr to -Rs.6.8cr to a Rs.293cr Q4 FY26 loss ending in outright closure of PSRIPL with Rs.600cr of equity infused to repay its banks. Management itself concedes the seasonality and price dependence rather than any flow-product stability.

> "Global sugar markets are softening with a clear downward price trajectory and a shift back to surplus conditions... raw sugar has declined from about $0.80 per pound to about $0.14 per pound" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: CPG/branded sweetener and staples business is operations-linked repeat demand, but at ~Rs.115-216cr a quarter it is too small to offset the commodity core*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 2 of the last 11 years with swings of ±17 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
