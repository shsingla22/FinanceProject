**Checks that improved:**

- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: Four simultaneous greenfield growth projects (AURIC Shendra castings, Bidkin alloy wheels, Chennai brakes, battery packs) plus a 5x ABS capacity expansion, with capacity pre-booked by OEM orders rather than maintenance spend. | The long view had said: Capex is clearly growth-led and rising — four greenfield plants (Chennai brakes, AURIC Shendra castings, AURIC Bidkin alloy wheels, Talegaon battery packs) with India capex up ~33% year-on-year, though returns look ordinary as EBITDA margin slipped (standalone 12.6% FY26) while revenue grew 20%.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: ABS and BMS electronics carry real technical and validation barriers that have kept the field to MNCs plus Endurance, but OEMs are simultaneously running price and share wars downstream and Chinese entrants are pressuring Europe. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.4 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Capital intensity, OEM validation and stable promoter ownership raise barriers, but pricing is set by powerful OEMs and Europe faces rising Chinese OEM competition, so industry rationality is only partial.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: Two new R&D centres commissioned within the window (suspension R&D at Waluj operational July 2025, integrated brakes/ABS R&D double the prior size commissioned January 2026) and R&D is visibly converting into new products and customers — dual-channel ABS, in-house ECUs, BMS across 2W/3W/tractors. | The long view had said: R&D consistently converts into new products and orders (ABS single/dual channel, in-house ECUs, driveshafts, aluminium forgings, Maxwell BMS/MCU/DC-DC), backed by a new brakes R&D facility, though several platforms rest on partner technology (BWI, KTM, Adler, FGM).
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. ### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: In every quarter of FY26 Endurance outgrew its end markets — standalone +10.1% vs Indian 2W -1.6% in Q1, +16.2% vs +10.3% in Q2 — and Europe grew 28.5%/32.5% against EU car sales of -1.8%/+7.7%, with claimed leadership in inverted front forks and new customer wins (JLR, Hyundai, Kia, Isuzu, Yazaki, Valeo) rather than share bought on price. (The long view had said: Persistent, quantified share gain across the whole 2022–2026 record: order wins from every major OEM beyond Bajaj (TVS, Hero, HMSI, Suzuki, Royal Enfield, Kawasaki, Piaggio, Ather), cumulative non-Bajaj wins of ₹5,323 crores since FY22, ABS capacity going from 0.4m to 1.8m units against Bosch/Continental, Hero brakes business scaled to ₹200 crores with a line of sight to double, and EV sales compounding far ahead of the industry — all won on new platforms rather than by discounting, with margins holding.)
- **Toll Roads**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: ABS/braking is a small, safety-critical, regulation-anchored content item where Endurance says it is the sole Indian player against MNCs, and the MoRTH mandate is expected to lift demand five-fold — but the calls also show costs must be renegotiated with customers rather than simply passed on, which is not toll-road behaviour. (The long view had said: Brakes, suspension, alloy wheels and castings are a material share of a two-wheeler's cost, and the calls show OEMs negotiating hard — raw-material inflation is recovered only with one- to two-quarter lags, the opposite of a customer who pays without negotiating; no certification-standard or regulatory anchoring gives Endurance toll-booth economics (ABS mandates benefit Bosch/Continental too).)

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: As a B2B component supplier the calls contain no consumer brand, A&P spend, heritage story or distributor-leverage discussion; leadership claims are about product share, not brand. (The long view had said: Endurance is a component supplier whose only consumer-facing brand asset is the acquired Italian aftermarket name Brenta plus a distributor-led aftermarket build-out; there is no heritage story, no sustained A&P spend and no premium pricing from brand — customers are OEM procurement departments.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
