**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±3.5 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Approvals and RERA are real gating barriers that repeatedly delay even an incumbent, and pricing has been rational with realizations rising rather than discounting, though land bidding remains competitive. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±4.7 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Approvals, land aggregation and capital form real entry barriers (6-8 month approval cycles, RERA), and pricing has been rational with realisations rising rather than discounting, favouring large branded developers.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Good → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹29 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±3.5 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹29 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±4.7 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: Deals are small, simple and structure-simplifying — ~INR1,500cr to buy out minority stakes in PPPL/PGEPL/Franklin Mumbai unlocking ~INR20,000cr GDV, plus land bolt-ons; the one distressed NCLT takeover (Siesta) diluted quarterly margin. | The long view had said: Deals are land/SPV stake buyouts and an NCLT takeover rather than operating bolt-ons, and the NCLT asset actually diluted margins when handed over, so acquisition activity is neither clearly value-accretive nor destructive.

Now: Openly owns misses and explains adverse numbers without spin — admitted falling short of the INR24,000cr FY25 target, attributed the 22.5% Q3 margin to legacy NCLT-era pricing, and declined to revise guidance opportunistically mid-year. | The long view had said: Management explains misses and weak numbers directly — attributing the 22.5% margin quarter to a legacy NCLT block, admitting launch slippage and regulatory arm-twisting — though launch timing guidance has repeatedly proved optimistic.

Now: Indian real estate is consolidating toward a few large branded developers but remains fragmented and land-competitive, with management reporting unchanged competitive intensity rather than any structural rationalisation. | The long view had said: Indian residential development is fragmented and competitive with buyers holding full choice — management concedes ongoing competitive intensity in land acquisition and must price to feasibility.

Now: The opposite of under-the-radar — a large listed developer covered on every call by Axis, Nomura, HSBC, HDFC Securities and JM Financial, with an IPO of its hospitality arm in progress. | The long view had said: This is a large, heavily covered listed developer with analysts from HDFC, Nomura, CLSA, Jefferies, Ambit and HSBC on every call — the opposite of an ignored niche.

Now: Evidence of patient multi-year execution — 200 million sq ft across 300 projects since inception, a deliberate multi-year effort to crack the NCR market, and annuity income roadmaps out to FY30. | The long view had said: Three-decade record of 300 projects and 200 msf delivered, and demonstrable tenacity in grinding through multi-year regulatory blockages (Sector 150, Indirapuram, the 4,041-home Jijamata Nagar rehab) rather than abandoning them.

Now: No monopoly-like position — residential development is contested in every one of its markets and the company must win each land parcel through bidding and corporate deals. | The long view had said: The Prestige brand carries genuine mindshare that pulls sales even in slow markets, but the company holds no monopoly-like position and competes for every land parcel and buyer.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 6 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Every call in the window pairs higher realizations with higher volumes — FY25 realizations +36% y/y, Q2 FY26 apartments +8% and plots +43%, Q3 FY26 +6% and plots +30% — while FY26 volumes still grew 77% and sales 76% to over INR30,000 crores, i.e. price increases with no volume loss. (The long view had said: Repeated realization increases without volume loss across the period — 19% in 9M FY24 (of which 12-14% pure price), 6% y-o-y in Q3 FY26 with plotted developments up over 30%, alongside 77% volume growth in FY26 — but management itself attributes much of it to mix and to a rising land/construction cost base rather than firm-specific pricing power, and Indian residential pricing is cycle-driven.)

**That weakened:**

- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Prestige owns and operates what it builds — malls at 99% occupancy, a hospitality vertical at INR1,050 crores top line and ~INR440 crores EBITDA, and a BIAL leasehold for a convention centre, hotel and retail — which is genuine ownership of the customer-facing end, but there is no franchising, royalty or D2C-share disclosure, and developers sell direct by default. (The long view had said: Prestige retains and operates what it builds — malls (99% occupancy, INR702 crores quarterly GTV), offices, and hotels it runs under Marriott/St. Regis flags with its own appointed GMs — moving from developer toward owning the end customer relationship and recurring rent, though there is no franchising, D2C or own-store economics in the classic sense.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Sales grew 76% to over INR30,000 crores in FY26 with successful entry into NCR (45% of H1 sales) and Mumbai overtaking Bangalore, and the gains came with rising realizations and record collections rather than discounting — but no explicit market-share percentages or named-competitor comparisons are given on any call. (The long view had said: Sales scaled from INR16,333 crores (9M FY24) to a record INR30,000+ crores in FY26 (+76%) with successful entry into NCR, Pune, Chennai and Goa, implying share gain against a slower market — but no explicit market-share numbers are ever cited, and FY25 saw a sharp air-pocket (9M presales of just INR10,065 crores) when approvals stalled, so the gain is not yet persistent.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (QUANT SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (QUANT SIGNAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

**That eased:**

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Technological Innovation (NO SIGNAL).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
