**Checks that improved:**

- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: The domestic cycle turned from the bottom during the window (industry -1% Apr-Aug to +15% in Q3) and the largest export market still has recovery runway, sitting at half its peak. | The long view had said: The company was near a cyclical trough in 2022 (semiconductor rationing, export FX stress) and has since ridden the upturn to record highs, so the current position is late-cycle rather than pre-upturn, with only Nigeria still below peak.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.4 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Mixed — legacy ICE segments show rational behaviour and stable ownership, but the EV scooter arena has many players and Chetak's C25 was launched explicitly on an attractive price point, indicating price-led competition. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.6 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing conduct is rational (price increases taken ahead of competition, cost pass-through rather than discounting) and ownership is stable, but the EV and African commercial-bike arenas do see new and Chinese entrants.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: Weaker rivals are visibly ceding ground — Bajaj grew at twice industry rates in exports, gained ~500bps of e-scooter share in one quarter, and outgrew the industry 1.5x in 150cc+. | The long view had said: Explicit weak competitors to take share from — Chinese commercial-bike rivals in Africa and sub-scale e-rick players being squeezed out by regulatory enforcement, consolidating the segment toward larger OEMs.
- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Two structural currents are visible in the results — premiumisation of Indian motorcycles and EV adoption, with EVs at 30% of domestic revenue and e-scooters growing 60%+ — though much of the recent acceleration is policy/GST-assisted rather than purely secular. | The long view had said: Two secular shifts are documented over the full period — premiumisation (125cc+ from 42% to 55% of industry in five years) and electrification (EV at 25% of scooter sales, EV now 30% of domestic revenue).
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. Now: Growth capacity is being added where demand is binding — Brazil expanded to 60,000 units p.a., three-wheeler/EV capacity being built after supply shortfalls — while EBITDA margins held at record 20.5-20.9%, implying healthy incremental returns, though no capex quantum or ROIC is disclosed. | The long view had said: Sustained growth capex across new EV plants, Brazil manufacturing (35k to 60k units p.a.) and a board-approved 25% capacity build-out from 7m to 9m units, funded from cash while EBITDA margin stayed above 20%, indicating high incremental returns.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: Reliability is an explicit purchase driver in both retail EVs and commercial vehicles, with management citing trusted durability for Chetak and driver earnings/paybacks for three-wheelers. | The long view had said: The commercial customer explicitly buys reliability and trust over price, and management builds propositions around assurance and earnability, transferring that trust equity into the EV franchise.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: The benefit type is matched to the buyer — image/aspiration for retail motorcycle customers upgrading to Pulsar/KTM/Triumph, and earnings-payback TCO logic for commercial three-wheeler operators. | The long view had said: Benefit types are deliberately matched to customer — image and styling for retail 2W buyers, and running-cost/TCO plus reliability for the commercial 3W driver, with product trials designed around exactly that trade-off.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 6 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 5. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 4 weakened, 6 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Share gains are quantified repeatedly and across geographies: exports growing ~2x industry in the top 30 markets, ~60% retail share in Nigeria, 70%+ ICE and 85%+ CNG three-wheeler share, 65%+ of India's 3W exports, Chetak share to ~23% (+170bps), and a couple of points of domestic motorcycle share regained over five months after candidly admitting prior erosion. (The long view had said: Explicit share numbers rise across the series and are earned with profit, not price: Chetak from ~10% (Q2FY24) to 19% to ~23%, e-3W from 35% to No.1, 125cc to 25%, ICE 3W ~70-78% and ~90% of CNG, Nigeria retail 50%→~60%, and growth at ~2x industry in the top-30 export markets — while EV EBITDA turned double-digit positive.)

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Across all four calls management leans on brand equity — Pulsar's portfolio revival, Chetak's 'trusted durability', the Boxer franchise powering ~60% retail share in Nigeria, and the KTM/Triumph marques — with an 80-year company / 100-year group heritage and brand stretched into new categories (Chetak EV, Riki, exports). (The long view had said: Heritage brands are the engine across the whole period — Pulsar/Boxer/Dominar in exports (Boxer franchise underpinning ~60% Nigeria retail share), the Chetak name revived and stretched into EVs, plus 3-wheeler trust equity leveraged into e-autos, all under a 100-year-old group with licensed Triumph/KTM franchises added on top.)
- **Forward Integrators**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Clear and expanding company-controlled retail — 500+ exclusive Chetak stores plus ~3,000 shared outlets, ~90 joint KTM-Triumph showrooms, 75 own-format stores in Brazil, new exclusive stores in the Philippines — but no franchise royalty, D2C revenue share or own-store share of sales is quantified. (The long view had said: Sustained move toward the customer: exclusive Chetak experience centres growing from 43 dealerships (2022) to 500+ exclusive stores and 4,500 touchpoints (2026), 120 Triumph stores and ~90 joint KTM-Triumph showrooms, own manufacturing plus ~75 stores in Brazil, and captive finance BACL rolled out from pilot to 100% store coverage and profitability.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: LIKELY FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: The four calls discuss vehicle volumes, revenue and margins but never attach rates, AMC/service revenue share, renewals, or deferred revenue/customer advances as a funding source. No basis to judge either way. (The long view had said: Spares/aftermarket is an explicit annuity off the installed base — ~20% of domestic turnover in Jul-2022, a record every quarter through FY25, and a steady INR1,700-1,800 crore per quarter run-rate with record margins in FY26/Q1FY27 — plus captive finance (BACL) attached to 100% of stores; but there is no discussion of renewals, deferred revenue or customer advances funding the business.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Demand is driven by direct consumer marketing and product launches, not by a professional intermediary specifying the product — management cites thematic consumer campaigns, brand events and retail finance rather than trade training or prescriber influence. (The long view had said: the calls are silent and the numbers are mixed (1 of 2 checks supporting).)

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 3 worsened, 5 unchanged. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: The ICE-to-electric platform shift is reshaping both of Bajaj's core categories at once — e-autos growing 50%+ against a 70-75% ICE 3-wheeler share, and EV now 30% of domestic revenues by Q1 FY27 — plus component-level dependence on rare-earth magnets that cut Chetak output by ~50% versus plan in Q2 FY26 and forced a re-engineering from HRE to LRE-based magnets. (The long view had said: The company's ICE installed base and infrastructure face a genuine large-scale platform shift — by Q1 FY27 EV scooters and 150cc+ drive essentially all industry growth ('the 100cc and 125cc segments were flattish'), and EV penetration is ~25% of ICE scooters nationally, above 50% in some states. Bajaj has however converted rather than been displaced: electric 2W+3W now ~30% of domestic revenue at double-digit EBITDA, plus a CNG dual-fuel platform.)
- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Policy is the single most-cited demand driver in every call of the window — the GST rate cut is credited with turning industry decline into 15% growth and lifting ICE auto growth ~5pts — and management has gone as far as re-engineering products around tax slabs (350cc KTM/Triumph variants); RTO restrictions killed E-Rick demand, and Nigerian growth is attributed to pre-election institutional buying. (The long view had said: Policy repeatedly moves the numbers: GST rate cuts drove the FY26 H2 motorcycle recovery, the PM E-DRIVE subsidy outlay for e-3Ws ran out and the company absorbed the withdrawal into margin in Q4 FY26, e-rick volumes swung on street-level regulation enforcement, Delhi e-3W permits gate that market, and management openly lobbies for 'lower GST for CNG powered 2-wheelers'. Tax policy also hit results directly (INR211cr exceptional deferred tax from the Finance Act 2024).)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The demand base is visibly migrating — 100cc and 125cc segments were flattish in Q1 FY27 while EV scooters and 150-400cc drove all industry growth — so a franchise anchored in entry commuter volumes would be eroding; Bajaj's exposure is bounded because its portfolio sits on the winning side of that shift. (The long view had said: The Indian 2W demand base is migrating hard — 125cc+ went from 42% to 55% of industry in five years, 100cc declines while 150cc+ leads, and EV scooters outgrow ICE at 60%+ — but Bajaj sits on the right side of every one of these shifts rather than defending an eroding base.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Fashion Risk (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 1 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
