**Checks that improved:**

- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹25 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±1.4 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹25 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±9.9 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.4 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Vertical integration, in-house R&D and long build cycles create real capital/technology barriers, and pricing appears rational with gross margin expanding to 55.9% and management deliberately restraining order intake rather than chasing volume. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±9.9 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Genuine technical and capital barriers exist in 5-axis precision machining and no new entrants are cited, but pricing is not rational in the company's favour — contracts are fixed-price and cost inflation can only be recovered on new orders.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Good. Now: Mix is improving toward higher-value aerospace/defence (42% of 9M FY26 revenue, 38-41% of order book) with gross margin up 270bps to 55.9% and standalone Q4 EBITDA margin at 31.9%, though headline growth is still fundamentally capacity/volume-gated. | The long view had said: Growth is overwhelmingly volume/capacity-led (4,072 machines FY25, 1,750 in Q4 FY26 at >100% utilization) and management concedes it cannot pass on cost increases on existing fixed-price contracts, only on new orders.
- **Candor** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Management volunteered an unprompted explanation of the high inventory days, flagged capacity as the binding constraint, attributed margin pressure to employee costs, and disclosed the Huron investigation and revenue reversal — though the reversal was framed defensively and the Aug-25 '10,000 machines' figure sat awkwardly against the later 16,000 target. | The long view had said: Candid about hard facts — capacity constraint capping growth, negative cash flow, EMS delays and the revenue reversal explained in detail — but the 2024 promise of 500+ crores of EMS orders quietly evaporated (EMS fell to 4% of the order book) without being owned, and guidance is habitually declined.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Foreign suppliers meeting ~60% of Indian CNC demand are the explicit donator pool, and government self-reliance policy is actively shifting that share toward domestic players like Jyoti. | The long view had said: Imports are the donator — the domestic market is still largely import-served and duty changes now cut the company's own input costs, giving it a structural cost position from which to displace foreign suppliers.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: Large, clearly growth-oriented capex — India capacity going 6,000→16,000 machines by Sep-2026, Huron France capacity doubled in Nov-2025, and 20 acres bought at Tumakuru — funded organically while standalone FY26 EBITDA margin ran at 28.9% and utilisation exceeded 100%, implying high incremental returns. | The long view had said: Large, clearly growth-oriented capex — India capacity from 6,000 to 16,000 machines for INR400-450 crores plus a doubling of Huron France — with utilization already above 100%, though it is debt-funded (INR300cr Union Bank term loan) and incremental returns are not yet proven in cash flow.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Proximity-based support and technology centres near customer clusters hint at stickiness, but no switching-cost economics or regional exclusivity are demonstrated in the calls. | The long view had said: Some captive-aftermarket and switching-cost effect from a 15,000+ customer, 135,000-machine installed base served by an owned pan-India service network, but no regional monopoly.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Excellent → Good. Now: Distribution and service reach is being actively deepened through customer-proximate support/technology centres in India and the French base serving Europe, but there is no evidence rivals cannot match it. | The long view had said: Distribution and service reach is the clearest moat — 29 owned service centres across 12 states, installations in 60+ countries and subsidiaries in France, Germany, Canada, Turkey with a US operation being stood up, in a business where machine uptime and service response are decisive.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: The promoter-CMD is driving multi-year, capital-intensive bets — a 2.7x capacity build, an in-house training institute, and backward integration into controllers/drives/motors — that only pay off well beyond the current year. | The long view had said: Founder-led since 1989 with a consistent multi-decade arc — CNC in 1998, Huron for 5-axis in 2007, own R&D centre 2008, EMS products anticipated in 2015, and now an attempt to break Siemens/FANUC controller dependence despite acknowledging the difficulty.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. Now: R&D is a stated strategic priority with concrete outputs and roadmap — the Huma control panel, planned in-house controllers/drives/motors to cut import dependence, and exploration of semiconductor equipment — but no R&D spend figures or revenue-from-new-products metrics are given to prove efficiency. | The long view had said: Leonardo Da Vinci R&D centre with 140+ engineers has yielded 200+ product variants across 44 verticals, seven new launches at IMTEX 2025, a patented HUMA HMI, and the Tachyon product developed from 2015 in anticipation of the EMS wave that later became a real order stream.

*Unchanged: Return on capital (headline); Structural end-market growth; Merger and acquisition; Geographical expansion; Assurance benefits; Halo effect (caution); Customer types (retail vs corporate); Technology advantage; Security by obscurity; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Intangible benefits; Asset turn; Cost of working capital; Gain in market share; Out of limelight; Disciplined stewards; Cyclical market growth; People matter (talent development); Convenience benefits; Mini-monopolies.*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
