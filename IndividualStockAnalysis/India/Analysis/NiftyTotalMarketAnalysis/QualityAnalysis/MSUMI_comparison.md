**Checks that improved:**

- **Candor** (Management): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Management volunteers unflattering detail — restating greenfield losses at ~INR 70 crores gross once one-off recoveries are stripped out, admitting customer ramp-ups are slower than promised, and conceding a permanent denominator drag on margins. | The long view had said: Mixed record: they openly quantify bad news — the ~2-2.5% copper hit, Rs.127cr greenfield start-up costs, Pune stuck at 50% utilisation for consecutive quarters — but routinely refuse basic disclosure on margins, segments, customers and order values and deflect awkward questions.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Harnesses are model-specific and plants are purpose-built and co-located at OEM sites against firm orders, so an OEM cannot switch supplier mid-programme — reinforced by back-to-back commodity and currency pass-through terms no weak supplier would obtain. | The long view had said: Switching costs are real — harnesses are designed and validated per model, suppliers are selected carefully and locked in for model life, and plants are co-located at OEM sites like Kharkhoda — creating a captive, model-life position.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Grew ~33% in FY26 against a single-digit industry, with presence on the majority of new OEM model launches — clear evidence of share and content capture with runway still left. | The long view had said: Consistent, sustained outgrowth of the underlying industry (11% outperformance FY24; 25% year-on-year in Q3FY26 and 33% for FY26 vs single-digit market) implies real share capture, though the group already supplies nearly all OEMs, limiting remaining runway.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: A concentrated Indian harness market where customers cooperate on cost recovery rather than squeezing, though the buyer side is a handful of large OEMs which caps the score. | The long view had said: The Indian harness market is a concentrated few-player structure, but the end customers are large OEMs with clear negotiating power — they dictate pass-through lags of three to six months and can defer volumes at will.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: The Indian auto cycle is described as healthy and boosted by GST/tax cuts — i.e. mid-cycle rather than near a trough with an identifiable upturn ahead. | The long view had said: The company is riding a strong upswing rather than sitting near a cyclical bottom — PV production +19%, CV +18%, 2W +15% year-on-year — so the cyclical tailwind is already in the numbers.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Operates on successive five-year plans — Vision 2025 completed and Vision 2030 launched, the seventh such plan across a 50-year history — and invests ahead of revenue to secure launches. | The long view had said: A consistent, self-referential long-term playbook — ROCE >40% group guideline, capacity added ahead of demand, indifference to peers — held steady across all four years, though the company operates within its parent's and Sumitomo's orbit rather than fully independently.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. - **Out of limelight** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Quarterly calls are terse and substance-focused with the Chairman deferring to operating executives, but the group also stages a large celebratory investor meet built substantially around the founder's persona. | The long view had said: Management is consistently low-key and substance-focused, declining to name customers, guide on margins, or claim market share, and repeatedly attributing results to teams rather than themselves.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: The opposite of under-the-radar — covered by Nomura, Bank of America and Nuvama on every call, plus a large annual institutional investor meet. | The long view had said: Wiring harness is an unglamorous, low-profile niche, but MSWIL itself is a large listed company densely covered by Nomura, Bank of America, Nuvama and others, so it is hardly ignored by capital.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: Plants are built adjacent to customer facilities (Kharkhoda, Pune, Navagam) giving just-in-time proximity, and teams work programme-by-programme with OEMs, though this is proximity rather than a broad distribution advantage. | The long view had said: A 26-plus plant network with plants deliberately built adjacent to customer sites (Chennai, Kharkhoda, Navagam, Pune) and capacity created ahead of demand gives an accessibility and intimacy advantage rivals cannot match.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Weak. Now: Nothing in the calls points to a technology edge — no R&D discussion, and the competitive story is capacity, location and execution in a labour-intensive assembly product. | The long view had said: The company handles genuinely demanding high-voltage processing and validation and is powertrain-agnostic, but the underlying technology and localisation come from Sumitomo and SAMIL, and management concedes the business is largely assembly with no R&D intensity disclosed.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Neutral → Weak. Now: The evidence base is contaminated by heavy founder hagiography at the investor meet ('pride of India', compared to Reliance), which makes it harder to separate genuine operating skill from the reflected glow of the parent group's story. | The long view had said: Headline ROCE of 48% owes much to structural asset-lightness, contractual pass-throughs and a captive Maruti-anchored OEM base rather than demonstrable managerial skill, and management's refusal to disclose segment or plant-level economics makes results hard to separate from structure.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Consistent, quantified outperformance across all four calls — mid-teens growth against 4-5% industry in Q3 FY26 and 33% FY26 revenue growth against single-digit market — driven by presence on most new OEM launches and rising content per car across ICE, EV and hybrid, funded without debt or credit-quality compromise. (The long view had said: Persistent, profitable outgrowth across the whole period: FY24 outperformed the industry by 11% with ROCE up 44%→48%, Q3 FY26 grew 25% y/y against mid-teens industry, and FY26 revenue grew ~33% versus single-digit market growth to cross Rs.10,000 crores — though management explicitly refuses to quantify market share ('we do not evaluate ourselves in terms of the market share').)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Pricing is contractual cost pass-through rather than pricing above inflation — gross margin fell ~600bps across FY26 on copper with a 3-6 month recovery lag, though full recovery has historically come through and content-per-car/'premierization' lifts realisation per vehicle. (The long view had said: Pricing is contractual pass-through of copper and yen with a 3-6 month lag, not price rises above cost inflation: gross margin fell ~600bps from Q1 to Q4 FY26 on copper, recovered only by mechanical pass-through, and management repeatedly declines to discuss margins at all.)
- **Brand Strength**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The Sep 2025 investor meet leans on 50 years of heritage and customer/banker relationships as the differentiator, but this is B2B reputation — there is no evidence in the FY26 calls of brand-led premium pricing or category stretch. (The long view had said: No consumer brand, A&P spend, heritage narrative or distributor leverage is discussed; the only 'brand' reference is the Sumitomo/Motherson parentage as a technology and customer-relationship credential.)

**That weakened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: LIKELY FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: No discussion of cost obsession, itemised savings or a no-frills proposition in the last-12-month calls; the only cost commentary is copper inflation and greenfield fixed-cost absorption. (The long view had said: Consistent cost/efficiency culture — 'BY BY' improvement drive (Aug 2022), cost-reduction and customer recoveries plus shop-floor digitisation (May 2024), automation capex (May 2026) — and asset-light assembly gives very high turns (ROCE 48%), but there is no itemised savings disclosure or low-price positioning; gross margins are actually 500-600bps above peers.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No R&D spend, pipeline or named new-product disclosure in the last-12-month calls; new revenue is described as new OEM model wins and greenfield capacity, not proprietary innovation. (The long view had said: Some capability build-out is named — dedicated high-voltage harness line in Chennai (Aug 2022), EV/hybrid powertrain-agnostic portfolio and localisation with Sumitomo inputs (Feb 2026), digitisation/automation capex — but R&D spend is never disclosed, product design is OEM-driven, and there is no pipeline or share-of-industry-R&D language.)

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 2 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The powertrain shift is the live technology question and the calls show it raising, not destroying, MSWIL's content — EV mix rose from 4% to 7% with management explicitly linking EVs to content increase, and orders are being won across all three powertrains. No competing wiring architecture or legacy-installed-base problem is raised in the last four calls. (The long view had said: The powertrain shift is a live theme across the whole 19-call arc — dedicated high-voltage lines from FY23, analysts asking whether EVs reduce wiring, greenfields split ICE/EV/hybrid — and management itself flags OEMs 'trying to innovate where they have assembly issues', the zonal-architecture threat to harness content; the offset is that this is content evolution, not a format that strands the installed base.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Every operational answer across four calls routes back to a handful of named OEM programmes — greenfield utilisation, breakeven timing and the EBITDA drag are all governed by whether specific customers hit their RFQ forecasts, and one Pune EV programme missing forecast alone leaves that plant at ~50%. Management repeatedly says outcomes 'all depend on how the volumes will take place from each of the customers,' and pass-through terms differ by 'major customer' versus the rest. (The long view had said: Management concedes Maruti is the largest customer and declines to disclose customer mix; the greenfields are effectively built for named customers (Kharkhoda, Navagam, Pune), and a single customer's volume shortfall left the Pune plant at ~50% utilisation and kept greenfield EBITDA negative for several quarters — a direct demonstration that one relationship moves results.)
- **Government Dependency**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Volumes are visibly sensitive to policy: management attributed the Q3 demand step-up to the government's indirect-tax cut and the timing distortion around the announcement. This is demand stimulus, not regulated pricing — no tariff, subsidy or government-contract dependence appears in the calls. (The long view had said: No revenue, pricing or tariff is set by a regulator; demand comes from private OEM production across segments, and the policy items that do appear (the GST/VAT cut aiding volumes, the new Labour Code) are described as marginal or insignificant.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW); New Entrants (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
