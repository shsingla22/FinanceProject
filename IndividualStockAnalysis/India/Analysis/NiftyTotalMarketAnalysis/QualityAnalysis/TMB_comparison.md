**Checks that improved:**

- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: The bank is riding a favourable phase — falling deposit costs repricing into NIM expansion — but its 46% gold-loan concentration sits against gold prices near cycle highs, so it is not positioned near a cycle bottom. | The long view had said: The bank sits near a cyclical peak, not a trough: credit quality is at a 40-quarter best with 9bp credit cost, and the growth engine depends on record gold prices management themselves say may stall.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: TMB is a sub-1% share regional bank now growing business 23% y/y — explicitly ~7% above industry growth and its highest in 14 years — so it has both runway and demonstrated capture. | The long view had said: Tiny base with long runway and recent demonstrable share capture (23% business growth, ~7pp above industry), though much of it is commoditised gold loans and the prior 10-year CAGR was only ~8.8%.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: Growth is predominantly volume-led (advances +27.01%, deposits +19.71%), though mix shift toward higher-yield gold/MSME plus deposit repricing lifted NIM 45bps — so mix helps, but volume is doing the work. | The long view had said: Growth is overwhelmingly volume-led (advances +27%, gold loans 46% of the book), with pricing power historically weak — management consciously declined to pass on rate hikes in 2022 — and NIM gains coming mainly from deposit repricing.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: There is systematic capability-building — training cohorts of credit and relationship managers, 12 credit management centres and newly introduced liability RMs — though no evidence on succession or internal leadership pipeline. | The long view had said: Heavy investment in headcount and incentives (170 officers, 47 IT staff, 325 hires in a quarter, performance-based incentive), but the reliance on lateral market hiring and an externally recruited MD/ED points to a thin internal pipeline.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Good → Weak. Now: No monopoly-like position is evidenced anywhere; TMB is one of many lenders competing for the same gold, MSME and agri customers and has to out-grow the industry to win share. | The long view had said: A genuine mind-share position with the southern Tamil Nadu trader/MSME community built over a century of relationship banking, though it does not extend beyond the region.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Growth is entirely organic with no M&A, unsecured exposure of just 10bps, capital adequacy of 32.33% and a deliberate shrink of the problematic corporate book — offset only by the sizeable gold-loan concentration. | The long view had said: Purely organic growth, no M&A, secured-only lending (unsecured 10bps), counter-cyclical over-provisioning ahead of ECL, and very conservative capital — a textbook disciplined steward record over four years.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: There is a degree of regional entrenchment — a 105-year-old Tamil Nadu franchise with 600+ branches and sticky community deposits — but bank customer switching costs are low and management is actively expanding out of the captive region. | The long view had said: Effectively a regional franchise in southern Tamil Nadu with sticky deposits and local relationship lock-in, but switching costs are ordinary banking-grade, not captive.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: The bank was entirely un-owned by domestic mutual funds a year ago and FPI holding is still under 7% — a genuinely under-the-radar small franchise only now attracting institutional attention. | The long view had said: A century-old bank in a humble niche that institutional capital simply ignored — no mutual fund held a single share until 2026 and FPI holding is under 7%.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Management laid out a multi-quarter transformation plan (CASA revival, MSME rebuild, CMCs, digital) in Jul-2025 and visibly delivered it by FY26 close, including turning around a long-degrowing MSME book. | The long view had said: A multi-year transformation flagged in FY25 (credit management centres, RM layering, digitisation, MSME course-correction) was pushed through and delivered on schedule in FY26 despite legacy shareholder and ED litigation.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: The bank remains genuinely under-followed — zero mutual fund ownership as recently as June 2025 — though the MD's call commentary leans promotional in tone. | The long view had said: A low-profile Thoothukudi bank whose management explicitly prefers understatement, and which had literally zero mutual fund ownership until 2026.

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
