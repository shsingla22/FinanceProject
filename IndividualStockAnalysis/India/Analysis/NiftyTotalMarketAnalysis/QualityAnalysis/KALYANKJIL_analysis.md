# Kalyan Jewellers (KALYANKJIL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Kalyan Jewellers is an Indian jewellery retailer that sells gold jewellery and studded (diamond/stone-set) jewellery to everyday retail shoppers, largely for weddings and festivals such as Diwali and Akshaya Tritiya. It makes money by running a large chain of branded showrooms — some owned by the company, some run by franchise partners under a FOCO arrangement where the partner puts up the store and inventory — and earns a retail margin on the jewellery sold; a small amount also comes from gold coin sales to retail and corporate customers. Most of the business is in India, split between its home South Indian markets and the faster-growing non-South regions (non-South was 49% of revenue in Q1 FY25 and by FY26 was growing faster than South), plus a Middle East operation and small pilot showrooms in the U.S. and U.K. In FY26 the group reported consolidated revenue of about INR 35,740 crores and profit after tax of about INR 1,350 crores, up from INR 3,333 crores of quarterly revenue and INR 108 crores of quarterly profit back in Q1 FY23.

**The parts of the business:**

- **India business (Kalyan showrooms — own and franchise/FOCO)** — The core chain of Kalyan-branded jewellery showrooms across India, run both as company-owned stores and as franchise (FOCO) stores where partners fund the store and inventory — this is by far the biggest part, at roughly INR 8,990 crores of the INR 10,275 crores consolidated revenue in Q4 FY26.
- **Middle East business** — Kalyan showrooms in the Middle East serving local and tourist demand, a much smaller and thinner-margin piece at about INR 1,074 crores of revenue and INR 21 crores of profit in Q4 FY26, with some stores being converted to franchise to pay down regional debt.
- **Candere (e-commerce / omnichannel brand)** — A separate digital-first jewellery brand, now a wholly-owned subsidiary, that has been built into an omnichannel business with 110 physical stores as of Q3 FY26; still small in absolute terms (INR 131 crores of revenue in Q4 FY26) but growing very fast — 160% revenue growth in FY26 — and it turned profitable from the second half of FY26 after years of small losses.
- **Franchise showroom expansion** — Management describes signing up franchise partners as a distinct strategic engine — the partner funds store inventory while Kalyan books the sale up front, which lets the chain expand without tying up its own cash; started as a pilot in FY23 (first store in Aurangabad) and by FY26 is a standing pipeline with plans for roughly 150 new showrooms across Kalyan, Candere and a new regional brand in FY27.
- **New international markets (U.S. and U.K.)** — Small pilot showrooms outside India and the Middle East — the first U.S. store was targeted before Diwali FY25 and the first U.K. Kalyan showroom opened in FY26 — funded out of free cash flow (about INR 300 crores went to Candere expansion plus these pilots in 9M FY26) and not yet a meaningful revenue contributor.
- **Gold coin sales** — Sales of plain gold coins to retail and corporate buyers, explicitly called out as a very small sliver of the business — about INR 13 crores, or 0.46% of total revenue, in Q1 FY23, with no bullion sales in that quarter.
- **New regional brand** — A further jewellery format mentioned in the FY26 year-end call as part of the FY27 store-opening plan alongside Kalyan and Candere; the calls give no revenue or size detail for it yet.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Kalyan Jewellers scores 68 out of 100 ("Strong", four stars), built from a business-quality score of 70, a multibagger-pattern fit of 56 and a risk-safety score of 80. The quality work is strongest where it matters most for a retailer: the company scores full marks on gaining market share (more than 35% of revenue from new customers, non-South sales rising from 44% to 49% of the mix, and same-store sales growth described as ahead of listed peers), on geographic expansion (a franchise pilot in 2022 scaled nationwide outside the South, then into the Middle East and first showrooms in the UK and US), and on the two customer benefits that actually sell jewellery — certainty of purity and fair dealing, and sheer convenience from 129 new showrooms in FY26 with 150 more planned plus 110 omnichannel Candere stores. Management scores well on candor for volunteering its own misses (a missed profit target from overspending on marketing, an INR 120-130 crore inventory hit from the customs duty change, a budgeting error on advance tax) and on discipline for growing organically with no large acquisitions while cutting non-gold-metal-loan India debt from INR 1,300 crore to INR 300 crore over three years. The weaker quality areas are return on capital (0.67) and capital allocation (0.8), and industry structure scores zero — a reminder that this is a good operator in a hard industry rather than an easy business.

The pattern screen tells the same story from a different angle, and the two patterns rated STRONG FIT are the two the quality checks already flagged. Market Share Gainers rests on exactly the evidence behind the share-gain and geography checks, and the "share donators" check names who is losing: unorganized jewellers being squeezed by tighter hallmarking, plus two named South competitors that made strategic missteps. Corporate Culture is a STRONG FIT because promises from older calls were visibly delivered in later ones — the franchise pilot became the core growth engine, the debt reduction happened, Candere turned profitable as guided, and the dividend policy was honoured — which is the same behaviour the candor and stewardship checks reward. Forward Integrators (LIKELY FIT) is the mechanism connecting these: franchise partners fund the store capex, which is why the company can open at this pace while its own debt falls, and it is directly why the capex-for-growth check scores full marks. Brand Strength is a LIKELY FIT, supported by steady advertising at roughly 1.8-2% of sales and by the decision not to sell lab-grown diamonds because unstable prices would betray customer trust. Below that the fits thin out honestly: the gold savings scheme at 18-20% of revenue is a real advance-funded stream but only a PARTIAL fit for recurring revenue; innovation is merchandising and format change rather than research; and the barriers to copying — brand, network density, savings-scheme base, vetted franchisees — are real but the calls themselves show well-funded competitors expanding. Three patterns are outright NO FIT, and the most telling is Pricing Power: realisations follow the gold price, and the response to expensive gold was to trim inventory volume, push 18, 14 and 9-karat product, and run exchange offers and promotions. That is the same fact that produces the zero on industry structure.

The risk review returns SOUND resilience with a safety score of 80: three of eight channels are LOW and none are severe, but five sit on WATCH, and each of those grows out of a strength rather than sitting apart from it. Cyclicality is the clearest example — the gold price that drives volumes also drives the P&L, evidenced by the INR 120-130 crore inventory hit when duty was cut, and it is only tolerable because demand is occasion-linked (weddings, festivals, the savings scheme at 18-20% of revenue, roughly 30% old-gold exchange) rather than dependent on anyone else's spending plans. Government dependency is likewise two-sided: the same policy machinery that caused the duty-driven loss is the hallmarking enforcement that is handing the company share from unorganized players, so the tailwind behind the market-share pattern and the policy risk are one and the same lever. New Entrants and Good Enough Goods both trace back to the missing pricing power — jewellery is a low-complexity product where regional and unorganized players compete on making charges, so management spends defensively on advertising to hold share, which is precisely how it missed its profit target in one quarter. Technological Innovation is the one watch item that is not simply a strength viewed from behind: lab-grown diamonds are being funded and rolled out by leading players, and Kalyan's principled refusal to stock them is both the proof of the brand and its exposure if customers decide the cheaper stone is good enough. Against that, the LOW risks are genuinely low — the customer base is broad with over 35% new customers and no named customer or licensor in five years, franchisees are deliberately spread, gold is sourced through multiple channels and geographies, and consumer preference shifts over the period (unorganized to organized, plain to studded, offline to online) have all run toward the company, which it met with the 18, 14 and 9-karat range, a profitable Candere and the non-South push.

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Revenue and margins are visibly levered to the gold price cycle — management repeatedly frames quarters around 'extreme volatility in gold prices', a customs-duty-driven price fall cost INR 120-130 crore of inventory value in FY25, and by Feb 2026 gold was up ~100% forcing inventory tonnage to be trimmed and karatage re-mixed. But demand is consumption/wedding-led rather than tied to any customer's capex, and the company kept expanding (129 showrooms in FY26) through the volatility.

> "we cannot keep the same volume of jewellery, when the price is going up. We will have to trim the volume... because our turns will also go bad otherwise" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/occasion-linked retail demand (weddings, gold savings schemes at 18-20% of revenue, exchange programmes) rather than customer capex; countercyclical store expansion and debt reduction during the volatile period*

## What to watch

- Lab-grown diamonds (Technological Innovation, WATCH; Innovation Dominance, PARTIAL) — if funded competitors' lab-grown stores start taking studded sales, the refusal to stock them turns from a trust asset into lost revenue.
- Advertising as a defensive cost (New Entrants and Good Enough Goods, both WATCH) — marketing spend rising again to hold share against regional players, as it did when the Q1 FY25 profit target was missed, would show the brand is not carrying its own weight.
- Same-store sales growth versus listed peers (Gain in market share check; Market Share Gainers, STRONG FIT) — the whole share-gain case rests on staying top-of-street, so a peer-level or below-peer print would undercut the strongest pattern in the file.
- Franchise-funded expansion holding up (Forward Integrators, LIKELY FIT; Capex for growth check) — if partner capital slows against the 150 stores planned for FY27, growth would have to come back onto Kalyan's own balance sheet after cutting non-gold-metal-loan debt from INR 1,300 crore to INR 300 crore.
- Gold price and policy shocks (Cyclicality and Government Dependency, both WATCH) — another duty or price move on the scale of the INR 120-130 crore inventory hit, with no pricing power (NO FIT) to pass it on, is the fastest route to a weaker rating.
