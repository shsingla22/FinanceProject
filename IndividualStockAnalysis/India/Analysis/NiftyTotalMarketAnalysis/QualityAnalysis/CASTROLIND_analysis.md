# Castrol India Ltd. (CASTROLIND) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: ENERGY*

Castrol India Limited makes and sells lubricants — engine oils, greases and related fluids — under the Castrol brand in India, and is part of the global Castrol/BP group. Most of its money comes from selling branded oil to ordinary vehicle owners through an aftermarket retail network: as of 1Q 2026 more than 150,000 outlets nationwide, including 40,000 rural outlets, 34,000 independent bike workshops, 13,000 multi-brand car workshops and 800 Castrol Auto Service points, alongside sales to industrial customers and tie-ups with vehicle makers (OEMs). It buys base oil (a crude-derived feedstock) and additives from outside suppliers, blends them at plants such as Patalganga, Silvassa and Paharpur, and earns a margin on top — so profits swing with crude prices, the rupee and how quickly it can push through price increases. It reports on a January–December calendar year; FY2025 revenue was INR 5,722 crores with profit after tax of INR 950 crores, and it pays regular dividends (INR 8.75 per share for FY2025).

**The parts of the business:**

- **Core automotive lubricants – personal mobility (cars and bikes)** — Engine oils for passenger cars and motorcycles sold through the retail aftermarket under brands like MAGNATEC, EDGE and POWER1; management calls this the core business, says its share of the automotive aftermarket retail market runs around 21–22%, and expects low car and bike ownership in India to keep this demand growing for years.
- **Commercial vehicle and agriculture lubricants** — Heavy-duty oils for trucks and farm vehicles, sold under the CRB range (CRB Plus CI-4, CRB TURBOMAX+ CK4), supported by trucker-focused campaigns and programmes; described as an important, long-standing part of the business rather than a new entry.
- **Industrial business** — Lubricants and fluids for factories and manufacturing customers, with products such as Hysol, Alusol and the Spheerol range increasingly made and tailored in India; management highlights it as a scale-up area that has delivered double-digit growth for multiple consecutive quarters and expects government manufacturing push to support it.
- **Castrol Auto Care** — A newer non-oil range of car and bike care products (for example the Shiner Sponge), sold through e-commerce, modern trade and roughly 67,000 physical retail outlets as of end-2025, up from about 45,000 outlets in mid-2024.
- **Services network (Castrol Auto Service, Express Oil Change, bike points, Rural Service Express)** — Branded workshop and quick oil-change formats that pull consumers toward Castrol products — grown from 163 auto service workshops in 2022 to 800 by March 2026, plus 30,000-plus bike workshops, over 500–700 Rural Service Express units for bikes, and Express Oil Change outlets sited at Jio-BP fuel stations.
- **Rural distribution** — A deliberate push into small-town and village markets — over 40,000 rural outlets, targeting villages under 20,000 population — which management repeatedly names as a double-digit growth driver behind recent volume gains.
- **EV fluids (Castrol ON)** — A range of e-coolants, e-transmission fluids and e-greases for electric vehicles, currently sold mainly to vehicle makers rather than the aftermarket because the EV vehicle population is still small; supported by BP's ~GBP 50 crore-equivalent (INR 500 crores) global battery R&D centre at Castrol's UK headquarters and an India Technology Centre at Patalganga.
- **Data centre immersion cooling fluids** — A global Castrol initiative to sell thermal-management fluids that cool data centre equipment; management explicitly says it is very early, still at testing and proof-of-concept stage with partners, and too soon to talk about revenue.
- **OEM partnerships and sustainability-linked products** — Collaborations with vehicle makers (Triumph Motorcycles for POWER1, an MOU with VinFast Auto India for EV after-sales at Castrol service centres) and re-refined base oil work, including India's first RRBO-based engine oil for BS-VI engines and an MOU with Hindustan Petroleum to explore a re-refined base oil ecosystem.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Castrol India is a business that earns very well on very little. Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.69 of sales, three plants and about ₹150 crore of capex support ₹5,722 crore of revenue at a 24% EBITDA margin, and the company gets its cash back from customers before it has to pay suppliers — so customers effectively fund the working capital. Put together, ₹100 of capital in the business threw off ₹55 of operating cash this year. The quality scoring reflects exactly this shape: Return on Capital (1.67) and Customer Benefits (1.50) are the strong areas, Capital Allocation is solid (1.17), while Competitive Advantage, Growth and Management all sit around 0.33 and Industry Structure is actually negative (-0.33). The two things doing the heavy lifting are a distribution footprint that has grown from 135,000 to 150,000+ outlets with 40,000 rural points and an owned service network, and a brand backed by sustained large-scale advertising — the Shah Rukh Khan EDGE campaign, MotoGP, T20 World Cup and Wimbledon, reaching 300 million-plus consumers.

Those same two strengths are what produce the only unqualified pattern fits. The distribution and workshop network is the engine behind Friendly Middleman (STRONG FIT): the mechanic or workshop recommends the oil and the vehicle owner pays, and that network went from 28,500 bike and 9,000 car workshops in 2024 to 34,000 and 13,000 by 1Q FY2026. The advertising and 115-year history drive Brand Strength (STRONG FIT), and both together feed Cost to Replicate and Toll Roads — lubricant is a small share of a vehicle's running cost but critical to protecting the asset, with OEM specifications and 100,000 km testing reinforcing the position. Where the story thins out is beyond those two. Pricing Power is only PARTIAL because, despite keeping ₹49 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs, volumes and margins visibly suffered in 2022 and management has conceded mix dilution while adding mass-segment ESSENTIAL lines. Innovation Dominance is PARTIAL because the product cadence is real (about 20 localised launches in 2025, an India Technology Center) but the heavy R&D — the £50 million battery centre — sits with the parent, not here. Market Share Gainers is PARTIAL because share was flat at 20–22% from 2022 through 2024 with weak volume growth before that; the genuine gain, 12 consecutive quarters of volume-led growth and 8% FY2025 volume growth, is recent. Recurring Revenue does not fit at all — management never talks about renewal or attach rates, and describes the service network as closer to a cost of doing business. Low Price Plus is a clear NO FIT, since the stated strategy is a deliberate premium.

The risk review then shows the same traits cutting both ways. The middleman channel that makes Friendly Middleman a strong fit is precisely what makes Good Enough Goods an ELEVATED risk: an analyst pointed out that the workshop, not the consumer, chooses the oil, and the company is simultaneously defending a premium price, launching cheaper ESSENTIAL variants, and paying mechanics and franchisees — so the party choosing the brand is not the party paying for it, and keeping that party loyal costs money. The premium-pricing strength and the New Entrants risk (WATCH) are two sides of one coin as well, since the mass-tier launches were a response to competitive intensity and to customers 'not being serviced by Castrol'. The largest single risk is Technological Innovation (ELEVATED): the entire installed demand base is internal-combustion vehicles, EV electrification has been the recurring analyst question in all four years, and the deepest R&D response sits with the parent — the same gap that holds Innovation Dominance to PARTIAL. Cyclicality is a WATCH because demand is a steady consumable but the cost side is pure commodity — crude, base oil, FX — with margins squeezed in 2022 and again later, though the 21–24% EBITDA band has held through it. Against that, three risks are LOW: no government price-setting, no customer or supplier concentration on either side, and no fashion exposure in a functional repeat-purchase product. Overall resilience is SOUND, and the combined rating is 62 out of 100 — Decent, three stars — from 67 on business quality, 51 on multibagger fit and 68 on risk safety. The honest reading is a high-return, cash-generative, well-defended distribution business whose returns are not in question, but whose long-run growth engine and technology answer are.
