# Tech Mahindra Ltd. (TECHM) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: IT*

Tech Mahindra is an India-based IT services company (headquartered in Pune, listed in Mumbai as TECHM) that sells technology and business services to large corporations around the world — building, modernizing and running their software applications, networks, data and AI systems, and back-office processes. Its clients are mostly big telecom operators (it says it works with seven of the world's ten largest), plus manufacturers, banks and insurers, retailers and logistics firms, healthcare and life-sciences companies, and technology firms. It earns money mainly from multi-year service contracts and outsourcing deals booked as "deal wins" (TCV) and delivered by a large workforce, largely from lower-cost delivery centers in India, with revenue billed in dollars, euros and other currencies. Annual revenue was about USD 6.4 billion in FY26 with an operating margin of roughly 12.6%, and its main markets are the Americas (its largest) and Europe, plus selected Asia-Pacific countries.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Communications / CME (telecom)** — Its historic core and long-time largest vertical — IT and network services for telecom carriers, including fiber rollouts, customer-management systems and network operations; it grew 2.6% in FY26 and 1.3% year-on-year in the June 2026 quarter, and the company calls itself the leader in this segment with a dedicated Communications Experience Centre in Pune.
- **Manufacturing** — Digital and enterprise IT work for automotive, aerospace and industrial/process manufacturers (for example a company-wide SAP rollout for a Japanese carmaker); described as a "billion-dollar" vertical as far back as 2022 and the fastest-growing part recently, up 5.9% in FY26 and 17.2% year-on-year in the June 2026 quarter.
- **BFSI (banking, financial services and insurance)** — Technology services for banks, payments firms, wealth and asset managers and insurers — payments modernization, wealth platforms, regulatory compliance and core banking systems; also a billion-dollar-plus vertical, up 3.7% in FY26 and 8.1% year-on-year in the latest quarter, and being expanded through acquisitions such as Canada's Avant Techno Solutions.
- **Retail, Travel, Transportation and Logistics** — Digital, data and customer-experience services for retailers and logistics operators (e-commerce, warehousing, automation, last-mile delivery); it was the company's fastest-growing vertical in FY26 at 7.3% and grew 8.6% in the latest quarter.
- **Healthcare and Life Sciences** — IT and digital services for hospitals/providers, insurers (payers) and pharma — including building a digital wellness app for Medicaid members; roughly flat in FY26 (down 0.6%) but back to 7.2% growth in the June 2026 quarter on AI-led work.
- **Hi-Tech / Technology** — Services sold to technology and semiconductor companies; a smaller and weaker line lately, down 2.7% in FY26 after a client restructuring and soft discretionary spending.
- **Business Process Services (BPS)** — Outsourced back-office and customer-service operations run on clients' behalf, headed by its own president in 2022 and bundled with network services in deals such as the UK fiber contract.
- **Comviva and other portfolio companies** — Owned software/product subsidiaries — notably Comviva (mobile money and telecom software, including the Yabx digital-lending fintech) plus CTCo and Tenzing — that sell products rather than pure services; Comviva's revenue is seasonally weighted to the second half and it grew double digits in FY26.
- **AI platforms and offerings (Makers Lab, VerifAI, TechM Helix / Orion)** — In-house AI research and productized offerings — the Makers Lab R&D units, the VerifAI validation framework, the Indus Indian-language large language model, and the newer "Helix" programme with the Orion agentic-AI platform — used to win and differentiate client work rather than reported as a separate revenue segment.

### The story in depth

Tech Mahindra comes out of these three reviews as a company that has been repaired rather than transformed, and the rating reflects exactly that split: 56 out of 100 on business quality, 60 on risk safety, but only 25 on the multibagger screen, blending to an overall 48 and a Mixed, two-star grade with resilience judged sound. The strongest scores sit in the financial and operating checks — 19 of operating cash returned on every 100 rupees of capital, 16 rupees of operating profit per 100 rupees of sales with year-to-year swings of only about 2.7 points, and sales growth averaging roughly 9% a year with unusual consistency. That is why Return on Capital (0.67) is the best-scoring area and Growth, Capital Allocation and Customer Benefits all sit at 0.5. The weak areas are the two that decide long-run pricing: Competitive Advantage and Industry Structure both score negative, and the pattern screen agrees — not one of the eleven patterns is a clean fit. Seven are partial and four are outright misfits, including the two that matter most for durability, Pricing Power and Cost to Replicate.

The most important connection is that the same cost discipline that produces the good numbers is also the source of the biggest risk. The named cost programme — Project Fortius, moving more work offshore after admitting an 8-10% gap to peers in 2022, and building a pyramid of younger staff — lifted operating margin from a low of about 8.5% in mid-2024 to 12.6% in FY26 and 14.4% by the first quarter of FY27. That is what earns the Profit margin check and the partial credit on the Low Price Plus pattern. But it is margin repair from a low base rather than a premium the customer pays, which is precisely why the Pricing, Mix and Volume check scores negative, why Pricing Power and Toll Roads are marked no fit, and why the Good Enough Goods risk is rated elevated: clients run vendor-consolidation and cost-cutting exercises, and the only pricing gain anyone can point to is a one-off benefit of about half a percentage point back in 2022. The same doubling applies to the spending on analysts and sourcing advisers — the Advisor Day in Pune and the repeated citing of ISG, Everest, Avasant and Forrester rankings — which earns the Advertisement and promotion check and the partial Friendly Middleman pattern, but is reputation built during a turnaround rather than a heritage brand, so Brand Strength also stops at partial and the Intangible benefits check scores negative.

The recovery story and the risk register are likewise two readings of one set of facts. Telecom, the largest vertical, fell 12.4% in FY24 and has since turned up, which is what the Cyclical market growth and Gain in market share checks reward alongside constant-currency growth of 6.6% in the first quarter of FY27 said to be ahead of peers, deal wins up 42% to $3.79bn in FY26, and 29 clients above $50m. But the same vertical concentration and swing-driven results give the Cyclicality risk a watch rating, with margins moving from 13.2% to 11% to 8.5% and back to 14.4% over five years, and drive the Stakeholder Concentration watch, where a single top client and Comviva's seasonal swing of roughly half a percentage point still explain quarters. Innovation cuts the same way: sustained work through Makers Lab producing Indus LLM, VerifAI, Orion and 350-plus deployable agents earns the Research and development check and partial credit on Innovation Dominance, yet management itself conceded early outputs were demonstrators rather than revenue, no R&D spend is disclosed, and the Technological Innovation risk is elevated because the outcome-based contracts now being sold explicitly promise the client fewer tickets and lower cost — the Shifting Consumer Preferences watch is the demand side of that same trade. Capital allocation earns credit for a stated policy followed with record dividends, but the FY26 payout of 104% of profit exceeded that policy and no valuation discipline on buybacks is visible, which is why the area sits at 0.5 rather than higher.

## What to watch
