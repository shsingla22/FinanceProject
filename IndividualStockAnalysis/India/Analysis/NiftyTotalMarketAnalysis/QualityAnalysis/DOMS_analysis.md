# DOMS Industries (DOMS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

DOMS Industries makes and sells stationery and art supplies — pencils, crayons, pens, sketch pens, art materials, notebooks and paper products, school kits, pencil boxes and school bags — mainly under its own DOMS brand (plus smaller brands like C3, Amariz and Fixy Fix). It sells to ordinary consumers, mostly school children and their parents, and to offices, reaching them through a large distributor network covering all 28 states and union territories of India as well as exports to 44 countries. It earns money by manufacturing these goods in its own heavily backward-integrated factories at Umargaon (Gujarat) and Bari Brahmana (Jammu) and selling them at a markup; sales are seasonal, peaking around the back-to-school season. India is by far the main market at over 85% of sales, with exports (Nepal, Sri Lanka, Middle East, Africa, and newer markets served through its Italian partner FILA's network) making up the rest; group revenue was about INR 2,326 crores in FY26 with roughly 17% EBITDA margin and about 10% net margin.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Scholastic stationery** — Core school-writing products such as pencils and, more recently, pens and mechanical pencils; one of the two biggest core categories management repeatedly names as driving growth, with capacity added for it almost every year.
- **Scholastic art material** — Crayons, colours, sketch pens, acrylic markers and similar art supplies for school children; the other big core category, with growth in FY26 helped by added capacity and modernised processes.
- **Paper stationery** — Notebooks and paper-based products, made partly through subsidiaries Pioneer Stationery and the proposed Super Treads acquisition (aimed at strengthening eastern India); management calls it a capacity-expansion priority rather than the largest category.
- **Kits and combos** — Bundled gift/stationery packs sold as a single unit; described as doing 'pretty well' and a repeated growth driver, partly taking sales that would otherwise sit in the stationery and art categories.
- **Office supplies** — Products for office use including newly launched stamp pads; called out as the fastest-growing category in Q3 and Q4 FY26 on the back of added capacity and good market reception.
- **Hobby and craft** — Craft and hobby products for children and hobbyists; a smaller category but flagged as seeing 'robust demand growth' in FY26 from new, utility-focused launches.
- **Fine art products** — Higher-end art materials for serious artists; listed as one of the seven categories but barely discussed on the calls, suggesting it is small.
- **Baby hygiene (Uniclan Healthcare)** — Acquired subsidiary selling diapers and baby hygiene products — a meaningful new leg that added roughly 10 percentage points to FY25 group growth, is seasonal (strong in winter, weak in Q4), and carries lower margins that pulled group EBITDA margin down in FY26.
- **School bags and pencil boxes (SKIDO Industries and the Seven SpA JV)** — A newer line launched in FY25/FY26 selling DOMS-branded school bags and metal pencil boxes, to be extended via a 50-50 JV with FILA group company Seven SpA for premium backpacks, bags and pencil cases; still early-stage but expected to be a future growth driver.
- **Micro Wood (subsidiary)** — In-house supplier of wood/packaging inputs to DOMS — part of the backward-integration strategy rather than a separate consumer business; its consolidation helped lift margins in FY24.
- **Exports / FILA distribution tie-up** — Direct export of DOMS-branded products to Nepal, Sri Lanka, the Middle East and Africa, plus newer markets like Chile, Mexico, Canada, Europe, Turkey, South Africa and Australia through partner FILA's network; grew over 15% in 9M FY26 but is under 15% of total sales.

### The story in depth

DOMS earns a Strong rating of 67 out of 100, and the three separate reviews agree on where the strength comes from: getting the product in front of the buyer. The quality framework gives top marks to distribution reach (general trade across 28 states, more than 75% of sales, over 4,000 distributors, plus 44 export markets), to convenience for the shopper, and to a market that is itself expanding at 11-12% a year on education spending. The pattern screen reads the same facts and calls Market Share Gainers a strong fit — roughly 22-25% revenue growth for three straight years against that 11-12% industry, from an already-leading 29% share of pencils but only 11-12% of stationery overall, so there is a long runway left. Brand Strength is a likely fit and Corporate Culture a strong one: a founder-family business that has met or beaten every target it set on its calls while openly naming its own shortfalls, which is exactly what the framework's top score for candour is picking up. The same distribution network is what lets the company keep launching into new shelves — school bags, pencil boxes, baby hygiene — and it is why the pattern screen judges the business genuinely hard, if not impossible, to copy.

The uncomfortable part is that the very thing that makes the product easy to sell also makes it easy to attack. Pencils, pens and notebooks are simple, low-ticket goods, so the risk review flags Good Enough Goods and New Entrants as things to watch, and the pattern screen can only call Pricing Power a partial fit. These are one story, not three. Prices do rise steadily, roughly 3-5% a year without losing volume, but when raw material costs jumped 15-20% in the FY26 West Asia shock the company passed on only 4-5% and absorbed the rest in its margin — and management said plainly that it was holding prices to protect its shelf space from new entrants. That single decision is what turns a stable, repeat-purchase consumable into an elevated cyclicality risk: demand itself is steady, but profit moves with crude-linked input costs because price is not the lever of first resort. It also explains why the pattern screen rates the low-cost model and the innovation record as partial rather than proven — the cost discipline and the steady stream of launches such as acrylic markers, mechanical pencils and stamp pads are real and revenue-generating, but neither is broken out in enough detail to count as a durable edge on its own.

The third thread is cash. Four of the eleven patterns are outright misses, and they cluster around the same point: this is a one-time product sale through independent retailers, with no subscriptions, service contracts, owned stores or royalties, and no professional intermediary recommending the brand. So customers do not fund the business — the company does. Working capital is a weak spot in the quality framework, with about 96 days of cash tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and it is growing on the receivables side, while capital spending has climbed every year from about INR150 crore in FY24 to INR213 crore in FY25 to INR292 crore in FY26, with INR250-275 crore guided for FY27 and held there even through the cost shock. That spending is genuinely for growth rather than upkeep, and it is converting into capacity, but the headline return is a moderate 19 paise of operating cash per rupee of capital employed, and the framework's weakest scores are on growth consistency — average growth near 24% a year, but lumpy, and the three-year sequence of 25%, 24.4% and 21.6% is drifting down even as it stays well ahead of the industry. Set against that, the risk review is the most reassuring of the three: four of eight channels score low, with no meaningful government, regulatory, fashion or digital-substitution exposure, and the one consumer shift discussed — buyers moving to combination packs — was captured by the company rather than lost, with kits and combos growing over 40% in FY25. The main relationship worth watching is FILA, which is at once a shareholder, an export distributor and the largest export customer at INR166.5 crore of roughly INR275 crore of FY25 exports, flat for two years, and now deepened further by a 50-50 joint venture.

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds"*

## What to watch

- Pass-through in the next input-cost cycle (Pricing Power, partial fit; Cyclicality, elevated): if another raw-material spike is again met with only 4-5% of price increase, the margin swing repeats and the pricing-power case weakens further.
- The FILA relationship (Stakeholder Concentration, watch): FILA sales have already been flat for two years at INR166.5 crore of roughly INR275 crore of FY25 exports, so the newly opened direct DOMS-branded routes into Chile, Mexico, Canada, Turkey and South Africa need to keep scaling.
- Cash tied up in the business (Cost of working capital check, negative; Recurring Revenue, no fit): the 96-day, receivables-led cycle sits alongside capital spending of INR292 crore in FY26 and INR250-275 crore guided for FY27, and customers do not fund any of it.
- Holding the growth gap (Persistence of growth check, weakest score; Market Share Gainers, strong fit): growth has stepped down from 25% to 24.4% to 21.6%, and the pattern rests entirely on staying well clear of an industry growing 11-12%.
- Shelf space against cheaper unbranded goods (Good Enough Goods and New Entrants, both watch): the defence so far has been channel schemes and margin rationalisation rather than brand pull alone, so any loss of shelf position despite that spending would be a real signal.
- Continued candour and guidance delivery (Candor check, top score; Corporate Culture, strong fit): a missed target that is not explained as openly as the pencil-capacity and paper-degrowth shortfalls were would undercut a pillar of the rating.
