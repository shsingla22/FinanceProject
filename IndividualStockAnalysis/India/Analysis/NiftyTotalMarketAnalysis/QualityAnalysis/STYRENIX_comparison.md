### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 4 weakened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: UNKNOWN on the long view, UNKNOWN on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Recent record is candid and return-focused — the Oct-'23 debottlenecking was completed as promised, ROE/ROCE of ~25%/32% on a prudent balance sheet, and management disclosed the Thailand inventory losses and refused to give EBITDA projections it could not stand behind; against that, the pre-FY23 leadership had heavy MD/Chairman churn and the Thailand ramp has slipped from the original framing. - **Low Price Plus**: LIKELY FIT → NO FIT. Why now: No cost-culture narrative, itemised savings or no-frills positioning appears in the FY26 calls; margin discussion is entirely about raw-material spreads, not structural cost advantage. (The long view had said: A consistent, itemised cost programme runs through the calls — debottlenecking PS from 66KT to 100KT with 'minor capital expenditure', switching Dahej fuel from natural gas to renewables, third-party power sourcing, formulation optimisation — delivering FY26 ROE/ROCE of ~25%/32%; but savings are retained rather than passed to price, and management explicitly walks away from the low-price GPPS import segment.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No R&D spend, pipeline, or named new-product launches are disclosed in the last-12-months calls; growth capex is capacity debottlenecking and a Phase 1 ABS line, not innovation-led. - **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Management itself describes the inputs and effectively the output as commodities whose regional price gaps normalise quickly, and its own re-branding showed customer qualification could be re-cleared within months — no articulable reason a funded entrant could not replicate the position. (The long view had said: There are articulable barriers — multi-year customer qualification per grade, India-specific customisation, 40 years of family process knowledge, and brand registration at the end customer — but a well-funded entrant is demonstrably rebuilding it: Supreme Petrochem's alternative ABS technology and a 'third now player' have entered, and imports still serve a large share of demand.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 2 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 2 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Management states raw materials are bought from multiple global sources with no disruption even through the Hormuz crisis, and the customer base is spread across appliances, autos and stationery in India plus China, Japan, Korea, Vietnam, Indonesia and Thailand, with new offices opened in Shanghai and Vietnam; the Thailand brand transition tested the customer link and ~90% were retained. (The long view had said: Real but bounded input-side concentration: styrene and acrylonitrile are largely imported and butadiene is 'largely or completely sourced locally' from a handful of Indian producers, and the Thailand acquisition required re-validating every customer through brand re-registration (~90% retained) — a reminder that specialty ABS relationships are qualification-locked; on the demand side no single customer is ever named as driving results.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Every call frames results as commodity spreads over styrene/acrylonitrile with formula-driven finished-goods pricing, and FY26 standalone revenue fell ~3.9% 'more on the pricing-driven things' while quarterly EBITDA margin swung from 11.7% (Q3) to 19.2% (Q4); end demand sits in cyclical auto (2W/PV), appliances/AC and consumer durables, with monsoon seasonality, Thailand inventory losses and a Strait-of-Hormuz raw-material shock all hitting inside 12 months. Partial offset: the resin is a flow input into customers' ongoing production rather than their capex, so volumes held up (195 KT, +5.2%) even as realisations swung. (The long view had said: Styrenix is a styrenics commodity converter whose selling prices track styrene/butadiene/acrylonitrile and whose spreads swing violently — PBT for 9M FY23 fell to INR188.7cr from INR312.5cr, standalone EBITDA margin ranged from ~10.9% (Q3FY25) to 19.2% (Q4FY26), Thailand booked inventory losses in FY26 as RM and FG prices fell, and by May 2026 ABS prices had moved from INR110-120 pre-war to INR190-200 on the Strait of Hormuz disruption, with management admitting demand contraction and an inability to guide volumes.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
