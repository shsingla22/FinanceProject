# Belrise Industri (BELRISE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Belrise Industries (formerly Badve Engineering, founded 1996, listed in May 2025) is an Indian auto-parts maker that builds the metal and plastic structural pieces vehicles are made of — chassis frames, body-in-white parts, exhaust systems, suspensions, steering columns and moulded plastic components — and sells them directly to vehicle manufacturers (OEMs) rather than to consumers. Its core skill is precision sheet-metal pressing, fabrication, robotic welding, coating and plastic moulding, and it earns money on a per-part basis under long-term OEM programs, often as the single-source supplier, with steel and plastic cost swings passed back to customers with a lag under a back-to-back pricing model. It serves 2-wheelers (its stronghold, ~24% share of Indian 2-wheeler metal components), plus 3-wheelers, cars, commercial vehicles and agri-vehicles, across roughly 22 plants in India (Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Rajasthan, Uttarakhand and more) plus France, with quarterly revenue around INR 23–25.5 billion and PAT around INR 1.3 billion. Management's stated push is to move from being a parts vendor to a 'Tier-0.5' supplier delivering whole systems and sub-assemblies, raising rupee content per vehicle, while diversifying into solar structures and aerospace/defence.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Two-wheeler metal components / chassis systems** — The historic core: pressed and fabricated metal chassis and frames for motorcycles and scooters, where Belrise is the largest Indian player at ~24% market share (roughly 1 in 4 chassis on Indian roads or exported from India), with content per 2-wheeler quoted at ~INR 12,500 in FY25 and said to have grown 65–70% during FY26.
- **Four-wheeler and commercial vehicle components (BIW, cross car beams, long members)** — Body-in-white sheet-metal parts, cross car beams and high-tensile structural 'long members' for cars, SUVs and medium/heavy trucks — only ~12% of manufacturing revenue in FY25, but the declared growth priority, and management says 4-wheeler revenue grew 37% and CV 35% in FY26 with content per vehicle up 40–45% helped by the H-One addition.
- **Polymer / plastic-moulded components** — Injection-moulded plastic parts, painting, aesthetic finishing and fairing assemblies for 2-wheelers, cars and even consumer-durables OEMs; Belrise alone holds ~10% share of Indian 2-wheeler plastics, rising to ~25% once promoter entities Badve Autocomps (FY25 revenue ~INR 14bn) and Eximius Infra Tech (~INR 7bn) merge in.
- **Proprietary / premium products** — Self-designed higher-value items — suspensions, steering columns, braking systems, high-tensile parts, air tanks, filtration systems and premium exhausts — which management calls a key growth driver and where it says it added four new marquee customers each in suspensions, steering columns and high-tensile, and two in braking, during FY26.
- **Aerospace and defence (Belrise Aerospace & Defence)** — A new, strategically emphasised arm built by acquiring SDM in France (Q3 FY26) and Chester Hall in the UK (Q4 FY26), now supplying two of the world's largest aircraft OEMs and their engine suppliers, plus a partnership with Israel's Plasan Sasa to bring the ATEMM platform to India; still loss-making (INR 94.7mn one-time operational loss in Q4 FY26) with SDM expected to turn EBITDA-positive in 2027.
- **Non-auto engineering / renewable structures** — Fabricated solar mounting structures made with the same fabrication and surface-treatment lines, with first supplies begun in Q2 FY26 to a large global solar tracker maker for North America and talks on for India and Europe; described as small but fast-converting because of its short execution cycle.
- **EV-related components** — Not a separate reported segment but repeatedly highlighted: over 73% of FY25 manufacturing revenue came from powertrain-agnostic products that fit both petrol and electric vehicles, plus specific EV wins — a single-source chassis and BIW package for a 2-wheeler EV platform out of Chennai (~INR 1,500mn at peak), a 3-wheeler EV suspension order, and copper bus bars for battery systems coming in via the Eximius merger.
- **Non-manufacturing revenue** — In every quarter management quotes total revenue separately from a smaller 'manufacturing revenue' figure (e.g. INR 25,528mn total vs INR 21,763mn manufacturing in Q4 FY26), so roughly INR 4–5bn a quarter sits outside manufacturing and carries much thinner margins, but the excerpts never say what this activity is.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

**Industry Structure — Neutral (+0.50)** · 6 of 6 checks had evidence

- **Share donators — Excellent** *(from the conference calls)*: Clear weak competitors to feed on — a distressed Tier-1 whose business Belrise absorbed in eight weeks, plus H-One's Japanese parent and the SDM/Chester Hall owners exiting sub-scale assets into Belrise's hands.

  > "we are currently the largest player in the Indian 2-wheeler metal component with a 24% market share" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Security by obscurity — Weak** *(from the conference calls)*: No longer under the radar — a May 2025 IPO, TTM P/E of ~30.9x, sell-side coverage from Jefferies, Investec and others, and an active M&A and aerospace narrative attract exactly the capital and attention this criterion prizes avoiding.
  > "Belrise is currently trading at a P/E multiple of approximately 30.9 on a TTM basis" — *management, on an earnings call*

**Customer Benefits — Good (+0.75)** · 4 of 4 checks had evidence

- **Assurance benefits — Excellent** *(from the conference calls)*: The core customer proposition is certainty on safety-critical parts — 31 quality awards, two JIPM TPM awards, 0.5-1% rejection rates at Chester Hall, and an eight-week emergency rescue that won a major follow-on order on trust rather than price.

- **Convenience benefits — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: Deliberate proximity model — plants sited where customers build (Chennai, Bhiwadi, Haridwar, Pune) and warehouses located next to OEMs to cut cost and delivery time, backed by direct senior-level customer intimacy such as the 300-delegate technology show.

- **Customer types (retail vs corporate) — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: Correctly matched to a corporate customer base — the pitch is TCO, lightweighting, single-source reliability and system-level integration (Tier 0.5) that creates lock-in, rather than any retail-style intangible.
  > "we are not just a Tier 1 supplier, but we are moving towards a concept called Tier 0.5 supplier, which is an integrated role getting more deeply entrenched with OEMs" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Intangible benefits — Weak** *(from the numbers and the calls together)*: After direct input costs, the company keeps ₹19 of every ₹100 of sales — thin pricing power; customers likely buy on price. And from the calls: No image- or taste-driven pricing power: this is a hidden B2B component with pass-through raw material pricing, 13.7% manufacturing EBITDA and 5.3% PAT margins, and Q4 cost pressures from wages, energy and logistics.
  > "we faced certain supply chain challenges arising from an ongoing oil crisis, along with elevated raw material, energy, and logistic costs" — *management, on an earnings call*

**Competitive Advantage — Neutral (+0.00)** · 3 of 3 checks had evidence

- **Technology advantage — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: A real multi-process technical edge — 1,000 MPa high-tensile forming versus the 600-700 MPa industry norm, sub-5-micron plastics, sub-2-micron aero-engine machining, 800 robots and IoT shop floors — but much of the frontier capability was bought rather than internally developed.
  > "while the average Indian industry for high-tensile strength is 600 megapascals, this component produces around 980 megapascals" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Distribution advantage — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: A pan-India manufacturing and service footprint of 22 plants plus co-located warehouses that management argues rivals cannot replicate, which matters because just-in-time delivery reliability is the OEM's decisive criterion.
  > "Our customized capabilities built through close collaboration with customers and backed by our Pan-India presence are tough to replicate" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Network effects — Poor** *(from the conference calls)*: The business model is bilateral OEM nomination and dedicated tooling with no platform or two-sided dynamic; nothing in any of the seven calls describes value increasing for one customer as others join.
