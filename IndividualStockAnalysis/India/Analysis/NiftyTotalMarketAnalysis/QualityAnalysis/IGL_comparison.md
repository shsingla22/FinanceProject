## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 29 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 7 improved, 5 regressed, 17 unchanged. 1 check carries the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them). 2 were newly assessable in the recent view.

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|

| Return on Capital | Neutral (+0.33) | Good (+0.67) | +0.33 |
| Growth | Neutral (+0.33) | Neutral (+0.17) | -0.17 |
| Management | Neutral (+0.20) | Neutral (-0.50) | -0.70 |
| Industry Structure | Good (+0.67) | Good (+1.17) | +0.50 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Good. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹11 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±1.4 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹11 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±5.8 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: IGL holds exclusive CGD authorisation in Delhi and 11 other GAs, making it the sole supplier of piped gas and CNG to 32.75 lakh households and 955 stations within those areas. | The long view had said: IGL is the default CNG provider in the customer's mind across Delhi NCR with 925-975 operating stations and ~20 lakh vehicles served, though the position is licence-granted rather than earned.
- **Distribution to shareholders** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: Consistent dividend policy (Rs 5.5 interim plus Rs 1.5 final on a doubled capital base after a 1:1 bonus) shows a steady payout habit, but there is no buyback and no valuation-linked distribution discipline described. | The long view had said: Regular dividends are paid (Rs 3 interim in FY23) and JV dividends received, but no buybacks and no articulated distribution policy or valuation discipline is discussed.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.4 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Entry barriers are high (licence exclusivity plus multi-thousand-km pipeline capex) and pricing is rational with no discount war evident — management even held retail prices steady rather than competing on price — though substitute fuels and EVs erode the protection. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±5.8 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Entry requires a PNGRB licence auction and enormous sunk pipeline capex, ownership is stable (GAIL/BPCL promoted), and pricing is administered and deliberately non-volatile rather than discount-driven.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: Sustained growth capex (~Rs 1,100+ cr in FY25) into 72 new CNG stations, 293 km steel and 3,834 km MDPE pipeline is clearly expansionary and new GAs are compounding at 16-32%, but incremental returns are unproven as EBITDA/SCM fell to Rs 5.4 despite the spend. | The long view had said: Capex is large and genuinely growth-oriented (new GAs, 45 CNG stations added in FY26, LNG stations) but incremental returns are poor — EBITDA/SCM fell from Rs 6.67 (Q3FY22) to Rs 5.4 (Q3FY26) while volumes grew only ~8.1 to ~9.4 mmscmd over three years.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Weak → Neutral. Now: The base is predominantly retail (CNG motorists and households) where IGL offers cost convenience rather than intangibles, while the corporate segment shows weakness — industrial demand was explicitly subdued in Q3. | The long view had said: The benefit match is weak on both sides: retail CNG buyers get no intangible premium and are highly price-sensitive, while industrial customers optimise pure TCO and defect to propane when LPG cheapens.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: The share to be taken comes from weaker incumbent fuels — petrol/diesel vehicles converting at 26,000/month, and in the Saudi opportunity entire industrial cities still running on liquid fuels and LPG. | The long view had said: There are no weak in-GA gas competitors to take share from; the substitution opportunity is from LPG/propane/diesel users, but that flow reverses when crude is soft and industrial customers switch back.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Neutral → Weak. Now: The five-year history shows a revolving door of nominee chairmen and MDs (six chairmen and one MD exiting since FY2021) and the calls show outcomes hinging on government gas allocation, PNGRB tariffs, state VAT and GST decisions rather than independent long-term action. | The long view had said: MD Chatiwal has articulated a consistent multi-year vision (100 LNG stations, queue-less CNG network) but the board has churned through six chairmen in five years and the company is dependent on PNGRB/government decisions rather than independent action.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: Management positions the offering around safety and reliability of supply, which matters for the 5,400 industrial and 32.75 lakh domestic connections, but the calls give no evidence customers pay a premium for that trust. | The long view had said: The core proposition to piped customers is uninterrupted, safe supply and management frames its mission around reliability, which industrial and domestic PNG users value above marginal price.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Neutral → Weak. Now: Alongside core network build, management committed to a 500 MW solar plant and a 40%-equity Saudi Arabian gas JV — capital moving away from the organic core into unrelated geographies and industries. | The long view had said: Leverage appears prudent and the focus is largely organic network build, but the unrelated 40% Saudi JV taken on while core unit margins compress cuts against pure organic discipline.
- **Candor** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Disclosure is detailed and honest about bad news — they volunteer ex-DTC growth, the Rs 28 cr labour provision and margin declines — but repeated Rs 7-8/SCM and 10% volume guidance has been missed (Rs 5.4/SCM, 3% volume) with the shortfall attributed to external factors each time. | The long view had said: Management discloses bad news directly — declining DTC volumes, missed EBITDA guidance, the EV threat — and corrects its own misstated figures on the call.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: IGL is already the licensed incumbent in its GAs so there is no rival to take share from; growth comes from new-GA penetration and fuel conversion, while its anchor Delhi volume is flat and DTC volumes are being lost to EVs. | The long view had said: IGL is already saturated in its core Delhi market (flat to 1% growth) but has genuine runway in newer GAs, which now contribute ~57% of incremental volume and grow 17%.

*Unchanged: Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Asset turn; Cost of working capital; Merger and acquisition; Security by obscurity; Halo effect (caution); Geographical expansion; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Intangible benefits; Convenience benefits; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Return on capital (headline); Technology advantage; Structural end-market growth; Cyclical market growth; Out of limelight; Distribution advantage.*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: EVs are a structurally different competitor with different economics entering IGL's core transport-fuel market, and management now positions CNG defensively against them and has held back price increases rather than pass through costs — 'we don't want to basically make the RSP of our CNG product volatile.' The bounded part is that the loss so far is confined to policy-driven public fleets, not private demand. (The long view had said: IGL's territorial economics are contestable: a rival state-nominated entity (Haryana City Gas) has been supplying part of Gurgaon pending a Supreme Court/PNGRB ruling on IGL's authorisation, and EV bus fleet operators represent a structurally different competitor entering the urban transport fuel market with government backing.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: IGL does not sell a branded premium that a cheaper equivalent can undercut — it is the low-cost option itself, priced against petrol/diesel/LPG, with exclusive distribution rights over its network and no intermediary standing between it and the end customer. Nothing in the four calls shows management defending a price premium or channel partners pushing a substitute. (The long view had said: Natural gas competes purely on delivered price with no brand protection, so industrial customers switch to cheaper substitutes whenever crude softens, and management repeatedly frames pricing decisions as defending competitiveness against neighbouring GAs and alternate fuels rather than earning a premium.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The calls affirmatively show the retail demand base expanding rather than eroding — monthly CNG vehicle conversions rose from 15,900 to 26,000 across the window, domestic PNG connections grew 8-12% and commercial 8-14%. The only volume decline is a policy-mandated public-fleet fleet switch, not a change in household or private-vehicle preference. (The long view had said: The migration of mobility toward electric — driven by green-policy and buyer preference, not the cycle — is eroding a slice of the demand base, most visibly in institutional buses and two-wheeler registrations, and management has repeatedly said conversion is happening 'in two wheelers only' at the margin.)

**That worsened:**

- **Fashion Risk**: NO SIGNAL → LOW. Why now: Demand is functional and repeat-purchase — vehicle fuel, household cooking and industrial process gas metered daily through a fixed pipeline network — with no product trend, brand moment or discretionary taste element cited anywhere in the four calls. (The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and all 2 computed fingerprints are clean.)

*Unchanged: Technological Innovation (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Cyclicality (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
