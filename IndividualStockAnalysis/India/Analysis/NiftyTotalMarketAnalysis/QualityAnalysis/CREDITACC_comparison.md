Now: Management pre-announced its own guidance misses in detail (70-100 bps FY26 credit-cost deviation, FY26 ending at 6.74% vs 5.5-6.0% guided), quantified each driver, and plainly labelled the period difficult rather than spinning it. | The long view had said: Management explicitly owned the FY26 guidance miss (6.74% credit cost vs 5.5-6.0% guided) and named the difficulty, though in Jul-2024 it repeatedly framed building stress as 'transitory' and reiterated guidance that later broke.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Attrition was systematically driven down from 35.2% to 20.6% through training, front-line leadership and proactive salary restructuring ahead of the new labour codes, and the CEO was promoted internally to MD. | The long view had said: Clear internal pipeline (Ganesh Narayanan CEO to MD, Nilesh Dalvi IR head to CFO) plus systematic training and sharply moderating attrition from 33.5% to 20.6%.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: The dazzling recent numbers (ROE 24.4%, PAT +720% year-on-year) are largely a cyclical rebound off a written-off base — 7.6% of the book was written off in FY26 — so results here overstate incremental skill and warrant discounting. | The long view had said: The assessment survives halo scrutiny because the FY25-26 downturn separated skill from luck — reported ROE fell to 10.7% yet the recovery playbook and cross-cycle ratios beat the COVID cycle, showing capability not just favourable conditions.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: No monopoly position in the borrower's mind — customers routinely hold multiple lender relationships and CAGL competes on being the lowest-cost lender rather than the only option. | The long view had said: Deep community trust and 6-8 year customer vintages give a privileged position in the borrower's mind, but microfinance customers routinely borrow from multiple lenders so there is no monopoly-like hold.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Good → Neutral. Now: Grameen Mahi is a competent, well-adopted customer app (34.5% of borrowers) with real-time audit analytics behind it, but it is table-stakes digital execution rather than a deep multi-product technology edge. | The long view had said: A genuine operating tech stack (business rule engine for district- and cohort-level credit policy, 30 Lakh transactions/day, AI in credit decisioning) supports multiple products, but it is an efficiency edge rather than a defensible multi-product technology moat.

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
