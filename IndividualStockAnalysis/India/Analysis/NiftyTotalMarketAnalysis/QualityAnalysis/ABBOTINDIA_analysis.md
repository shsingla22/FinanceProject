# Abbott India (ABBOTINDIA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Abbott India Limited (AIL) is the Indian arm of a global healthcare company (about 130 years old globally, 110 years in India) that sells branded prescription medicines, vaccines and a few over-the-counter products across India. It makes money by getting doctors to prescribe its brands, which patients then buy from chemists and retail chains, and it is pushing into public/government channels (like ESIC and immunization programmes) and online pharmacies. Management describes its approach as not just selling a pill but building whole treatment categories — running disease-awareness campaigns, funding diagnostic tests (25 lakh+ thyroid tests a year, Fibroscan liver scans) and patient-adherence programmes so more patients get diagnosed and stay on therapy. It is the only multinational among the top 10 companies in the roughly $20 billion Indian pharma market, grew its top line 14%+ last year (about 7.6% volume, rest price), and expects to cross $1 billion in sales by 2025.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Women's Health** — Sells hormone and pregnancy-related medicines led by the mega-brand Duphaston, plus newer launches Preservgest, Femoston (post-menopause) and Riligol (a heat-stable carbetocin for postpartum bleeding, marketed in partnership with Ferring); the portfolio grew over 23% and Duphaston went from being the only brand for 60 years to one of ~41 in two years — share lost in gynaecology but held in IVF, with the overall market roughly doubling.
- **Gastroenterology (GI)** — Called an extremely strong growth driver at ~27% growth, this is where Abbott says it is the market leader — brands include Udiliv (chronic liver disease), Duphalac and Cremaffin/Cremaffin Plus (constipation), Digene and its extensions (antacids, with Digeraft already No. 1 in its category), plus 'hidden gem' second-line brands Creon, Ganaton, Librax and Heptral; management is adding headcount and launching another business unit inside GI.
- **Metabolics** — Mainly hypothyroidism treatment through Thyronorm, where Abbott is market leader with roughly 53% share and is still gaining share against ~11 competitors, backed by the 'MITA — Making India Thyroid Aware' campaign and the widest SKU range (12.5 to 200 mg); the whole Thyronorm range up to 150 mg sits under NLEM price control, part of the ~26% of company sales that are price-controlled.
- **Vaccines** — A four-product business of about ₹200 crore, led by the flu vaccine Influvac (a ₹100 crore+ brand, always first to market with both Northern and Southern hemisphere strains), plus typhoid (Enteroshield), rotavirus (Rotasure) and the new Hepatitis A vaccine Havshield, which should cross $1 million in its first year; growth has been flat post-COVID due to jab fatigue and school closures, and management sees adult vaccination as the big future opportunity.
- **OTC / consumer health** — A 'robust' over-the-counter portfolio covering antacids, laxatives and a little pain relief, sold direct to consumers and increasingly promoted through digital and online channels rather than only doctor prescriptions.
- **Portfolio structure (how management buckets the business)** — Nine to ten 'mega brands' of $15 million+ each, a 'next 10' brands in the $5–15 million range, and new product introductions — the top 20 brands contribute roughly 90–93% of the business; 35 products were launched in the last three years and about 75 more are planned over the next five, helped by 500+ patent-expiry (loss-of-exclusivity) opportunities in branded generics.
- **Geography and channels** — About 70% of sales come from metros and class-one towns, with a stated push into class two, class three and 'extra urban' markets (the Udaan initiative), alongside new routes to market through government/public-health tenders, retail chains and online pharmacies, and a digital sales model (iPad-based doctor detailing, hybrid CMEs, the a:care patient app).

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*
