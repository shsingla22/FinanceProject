**Checks that improved:**

- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹17 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹17 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±5.2 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: The Scorpene installed base creates a captive aftermarket with high switching costs — AIP plug retrofits and prospective refits of the entire programme flow back to MDL as the builder. | The long view had said: Switching costs are real — medium refits and life certification of the very boats MDL built come back to it (a submarine was physically handed over before the formal order existed), and P-75 add-ons can start revenue immediately because they are follow-ons of its own design.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Rivals disqualified themselves on the largest tender in the window, leaving MDL as the sole technically compliant bidder on P-75(I). | The long view had said: No weak domestic competitor is identified to take share from — MDL instead partners with a private yard (Swan) rather than displacing it — and the one distressed asset in view (Colombo Dockyard) was bought, not out-competed.
- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Neutral. Now: No acquisitions in the window — expansion into large platforms is via an exclusive MoU/partnership with Swan Shipyard rather than a bolt-on purchase, so there is no acquiree quality or post-deal OPM to judge. | The long view had said: The one acquisition, Colombo Dockyard, is a cross-border commercial-shipbuilding yard that is loss-making rather than already strong, still not closed at Nov 2025, and requires MDL management to be 'pitched in' for control — the opposite of a simple bolt-on with immediate OPM accretion.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Weak → Neutral. Now: MDL has genuine integration and build capability but the core submarine technology is licensed from foreign designers, with the P-75(I) bid dependent on a TKMS collaboration. | The long view had said: Core technology is licensed rather than owned — Scorpene from the French platform, P-75I bid dependent on TKMS Germany, and for the LPD the technology partner is simply undecided pending the RFP; MDL's edge is integration and project management, not proprietary tech.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Entry barriers are high (only MDL cleared P-75(I) technically), but competitive tendering has produced irrational bids — two contracts booked as onerous — and private yards such as Swan are entering the large-platform space. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±5.2 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Entry barriers are very high (250-year yard, dock infrastructure, security clearance, submarine know-how) and ownership is stable, but pricing rationality is imperfect — MDL's own competitive bids on Coast Guard FPVs and the Denmark order proved loss-making.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: MDL is winning the biggest available programmes — nominated for the follow-on Scorpenes and the only technically qualified bidder on P-75(I) — but it is already the dominant submarine yard, limiting further share runway. | The long view had said: MDL is already the dominant incumbent in its niches with little runway to gain share, and future work is moving to competitive bidding against L&T/Navantia and private yards rather than nomination, so no clear share-capture trajectory is evidenced.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Neutral → Weak. Now: As a Government undertaking, management has little independence — order timing sits entirely with MoD, repeatedly slipping past its own confident timelines, and the CMD changed mid-window. | The long view had said: Operationally tenacious — five Scorpenes delivered 2017-22 and destroyers handed over up to five months early — but strategic independence is structurally absent, with order timing, subsidiary listing and even sanction of signed-out negotiations resting with MoD/DIPAM.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Weak. Now: Physical yard capacity is the analogue of distribution here and MDL lacks it for large platforms, having to tie up with Swan Shipyard's infrastructure to bid for the ~200m Landing Platform Docks. | The long view had said: Dock infrastructure is a real physical barrier (11 submarines and 10 warships simultaneously) and repairs are a genuine service vertical, but the reach is not unmatched — MDL must lean on Swan Shipyard's larger infrastructure for the 200-metre LPD and can only take large ship repairs once its own docks free up.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Growth is organic with no large M&A and expansion via partnership, but INR 1,000 crores of onerous-contract provisions on the Coast Guard FPV and Denmark orders points to weak bid discipline. | The long view had said: Consistently zero-debt with a Rs 4,000 cr capex funded internally and no plan to lever up, and growth is essentially organic; only the Colombo Dockyard purchase departs from the organic-only discipline, and it is small.

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 3 worsened, 2 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Revenue is tied to the customer's capex programmes (naval ship/submarine construction) rather than operations, and the calls show exactly the promised volatility: EBITDA margin swung from 35.1% in Dec-24 to 13.74% in Mar-25 on INR 1,000 crore of onerous-contract provisions, while P-15B/Scorpene deliveries wound down leaving an admitted lull of 'a few months where we'll have only the Coast Guard and the MPV project'. Guidance oscillated between 12-15% PBT, 'significantly improve', and back to ~15%. (The long view had said: Revenue is pure customer-capex: it rides the Navy's acquisition programmes, recognised on an S-curve, so results swing violently — EBITDA margin 35.1% in Dec-24 quarter versus 13.74% in Mar-25 quarter, PBT margin guidance cut from a reported 26-27% back to ~15%, and the order book ran down from ~INR38,500cr (2023) to INR27,415cr (Nov 2025) as P-15B and P-17A completed, with growth guidance falling from 20%+ to 8-10% and then ~5%. Management also flagged that the global shipbuilding boom inflated equipment prices after bids were fixed, forcing ~INR1,000cr of onerous-contract provisions.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Effectively a single-customer business: the Indian Navy (plus Coast Guard) drives revenue, order book and margins, and management openly concedes it has no firm commitment on the next big programmes ('17 Bravo is also a possibility, but it is not yet crystallized'). The order book fell from ~INR 32,000 crore to INR 27,415 crore by Q2FY26 while waiting on that one customer; submarine capability also depends on single foreign design partners (Scorpene/TKMS). (The long view had said: Effectively one customer, the Indian Navy (plus Coast Guard), determines revenue, margins, delivery acceptance and even cash on the balance sheet ('substantial amount of this cash... belongs to Navy against the project'). Technology on the flagship bid is also single-sourced: MDL is contractually locked to TKMS Germany via a 'mutually exclusive agreement' for P-75I, so losing that one tender to L&T/Navantia would remove the largest pipeline item entirely.)
- **New Entrants**: NO SIGNAL → WATCH. Why now: Large private yards are now credible bidders in defence shipbuilding — the Landing Platform Dock opportunity required MDL to sign an exclusive MoU with a private yard for infrastructure it lacks, and management points analysts to 'other shipyards who are listed' as the margin benchmark. Exposure is bounded by MDL's submarine monopoly position (sole technically-qualified bidder on P-75(I)). (The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and all 2 computed fingerprints are clean.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL); Fashion Risk (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
