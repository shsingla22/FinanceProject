### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 4 weakened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Realisation rose from INR4,569 to INR4,732 per ton in FY26 (+3.6%) with premium product share climbing 15% to 21% year-on-year, but the price was bought with volume sacrifice (FY26 volume +2.2% versus industry mid-to-high single digits), which is a pricing discipline trade-off rather than true pricing power; management also concedes the industry has no entry barriers and prices moved down sequentially in Dec '25. (The long view had said: The record shows price deflation, not pricing power: realisation fell from INR4,854 (Dec'22) to INR4,469 (Q1FY25), the company sold at a INR30/bag discount to the leader, and Bhandari states plainly that price is set by demand and refuses to guide on it; the FY26 3.6% realisation gain was bought by giving up volume growth (2.2% for the year).)

**That weakened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Cost obsession is itemised and genuine in the Feb 2026 and May 2025 calls — lowest kilocalorie fuel cost in the industry at 1.56, ~61-63% green electricity (highest in Indian cement), waste heat recovery at Kodla — but the low-price/high-volume half of the pattern is explicitly rejected in favour of restraining volumes for price, with utilisation in the mid-50s. (The long view had said: Cost leadership is the consistent thread across all four years: Akhoury's FY23 pledge to "remain the cost leader" is backed in FY26 by the lowest fuel cost per kcal in the industry (1.56 vs peer 1.80), 61% green power, multi-fuel burners chosen on landed cost, and the claim of the peer group's highest EBITDA per ton for FY26.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: the calls are silent and no computable check supports the pattern. (The long view had said: the calls are silent and the numbers are mixed (1 of 3 checks supporting).)
- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The company is deliberately ceding share: FY26 volume grew just 2.2% (35.6 to 36.4 million tons) against industry growth guided at 6.5-7.5%, with utilisation in the mid-50s and analysts on three of four calls pressing on volumes lagging peers. (The long view had said: Shree gained share convincingly through FY23-FY25 (8% volume growth in Q1FY25 while Ramco, Dalmia and UltraTech shrank), but from Oct'24 management deliberately handed share back to chase price, closing FY26 at just 2.2% volume growth and guiding only to industry-plus-1%.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Management itself states the industry has no entry barriers, and the only durable-looking edges cited — fuel procurement cost and a 666.5 MW green power portfolio — are advantages a well-funded entrant could rebuild with capital; the price gap to the leader is narrowed by restraint, not by anything a newcomer could not replicate. (The long view had said: Some genuinely hard-to-copy assets exist — limestone reserves secured to 80 mt, 1 GW of captive power with 61% renewables, and 40 years of lowest-cost fuel procurement — but the calls also show the advantage is contestable: capacity is added freely by rivals, utilisation sat at 56% in Dec'25, and Shree still had to discount to the market leader.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Three of four calls anchor the demand narrative in policy — Q4FY25 opens with demand "driven by increase in government capex", Q2FY26 is dominated by the GST cut from 28% to 18% which the company passed through in full, and Q4FY26 credits the Union Budget's public capex thrust; limestone mining leases and state power/transmission arrangements (the Guntur substation gift deed write-off) add further policy touchpoints. Pricing itself is market-set, not regulated, which bounds the exposure. (The long view had said: Management explicitly ties quarterly demand to the Union Budget cycle, tender flow and government housing/road schemes, and the business rests on state-granted limestone leases and 'statutory approvals' for new sites; in Aug'24 they attributed weak Q2/Q3 demand purely to the budget not yet having received Presidential assent, and in Feb'26 credited the Q4 pickup to central agencies having to exhaust the budget by 31st March.)
- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Management states plainly that the industry has no entry barriers, and the whole value-over-volume strategy is a defensive response to competitive pricing — deliberately sacrificing volume (FY26 growth just 2.2%) to narrow the price gap to UltraTech from INR30 to INR15 a bag while sitting on mid-50s utilisation amid industry capacity overhang. (The long view had said: The threat discussed is not a new format but recapitalised large incumbents: management blamed the North volume decline on 'two very powerful competitors' (UltraTech and Ambuja/Adani) and from Oct'24 deliberately sacrificed volume growth (FY26 volumes +2.2% vs industry ~7%) to close a INR30/bag price gap — margin/price investment framed squarely as a competitive response.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Cement is a substitutable, technically simple product bought through dealers and masons who influence the choice, and management's own framing shows how contestable the premium is — it spent five quarters restricting volumes purely to narrow a INR30/bag price gap to the market leader, and is layering branded premium SKUs onto a commodity in a no-entry-barrier industry. (The long view had said: Cement is a low-differentiation product where the dealer/mason often steers the choice, and Shree sat at a INR30/bag discount to UltraTech — it spent five quarters deliberately forgoing volume just to halve that gap, while simultaneously spending heavily on brand (new identity, Bangur MAGNA, 'Solid Ghar Sirf Bangur') to defend a premium that the market only partly grants.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Cement is a construction commodity whose demand management ties to government capex, monsoons, festivals and labour availability, and the calls show volumes/EBITDA swinging violently within four quarters (7.9mt → 8.7mt → 10.56mt; EBITDA/ton INR1,406 → 1,105 → 1,032 → 1,125), while the industry-wide capacity overhang forces an explicit price-vs-volume trade-off; Shree is simultaneously adding capacity (62.8mt → 69.3mt, target 80mt) at mid-50s to 66% utilisation — the classic expansionary over-investment signature. (The long view had said: Cement is a classic capacity/price cycle business and the calls show it repeatedly: EBITDA/ton swung from INR1,260 (Dec'21) to INR881 (Dec'22) to INR950 (Q1FY25) to INR1,125 (Q4FY26), realizations fell 6% Y-o-Y in Q1FY25, utilisation ranged from 56% to 76%, and management admits costs are hostage to international pet coke/coal prices while ~INR2,900-3,500 crores/year of capex was committed into a market whose demand later disappointed (80mt by FY29 now openly at risk of deferral).)

*Unchanged: Technological Innovation (LOW); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
