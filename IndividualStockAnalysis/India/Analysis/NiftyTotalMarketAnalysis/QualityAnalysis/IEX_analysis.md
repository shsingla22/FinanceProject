# Indian Energy Exchange Ltd. (IEX) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

IEX runs an online marketplace in India where electricity is bought and sold — sellers (power generators, and more recently battery-storage operators) post offers, buyers (state distribution companies and large industrial/commercial users) post bids, and the exchange's auction matches them and sets a clearing price, such as the Rs 7.7 per unit average in the day-ahead market in the June 2022 quarter. It is a platform, not a power producer: it does not own generation, and its business is measured almost entirely by the volume of electricity traded across it, quoted in billion units (BU) — 23.4 BU in the quarter to June 2022, growing about 21% year-on-year in the June 2024 quarter. Alongside actual electricity it also runs a market in renewable energy certificates (RECs), tradable green credits, and management says trading of carbon credit certificates on power exchanges is being set up. It operates only in India, under rules set by the central regulator CERC and the Ministry of Power, and has been in business about 18 years as of 2026; its main stated risk is 'market coupling', a regulator-driven change that would pool day-ahead bids across exchanges via Grid India.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Day-Ahead Market (DAM)** — A once-a-day auction for electricity to be delivered the following day — historically the core of the business (about 95% of total volume six to seven years before 2026, and 11.3 BU of 23.4 BU in the June 2022 quarter), and the one segment the regulator intends to 'couple' across exchanges.
- **Real-Time Market (RTM)** — Auctions held through the day for near-immediate delivery, used to balance last-minute shortfalls; 6.2 BU in the June 2022 quarter and growing fast (up 34.5% year-on-year), with CERC proposing to cut the bidding window from 15 minutes to 5 to help manage renewables.
- **Term-Ahead Market (TAM) / longer-duration contracts** — Contracts for delivery further out — daily and monthly contracts up to 90 days, launched June 2022 after CERC approval — letting distribution companies lock in supply for longer; 3.1 BU in the June 2022 quarter, more than double a year earlier, and the fastest-growing conventional segment at the time.
- **Green Market (green day-ahead and term-ahead)** — The same kind of trading but specifically for renewable power, serving open-access consumers going green and renewable generators; a small piece at 1.5 BU in the June 2022 quarter, but management ties its growth to open-access rules, virtual PPAs and renewable consumption obligations.
- **Renewable Energy Certificates (REC) market** — Trading of certificates that prove renewable generation, bought by companies who must meet green-power obligations; 11.97 lakh certificates in the June 2022 quarter, which management equates to roughly 1.2 BU of electricity.
- **Carbon credit certificates (in development)** — A planned market in carbon credits for heavy-emitting sectors such as cement, steel and petrochemicals, with trading procedures still being drafted by BEE and Grid India as of April 2026 — described as a foundation laid, not yet a revenue line.
- **Technology and customer platform** — Not a separate revenue segment but repeatedly flagged as what keeps customers loyal — web-based bidding, online financial reconciliation, API links for automated bidding and back-office, and data analytics, which management argues will preserve its market share even if market coupling arrives.
- **Indian Gas Exchange (IGX)** — A gas trading exchange whose Chief Business Officer presents alongside IEX management at the analyst meet; the excerpts show it as part of the group's line-up but give no volume or financial detail.

### The story in depth

The core of this business is a very small toll on a very large flow, and almost every strength in the record traces back to that one design. IEX charges roughly 2 paise per unit on power that clears at Rs 3.6 to 7.7 per unit — well under 1% of what the customer pays for the electricity itself, but a step they cannot skip if they want to buy or sell in this market. That is why the Toll Roads pattern is the only outright strong fit. The economics that follow are unusually good: Rs 84 of every Rs 100 of sales is operating profit, with margins moving only about 2.4 points a year; the business turns Rs 31 of operating cash for every Rs 100 of capital employed; and only about one day of cash sits between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, helped by members pre-funding their trades with advance and margin money. Growth spending is not really factory spending — it is platform and technology build-out plus new exchanges (IGX, ICX, the Indian Coal Exchange) paid for out of internal cash. The market underneath is genuinely expanding: renewables crossed half of installed capacity four years early, demand is projected toward 2,700 units-billion by FY32, and the share of national electricity consumption traded on exchanges has roughly doubled from about 4-5% to over 8%. Leadership has been stable across all 18 calls, the balance sheet carries no debt, and dividends keep coming (Rs 2 final in FY26).

The awkward part is that the same features that create the strengths also create the risks, so this is not a case of good news and bad news sitting side by side — they are the same news read two ways. The fee is tiny and essential, which is what makes it toll-like; but the fee is set by the regulator, not by IEX, and the company has had to justify the 2 paise charge to CERC rather than lift it. That is why Pricing Power is scored as no fit at all: across FY26 volumes rose 17% while consolidated revenue rose only 13.6%, and in the fourth quarter volumes rose 24.3% against revenue up 12.5% — the company is being paid slightly less per unit as it grows. The same regulatory frame that licenses every new product also makes Government Dependency the single high risk in the review, and diversification does not really escape it, because IGX, ICX, carbon and the coal exchange are each themselves created and licensed by ministries and regulators. Likewise, the deep pool of buyers and sellers is a real barrier — PXIL and HPX both arrived with utility backing and promotional pricing and failed to dent the 80-85% share over four years, which is what earns Cost to Replicate a partial fit and keeps New Entrants only at watch level. But the barrier is described in the records as regulator-granted and regulator-removable, and market coupling would move the matching and price-discovery engine itself to Grid India, pushing exchanges toward being order-collection points. That is the sharpest single thread in the file: the moat and the threat share a source. And because the product is simple — order matching for fractions of a paisa — rivals attacked exactly where price is all that differs (the term-ahead and REC segments, where all exchanges are described as at par), which is both why Low Price Plus fits only partly and why Good Enough Goods sits on the watch list.

Put together, the scoring lands at 66 out of 100 — 45% weight on business quality at 69, 30% on multibagger fit at 62, 25% on risk safety at 64 — a Strong, four-star reading with resilience judged sound. The gap between the excellent returns and the merely strong overall score is explained by two soft spots in the quality work: Growth and Industry Structure both score around 0.17 against a Return on Capital area score of 1.33 and full marks on competitive advantage and customer benefits. Sales have compounded at roughly 13% a year, which sits inside the steady band the framework likes, but it arrives lumpily — the FY23 coal crisis cut day-ahead volumes 21% with clearing prices at Rs 7.7 per unit, while FY26 saw flat national consumption on a good monsoon, which is why Cyclicality is a watch rather than a clean pass. The pattern screen shows the same split picture: the company is capturing a market that is growing (exchange share of consumption doubling), yet its own share within the exchange market has been flat to slightly down, from 85% in Q1 FY23 to a 80-85% range — so Market Share Gainers is only a likely fit, riding the pool getting bigger rather than the slice getting wider. Innovation is real and revenue-bearing (real-time market reached 39% of volumes and 55 units-billion in FY26, alongside green and peak products, longer-duration contracts and EnergyX) but research spending is never disclosed, so Innovation Dominance stays partial. Two patterns — Friendly Middleman and Forward Integrators — are flagged only as number-driven hints with no supporting evidence from the calls, and should be treated as screening noise, not findings. One more point worth holding onto: the framework actively penalises how visible this company is, scoring it minus two on obscurity, because a listed near-monopoly whose fees are repeatedly challenged and whose market is being redesigned by regulation attracts attention from regulators, competitors and analysts alike. On the reassuring side, the customer base is broad and broadening — 9,000-plus registered participants, every distribution utility, every merchant renewable generator, plus a new class of battery storage sellers — so no single counterparty carries the business, and the industry's drift away from long-term contracts toward merchant and short-term buying (with about 80% of commissioned battery capacity traded merchant) runs in IEX's favour.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Across all 18 calls the business is defined by regulator action: CERC sets/reviews the transaction fee (a 2022 petition to justify the Rs 0.02 fee), every new product (11-month TAM, green RTM, peak DAM/RTM, capacity market, coal exchange) requires CERC/Ministry approval, and volumes are manufactured by policy (LPSC rules forcing un-requisitioned power onto exchanges, Section 11 directives, VGF/BESS tenders mandating exchange sale). The July 2025 CERC market-coupling order — handing day-ahead price discovery to Grid India, with IEX's APTEL appeal dismissed in Feb 2026 and draft regulations out in April 2026 — shows a single policy change can reset the core franchise.

> "CERC issued an order on implementing market coupling on 23rd July 2025... IEX had filed an appeal in the APTEL... APTEL mentioned that as market coupling cannot be implemented without regulations, IEX is not an aggrieved party at this stage." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Diversification into IGX, ICX/I-RECs, carbon and coal exchange — but each of these is itself created and licensed by ministries/regulators, so it does not reduce policy dependence.*

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

Revenue is a per-unit fee, so price collapse (DAM down 14% in FY26) does not hurt directly, and PAT has still compounded through the cycle.

> "electricity consumption for FY26 remained almost flat... this year, right from the month of April, the summer has been very harsh" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Fee-per-unit, operations-linked flow demand rather than customer capex; volumes grew 17% even in a flat-demand year, and structural policy tailwinds (URS power, BESS merchant arbitrage) add liquidity independent of the cycle.*

## What to watch

- Market coupling: whether the matching engine actually moves to Grid India, which is the Technological Innovation watch risk and would directly undercut the Cost to Replicate barrier.
- Fee per unit: the gap between volume growth and revenue growth (17% vs 13.6% in FY26) widening further, confirming the Pricing Power no-fit and any fresh CERC review of the 2 paise charge.
- Share within the exchange market: whether the 80-85% band holds against PXIL and HPX, since a break would downgrade both Market Share Gainers and the New Entrants watch risk.
- Any new CERC or ministry decision on product licences or the transaction fee — the Government Dependency high risk, which also gates every diversification effort (IGX, ICX, coal, carbon).
- Growth steadiness: whether the roughly 13% average holds through the next fuel or weather swing, given the Persistence of Growth check and the FY23 volume drop under the Cyclicality risk.
