### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 5. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: All four calls pair aggressive accessible pricing (₹69 McSaver's, ₹99 everyday value meal) with an explicit cost-governance culture and supply-chain efficiency programme that lifted like-for-like FY26 gross margin ~140bps to 67.7% and ROM ~100bps to 20.3% — low price funded by cost, not by giving up returns. (The long view had said: The FY25–FY26 strategy is explicitly accessible everyday value (₹99 meal, ₹69 1+1) combined with McDonald's quality/consistency, and management shows a genuine cost culture — supply chain efficiencies and an internal cost project lifted FY26 like-for-like gross margin 140bps to 67.7% while value was being deployed. What is missing is the high-asset-turn/strong-return proof: operating EBITDA is stuck at 13.2%, well below the 19–20% Vision 2027 promise.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Explicit share-gain claim in a shrinking category in Nov 2025, followed by improving guest counts through Q3-Q4 FY26 including a turnaround in the previously lagging South, plus a record 48 store openings in FY26 with 60+ per year planned — share bought with footfall, not with margin (ROM up 100bps). (The long view had said: FY23 calls claimed sustained QSR traffic share gains, leadership in West and closing on South, and store count has compounded (331→478); but the middle years show negative SSSG, a persistently underperforming South cohort, and admitted under-growth on third-party delivery platforms — share numbers are asserted qualitatively rather than quantified over time.)
- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Consistently candid disclosure across the four calls — reporting -3% SSSG, refusing twice to call a demand revival, flagging the 400-500bps optical gross-margin regrouping and the exceptional impairment, and announcing the CFO exit openly — alongside promises kept (Vision 2027 store pipeline on track, margin expansion delivered every quarter) under stable family leadership; the one blemish is three CFOs in five years. (The long view had said: Strong marks on candour and stability — Jatia family ownership with continuity into Akshay Jatia as CEO, unusually detailed unpicking of accounting optics (MTM reversals, reimaging write-offs, the FY26 processing-charge regrouping), explicit admissions ('we are absolutely not satisfied'), refusal to normalise away Navratri/LPG disruptions, and store-opening promises repeatedly delivered (45–50 guided, 48 opened, now 60+). Against that, the Vision 2027 19–20% EBITDA promise made in 2024 sits far from FY26's 13.2%.)

**That weakened:**

- **Forward Integrators**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Westlife operates 100% company-owned restaurants (478 across 78 cities) and is investing heavily to shift delivery off third-party aggregators onto its own McDelivery platform with a global consultant, 20-minute delivery and UI/UX rebuild — direct control of customer and economics, though it is a franchisee rather than a manufacturer integrating forward. (The long view had said: Westlife owns and operates its entire estate — 331 restaurants in Aug 2022 to 478 across 78 cities by Mar 2026, with drive-thrus at 24% and all eligible stores EOTF/McCafé — and has pushed hard into D2C: digital 55% of sales in FY23 rising to 76% by FY26 via own app, kiosks and its own McDelivery platform, deliberately reducing reliance on third-party aggregators.)
- **Pricing Power**: LIKELY FIT → NO FIT. Why now: The last 12 months show the opposite of pricing power: growth is explicitly guest-count-led with average check flat or lower, SSSG was -3% in Q3 FY26, and menu prices were adjusted down post-GST while the strategy centres on everyday affordability rather than price increases. (The long view had said: There is a steady formulaic pass-through — a 5% blended hike in Q1FY23 and a stated 2–4% annual policy — but it is inflation-recovery, not pricing above cost inflation without volume loss: GST benefits were fully passed to consumers, no price rise was taken for four to five months to May 2026, and the recent growth is deliberately volume-led rather than value-led.)

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 6 unchanged. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Revenue is entirely consumer-paid with no regulator, tariff, subsidy or PSU tender determining pricing; government touchpoints in the window are limited to the GST rate change (passed through via menu price adjustment) and monitoring the LPG supply situation, both ordinary input/tax items affecting the whole industry. (The long view had said: Revenue is not regulator-set, but policy and state-controlled inputs repeatedly touch results: the GST rate change forced menu price adjustments with benefits fully passed through (no retained margin), and an LPG supply disruption from 10 March 2026 left ~10% of restaurants on a limited menu, denting Q4FY26 SSSG.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The entire business rests on a single licensor relationship — the McDonald's master franchise for West and South India — and on the delivery side management repeatedly flagged dependence on third-party aggregators, which contribute close to 40% of business and whose volatility directly caused off-premise to decline in Q3 FY26. (The long view had said: The entire business is a single master-franchise licence from McDonald's Corporation, with royalty stepping 4%→4.5%→5% and open-ended renegotiation from FY27 that management flags will rise 'progressively' — a Safilo-style licensor dependency where the counterparty sets a rising claim on margin; secondarily, off-premises sales depend on two third-party aggregators, one of which visibly cut volumes.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Fashion Risk (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 1 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
