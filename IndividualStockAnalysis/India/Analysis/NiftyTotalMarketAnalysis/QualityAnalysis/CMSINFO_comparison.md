## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 31 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 5 improved, 3 regressed, 23 unchanged. 2 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them). 1 was newly assessable in the recent view.

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Allocation | Excellent (+1.80) | Excellent (+1.50) | -0.30 |
| Return on Capital | Neutral (+0.00) | Good (+0.67) | +0.67 |
| Growth | Good (+1.17) | Good (+0.83) | -0.33 |
| Management | Excellent (+1.50) | Excellent (+1.50) | +0.00 |
| Industry Structure | Good (+0.67) | Good (+1.33) | +0.67 |
| Customer Benefits | Good (+1.00) | Good (+1.25) | +0.25 |
| Competitive Advantage | Good (+1.00) | Good (+0.67) | -0.33 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.4 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Capital scarcity is blocking new entrants and pricing rationality is visibly returning — a 4,500-ATM tender bid near interchange levels and the transaction-fee model declared dead — after a genuine discount war in FY24-25. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±4.6 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Entry barriers are high (RBI norms, capital, 25-year networks — no new entrants cited) but pricing rationality broke down badly in FY24-25 with sub-economic transaction-fee bidding that CMS had to sit out; rationality is only now returning via the fixed-fee shift and consolidation.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹24 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±1.4 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹24 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±4.6 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: The proposition is certainty and trust in cash handling and security — management frames the business as trust-dependent, banks pay for premium delivery, and CMS won on sole qualification rather than price. | The long view had said: Customers are buying certainty — cash custody risk, insurance cover, CERT-IN and ISO certification, 25 years of relationships with almost every bank — but price remains decisive enough in RFPs that trust alone does not command the price.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Cash logistics and ATM servicing is an unglamorous, capital-shy niche that competitors are actively exiting rather than entering, though CMS itself is listed and analyst-covered. | The long view had said: The business is an unglamorous, capital-intensive cash-logistics niche, but it is a listed midcap with active sell-side coverage (PhillipCapital, JM, Nuvama, Emkay, IIFL) hosting quarterly calls, so it is hardly ignored by capital.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: CMS holds 58-60% of the ATM cash market and was the only qualified bidder on a 10,000-ATM PSU bank RFP — a monopoly-like position in its customers' minds. | The long view had said: Leadership positions exist in the customer's mind in specific niches — 36% pole share in BFSI remote monitoring, market leadership across all three platforms, sole eligible bidder on the SBI 10,000-ATM RFP — but banks still run competitive price-discovery RFPs, so it falls short of a true mini-monopoly.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Real share capture this year — cash logistics +200bps and managed services from #5 to #3 — but at 58-60% ATM cash share the runway in the core is limited. | The long view had said: Share was captured even in the worst operating year — 200bps gain in cash logistics, a move from #5 to #3 in managed services, 36% pole share in BFSI RMS, and touchpoints grown from 115,000 in FY23 to 140,000+ — with a stated Rs20,000cr TAM leaving ample runway.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Expansion is domestic-only, extending the gig model into tier 3/4 India; there is no international footprint or travelling premium brand. | The long view had said: Expansion is domestic-only (16,000+ pin codes, tier-3/tier-4 push via a 2,000-partner gig model) and is being executed credibly, but there is no international footprint and no evidence of a premium brand that travels.
- **Network effects** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Weak. Now: No two-sided network exists; the advantage is route density and scale economics, and the calls describe no self-reinforcing effect that strengthens with each added user. | The long view had said: Partnerships with fintechs and payment banks create an ecosystem, but there is no evidence of a self-reinforcing two-sided network — CMS explicitly is not the payments/settlement layer and each customer is won contract by contract.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 6 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 7. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 0 weakened, 9 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Management repeatedly frames the business as annuity: FY26 services revenue of Rs.2,312cr (93% of total) on multi-year contracts (SBI cash outsourcing worth Rs.1,000cr over 10 years), with installed-base metrics tracked every quarter — 153,000 retail touch points, 74-75k ATMs, HAWKAI sites from 30k to 50k+ heading to 80k by FY30. (The long view had said: Annuity/services revenue is the core metric management tracks (Rs 2,312cr of Rs 2,487cr in FY26, ~93%), with SaaS-like RMS/Hawkai ARR scaling 0→Rs 200cr and long-term contracts (SBI Rs 1,000cr over 10 years); but the recurring stream is capital-hungry (Rs 200-350cr annual capex) and customer-funded working capital is absent — FY26 saw DSO stress, ECL provisions and negative 40% OCF/EBITDA in H1, the opposite of billed-in-advance economics.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (LIKELY FIT).*

*Unchanged: Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

Offset by a largely annuity, operations-linked cash-logistics base (cash pickups/replenishment are flow activities, not customer capex) and explicit countercyclical capital preservation then re-investment.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
