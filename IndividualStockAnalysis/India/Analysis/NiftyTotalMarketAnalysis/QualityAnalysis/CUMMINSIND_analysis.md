# Cummins India Ltd. (CUMMINSIND) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: INDUSTRIAL MANUFACTURING*

Cummins India Limited (Pune-based, listed as CUMMINSIND) makes and sells diesel and gas engines, generator sets ('gensets') and the parts and service that keep them running. Its customers are Indian buyers of backup and prime power — data centres, infrastructure and construction projects, realty and residential, rental operators, pharma and biotech — plus equipment makers in rail, mining, marine, compressors and defence, and overseas distributors and OEMs. It earns money three ways: selling new engines/gensets in India, selling spare parts and after-sales service through its Distribution arm, and exporting engines (split into 'high horsepower' and 'low horsepower') to markets like the Middle East, Africa and Europe. In FY2025-26 it reported sales of ₹11,950 crore, up 18%, of which ₹9,961 crore was domestic and ₹1,989 crore exports, with profit before tax (before exceptional items) of ₹3,104 crore; it sources some products from Cummins group related parties (e.g. the QSK60 built by CTIPL, the QSK95 imported) while saying most content is localised in India.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Power Generation (domestic)** — Sells generator sets in India across low horsepower, mid-range, heavy duty and high horsepower nodes, and is the biggest single line at ₹4,758 crore in FY26 (up 24%); management says data centres alone were 30-35% of this business in FY26 and that the whole line has just moved from CPCB-II to the tighter CPCB-IV+ emission norms, which added after-treatment content and helped pricing.
- **Distribution** — The after-sales arm selling spare parts, service and support on the installed base of engines — including servicing GAIL's hydrogen installation, described as the country's largest working hydrogen setup — and the steadiest grower at ₹3,278 crore in FY26 (up 22%).
- **Industrial (domestic)** — Supplies engines to Indian equipment makers and end users in construction, rail, mining, compressors and marine; the smallest domestic line at ₹1,650 crore in FY26 and roughly flat (down 1%) after a very strong prior run, with management tying its fortunes to Indian infrastructure spending.
- **High Horsepower exports** — Exports of the larger engines, ₹984 crore in FY26 (up 20%), sold mainly into Middle East, Africa and European markets and described as volatile because of geopolitics and tariffs.
- **Low Horsepower exports** — Exports of smaller engines, ₹807 crore in FY26 (up 3%), the weakest-growing line, having gone through several quarters of decline before bottoming out and partially recovering.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

The heart of this business is that customers buy certainty, not equipment. The genset is a small share of a project's capital cost but the whole project depends on it — data centre buyers want 99.99% uptime — so when the CPCB-IV+ emission rules forced prices up 15-20%, demand did not fall away. That single trait is what earns the two strongest pattern verdicts, Toll Roads and Pricing Power, and it shows up in the numbers: growth led by price and a richer product mix, a five-year-high gross margin of about 37.8%, roughly INR21 of operating profit per INR100 of sales with margins moving only about plus or minus 2.9 points a year, INR1.07 of sales from every INR1 of assets, and a 20% cash return on capital. Customer Benefits and Return on Capital both score full marks. The same trait, though, is exactly what the risk review keeps poking at. Good Enough Goods is on watch precisely because management has to keep defending that price — it warns that intensifying competitive pricing will pressure the record material margin, and when challenged on Perkins, Baudouin and Adani winning large data centre orders on imported engines it changed the subject from price to the total proposition. Government Dependency is a second double edge: the emission rules that handed the company its price step-up also set specification, transition timing and pre-buy economics, so the regulator is both the source of the pricing win and a variable nobody at the company controls.

The second thread is the parts and service arm, and it is doing double duty. It is the standout structural asset in the quality work — INR3,278 crore in FY26, growing 22-26%, servicing even projects the parent executed, with a dedicated data centre aftermarket team — and it is what pushes the Forward Integrators and Friendly Middleman patterns to likely fits and Recurring Revenue to a partial one, since it is disclosed every quarter and has grown from INR416 crore in Q1FY23 to INR939 crore in Q3FY26 while consistently outgrowing equipment sales. It is also, word for word, the silver lining the risk review leans on for Cyclicality, which is the one high risk in the whole review. That matters, because the cyclical evidence is not thin: compressors were called highly cyclical in 2022 and guided into a down cycle again in June 2026, construction is tied to road-building pace, mining moves with tender cycles, and exports have been weak throughout. So the same flow business is simultaneously the best growth engine and the only real shock absorber — if its growth rate slows, two separate parts of this story weaken at once. The patterns stop short of full marks here for an honest reason: there are no own stores, franchising or direct-to-customer numbers, and no installer certification or trade programmes, so this is closeness to the customer rather than genuine ownership of the channel.

What holds the score back is everything upstream of the Indian operation and everything beyond the domestic market. The company depends on its parent for the largest engines — the QSK95 is imported, the QSK60 comes through a related entity, and the biggest data centre sets are supplied pretty much on an allocation basis with lead times lengthening as global data centre demand absorbs the parent's capacity — which is why Stakeholder Concentration is elevated and why Cost to Replicate only rates a partial fit, management having conceded that the larger 78 and 95-litre engines are not made in India. That is the same gap the New Entrants risk points at. Capital allocation scores zero: the listed company does no acquisitions of its own, the only relevant deal is the parent's Meritor purchase, and management itself acknowledged it further complicates a multi-entity India structure that leaves value outside the listed vehicle. Growth scores poorly too, with exports the persistent weak spot — down 22% in one quarter, no discernible regional pattern for years, dumping pressure across South Asia, Latin America and Africa, and the FY27 export outlook explicitly withheld — while management twice declined to produce evidence of market share gains. Research spending is never quantified, which caps Innovation Dominance at partial even though the technology cadence is real, and battery storage is still only being evaluated, which is why Technological Innovation sits on watch. The offsetting comfort is culture, a strong fit: management set a two-times-GDP ambition and delivered it with FY26 sales up 18% and pre-tax profit up 24%, and volunteers the unflattering facts — calling its own record margins unsustainable, saying construction underperformed its own expectations, disclosing a INR50 crore one-off. When a management team discloses its problems unprompted, the disclosed risks above are more likely to be the full list. Net of all this, the combined rating is 69 out of 100, a Strong four-star verdict with resilience judged sound: 65 for business quality, a high 86 for pattern fit, and a much thinner 54 for risk safety, which is a fair picture of a high-quality franchise whose main vulnerabilities are the cycle, the parent and the export book rather than the core product.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Stakeholder Concentration. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.0× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 73% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 22% to 37% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across the four years management repeatedly frames segments in cycle language — compressors called "highly cyclical" in Aug 2022 and guided into a "down cycle" again in Jun 2026, construction tied to road-building velocity, mining to tender cycles, and exports swinging from 58% growth (Q1FY23) to -22% (Q1FY25); margins swung with commodities from a 300bp gross-margin drop in FY23 to "5-year high" 37.8% in FY25, with management itself saying that level is not sustainable. Demand is largely linked to customers' infrastructure and data-centre capex programmes rather than their operations.

> "Compressor segment will now undergo its low cycle, as it always does after a few years." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Distribution/aftermarket is a genuine flow business growing 22-26% y/y (FY26 INR3,278 crore, +22%) and cushions the equipment cycle; data-centre aftermarket is a separate dedicated stream.*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 3 of the last 11 years with swings of ±16 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 11% and 21% (±2.9 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 10% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 4% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Stakeholder Concentration — ELEVATED
*"Too few customers, suppliers or channels"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but the only computed fingerprint does not confirm it — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Two concentrations compound: dependence on parent Cummins Inc. for the largest engines (QSK95 imported, QSK60 via CTIPL, "pretty much on allocation basis" for the biggest data-centre sets, lead times lengthening as global data-centre demand absorbs parent capacity), and end-market concentration in data centres, which rose to 30-35% of domestic PowerGen revenue in FY26 with a handful of hyperscalers and colo players, making revenue visibly lumpy (PowerGen -16% in Q3FY26 purely because a data-centre execution fell in the prior quarter).

> "the data center business would have contributed between 30% to 35% of our overall power generation domestic revenue, and for the quarter, approximately 35%" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Distribution and industrial segments broaden the base, and management notes high localisation on the mainstream products it sells.*

- ✅ Customers pay in about 83 days (was 72) — no sign of weakening bargaining power in collection terms.

### Technological Innovation — WATCH
*"Out-invented by the industry"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** The core product is diesel backup power, and analysts probe the platform life cycle and next-generation technology (battery energy storage, hydrogen/electrolyser tenders, the GAIL hydrogen installation); management concedes it is only "evaluating whether battery energy storage systems could be something that our customers would include in their backup power mix." Emission-driven change so far has been incumbent-favourable rather than disruptive.

> "For now, for a few years, we do see the backup power space to be provided by the diesel gensets." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Regulatory tech steps (CPCB-IV+) have historically raised Cummins' price realisation and volumes — "whenever emissions have become tighter... the impact on Cummins has been on better volumes, better price realization" — i.e. incremental, value-adding innovation rather than platform reset; no new emission norm on the horizon.*

- ✅ Growth is holding up: -2% a year earlier, 23% recently — no structural stall.
- ✅ Operating margin (21%) remains near its historical peak (21%) — no collapse in the premium.

### Government Dependency — WATCH
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Product specification, transition timing and pre-buy economics are set by CPCB norms and ministry decisions, and several end segments are policy/tender driven — railways (INR104 crore in Q3FY26), mining tenders that were dry for two years and revived, defence, plus explicit reliance on the Union Budget's infrastructure capex and the new data-centre tax incentive. Pricing itself is not regulated and the customer base is largely private.

> "We are very positive about the government capex story. We do appreciate the announcements made in the recent budget, on the capex that the government has announced." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Diversified across private data centres, realty, manufacturing and exports to five regions, so no single policy line item sets revenue.*

### New Entrants — WATCH
*"A new player changes the rules"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Analysts raise large data-centre orders won by Perkins, Baudouin and Adani on imported engines, arguing Cummins' localisation cost advantage narrows once sizes move above the QSK60; separately management flagged "increased dumping from other countries" into South Asia, Latin America and Africa export markets to keep foreign factories utilised.

> "if competition is based at equal footing on imported engines, the relative cost benefit which Cummins was having on a 60 liter fully localized base will definitely narrow off versus peers" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: At 95-litre sizes no competitor manufactures in India either, so the playing field is level rather than adverse; management points to end-to-end site engineering and service as the differentiator.*

- ✅ Operating margin held (about 16% then 20%) — no drift down under competition.
- ✅ Growth is holding up: -2% a year earlier, 23% recently — no structural stall.

### Good Enough Goods — WATCH
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Management repeatedly has to defend price against cheaper alternatives — flagging that "competitive pricing intensifies" will pressure record material margins, and answering the Perkins/Baudouin/Adani cost-parity challenge by pivoting away from price to the total solution — while export markets face outright dumping by lower-cost foreign producers.

> "it is not just about the cost and the pricing of these engines... It is the whole value proposition that we provide to the customers" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Genuine scale/complexity protection: CPCB-IV+ after-treatment and electronics raise the technical bar, the buyer is a sophisticated industrial customer (not a brand-unaware end consumer with an intermediary choosing for them), and the captive service/parts network plus global data-centre engineering experience is hard for a cheap substitute to replicate.*

- ✅ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs held near 37% (was 37%) — no gross-level price erosion.
- ✅ Operating margin (21%) remains near its historical peak (21%) — no collapse in the premium.
- ✅ Operating margin held (about 16% then 20%) — no drift down under competition.

### Shifting Consumer Preferences — LOW
*"The customer is changing"*

**Why this verdict:** the judge affirmatively found the risk absent or mitigated in the calls.

**What the calls show:** The obvious erosion thesis — a more reliable grid and cleaner-energy attitudes reducing diesel backup demand — was put to management directly and answered with evidence to the contrary: India stopped being power-deficient yet backup demand kept growing, and CPCB-IV+ price increases of 15-20% did not dent volumes.

> "India was a power deficient market and has kind of sorted itself out... even though you have more reliable grid power, you still need the backup power" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Rising affluence and 24x7 power expectations, plus genset cost being "a pretty small percentage of capital costs to run infrastructure," keep the demand base intact; data-centre uptime requirements (99.99%) structurally increase backup need.*

- ✅ Growth is holding up: -2% a year earlier, 23% recently — no structural stall.

### Fashion Risk — LOW
*"Cool today, uncool tomorrow"*

**Why this verdict:** the judge affirmatively found the risk absent or mitigated in the calls.

**What the calls show:** Demand across all 16 calls is functional and specification-driven — backup power, rail, mining, construction, marine — with no growth ever credited to a trend, brand moment or taste shift; the recurring parts and service business is repeat-purchase by nature.

> "diesel power backup is one of the most reliable backup powers that is available for our customers" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Functional, uptime-critical, repeat-purchase demand base with a large installed-base aftermarket (Distribution INR3,278 crore in FY26).*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 3 of the last 11 years with swings of ±16 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Growth is holding up: -2% a year earlier, 23% recently — no structural stall.

## What to watch

- Gross margin retreating from the roughly 37.8% record as management's own warning about intensifying competitive pricing plays out — this is the direct test of the Pricing Power pattern and the Good Enough Goods risk.
- Parent engine supply: allocation-based availability and lengthening lead times on the QSK60 and QSK95, and any further large data centre orders lost to Perkins, Baudouin or Adani above QSK60 size — the Stakeholder Concentration and New Entrants risks and the Cost to Replicate pattern all turn on this.
- Distribution and service growth slipping below the 22-26% range off the FY26 base of INR3,278 crore — it is both the Recurring Revenue pattern and the stated cushion for the Cyclicality high risk, so a slowdown hits twice.
- Whether the compressor down cycle guided for June 2026 and the construction underperformance stay contained or spread into core power generation — the Cyclicality risk is the only high-risk item in the review.
- Exports: any resumption of decline, worsening dumping pressure in South Asia, Latin America and Africa, or a continued refusal to give an FY27 export outlook — this is the negative-scoring Geographical expansion check and the main reason the Growth area rates so low.
