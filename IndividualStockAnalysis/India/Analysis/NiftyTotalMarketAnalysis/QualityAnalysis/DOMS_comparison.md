### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 7 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Forward Integrators**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The only forward-integration evidence in the window is rising e-commerce contribution in baby hygiene (which management flagged as margin-dilutive via higher A&P and freight) and a manufacturing JV rather than a retail one; there is no own-store count, franchise royalty or D2C revenue share disclosed anywhere in the four calls. (The long view had said: The stated strategy is greater backward integration and distributor-led general trade (75%+ of sales); there are no owned stores, franchising or royalty streams across ten calls, with e-commerce mentioned only as a small, margin-dilutive channel in baby hygiene.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Growth of 26.4%, 24.1%, 18.2% and 18.7% through FY26 with management explicitly claiming deeper penetration and a GST-driven shift from unorganised to branded, plus DOMS-branded export entry into Chile, Mexico, Canada, Turkey and Australia via FILA — but the calls never give an actual market-share number or a comparison against named competitors, so persistent share gain is asserted rather than evidenced. (The long view had said: DOMS has compounded revenue at roughly 22%-25% for three straight years (FY24 ~25%, FY25 24.4%, FY26 21.6%) against a stationery industry growing 11%-12%, from an already-leading 29% pencil share, while adding export geographies via FILA (Chile, Mexico, Canada, Europe, Turkey, Australia) — and the share gain is funded by margins held at 17%+, not bought.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 2 worsened, 4 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: Demand is consumable/operations-linked — school and office stationery, art materials and diapers bought repeatedly — and the four calls show steady 18–26% year-on-year revenue growth with EBITDA held in a narrow 16.7–17.6% band, not cycle-like swings; the only volatility discussed is input-cost (polymers, waxes, West Asia crisis), which management buffered via strategic low-cost inventory. Revenue is driven by back-to-school seasonality and its own capacity additions, not by any customer's capex cycle, and it is investing heavily (INR292 cr FY26, INR250–275 cr guided FY27) through a period of 'elevated uncertainty'. (The long view had said: Demand is a stable back-to-school consumable flow product with 25%/24.4%/21.6% revenue growth across FY24-FY26, but margins swing with crude-linked inputs — polypropylene reversal in FY24, and a 15-20% RM basket inflation from the West Asia crisis in FY26 against only 4-5% pricing, pushing Q1 FY27 margins under pressure.)
- **New Entrants**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No new-format or structurally different competitor is discussed defensively in any of the four calls; the competitive shift management describes runs the other way, with the GST cut moving demand from unorganized players toward branded product. The Q4 pricing caution is framed as raw-material pass-through discipline, not as margin investment against an entrant. (The long view had said: Management explicitly frames its refusal to raise prices as protecting shelf space from new entrants, and the FY26 pricing response was routed through channel-scheme and margin rationalisation precisely to avoid ceding competitive position — a defensive posture, not a dominant one.)

**That worsened:**

- **Government Dependency**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Policy visibly moves the P&L: GST 2.0 took 45–50% of the product base to 0%/5%, causing a quantified ~3–4% sales hit in Q2 FY26 through destocking and making input tax credit ineligible (a direct cost increase), while US tariffs on a core export product jumped from 6.5% toward ~50%. But this is tax/tariff exposure on a consumer business, not regulated pricing, subsidies, government tenders or fixed infrastructure, and the net GST effect is presented as structurally favourable. (The long view had said: Revenue is consumer-funded through general trade (>75% of sales) and is not priced by any regulator, tender or subsidy; government appears only as a background demand tailwind (education initiatives) and as US tariff friction on a small export slice, which the company grew through anyway.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The one live shift is channel, not category: rising e-commerce mix in baby hygiene measurably raised advertising, marketing and freight costs and contributed to Q4 FY26 margin moderation to 16.7% from 17.3%. There is no evidence of demand-base erosion in the core stationery categories — growth is volume-led across every segment — and the company is participating in the new channel rather than defending against it. (The long view had said: The one preference shift discussed — buyers moving from individual items to combination packs — was captured by the company rather than lost to it, with kits and combos growing over 40% in FY25; the shift to e-commerce in baby hygiene is being served directly, at some cost to margin.)

*Unchanged: Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
