# Awfis Space Solution (AWFIS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Awfis Space Solutions is an Indian company that rents out ready-to-use office space and sells workplace services — it takes commercial property, fits it out, and then charges clients per seat or per office for the use of it, on terms as short as an hour or as long as several years. Its customers range from single freelancers and startups to SMEs, large Indian corporates and multinational "global capability centres" (GCCs), with 3,400-odd client companies as of Q3 FY26. Money comes mainly from recurring seat/office fees plus add-on services (meeting rooms, day passes, virtual offices, food and beverage), and secondarily from one-off construction and fit-out projects it builds for clients; it sources space either by leasing it outright and paying for the fit-out itself (Straight Lease) or, increasingly, through capital-light profit/revenue-sharing deals where the landlord funds the fit-out and Awfis pays a minimum guarantee (Managed Aggregation). It operates only in India — 181 centres and ~110,540 seats across 17 cities in March 2024, growing to 257 centres and about 177,000 seats across 18 cities by Q3 FY26 — with FY26 revenue of INR1,493 crores, EBITDA of INR550 crores and PAT before exceptionals of INR71 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Co-working and allied services (Space Solutions)** — The core business: renting desks, cabins and whole managed/enterprise offices by the day, week, month or year, plus allied add-ons like meeting rooms, day passes, virtual office and virtual office plus, and food and beverage — this is by far the biggest part, at INR916 crores or 76% of revenue in FY25 and INR1,237 crores (up 35%) in FY26, running at 84% of quarterly revenue by Q3 FY26.
- **Enterprise / managed office solutions (within co-working)** — Custom-built, larger offices for big corporates and GCCs priced on the scope of services rather than a standard seat rate — management calls this its strategic priority, with 80-plus GCCs on the platform and clients taking 500+ seats now making up 36% of the portfolio.
- **Awfis Transform (design and build / construction fit-out)** — The design-and-build arm that fits out Awfis's own centres and also builds offices for outside clients — the second revenue line at INR278 crores, roughly 23% of FY25 revenue, though it fell to INR60 crores in Q3 FY26 on project deferrals and pollution-rule delays, with management pointing to a third-party pipeline of about 9 lakh sq ft worth roughly INR200 crores.
- **Awfis Care** — End-to-end property and facility management services run for clients at their own conventional offices; described in the FY24 call as one of the three primary offerings but never given a separate revenue figure in these excerpts.
- **Supply models: Straight Lease and Managed Aggregation** — Not a revenue line but how Awfis gets its space — Straight Lease means a 5-9 year lease with Awfis funding the fit-out and carrying the risk, while Managed Aggregation is a capital-light revenue/profit-share with the space owner funding fit-out against a minimum guarantee (averaging 46% of micro-market rent in FY24); MA covered 64% of centres and 66% of seats by FY24 and is being pushed further, alongside newer forward-leasing, developer-partnership and partially pre-anchored formats.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Awfis comes out of the three reviews as a genuinely good business sitting in a difficult industry, which is why the combined rating lands at 61 out of 100 — "Decent" — from 69 on business quality, 49 on multibagger fit and 60 on risk safety. The quality work is strongest on what the customer gets and what the owner earns: enterprise and GCC clients are described as buying certainty (speed-to-market, compliance readiness, scalability) rather than the lowest price, first-time GCCs are called highly sticky, and 250 centres across 18 cities with bundled café, IT, transport and events give unusual convenience. That translates into returns: ₹30 of operating cash for every ₹100 of capital, and return on capital rising from 43% to above 60% while the company kept negative net debt through a heavy build-out, grew only organically, and promoted both its new CEO and CFO from inside. The pattern screen picks up the same evidence and awards its single STRONG FIT to Corporate Culture — FY25 guidance of 30% revenue growth turned into 42%, and a promised 150 basis points of margin turned into 440.

The growth engine and the biggest danger are the same engine. Seats went from 110,540 in FY24 to about 184,000 signed by FY26 and revenue from ₹849cr to ₹1,208cr to ₹1,493cr, with EBITDA margin improving from 31% to 36.8% — profitable share gain, which is why Market Share Gainers scores a LIKELY FIT and capex is judged to be for growth rather than upkeep. But that same aggressive capacity addition (39,000 seats in FY25, roughly 30,000 in FY26) is being laid down into what management itself calls a record Indian office market — 82.6 msf leased in CY25, a third consecutive record — against leases running 5 to 9 years. The risk review therefore marks Cyclicality as the one HIGH RISK and rates overall resilience STRAINED. The offsetting facts are real but shorter-dated than the lease commitments: 37-month average client tenure, 26-month lock-ins, 84% occupancy at mature centres, and a stated shift toward capital-light forward leasing and shared-capex developer partnerships.

The second link runs through pricing. The end market is genuinely expanding — flex penetration has gone from 5% to 21% and is expected to reach 25% by 2027, with 20 to 30 first-time GCCs arriving each quarter — and Awfis is on the winning side of that migration, which is why Shifting Consumer Preferences is rated LOW risk. But the very openness that lets flex take share also lets capital in: flex operators took about 23% of all leasing, the quality framework scores Barriers to Entry at -2 on profit margins that swing by 20.3 points, and the risk review flags New Entrants and Good Enough Goods as items to watch. Management concedes headline pricing simply tracks the prevailing micro-market rent rather than reflecting the brand, so Pricing Power is only a LIKELY FIT resting on contracted escalations of 4-7%, Cost to Replicate is only PARTIAL because leases and fit-outs can be rebuilt, and Toll Roads and Low Price Plus are outright NO FITs — workspace is a large line item for the client, and the strategy is deliberately premium (100% of new supply in Grade A/A+). Client concentration is not the worry: the base widened from 2,459 to over 3,400 clients with the largest under 5% of space revenue. The worry is that a well-run, well-liked operator is compounding hard inside a structure that does not protect it.
