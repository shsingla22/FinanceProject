# Marico Ltd. (MARICO) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSUMER GOODS*

Marico Limited is an Indian consumer-goods (FMCG) company that makes and sells branded everyday household products — coconut and value-added hair oils (Parachute, Nihar, Oil of Malabar, Hair & Care), cooking oils and packaged health foods under Saffola (oats, honey, muesli), and premium personal-care and grooming brands (Beardo, Just Herbs, Plix, Kaya, True Elements, 4700BC, Cosmix). It sells to ordinary retail consumers through kirana/general-trade distributors and wholesalers, modern retail, e-commerce and quick commerce, and direct-to-consumer digital channels, with a large direct-reach expansion programme called Project SETU. It earns money on the spread between commodity inputs — mainly copra (dried coconut) and edible oils — and branded retail prices, so profit swings with input costs and pricing actions; management says commodity-linked businesses are over 70% of revenue today and it wants that down to about 50% by FY30. India is the main market, and an international business (Bangladesh, Vietnam, MENA/Egypt and the Gulf, South Africa and exports) is the second engine, with the Middle East alone about 4% of total turnover.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Parachute (coconut oil) / core coconut oil portfolio** — The flagship branded coconut hair oil, plus flanker brands Nihar and Oil of Malabar that fight regional and discount players; it is the company's biggest and most profitable anchor, with the composite coconut oil portfolio holding roughly 64% volume market share, and its economics are driven by copra prices.
- **Value Added Hair Oils (VAHO)** — Non-coconut hair oils across mass to premium price points (including Shanti Amla at the bottom of the pyramid and Aloe, Jasmine and a new almond oil under Hair & Care in the mid/premium tiers); it was sluggish and competitively squeezed in 2022-24 but grew 20% in FY26 with volume growth in the low 20s in Q4.
- **Saffola Edible Oils** — Premium refined cooking oils sold on a health positioning; a large revenue contributor whose growth swings hard with edible-oil price inflation and consumer down-trading, and which management runs to a 'threshold level of profitability' rather than chasing volume.
- **Foods** — Packaged health and snacking foods — Saffola oats, honey and muesli, True Elements clean-label breakfast, 4700BC gourmet popcorn (about Rs 140 crore run-rate, number two in popcorn), and functional nutrition from Plix and Cosmix (about Rs 100 crore run-rate); it exited FY26 above Rs 1,000 crore of revenue and management wants it at 15x FY20 levels by FY30.
- **Premium Personal Care / digital-first brands** — Higher-priced skin, beauty and male-grooming brands sold mostly online — Beardo, Just Herbs, Plix personal care and Kaya — where the digital-first portfolio exited FY26 at over Rs 1,100 crore annual run-rate and is targeted to hit double-digit EBITDA margins in FY27.
- **International business** — Overseas operations selling similar hair-care, personal-care and baby-care products, led by Bangladesh and Vietnam with South Africa, MENA/Egypt, the Gulf (about 4% of total turnover) and exports; it has grown in constant-currency terms for many consecutive quarters and is guided to mid-teen constant-currency growth in FY27.
- **New businesses (Foods + Premium Personal Care combined)** — The label management uses for its diversification away from commodity-linked core products; these reached 23% of India revenue in FY26, targeted at about 27% in FY27 and roughly one-third by FY30, built partly through acquisitions of profitable digital brands of Rs 100-150 crore scale.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Marico scores 79 out of 100 — Strong, four stars — and the arithmetic behind that (45% × 75 on business quality, 30% × 89 on multibagger fit, 25% × 76 on risk safety) says something useful on its own: the pattern screen rates this business more highly than the quality framework does, and the risk review holds it back only mildly. The engine is a single, very well-defended idea. Parachute holds a record ~64% composite volume share in branded coconut oil while charging more than rivals, which is what earns the top mark on mini-monopolies and drives two STRONG FIT patterns at once — Brand Strength and Pricing Power. Those two are really the same fact seen twice: management raised prices repeatedly across all 17 calls (5% on value-added hair oils in FY23, coconut oil through FY25, immediate hikes on crude-linked inflation in FY26) and share went up rather than down. The advertising check is the mechanism underneath it — spend was protected even when margins were squeezed, moved to master-brand and digital media (55% of core ad spend), and more than 95% of the portfolio is gaining or holding share. That flows straight into the money: ₹49 of every ₹100 of sales kept after direct input costs, ₹17 of operating profit per ₹100 of sales with only about ±1.7 points of yearly movement, and ₹44 of operating cash for every ₹100 of capital employed.

The awkward part is that the very concentration that creates the strength also creates most of the risk. Because so much rests on coconut oil, results swing with copra and edible-oil prices — Saffola volumes fell around 20% on edible-oil inflation in Q1 FY23, copra inflation drove FY26's price-led growth, and copra has since corrected roughly 35% from its peak. That is the Cyclicality risk, and it is the flip side of the Pricing Power pattern: pricing works so well partly because inputs move so much. The same premium position invites the Good Enough Goods risk — deep discounters running negative gross margins in coconut oil, Rs.10 and Rs.20 price packs in hair oils, down-trading from super-premium edible oil — and management's deliberate refusal to fight on price (why Low Price Plus is a NO FIT) means that pressure is absorbed through brand and flanker labels like Nihar rather than removed. This is also where the quality framework is bluntest: Competitive Advantage scores 0.0 and Industry Structure 0.33, which lines up with Cost to Replicate rating only PARTIAL — copra procurement, back-end supply chain and Project SETU distribution depth are real advantages a smaller player cannot match in a volatile year, but a well-funded rival could build them. New Entrants stays on WATCH for the same reason: discounters, D2C-native premium hair oil brands and national players pushing below-the-line pricing are all structurally different attackers rather than familiar ones.

The response to all of this is diversification, and it has largely worked — which is why the story hangs together rather than reading as a defensive crouch. Geography was deliberately broadened out of Bangladesh into Vietnam, MENA/Egypt, South Africa and exports, delivering sustained double-digit constant-currency growth, with MENA now only about 4% of turnover; the residual Stakeholder Concentration risk is visible in the 2024 Bangladesh political crisis and the March 2026 MENA shipment disruption, but it is shrinking. Foods was taken past the Rs.1,000 crore target set back in 2022, digital-first brands went from Rs.200 crore of annual run-rate revenue in FY23 to over Rs.1,100 crore by FY26 (the basis of the PARTIAL Forward Integrators read — real direct customer ownership, but no stores, franchising or royalties), and new businesses are 23% of India revenue heading toward a third by FY30. That pivot is what makes Technological Innovation a LOW risk: the disruption in this industry is channel and digital, and the company is leading it rather than defending against it. But the pivot imports fresh risk of a kind the coconut oil core never had — Fashion Risk, from male grooming, K-Beauty-inspired skincare, gourmet popcorn and vegan nutraceuticals, with management openly conceding fad risk in Beardo — and it depends on a string of small bolt-ons (Beardo, True Elements, Plix, Just Herbs, Kaya, 4700BC, Cosmix, Skinetiq) bought at disciplined prices, one of which (4700BC) was disclosed as loss-making at purchase. The reason to give the transition the benefit of the doubt is the Candor check and the Corporate Culture pattern: stable leadership across all 17 calls, a multi-year target set and hit, and bad news stated with numbers attached — an 800 basis point foods margin correction, a deliberate pause, honey share lost. No risk channel rates worse than WATCH, resilience is SOUND, and the honest summary is a high-return, cash-generative brand business whose moat is narrower than its results imply, buying itself a broader base before the pressure on the core gets worse.
