# L T Foods (LTFOODS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

LT Foods is an Indian branded packaged-food company whose core business is buying, milling, ageing, packing and selling basmati and other specialty rice under its own consumer brands — Daawat in India, Royal and Golden Star in the US, plus organic foods and ready-to-eat lines. It makes money mainly by selling branded rice packs to households through supermarkets, distributors and increasingly e-commerce and quick-commerce channels, earning a spread between the paddy/rice it buys and the branded price it charges; management repeatedly frames itself as transforming from a rice exporter into a 'global branded FMCG company'. Its biggest market is North America (48% of FY26 revenue, where Royal has over 60% share of the basmati market and Golden Star is the No. 1 jasmine rice brand), followed by India (23.7% market share per Nielsen, reaching 64.4 lakh households), Europe/UK and the Middle East. FY26 revenue was about INR 11,023 crore with EBITDA of INR 1,236 crore and profit after tax of INR 625 crore; results swing with rice input prices, ocean freight costs, currency and, recently, US tariffs.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Basmati & Other Specialty Rice** — The core business — branded basmati and specialty rice (Daawat, Royal, Golden Star, including health-focused rice varieties) sold to consumers in India, North America, Europe and the Middle East; it is 88% of revenue at INR 9,742 crore in FY26, grew 29% that year and carries about a 12.3% EBITDA margin.
- **Organic Foods and Ingredients** — A more than 30-year-old organic sourcing and export business supplying organic food ingredients to partners in 25 countries, backed by 110,000 hectares of certified organic farmland and around 80,000 farming families, holding roughly 12% of India's organic food exports; it crossed INR 1,016 crore in FY26 (up 9%) but management says it is under stress and is being 'remodeled', with new capacity yet to be filled and a move into branded consumer packaged goods.
- **Ready-to-Heat / Ready-to-Cook (RTH/RTC) and convenience foods** — Small but fast-growing convenience range — Biryani Kits, Cuppa Rice, the Royal ready-to-heat line, plus healthy snacks — that has grown 2.5x in five years to INR 187 crore in FY26, with demand currently outstripping capacity until expanded facilities come online in Q2 FY27.
- **Geographic businesses (India, North America, Europe/UK, Middle East)** — Management reports by region as well: North America is the largest at 48% of FY26 revenue (53% reported growth, 9% normalised for tariffs), India grew 10% in value and 12% in volume with quick/e-commerce up over 45%, Europe grew 34% with a separate UK entity in an investment phase targeting GBP 100 million of UK revenue in five years, and the Middle East is a smaller but expanding market including a recent entry into Saudi Arabia.
- **Associates, subsidiaries and acquisitions** — The group operates through subsidiaries such as Daawat Foods and Raghunath Agro (being merged into LT Foods) and books associate profits from Golden Star, and in May 2025 proposed acquiring Global Green Group, a canned-food business of roughly USD 40 million described as having good synergy with the existing portfolio.

### The story in depth

L T Foods earns its "Decent" 58-out-of-100 rating on the strength of a few things it does genuinely well and repeats every year. It owns two number-one brand positions that are hard to dislodge — Royal, with more than 60% of North American basmati across both mainstream and ethnic channels, and Golden Star, the leading jasmine brand with a loyal community behind it — which is why the quality framework gives its "mini-monopolies" check top marks. Sales have compounded at about 14% a year with very little wobble, operating profit sits at roughly ₹11 on every ₹100 of sales and has moved only about ±0.8 points a year, and the company is putting real money behind growth: capex stepped up from roughly INR160-200cr to INR340cr for new plants in the UK, a US ready-to-heat facility, Raichur, Saudi Arabia and warehousing, with return on capital still around 21%. Alongside that sit small, sensible acquisitions — buying in the last 49% of Golden Star, taking Raghunath Agro to 100%, a roughly $40m canned-food deal — and a funded international build-out (UK at GBP45m against a GBP100m 2030 goal, Europe up 34%, Middle East up 37%, Southeast Asia INR53cr). The risk review backs this up: five of eight risk channels come out LOW and the business is judged SOUND, which is why the safety score of 88 is the highest of the three inputs.

The multibagger screen tells a narrower story, and it is the same story viewed from the other side. Only one of eleven patterns is a strong fit — Market Share Gainers — and it is strong precisely because of the evidence above: Royal's US share rising from about 55% to over 60%, a 23.7% Nielsen share in India, household reach up 22.8%, quick-commerce growth above 45%, 12% of India's organic food exports, and customers won during the US tariff disruption. Brand Strength is a likely fit for the same reasons. Everything else is partial or absent, and the common cause is the product itself. Pricing Power is only partial because, next to genuine premiumisation (the premium range growing at twice the base) the record also shows prices falling — 7% value growth on 15% volume in India in FY25 — and management conceding it must hand input-cost falls straight back to food-service buyers. That same concession is why Toll Roads is a clear non-fit. Low Price Plus does not fit either, but for the opposite reason: this is a high-cost, slow-turning model running on 176-227 days of working capital and 190-200 days of aged inventory. The quality framework agrees — competitive advantage scores zero and industry structure scores low, with the call transcripts stating plainly that barriers are low in commodity rice because paddy sourcing is open to anyone. Strong brands sitting on a product that is easy to copy is what caps multibagger fit at 33.

Several single traits show up twice — once as a strength and once as a cost — and that is the clearest way to read this company. The 18-24 month aged premium paddy inventory is one of the few genuinely hard-to-replicate assets the screen identifies, and it is also the direct source of the roughly 176-day cash cycle that draws the framework's worst score, a -2. The doubled marketing budget is what produced the measurable brand gains (household reach up 22.8%, 23.7% India share) and it is funded by premiumisation, but it is dilutive to margins right now. Simplicity of product is what makes distribution and premiumisation work so well, and it is also the reason "good enough goods" is flagged as a risk to watch — cheaper alternatives are easy to field, and India realisations have already fallen. The US tariff swing from 10% to 50% and back to 10% cut about 100bps from reported margins, yet it is also credited with winning customers, which is why government dependency is a watch item rather than a structural flaw — policy moves the profit line without changing the business. Cyclicality works the same way: results swing with paddy prices and ocean freight (FY25 realisations down about 6.5%, logistics moving from 7.1% to 5.8% of sales), but demand is a staple bought repeatedly, not a capex cycle. The one soft spot on the management side is that promised margin recovery has slipped more than once, which is why corporate culture rates only a partial fit despite a conservative balance sheet at 0.6x net debt to EBITDA and 0.16 debt to equity.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

### Market Share Gainers — STRONG FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence and the numbers agree (1 of 1 check computable supporting).

**What the calls show:** Share gain is documented with numbers and repeated over years: ~55% Royal US share in FY25 rising to 60%+ in FY26, 23.7% Nielsen share in India, #1 Jasmine brand, 12% of India's organic food exports, plus funded international expansion (UK toward GBP 100m, Saudi, Southeast Asia INR53 crores) — and it was won on service during the tariff shock rather than by discounting.

> "acquiring consumers, acquiring customer was a big achievement for us" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Sales compounding at 14% while margins held or improved (11% → 11%) — growth that was WON, not bought with price cuts.

### Brand Strength — LIKELY FIT
*"The name itself sells"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern, but the numbers push back (0 of 2 checks supporting) — held one step below a full confirmation.

**What the calls show:** Daawat and Royal are described consistently across calls as the leading basmati brands with premium price points, heritage (US business owned since 2008, 30+ years of organic exports), doubled marketing spend in FY26, household reach up 22.8% to 64.4 lakh, and stretch into RTH/RTC, organic and new geographies under the same names.

> "Our flagship brand, Royal commands over 60% market share and Golden Star continues to be number 1 Jasmine rice brand in U.S.A." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹33 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — limited pricing headroom.

### Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) — PARTIAL
*"Sells once, earns forever"*

- ✅ Capital spending is about 3% of sales — growth needs little capital.
- ✅ Operating cash was positive in 12 of 12 years — the reliable cash engine recurring-revenue businesses show.

### Pricing Power — PARTIAL
*"Raises prices, keeps the customers"*
