# Garware Hi Tech (GRWRHITECH) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Garware Hi-Tech Films is an Indian manufacturer of specialty plastic films — thin coated polyester films that are stuck onto glass and car bodies, plus industrial films sold to other manufacturers. Its main products are sun-control films (window films for cars and buildings that block ~99% of harmful UV rays), paint protection film (PPF, a clear protective wrap applied over car paint), and a range of industrial films such as shrinkable/PCR shrink films, low-oligomer films for electrical and electronic uses, release liners and some commodity items like thermal lamination, packaging and lidding films and metallized film. It makes money by selling these films at high margins through distributors, dealers, car OEMs and its own branded 'Garware Application Studio' outlets, under brands including 'Global'; it is fully vertically integrated, making its own PET resin, metallized, dyed and laminated components in-house at its plants in Aurangabad/Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar. It is mainly an exporter — roughly 74–81% of revenue comes from outside India, shipping to 90-plus countries with North America, India, the rest of Asia, Europe and now the Middle East as key markets — and FY26 revenue was about ₹2,120 crore with ₹500 crore EBITDA and ₹338 crore PAT, debt-free with ₹774 crore of cash.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Sun Control Films (SCF) / Solar Control Films — automotive and architectural** — Window films for car glass and building glass that cut heat and block 99% of UV rays, sold under brands like Spectra Pro and DecoVista; management calls this the largest business at roughly 50% of FY26 revenue, says Garware has the world's second-largest sun-control capacity and is the third-largest branded player in the US and Europe, with capacity expanded 75% to 1,800 lakh sq ft and a further ₹191 crore, 1,200 lakh sq ft line announced.
- **Paint Protection Film (PPF)** — Clear (and now coloured, matte, white, black and Titanium series) film wrapped over car paint to protect it, sold to dealers, OEMs and consumers; about 25% of FY26 revenue, over 70% exported, with Garware described as the only PPF manufacturer in India and capacity doubled to 600 lakh sq ft in September 2025.
- **Industrial Product Division (IPD)** — Business-to-business films — shrinkable and floatable/PCR (recycled-content) shrink films, low-oligomer films for electrical and electronic applications, release liners/PRU, plus commodity lines like thermal lamination, plain, packaging and lidding films and metallizers; about 25% of FY26 revenue, with management steering it toward the higher-margin specialty items.
- **Garware Application Studios (GAS)** — A direct-to-consumer retail network of branded studios that fit premium PPF and glazing films onto customers' cars, plus training of applicators; it grew from around 65 studios in 2023 to over 250 by FY26 across Tier-1, 2 and 3 cities, targeting 300-plus, and management treats it as the main way it created and now feeds India's PPF market.
- **Garware Home Solutions (GHS)** — A newly launched direct-to-consumer vertical selling architectural/home window films through studios run with local interior design firms, with the first studio in Chembur, Mumbai and 6 operational by FY26, targeted to reach 50 by end-FY27 — small today but positioned as a higher-margin domestic growth engine.
- **UAE subsidiary / MENA trading arm** — A newly announced wholly owned subsidiary in the UAE to handle trading and export of films, ceramic coatings and paint protection films across the Middle East/North Africa and other international markets, alongside a flagship global application studio there; described as a step to expand the fast-growing Middle East architectural film business.
- **Backward integration and new product lines (TPU, graphics, PDLC)** — In-house production of raw materials and components — PET resin from PTA and MEG, metallized film, dyed film and lamination — plus an upcoming TPU manufacturing line to be commissioned by October 2026 (25% of its capacity earmarked for new-generation products such as TPU-based UV-printable films, graphic solutions and PDLC privacy films); over ₹500 crore of capacity expansion in recent years, all funded from internal cash.

### The story in depth

Garware Hi Tech comes out of these three reviews as a genuinely good business that has spent a decade earning its position and is now being tested by something outside its control. The quality framework gives it top marks on ten separate checks, and they all point the same way: a 2017 pivot out of commodity polyester into specialty film that management has refused to abandon through commodity upcycles, a 50-plus person R&D team turning out named products every year (matte and coloured PPF, Spectra Pro, DecoVista, floatable and recycled-content shrink film, now PDLC switchable film), and roughly INR 500 crores of pure growth capacity built between FY23 and FY26 without borrowing a rupee. Debt is nil, cash is INR 774 crores, and the company announced a fresh INR 191 crore sun control line in its worst tariff year. The multibagger screen reads the same evidence and scores it 93 out of 100, its strongest of the three lenses.

The screen's five strongest patterns are really one story told five ways, and that same story is where the biggest risk lives. Film is sold through tinters, applicators, dealers and OEM showrooms, who pick and install the product while the car or home owner pays. That channel structure is what makes the Friendly Middleman and Brand Strength patterns fit so well, with a 20-year-old top-three brand in the US and Europe and a trained network of 5,000-plus tinters. But the risk review names the identical trait as its Good Enough Goods problem: the buyer who chooses the film is not the person who lives with it, and cheap Chinese and Korean imports sit right there as substitutes. The company's answer to the pattern and the risk is the same move, which is why Forward Integrators also scores a strong fit: application studios from 65 in FY23 to 250-plus heading to 300, eleven new global studios, direct-to-consumer selling in the USA, and the new Garware Home Solutions vertical. Consumer advertising at roughly 8 crore Meta impressions and 18 lakh site visits is the other half of the same defence, going around the intermediary to build end-user pull.

The weak spots are concentrated rather than scattered, which is why risk safety scores only 58 against business quality of 76 and the blended rating lands at 77, or Strong. Exports run 74 to 81 percent of sales, and the deliberate shift into North America from 48 to 55 percent that earns full marks under geographical expansion is exactly what makes Government Dependency a high risk and Stakeholder Concentration a watch item. A single policy decision, the 50 percent US tariff, turned growth of over 20 percent into flat revenue of INR 2,120 crores and cut nine-month operating profit 8.3 percent, and management openly ties the recovery to a US Supreme Court ruling or a trade deal. The tariff also explains why Pricing Power is only a likely fit rather than a strong one: some cost was passed to distributors while customers were kept, but a meaningful chunk was absorbed, with operating profit down 7.4 percent year on year, even as margins over the full period rose from 18.7 percent in FY23 to 23.6 percent in FY26. Two quieter gaps deserve naming. Industry structure scores 0.67 out of 2, consistent with an anti-dumping petition the company is actively pursuing against low-cost imports, and return on capital scores 0.0, the only area with no credit at all, which sits awkwardly next to a large cash pile and heavy self-funded expansion.
