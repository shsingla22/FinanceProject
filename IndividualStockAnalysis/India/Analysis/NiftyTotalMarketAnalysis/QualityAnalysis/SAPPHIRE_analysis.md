# Sapphire Foods (SAPPHIRE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Sapphire Foods India Limited runs fast-food restaurants under franchised international brands — KFC, Pizza Hut and (in Sri Lanka) Taco Bell — selling fried chicken, pizza and related items directly to walk-in and online customers. It makes money purely from restaurant sales, earned three ways: dine-in, takeaway and home delivery (delivery comes largely via aggregators such as Swiggy and Zomato), and it grows by opening more outlets and by lifting sales at existing ones (what management calls SSSG, or same-store sales growth). It operates mainly in India, plus Sri Lanka and a very small presence in the Maldives; the store count went from about 550 in December 2021 to 1,074 by June 2026. Quarterly revenue rose from about INR 544 crore in Q1 FY23 to INR 888 crore in Q1 FY27, and management announced a merger with Devyani International.

**The parts of the business:**

- **KFC (India)** — Its chicken quick-service chain and the biggest, most profitable part of the group — 575 restaurants at March 2026, growing revenue 11-17% recently with restaurant-level EBITDA margins around 16-17%, and the main engine of new store additions (about 73 opened in FY26).
- **Pizza Hut (India)** — Its number-two pizza brand run on a 'dine-in forward omni-channel' compact-store model — 341 restaurants and INR 507 crore of FY26 revenue, but the weak spot: sales fell about 7% in FY26 and restaurant EBITDA was negative (-3.3%), so store openings were slowed to a trickle while management invests in marketing and new products.
- **Sri Lanka business** — A separate international arm operating KFC, Pizza Hut and Taco Bell outlets in Sri Lanka, described as the country's number-one QSR position; it contributed around 25% of group profit in FY22, fell sharply during the 2022 currency and economic crisis, and by FY26 was growing 16% in local-currency revenue with roughly 14-15% restaurant margins, though wage, fuel and currency inflation squeeze profits.
- **Maldives** — A tiny overseas operation of two KFC and two Pizza Hut stores, about 0.4% of group revenue, whose sales dropped roughly 60% after Middle East geopolitical disruption, prompting a non-cash impairment of INR 11.4 crore in Q2 FY25.

### The story in depth

Sapphire Foods comes across first as a well-run operating company rather than a structurally advantaged one. The strongest scores in the quality work are all about people and honesty: the founders-in-charge have fronted every one of the 18 earnings calls since FY22, they disclose bad news promptly and without spin (the Maldives impairment, quarter-after-quarter Pizza Hut losses, an admission that campaigns 'were not clicking' and that they were 'surprised by the intensity of the drop'), and the company has earned Gallup Exceptional Workplace recognition on top-quartile global engagement for four to five straight years, ranks #1 among Indian quick-service restaurants on DJSI social and governance measures, and promotes its own people into CEO roles. That is exactly why Corporate Culture is the one multibagger pattern rated a strong fit, and why Management is far and away the best-scoring quality area. The day-to-day economics support it: customers pay about 59 days before suppliers need to be paid, so the business is effectively funded by its own customers, ₹68 of every ₹100 of sales is left after direct input costs, and ₹100 of capital produced ₹18 of operating cash this year. Tenacity is real too — delivering by bicycle through Sri Lanka's fuel crisis, holding one strategy through COVID and currency shocks, and sticking with a marketing rebuild through three years of failed campaigns.

The problem is that several of the same traits that create the appealing patterns also create the risks. The Yum! relationship is the clearest case: exclusive territory rights, a six-year wait to secure the Phoenix Palladium site and a 1,000-plus store base a newcomer could not rebuild quickly are genuine barriers, which is why Cost to Replicate scores a partial fit — but those brands are licensed, not owned, Yum! is selling Pizza Hut globally, analysts asked directly what that means for the master franchise agreement, and even store-opening plans are set on Yum!'s calendar, which is what pushes Stakeholder Concentration to elevated and makes Forward Integrators a clear non-fit (Sapphire funds Yum!'s growth with its own capex and pays royalties out). Expansion works the same way: store count roughly doubled from about 550 in Dec-21 to 1,074 by Jun-26, KFC went from roughly 200 stores to 575, and Sri Lanka delivered six consecutive quarters of double-digit same-store growth — enough for a likely fit on Market Share Gainers — yet India same-store sales were negative for roughly ten quarters through that build-out, incremental returns diluted, and the risk review flags this as a textbook over-investment pattern. And the recovery lever is the third example: a deliberate 75-100bps step-up in KFC marketing plus a permanent ₹99 value layer finally turned same-store sales positive, which is the Low Price Plus pattern at work, but defending a price point with permanent discounts, monthly buy-one-get-one buckets and vendor cross-subsidy is also precisely why Pricing Power is a no-fit and why Good Enough Goods sits on watch, with an acknowledged 50-70bps gross-margin hit once the vendor subsidy lapses.

Netting it out, the combined rating of 54 out of 100 — 60 on business quality, 41 on multibagger fit, 58 on risk safety — lands the company at Decent, and the resilience read is strained. The gap between the quality score and the pattern score is the whole story: management is excellent, the customer proposition is liked, and the cash cycle is a rare strength, but competitive advantage scores negative, growth has averaged about 16% a year in a lumpy rather than dependable way, and brand power is rented. Pizza Hut is where the strain shows most — five straight quarters of negative same-store sales, FY26 revenue down 7% and restaurant profit down 3.3%, taste-based erosion rather than execution failure, and marketing money that has not yet fixed it while the equivalent KFC spend did. On top of that, after years of promising a disciplined bolt-on filter and saying there was 'no hurry', the company has entered a large merger with Devyani International, with retention-related employee costs already hitting the profit and loss — the one check where the record contradicts management's own stated approach, and the single biggest new variable sitting on top of an otherwise honest, hard-working, but structurally dependent franchise business.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** Purohit and Jain have fronted every one of the 18 calls since FY22; bad news is disclosed promptly and without spin (Maldives impairment taken in Q2FY25, Pizza Hut losses called out quarter after quarter, 'it has taken us time to find the right marketing mix'), guidance given in Dec-21 was delivered by Dec-24, and management refuses to chase M&A or store growth without meeting stated criteria.

> "we were not getting positive SSSG... We tried various campaigns, but things were not clicking." — *management, on an earnings call*

### Innovation Dominance — LIKELY FIT
*"Out-inventing the competition"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and every one of the 2 computable checks supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** Named launches appear every year with revenue commentary — Flavour Fun pizzas (FY23), Melts and Momo Mia (FY25), Dunked, ShaWOWrma and Double Chicken Dynamite (FY26), plus digital kiosks in 73–75% of stores — but there is no disclosed R&D spend, and management deliberately limits innovation pace; it has not translated into pricing power.

> "Typically we used to run 3 to 4 innovations in a year. We have upped our innovation intensity." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹68 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ✅ High retained margin (68% of sales) alongside 16% yearly growth — the profitable-innovation flywheel: margins fund the spending that drives the growth.

### Market Share Gainers — LIKELY FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and the only computable check supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** Sri Lanka shows durable share leadership and six consecutive quarters of double-digit SSSG, and KFC India doubled its store count from Dec-21 to Dec-24 as promised; but India same-store sales were negative for roughly ten quarters and Pizza Hut ceded ground to a more aggressive Domino's, so system growth came from new stores rather than winning share.

> "we would double the count by December 24, and we should be close to 500, so that's on track" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Sales compounding at 16% while margins held or improved (6% → 17%) — growth that was WON, not bought with price cuts.

### Cost to Replicate — PARTIAL
