# Kansai Nerolac (KANSAINER) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Kansai Nerolac Paints is an India-listed paint and coatings maker (NSE symbol KANSAINER), majority-owned by its promoter Kansai Paint Co., Ltd. of Japan, which supplies much of its technology. It makes money two ways: selling decorative paints, waterproofing and construction chemicals and wood finishes to households, painters, architects and building projects through a dealer/retail network it keeps expanding (3,500 dealers added as of December 2025, 600-plus branded Shoppe/shop-in-shop outlets), and selling industrial coatings directly to manufacturers — above all car, two-wheeler, three-wheeler, commercial-vehicle and tractor makers, where management says it is the market leader. It manufactures in India across eight plants (expansions cited at Vizag, Jainpur and Hosur) and keeps 200–300 technical staff stationed on customers' production lines. Growth is tied to Indian housing/repainting demand, monsoon and festive timing, government infrastructure spending and vehicle production, while raw-material costs move with crude and the rupee; recent quarters show low-single-digit revenue growth amid heavy new competition in decorative.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Decorative** — Paints and coatings for homes and buildings sold through retail dealers, plus the newer premium 'Paint+' range that management says now contributes more than 10% of decorative sales; this is the larger, more competitive side of the business and the focus of most of the calls.
- **Decorative – Retail** — The core over-the-counter business where Nerolac is the number-two brand nationally, supported by painter and architect loyalty programmes, branded experience stores and heavy advertising (TV jingle, Asia Cup/World Cup campaigns).
- **Decorative – Projects & Institutional** — Bulk supply to builders, institutions and construction projects through a dedicated on-ground team now covering 80-plus towns; started from a small base, grows in double digits and is described as growing faster than the industry, though its share of decorative was still below 10% as of early 2025.
- **Decorative – New Businesses (construction chemicals, waterproofing, wood finishes)** — Adjacent products like waterproofing and repair chemicals that Nerolac had no presence in until roughly 2021-22; salience has crossed 10%, it grows in strong double digits, and it was expanded by acquiring Nerofix and Perma — management sees big headroom since only about three in ten buildings currently use construction chemicals.
- **Services (paint-as-a-service / influencer programmes)** — A painting-service and architect/interior-designer programme that generates digital leads and executes jobs in 150-plus cities; it now contributes more than 5% of decorative business, the 'critical mass' target management had set.
- **Industrial – Automotive (OEM)** — Coatings supplied directly onto vehicle assembly lines for passenger cars, two- and three-wheelers, commercial vehicles and tractors, where Nerolac is the market leader and is still gaining share; demand jumped roughly 20% year-on-year after the October 2025 GST cuts, and management expects support from bigger SUVs (more paintable area), OEM capacity expansion and vehicle exports.
- **Industrial – Auto Refinish** — The after-market side, selling repair paints to car body shops, with a push to win premium 'A-class' body shops and convert them from solvent-based to waterborne systems; described as stable rather than fast-growing.
- **Industrial – Performance Coatings (liquid and powder)** — Non-automotive industrial coatings — coil coating, fusion-bonded epoxy powder, general industrial for construction equipment and pre-engineered buildings, plus powder coatings for auto ancillaries, appliances and furniture; management has deliberately exited low-end, low-margin work here to raise the premium mix, and wins depend on long approval cycles.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## The verdict: Mixed — 48 out of 100 ★★☆☆☆

In one breath: the quality framework finds a good business today (62/100); no winning pattern is strongly confirmed yet; the risk review found 1 high risk (New Entrants). Weighing those together gives 48 out of 100 — mixed.

### The story in depth

Kansai Nerolac's strongest ground is the industrial and automotive side of the business, and almost everything the company does well traces back to it. The quality framework gives top marks to the reliability customers are buying: an unblemished supply and quality record, plus quality awards from Toyota, Maruti Suzuki, Suzuki Motorcycle and TAFE. It also gives top marks to the end markets themselves, which are growing about 10% on a large base, with construction chemicals carrying roughly three times the current penetration headroom and vehicle-making capacity set to double by 2030. Management scores highly for owning its misses out loud rather than dressing up weak numbers. Those same facts are what let the multibagger screen call two patterns a likely fit. The Friendly Middleman pattern fits because painters, architects, contractors, dealers and approved body shops pick and apply the paint while the homeowner or the vehicle maker pays, and the company puts numbers to that influencer programme every quarter. Innovation Dominance fits because named products with disclosed revenue impact turn up in every call, from Beauty Little Master Sheen and Crystal Seal through Wow Whites to Excel Everlast 20 and the low-bake and BTX-free industrial coatings. The Toll Roads and Cost to Replicate patterns land only partly, and for the same reason: they clearly hold on the industrial side, where approvals gate entry, 200 to 300 technical staff sit inside customer plants and four-wheeler share is above 50%, but they do not hold on the decorative side, where a well-funded newcomer can simply buy its way in.

The awkward part is that the traits driving those patterns are the same traits driving the biggest risks. The 50%-plus four-wheeler share that supports the market-share and toll-road readings is also the Stakeholder Concentration risk, because that share sits with a handful of vehicle makers and losing one supply line would be material. Industrial and automotive now being more than half of revenue is what makes the Cyclicality risk live, since quarterly demand tracks vehicle production, including the roughly 20% jump that followed the October 2025 tax cut that analysts are already asking about the payback on. The same policy dependence shows up again in the performance-coatings story, which is anchored in government spending on railways, bridges, roads, airports and power. Meanwhile the influencer and dealer machine that makes the Friendly Middleman pattern work is exactly what the New Entrants risk is about: with a structurally different, deep-pocketed newcomer and a large acquisition dominating the questions on every call since late 2023, the company's answer has been to spend, with advertising up more than 20% and elevated staff and influencer costs. That is a defence that costs money rather than one that earns it. The deliberate choice to walk away from cheap volume, which the framework praises as disciplined stewardship and which the screen records as an outright non-fit with the Low Price Plus pattern, has a matching cost too: putty volumes were allowed to go negative rather than match irrational pricing, and the Good Enough Goods risk is rated elevated because cheaper substitutes are visibly attacking from the bottom of the market.

Put together, the numbers explain why the combined rating lands at 48 out of 100, a Mixed verdict, with financial resilience judged strained. Business quality scores 62, pattern fit only 26, and risk safety 50. Three of the seven quality areas score zero: capital allocation, return on capital and industry structure. The capital-spending check is outright negative, because growth plants at Vizag, Jainpur and Hosur were built when utilisation was only 65%, and by March 2026 management says no significant further investment is needed at all, which is capacity sitting ahead of demand while revenue is close to flat. About 95 days of cash stays tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. And the Pricing Power pattern is a clear non-fit: across nine calls there is no instance of raising price above cost inflation while keeping volume, with average selling prices held up by product mix rather than price, revenue growing only 1.5% to 3.5%, and decorative described as marginally negative to flat. So the honest reading is a genuinely good industrial franchise with real innovation output and candid management, attached to a decorative business that is spending to hold flat share against a better-funded attacker, on an asset base built for growth that has not yet arrived. What is missing is the evidence that any of this converts into higher prices, higher returns on the money already spent, or share gains outside automotive.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are New Entrants, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.0× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 84% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.

### New Entrants — HIGH RISK
*"A new player changes the rules"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 2 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** A structurally different, deep-pocketed entrant (Grasim) plus JSW's acquisition of AkzoNobel dominate the Q&A in every call from Nov-2023 to Mar-2026, and the response is explicitly cost-led — 20%+ ad-spend increases, elevated staff costs, heavy influencer/dealer investment — while decorative revenue has gone from 'marginally negative' to flat and 9M FY26 PBT is down 4%; management concedes 'redistribution of the market is happening'.

> "competition intensity still remains pretty intense... everyone is trying to protect their own market" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Management reports intensity has stabilised rather than kept rising, weighted-rich dealers retained with unchanged churn, and a selective town-level over-indexing strategy plus Paint+ differentiation as the response*

- ⚠️ Operating margin drifted down from about 16% to 12% — steady profit erosion, the mark of rising competitive pressure.
- ✅ Growth is holding up: 6% a year earlier, 10% recently — no structural stall.

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

> "we saw a very good demand for automotive products fuelled primarily by GST cuts" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/consumable demand tied to customer production volumes and repaint cycles rather than customer capex; continued investment in dealers, CCD machines and influencer programmes through the downturn*

## What to watch

- Whether decorative share stops being flat and starts rising, the specific test the Market Share Gainers and Brand Strength patterns both fail today
- Whether the defence against the new entrants stays cost-led: advertising up more than 20% and elevated staff and influencer spend is the New Entrants risk in its current form, so watch whether that spending falls back or becomes permanent
- Any evidence of price rises above cost inflation without losing volume, which is the exact test the Pricing Power pattern fails and the reason product mix, not price, is holding up realisations
- Utilisation on the Vizag, Jainpur and Hosur capacity and any improvement in the roughly 95-day cash cycle, the two items behind the negative capital-spending check and the zero scores on capital allocation and return on capital
