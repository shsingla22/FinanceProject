### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Every call in the window benchmarks TCIL volumes against RBI LRS industry data and shows outperformance — Q2 flat vs industry -5%, Q3 retail +25% and education +39% vs industry -22%, Q4 holiday +13%/education +17%/remittance +19% vs industry -3%/-22%/-5% — with margins expanding (forex EBIT 46–49%), so the share is not bought with profitability. (The long view had said: The FY26 calls benchmark performance explicitly against RBI LRS industry data and show repeated gains: retail forex +25% while industry travel fell 6%, education +39% against a 22% industry contraction (Feb 2026), and full-year holiday +13%/education +17%/remittance +19% against industry declines of 3%/22%/5% (May 2026), plus 8 new corporate accounts each quarter toward 8,000 clients.)
- **Forward Integrators**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Clear and repeated: owned outlets, airport counters and franchise additions in Q4 FY26 (5 franchises, 1 owned outlet, 1 airport counter), new stores in Kottayam and Varanasi in Q3, plus a deliberate D2C stack — TCPay app, website (+65% year-on-year), WhatsApp, and quick-commerce delivery — taking digital penetration to 23%. (The long view had said: Consistent build-out of owned distribution — franchises, owned outlets, airport counters, Tier-2/3 store additions, and digital penetration rising from 21% to 23% with app bookings up 4.9x — plus Sterling's own proprietary platform; but it is a services group extending distribution rather than a manufacturer capturing downstream margin, and D2C is still a minority of sales.)

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Long-tenured leadership (Iyer MD since FY2023, Modi CFO since FY2021, Menon as chairman), Fairfax backing and a CRISIL AA upgrade point to stability, and management flags one-offs (ex-gratia, Labour Code) transparently — but FY26 misses are attributed almost entirely to external events rather than owned, and EBT fell 14% against a repeatedly restated 15% growth target. (The long view had said: Leadership is stable and long-tenured (Menon, Iyer, Nandy, Modi across the full period), guidance is given quarter-by-quarter and then measured against outcome, and bad news is disclosed promptly and specifically — the FTI bankruptcy provision, the Bangalore Airport exit, Sterling's ramp-cost margin dip, the Labour Code and GST ITC one-offs — with Sterling run debt-free with INR3.4bn cash through the cycle.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across FY26 management describes being a price-taker on elevated airfares, rupee weakness and 'increasing pressure on pricing across the supply chain', and in Q3 FY26 explicitly gave up margin in Desert Adventures to hold volume; travel EBIT margins sit at 3.1–4.1%. (The long view had said: Sterling shows real pricing power (ARR up 5% then 12% y/y with occupancy also rising, RevPAR +16-17%) and forex EBIT margins expanded to 41-48%; but the core outbound travel business is price-taking — long-haul volumes fell as airfares rose 30-50% and management is repricing Europe downward to hold volume.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Low Price Plus (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 4 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The company is a distribution intermediary in travel and forex — structurally exposed to online/AI disintermediation — and only ~21-23% of forex transactions are digital, so most of the base is still offline. The calls show a steady, small-scale innovation cadence (TACY/Ezy/Dhruv AI assistants, TCPay app, Google Pay/Visa contactless, Blinkit delivery) rather than any defensive discussion of a competitor platform making its model legacy. (The long view had said: The legacy branch/agent model faces OTA and now AI-agent disintermediation, and digital adoption is still only ~21-23% of transactions after four years of investment; management responds with a steady cadence of small-scale innovation (omnichannel, VKYC, TCPay, Sterling ONE, TACY and Rahi AI tools) rather than facing a platform reset it must defend against.)
- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: In Q3 FY26 the Middle East DMS unit met 'stiff competitive intensity' and management conceded margin to defend volume, which is exactly the margin-investment-as-competitive-response pattern; elsewhere the company is co-opting the new format rather than fighting it, becoming the first forex provider distributing through Blinkit. (The long view had said: Structurally different competitors — OTAs, direct-booking platforms and low-cost DMS operators — are visible in the Middle East where competitive intensity forced an explicit margin sacrifice, and in forex where fintech/bank apps commoditise the product; management's response is to build the new format itself rather than defend the old one.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The structural shift is channel and destination rather than an eroding demand base: customers increasingly buy online (app bookings up 3x-4.9x, website +65%) while only ~23% of forex is digital, and travellers are re-routing to alternate destinations, which management says it captured. This is a pivot the company is executing rather than a franchise being hollowed out. (The long view had said: Demand mix is migrating structurally — long-haul/Europe eroding in favour of visa-free short-haul (short-haul +23% vs long-haul +5% on a 9-month basis), booking moving online, and Sterling completely sunsetting long-term membership sales in July 2023 to become hospitality-led; management is pivoting portfolio rather than defending the old mix.)
- **Fashion Risk**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: Nothing in the four calls credits growth to a trending product, brand moment or celebrity; demand is functional and repeat — corporate travel (+21% in Q3, ~1,100 corporates), education forex, remittance and B2B DMS, with B2B alone about 80% of the travel segment. (The long view had said: Leisure demand rests on destination fashion that moves fast — China up 10x, Vietnam +30%, Japan +15%, with UAE and CIS volumes dropping to zero — and growth is partly credited to trend and celebrity-led product (Anuradha Paudwal spiritual packages); no single destination bet dominates.)

**That worsened:**

- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Every call frames forex performance against RBI's LRS data, and policy directly moves the P&L: TCS rates on overseas packages (20%/5% cut to a flat 2% in the Feb 2026 budget) and on education/medical LRS, H1B visa escalation driving a 22% study-abroad contraction, airspace closures and travel advisories flattening the India DMS business. Demand in the highest-margin segment is set by regulator and policy rather than by the company. (The long view had said: Policy repeatedly moves the P&L — TCS on overseas packages cut from 20%/5% to 2%, RBI LRS caps and data framing the forex outlook, visa regimes and airspace closures throttling long-haul and inbound, and a one-off INR 100 crore government MICE order whose absence was a named cause of the 18% Q4 B2B decline; but revenue and pricing are not regulator-set and the business is not anchored in fixed infrastructure.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
