### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 2 worsened, 1 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The company is committing a very large capex to blast-furnace-based capacity while itself naming hydrogen steelmaking and giga/high-strength steels replacing aluminium as areas it needs partners for — a decarbonisation shift that would strand BF infrastructure, though nothing in the call shows it happening now. (The long view had said: The expansion is overwhelmingly blast-furnace based just as the industry faces a decarbonisation transition; an analyst pressed exactly on this, asking how far India is investing in scrap-based EAF and whether BF will keep dictating expansion for a decade. Management defends BF on scrap scarcity and cost — a reasonable answer, but it means a very large new asset base is being built around the incumbent method.)
- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: The two structurally new entrants into Indian steel this year — JFE and POSCO — have been co-opted as 50-50 JV partners on JSW's own sites rather than arriving as competitors, and management explicitly dismissed conflict between them; JSW is also building its own new-format channel. (The long view had said: Structurally cheaper imported steel from China and Vietnam is the entrant that resets economics — it made India a net importer for two years until duty reversed it, and management tracks Vietnamese/Chinese price hikes as a driver of its own pricing. Domestically, capacity is being added rapidly by many players, including POSCO's own India ambitions, though JSW has converted that particular entrant into a partner.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Commodity hot-rolled coil is inherently a good-enough good competing on price against imports — the entire price recovery narrative depends on a duty keeping cheaper Chinese/Vietnamese steel out, and management benchmarks itself purely on $/tonne versus Europe, US and Korea rather than on any brand premium. (The long view had said: Commodity HRC is inherently a good-enough product with no end-customer brand preference — the entire safeguard-duty discussion exists because cheaper Chinese/Vietnamese steel at ~$510-560 FOB sets the domestic ceiling, and management explicitly says international prices 'will always be a guiding factor' for Indian prices. The defence is deliberate premiumisation rather than any structural moat.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Pure commodity steel: management frames the quarter around coking coal cost swings ($16/t up, guiding $12-15/t higher in Q1), NSR movements of Rs.3,800/t, price 'range bound', and China supply-demand balance — and is committing Rs.2,26,000 crores of capex to go from ~30mt to 62mt (78mt with JVs) into an assumed decade-long demand upcycle, the textbook expansionary over-investment pattern. (The long view had said: This is a pure commodity steel cycle business: management frames the quarter through coking coal ($16/t up, $12-15/t guided higher), iron ore, NSR moves and international HRC benchmarks, and admits raw materials that make up ~60% of COGS are cyclical. On top of that it is committing roughly Rs.2.26 lakh crore of capex to more than double capacity to 62-78mt by FY32 — classic expansionary over-investment into a demand forecast, with margins already swinging (Q4 EBITDA margin 19%, FY26 normalised PAT only ~Rs.8,700cr on ~Rs.1.9 lakh cr of revenue).)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Exposure is on the supplier side rather than customers — imported coking coal and iron ore are ~60% of COGS and management fielded questions on global iron ore majors ramping exports to JSW; the customer base is broad (auto, packaging, infra, retail branded ~50% of retail sales), so no single-relationship dependence is evident. (The long view had said: No single customer, licensor or supplier drives results: demand is spread across infrastructure, construction, automotive, renewables, appliances and packaging, with branded retail at ~50% of retail sales and a growing JSW One digital channel (GMV Rs.6,200cr, +57%). Supply is being deliberately de-concentrated through captive mines across Goa, Karnataka, Odisha, Mozambique and Australia.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
