### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 6. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 4 strengthened, 1 weakened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is a genuine premiumisation push — premium share rising 22%→23%→25%, the Weather365 premium-plus launch, and Rs 10-15 (South) / Rs 15-20 (East) increases passed on against petcoke at ~$160/ton — but this is cost-recovery pricing in a commodity, and the same window shows a ~4% quarter-on-quarter NSR drop from price correction and 100% pass-through of the GST cut, so pricing power is conditional on cycle, not structural. (The long view had said: Premiumisation is a real and rising lever (premium share 24%→25%, Weather365 launch, discount rationalisation, +6% quarter-on-quarter realisation in Q1 FY27), but for most of the period pricing power was absent — management repeatedly flagged multi-quarter price deflation and could not pass through fuel inflation.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: One named new product per year with a stated commercial purpose (Weather365 premium-plus, launched Q4 FY26 and reported as well-received in Q1 FY27, with premium share reaching 25%), plus process innovation in fuel mix and renewables — but no R&D spend is disclosed, no pipeline is described, and there is no claim to a share of industry R&D. (The long view had said: Innovation is process- and sustainability-led (83% blended ratio, 471 kg/t net emissions among the lowest globally, floating solar, WHRS) with occasional named premium products, but no R&D spend, pipeline or share-of-industry-R&D is ever disclosed.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Volume growth outran the industry in three consecutive quarters — 10% in Q3 FY26 and 9% in Q1 FY27 against industry growth of ~6-8% — while capacity moves from ~50 mt toward 61.5 mt, then 67 mt by Q3 FY28 via the Jaypee acquisition, and the share gain is coming with rising, not falling, quality (premium mix 22%→25%, core-cement ROCE 9.9%→12.1%), not bought with price. (The long view had said: Management targets 1.5x industry growth and pan-India scale (46.6mt → ~50mt → 67mt by FY28 including the Jaypee assets), and volumes grew 27% in FY23 and 9% in Q1 FY27, but analysts flagged continued share loss in recent years and management pivoted to 'profitable volume growth' rather than defending share numbers.)
- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Promises made in earlier calls in this window are delivered in later ones (Umrangso clinker trial run in Sep 2025 → commercial production by Q3 FY26; Belgaum tracking ahead of schedule; Chunar and Rewa running within 50 days of the Jaypee close), bad news is disclosed promptly and specifically (incentive accrual guidance cut Rs 300cr→Rs 240cr, Q4 project slippage, Q3 price correction), the CFO succession was pre-announced and phased over 16 months, and management measures itself explicitly on ROCE and balance-sheet strength — with the founding family in the MD/Chairman seats throughout. (The long view had said: Strong on the disclosure and capital-discipline side — promptly provisioned INR113 crores against JAL, kept net debt/EBITDA at 0.17x–1.47x versus a stated 2x framework, delivered the multi-year cost guidance, and executed a 16-month seamless CFO transition — but the capacity promises slipped repeatedly (75mt moved from FY27 to FY28, expansion locations deferred 'after 12 months').)

**That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across all four calls revenue is described purely as tons sold × realization (volume growth, NSR, EBITDA/ton); there is no licence, AMC, subscription or renewal stream, and the only 'advance' funding discussed is government incentive receivables (Rs 776–822 crores outstanding) that the company is owed, not customer prepayments. (The long view had said: the calls are silent and the numbers are mixed (2 of 3 checks supporting).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (LIKELY FIT); Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 2 worsened, 4 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: Rather than defending against a structurally different entrant, management describes the industry moving the other way — consolidation accelerating — and Dalmia is itself a consolidator, buying 5.2mt of Jaypee capacity and integrating it in 50 days; discounting is discussed as regional price softening in East/South, not as a response to a new-format competitor. (The long view had said: Adani's entry via the Holcim exit reset industry structure — top-4 share jumped from 48% to 55% in 15 months — and management is asked about it repeatedly and answers reframing rather than denying, while prices stayed soft for 7-8 months and Dalmia conceded 'continued market share loss' in FY26; the entrant is a deep-pocketed adjacent conglomerate, not a new format, and Dalmia is competing for the same assets.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Cement is a low-complexity product where the dealer/mason largely steers the buyer, and roughly three-quarters of Dalmia's volume is still non-premium; prices in its core East and South markets softened beyond the GST pass-through in Q3 FY26 and management's answer is brand refresh, dealer conferences and premiumisation — the classic posture of defending a premium against cheaper equivalents. (The long view had said: Cement is a near-commodity where the dealer, not the end consumer, largely steers the brand choice, and management's language shows constant defence of price: 'rationalization of discount schemes', 'brand mix and price positioning', a brand-identity refresh, and a premium product line that still covers only ~24-25% of volume — meaning three-quarters of sales compete on price, and NSR fell with sector prices in FY25 despite these efforts.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Cement is a price-taking commodity and all four calls frame results around swings in realisation and input prices — NSR fell ~4% quarter-on-quarter in Q3 FY26, petcoke spiked from $99-100/t to ~$160/t on the West Asia conflict, and EBITDA margin moved from 14.1% to 20.4% year-on-year in Q2 FY26; meanwhile the company is committing ~Rs 3,200-3,400 crore/yr of capex to go from ~50mt to 67mt and an ambition of 110-130mt by 2031, i.e. heavy expansion into a cycle whose peak management admits it cannot forecast. (The long view had said: Cement is a classic construction/commodity cycle: across the 18 calls management explains results almost entirely through petcoke/coal prices ($218/t in Q1FY23, $106 in Q1FY25, back to $160 after the West Asia conflict) and swinging regional cement prices, with EBITDA/t moving from ~Rs901 to Rs1,055 and pricing weak for 7-8 straight months in FY25; simultaneously the company is in a heavy multi-year capex cycle (49mt to 75mt to 110-130mt, Rs3,200-3,400cr capex a year plus the Rs2,850cr Jaypee buy) funded into an uncertain price environment.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: NO SIGNAL → LOW. Why now: Demand is functional construction volume growing 7-9% with no volume decline attributed to changing habits; where preferences are shifting — toward premium and lower-carbon product — Dalmia is on the right side of it, lifting premium share from 22% to 25% and running 48% renewable energy in its consumption mix. (The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and the only computed fingerprint is clean.)

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (LOW); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
