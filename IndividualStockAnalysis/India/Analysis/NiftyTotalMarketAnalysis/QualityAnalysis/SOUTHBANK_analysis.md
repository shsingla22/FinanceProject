# South Indian Bank (SOUTHBANK) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

South Indian Bank is an Indian commercial bank, headquartered in Thrissur, Kerala and listed on the NSE and BSE, that takes deposits from the public and lends the money out. It gathers savings, current and term deposits from retail customers, small businesses and non-resident Indians (NRI deposits were about 31% of total deposits in mid-2022, and it also runs an NRI remittance business), and lends against gold, homes, vehicles, property, farm activity and working capital, plus loans to companies from small MSMEs up to large highly-rated corporates. It earns mainly the spread between loan yields and deposit costs — net interest income of roughly INR3,437 crores for FY26 on a net interest margin around 2.9-3.2% — topped up by fee income, card spends and treasury/investment gains. By June 2026 it had grown to roughly INR1,04,368 crores of gross advances and INR1,25,817 crores of deposits (about INR2,30,185 crores of total business), run through a branch network and increasingly through digital channels.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Deposits / Retail Liabilities (CASA, retail and NRI deposits)** — Collects current, savings and term deposits from households, businesses and non-resident Indians — the bank's funding base, INR1,25,817 crores by June 2026 with CASA of INR41,496 crores, and management treats cheap CASA and shrinking bulk deposits as the main lever on its cost of funds.
- **Gold Loan** — Lends against pledged household gold at an average loan-to-value in the 57-77% range and ticket sizes of roughly INR1.3-2.9 lakhs; called out on nearly every call as the standout grower, from INR11,961 crores in June 2022 to INR24,930 crores by June 2026.
- **Corporate / Wholesale banking** — Loans to large companies, deliberately concentrated in 'A and above' rated borrowers (88% of the book by June 2022) — once about 42% of total advances but low-yielding and short-duration, and management has been shrinking its share in favour of retail and MSME.
- **MSME / Business Banking (SME, business loans)** — Working-capital and business loans to small and medium enterprises, described as a core focus area; the business loan book was INR14,391 crores in June 2026, growing about 15-18% a year.
- **Mortgage and Home Loans** — Housing and loan-against-property lending across super-prime to affordable-housing borrowers, where management says spreads run 300-600 basis points versus the thin corporate spreads; the home loan book was INR5,138 crores in mid-2024 and mortgages grew 34% in the year to June 2026.
- **Auto Loans** — Vehicle finance, a smaller but fast-growing retail line — INR1,741 crores in mid-2024, with disbursals up 161% year-on-year then and the book up 34% by June 2026.
- **Personal Loans and Credit Cards** — Unsecured consumer lending, including pre-approved personal loans (book past INR1,100 crores by mid-2022) and a credit card launched in FY2022 that had over one lakh cards and a INR330 crores book by June 2022, with average monthly spends of about INR21,344.
- **Agri Loans** — Farm and agriculture lending, a smaller focus area where disbursals grew 108% year-on-year in the June 2024 quarter.
- **Treasury / Investments** — Manages the bank's bond and investment portfolio (INR23,489 crores in June 2022, split into held-to-maturity and available-for-sale) and trades it for gains — a swing factor in other income, contributing about INR77 crores in one quarter of FY26 and almost nothing the next.
- **Other income / fee and remittance business** — Non-interest earnings from fees, cards and NRI remittances — INR246 crores in the June 2022 quarter with core fee income of INR128 crores; management describes it as a revenue stream it is trying to broaden beyond corporate sources.
- **Recovery / stressed assets** — A dedicated function chasing recoveries, upgrades and write-offs on the older bad-loan book, which management credits for gross NPAs falling from 5.87% in June 2022 to 1.38% by June 2026.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## The verdict: Mixed — 48 out of 100 ★★☆☆☆

In one breath: the quality framework finds a weak business today (42/100); the risk review found 1 high risk (Government Dependency). Weighing those together gives 48 out of 100 — mixed.

**The exact arithmetic:** Overall = 64% × 42 (business quality) + 36% × 60 (risk safety) = 48 out of 100 → Mixed (2 stars).

- **Business quality (42/100):** The 34-check quality framework scored the business -0.30 on its −2 (poor) to +2 (excellent) scale, with 82% of checks backed by evidence; mapped onto 0–100 that is 42 points.
- **Multibagger fit (—):** The foundation test was essentially untestable (only 1 of its 3 checks had data) and no pattern shows any evidence — this pillar is left unscored rather than guessed.
- **Risk safety (60/100):** started from a clean 100; 1 high risk (Government Dependency) (−20); 5 worth watching (−20) → 60 of 100.

### The story in depth

South Indian Bank comes across as a genuinely repaired bank rather than a genuinely advantaged one. The clean-up is the clearest fact in the record: bad loans fell from 8.02% in June 2021 and 5.87% earlier to 1.38%, provision cover reached 94%, new slippages ran at 12 basis points in the first quarter of FY27 and credit costs at 3-9 basis points, all while advances grew 17%, gold loans 43-46%, mortgages 34%, MSME 15-18% and deposits 11-15%. The quality framework credits the people behind this — candor scores its highest mark, because management admits its own misses (the fee-income shortfall was called their own lack of focus) and says plainly that margin repair 'cannot be done overnight'; stewardship scores positively for organic, conservatively funded growth with capital adequacy of 19.66% almost entirely in the highest-quality form, bulk deposits halved and costs held to 5-6% growth. The multibagger screen sees the same evidence and rates Corporate Culture and Market Share Gainers as partial matches: the July 2022 promises on bad loans, provision cover, margin and growth were all met or beaten by FY26, and the growth was not bought by loosening credit standards. Growth is the only quality area with a clearly positive score.

The trouble is that the very engine driving those patterns also drives the biggest risks, because the growth is volume and mix, not price. Margin improvement is explicitly described as mix-led — moving out of low-yield short-dated corporate lending into gold, mortgage, auto and MSME — which produced a 28 basis point sequential margin gain. But the record is equally explicit that the bank cannot set prices: it passes central bank rate cuts through the next day and calls itself 'perhaps the most impacted bank', its high-quality corporate book is won on bids at thin spreads, and gold loans compete head-on with non-bank lenders on price. That single trait shows up three times over: it sinks the Pricing Power and Cost to Replicate patterns to no fit, it drags the competitive advantage and industry structure scores negative, and it is the substance of both the Government Dependency risk, rated high, and the Good Enough Goods risk, where roughly 42% of the book sits in low-yielding corporate assets holding the margin near 2.9-3.3%. Return on capital scores the worst of any area at -1.0, consistent with returns on assets of about 1.0-1.17% and a margin under 3.3% with no protective moat behind them. Brand Strength and Low Price Plus are only partial for the same reason — the Kerala and non-resident deposit franchise is real and cheap funding is growing, but it buys no premium pricing.

The other risks are mostly the unfinished half of the same turnaround. Corporate lending is still about 40% of the book against a stated 30% target and was the source of the historic damage of over Rs.4,500 crores of legacy slippages, so Stakeholder Concentration stays on watch even with 88% of that book rated A and above and short-dated assets that roll off quickly; non-resident deposits at 31% of the total add a second concentration, with management itself flagging uncertainty in the Persian Gulf. Meanwhile the cost of getting safe was a freeze on growth spending: branches were frozen and slightly shrunk for three years with only a token restart in FY27, which is why capex-for-growth and geographic expansion both score negative, why the franchise stays concentrated in the southern peninsula, and why the Shifting Consumer Preferences and Technological Innovation risks sit on watch — customers are moving to digital while the bank describes branches as 'currently the only distribution channel that we have' and is mid-way through multi-year system replacements. The Innovation Dominance pattern is rated partial for exactly this reason: the new systems and products are catch-up infrastructure a funded newcomer could rebuild. The combined rating of 48 out of 100, from a business-quality score of 42 and a risk-safety score of 60, reads as a fair reflection: a well-run, well-capitalised, cleanly de-risked bank operating in secularly growing end markets, but without pricing power, and resilience is recorded as unknown.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** As a regulated bank, pricing, capital and even leadership are set by the regulator: loan yields reprice T+1 to RBI's repo decisions ('we were perhaps the most impacted bank'), the CEO succession requires RBI approval within RBI's timeframe, and income lines include PSLC sales tied to priority-sector rules. There is no diversification away from this — it is structural to the charter.

> "we give effect to a repo rate change on a T+1 basis. So if rates go down, we are the most impacted... it's in line with RBI's policy of rate transmission" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Bank earnings swing with the credit and rate cycle: legacy corporate slippages of >Rs.4,500 crores forced a multi-year clean-up (GNPA 8.02% in Jun-2021), NIM was compressed by repo cuts and then clawed back 28bps sequentially in Q1FY27, and treasury income collapsed from INR77 crores in Q3FY26 to almost nil in Q4FY26. Demand is flow-like (deposits, gold, MSME, retail operating credit) rather than tied to customer capex, and the shift to granular retail/MSME plus 19.6% CRAR and 94% PCR bounds the exposure.

> "in Q4, due to the market conditions, we were not able to generate much income over in the Treasury segment. So in Q3, we had an income of around INR77 crores, so that is almost nil in Q4" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/operations-linked lending (gold, MSME, retail), granular book replacing lumpy corporate, high capital and provision buffers; gold LTV managed via VaR and margin calls through the Jan/Feb-2026 gold price drop*
