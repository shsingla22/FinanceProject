# Federal Bank Ltd. (FEDERALBNK) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

Federal Bank is an Indian commercial bank, headquartered at Federal Towers in Aluva, Kerala, with a legacy the management calls 90 years old and shares listed on the NSE and BSE. It takes deposits from the public through a branch network it keeps expanding (about 65 branches added in one half-year, six in a single recent quarter) and lends that money out to individuals, small and mid-sized businesses, and large corporates; a distinctive strength management repeatedly calls its "moat" is deposits from non-resident Indians (NRE deposits crossed INR 1 lakh crore by March 2026). It earns money mainly on the spread between what it pays depositors and what it charges borrowers — net interest income of INR 2,652 crore in Q3 FY26 at a net interest margin of about 3.18% — plus fee income (INR 896 crore in that quarter, growing ~19% year-on-year) from cross-sell and other services, with a treasury operation that manages investments and can swing results with bond-market moves. As of March 2026 total business was about INR 5.79 lakh crore (deposits roughly INR 2.98 lakh crore, gross advances INR 2.68 lakh crore), and quarterly net profit reached a record INR 1,145 crore; it also owns a listed subsidiary, Fedfina, and has agreed a strategic investment from Blackstone.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Deposits / liability franchise (CASA, retail and NRI deposits)** — The money-gathering side of the bank — current and savings accounts plus term deposits, overwhelmingly retail in nature (management says "high 90s" percent retail), with CASA balances crossing INR 1 lakh crore and a CASA ratio of about 32.9% that management calls among the best in the industry; the NRI/NRE book, also above INR 1 lakh crore, is described as the bank's key differentiator.
- **Retail banking** — Everyday lending to individuals — housing and personal loans, credit cards, and related products — growing about 15% year-on-year and one of the larger pieces of the loan book.
- **Commercial banking (mid-market / medium lending)** — Loans to mid-sized companies; management calls it the "primary growth engine," growing roughly 26% year-on-year through FY26.
- **Business banking** — Lending to smaller businesses; it was weak at the start of FY26 but turned around, growing about 4-6% quarter-on-quarter by late FY26.
- **Corporate and institutional banking (wholesale banking)** — Large-ticket lending and banking services to big companies and institutions, headed by a dedicated country head, growing about 14% year-on-year.
- **Gold loans** — Loans against gold, described as one of the bank's medium-lending portfolios, growing roughly 26% year-on-year even while part of the book was deliberately shrunk to fit new regulatory rules.
- **Loan against property (LAP)** — Secured loans backed by property, one of the fastest-expanding books late in FY26 at about 8% growth in a single quarter.
- **Agriculture and microfinance** — Farm lending and very small-ticket loans, both described as new-age/newer businesses that gained traction after COVID and grew 5% and about 7% quarter-on-quarter respectively.
- **Commercial vehicle / commercial equipment (CV/CE) finance** — Loans for trucks and business equipment, another of the newer businesses management has been scaling, up about 8.5% in the March 2026 quarter.
- **Treasury** — Manages the bank's own investments and funding; run by a dedicated group president and head, it can add to or subtract from profits depending on bond-market rates, which management flags as outside its control.
- **Fee income and wealth management** — Non-interest earnings from services and cross-sell — a record INR 896 crore in Q3 FY26 — now being extended by a wealth management business launched in the March 2026 quarter to serve mass-affluent customers and deepen the fee pool.
- **Fedfina (subsidiary)** — A separately listed subsidiary whose IPO management called a standout success and which reports its own quarterly numbers; it contributes to the group but is not the bank's core operation.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## The verdict: Decent — 56 out of 100 ★★★☆☆

In one breath: the quality framework finds an average business today (56/100); the risk review found 1 high risk (Government Dependency). Weighing those together gives 56 out of 100 — decent.

**The exact arithmetic:** Overall = 64% × 56 (business quality) + 36% × 56 (risk safety) = 56 out of 100 → Decent (3 stars).

- **Business quality (56/100):** The 34-check quality framework scored the business +0.24 on its −2 (poor) to +2 (excellent) scale, with 85% of checks backed by evidence; mapped onto 0–100 that is 56 points.
- **Multibagger fit (—):** The foundation test was essentially untestable (only 1 of its 3 checks had data) and no pattern shows any evidence — this pillar is left unscored rather than guessed.
- **Risk safety (56/100):** started from a clean 100; 1 high risk (Government Dependency) (−20); 6 worth watching (−24) → 56 of 100.

### The story in depth

Federal Bank comes across as a well-run bank that has been steadily improving itself rather than a business with a structural edge. The strongest marks in the quality framework are all about the people running it: management scored highest of any area, with top marks for candour (admitting mistakes, disclosing wasted account openings and fraud filtering, flagging that microfinance has not fully recovered) and for being independent and tenacious (a multi-year plan carried across two chief executives that lifted return on assets from 0.7% to about 1.4%). That patience shows up in the numbers the bank can control: bad loans at a decade best (gross 1.62%, net 0.37%), credit cost at 47 basis points, a low-cost current and savings account ratio rebuilt to 32.94% while cost of deposits fell to 5.43%, and fee income at a record INR990.92 crore, up 24% year-on-year. Growth scored full marks — a steady branch build-out (65 branches in the first half of FY24, 39 in the fourth quarter of FY26) alongside rising returns, and a small ~1.2-1.3% share of national credit that leaves plenty of room to grow. The culture pattern in the multibagger screen reads the same way: bad news flagged early, a voluntary INR456 crore buffer set aside from a one-off gain, provision cover held near 75%, and credit cost beating its own guidance.

What is missing is the thing that would turn good management into a durable advantage. Competitive advantage, industry structure and return on capital all scored at or below zero: deposits and loans are price-sensitive commodities, margins sit near 3.2%, return on assets is 1.24% and return on equity 12.47% even in a record quarter, and management itself concedes this has never been a high-margin bank and that it cannot price its way out of the market. This single fact explains why nine of the eleven multibagger patterns came back only PARTIAL and the two most powerful ones — pricing power and toll roads — came back as no fit at all. It also drives the risk review's only HIGH RISK verdict, government dependency: because loans are linked to the policy rate and reprice the day after a change, the 125 basis points of rate cuts in FY26 flowed straight through to margin with nothing the bank could do about it, and regulatory decisions have repeatedly forced changes to the business. The same commodity nature feeds three more watch-rated risks — good enough goods, new entrants and cyclicality — because cheaper rivals set the price and Federal's honest response has been to give up volume rather than match it: the corporate book flat, business banking up only 6%, bulk deposits deliberately shrunk, and headline loan and deposit growth in FY26 running below the system by choice. That choice protects credit quality and is consistent with the discipline noted above, but it is the reason the market-share pattern is only partly satisfied.

Two of the bank's best assets are also its named vulnerabilities, and this is the heart of the story. First, the non-resident Indian and Gulf remittance franchise — NRE deposits past INR1 lakh crore with rising share, built over decades and called by management 'our moat' — is the one thing a well-funded newcomer would struggle to rebuild, so it powers the cost-to-replicate, brand and market-share patterns. It is simultaneously the stakeholder concentration risk, with analysts pressing directly on Gulf job losses and regional conflict; management's answer is that resident savings, current and term deposits are now growing even faster. Second, the granular retail deposit base that makes earnings feel annuity-like and funds the bank cheaply is the same pool that the shifting-consumer-preferences risk says is structurally draining into capital markets, a drag management has openly conceded. The fintech partnerships that create the friendly-middleman pattern are likewise the technology risk, since the bank had to cut daily partner-led account openings after filtering. Set against all this, capital allocation scored poorly for a specific reason: there is no buyback or distribution policy anywhere, and the recurring capital story is dilution — the FY23-24 raise and now the Blackstone infusion. The combined rating lands at 56 out of 100, a 'Decent' three-star business, with resilience recorded as unknown. In short: a candid, disciplined operator compounding steadily inside an industry that will not let it earn a premium price.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Fortunes are directly set by the regulator across all 17 calls: repo-linked EBLR repricing at T+1 means RBI rate decisions move NIM mechanically (125bps of FY26 cuts flowed straight through), and RBI actions repeatedly forced business changes — CD-ratio moderation to 80%, higher risk weights on unsecured, the AIF circular, gold-loan book downsizing 'in line with recent regulatory guidelines', wage/pension settlements and the coming ECL transition.

> "we have to work through that over a period of time... our own direction for ourselves is get closer to an 80% CD ratio" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Diversification of the earnings base toward fee income (record INR990.92 crores, +24% year-on-year) and non-rate levers, so ROA was defended at 1.24% despite the rate cuts*

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Bank earnings swing with the rate and credit cycle: NIM compression through FY24's high-deposit-cost phase, then recovery after the 125bps FY26 easing; MFI credit costs implied at 10-11% in Jan 2026 and restructured-book slippages in FY23 show cyclical loss content. ## What to watch

- Whether the NRI/Gulf deposit franchise keeps gaining share — it is the 'cost to replicate' pattern and the 'stakeholder concentration' risk at the same time, so job losses or regional disruption in the Gulf would remove the one asset judged genuinely hard to copy.

- Whether giving up volume to competitors stays a temporary choice — the 'new entrants' and 'good enough goods' risks, plus the flat corporate book and 6% business banking growth, are already holding the 'market share gainers' pattern to partial and pulling the growth score down.
