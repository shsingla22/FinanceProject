# Enviro Infra Engine. (EIEL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Enviro Infra Engineers (EIEL) is an Indian engineering and construction company, set up in 2009, that designs, builds, and then runs water and wastewater treatment infrastructure — sewage treatment plants (STPs), common effluent treatment plants (CETPs) for industry, sewer networks, and water supply schemes. Almost all its customers are government: state government bodies, urban development corporations, and municipal corporations, with the money largely flowing from centrally funded schemes such as Namami Gange (100% central funding), AMRUT (25% centre / 75% state), Jal Jeevan Mission, and Swachh Bharat Mission. It earns money mainly by building projects on contract (EPC), plus long-term operation-and-maintenance fees on the plants it has built (management says O&M revenue runs at roughly 3%–5% of a project's execution value per year), and on a few 'HAM' projects it funds construction itself and is repaid through quarterly annuities from the government. It works across many Indian states — Gujarat, Rajasthan, Punjab, Karnataka, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Maharashtra, Odisha and Bihar — and since FY26 has added a renewable energy arm; FY26 revenue was about INR1,146 crores with profit of about INR188 crores, and the total order book stood at over INR6,814 crores.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Water and wastewater treatment (EPC / execution)** — The core business — designing and constructing sewage treatment plants, common effluent treatment plants, sewer networks and water supply schemes for government clients (recent examples: a 100 MLD STP, 55 MLD STP at Varanasi, 80 MLD STP at Jaipur, 25 MLD CETP at Sarigam, a INR250 crore Bhopal sewerage project, INR348 crore BUIDCO Bihar project, and INR824 crores of Pune/Nashik sanitation work); its execution order book grew from about INR1,960 crores in Dec 2024 to over INR2,733 crores by FY26-end, and it is described throughout as the company's primary segment.
- **Operation and maintenance (O&M)** — Long-term contracts to run and maintain the plants it has built, typically stretching about 10 years, which management values at roughly 3%–5% of a project's execution cost per year and calls a source of steady, visible revenue; the water/wastewater O&M order book moved from around INR750 crores (Dec 2024) to about INR951 crores by FY26.
- **HAM (Hybrid Annuity Model) projects** — A smaller set of projects where EIEL arranges the financing and is paid back in quarterly annuities rather than upfront construction bills — the three discussed are Bareilly (~INR230 crores, completed 71 days early in July 2024), Mathura (~INR190 crores) and Saharanpur (~INR267 crores), and management said it planned to bid for more such work.
- **ZLD, tertiary treatment and water reuse** — A newer, more technically advanced line using ultrafiltration, reverse osmosis, MBR and MVR to clean water enough to be reused — entered in Q1 FY26 with a INR395 crore Zero Liquid Discharge CETP in Maharashtra, and management flags reuse of treated water by process industries and thermal power plants as a potential new revenue segment.
- **Renewable energy platform (solar, wind, BESS)** — A diversification begun in FY26 covering solar assets in Maharashtra and Odisha, wind EPC via the acquisition of Suyog Urja Limited, and battery storage — including four NTPC BESS EPC projects totalling 930 MWh worth about INR1,070 crores plus a 150 MWh Bihar project — run under both EPC and IPP (own-and-operate) models by a team of over 250 people; by FY26-end it carried an execution order book of over INR2,051 crores plus INR1,079 crores of O&M/IPP, making it roughly as large as the core water business by order book.
- **Waste-to-energy / circular economy add-ons** — Compressed biogas, biogas-to-electricity and solar generation built into its own treatment plants (for example at Jodhpur) so the plants can power themselves and cut running costs — presented as a feature that strengthens its core projects rather than as a separately sized business.

### The story in depth

Enviro Infra Engineers is a water and effluent treatment builder whose fortunes rest almost entirely on one customer type — state government bodies — and that single fact runs through nearly everything the three analyses found. The genuinely strong parts of the business are real: the end market is in permanent expansion, with NGT rules mandating zero-liquid-discharge across industrial areas, worsening water scarcity, a $22 billion reuse market and a 90 GW battery-storage pipeline nearby (structural end-market growth, score +2). Customers effectively fund the operations — cash comes back 49 days before suppliers must be paid, a negative working capital position that is a rare strength (score +2). The order book grew from Rs 1,960 crore in December 2024 to Rs 6,814 crore by FY26 against a Rs 26,000 crore AMRUT pipeline, and the company pushed into Maharashtra, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Bhopal, Bihar and Pune/Nashik. In-house process design has repeatedly opened new revenue lines — ZLD with MVR/RO, tertiary reuse, solar cells mounted on the reactor (an India first) — which functions as productive research even without a formal R&D budget.

The trouble is that the same government-tender structure that supplies the growth also caps what the company can earn from it, and this is where the patterns and the risks converge on one point. Work is awarded to the lowest bidder (L1) by a buyer who is both the specifier and the payer, which is why the industry structure check scores -2 and why four separate multibagger patterns come back NO FIT: there is no pricing power (Q4 FY26 EBITDA margin fell to 18.7% from 27.1% in Q3, with PAT margin down to 12.4%), no brand strength (no advertising spend, no premium on the name), no friendly middleman (no independent expert recommends the company to a price-insensitive buyer), and no toll-road position (the company sells the client's entire project, not a small critical input, and every order is re-competed at the next tender). After direct input costs the company keeps only Rs 35 of every Rs 100 of sales — thin pricing power, customers buying on price (intangible benefits, -1). The Low Price Plus pattern is only a PARTIAL fit for a telling reason: there is a genuine cost culture — in-house design, self-execution with no back-to-back subcontracting, manufacturing cost down about 1 percentage point in FY26 — but management refuses to pass that into lower bids, choosing to lose orders instead. That choice is visible in the numbers: growth fell from roughly 50% in H1 FY25 to 7.5% in FY26, and the company lost bids in FY26 to cheaper rivals. The same trait shows up as the Good Enough Goods risk at ELEVATED and helps explain why Market Share Gainers is only a LIKELY FIT — no market-share figure is ever quoted, and roughly 35-40% of the new order book is renewables, part of it bought rather than won.

That renewables push is where the strengths and the risks collide most directly. Within a single year FY26 brought solar assets, a 150 MWh battery project and the Suyog Urja wind-EPC acquisition, building a Rs 2,051 crore renewable order book — a rapid, non-complementary diversification with post-deal margin questions, which is why the M&A check scores -1. It does feed two partial patterns: Forward Integrators, through HAM annuities at Bareilly, Mathura and Saharanpur, solar IPP assets and a stated intent to sell reused treated water under BOT; and Recurring Revenue, through an O&M book that grew from about Rs 750 crore in December 2024 to Rs 951 crore in water plus Rs 1,079 crore in renewable O&M/IPP on 5-15 year tenors — but that annuity stream is still only 3-5% of execution volume. Meanwhile four NTPC battery orders alone account for about Rs 1,070 crore of the Rs 2,051 crore renewable book, so the diversification meant to reduce dependence has itself created concentration (Stakeholder Concentration, WATCH). Against all of this sits the hardest number in the record: for every Rs 100 of capital in the business, it generated minus Rs 4 of operating cash this year — a -4% cash return on capital (score -2). Growth has averaged about 48% a year but is lumpy year to year (persistence of growth, -2). Management behaviour reads honestly — the Rs 11.15 crore cyber fraud was disclosed on the call with recovery detail, both promoters gave up their pay, the FY26 revenue miss was owned outright, and Bareilly HAM was delivered 71 days early — but that does not change the structure. The technology and demand risks are genuinely LOW, since the shift toward reuse and ZLD expands the market rather than erodes it and demand rests on statutory obligations rather than fashion. The combined verdict is Mixed at 38 out of 100, with resilience STRAINED: a real business in a growing market, held back by a customer that sets the price and a balance sheet not yet turning that growth into cash.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Management states outright that every project is owned by state government bodies and centrally funded, with fortunes set by scheme budgets (JJM funding froze for a year around the election Code of Conduct), by NGT mandates creating the ZLD market, and by AMRUT DPR approvals driving the INR26,000 crores pipeline; receivables plus unbilled stood at ~INR225 crores and an analyst warned explicitly about stressed state finances. > "Our all of the projects I will say are from the state governments only" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED

**What the calls show:** Revenue is order-book-driven EPC tied entirely to government capex programmes (AMRUT, JJM, Namami Gange), so inflow timing swings the P&L: growth fell from ~50% (H1 FY25) to 7.5% in FY26 and Q4 FY26 EBITDA margin dropped to 18.7% from 27.1% in Q3, with management admitting the 35% growth guidance was missed because bid evaluation and design stages elongated. ## What to watch

- Cash return on capital: the -4% operating cash return (Return on capital check, -2) needs to turn positive — this is the single number most at odds with the order book growth.
- Margin trend after the renewable acquisitions: Q4 FY26 EBITDA at 18.7% versus 27.1% in Q3, plus the unresolved post-deal margins flagged in the M&A check (-1), will show whether the Suyog Urja and solar/BESS diversification adds or dilutes earnings.
- Bid discipline versus growth: management chose to lose FY26 orders rather than cut price (Low Price Plus, PARTIAL; Good Enough Goods, ELEVATED). If they start winning at lower margins, the cost culture stops being a strength.
- Recurring revenue share: the O&M and annuity book is still only 3-5% of execution volume (Recurring Revenue, PARTIAL). Watch whether that share rises — it is the main route out of the lumpy EPC cycle.
- Concentration inside the renewable book: four NTPC battery orders are ~Rs 1,070 crore of Rs 2,051 crore (Stakeholder Concentration, WATCH), and government scheme budgets can freeze as JJM did around the election Code of Conduct (Government Dependency, HIGH RISK).
