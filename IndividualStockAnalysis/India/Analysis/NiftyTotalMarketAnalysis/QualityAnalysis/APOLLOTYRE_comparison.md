- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: This is a large, heavily covered listed company hosting calls with Kotak, Emkay, ICICI and Elara, and it has just made itself the most visible tyre brand in India via the national cricket jersey. | The long view had said: The opposite of obscure — a heavily analyst-covered listed large cap holding quarterly calls with a dozen brokerages and sponsoring the national cricket team jersey.

Now: Nothing in the business model described across the four calls is two-sided or self-reinforcing with scale; growth comes from capacity, brand spend and dealer reach. | The long view had said: Nothing in the business model is two-sided or self-reinforcing with scale — tyres are sold through conventional OEM, replacement and export channels with no network dynamic claimed across five years of calls.

Now: There is credible product technology depth — UHP development, repeated premium OEM homologations, and AI deployment in manufacturing and logistics — but no evidence of a multi-product technological lead rivals cannot replicate. | The long view had said: There is credible applied technology (EV tyres, foam/RFID, AI in plants recognised by AWS) that wins premium fitments, but R&D intensity is not high enough to constitute a durable edge over Michelin, Bridgestone or Continental.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 4 strengthened, 0 weakened, 7 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Consistent across all four calls: a working dual-brand structure (Apollo mass, Vredestein premium with record volumes each quarter), a landmark national-scale A&P commitment in the BCCI jersey sponsorship whose cost was visibly absorbed in Q3/Q4 margins, and management attributing the Q3 consumer-tyre outperformance directly to that brand visibility. (The long view had said: Real, sustained brand investment across the period — Vredestein positioned as a European premium brand with record volumes and UHP mix rising 48%→52%, Fisichella as ambassador, the BCCI jersey sponsorship lifting FY26 India growth — but A&P is only ~2% of sales rising to 2.5%, and the brand does not deliver pricing independence.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: R&D is a named pillar every quarter with concrete outputs — Aspire 5 UHP launch, repeated podium finishes in independent European tests, and fresh OEM approvals from BMW, MINI, Genesis, KIA and Mahindra — but no R&D spend figure, growth rate or share-of-industry-R&D is disclosed, and gross margins are raw-material-driven rather than innovation-funded. (The long view had said: R&D is a named pillar in every call with concrete outputs — EV tyre ranges (2022), supplies to a marquee German OEM in India (2024), BMW/MINI/Genesis/KIA approvals and European test podiums (2026) — but R&D spend is never quantified, gross margins are commodity-driven, and innovation has not translated into pricing power.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The dealer channel is clearly the route to market and management tracks dealer sentiment and distribution reach as an outcome of brand spend, but there is no discussion of trade training programmes or professional-prescriber lock-in, and the marketing push (BCCI jersey, consumer campaign) is aimed at the end customer rather than the intermediary. (The long view had said: Dealers appear only in passing (BCCI sponsorship 'boosted dealer morale', 'adding distribution', a B2B e-commerce rollout in Europe), with no discussion of trade training, prescriber-driven specification, or channel switching costs.)
- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Over the 12 months price moves are cost-push, not value-led: no pricing action in Q1 FY26 because raw materials fell, then 6–8% increases announced in Q4 FY26 purely to chase high-teens sequential raw-material inflation with margins still guided down. The premiumisation mix shift and record Vredestein volumes are genuine, but the calls do not show price rising above cost inflation without volume loss. (The long view had said: Price increases are reactive cost pass-through, chronically lagging (Aug 2024: still short of recovering Q1 RM, needing 'two to three price increases'; May 2026: 6-8% taken against ~20% RM inflation with more rounds still needed), and when they priced ahead of peers in FY25 they lost volume and share.)

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
