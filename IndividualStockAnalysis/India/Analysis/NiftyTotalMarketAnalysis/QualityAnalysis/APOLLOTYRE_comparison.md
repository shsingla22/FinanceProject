And from the calls: Entry barriers are high — INR 5,800 cr to add one plant's capacity — and pricing behaviour is rational, with cost inflation being passed through rather than absorbed in a discount war, though Europe's structurally weak market forced capacity closure. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.3 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Capital intensity is a real barrier (INR 5,800 crore for one plant expansion) and stable promoter ownership persists, but pricing rationality is imperfect — price increases are only taken in step with competitors and peers have run share-grab strategies.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: The demand drivers cited are cyclical and policy-led (GST cut, post-monsoon infra/mining rebound) rather than secular; tyres remain a mature, GDP-linked end market with Europe in outright decline. | The long view had said: The Indian replacement and OE tyre market shows durable secular expansion — replacement demand described as rarely declining, plus EV and premiumisation tailwinds prompting a three-year capacity programme.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Neutral → Weak. Now: FY26 growth was overwhelmingly volume-led (mid-teens to high-teens volume growth) with management explicitly ruling out pricing action for most of the year, and the Q4 6-8% price increases are defensive raw-material pass-through rather than pricing power; only the UHP/Vredestein mix shift is a positive. | The long view had said: Mix genuinely improves (UHP 52% vs 48%, replacement/premium OE skew) but recent growth is volume-led and price actions are reactive raw-material pass-through taken only in line with competition.

Now: The overseas footprint contracted rather than expanded this year — Europe revenue was flat-to-down all four quarters with the Enschede plant being closed, and exports were hit by West Asia geopolitics. | The long view had said: Europe gives a genuine premium platform (Vredestein at record volumes, UHP mix 52%, Hungary PCR expansion) but the region has been flat-to-negative for two years, forcing the Enschede plant closure, and US exports are weak.

Now: Evidence is mixed within the year — Q1 saw lost PCR OEM accounts and exports where peers outperformed, while Q4 claimed performance in line with and in some segments ahead of the market and European PCR outperformance. | The long view had said: After conceding share in FY25 while chasing profitability, the company reported FY26 share gains in TBR replacement/overall and PCR replacement with mid-to-high-teens volume growth, and remains well short of dominance so runway exists.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 6 strengthened, 0 weakened, 5 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Mix-led premiumisation is real (Europe UHP mix 46%→52%, Vredestein record volumes several quarters running), but pricing is explicitly raw-material-driven in both directions — no action when RM fell in Q1 FY26, catch-up 6-8% increases with margins under pressure in Q4 FY26 — which is cost pass-through, not pricing above cost inflation. (The long view had said: Price increases are frequent and proactive (8% in Q1FY23, 6-8% announced in Q1FY27) but they are cost-recovery, not value capture — management admits being 'short of recovering' RM, needing 'two more rounds', and losing share when it priced ahead of peers; premiumisation and Vredestein mix are the only genuine pricing-power elements.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Dual-brand strategy with Vredestein hitting highest-ever volumes in three of the four quarters, sustained heavy A&P including the BCCI India-jersey title sponsorship, and management directly attributing Q3/Q4 consumer-tyre outperformance to that brand visibility. (The long view had said: Real and sustained brand investment — Vredestein as a European premium brand, Fisichella and BCCI jersey sponsorship, A&P rising from ~2% to ~2.5% of sales, with the FY26 cricket campaign explicitly credited for consumer-tyre growth — but there is little heritage-driven premium pricing; margins still swing with rubber prices.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Every call names R&D output — Aspire 5 UHP launch, repeated podium finishes in independent European tests, fresh approvals from BMW, MINI, Genesis, KIA and Mahindra — but no R&D spend figure, share-of-industry-R&D, or high-gross-margin flywheel is disclosed in the last-12-months calls. (The long view had said: R&D is a named pillar in every call with concrete output (EV tyre range 2022, marquee German OEM supply 2024, BMW/MINI/Genesis/KIA/Mahindra approvals 2026, podiums in European tests, AI in plants), but R&D spend is never disclosed or benchmarked and gross margins are commodity-driven rather than innovation-funded.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Dealers clearly matter as the route to market — the cricket sponsorship is credited with lifting dealer morale and adding distribution — but demand is pulled by mass consumer-facing brand spend rather than a professional prescriber choosing on the end customer's behalf, and no trade-training/switching-cost mechanism is described. (The long view had said: Dealers are mentioned only in passing (brand campaign 'boosted dealer morale', 'adding distribution'), with no discussion of trade training programmes, prescriber-led specification or channel switching costs.)
- **Low Price Plus**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: There is a genuine and itemised cost/capital discipline — Enschede closure, asset sweating, AI-led cost optimisation, capex restraint — but the strategy is premiumisation and price realisation, not passing savings through to a low price, so the low-price-plus model does not fit. (The long view had said: The stated strategy is the opposite of low-price scale — exiting unprofitable small sizes, avoiding low-margin bus OE, and premiumising mix even at the cost of volume and share (FY25 calls).)
- **Forward Integrators**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Some movement toward the customer — a new B2B e-commerce platform rolled out in Europe and digital/customer-experience investment in India — but no own-store count, franchise royalty or D2C revenue share is given in these calls. (The long view had said: The one forward-integration venture discussed, Trumigo doorstep car servicing, was written off in Aug 2024; the only later move is a B2B (not D2C) e-commerce platform in Europe, and no own-store count, franchise royalty or D2C revenue share is ever given.)

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Every one of the four calls frames margins around the raw-material cycle — Q1 FY26 gross-margin gain came from falling input costs, Q4 FY26 flags high-teens sequential raw-material inflation from West Asia geopolitics forcing 6-8% price hikes — and the board approved INR 5,800 crore of AP-plant capex plus a INR 35 billion FY27 capex (80% growth) precisely at 90% utilisation and peak demand, the classic expansionary over-investment pattern; Europe EBITDA margin swung from 10.8% to 17.9% within three quarters on operating leverage. Bounded, though, because the bulk of volume is replacement tyres — a consumable bought out of the customer's operations, not capex. (The long view had said: Across the 16 calls, margins swing violently with commodity raw material (natural rubber INR 180 → 195 → 200 → 250/kg; consolidated EBITDA margin ranging ~11.2% to 16.4%), and management repeatedly frames the outlook in terms of RM basket direction and price-hike catch-up rather than volume; OEM/CV demand swings add a customer-capex overlay (truck OE 'significantly lower than projected' in FY25), and after four years of deliberately capex-light sweating of assets the company has now announced a INR 5,800 crore expansion plus INR 3,500 crore FY27 capex at exactly the moment demand and utilisation peaked (high 80s to 90%) — the classic expansionary over-investment pattern.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); New Entrants (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
