# PN Gadgil Jewellers (PNGJL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

P N Gadgil Jewellers (PNGJL), which trades under the PNG Jewellers brand, is an Indian jewellery retailer that dates its legacy to 1832 in Sangli and is headquartered in Pune, Maharashtra. It sells gold jewellery (traditional and lightweight), studded/diamond jewellery, some silver, and plain gold bars and coins directly to retail consumers, with demand concentrated around weddings and festivals such as Akshaya Tritiya, Gudi Padwa, Dussehra and Diwali. It makes money on the spread between the gold it buys (partly financed and hedged through gold metal loans) and the price plus making charges it collects from customers — roughly 12% gross margin, about 6.6% EBITDA margin and about 3.8% net margin in FY26 on revenue of INR 10,739 crores. It began as a Maharashtra and Goa business and by March 2026 ran 78 stores across five states — Maharashtra, Goa, Madhya Pradesh, Bihar and Uttar Pradesh — through a mix of company-owned stores, franchise stores and an online channel.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Retail (COCO — company-owned, company-operated stores)** — Its own showrooms selling gold and studded jewellery to walk-in customers; management calls it the primary growth driver, at about 70-77% of sales and INR 8,131 crores of revenue in FY26 (up 51%), with same-store sales growth of 43% and roughly INR 109 crores of revenue per store.
- **Franchisee (FOCO — franchisee-owned, company-operated stores)** — Stores funded by franchise partners but run under the PNG brand, described as an asset-light way to expand into new towns; revenue of INR 1,292 crores in FY26 (up 83%), but at thin gross margins of only about 2.5-3%.
- **E-commerce** — Online jewellery sales through the company's digital channel — the smallest of the three platforms at INR 529 crores in FY26, but the fastest growing at about 105% year-on-year.
- **LiteStyle by PNG (LS by PNG)** — A separate lightweight, everyday-wear brand skewed toward diamond and studded pieces, aimed at value-conscious buyers as gold prices rose; it ran six stores as of Q3 FY26 and contributed roughly 5-6% of sales, but is called a key future growth driver because it carries higher margins.
- **Studded jewellery mix** — The diamond/stone-set portion of retail sales that management tracks separately as a margin lever — it rose to about 9.9% of sales for FY26 and is repeatedly named as a main reason gross margins improved.
- **Gold bars and coins** — Plain investment gold sold to retail customers (management stresses it is all B2C, not B2B); it jumped from 28% to 40% of the Q4 FY26 sales mix — INR 450 crores to INR 1,400 crores — but earns a structurally very thin spread and therefore drags reported gross margin down.
- **Made-to-order business** — Custom orders placed by customers, described in the first call as close to 50% of total sales, which lets the company hold less inventory per store and run stock turns above the industry.
- **Refinery business (discontinued)** — A zero-margin refining/bullion line that ran for about six months of FY26 before being stopped; management cites its removal as one of the main reasons reported gross margins later rose.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

PN Gadgil Jewellers scores 63 out of 100 — a "Decent" three-star rating built as 45% of a business-quality score of 62, 30% of a multibagger-fit score of 57, and 25% of a risk-safety score of 72. The strongest part of the story is the shop floor and the people running it. A 194-year, six-generation history in Maharashtra is the actual product being sold — customers are buying trust in purity and fair dealing — and it shows up in hard numbers: 30-40 lakh repeat customers and 92-94% of the people who walk in buying something. The business is also unusually light on assets for a jewellery retailer: every ₹1 of assets produces ₹2.16 of sales, stores cost 40-45% less to build and run than peers', and roughly 40% of Maharashtra business is made to order against customer advances, so less capital sits in display inventory. On top of that, management has done what it said it would do. The store plan laid out at the IPO was delivered leg by leg — 39 stores then, 78 across 36 cities and five states by March 2026, nine of them opened in nine days at Navratri — alongside 40% revenue growth to ₹10,739 crores, 43% same-store growth, headline ROCE of 30.5%, and revenue per store of ₹137.7 crores. Management also volunteers bad news rather than hiding it, putting exact figures on the ₹18.55 crore customs-duty hit and the ₹25 crore hedging loss, and breaking down the 230 basis point gross-margin squeeze in the March quarter unprompted. That track record is why Corporate Culture is the one pattern rated a strong fit, and why Management and Competitive Advantage are the highest-scoring quality areas.

The awkward part is that several of the traits driving the good scores are the same traits driving the warnings. The heritage brand is genuinely impossible to buy or copy, but the Cost to Replicate pattern is only a partial fit precisely because that trust does not travel — moving into Uttar Pradesh, Bihar and Madhya Pradesh required a new national face (Ranbir Kapoor) and, more expensively, discounts to buy the first customers. Those discounts are exactly what the New Entrants risk is flagging: March-quarter gross margin dropped to 9.7%, with over ₹50 crores of trade discounts and promotions, including a flat ₹399-per-gram making charge that cost ₹40-45 crores. So the same expansion that makes Market Share Gainers and Forward Integrators look like fits is what makes Pricing Power a clear no-fit — margin improvement across the year came from mix and hedging, not from charging more, and when volumes needed defending the company cut price. The same doubling-back appears on working capital. Customer advances on made-to-order pieces are the closest thing here to a recurring-revenue trait and are the reason that pattern rates partial rather than no-fit; yet the working-capital check still scores negative, with about 121 days of cash tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. And the sharpest contradiction is on returns: the headline ROCE of 30.5% sits beside a headline return-on-capital check of -2, because for every ₹100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings, the business produced ₹-19 of operating cash this year. Growth is being funded partly by cash going out the door, which is why Capital Allocation scores 0.25 and Return on Capital scores 0.0 while Competitive Advantage and Customer Benefits score 1.0 and 1.25.

The risk review rates five of eight channels as watch and none as high, giving the best of the three component scores at 72, but the overall resilience reads STRAINED — and the reason is that the watch items reinforce each other rather than sitting apart. Earnings move with the gold price, not with any customer's spending plans: gold rose about 35% year-on-year in one quarter and over 70% in another, and management keeps describing demand as a trade-off between volume and value. High prices then push the mix around, which is the Shifting Consumer Preferences risk in action — bars and coins jumped from 28% to 40% of March-quarter sales, from ₹450 crores to ₹1,400 crores, as buyers moved from wearing gold to investing in it, and because bars carry thinner margins that alone compressed gross margin by roughly 150 basis points. Underneath both sits the Good Enough Goods problem: the company's profit is the making charge on a metal whose value the customer can look up independently, so when prices spike there is little to defend except the brand and the willingness to discount. Government policy adds a third push in the same direction, with customs duty changes repeatedly moving reported profit and expected to reshape the bars-versus-jewellery mix. The offsets are real but partial — hedging has gone from 57% to 67% with a target of 75-80%, the lightweight LiteStyle range and a 52% rise in studded jewellery are deliberate answers to the mix shift, e-commerce is up 105% to ₹529 crores, and franchise revenue is up 83% to ₹1,292 crores. Meanwhile three risks are genuinely low: no single customer, supplier or partner controls the outcome; demand is tied to weddings and festivals rather than fashion cycles; and nothing in seven earnings calls suggests a technology shift threatening the model. The honest read is a well-run, trusted, fast-growing retailer whose economics are hostage to the gold cycle and whose expansion is currently being paid for in margin and cash.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** no risk channel shows both meaningful evidence and severity today. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Revenue and margins are driven by the gold price cycle rather than by any customer's capex — gold rose ~35% year-on-year in Q1 FY26 and >70% year-on-year in Q3 FY26, and management repeatedly frames demand as volume-versus-value trade-offs, while hedging swings (Rs 25 cr hedging loss in Q1 FY26; unhedged gains of Rs 7.4 cr, Rs 45 cr and Rs 20 cr across Q4 FY25/Q3 FY26/Q4 FY26) visibly moved reported margins. Underlying demand is consumer, festival- and wedding-led (a flow, repeat-purchase base) rather than a capex cycle, and hedging has been raised from 55-57% to 67% with a target of 75-80%, which bounds the exposure.

> "this quarter gold prices continued their upward trajectory, marking a rise of over 70% year-over-year" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Occasion/wedding-led consumer demand rather than customer capex; making-charge-based income and rising hedging (57%→67%, targeting 75-80%)*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 6 years with swings of ±32 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 3% and 6% (±1.1 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 1% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 6%).

### Government Dependency — WATCH
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Customs duty and policy repeatedly move earnings: the July 2024 duty cut caused a Rs 18.55 cr profit hit (Rs 3.31 cr net), the FY26 duty increase plus 'the honourable Prime Minister's appeal' is expected to reshape the bars-versus-jewellery mix, and the discontinuation of Sovereign Gold Bonds was cited as boosting bullion demand. The business is purely domestic B2C with no government customers, tariffs or concessions, and duty is passed through to the consumer, so the dependency is on policy shocks to demand/inventory value rather than on regulated revenue.

## What to watch

- Cash returns versus headline returns — the Return on capital (headline) check is at -2 with a -19% cash return on capital while ROCE reads 30.5%; if operating cash turns positive as store additions slow, the Capital Allocation and Return on Capital areas re-rate upward.
- Whether the new-market discounting stops — the New Entrants risk is showing up as 9.7% gross margin, over ₹50 crores of promotions and the ₹399/gram flat making charge costing ₹40-45 crores; if that fades as management says it should, the Pricing Power no-fit verdict is the thing to re-test.
- The bars-and-coins mix — under Shifting Consumer Preferences, bars and coins went from 28% to 40% of quarterly sales and cost roughly 150 basis points of gross margin; a further shift toward investment gold hollows out the making-charge profit pool that Good Enough Goods already flags.
- Hedging coverage reaching the stated 75-80% target — this is the main stated defence against the Cyclicality risk, and progress on it (57% to 67% so far) is a promise the Corporate Culture strong-fit verdict is partly staked on.
- The 121-day cash cycle in the Cost of working capital check — the customer-advance model that earns the Recurring Revenue partial fit should be shortening this; if the cycle lengthens as new-state stores scale, the asset-light case behind the Asset turn and Low Price Plus verdicts weakens.
