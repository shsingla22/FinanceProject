# Westlife Development (WESTLIFE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Westlife (listed as Westlife Development Ltd., renamed Westlife Foodworld Ltd.) runs McDonald's fast-food restaurants in India under a licence from McDonald's Corporation, to which it pays a royalty on sales (4% in FY23, rising to 4.5% in FY24 and 5% in FY25-FY26, with further increases being negotiated from FY27). It sells burgers, chicken items, fries, desserts and coffee to everyday walk-in and delivery customers, earning money from restaurant sales across a company-operated network that grew from 331 restaurants in 48 cities (June 2022) to 478 restaurants in 78 cities (March 2026). It operates only in the west and south of India, where it says it is the market leader in the west and is closing in on leadership in the south. Full-year FY26 revenue was about INR 26.3 billion with cash profit after tax of about INR 2.4 billion (9% of sales); average sales per store run around INR 61-67 million a year.

**The parts of the business:**

- **On-premises (dine-in) business** — Customers eating in the restaurant, ordered at the counter or on self-ordering kiosks; it is roughly 55-60% of sales and was the weaker half during the FY24-FY25 slowdown before returning to 6-9% year-on-year growth in FY26 on the back of the INR 99 everyday value meal.
- **Off-premises / convenience (McDelivery, takeaway and drive-thru)** — Orders taken away or delivered — through the McDonald's app, McDelivery with a 20-minute delivery promise, and third-party aggregators — contributing about 42% of sales in Q1 FY25 and described as a robust and growing growth engine; drive-thrus sit in about 20-24% of the network.
- **McCafé** — The in-store coffee and bakery counter (cookies, brownies, desserts) attached to essentially all eligible restaurants — 267 McCafés in 2022, 92% of stores by 2024 — which management wants to lift to 15-18% of a store's annual sales by 2027, now supported by a monthly coffee subscription.
- **Digital channels (mobile app, kiosks, loyalty)** — Not a separate business but the main way orders come in: digital was over 55% of system sales in FY23 and about 76% by FY26, with roughly 52 million cumulative app downloads, 3.5 million monthly active users and the My McDonald's Rewards loyalty programme.
- **Network expansion and store formats (EOTF / drive-thru)** — Building and reimaging restaurants — 'Experience of the Future' formats, McCafés and drive-thrus — which management calls a growth driver with over 30% return on the extra capital; 48 stores opened in FY26, a target of 60+ a year going forward, and a Vision 2027 goal of 580-630 restaurants.

### The story in depth

Westlife's story starts with one asset — the McDonald's brand — and everything else in the file is either an expression of it or a bill for it. The quality work scores customer-facing traits highest: after direct input costs the company keeps about ₹70 of every ₹100 of sales, gross margins run around 68–71% and improved 140 basis points in FY26 to 67.7%, and cash from customers arrives 82 days before suppliers have to be paid, so customers effectively fund the business. The multibagger screen reads the same facts as its two clearest hits: Brand Strength, backed by advertising held at roughly 5% of sales and campaigns like Real Food Real Good and the Sanjeev Kapoor and anime tie-ups; and Forward Integrators, because Westlife owns and runs its whole estate — 331 restaurants in August 2022 to 478 across 78 cities by March 2026, McCafé in over 92% of stores, drive-thrus at 24%, digital up from 55% of sales in FY23 to 76% in FY26, with 52 million app downloads and about 3.5 million monthly active users on its own McDelivery platform rather than someone else's. The decades-old cold chain, the prime sites chosen with location intelligence and the exclusive West and South India franchise right are what the Cost to Replicate pattern calls real time-based barriers.

The same owned-and-operated design that earns those two strong pattern fits is also where the risks live. Owning every restaurant means the company carries the capital, and the quality framework scores Return on Capital at zero and Competitive Advantage and Industry Structure at roughly a third — the moat shows up in the customer experience, not yet in the returns. Owning the estate under someone else's brand also means the whole business sits on a single master-franchise licence, with royalty stepping from 4% to 4.5% to 5% and open-ended renegotiation from FY27 that management says will rise progressively; that is the one risk rated ELEVATED, and it is the direct cost of the brand that makes the Brand Strength verdict strong in the first place. The pricing story splits the same way: Pricing Power is only a LIKELY FIT because the 2–4% annual policy is recovering inflation rather than charging above it, GST benefits were passed straight to customers with no margin retained, and the ₹99 everyday meal and ₹69 1+1 exist to hold footfall — which is exactly the continuous value response the Good Enough Goods risk describes, and exactly why Toll Roads is a NO FIT. That management can fund those value platforms and still hold gross margin near historic highs is the supply chain earning its keep; that it has to fund them at all is the demand pressure showing through.

Demand is where the strains concentrate, and it explains why resilience is judged STRAINED despite the operating strengths. Same-store sales swing from +97% with 17.1% EBITDA in Q1FY23 to −6.7% with about 400 basis points of margin compression in Q1FY25 and −3% in Q3FY26, which is why Market Share Gainers lands only at PARTIAL — store count compounded and FY23 claimed traffic-share gains, but the middle years show negative like-for-like sales, a persistently weak South cohort and admitted under-growth on third-party platforms. Management's response is to keep investing through the weakness — a record 48 openings in FY26, guidance raised to 60-plus a year toward a 580–630 store target, debt squared off in FY22, no acquisitions, unviable sites closed — which the framework credits as disciplined stewardship and which the Cyclicality risk credits as countercyclical, but it means new capital keeps going in while like-for-like sales are soft. Two further risks are versions of strengths: the Gen Z merchandise drops and celebrity collaborations that make the brand feel current are, in the Fashion Risk view, taste-dependent; and the shift to 76% digital that anchors the Forward Integrators fit is itself the consumer-preference migration the review flags. Add government-touched inputs — the GST change and the LPG supply disruption from 10 March 2026 — and the combined result is 61 out of 100, a Decent three-star business: a genuinely strong customer proposition and cash cycle, priced against uncertain returns on the capital it keeps deploying.
