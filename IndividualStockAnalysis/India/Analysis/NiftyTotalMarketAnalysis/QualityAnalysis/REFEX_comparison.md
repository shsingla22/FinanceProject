### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Revenue is contracted, multi-year ash & coal handling work with an open order book of ~INR1,200-1,500 crores and management repeatedly citing stickiness of contracts, but there is no product-upfront-plus-annuity model — no attach rates, AMC/licence fees, renewal rates or customer advances funding the business are discussed; contract assets are unbilled receivables (working capital drag), not deferred revenue. (The long view had said: Revenue is tender/contract-based ash and coal handling services and now wind turbine supply — no product-upfront sale with an attached licence/AMC/subscription stream, and working capital is customer-funded in the wrong direction (receivables of 3-4 months, not advances). Management talks of 'contractual stickiness' and multi-year contracts, but never of attach rates, renewals as a revenue share, or deferred revenue as funding.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The ash & coal order book grew from ~INR1,200 crores (Nov 2025) to ~INR1,500 crores (Jan and May 2026) with cumulative wind orders of INR1,860 crores, but management never cites a market share number or names competitors it is taking share from, and FY26 standalone revenue actually fell to INR2,039 crores from INR2,259 crores. (The long view had said: Persistent, delivered share gain against a fragmented unorganised field: daily ash handled went 35,000t (May 2024) → 70,000t (2025/26) with 90-95,000t targeted, presence expanded to 41-42 plants across 15 states versus single-plant regional rivals, ~20% CAGR over six years and 28% ash-segment growth in FY26 — and the gain came with margin expansion, not bought with profitability.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Nothing in the last four calls articulates why a funded newcomer could not replicate the business — ash and coal handling is contract logistics won plant by plant at 7-11% margins, and the wind vertical is a first-year entrant competing against established players on price and unproven-at-scale 5.3MW technology. (The long view had said: There is an articulable barrier — pan-India presence across 41-42 plants, 100% compliance track record that saves plants penalties, telematics systems and NTPC relationships that a single-plant regional operator cannot match — but it is a leased-truck logistics business rebuildable by a well-funded entrant, and management itself concedes competition is arriving in a wide-open TAM.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The core service earns only 7–10% (ash handling) to ~11.8% margins and is won through tenders with large power-plant buyers, and in wind management defends its position on price rather than on a protected premium — a low-differentiation offering where a cheaper adequate provider is hard to fend off. (The long view had said: Ash haulage is not a complex product and 99% of competitors are cheap local single-plant operators, so management must repeatedly justify its premium on compliance guarantees, GPS tracking and nationwide coverage rather than on a protected product; the fact that the customer is a large PSU procurement department that can tender on price limits the durability of that premium.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Revenue swung violently within the 12 months — INR366cr (Q1) to INR431cr to INR590cr to INR701cr, with EBITDA margin moving from ~10.4% to 20.1% — driven by monsoon-hit site access, cement/fly-ash offtake and coal trading volumes; the new wind vertical (INR233cr in Q4, INR1,860cr order book) is capital equipment sold into IPPs' capacity-expansion capex, the dangerous customer-cyclical form. (The long view had said: FY24 revenue fell on a ~45% plunge in coal prices, Q1 FY26 revenue dropped ~INR246 crores quarter-on-quarter on monsoon/cement demand, and quarterly EBITDA margins have swung from 7.5% to 20.1%; the new wind turbine vertical is pure customer-capex equipment sold to IPPs. Offsetting this, the core ash business is operations-linked (ash is generated whenever plants burn coal), sits on multi-year contracts with diesel escalation pass-throughs, and management has deliberately exited the commodity-price-exposed coal/power trading revenue.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
