### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Family-owned and led (Tarang Jain with sons Arjun and Dhruv as business-unit CEOs), with promises made and then visibly delivered inside the window: deleveraging from >2x net debt/EBITDA in FY23 to ~0.27x, ROCE from 12% to 24.4%, and the overseas turnaround flagged in Q2/Q3 showing up as 24% overseas growth in Q4; bad news is disclosed promptly and specifically — the VRS charge, the labour-code gratuity hit, net debt rising 'for the first time after many quarters', and working-capital slippage — alongside a raised dividend and long-term-over-short-term framing. (The long view had said: Promise-keeping is real on the balance sheet — the European four-wheeler lighting divestment closed on schedule, net debt fell from ~INR 2,700 crore (FY23) to ~INR 495 crore (FY26), ROCE reached 24.4%, dividend was raised, and bad news (EV customer receivable provision, overseas losses, VRS, net-debt increase) is disclosed promptly — but the Aug 2022 promise of 12% group EBITDA is still unmet four years later at 9.4%, and the overseas turnaround has been pushed out repeatedly ('1 to 2 years' in FY24, now H2FY27).)

**That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: LIKELY FIT → NO FIT. Why now: Revenue is program-based OEM supply won as discrete order wins with annualised peak revenue (INR 32,889 mn in FY26); no licence fees, AMC, renewals, attach rates or customer advances are discussed, and the only quasi-recurring stream — aftermarket — is a modest ~10% of revenue with no recurring-contract economics described. (The long view had said: The only annuity-like stream is the aftermarket division, described across calls as faster-growing and higher-margin at ~10% of continuing-operations revenue; there is no discussion of licences, AMCs, renewal/attach rates or customer advances funding the business — working capital is a recurring stress point, not a source of funding.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: A third of revenue is polymer body parts and another quarter ICE powertrain — capacity a well-funded competitor can rebuild — and management's own framing is about filling existing capacity and sweating assets rather than any barrier a newcomer could not cross; the overseas units' vulnerability to customer concentration underlines the absence of a replication barrier. (The long view had said: There are genuine but limited barriers — model-life programme lock-in once an order reaches SOP, 130+ patents, e-powertrain and lighting qualification with global OEMs, and speed from win to SOP — yet the economics (46% single-customer dependence, cost pass-through pricing, sub-10% group EBITDA, Romania/Italy still loss-making) show nothing a well-funded competitor could not rebuild.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
