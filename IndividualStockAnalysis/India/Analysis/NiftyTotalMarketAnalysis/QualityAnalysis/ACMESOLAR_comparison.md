The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 6 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management shows repeated first-mover business-model innovation in storage — first standalone BESS PPA (550 MWh with NHPC, signed in one month), first private-discom FDRE PPA with Tata Power, and early merchant BESS running at 88-90% round-trip efficiency and ~Rs 2.2 crores/day — but no R&D spend, pipeline or share-of-industry-R&D is disclosed, so this is deployment/structuring innovation rather than R&D-funded dominance. **That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Revenue comes from 25-year fixed-tariff PPAs and merchant BESS arbitrage, not from a front-end product sale with an attached service/licence stream; across all four FY26 calls there is no mention of attach rates, renewals, AMC share, installed-base density or customer advances, and receivables (36 days DSO) rather than deferred revenue fund the business. (The long view had said: Revenue is contracted and long-dated — 25-year PPAs, 86%+ central offtakers, grid/plant availability >99%, DSO down to 14 days — but this is an annuity from a capital asset, not a front-end product sale followed by attach/AMC/renewal income; no deferred revenue or customer advances fund the business (capex is funded by debt, IPO proceeds and refinancing).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The output is an undifferentiated commodity sold through reverse auctions where the lowest tariff wins — there is no brand or premium to defend, and management's own framing of every win is price competitiveness (Rs 2.764/unit at RUMSL, Rs 4.35/unit RTC beating thermal). Returns depend on capex and financing cost advantage rather than on any protected niche. (The long view had said: Electricity is fully commoditised and the offtaker chooses purely on discovered tariff in reverse auctions — there is no brand or premium to defend, so returns rest entirely on cost of capital and execution; management's only defence is walking away below a return threshold. Falling module/battery capex is being passed straight through to lower tariffs (the stated reason for the VGF cut).)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Management repeatedly highlights that 86% of the portfolio sits with central off-takers, and the named counterparties recur every quarter (SECI, NHPC, SJVN, RUMSL, REMC) — with SECI now designated the single REIA for bidding, further narrowing the counterparty set. Supplier concentration is comparable: BESS orders placed with Narada and Trina, and module/battery cost exposed to Chinese policy (VAT export rebate withdrawal). (The long view had said: Offtake is deliberately concentrated in a handful of central counterparties — 86% central offtakers in Q1 FY26, moving to "100% central" for the under-construction book (SECI, NHPC, REMCL) — while supply is concentrated in Chinese cells/modules and a couple of battery vendors (Narada, Trina), with management explicitly rejecting Indian assembly on warranty grounds. The China VAT-rebate withdrawal in Feb 2026 showed how directly that supply link feeds capex.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (ELEVATED); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Cyclicality (WATCH); Technological Innovation (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 8 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
