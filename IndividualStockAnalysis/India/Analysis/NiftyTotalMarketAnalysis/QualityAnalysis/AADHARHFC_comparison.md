**Checks that regressed:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Good → Weak. Now: Banks are actively encroaching on urban segments and rate-based competition is visible in the need for 'rate retention efforts', indicating limited entry barriers and price-led rivalry at the overlap. | The long view had said: The competitor set is described as stable with no new entrants named, licence plus NHB-refinance access and a physical branch footprint are real barriers, and pricing has stayed rational with spreads held at 5.7-5.97% through a full rate-cut cycle.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Weak. Now: Price is clearly not secondary for these borrowers — retention depends on matching rates, and outflows are managed through 'focused rate retention efforts' rather than through trust-based stickiness. | The long view had said: First-time EWS/LIG borrowers buy certainty of access and of getting their subsidy, where Aadhar is the acknowledged leader — the first to draw PMAY 2.0 subsidies and the largest subsidy provider with 10,000+ customers already paid.

Now: As a mortgage lender the model is structurally balance-sheet-heavy — ₹30,571 crores of AUM funded by ₹18,744 crores of borrowings — and the 44% capital adequacy means an unusually large equity base per rupee of income, the opposite of asset-light. | The long view had said: Lending is inherently balance-sheet-heavy (borrowings INR18,744 crores against a INR30,571 crore book), but returns on those assets are strong and stable at 4.4-4.8% ROA with a 5.8% spread and modest 3.1x leverage, so asset-light it is not, yet asset productivity is high.

Now: The customer base is purely retail (3.2 lakh live borrowers, 55% salaried, ₹10.9 lakh average ticket) and is served well on convenience/access, but the retail-appropriate intangible and lock-in benefits are absent given the refinance leakage. | The long view had said: The benefit set is well matched to a retail EWS/LIG base — proximity, hand-holding, subsidy facilitation and cash-flow-based branch underwriting for the self-employed — though the lock-in is weak, as the 5.6% balance-transfer outflow shows.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 4 weakened, 6 unchanged.

*Foundation test: FAIL on the long view, FAIL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Not a product-plus-service model, but the mortgage book is a long-duration contractual income stream whose stickiness management actively manages and reports on — balance-transfer-out improved 50-60 bps across FY26 via a centralised retention and data-science team. No deferred revenue/customer-advance funding; the business is funded by borrowings (INR18,744 crores at 31-Mar-26), not by customers. (The long view had said: Across all eight calls there is no discussion of attach rates, service/AMC contracts, renewals, subscriptions or deferred revenue/customer advances as a funding source; the model is spread-based lending funded by bank/NHB/NCD borrowings.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Consistent 20-22% AUM growth and 17% disbursement growth through FY26 with asset quality improving rather than deteriorating (GNPA 1.30%→1.08%, collection efficiency 99.8%, Stage-2 down 30 bps) — growth not bought with credit standards. But no explicit market-share numbers or share-versus-named-competitor data are given in any call. (The long view had said: Share gain is explicit and quantified: 16–17% rising to ~18% of the low-income HFC pool, AUM compounding 22–23% (FY24) to 20% (FY26) to INR30,571 crores, record Q4 FY26 disbursement, and number-one position on PMAY 2.0 subsidies — and crucially it was not bought with credit standards (GNPA 1.08% in FY24 and again 1.08% in FY26, credit cost held at ~25 bps, LAP deliberately slowed on tariff risk).)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Portfolio yield drifted down through FY26 (13.8% at Q2 to 13.71% at Q3) and spread expansion came from falling funding costs, not price increases; management flags elevated competitive intensity from banks and needs a dedicated retention team to stem rate-driven balance-transfer outflows. (The long view had said: Aadhar has repriced when needed (75 bps RPLR in Apr-23, another 25 bps from Jun-24) and held spreads at 5.7–5.97% through a 125 bps repo easing cycle, but with ~75% of assets floating it is obliged to pass rate cuts on, portfolio yield drifted 13.8%→13.71%, and BT-out shows customers do leave on rate — this is spread management, not true pricing power.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No premium pricing or brand-led stickiness is evidenced in the last 12 months; growth is attributed to branch expansion, policy tailwinds (PMAY 2.0, GST 2.0, repo cuts) and execution, while ~5.5% of customers refinance out annually. Awards and the CARE AA+ upgrade are credit/recognition signals, not consumer brand power. (The long view had said: There is a 2010-founded IFC-JV heritage, a consistent mission line ('Ghar Banega Toh Desh Banega'), sector awards and a CARE rating upgrade to AA+ that lowers funding cost — but no A&P spend, no price premium and no brand-led adjacency; growth is attributed to branches and PMAY rather than brand pull.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Nothing in the last 12 months articulates why a well-funded entrant could not replicate the position: management itself reports banks competing actively in urban markets and ~5.5% of the book refinancing out each year, and the assets (branches, a licensed TCS core system, analytics) are all buyable. The advantage is execution and distribution depth, not an un-replicable barrier. (The long view had said: The replication barrier is real but not insurmountable: 626 branches across 22 states and 550+ districts, ~11,000 PIN codes, 2,000 in-house collectors and four years of accumulated underwriting/behavioural data on 3.2 lakh sub-INR15-lakh borrowers is a decade of patient build — yet management itself concedes competitors are 'the same set of people... all of us are expanding reach', and banks are pressing the urban segment, so a well-funded entrant could rebuild much of it.)

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
