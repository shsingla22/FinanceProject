# SRF Ltd. (SRF) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: TEXTILES*

SRF is an India-based manufacturer that makes industrial chemicals and packaging/technical materials and sells them mainly to other businesses rather than to consumers. Its biggest earner is chemicals: refrigerant gases and related fluorochemicals (plus chloromethanes and PTFE), and custom-made specialty chemicals — mostly ingredients for crop-protection/agrochemical companies and, increasingly, pharmaceutical intermediates — supplied to global innovator customers under long-running R&D-backed relationships (506 patents applied for, 153 granted). It also makes packaging films (BOPET, BOPP), aluminium foil, and technical/coated/laminated fabrics. Revenue comes from selling these products in India and abroad — exports to the US, Southeast Asia and the Middle East matter a lot — with manufacturing in India (sites named include Dahej and a new site coming up in Odisha) and overseas film operations in Thailand, Hungary and South Africa; in the quarter ended December 2025 total revenue was about INR3,713 crore with EBIT of INR653 crore and profit of INR433 crore.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Chemicals Business (Fluorochemicals + Specialty Chemicals)** — The largest and most important segment, with revenue of INR1,825 crore in Q3 FY26 (up 22% year-on-year), covering both the fluorochemicals and specialty chemicals arms.
- **Fluorochemicals** — Makes refrigerant gases (such as R-134a), chloromethanes and PTFE, and had a record quarter helped by firm global HFC prices and China's quota-led supply cuts; new-generation refrigerant gas plants and new fluoropolymer projects are planned at the new Odisha site, with the Kigali phase-down of HFCs framed as the key long-term opportunity.
- **Specialty Chemicals** — Custom-manufactures complex molecules for global agrochemical customers (the mainstay) and, increasingly, pharma intermediates — a second pharma intermediate plant costing INR180 crore is being built at Dahej — though pricing is currently squeezed by aggressive Chinese competition and deferred orders from agro majors.
- **Performance Films & Foils** — The second-largest business at INR1,342 crore of revenue in Q3 FY26 (down 3% year-on-year, EBIT INR95 crore), making BOPET and BOPP packaging films plus aluminium foil in India and overseas (Thailand, Hungary, South Africa), positioning SRF as a 'one-stop packaging solutions provider' with new BOPP capacitor-grade and BOPP-BOPE lines coming.
- **Technical Textiles** — A smaller segment at INR454 crore of revenue in Q3 FY26, including belting fabrics used in conveyor belts, currently under margin pressure from cheap Chinese pricing and weaker conveyor belt exports to the US.
- **Other Businesses (Coated Fabrics and Laminated Fabrics)** — The smallest part, making coated fabrics (including pond/canal liners for the government's Jal Jeevan mission) and laminated fabrics, with soft demand and margin strain from cheap imports; management is pushing value-added products to protect margins.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

SRF comes out of these three reviews as a genuinely good business that has not yet been able to turn its strengths into pricing. The quality framework scores it highly on the things that build a business over years: deep process technology across fluorochemicals, next-generation gases, fluoropolymers and pharma intermediates; 506 patent applications with 153 granted; a seven-to-eight year push that finally cracked pharma; and customers who come to SRF because it is reliable on complex molecules, with Chemours handing it high-end fluoropolymer manufacturing as the opening of a deeper partnership. Sales have compounded at about 12% a year, lumpy but inside the 10-15% band the framework prizes, and roughly 22 of every 100 rupees of sales is operating profit, with margins moving only about three points either way. The overall rating lands at 50 out of 100 - 'Decent', three stars - built from 71 on business quality, 21 on multibagger fit and 48 on risk safety, with resilience judged sound. The gap between the 71 and the 21 is the whole story: the quality is real, the evidence that it converts into durable excess profit is not.

The multibagger screen makes that gap explicit. Innovation Dominance is the one likely fit, and even there the note is that the innovation 'is not yet showing up as margin or pricing power.' Three patterns are outright rejected, and they are rejected for the same underlying reason. Pricing Power fails because across FY26 the company is taking price deflation rather than putting prices up - the specialty chemicals shortfall is described as largely pricing-led, with margins falling. Market Share Gainers fails because share is being defended by conceding price, the classic case of share bought with profitability. Toll Roads fails because customers push price down hard and Chinese supply is substitutable, with no lock-in once a customer has qualified. That same trait shows up on the risk side as 'Good Enough Goods', rated elevated: cheaper substitutes are compressing price across most of the portfolio at once, in core specialty categories, in belting and coated fabrics, and in films. It is one condition seen through two lenses - the screen calls it an absent moat, the risk review calls it margin erosion - and it is consistent with the low scores on Return on Capital and Industry Structure among the quality areas.

The strongest structural advantage and the single largest risk are also the same fact. The Kigali Amendment mandates a permanent phase-down of HFCs, which creates the demand SRF is building capacity for and, through production quotas based on 2024-26 baselines, hands the company a real barrier - a well-funded newcomer starting now gets no quota from 2028. That is why 'Cost to Replicate' registers as a partial fit and why the New Entrants risk, despite Tanfac's new R32 plant and Chinese entry into SRF's specialty categories, sits only at watch. But the same regime makes policy, not management, the author of results in the largest profit driver: Government Dependency is the one high risk, with anti-dumping duties, US tariffs, EU carbon border rules and GST changes layered on top. It also makes today's HFC asset base a transitional platform, which is the Technological Innovation watch item - SRF must build entirely new plants using equipment it has never handled before. Management's answer to all of this is to keep spending: roughly INR 2,200-2,300 crore of capex, a further INR 1,500-2,000 crore first stage at Odisha for next-generation gases, and INR 180 crore for a second pharma intermediate plant, explicitly refusing to cut back during the tariff dislocation. That counter-cyclical stance earns top marks for disciplined, independent, long-term stewardship and is the stated cushion against elevated cyclicality; it is also what raises the stakes, since the spending is organic and greenfield, aimed at unfamiliar technology, and partly anchored on a single Chemours relationship - the concentration the risk review flags alongside agro majors deferring offtake and US market access shaping both refrigerant and packaging routing.
