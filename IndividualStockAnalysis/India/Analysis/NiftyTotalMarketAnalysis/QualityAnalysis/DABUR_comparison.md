## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 30 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 3 improved, 6 regressed, 21 unchanged. 2 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them). 1 was newly assessable in the recent view.

**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing behaviour is rational rather than promotional — the whole hair-oil industry passed through coconut inflation via price increases, and Dabur fully mitigated 7-10% inflation with 3-4% price hikes plus savings rather than discounting. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.3 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Evidence of irrational pricing and low entry barriers — deep discounting in coconut oil squeezing margins for the whole category, new entrants appearing in honey, and aggressive modern-trade pushes by the oral care leader.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: No R&D spend disclosure, but new formats consistently convert into share — aseptic PET coconut water took 25% of a fast-growing category, Hajmola Soft Chews/Imli Chews and new variants now contribute ~50% of the Hajmola franchise. | The long view had said: No R&D spend disclosure; innovation output is steady but modest (NPD ~4-5% of sales) with management itself admitting a string of failures (Dant Rakshak, Red Gel, Amla Aloe Vera) and COVID-contextual launches collapsing to near zero.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Core categories are oligopolies with fragmented, powerless end customers, but competitive intensity is explicitly elevated — analysts flagged 3-4 quarters of heightened toothpaste competition and hair oil is contested by Marico and Bajaj Consumer. | The long view had said: Core categories are fragmented and contested rather than stably oligopolistic — a third player entered hair oil aggressively, oral care intensity is persistently high, and modern trade/e-commerce channels add buyer power.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Neutral. Now: Management is candid on specifics — quantifying the GST hit at ~₹100 crore, calling hair-oil growth a price-driven one-off, admitting Babool has failed and that thunderstorms make him 'a worried man' — but it materially cut disclosure and deflected a direct analyst request to restore it. | The long view had said: Consistently owns failures and unflattering facts across years — naming failed launches, Babool's underperformance, primary-vs-secondary inventory distortions, and expressing dissatisfaction with its own oral care numbers rather than hiding behind category growth.
- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: The only capital-deployment news is Dabur Ventures — ₹500 crore into unproven 'new age digital-first' businesses, which is small and non-leveraging but the opposite of the preferred already-strong complementary bolt-on, with no post-deal margin evidence available in a one-year window. | The long view had said: Badshah is a small complementary bolt-on that has delivered 15-16% volume-led growth with a 500bps gross margin improvement post-deal, and Hobi/Namaste remain modest international bolt-ons.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Excellent → Good. Now: Distribution is the clearest structural asset — sustained rural outperformance, broad-based channel growth in honey, and an ongoing GTM transformation cited as a driver of forward acceleration, though no reach numbers were disclosed this year. | The long view had said: Distribution is the clearest structural edge — continuous outlet, village and rural-promoter (Yoddha) expansion, an ongoing GTM transformation, and modern-trade margins now at or above general-trade levels, repeatedly credited as the driver of broad share gains.
- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Herbal/natural consistently outgrew non-herbal by 440bps, 770bps, 530bps and ~2x across the four quarters, and GST rationalisation is accelerating an unbranded-to-branded shift in Ayurvedic medicines. | The long view had said: The herbal/natural shift is a durable secular tailwind cited consistently across years — the natural segment grew from 30% to 31% of oral care and herbal now grows 500bps (roughly 2x) faster than non-herbal, with Dabur the herbal leader.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Neutral → Weak. Now: The key leadership addition in the window was an external hire rather than an internal promotion — a newly created CEO-India role filled from outside, alongside turnover in the IR head — with no evidence of a talent-development system. | The long view had said: Management credits team execution repeatedly, but the key India CEO role in FY26 was filled by an external hire rather than an internal successor, and no systematic talent-development programme is described.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Weak. Now: There is product-format innovation (aseptic PET, soft chews, pack revamps) but no evidence of a deep, multi-product technology edge or meaningful R&D intensity; the stated advantage is branding and distribution, not technology. | The long view had said: Management claims ayurvedic formulation know-how competitors lack, but there is no R&D intensity disclosure and it admits execution/SOV, not technology, is the binding constraint (e.g. charcoal whitening 'not cutting ice').

*Unchanged: Share donators; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Convenience benefits; Profit margin; Halo effect (caution); Cyclical market growth; Security by obscurity; Advertisement and promotion; Gain in market share; Asset turn; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Return on capital (headline); Out of limelight; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Mini-monopolies; Disciplined stewards; Intangible benefits; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Cost of working capital; Assurance benefits; Geographical expansion.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 5. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 3 strengthened, 1 weakened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Every one of the four calls quantifies share gains in basis points across most categories — hair oil to an all-time-high 20% volume share, 183/127/131/243 bps in air fresheners, 195-650 bps in juices and nectars, plus toothpaste and honey — and Q1 FY26 claimed gains across 95% of the portfolio, alongside 20-30% growth in Sub-Saharan Africa, UK/EU and Bangladesh. (The long view had said: Persistent, quantified share gain is the single most consistent thread across the whole 2022-2026 record: 98% of portfolio gaining share in Q1 FY23, 95% in Q1 FY25, and in FY26 hair oils +193bps to an all-time-high 20% volume share, juices +650bps, nectars +195bps, air fresheners +131bps, Hajmola +233bps — and it is profitable share, with margins protected while outgrowing a 2.5% toothpaste category.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Price increases are taken every quarter (3-4% in Q1 FY26, another 4% announced in Q4 FY26) and operating profit and PAT grew ahead of the top line in each of the four calls; but the increases are described as mitigating 7-10% cost inflation rather than exceeding it, gross margins were 'under pressure', hair oil growth was admitted to be price- not volume-led ('one-off'), and GST cuts were passed straight through to consumers. (The long view had said: Pricing power is explicitly conditional on category leadership — strong in healthcare/foods/home care ('we are the ones who set the pricing table'), absent in hair oils and Re.1/Rs.2 shampoo where they wait for competitors and absorb margin compression; FY23 saw only 6.5% pricing against 9-10% inflation, and by Q1 FY25 pricing had collapsed to ~2%, though 4% hikes were pushed through in Q1 FY27.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Named launches carry measurable revenue impact each quarter — Hajmola's Chatcola/Limcola/Mr. Aam variants at ~50% of the franchise, aseptic-PET coconut water taking 25% of that market, plus a Rs.500 crore 'Dabur Ventures' allocation for innovation-led growth — but no R&D spend, pipeline metric or share-of-industry-R&D is disclosed, and gross margins are under pressure rather than funding outsized R&D. (The long view had said: Named launches appear every year with revenue impact and management targets ~5% of turnover from NPD, but no R&D spend or share-of-industry-R&D is ever disclosed, the innovation rate was only 3% in Q1 FY25, and management openly concedes a high failure rate (Dant Rakshak, Red Gel, Amla Aloe Vera).)

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management does own underperformance on calls (Babool 'has not done very well for us', Chyawanprash flat on trade inventory, Nepal decline) and CEO Mohit Malhotra has been consistent across the window with an orderly India-CEO addition in Q4; against that, disclosure was materially reduced in Q1 FY26 to analysts' open objection, and the Q1 'gunning towards double-digit growth' in Q2 landed at 5.4%. (The long view had said: Leadership is stable and family-anchored (Malhotra CEO, Ankush Jain CFO and Gagan Ahluwalia present on all sampled calls 2022-2026 under Burman chairmanship), and management volunteers bad news rather than burying it — naming failed launches in 2022, flagging Sarson Amla share loss to competitors in 2024, and refusing to accept a market-share-beating oral care quarter in 2026.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (QUANT SIGNAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Dabur's categories (toothpaste, hair oil, honey, juices) are not technically complex and face cheap branded, local and unbranded substitutes, with management acknowledging elevated toothpaste competitive intensity for 3-4 quarters and Babool underperforming at the mass end; against that, price increases of 3-4% and 4% were absorbed while volume market shares hit all-time highs (hair oil 20%, Odonil 44%), and GST cuts are pulling consumers from unbranded to branded. (The long view had said: Management describes real price-led erosion of premiums: competitors cutting prices in coconut oil and gaining share, Dabur's own branded-vs-unbranded premium in Sarson Amla widening to the point of needing price correction, down-trading toward base oils, and having to hold Re.1/Rs.2 shampoo price points while absorbing inflation; the economy brand Babool is also underperforming and being revamped.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The calls affirmatively show Dabur on the winning side of the preference shift: herbal/natural oral care outgrew non-herbal by 440bps, then 770bps, then 530bps, and nearly 2x in Q4, with Dabur gaining share each quarter; premiumisation (Real Activ +45%, coconut water +100%, Sundarbans/organic honey, Gur Chyawanprash) is scaling faster than the base portfolio. (The long view had said: The COVID-immunity demand base collapsed (healthcare -20% in Q1FY23, sanitizer revenue to near zero), Chyawanprash has stayed flat with analysts pressing on 'contemporization of the category' as a key challenge, and consumers switched from juices to carbonated thirst-quenchers in a hot summer; management is actively pivoting with sugar-free, Gur/Ratnaprash premium variants and planned gummies and bars.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Demand is consumption/flow-linked (toothpaste, hair oil, honey, digestives) rather than tied to any customer's capex, but the calls repeatedly show earnings swinging on weather-linked seasonal portfolios (beverages, glucose down ~30% in Q1 FY26) and on commodity input swings — coconut oil up ~100%, amla inflation, and a jump from budgeted flat inflation to ~10% in Q4 FY26 on Middle East war. (The long view had said: Across all four calls demand is daily-consumption/flow in nature (toothpaste, hair oil, digestives, air fresheners, honey) with no linkage to any customer's capex; volatility that does appear is input-cost and weather-driven (coconut oil spiking from ~INR130 to ~INR400, summer-dependent glucose and juices), which management absorbs through calibrated price increases rather than through a boom-bust investment cycle, and they keep investing in distribution (50,000 outlets, 21,000 villages added) through weak quarters.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
