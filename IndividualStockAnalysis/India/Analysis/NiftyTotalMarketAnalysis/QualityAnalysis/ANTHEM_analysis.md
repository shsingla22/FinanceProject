# Anthem Bioscienc (ANTHEM) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Anthem Biosciences is an India-based, listed (NSE: ANTHEM) contract research, development and manufacturing company that does the lab work and factory production of drug ingredients on behalf of other drug companies rather than selling its own branded medicines. Its customers are mostly small biotech firms plus a growing list of large global pharma companies, who pay it to run early R&D programmes on new molecules and then to manufacture and supply the active ingredients and advanced intermediates once those molecules progress or reach the market — over 100 R&D projects are in early stages and about 10 in Phase 3, and revenue grows as partners' commercial products sell more. Alongside this it sells specialty ingredients, a steadier month-to-month B2B business largely within India. Revenue is earned per project and per kilo shipped, so quarters are lumpy; FY26 consolidated revenue was Rs. 2,124 crore with EBITDA of Rs. 990 crore (43.4% margin), PAT of Rs. 592 crore, and a net cash position of Rs. 1,375 crore, with production run out of Units 1, 2 and 3 (NeoAnthem) and Unit 4 under construction.

**The parts of the business:**

- **CRDMO (Contract Research, Development and Manufacturing Organisation)** — The core business — running research and development programmes for pharma/biotech clients and then manufacturing the drug ingredients and advanced intermediates for them, at 83% of FY26 revenue (Rs. 1,773 crore, up 18%) and 81.5% in Q1 FY27 (Rs. 341 crore).
- **R&D revenues (within CRDMO)** — The research/development portion of CRDMO work, which management says runs at roughly 11%–16% of revenues in a given quarter and carries a different (lower material) margin profile than manufacturing; visibility is typically one to two quarters ahead from signed contracts.
- **Custom synthesis (chemistry capacity within CRDMO)** — Chemical manufacturing measured in kilolitres of reactor capacity across Unit 1 (~74%–78% utilised), Unit 2 (expanded by ~130 KL, ~50%–65% utilised) and Unit 3/NeoAnthem, including about 16 KL of peptide synthesis capacity; Unit 4 will add another 365 KL.
- **Fermentation (biology capacity within CRDMO)** — Biology-based production of about 140–142 kilolitres running at roughly 50% utilisation, with 40 KL added at NeoAnthem and a further 100 KL planned at Unit 4.
- **Specialty Ingredients** — A smaller, steadier ingredients business sold B2B and largely focused on India, contributing 17% of FY26 revenue (Rs. 352 crore, up 8% in Q4) and 18.5% (Rs. 78 crore) in Q1 FY27, ordered month-to-month with some annual rolling forecasts and long-term contracts.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Anthem Bioscience earns its "Decent" 62-out-of-100 rating (45% x 68 business quality, 30% x 56 multibagger fit, 25% x 60 risk safety) mostly on the strength of how it is run and what it earns. Management scores at the top of the framework on candor, stewardship and independence: it cut its own FY26 revenue guidance from about 20% to the mid-teens mid-year and named the miss afterwards, refuses to give guidance it cannot back, and has grown purely organically with no acquisitions. It is doing this from a position of financial comfort — net cash rising from Rs.1,375 cr to Rs.1,720 cr while self-funding a Rs.1,200 cr Phase-1 Unit 4 that roughly doubles custom synthesis capacity, on top of expanding Unit 2 by about 50% (246 to 376 KL) and commissioning Unit 3. The economics support that spending: Rs.39 of every Rs.100 of sales is operating profit, and every Rs.100 of capital in the business generated Rs.27 of operating cash this year. The end market is genuinely expanding — outsourced CRDMO demand, China+1 supply-chain diversification, and new modalities such as peptides/GLP-1, ADCs and RNAi feeding a funnel of 100+ early-stage programmes. This combination is why Corporate Culture is the one pattern rated a STRONG FIT, and why the framework's Management, Competitive Advantage, Customer Benefits and Return on Capital areas all score at or above 1.0.

The patterns that fit are the ones about winning customers and building capability, not about controlling price — and that distinction matters. Market Share Gainers and Innovation Dominance are both LIKELY FIT: two big-pharma customers added directly in FY26 and another in Q1 FY27, lateral entry into molecules already served by incumbent suppliers, positioning to replace Chinese imports for Indian GLP-1 players, and real technology investment in continuous processing, automation, green chemistry and commercial-scale ADC and peptide plants. But Pricing Power is a clear NO FIT: across five calls not one price increase is discussed, customers open price discussions as volumes rise, and the entire FY26 margin expansion is credited to backward integration and yield, not price. Low Price Plus is also NO FIT for the opposite reason — margins ran from 38% in Q1 FY26 to 48.1% in Q4 FY26 and the cost savings are kept rather than passed on. So the profit picture rests on self-generated efficiency, which fits the framework's note that margins have swung about ±5.2 points a year, suggesting key costs sit outside management's control. Toll Roads and Recurring Revenue are only PARTIAL for a similar reason: customers do stay once a molecule is qualified into their regulatory filings and management frames each win as a decade-long stream, with 4 molecules going commercial in FY26, but there are no subscription or contracted-fee structures underneath, and new-customer economics take 3-4 years to mature. Assurance benefits scores full marks precisely because customers are buying regulatory-grade certainty and supply-chain security rather than a low price — the same trait that builds loyalty is the one that keeps price off the table.

The risks are largely the flip side of the same traits. The deep, multi-year, qualification-heavy relationships that make the Toll Roads and Cost to Replicate patterns work also mean a handful of undisclosed customers swing the results — Stakeholder Concentration is the single HIGH RISK item, with the Q1 FY26 beat coming from "about 5 or 6 of our clients," the FY26 shortfall from destocking on a couple of key products, and the backward integration undertaken for "one of the customer products." That same lumpiness shows up in the framework as a negative mark on persistence of growth (roughly 22% average sales growth but uneven year to year) and as the Cyclicality watch item: Q1 FY27 revenue fell about 25% year-on-year on timing shifts, with Unit 2 utilisation dropping to 50%. The growth engine and the competitive threat also share a source — the peptide/GLP-1 opportunity behind the Market Share Gainers pattern is the same arena where New Entrants is flagged, with structurally cheaper Chinese suppliers, a crowd of Indian entrants and a listed peer already expanding full peptide-chain capacity. Technological Innovation is a watch item because a sizeable portion of work still comes from small-molecule NCE synthesis while the industry shifts toward peptides, ADCs, RNAi and biologics, and management has conceded it has no commercial monoclonal-antibody capability — a gap sitting directly beneath the Innovation Dominance claim. Two further drags are more mundane: about 124 days of cash stays tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and earnings are policy-sensitive even though no regulator sets prices, with RoDTEP export incentives part of Rs. 63 cr of other operating income in FY26 and the new Labor Codes forcing a Rs. 25.4 cr exceptional charge. Overall resilience reads as SOUND, and the offsets are real — counter-cyclical building through the biotech downturn, a customer base broadened by three big-pharma additions, backward integration that has taken China sourcing to nil, and a roughly 60% full-year order book at Q1 — but the verdict lands at Decent rather than higher because the customer concentration, the lumpy delivery schedules and the absence of pricing power all cut against an otherwise high-quality operator.
