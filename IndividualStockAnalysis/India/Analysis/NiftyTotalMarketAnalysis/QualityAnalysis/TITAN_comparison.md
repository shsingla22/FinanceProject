## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 29 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 1 improved, 12 regressed, 16 unchanged. 4 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them).

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|

| Return on Capital | Good (+0.67) | Neutral (+0.33) | -0.33 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Management itself separates skill from tailwind — attributing buyer recovery partly to a powerful exchange offer rather than pure brand pull, and stripping bullion/coin sales out of performance metrics — which supports a halo-robust read. | The long view had said: Headline growth is substantially a gold-price artefact rather than proof of skill — ticket size +44% on flat buyers — which warrants caution, though management's own disaggregation of price, mix and buyer effects makes the separation possible.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. Now: Management confirms it has not throttled store expansion or renovations to protect margin and cites 'sizeable investment' in international/subsidiary businesses, though incremental ROIC on the new international capex is not yet demonstrated. | The long view had said: Sustained, record-setting growth store capex across every division through good and weak quarters — 125 net additions in one quarter, 40-50 Tanishq plus 70-80 Mia plus similar CaratLane annually, plus 20-30 store enlargements that each 'is like adding a store' — in a high-return retail model.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Neutral → Weak. Now: Growth is driven by gold-price pass-through rather than pricing power or mix — buyer growth was flat-to-negative for nine months, studded mix stayed flat, coins skewed the mix, and the deliberate pivot is down-mix to 14/18/9-carat and lower making charges. | The long view had said: Growth is price-led but that price is gold-cost pass-through, not pricing power — ticket size up 44% while buyer growth was flat for nine months and mix worsened toward gold coins and away from studded, costing ~100bps of gross margin.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Some stickiness exists via the repeat-customer base and the old-gold exchange programme, but jewellery buyers face essentially no switching costs and management concedes competitive responsiveness on gold rates is required. | The long view had said: Modest lock-in exists via Encircle loyalty, Tata Neu, the Golden Harvest/Gramage Purchase Plan and the exchange programme, but jewellery switching costs remain inherently low.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: The old-gold exchange proposition works precisely because customers trust Titan to value and handle their gold honestly, and Titan supplies gold to its own vendor ecosystem to control integrity of the chain. | The long view had said: The core purchase driver is certainty — purity, transparent GST-paid billing, guaranteed exchange value — which management says customers will not trade back for an unbilled discount.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Share is being sustained-to-marginally-gained in a fragmented multipolar market that leaves runway, and Q4 delivered best-in-recent-past topline with buyer growth back to 8%, though same-store growth has lagged other large listed players. | The long view had said: Share moved from 4-5% to 6-7% of a growing ~₹4.2 lakh crore market over the period with long runway left, and management targets 15-20% growth against a formalizing industry — despite an 'even steven' patch in FY25.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: Growth is partly taken from weaker regional and unorganized players as formalization proceeds, though management saw no new wave of small-player wind-ups despite the gold spike. | The long view had said: Abundant weak donators — unorganized mom-and-pop jewellers and illegal-gold importers whose cost edge collapsed with the 9% customs-duty cut, feeding the formalization shift toward players with stronger balance sheets.
- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Formalization of the Indian jewellery market continues as a secular tailwind, though management explicitly says gold prices have not accelerated any new consolidation of small players. | The long view had said: The end market has a durable secular driver in the formalization of Indian jewellery plus low diamond penetration and India income growth, repeated consistently as the basis for the 15-20% growth ambition.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Tanishq holds a large mindspace position with a much higher base than rivals, but management explicitly describes the market as multipolar rather than anything monopoly-like, and there is no aftermarket/service annuity. | The long view had said: Tanishq owns a trust/purity position in the Indian consumer's mind and the old-gold exchange and repair/refurbish services create an aftermarket back to its own stores, but with only 6-7% share it is far from monopoly-like in economics.
- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Good → Neutral. Now: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.45 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: The model is inventory-heavy — gold funded by metal loans at ~3% with tenure stretched to 270 days plus sizeable subsidiary investment — offset by a genuinely strong brand, so asset-light it is not. | The long view had said: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.45 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: The moat is strong but the model is deliberately inventory-heavy — capital employed rising on a conscious 'aggressive bet on inventory' — partly offset by good stock turns, gold-on-lease at ~3% with tenure extended to 270 days, and a franchised store network.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: Accessibility is the explicit operating strategy — holding price points, lightweight and lower-caratage ranges across Tanishq/Mia/CaratLane — backed by continued store expansion and Damas's wide GCC network. | The long view had said: Presence across ~270 towns with continuous store enlargement, franchising, digital and CRM/Encircle outreach, plus a deliberate accessibility agenda (lightweight, 18/14/9-carat, purchase plans) to keep entry points open.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Largely organic execution and no distress on funding, but the period includes a sizeable majority acquisition of Damas plus reliance on gold metal loans, which sits against the organic-only, no-big-M&A ideal. | The long view had said: Predominantly organic growth funded by cheap gold metal loans with counter-cyclical store and inventory investment through weak quarters, tempered by the sizeable, currently loss-making Damas acquisition.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Management sticks with multi-year bets through volatility — seeding 18-carat in resistant South/West markets, sustaining exchange-campaign investment, and building international over two-three years to profitability — through an orderly MD transition. | The long view had said: A consistent multi-year playbook — network prominence over count, low-ticket 'core' funnel, digital, low-share market conquest — was articulated in 2022 and still visibly executed in 2026, with growth and market share explicitly prioritized over near-term margin optics.

*Unchanged: Advertisement and promotion; Intangible benefits; Cost of working capital; Barriers to entry and rationality; Geographical expansion; Candor; Return on capital (headline); Profit margin; Cyclical market growth; Out of limelight; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Security by obscurity; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Merger and acquisition; People matter (talent development); Distribution advantage.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Titan is a manufacturer that sells almost entirely through its own and franchised retail — continued store expansion and renovation, CaratLane's direct/online model, and the Damas 67% acquisition converting an acquired GCC retail network into Tanishq stores; franchise economics are discussed explicitly on the Q4 call. (The long view had said: Pure forward integrator: manufacturer running its own retail network with aggressive store adds (125 net in Q1 FY23; 40-50 Tanishq plus 70-80 Mia targeted in FY25), a long-standing L1/L2/L3 franchising model, ~6% of jewellery topline from digital in 2022, and the 67% Damas acquisition in FY26 converting an overseas retail network to Tanishq.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management claim share gain in FY25 and sustained share in Q1 FY26 but concede same-store growth has lagged larger listed peers for several quarters and that the multipolar market makes share hard to measure; international expansion (Damas/GCC, CaratLane) is real but loss-making in Q4. (The long view had said: Share quoted rising from 4-5% to 6-7% (Aug 2022) with new-buyer contribution at 45-46%, and formalisation plus stronger balance sheets cited as a durable share tailwind through Jun 2026; management was candid that FY25 was 'even steven' with no share gained, and international expansion (GCC/Damas, ~3% of jewellery in 2024) is being localised — gains look organic rather than bought.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across FY26 Titan passed gold inflation through and lost volume — buyer growth was flat-to-negative for nine months — while responding with making-charge discounts, a 'never before' exchange offer and cheaper caratages; that is the opposite of raising price above cost inflation without volume loss. (The long view had said: Titan passes through very large gold inflation with buyers holding (ticket size +44%, buyer growth returning to 8% in Q4 FY26) and management credits price elasticity — but this is commodity pass-through, not pricing above cost inflation: gross contribution fell ~100-200bps in Q3 FY26, making-charge discount windows persist, and the mix is being pushed down to 18/14/9-carat to stay accessible.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 6 unchanged. Financial resilience: Stressed on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The value Titan captures is the making charge on a commodity input, and the calls show that premium being defended with price: management says it is 'very competitive and aggressive' on gold rates and is deliberately introducing lower-making-charge, lower-complexity gold products, while an analyst notes making-charge discounting has become a through-the-year phenomenon across branded players. (The long view had said: The premium is under steady attack from cheaper substitutes — unorganised jewellers undercutting formal players using illegally imported gold, industry-wide making-charge discounting that analysts ask whether Titan must run year-round, and lab-grown diamonds as a low-cost stand-in for natural stones; management has so far held its discount playbook unchanged.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Across all four calls demand is described almost entirely through the lens of the gold price — the 'meteoric'/'parabolic' rise caused buyer holdback and flat-to-negative buyer growth for nine months, a 900bps customs duty cut distorted the base, and hedging MTM swung jewellery margin by 50bps in Q1FY26; management repeatedly refused to guide because gold prices are bidirectional and volatile. (The long view had said: Across the 2022–2026 calls, management frames demand, mix and margins almost entirely in terms of the gold price — a commodity input that swings ticket sizes (+44% in Q3FY26), suppresses buyer growth to flat for three quarters, then releases pent-up buying, while gross margin fell ~200bps and CFO says margin is now 'an outcome' he cannot guide to; reported revenue growth is inflated by gold inflation rather than volume.)

*Unchanged: Technological Innovation (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Stressed on the long view (2 of the 3 stress checks are flagged — multiple warning lights at once.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
