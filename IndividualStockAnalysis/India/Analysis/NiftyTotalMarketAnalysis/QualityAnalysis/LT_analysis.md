# Larsen & Toubro Ltd. (LT) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSTRUCTION*

Larsen & Toubro is an Indian engineering and construction group that wins large contracts to design, build and equip big physical projects — roads, metros, tunnels, buildings and factories, water systems, power transmission lines, oil & gas facilities and power plants — and books revenue as it executes those projects over time. Its customers are mainly governments and state-owned enterprises (central government, state governments and public sector corporations make up roughly 80% of its Indian order book, with private companies the rest), plus government-linked clients in the Middle East; about a quarter of the order book has historically been funded by bilateral and multilateral lending agencies. It runs on an order-book model: it reports order inflows, a backlog (Rs 3.63 trillion in June 2022 rising to Rs 7.33 trillion by December 2025), revenue conversion from that backlog, and thin project margins (Projects & Manufacturing EBITDA margin of roughly 7.5%–8.2%). India and the Middle East are its two primary markets — international work grew from 28% of the order book in 2022 to 49% by December 2025, with the Middle East alone accounting for close to Rs 3 trillion of backlog — and alongside the project businesses it also owns a lending arm, IT services companies, data centres, semiconductors and a real estate business.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Infrastructure** — Builds heavy civil works, transportation infrastructure and roads, buildings and factories, water systems, minerals and metals plants, and power transmission & distribution networks; it is the largest single business, and together with Energy accounts for about 90%–92% of the total order book.
- **Energy (Hydrocarbon and Power)** — Designs and builds oil & gas facilities onshore and offshore plus power projects, and increasingly energy-transition work such as renewables, green energy, offshore wind (including a role in the TenneT HVDC offshore wind programme) and 'CarbonLite Solutions'; it is the second-biggest project business, crossing Rs 1 trillion of annual order inflow for the first time in FY26.
- **Hi-Tech Manufacturing (Heavy Engineering and Precision Engineering & Systems)** — Manufactures heavy fabricated equipment and defence/precision systems, and is expanding through partnerships — General Atomics for MALE drones under India's 87-aircraft defence programme, Bharat Electronics for the AMCA fighter programme, and Holtec for nuclear/thermal heat transfer equipment; it is much smaller than Infrastructure or Energy, with an order prospects pipeline of about Rs 0.42 trillion versus Rs 4 trillion for Infrastructure.
- **IT and Technology Services** — Comprises the listed software services subsidiaries (L&T Infotech, L&T Technology Services and Mindtree), plus newer businesses — data centres now branded Larsen & Toubro Vyoma (30 MW of capacity, expanding into hyperscale sites in Mumbai, Chennai and Bangalore), e-commerce/digital platforms, and L&T Semiconductor Technologies; the calls describe it as a consistently fast-growing part of group revenue.
- **Financial Services (L&T Finance)** — A lending business, now 95% retail loans, that also entered gold loans and earned investment-grade international ratings; it reported quarterly profit of about Rs 686 crores in Q1 FY25 and is reported separately from the group's working-capital and collections figures.
- **Development Projects (concessions)** — Owned and operated infrastructure assets such as Nabha Power and Hyderabad Metro; management is deliberately shrinking this, having signed agreements to sell both stakes as part of its planned exit from the concession portfolio.
- **L&T Realty** — The group's real estate development arm, being consolidated into a single wholly owned platform via a slump sale; pre-sales more than doubled to Rs 94 billion in FY26 on launches in Noida and Panvel.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Larsen & Toubro comes through these three reviews as a genuinely well-run, well-managed company whose main limitation is the kind of business it is in rather than how it is being run. The quality framework gives it top marks for management: candid guidance (including openly reporting the FY26 revenue shortfall against a 15% guidance and pre-flagging hydrocarbon cost overruns), disciplined stewardship over five years with organic growth, prudent borrowing and the promised exits from IDPL, Nabha and Hyderabad Metro, and two successive five-year plans — Lakshya 26, largely delivered, and now Lakshya 31 — that show a long-horizon strategy actually followed through. Growth scores full marks: sales have compounded around 11% a year with unusual consistency, end markets in infrastructure, energy transition, nuclear, data centres and semiconductors are structurally expanding, and the order book has grown from Rs 3.63 trillion in June 2022 to Rs 7.40 trillion by March 2026. The combined rating lands at 51 out of 100 — 'Decent', three stars — built from 60 on business quality, 41 on multibagger fit and 48 on risk safety, with resilience judged sound.

The most revealing thing in the records is how often a single trait shows up as a strength, a pattern and a risk at the same time. The candid, target-and-report management culture that earns the top Candor score is exactly what makes Corporate Culture the one STRONG FIT among the eleven patterns — but the very disclosures being praised are misses: revenue growth of 12% against 15% guidance, a Q4 margin shortfall, cost overruns in hydrocarbons expected to persist. Likewise, the huge order book that drives the Market Share Gainers pattern (order book more than doubling, international share rising from 28% to 52%, domestic private share from 17% to 39%) is the same book that makes Cyclicality an elevated risk, because over 90% of that Rs 7.4 trillion sits in Infrastructure and Energy and moves with government capex budgets and oil-driven Middle East spending. The negative working capital that the framework calls a rare strength — cash arriving 151 days before suppliers must be paid, with working capital falling from 22.9% of sales to 4.1% — is customer-funded growth, which ties the company's cash position directly to continued order inflow from those same cyclical clients. And the international expansion scored a full 2 for execution is the same expansion that creates the stakeholder concentration risk: roughly Rs 3 trillion of the book sits in the Middle East, about three-quarters of the international portfolio, enough that a whole section of the May 2026 call is devoted to West Asia conflict risk.

What holds the score back is that the business, for all its scale, does not appear to earn premium economics. On the quality framework, Competitive Advantage and Return on Capital both score zero and Industry Structure is slightly negative; the headline return on capital is a negative check, with Rs 100 of capital generating only Rs 7 of operating cash, and operating margin sits at Rs 12 per Rs 100 of sales. The pattern screen tells the same story from the other side: Pricing Power is a flat NO FIT because fixed-price EPC contracting means the calls discuss cost headwinds squeezing margins rather than price increases, and competitive pricing is named as the pressure itself. Toll Roads and Recurring Revenue are also no-fits — the work is the customer's core capex, competitively bid, lumpy, with no service or renewal base underneath it. Brand Strength, Cost to Replicate and Low Price Plus are all only partial: the reputation, the sovereign-plus BBB+ rating, the ability to absorb single orders above Rs 1 trillion and decades of execution references are real barriers at the top end, but the underlying business remains contestable, which is precisely what makes the Good Enough Goods risk elevated — management repeatedly blames aggressive bidding, not poor execution, for margin damage. Government Dependency is the single high risk, with the domestic book historically around 83% government and PSU and every call opening on Union Budget allocations; the deliberate push into private-sector demand, taking that share from 21% in March 2025 to 39% a year later, is the clearest evidence that management sees the same problem the risk review does. L&T is also a poor fit for anything discovery-driven — Security by Obscurity scores a full negative 2, since this is one of India's most closely tracked large caps.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

**Overall:** the company partly passes the foundation every multibagger shares (steady cash + high returns on capital + growth), 1 of 3 testable checks passing, and fits 3 of the 11 patterns: Corporate Culture, Innovation Dominance, Market Share Gainers.

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** Management sets explicit multi-year targets (Lakshya 2026, then Lakshya 31) and reports against them candidly, including misses — FY26 revenue 12% vs 15% guidance, Q4 margin shortfall, Hydrocarbon cost overruns flagged in advance as persisting for a few quarters — while delivering the promised concession divestments and the NWC and ROE trajectory laid out in 2022; leadership (Ramakrishnan on IR, Shankar Raman as CFO) is long-tenured across all 16 calls.

> "On the revenue front, growth for FY26 stood at 12% compared to our guidance of 15%. The variance was primarily attributable to subdued execution progress in certain domestic projects" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Innovation Dominance — LIKELY FIT
*"Out-inventing the competition"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and every one of the 2 computable checks supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** New capability is added steadily (green energy EPC, offshore wind HVDC for TenneT, semiconductors, nuclear, data centres) but technology is largely imported through partnerships — General Atomics, Holtec, Bharat Electronics — and no R&D spend, pipeline economics or share of industry R&D is ever disclosed on the calls.

> "a partnership with General Atomics to manufacture Medium Altitude Long Endurance (MALE) drones in India, and an MoU with Holtec International Asia" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹67 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ✅ High retained margin (67% of sales) alongside 11% yearly growth — the profitable-innovation flywheel: margins fund the spending that drives the growth.

### Market Share Gainers — LIKELY FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern, but the numbers push back (0 of 1 check supporting) — held one step below a full confirmation.

**What the calls show:** Across the four years covered the order book more than doubles from Rs 3.63 trillion (Jun'22) to Rs 7.40 trillion (Mar'26), with international share rising from 28% to 52% and private-sector domestic share from 17% to 39% — share gained on record inflows while working capital and ROE improved, not bought with balance-sheet quality.

> "The order book is at Rs 7.40 trillion as of March '26, a 28% YoY increase and providing a strong revenue visibility." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Sales compounding at 11% a year — below the 12% pace the share-gainer pattern looks for.

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*
