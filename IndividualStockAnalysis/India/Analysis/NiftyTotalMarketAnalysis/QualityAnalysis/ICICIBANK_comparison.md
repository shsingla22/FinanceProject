## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 27 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 5 improved, 3 regressed, 19 unchanged. 1 check carries the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them). 1 was newly assessable in the recent view.

**Checks that improved:**

- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: Brand is explicitly managed as an asset and the sustained 4.30-4.32% NIM — among the best in Indian banking — evidences pricing power, though a bank's intangible pull is weaker than a consumer brand's. | The long view had said: Brand ICICI is invoked as a value system rather than a pricing lever, and the admission that price must be found within competition shows image does not confer pricing power in a commoditised product.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Expansion is real but modest and mostly domestic-density plus NRI corridors — 528 new branches, Money2India live in USA/UK/Canada/Europe/UAE and iMobile Global launched in the UK — while the overseas loan book remains just 2.4% of the total. | The long view had said: The international book was deliberately shrunk from 5.1% of loans (Jun-2022) to 2.4% (Dec-2025) with non-India corporate exposure run down, so geographic expansion is domestic branch densification plus a nascent NRI app rather than a travelling premium brand.
- **Candor** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: The RBI-directed ₹1,283 crore PSL provision was disclosed unprompted in opening remarks with the cause (facilities non-compliant since 2012 origination), the remedy, the duration of the drag, and both reported and adjusted profit figures — owning an unflattering regulatory finding rather than burying it. | The long view had said: Generally high disclosure quality — they voluntarily normalise for one-offs (AIF release, KCC seasonality, the Rs 12.83bn RBI provision) and size the affected agri portfolio at Rs 200-250bn — but they declined to explain what was actually non-compliant and deflected the Iran-payments question, so ownership of the lapse is incomplete.
- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Weak → Neutral. Now: A bank is structurally balance-sheet heavy (₹3.4 lakh crore net worth carrying a much larger loan book), so asset turn is inherently low, though a sector-leading 4.32% NIM shows the moat earns a high yield on those assets. | The long view had said: A bank is structurally the opposite of asset-light — returns come from a leveraged Rs 3.4 lakh crore net-worth balance sheet earning a ~4.3% NIM and 2.3% RoA — though a genuine low-cost deposit moat partly offsets the capital intensity.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Share is visibly being taken in segments weaker lenders once dominated — business banking grew 22.8-24.4% year-on-year while the bank simultaneously cut exposure to NBFCs/HFCs from ₹874 billion to ₹791 billion, i.e. lending direct rather than through the donators. | The long view had said: Management asserts share-gain runway across key segments but never identifies weaker competitors ceding ground, and its own deposit growth of 9-11% suggests it is also losing ground on funding.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: The identical strategic framework — PBT-ex-treasury, 360-degree customer centricity, ecosystems and micromarkets — is repeated verbatim across all four calls and executed through a rate-compression year without chasing growth, showing consistency, though the window is too short to prove multi-year tenacity. | The long view had said: The identical strategic framework — risk-calibrated core operating profit, 360-degree customer, ecosystems and micro-markets — is repeated verbatim from Jul-2022 to Apr-2026 and management explicitly refuses to chase growth targets or forecast, sticking to the framework through the margin downcycle.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Only culture slogans are offered — no discussion of talent development programmes, attrition, or an internal leadership pipeline, so there is directional but not substantive evidence. | The long view had said: Recurring evidence of systematic talent investment — reorganisation into city business heads, cross-functional skilling, ESOPs granted to all employees, ~7,250 net hires in a year — and a stable named leadership bench (Batra, Rakesh, Ajay, Anindya, Abhinek) present on calls throughout.
- **Network effects** (Competitive Advantage): Good → Neutral. Now: The ecosystem/micromarket model creates some cross-referral density between corporate clients and their employees/suppliers, but nothing in the calls evidences a self-reinforcing two-sided network that strengthens with scale. | The long view had said: Real but modest two-sided effects exist where platforms are opened to non-customers and to corporate ecosystems — 7.3 million iMobile Pay activations by non-ICICI holders, 120,000 non-ICICI InstaBIZ registrations, and supply-chain platforms linking corporates with their vendors and dealers.

*Unchanged: Barriers to entry and rationality; Technology advantage; Convenience benefits; Research and development; Assurance benefits; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Halo effect (caution); Capex for growth (not maintenance); Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Distribution to shareholders; Mini-monopolies; Cyclical market growth; Structural end-market growth; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Out of limelight; Disciplined stewards; Distribution advantage; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Gain in market share.*

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these — identical by construction): Advertisement and promotion (Neutral).*

**Newly assessable in the recent view:**

- **Security by obscurity** — Poor: The opposite of obscure — an NYSE/SGX/SIX-listed ₹3.4 lakh crore net-worth bank with SEC filings, quarterly media calls and dense analyst coverage.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 6 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 2 could not be compared.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: NIM was held essentially flat and slightly up across the window (4.30% in Q2/Q3-2026, 4.32% in Q4-2026), which shows spread defence, but NII grew only 7.7-8.4% y-o-y against loan growth of 11.5-15.8%, i.e. yields compressed with the rate cycle; no call cites a price increase taken without volume loss. (The long view had said: Evidence contradicts it: NIM fell from 4.78% (Q1-FY24) to 4.30-4.32% by FY2026, management repeatedly cites 'aggressive competition in mortgage pricing' and corporate price competition, and in Apr 2026 explicitly denied that pricing sits with the bank.)

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Positives are real and repeated: an unchanged stated framework across all four calls, conservative buffers held through the window (13,100 crore contingency provisions, PCR ~75%, CET-1 16.3-16.5%), stated principles of 'Fair to Customer, Fair to Bank' and 'Return of Capital', and full, unhedged disclosure of the RBI-directed 1,283 crore standard asset provision with its cause and duration. Against that, the underlying lapse was an agri-PSL portfolio originated since 2012 that regulators — not management — found non-compliant, which is a governance miss rather than a self-caught error. (The long view had said: The same leadership team and the same stated principles ('Fair to Customer, Fair to Bank', 'One Bank, One Team', 'Return of Capital') run unchanged across all 16 calls; guidance is delivered and referenced back (the ~50bps adjusted credit cost stated in prior calls and then met), contingency provisions were built from 85bn to 131bn and left untouched, and the Jan 2026 RBI agri-PSL adverse finding was disclosed up-front, quantified (Rs 12.83bn provision, Rs 200-250bn portfolio) and led on the call rather than buried.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

*Not comparable: Friendly Middleman (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view); Toll Roads (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
