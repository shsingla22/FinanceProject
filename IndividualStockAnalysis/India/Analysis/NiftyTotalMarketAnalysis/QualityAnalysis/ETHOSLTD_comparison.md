## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 28 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 3 improved, 5 regressed, 20 unchanged. 4 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them). 2 were newly assessable in the recent view.

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Allocation | Neutral (-0.40) | Neutral (+0.00) | +0.40 |
| Return on Capital | Neutral (-0.33) | Neutral (-0.33) | +0.00 |
| Growth | Good (+0.83) | Good (+1.17) | +0.33 |
| Management | Neutral (+0.50) | Neutral (+0.33) | -0.17 |
| Industry Structure | Good (+0.83) | Neutral (+0.33) | -0.50 |
| Customer Benefits | Good (+1.25) | Good (+1.25) | +0.00 |
| Competitive Advantage | Good (+1.00) | Neutral (+0.33) | -0.67 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Growth is explicitly led by price/mix — ASP rose from INR1.87 lakh to INR2.16 lakh, luxury/high-luxury share hit an all-time high, and sub-INR50,000 price points are being deliberately exited, while H1 volume grew only 5%. | The long view had said: Growth has been decisively mix-led — ASP rose from Rs 84,000 to Rs 2.16 lakh in a few years by exiting low price points — although FY25 rebalanced toward volume (15.5% volume vs 7.4% ASP).
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Ethos holds a small slice of a fragmented Indian luxury-watch market and is visibly capturing share — 25.3% FY25 revenue growth, 15.5% same-store growth, and international brands choosing it as their India entry partner. | The long view had said: Clear runway and evidence of capture — exclusive brand share rose from 24% pre-COVID to 31%, brands like Ulysse Nardin defected to Ethos after two decades in India, and same-store growth was 15.5% — though management admits it cannot quantify its share.
- **Advertisement and promotion** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: Management is funding a deliberate brand-equity build — a full brand refresh with top creative agencies covering every touchpoint, plus limited-edition collaborations that already carry INR70 crores of bookings — though spend is not quantified. | The long view had said: Marketing is referenced as a differentiator (social following, website traffic, brand visibility) but no A&P quantum, efficiency or brand-equity payback is ever discussed across the eleven calls.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Candor** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Management does disclose shortfalls plainly (14 boutiques against a 20-plus target, FX loss quantified at INR4.65 crores) but leans heavily on long external-excuse lists for weak quarters and highlights ex-FOREX EBITDA, so candor is partial. | The long view had said: Management quantifies misses and separates them (forex loss of Rs 4.65 crore split by line, 14 boutiques against a 20-plus target with GRAP IV explained, and an honest "I have no factual data" on market share), though H1 FY25 leaned on a long list of external excuses.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Organised Indian luxury watch retail is concentrated with Ethos the largest player, but customers retain real alternatives — overseas purchases, grey market and brands going direct — so end-customer negotiating power is not eliminated. | The long view had said: Organised Indian luxury watch retail is concentrated among few credible players with brand allocation acting as a gatekeeper, and buyers show low price sensitivity as discounts recede.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Weak. Now: Technology investment is catch-up infrastructure — a first ERP replacement after 15 years plus four initial AI use cases — not a deep multi-product tech edge with high R&D intensity. | The long view had said: The digital platform is a real capability contributing about a third of billings, but it is a retail channel rather than a defensible multi-product technology edge, and there is no R&D intensity.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: Exclusive brand agreements — 31% of Q2 FY25 revenue across a 51-brand portfolio, including a long-term exclusive for Favre Leuba — give Ethos a monopoly-like position for those brands in India, reinforced by owned service centres. | The long view had said: Exclusive retail rights over a large independent-brand portfolio (35 brands in 2022 growing to ~60, over 30% of revenue) plus a nationwide authorised service network create a monopoly-like position for those brands in India.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.1 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing is rational (brand-set MRPs, no discount war discussed), but the EFTA duty phase-out that management celebrates simultaneously lowers the barrier for global brands and rival retailers to enter India directly. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Entry requires multi-year brand relationships that are hard to replicate and pricing has grown more rational as discounting fell, though management gave little colour on brands opening their own boutiques.

*Unchanged: People matter (talent development); Customer types (retail vs corporate); Share donators; Asset turn; Out of limelight; Cost of working capital; Geographical expansion; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Return on capital (headline); Independent, long-term and tenacious; Disciplined stewards; Convenience benefits; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Distribution advantage; Assurance benefits; Structural end-market growth; Intangible benefits; Profit margin; Security by obscurity; Halo effect (caution).*

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these — identical by construction): Persistence of growth (Neutral); Merger and acquisition (Neutral); Cyclical market growth (Neutral); Distribution to shareholders (Weak).*

**Newly assessable in the recent view:**

- **Network effects** — Neutral: There is a mild two-sided loop — more exclusive brands attract more customers, which attracts more brands, and pre-owned consignment links buyers and sellers — but nothing self-reinforcing enough to be a genuine network moat.
- **Research and development** — Good: Not classic R&D, but the in-house design studio (2 designers) converted into INR70 crores of limited-edition bookings and a new ERP plus four proprietary AI/LLM use cases are being funded, showing development spend that yields sellable output.

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 6 unchanged. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The business sits in the scale-protected luxury niche the framework describes: rising ASPs, an all-time-high share of luxury and high-luxury watches, and end customers with strong independent brand awareness of the Swiss marques — no discussion in any of the four calls of defending price against cheaper substitutes or of channel own-label pressure. (The long view had said: The threat is not private label but cheaper channels for the identical product — grey/unauthorised sellers and overseas purchase, with India pricing running only 5–7% cheaper than global destinations and a GST hike capable of flipping that arbitrage against Ethos; management's own framing shows price is a live competitive variable managed through discount levels and brand-borne rebates.)

**That worsened:**

- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Policy is repeatedly presented as the single biggest swing factor in the business: the EFTA duty phase-down from 22% to zero is framed as the driver of both future gross margin and of which global brands will even enter India, while a GoM GST-increase proposal, election-period cash-movement restrictions and 90 days of GRAP IV disruption in Delhi NCR each directly hit sales in this 12-month window. (The long view had said: Economics are materially set by policy the company does not control: a proposed GST hike from 18% to 28% requiring an ~8.5% revenue-neutral price increase dominated the Nov 2024 call, EFTA/customs-duty cuts are the swing factor for FY26 gross margin, and GRAP IV pollution restrictions shut Delhi-NCR construction for 90 days, delaying the flagship and stranding inventory; management is actively lobbying ministers.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Fashion Risk (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
