# IndusInd Bank Ltd. (INDUSINDBK) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

IndusInd Bank is an Indian private-sector bank that takes deposits from the public and lends the money out, earning most of its income from the spread between what it pays savers and what it charges borrowers (net interest margin, run at roughly 4.2% in the earlier years), plus fees from things like credit-card spends, transaction banking and wealth products. It lends to a wide mix of customers: truck, car and tractor buyers through a large vehicle-finance arm; very small rural borrowers and village shopkeepers through its Bharat Financial Inclusion (BFIL) unit; homeowners, credit-card and personal-loan customers in cities; small and mid-sized businesses; and large companies including the global diamond and jewellery trade. On the funding side it gathers current, savings and term deposits through a branch network that crossed 3,000 branches in 2024, plus dedicated affluent and non-resident-Indian (NRI) franchises and a digital app called INDIE. It operates essentially across India, from metros to deep rural 'Bharat' locations, with the diamond business serving international trade hubs.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Vehicle Finance** — Loans to buy commercial vehicles, cars, utility vehicles, tractors, two- and three-wheelers and construction equipment, at fixed rates; it is described as one of the key pillars of the bank, with a loan book of about Rs.99,876 crores and quarterly disbursements of roughly Rs.11,000–13,000 crores.
- **Rural and Priority Sector Banking / Bharat Financial Inclusion Limited (BFIL)** — Tiny group loans to rural, mostly women borrowers plus other rural products, delivered at the customer's doorstep; the micro-loan book peaked far higher but shrank to about Rs.16,782 crores after a bad-loan cycle, with roughly 57% now covered by a government CGFMU credit guarantee, and it also helps the bank meet mandatory priority-sector lending targets.
- **Bharat Super Shop (merchant acquiring / merchant loans)** — Business loans to small shopkeepers and merchants sourced through the BFIL network, grown from about Rs.2,237 crores in 2022 to roughly Rs.8,042 crores across some 570,000 merchant borrowers.
- **Bharat Money Stores** — A network of roughly 88,000 kirana (corner-shop) outlets acting as banking points in remote villages, which had signed up about 2.6 million savings accounts.
- **Consumer Banking Assets (other retail assets)** — Traditional city retail lending — home loans (about Rs.6,510 crores, growing fast off a small base), personal loans (~Rs.10,358 crores), credit cards (~Rs.9,751 crores of loans and about Rs.15,000 crores of quarterly card spends) and a new gold-loan product past Rs.1,000 crores — totalling about Rs.31,075 crores, with management deliberately shrinking the unsecured pieces.
- **SME Banking (earlier called business banking / MSME)** — Working-capital and property-backed loans to small and mid-sized firms, about Rs.44,347 crores, repeatedly flagged by management as a relatively small presence and the bank's next big growth opportunity.
- **Wholesale Banking (earlier Corporate Bank)** — Lending and transaction banking to large, mid and small companies; once a fast-growing book led by mid and small corporates, it is now being deliberately shrunk where returns are poor (down ~5-6% quarter on quarter), with 83% of clients rated A or better and transaction-banking fees making up 66% of wholesale and SME fee income.
- **Global Diamond & Jewellery / Gems & Jewellery business** — Financing for the international rough- and polished-diamond trade, about 4.2% of the total loan book in 2022, and consistently described as having pristine asset quality with no stressed borrowers.
- **Liabilities / Deposits franchise** — The deposit-gathering side — current and savings accounts (CASA ratio was around 43% in 2022), retail term deposits (now about 47.9% of deposits), affluent banking including Pioneer Private for clients above Rs.3 crores of relationship value, and NRI deposits which crossed Rs.50,000 crores; management calls deposit quantity and quality its foremost priority.
- **INDIE (digital banking)** — The bank's mobile-app banking offering, which had passed 1.3 million customers by mid-2024.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

IndusInd Bank scores 41 out of 100 — a Mixed, two-star rating built from a weak business-quality score of 37 and a middling risk-safety score of 48. The three analyses tell one story from three angles: this is a bank whose returns, its management's credibility and its customers' trust all broke at the same time, in the same episode. On the quality framework, every scored area is neutral or negative, with return on capital the worst at -1.0 and both management and industry structure close behind at -0.83. The two harshest single checks are about people rather than numbers — 'Disciplined stewards' at -2, because the MD & CEO, Deputy CEO and CFO all left after growth guidance of 18–23% turned into a shrinking loan book, and 'Assurance benefits' at -2, because deposits fell for three straight quarters and the new CEO now lists repairing stakeholder perceptions as an explicit priority. The multibagger screen confirms the same thing from the outside: 'Corporate Culture' and 'Brand Strength' are both flat rejections, citing the same management exits and the same need to rebuild perception. When trust is the product a bank sells, a governance failure is not a separate issue from the business — it is the business.

The pattern screen finds almost nothing to build on. Of the patterns tested, only two register at all and both only partially. 'Friendly Middleman' is a partial fit on the strength of the BFIL and Bharat Money Stores network — roughly 88,000 kirana-shop correspondents and merchant agents sourcing deposits and loans in rural India — but that same acquired microfinance platform is what the quality framework marks down under 'Merger and acquisition' (-1), with the book collapsing from Rs.42,350 crore in 2024 to Rs.16,782 crore, and what the risk review names as the trigger for the highest-rated risk, Cyclicality. The one asset distinctive enough to look like an edge is the same asset that caused the damage. 'Innovation Dominance' is the other partial fit — a steady run of digital launches, with INDIE reaching 2.7 million monthly active users — and it does useful defensive work elsewhere: it is why Technological Innovation sits at merely 'Watch' rather than high risk, and why Shifting Consumer Preferences is rated 'Low', with the bank ahead of the move to digital rather than losing ground to it. But the screen refuses to call it dominance because the cadence is incremental with no research spend or pricing proof behind it, which is exactly why it protects the bank without yet earning it anything. Everything price-related fails outright. 'Pricing Power', 'Low Price Plus' and 'Cost to Replicate' are all rejected, and the same evidence appears as the 'Good Enough Goods' risk on 'Watch' and the negative 'Intangible benefits' check: net interest margin fell from around 4.25% to 3.39%, the bank pays 6.07% for deposits against larger rivals, and the CEO himself calls the industry largely commoditised with execution as the only differentiator. Competitors relaxing pricing and collateral in secured retail, plus industry-wide microfinance over-leverage, also drag down the 'Barriers to entry and rationality' check.

The single positive check is 'Cyclical market growth' at +1, and it comes from the same cyclicality the risk review rates HIGH — the bank looks past its trough, with net slippages down 37% quarter-on-quarter, microfinance 31–90 day overdues down to 0.9% from 2.4%, profit recovering to Rs.594 crore from Rs.128 crore in Q3 FY26, and management saying credit costs have peaked. That is a recovery off a very low base: return on assets is roughly 0.45%, average advances are still down about 2% quarter-on-quarter, and the ambition is only to match system growth a year out, which is why 'Market Share Gainers' and 'Gain in market share' both score negatively despite genuine runway in SME and a 5% share of card spends. The risk review's better news is structural rather than earned: Stakeholder Concentration is LOW because the book is granular by construction, spread across millions of small borrowers with no single dominant customer — though the microfinance blowup shows that granularity protects against one customer failing, not against a whole borrower segment turning at once. Government Dependency is the second HIGH risk and compounds the first, since the RBI's microfinance rules, priority-sector requirements, liquidity guidelines and the CGFMU guarantee covering around 57% of the micro book repeatedly set the terms the bank operates under. Overall resilience is marked UNKNOWN, which is the honest reading: the credit cycle appears to be turning, but the things that would make the recovery durable — pricing power, a trusted brand, a proven management team — are precisely what the other two analyses say are absent.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; the numeric fingerprints could not be computed, so the call evidence decides alone.

**What the calls show:** Earnings swing hard with credit cycles: vehicle finance (MHCV, construction equipment, tractors) is levered to freight/capex cycles and government stimulus, while micro loans went from 'highest Q1 disbursements in BFIL's history' (Jul-2022) to a book contracting 17% quarter-on-quarter with elevated slippages (Jan-2026). RoA fell from 1.73% (Q1FY23) to ~0.45%, NIM from 4.25% to 3.35-3.39%, and PAT from Rs.1,631cr to Rs.128cr — with the entire top team (MD Kathpalia, Dy CEO Khurana, CFO Jain, two Chairmen) exiting through the downcycle, which is the classic over-extend-then-repair pattern.

> "trends give us confidence that credit costs are past their peak, subject to macro stability and seasonality" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Diversification into flow-type retail (home loans, gold loans, SME, transaction banking fees at 66% of wholesale fee) and CGFMU guarantee cover on ~57% of the micro book*

- ⬜ Not enough sales history.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Fortunes are repeatedly set by policy: RBI's March-2022 MFI master direction halted disbursements ('the time available was short'), LCR draft guidelines, PSL requirements shaping the rural book, PSLC fees, CGFMU guarantee cover, the RBI FX NOP rule, labour-code provisioning of Rs.230cr, and demand itself credited to GST changes and Budget capex/MNREGA allocations.
