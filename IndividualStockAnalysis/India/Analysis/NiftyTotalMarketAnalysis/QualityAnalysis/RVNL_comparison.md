### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 3 weakened, 7 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Forward Integrators**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: There is genuine movement from pure EPC toward owning/operating the asset — HAM concessions with 20–25 year revenue, four multimodal logistics parks with NHLML, rolling-stock O&M, and a Visakha Port MoU — but this is asset-ownership diversification, not brand-led forward integration, and no revenue share from these streams is quantified. (The long view had said: The evidence is silent — RVNL sells to institutional clients and its vertical moves (SPVs, JVs, manufacturing at Latur) are toward supply, not toward owning stores, franchising or D2C.)

**That weakened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Low price is present but the second half — quality/differentiation, cost culture and strong returns — is absent: bid-won work is being recognised at or below cost, with onerous contracts booked in Q1 and again INR54 cr in Q4 FY26, and no itemised cost-savings culture is described. (The long view had said: There is a real lean-cost culture — ~1,300 staff against INR 20,000 crores turnover, employee productivity disclosed rising from INR 21.23 crore to INR 22.46 crore, and Feb 2026 cites cost cutting lifting gross margin 50-60bps — but the low price is imposed by tendering rather than converted into durable share or high returns, and margins fell rather than held.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No heritage, A&P, distribution or premium-pricing discussion appears in any FY26 call; the company is a PSU contractor winning on lowest-bid price, and the only brand-like reference is a DPE 'very good' MoU rating that carries no pricing benefit. (The long view had said: RVNL carries institutional credentials — Navratna status, CARE AAA, 'excellent' DPE rating for 12 years, 16,000 route km commissioned — which is reputation with government clients, but it produces no premium pricing (margins are falling) and there is no consumer brand, A&P spend or adjacency stretch.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The only genuine barrier was nominated Ministry of Railways work, and the FY26 calls document that advantage being withdrawn by the parent itself — railway turnover falling ~25% and being replaced by open-tender work any well-funded EPC contractor can bid for at similar or better prices. (The long view had said: There is a genuine hard-to-copy capability — 21 years and 16,000 route km of complex brownfield rail integration, 100+ km of Himalayan tunnelling on Rishikesh-Karnaprayag, and full project-lifecycle skills a private entrant lacks — but the principal advantage was policy-granted nomination work, and once MoR withdrew it RVNL's returns fell to open-market EPC levels, which a well-funded contractor can contest.)

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Innovation Dominance (NO FIT); Market Share Gainers (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **New Entrants**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: The shift from protected nomination work to open competitive tendering exposes RVNL to the full field of private EPC bidders, and management concedes margin compression is the direct consequence — with three onerous contracts recognised in Q1 FY26 and a further INR54 crore onerous hit in Q4. The response is defensive (design standardisation, 'selective bidding') rather than a structural advantage. (The long view had said: The move to open tendering exposed RVNL to private EPC bidders it never faced under nomination; management's 2022 response was defensive ('private players require a lot of technical expertise which I do not think the private players are having'), and by FY26 the mix shift to bid work had visibly reset economics — flat top line, EBITDA margin down from 10.36% to 5.83% in Q4FY26, and guidance cut to a ~7% gross margin.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: RVNL is a pure customer-capex business: revenue is entirely project execution funded by Indian Railways' and state capex budgets, and management explicitly frames the outlook off the Union Budget capex line. Across FY26 the swings are visible — Q1 revenue -3.4% year-on-year, Q4 standalone turnover +47.6% quarter-on-quarter but EBITDA margin collapsing from 10.36% to 5.83% — i.e. lumpy sales and violently fluctuating margins with no flow/consumable revenue to steady them. (The long view had said: Revenue is entirely customer-capex-linked project construction, not flow/operations demand, so the top line rides Indian Railways' and states' investment programmes and the P&L swings sharply — FY23 grew 20%+ per quarter, FY26 turned 'stagnant' with PAT down 32% year-on-year and EBITDA margin halving quarter-on-quarter. Damage so far has come more from contract-mix and onerous contracts than from a demand cycle, and government infrastructure capex is steadier than a commodity cycle, which bounds the exposure.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (QUANT FLAG); Fashion Risk (QUANT FLAG); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (HIGH RISK); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
