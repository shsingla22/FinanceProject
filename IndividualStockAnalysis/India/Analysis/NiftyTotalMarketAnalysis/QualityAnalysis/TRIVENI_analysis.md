# Triveni Engg (TRIVENI) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Triveni Engineering & Industries is an Indian company, based in Uttar Pradesh with corporate offices in Noida, that is mainly a sugar producer with an engineering side-business. It buys sugarcane from farmers, crushes it at its own mills (about 8.25-8.41 million tonnes of cane a season, producing roughly 0.9 million tonnes of sugar) and sells sugar domestically to retail and institutional buyers, plus some exports when the government allows; it also turns molasses and grain into alcohol/ethanol which it sells largely to state Oil Marketing Companies under India's fuel-blending programme, and sells surplus power generated from cane waste to the grid. Alongside this, it builds and sells industrial equipment: high-speed gearboxes (Power Transmission) and water/wastewater treatment projects, both run on an order-book basis. Group revenue was about ₹6,291 crore in FY26 with net profit of ₹268.7 crore, so sugar and alcohol together are the bulk of the business and engineering is much smaller.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Sugar** — Crushes sugarcane into sugar — increasingly premium refined and pharmaceutical-grade sugar (about 70% of output) — and is the largest business, contributing roughly ₹1,000 crore of revenue in a single quarter and ₹272 crore of segment profit (PBIT) in FY26.
- **Alcohol / Distillery** — Makes ethanol and alcohol from sugarcane by-products (molasses) and grain (maize, FCI rice) and sells it mostly to Oil Marketing Companies for petrol blending; capacity was expanded repeatedly (to 660 KLPD and beyond) and it reached about ₹1,550 crore of net revenue in FY26 with grain accounting for 56% of ethanol sales.
- **Co-generation / Power** — Burns cane residue to generate electricity, using it in-house and exporting the surplus to the grid — a small contributor (roughly ₹17 crore of external sales in a quarter), though a tariff revision added ₹31 crore of income in FY26.
- **Power Transmission (Engineering)** — Designs and sells high-speed gears and gearboxes to industrial customers on an order-booking basis; small but growing, crossing a record ₹306 crore closing order book for the first time in 2024 and booking 25% higher orders in Q4 FY26.
- **Water (Engineering)** — Executes water and wastewater treatment projects for clients in India and abroad (including a ~₹250 crore European project), with revenue that swings quarter to quarter depending on when contracts are awarded.
- **IMFL (branded spirits)** — A new consumer liquor business launched in July 2024 selling Indian-made foreign liquor in Uttar Pradesh under two brands, THE CRAFTERS STAMP (super premium) and MATSYA (premium); the smallest and newest part of the group.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Triveni's clearest strength is the people running it, and all three reviews agree on that point. Management scores the maximum on candour for owning its misses in plain terms — the surprise fall in West UP cane crushing, the order booking in Q3 that never arrived, forecasts set deliberately below what the industry was claiming — and Corporate Culture is the one multibagger pattern rated a full fit, because the same three-person leadership team has fronted all sixteen calls, set targets and then come back and reconciled them (distillery capacity taken to 660 KLPD, the problem cane variety cut from 77% to 50% of area, the ₹340 crore capex tracked tranche by tranche). That reliability shows up in the growth record too: sales compounding at about 10% a year, growth capex that was board-approved and broadly delivered on schedule (roughly ₹460 crore on new dual-feed distilleries taking capacity to 1,110 KLPD, ₹340 crore on gearboxes and defence work aimed at around ₹700 crore of output), documented share gains in export gearboxes and in UP sugar against a state average that fell 3.4%, a top-five position in UP country liquor from a standing start, and a water business that has moved from the Maldives to Bangladesh to a roughly ₹250 crore European project. The management area of the quality framework scores full marks and growth scores 0.83 — the two brightest spots in the whole record.

Where the story turns is that the same activity that produces the growth also produces most of the risk, and it produces very little cash return. The company earned about ₹3 of operating cash for every ₹100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings employed — a 3% return — and about 241 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. That is why return on capital scores -0.67 and capital allocation scores zero even though the spending itself is aimed at growth rather than mere upkeep. The ethanol build-out is the growth engine and the concentration problem at once: ethanol is 92% of alcohol sales and is sold almost entirely to three state oil marketing companies whose tender cycles set the volumes, which is why Stakeholder Concentration is flagged as elevated and why Q4 FY26 revenue fell on a deliberate scaling-back of supply. The blending mandate that created the opportunity — rising from about 10% in 2022 to about 20% by 2026 — has now hit its E20 ceiling, so the structural tailwind is real but capped pending policy work on going beyond E20. Meanwhile prices are not the company's to set: cane support price, a sugar MSP unchanged since 2019, release quotas, export quotas and ethanol rates are all administered, which is why Pricing Power is rated no fit and Government Dependency is rated high risk. The genuine cost discipline the screen credits under Low Price Plus — maize sourcing, steam and energy savings, borrowing costs cut to 6.1% — exists precisely because price is off the table; it is what absorbed the record ₹300 per tonne rise in UP cane cost while ethanol realisation actually fell to ₹61 a litre.

The engineering and services side is what keeps the whole thing from being a pure commodity bet, and it is the thread running through most of the partial pattern fits. Gearboxes are a small-cost, mission-critical part inside compressor trains and turbines, tied to API oil-and-gas standards and import substitution, with a repeat R&D record that converts into lower cost and new markets and an order book moving from ₹243 crore in FY23 to ₹306 crore in FY25 to ₹500 crore in FY26, capped by a first-in-Asia axial compressor test gearbox order — that is the basis of the Toll Roads and Innovation Dominance partial scores. Water carries roughly ₹1,100 crore of long-duration operations-and-maintenance work (₹1,077 crore of a ₹1,500 crore order book in Q4 FY26), which is the recurring-revenue tail. Moving forward into own-brand spirits and into refined and pharma-grade sugar at 70% of output gives the forward-integration and brand credit. Each of these is also cited as the silver lining against the two high risks, cyclicality and policy dependence — but each stops short of a full fit for the same reason: attach rates and aftermarket economics are never discussed, R&D spend is never quantified, there is no advertising spend or heritage story behind the brands, and the water and export work is tendered rather than owned. And the premium sugar sits next to a near-identical cheaper substitute selling ₹0.75-1 a kilo below it, which is why Good Enough Goods is elevated. New entrants have already done visible damage: unregulated gur and khandsari units outbidding mills for cane cut West UP crushing 17% and group crushing 8.8%, and grain distilleries overtook sugarcane as the ethanol feedstock. The upshot is the Mixed rating of 39 out of 100 — 56 for business quality, 25 for pattern fit, 26 for risk safety — with resilience judged sound and the sugar cycle currently turning the company's way as closing stocks fall from around 8 million tonnes to 4.6-4.8 million tonnes with an MSP revision pending. Good operators, honestly run, in an industry whose economics they do not control.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Government Dependency, Stakeholder Concentration, New Entrants, Good Enough Goods, Shifting Consumer Preferences. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Across all 16 calls management frames results in commodity-cycle terms — New York/London sugar futures, national production/closing-stock balance sheets, inventory write-downs, and swings in cane recovery and crush — while the engineering arms (gearboxes, water EPC) ride customer capex, which visibly stalled in FY26 on 'delayed decision-making' and geopolitical inactivity. Sugar PBIT fell 26.5% in Q1FY25 on lower production and off-season charges, and international prices moved from ~20 cents/lb (2022) to 14.7 cents/lb (Feb 2026).

> "we've actually seen near historic lows, such catastrophic fall-off in terms of commodity pricing has not left sugar as well unscathed" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Multi-segment mix (sugar, distillery, gearboxes, water O&M) plus ~₹1,100 crore of long-duration water O&M contracts and countercyclical capex (₹340 crore gearbox/defence programme continued through the FY26 slowdown)*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 4 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between -1% and 18% (±4.7 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 3% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 8%).

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Essentially every economic variable is set by policy: sugarcane SAP/FRP (₹300/tonne UP increase), sugar MSP frozen since 2019, DFPD monthly release quotas, DGFT export quotas, OMC-administered ethanol prices and blending targets, feedstock restrictions on rice/grain, UPERC power export tariffs (+₹31 crore), interest-subvention loans, and a water order book built on NMCG/Jal Nigam/state tenders. ## What to watch
