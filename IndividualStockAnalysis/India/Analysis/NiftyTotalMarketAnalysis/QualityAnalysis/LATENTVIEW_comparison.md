### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 2 worsened, 1 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Revenue is project-based discretionary analytics spend that swings with client budget cycles: Q3 is admittedly seasonally strong because clients exhaust budgets and year-end contracts must be delivered, while Q3 CPG/retail went flat because one-time projects produced no follow-on work and a large beverage client's projects slipped. Twelve consecutive quarters of sequential growth and ~19-20% FY26 growth bound the damage, but the demand base is discretionary transformation work rather than operations-linked consumable spend. (The long view had said: Revenue is discretionary project work tied to client analytics budgets: FY23-FY24 calls describe deals stalling, large discretionary projects not fructifying and 25-30% growth targets cut to ~19%, while Q3 is structurally strong because clients 'exhaust their budget' — a budget-cycle swing, not a commodity cycle. Q4 FY26 shows the same pattern in reverse, with one tech account consolidating and deprioritising work, producing a 'gap down' into the new fiscal.)
- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The model labs themselves are entering enterprise services — a structurally different competitor with different economics — and management addresses it defensively, arguing that models alone cannot deliver value without a services layer for governance, evaluation and provenance. The threat is stated as forward-looking rather than yet visible in pricing or win rates, so exposure is real but bounded on current evidence. (The long view had said: A structurally different competitor is entering the analytics-services market — the model labs themselves — and management addresses it defensively on the May 2026 call, reframing the threat as 'validation' that a services layer is needed. Exposure is real but so far bounded: no discounting or margin investment has been framed as a competitive response, and EBITDA held at 23-24%.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Analytics and data-engineering services are a scale business without strong brand protection at the buyer level, and AI tooling lowers the bar for cheaper challengers to deliver an acceptable outcome — management's argument that a 'strong services layer is required' is precisely a defence of why the premium should persist. Against that, the calls show pricing power rather than discounting, with a price increase secured in the second-largest account and no mention of competitive fee pressure. (The long view had said: Analytics delivery is a scale services offering where the buyer increasingly routes decisions through procurement — flagged as early as Aug 2022 ('more layers of approvals including procurement coming into the picture') and again in May 2026 as the company engages a client's 'vendor ops organization' — and where the FY26 vendor consolidation at the largest tech account shows clients able to substitute suppliers. The offering is not structurally protected from cheaper offshore or AI-assisted alternatives.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The company sits directly in the path of the largest platform shift in IT services: management devotes most of the Q4 FY26 preamble to AI, concedes market concerns that OpenAI and Anthropic building their own services arms threatens services companies, and describes the delivery model itself being rebuilt around 'forward-deployed engineers'. The economics of the entire analytics-services layer are being reset by technology the company does not own. (The long view had said: The business sits directly in the path of the largest platform shift in its industry: management devotes most of the FY26 calls to generative/agentic AI, reports ~28% of revenue with visible AI and ~21% 'under the hood', is rebuilding delivery around 'forward-deployed engineers', and must address OpenAI/Anthropic launching their own services arms — i.e. its model suppliers becoming competitors. The skill base, pricing model and headcount-linked economics all have to be re-tooled rather than incrementally improved.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Results are repeatedly explained by a handful of named relationships: the Databricks partnership alone accounts for roughly $19mn of ~$119-120mn FY26 revenue (targeting $50mn), pricing gains came from 'our second largest account', BFSI growth from 'a couple of accounts' added 12 months ago, and a whole segment went flat because one client — the world's largest beverage manufacturer, served via Decision Point — delayed project start dates. (The long view had said: Results are repeatedly moved by single accounts: the technology vertical was ~70% of revenue in Q1 FY25 and the US ~94%; Q4 FY26 opened with a 'gap down' from shrinkage and vendor consolidation at one large tech account, while Q3 FY26 growth leaned on a price increase in 'our second largest account' and a couple of BFSI logos added 12 months earlier. The stated 'diamond account' strategy — top 6 accounts, $10m today to a claimed $150m potential — deliberately increases this dependence.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (QUANT FLAG); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 8 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
