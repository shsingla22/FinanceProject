**Checks that improved:**

- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: No monopoly-like position is evidenced — the company is one of many API suppliers competing on price, with 176 molecules spread across ~900 customers rather than a dominant hold on any niche. | The long view had said: No monopoly-like position — generic APIs face routine annual price erosion that management describes as universal in the industry, and it competes across a 176-molecule basket rather than owning a customer's mind.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: Customer acquisition points to effective commercial reach — 49 new customers added in FY26 to a base of about 900 across multiple regulated and emerging geographies. | The long view had said: A base of ~900 direct customers with 49 added in FY26 implies decent commercial reach, but there is no evidence of a distinctive distribution, service-accessibility or sales-force advantage in any call.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: The core customer proposition is regulatory certainty, and Alivus delivered it — NAI classification at Dahej and an EIR at Ankleshwar in the same window that management identifies compliance and reliability as the industry's decisive purchase criterion. | The long view had said: Customers are buying regulatory reliability and quality compliance — management frames the industry shift explicitly this way — though the GPCB closure notice and the Dahej fire show the assurance is not unbreachable.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Weak. Now: This is an asset-heavy API manufacturer — INR2,552cr of revenue against a multi-hundred-crore annual capex programme across Solapur, Ankleshwar and Dahej — so asset turn is low even though margins are strong. | The long view had said: This is an asset-heavy multi-plant API manufacturer with revenue of INR 2,552cr against continuous multi-hundred-crore capex and a stated asset-turn ambition of only 1.3–1.5x sales per rupee invested, offset by genuinely high returns (~30% ROCE) and regulatory-filing protection.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Regulatory approval and USFDA-compliant capacity are meaningful entry barriers, but pricing in the category is plainly not rational given the recurring price-erosion drag on revenue. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Entry barriers are meaningful (FDA/regulatory inspections, DMF/CEP filings, multi-year plant qualification — Solapur will not serve regulated markets until late FY28), pricing behaviour is rational with no discount war, and ownership is stable under Nirma.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Mix is clearly favourable (gross margin 55.1%→60.7% on new launches and high-value products) but pricing is a headwind — management explicitly cut revenue growth below volume growth because of price erosion. | The long view had said: Growth and the entire 360bp EBITDA margin expansion are explicitly mix- and launch-led (chronic CVS/CNS, CDMO up to 7% of sales, high-value differentiated products) rather than volume-led, though underlying base pricing erodes 4–4.5% annually.

*Unchanged: Gain in market share; Structural end-market growth; Out of limelight; Halo effect (caution); Intangible benefits; Distribution advantage; Cyclical market growth; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Candor; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Disciplined stewards; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Geographical expansion; Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Profit margin; Technology advantage; Research and development; Share donators; Security by obscurity; Capex for growth (not maintenance).*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 5 unchanged; 4 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: UNKNOWN on the long view, UNKNOWN on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: NO FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: the only computable check points this way but the calls offer no evidence — numbers alone are only ever a screening hint, never a confirmed fit. (The long view had said: A B2B ingredient supplier with no A&P spend, no heritage or consistency-promise narrative, and no premium pricing; the company rebranded outright from Glenmark Life Sciences to Alivus after the Nirma buyout with no discussion of brand equity at risk, indicating the name is not the asset.)

**That weakened:**

- **Toll Roads**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Regulatory anchoring is real and repeatedly cited — 611 DMF and CEP filings by Mar-2026, US FDA EIR with NAI classification at both Dahej and Ankleshwar, and management framing the industry as needing 'quality compliance and reliable partners' — which creates registration-level switching costs typical of toll-road suppliers. But it falls short of the pattern's core test because customers clearly do negotiate: the company repeatedly flags pricing pressure capping revenue growth below volume growth. (The long view had said: Strong regulatory anchoring and switching costs — 611 DMF/CEP filings mean a customer changing API source must re-file variations, and CDMO revenue only starts once regulatory approvals land — but the API is a material, not trivial, share of a formulator's cost, and 4–4.5% annual erosion shows customers do negotiate.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (QUANT SIGNAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
