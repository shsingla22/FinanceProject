# Vedant Fashions (MANYAVAR) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Vedant Fashions (stock ticker MANYAVAR) is an Indian company that designs and sells branded Indian ethnic clothing for weddings and celebrations — it calls itself the category creator and India's largest, leading wedding and celebration wear company. It runs a "house of brands" (Manyavar, Mohey, Twamev, Mebaz, Diwas) aimed at different customer groups and price points, selling mainly through exclusive brand outlets (EBOs), plus a dealer/multi-brand network, online marketplaces and an omnichannel setup. Most stores are run by franchise partners, so the company's own reported revenue (about INR 1,436 crores in FY26) is smaller than the retail "sale of our customers" at the shop counter (about INR 2,008 crores in FY26) — it earns by selling stock into that network at very high margins (gross margin ~65-69%, EBITDA margin ~44-51%, PAT margin ~26-31%) with light working capital and strong cash conversion. It operates across India (about 669 stores, 1.79 million sq ft, 252 cities and towns as of March 2026) plus a small international presence (13 stores across three countries as of June 2022, including UAE), and its sales swing sharply with the number of auspicious wedding dates in a given quarter.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Manyavar** — The flagship and best-known brand for Indian wedding and celebration wear, carrying the group's biggest marketing pushes (the Karan Johar-hosted "Manyavar Shaadi Show" podcast, the Rinku Singh campaign, the Aranya collection) and effectively the core of the business — the company's stock ticker is literally MANYAVAR.
- **Mohey** — The women's / bridal-facing brand, marketed with campaigns like "DulhanWaaliFeeling" with Alia Bhatt and "Mohey Rang Do," and given its own dedicated exclusive brand outlets from around FY25.
- **Twamev** — The premium end of the portfolio, described as doing very well — about 40% growth and 12-16% same-store growth in FY26 — with new large flagship EBOs (a 9,000 sq ft Mumbai store) and management saying it plans to scale this premium segment faster.
- **Mebaz** — A brand kept anchored in core wedding and occasion wear, supported by its own "Celebrations Begin with Mebaz" campaign; the calls give no separate size for it.
- **Diwas** — A newer festive-wear brand launched softly around FY25, deliberately run through online marketplaces and the dealer network rather than own stores at first, with an EBO rollout to be considered only once the brand gains traction — so still small.
- **EBO retail network (own/franchise exclusive brand outlets)** — The main selling channel and the company's key operating metric — roughly 1.79 million sq ft across 669 stores in 252 cities and towns globally as of March 2026 (up from 1.28 million sq ft / 603 stores in June 2022), with management adding stores selectively while closing small or underperforming ones (about 2-2.5% of the fleet churned in a year).
- **Dealer / MBO and marketplace channel** — A secondary route to market used mainly for newer brands like Diwas and for online marketplaces, described as the starting channel before any exclusive-store rollout.
- **International stores** — A small overseas footprint serving Indian celebration wear abroad — 13 stores across three countries as of June 2022, including a store opened in Abu Dhabi, UAE.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Vedant Fashions earns a "Decent" 62 out of 100 (45% × 55 on business quality, 30% × 71 on multibagger fit, 25% × 64 on risk safety), and the reason the three scores diverge is that the same handful of traits show up as the biggest strength and the biggest worry. The quality framework's best marks go to distribution (+2) and intangible benefits (+2): a franchisee-funded network of 669 exclusive brand outlets covering 1.79 million square feet in 252 cities, carrying more than 92% of sales, and pricing power that leaves ₹73 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs. Those same two facts are what make "Brand Strength" and "Forward Integrators" the screen's only STRONG FIT patterns, and they show up in the financials as ₹48 of operating profit per ₹100 of sales, a 24% cash return on capital, and 95-98% cash conversion. Risk-wise this is also the sturdiest part of the story: stakeholder concentration is LOW because no single customer, licensor or supplier swings results, and technological innovation is LOW because in four years of calls nobody raises a platform shift that could make the format obsolete. Resilience overall reads SOUND.

The trouble is that the growth engine attached to this strong base has stalled, and the patterns and risks describe the same stall from two sides. "Market Share Gainers" is only a PARTIAL fit because the trajectory reversed — from 105% same-store growth in FY23 to just 2.7% in FY26 with revenue up 3.5%, a third consecutive year of subpar growth — and that identical evidence is what puts the New Entrants risk on WATCH, with analysts directly challenging management about Tasva and Sai Silk outgrowing them and management defending with a low-base argument rather than proof of share capture. The quality checks record the same thing independently: gain in market share (-1), geographical expansion (-1, city count barely moving from 228 to 252 with five Tier-3 cities exited), persistence of growth (-1), and growth capex (-1) as expansion is deliberately paused with net additions of only about 4,200 square feet in FY26. That is why the Growth and Capital Allocation areas both score -0.5 while Customer Benefits scores +1.25 — the company is very good at what it already does and is adding very little new ground to do it on. High margins earning a PARTIAL on "Low Price Plus" and "Cost to Replicate" reflect the same tension: the economics are excellent, but the barrier is asserted more than demonstrated.

The margin story ties the last threads together. The company has chosen not to raise prices — average selling price growth of 1.4% back in Aug 2022 with management saying they don't take direct price increases, and then absorbing the GST move from 12% to 18% on about 90% of products rather than lifting MRP. That restraint is exactly why "Pricing Power" is a LIKELY rather than confirmed fit, and it is also why Government Dependency sits on WATCH: one tax change compressed gross margin from roughly 68.8% to 65-65.7% without any government being a customer. The "Good Enough Goods" risk reads the same squeeze from the other end — a structurally simple product at 65-69% gross margin invites cheaper substitutes, and margins have drifted from 68.8% to 65% at the gross line and from 51% to about 44% at the EBITDA line while prices stayed frozen for two years. Cyclicality and Fashion Risk are both ELEVATED and both trace back to strengths already named: demand is wedding-calendar bound, so a quarter with no weddings in January can wreck it, and the brand equity built by celebrity campaigns is also a dependence on celebrity campaigns. Meanwhile Shifting Consumer Preferences is on WATCH because management itself says the middle class is the segment getting affected and that is where Manyavar is getting affected, with the answer being Twamev growing 40% on 12-16% same-store growth. Add the -2 on working capital, where about 200 days of cash sits between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and the picture is a genuinely high-return, well-defended business whose next few years hinge on whether the premium end can grow fast enough to offset a softening core.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Fashion Risk. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.3× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 71% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 26% to 34% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 2 numeric fingerprints reinforce it.

**What the calls show:** Demand is discretionary and calendar-bound rather than commodity- or capex-driven, but the swings are large: Q1 FY25 'behaved like a non-wedding quarter' with sales collapsing, and Q3 FY26 was wrecked by 'no weddings in January at all... vis-a-vis last year, there were 11 wedding dates', driving EBITDA margin from 51% (Q1 FY23) to ~44% (FY26). Revenue is tied to weddings/festivals in consumers' lives, not to any customer's capacity expansion, so this is occasion-timing volatility layered on a discretionary consumption cycle, not a pure commodity cycle.

> "this year, there were no weddings in January at all, no single date of weddings vis-a-vis last year, there were 11 wedding dates" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Occasion/repeat-purchase demand base tied to weddings that recur regardless of the industrial cycle; asset-light franchisee EBO model with 95-98% cash conversion means no heavy fixed-capital over-investment at the peak.*

- ⚠️ Year-to-year sales growth swings by ±40 percentage points — boom-and-bust revenue, not a steady compounder.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 31% and 50% (±7.3 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 21% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 1% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Fashion Risk — ELEVATED
*"Cool today, uncool tomorrow"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Management repeatedly credits growth and brand salience to celebrity moments — Alia Bhatt for Mohey, the Virat-Anushka campaign, and the Vijay Deverakonda-Rashmika 'Made for Each Other' campaign with 1bn+ views described as carrying the brand 'for the next 5 to 6 years' — and concedes merchandise mix churns faster than two years. Taste-led salience that needs periodic celebrity refresh is a real, if bounded, dependency.

> "campaign like Vijay-Rashmika helps us for the next 5 to 6 years, similar to the Virat-Anushka campaign we did in 2017" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Functional, occasion-driven repeat demand (weddings must be dressed for) anchored in cultural attire rather than trend cycles, spread across a portfolio of five brands rather than one trend bet.*

- ⚠️ Year-to-year sales growth swings by ±40 percentage points — boom-and-bust revenue, not a steady compounder.
- ⬜ Insufficient sales history.

### Government Dependency — WATCH
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Not an infrastructure/subsidy business, but a single policy change materially hit the P&L: the GST rise from 12% to 18% on 90% of products compressed gross margin from an exceptional ~68% to 65-65.7% because management chose not to pass it through, and it distorted the reported-vs-retail sales gap for a full year. Management is also explicitly waiting on policy to revive demand rather than on its own levers.

> "in 90% of our products, GST rates have increased from 12% to 18%. However... we have not increased MRP of all the products" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: No government customers, tenders or regulated pricing; exposure is indirect tax policy on a private-consumption product, and management expects the impact to normalise in the next financial year.*

### Good Enough Goods — WATCH
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** A structurally simple product carrying 65-69% gross margin invites cheaper substitutes, and the trajectory shows the classic squeeze: gross margin down from 68.8% (Q1 FY23) to 65% (Q4 FY26), EBITDA from 51% to ~44%, ASPs deliberately frozen for two years, GST absorbed rather than passed on, and management strengthening an entry Kurta-set price point of INR 2,624 after 'a lot of market surveys on the price point' to stay 'very competitive'. *Silver lining: Company controls the choice architecture — ~92% of sales through its own exclusive brand outlets with high end-consumer brand awareness, so no intermediary (unlike Nobel Biocare's dentists) can pocket the margin by substituting; premium Twamev demonstrates a defensible niche above the contested middle.*

- ✅ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs held near 75% (was 72%) — no gross-level price erosion.

## What to watch

- Same-store growth: FY26's 2.7% is the crux of both the 'Market Share Gainers' pattern downgrade and the New Entrants risk — a fourth subpar year would harden both; a clear reacceleration would repair both.
- Twamev's scaling: the Shifting Consumer Preferences risk rests entirely on the premium pivot working (40% growth, 12-16% same-store growth) — if that stalls, the softening middle-class core has no offset.
- Store and city expansion restarting: the -1 checks on 'Capex for growth' and 'Geographical expansion' (net additions of ~4,200 sq ft in FY26, 228 to 252 cities, five Tier-3 exits) mean the 'Forward Integrators' strength is no longer being extended.
- Gross and EBITDA margin direction: the 'Good Enough Goods' risk is evidenced by the slide from 68.8% to 65% gross and 51% to ~44% EBITDA — further erosion would undercut the +2 'Intangible benefits' check and the 'Pricing Power' pattern.
- Whether prices are ever raised again: two years of frozen average selling prices and the decision to absorb the 12%→18% GST is what keeps 'Pricing Power' at LIKELY rather than STRONG and keeps Government Dependency on WATCH.
- The ~200-day working-capital cycle (-2 check): any further lengthening would eat into the 24% cash return on capital and 95-98% cash conversion that anchor the Return on Capital area.
