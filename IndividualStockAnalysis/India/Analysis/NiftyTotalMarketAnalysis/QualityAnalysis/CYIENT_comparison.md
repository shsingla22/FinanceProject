**Checks that improved:**

- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Weak → Good. Now: Evidence of internal pipeline and systematic team building — the outgoing CFO moved into the COO role, a new CFO was appointed, and leadership benches were built in semiconductors and DLM. | The long view had said: Abrupt CEO exit with the MD taking interim charge and no internal successor, followed by senior leadership repeatedly hired in from outside (CEO, avionics head, alliances head, semicon CEO/CFO).
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Weak → Good. Now: Runway is real and there is some capture evidence — five consecutive quarters of >5% organic semiconductor growth, a record DLM order book at 1.5x book-to-bill — though core DET revenue was muted. | The long view had said: DET has degrown in constant currency across FY25 and into FY27 despite record order books, indicating share loss rather than capture in a growing ER&D market.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Good. Now: Growth intent is mix-led — technology/software share moving from high single digits to high teens, plus a shift toward annuity, IP and outcome-based models rather than headcount volume. | The long view had said: Growth is headcount/volume and currency driven — rupee growth of 10.6% against a constant-currency decline of 0.9% — with pricing power only ever described as available 'in pockets'.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: Capex/investment is clearly growth-directed — R&D, lab infrastructure and organic high-power ASSP development plus a funded semiconductor roadmap — but incremental ROIC is unproven with the segment still loss-making. | The long view had said: Real growth investment exists in semiconductors and DLM, but the DET capex called out is maintenance-type IT refresh and the growth spend is currently depressing returns rather than showing high incremental ROIC.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Weak → Neutral. Now: Distribution rests on ecosystem partnerships and a sales engine still being built rather than a reach rivals cannot match. | The long view had said: No distribution advantage — in the flagship semiconductor product business they were still selecting a distribution partner and hiring an India sales head in mid-2026.
- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: End markets are in secular expansion — AI data-center power demand rising from 2% to 8% of power by 2030, India's semiconductor mission, rising electronic content and exponentially growing engineering data. | The long view had said: End markets cited — ER&D/lifecycle outsourcing, rising electronic content, AI data-centre power and India's semiconductor mission — are genuinely secular, though the portfolio also carries cyclical energy and auto exposure.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: There is an aftermarket/MRO and one-stop-shop ambition, but the calls describe it as an opportunity being built rather than an established monopoly-like position. | The long view had said: No monopoly-like hold on customers; work is discretionary engineering spend that clients defer at will, with no aftermarket lock-in yet monetised.
- **Distribution to shareholders** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: A ₹720cr buyback at ₹1,125 was announced on an explicit undervaluation rationale, completed within the quarter, with promoters and KMP abstaining, inside a stated 40–50% payout policy. | The long view had said: Completed a Rs.720 crore buyback extinguishing 5.76% of capital after a sustained de-rating, with promoters and KMP abstaining — a disciplined, shareholder-aligned action.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: The customer base is corporate and the value proposition is correctly matched — reliability, lifecycle TCO and data/platform lock-in rather than brand intangibles. | The long view had said: Customers are corporate/OEM, and the lifecycle-and-MRO push is the right TCO/reliability play, but demonstrated switching-cost lock-in is limited given how quickly programs were deferred.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Decades-long product lifecycles and embedded engineering data create genuine switching costs, which management is converting into multi-year annuity engagements. | The long view had said: Some embedded, multi-year customer positions (dedicated Allegro CoE, decades-long largest aerospace client) create modest stickiness, but nothing approaching a captive aftermarket or regional monopoly.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: There is direct customer intimacy and active go-to-market investment — executive-level CSAT surveys shaping strategy plus investment in sales channels and engagement. | The long view had said: Deliberate investments in customer intimacy and local presence (customer success partners for top 100 accounts, Sydney office) are offset by an admitted need to rebuild go-to-market teams in key geographies.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Good. Now: A genuine multi-product technology push exists — custom ASIC, GaN, high- and low-power ASSP, patents and OCP standards participation — but much of it is newly acquired or still in development rather than a proven edge. | The long view had said: Real IP is being assembled (Kinetic's ~250 products and 100 patents, GaN launches, agentic MRO platform) but the moat is asserted prospectively while the bulk of revenue remains people-based services.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: DLM shows a classic trough-to-upturn setup: muted FY26 revenue but the highest order book in company history entering FY27. | The long view had said: Mixed cycle position: aerospace/transportation is in a multi-quarter upturn while energy and automotive are contracting, so no clean bottom-of-cycle setup.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: R&D is visibly producing output — 7 new GaN products launched, 4 patents filed, a >$100m ASIC pipeline — though efficiency is unproven and the spend is currently depressing profitability. | The long view had said: R&D is now substantial and producing patents and products in semiconductors, but it is pre-revenue with a 2-3 year commercialisation cycle and is currently reducing profitability, so long-term R&D efficiency is unproven.

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 2 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Revenue rides on customer engineering and capex programmes across aerospace, energy and semis, and the calls repeatedly flag geopolitical instability, supply-chain disruption and muted DLM revenue in Q4 FY26; the deliberate pivot to lifecycle/annuity and aftermarket-MRO work is an explicit attempt to move demand from project-based capex toward operations-linked flow. (The long view had said: Revenue is tied to customers' discretionary ER&D and project budgets rather than their operations, and the calls repeatedly show this swinging results: aerospace recovery pushed out 12-18 months in FY23, sustainability/energy projects rolling off at end of life-cycle in FY25 producing a full-year degrowth of ~2.5% and margins falling from 16.2% to 13.5%, and in Q1 FY27 an -8.2% quarter-on-quarter contraction in strategic units driven by energy plus 'slowness in awarding of discretionary projects'.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Management explicitly paused a transformational acquisition (Project Astro) after taking a large diligence charge because AI's rapid evolution made them unsure what it means for the target's business — an admission that AI is resetting the economics of the services they sell; the whole TAO Digital rationale is that pure-play mechanical/electronics ER&D is becoming legacy and must converge with software, data and AI. (The long view had said: As a headcount-linked engineering services provider, Cyient sits directly in AI's path, and management now addresses it defensively — Sukamal Banerjee closes the Q1 FY27 call asserting domain expertise 'is not something AI can take over' — while simultaneously buying TAO Digital and building an agentic MRO platform to stay on the right side of the shift.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: NO SIGNAL → WATCH. Why now: The demand base itself is migrating away from what Cyient historically sold: management states customers no longer want pure-play engineering but software/data/AI convergence, corroborated by two years of CSAT feedback (70% of executives polled), and notes that in aerospace 'there are no significant new designs' — the traditional design-services pool is shrinking even as execution holds up. (The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and the only computed fingerprint is clean.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Good Enough Goods (ELEVATED); New Entrants (NO SIGNAL); Fashion Risk (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
