### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 6 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 4 weakened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The four calls discuss NSV, retail LTL, online B2C and wholesale billing only; there is no mention of subscriptions, AMC/service revenue, renewals, attach rates or deferred revenue as a funding source. (The long view had said: Across all four calls the revenue model is one-off apparel sale through retail/online/wholesale — LTL, sell-through and discounting are the metrics discussed; there is no licence fee, subscription, AMC or deferred-revenue funding stream, and royalty in fact flows OUT of the company as an expense (AFL is a licensee of USPA/PVH).)

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Brand investment is real and rising (A&P up ~50bps in Q1 FY27, 'marquee advertising campaigns', new retail identity) and U.S. Polo compounds at 20–25%+, but the heritage sits with licensors (USPA, PVH's Tommy/Calvin Klein), and PVH brands and Arrow were visibly knocked off course by a GST rate change and a weak wedding calendar — brand strength did not insulate pricing. (The long view had said: Five heritage brands with sustained A&P above 4% of sales, marquee events (USPA Legends, Tommy Hilfiger's Mumbai visit) and repeated adjacency stretch — footwear, innerwear, kids, womenswear now 24% of business — plus repositioning of Flying Machine that produced double-digit LTL and 70% B2C growth; the caveat is that the marquee brands are licensed, not owned.)
- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Within the window management set FY26 objectives (1.5 lakh sq ft, double-digit growth, ROCE) and largely delivered (1.4 lakh sq ft, 14% growth, 23% ROCE), names ROCE as its 'North Star', and discloses weak spots plainly (Arrow subdued, PVH GST hit, wage-code charge) — but the board/leadership record shows heavy churn (multiple chairs and the MD changing across FY24–FY26), which cuts against long-tenured stability. (The long view had said: Promises made in early calls were delivered in later ones: Sephora proceeds used to retire debt (net debt INR 476cr → ~INR 260cr), the guided 100 bps annual EBITDA expansion delivered, ROCE taken from small negative to 20% (FY25) and 23%+ (FY26); management also owns unflattering facts openly (70 stores closed, Arrow subdued, DTA charge, footwear BIS disruption) and Lalbhai family ownership provides continuity through a CEO transition.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Growth of 11.3% → 14.5% → 14.8% → 15.5% with retail LTL of 7.8–11.6% in a stable demand environment implies share gain, and it is accompanied by rising margins and ROCE rather than bought with discounting — but management never cites explicit market-share numbers or names competitors. (The long view had said: Growth accelerated from 8.5% (FY25) to 14% (FY26) to 15.5% (Q1FY27) against a soft apparel market, and management repeatedly claims share gains while insisting growth be profitable — but explicit share percentages versus named competitors are never given, so the claim rests on relative growth rates.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Nothing in these calls articulates a barrier a funded newcomer could not rebuild: the strongest assets are licensed brands (USPA, PVH) held under contract plus an EBO and dotcom network, and the business proved sensitive to GST rate changes, BIS norms and West Asia supply/forex shocks within a single year. (The long view had said: The durable assets are long-held India licence rights to USPA/Tommy/Calvin Klein plus a physical EBO estate and D2C stack built over years — hard to assemble quickly, but the core brands are licensed rather than owned, and management itself notes only one strong rival in denim, so a well-funded entrant could contest positions rather than face an absolute barrier.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Forward Integrators (STRONG FIT); Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 4 eased, 1 worsened, 2 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: Across all four calls management describes demand as stable and revenue as driven by like-for-like retail growth, square-footage addition and online D2C — consumption-linked, repeat-purchase apparel, not commodity prices or customer capex; growth was remarkably consistent (11.3%, 14.5%, 14.8%, 15.5%) with EBITDA margin expanding every quarter rather than swinging. (The long view had said: Discretionary apparel demand swings with the consumer cycle — the calls run from 'soft market condition' and 'difficult demand environment' in FY24-25 to 'demand environment remains stable' in FY26, with FY27 flagging consumption-slowdown risk from West Asia-driven inflation, cotton/forex pressure. But this is a flow product bought out of household operating spend, not customer capex, and AFL invested countercyclically through the downturn — 1.2-1.4 lakh sq ft added annually and marketing raised to 4%+ of sales while demand was weak — with margins rising every year regardless (EBITDA 12% to 13.4%, ROCE negative to 23%).)
- **Fashion Risk**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: This is discretionary branded apparel where demand rests on taste: Flying Machine has been explicitly repositioned around 'on-trend expression' for Gen Z, and Arrow was called out as subdued on a model change and a weak wedding calendar, showing quarter-level sensitivity to trend and occasion. Bounded, though, because the stated strategy anchors on the five largest, largely staple categories. (The long view had said: The entire price premium is brand desirability, and management concedes it must keep raising marketing as a share of revenue 'to keep our brand salient' — a permanent tax, not a one-off. Two of five brands needed rescue inside the window: Arrow went loss-making post-COVID and was still subdued on a 'onetime model change and a weak wedding calendar' in FY26, and Flying Machine required a wholesale repositioning to a 'unisex denim-anchored, on-trend youth brand' to recover.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The relevant shift is channel — consumers moving online — and the calls show the company capturing rather than losing it: online B2C grew 38–50% every quarter with share rising from ~11% to 18%, direct channels moving from ~50% to 62% of sales, and a deliberate pivot away from online B2B. No volume decline is attributed to changing habits. (The long view had said: Two genuine migrations are reshaping the demand base — casualization away from formals (which is exactly where Arrow sits and where Arrow lags) and the shift of buying to online, where B2C has gone from a minor channel to 18% of sales growing 38-40%. AFL is on the right side of both rather than defending: it is pivoting the portfolio toward casual/denim/Gen Z and deliberately reallocating from online B2B to owned B2C.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The calls affirmatively show the price premium holding rather than being defended: full-price sell-through up, discounting down, gross margin up 210bps, 50bps, 20bps and 90bps across the four quarters to 56.7%, premiumisation working at USPA, and management planning selective price increases rather than price defence. There is no discussion of retailer own-label or cheaper substitutes taking share. (The long view had said: Branded apparel sits directly in the path of private label and cheap online substitutes, and management is asked point-blank how it prices against the many labels on marketplaces. The affirmative defence is real but not absolute: gross margin rose 130bps in FY25 and a further 90bps to 56.7% in Q1 FY27 on lower discounting and higher full-price sell-through, and by selling 62% direct AFL removes the intermediary that decides for the customer — unlike the Nobel Biocare dentist. Residual exposure sits in the wholesale/department-store and marketplace channels where retailers push own-label.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The portfolio is built on licensed brands the company does not own — USPA (US Polo Association) and PVH's Tommy Hilfiger and Calvin Klein — and USPA alone (~INR2,000 crore) 'led the pack' with 20–25%+ growth in every call while other brands were muted, so results depend on one licensor's brand plus PVH's decisions; PVH's own global sourcing change flowed straight into AFL's inventory and supply in Q1 FY27. The Flipkart stake in Flying Machine also had to be bought back to control that brand. (The long view had said: AFL's economics rest almost entirely on licensed/JV brands it does not own — U.S. Polo Assn (licensor USPA, whose Global CEO Michael Prince appears at AFL's own marketing events), Tommy Hilfiger and Calvin Klein (PVH JV, driving the minority interest line) and Arrow — and a single brand, USPA, is roughly half of group revenue by FY26 (~INR2,400-2,500 cr on ~INR5,000 cr), described as leading the pack every quarter. PVH's own global sourcing changes flowed straight into AFL's Q1 FY27 inventory commentary, and the Safilo pattern (licence loss to a stronger retail player) is the live tail risk; the only structural offset is the acquisition of the residual Flying Machine stake, one of the few brands they now fully control.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
