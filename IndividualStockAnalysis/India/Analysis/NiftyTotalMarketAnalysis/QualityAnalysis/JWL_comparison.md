**Checks that improved:**

- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: The company is building direct reach in EVs — one Bengaluru showroom in June 2025 to ~10 dealerships by Nov 2025 with 4-5 more planned — though this is nascent and small relative to the group. | The long view had said: Some direct customer intimacy via repeat private-sector relationships and OEM bodybuilding ties, but no evidence of a distinctive accessibility or sales-force advantage.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: The INR 2,500 crore Odisha greenfield forged wheel/axle plant (100,000 wheelsets p.a.) plus Indore cell-to-battery line and container capacity expansion are clearly growth, not maintenance, capex, with equipment ordered and construction advanced. | The long view had said: Sustained large growth capex — INR 1,000–1,500 cr for wheelset backward integration plus the Odisha greenfield plant — but returns are still pending and commissioning has slipped, so incremental ROIC is unproven.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: Wagon manufacturing is a tender-driven industry where the single largest buyer, Indian Railways, holds all the negotiating power over timing and price, and new wheelset capacity is entering. | The long view had said: The wagon industry is a concentrated few-player market, but the largest end customer (Indian Railways) holds overwhelming negotiating power through L1 tendering and can stall the entire industry by delaying tenders.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: RDSO approvals and Indian Railways vendor status create regulatory gatekeeping, but JWL is one of several approved wagon builders and explicitly faces new wheelset capacity from Titagarh/RK Forgings. | The long view had said: Sole or near-sole supplier position in specialised wagon designs and a scarce approved-source position in braking, giving a monopoly-like standing in specific niches rather than the whole market.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.4 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: RDSO approval and heavy capex are real barriers, but capacity is actively being added by competitors in wheelsets and the BESS segment is crowded and import-driven. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.4 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Genuine regulatory/technical barriers (UIC and RDSO approvals, 2–3 year homologation, only 3–4 globally approved brake sources), tempered by L1 price bidding for railway orders and new wheelset capacity from the Titagarh–RK Forgings JV.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Neutral → Weak. Now: Leadership is bought in rather than grown — a new CFO in Nov 2025 (after CFO churn in the 5-year history) and Mark Damian Stevenson as Additional Director — with no mention of internal pipelines or talent development. | The long view had said: Leadership gaps are filled by external hires (Mark Damian Stevenson as director, a new CFO replacing Sanjiv Keshri) and the five-year history shows high churn in Chairman/MD roles, with no evidence of a systematic internal pipeline.

- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Neutral. Now: Backward integration and RDSO approvals give some captive-supply advantage, but the dominant customer is Indian Railways via open tenders — the opposite of switching-cost lock-in. | The long view had said: Switching costs arise from RDSO/UIC certification cycles and design lock-in, evidenced by repeat business from the same private customers and a captive Tatravagonka offtake of ~50,000 wheelsets annually.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Forward Integrators**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Jupiter Electric Mobility moved to owned retail — first Bengaluru showroom in June 2025 scaling to ~10 dealerships by Nov 2025 with 4-5 more planned — but no store economics, royalty or D2C revenue share is disclosed, and the dominant strategic direction on these calls is backward integration, not forward. (The long view had said: The entire strategic direction is backward integration (wheelsets, brakes, couplers, Bonatrans, Odisha greenfield), and management deliberately refuses the one move toward the customer — wagon leasing — leaving it to partner GATX.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Named wins in adjacent segments (9,000 LHB axles, 5,376 Vande Bharat wheelsets, long-term Tatravagonka export supply, 110MW BESS) suggest share gain in components and clean energy, but no explicit share percentages are given and the consolidated order book fell every quarter of FY26 from INR5,972cr to INR4,675cr. (The long view had said: Dominance of the private wagon segment is claimed repeatedly and consistently (60-70% share in FY24, private book at 70% of order book by FY26, 100+ rakes delivered versus a distant next competitor), but revenue fell from INR 3,600cr in FY24 to INR 2,961cr in FY26 with FY27 guided muted, so this reads as holding a strong position rather than persistent compounding share gain.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No cost-culture language, itemised savings or no-frills positioning appears in any of the four calls; margin commentary is about volume and mix, not a structural cost advantage. (The long view had said: Management repeatedly frames backward integration (brakes, wheelsets, couplers, crossings — 'full backward integration' achieved via Stone India's RDSO approval in FY26) as the route to best quality at lowest price and says railway-side margin gains come only from efficiency, but there is no itemised cost culture and container pricing needs a PLI subsidy to match China.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
