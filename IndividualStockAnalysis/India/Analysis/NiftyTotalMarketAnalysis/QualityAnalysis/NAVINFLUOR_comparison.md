**Checks that improved:**

- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Growth is explicitly led by realization and mix — margins expanded 992bps for FY26 to 32.6% while revenue grew 41%, with management attributing it to favourable mix, better realisation and operating leverage. | The long view had said: Management explicitly attributes FY26's ~990bps margin gain 70% to new capacity/volume and only 30% to pricing actions, so growth is volume-led, though genuine pricing power and a richer CDMO/specialty mix are demonstrated.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Evidence of share capture from a non-dominant base — new molecule wins from global innovators, CDMO growing 61% y-o-y, and R32 capacity being expanded under available Kigali entitlement — though no explicit market-share data is given. | The long view had said: Clear share capture as global innovators de-risk from China, evidenced by moving from transactional supply to technology partner, R32 quota-backed capacity, and sole-site status for the Chemours product.
- **Distribution to shareholders** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: A consistent dividend policy is evident (₹6.5 interim, ₹8.6 final) but there are no buybacks, and the ₹750cr QIP at ₹4,680/share means the company was issuing rather than repurchasing equity — sensible, but not the buyback-at-low-valuation discipline this parameter rewards. | The long view had said: A regular, earnings-linked dividend (Rs 8.6/share final, 430% of face value) alongside a near-zero-debt balance sheet, but no buyback discipline or articulated distribution policy is evidenced.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: The refrigerant-gas cycle is already firmly in upswing and the company is harvesting it (six consecutive quarters of growth, firm pricing), so it sits mid-to-late cycle rather than near a bottom, with an analyst flagging that a government notification allows competing plants until 2027. | The long view had said: The company invested through the FY24-25 agrochem destocking trough and is now riding the refrigerant/agro upturn, but the cyclical upswing is already substantially in the numbers rather than ahead.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Excellent → Good. Now: A real multi-product fluorination platform spanning refrigerants, fluoro-specialties, CDMO and now electronic-grade HF and advanced materials, though key technologies for HF purity and Opteon are licensed from partners rather than proprietary. | The long view had said: A deep, escalating fluorination capability stack (helix to HF to complex chemistries) spanning HPP, specialty, CDMO and now high-purity electronic gases, evidenced by stabilising a licensed plant in four weeks versus a three-to-six-month expectation.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: The customer base is entirely corporate (global pharma innovators, agro majors, Chemours) and the benefit offered is correctly matched — reliability plus validation-based lock-in and long-term contracts rather than brand appeal. | The long view had said: Customer base is entirely corporate (top-five global agro innovators, big pharma, Honeywell, Chemours) and the benefit offered — reliability, TCO through route optimisation, and validation-based lock-in — matches that type precisely.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. Now: R&D is repeatedly credited with winning new molecules and innovator validations (fluoro intermediates for a novel AI, new specialty launches, advanced materials), but no R&D spend or efficiency metric is disclosed to judge intensity. | The long view had said: R&D spend (Surat R&D/pilot-plant upgradation) consistently converts into commercialised molecules — ~13 new molecules in FY26 plus named agro wins with Rs 40-50cr and Rs 20-30cr peak revenue potential and advanced-materials/semiconductor programs.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 4 weakened, 5 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Management leans on reputation and trust with global innovators ('globally trusted provider') and long customer relationships, but there is no heritage/A&P/consumer-brand machinery or evidence of brand-driven premium pricing in these calls. (The long view had said: No heritage story, A&P spend, distributor leverage or brand-adjacency discussion anywhere in the calls; 'globally trusted provider' is customer-relationship language, not brand economics.)
- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Promises made in the Aug 2025 call — AHF commissioning, cGMP4 Phase 1 commercialisation, Chemours and R32 timelines — were tracked and delivered in the Feb/May 2026 calls, alongside a conservative balance sheet (net debt/equity 0.01x), explicit return-ratio focus and stable promoter-led leadership. (The long view had said: Promises made on old calls were delivered on later ones — Honeywell Dahej, cGMP-4 Phase 1, AHF and the Wave-1 capex all commissioned broadly on the timelines given, guidance that FY25 would be 'a story of two halves' played out, and the balance sheet stayed conservative (net debt/equity 0.38x falling to 0.01x, working capital 90→74 days, ROCE 21%); offsetting this is notable churn at the top — CFO Ketan Sablok's exit, MD Radhesh Welling replaced by Nitin Kulkarni in June 2024 — against the pattern's stable long-tenured leadership marker.)

**That weakened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Each of the four calls names new molecules, tech tie-ups (Buss ChemTech electronic-grade HF, Chemours Opteon) and a growing pipeline funded by high margins, but R&D spend is never disclosed or quantified as a share of industry R&D. (The long view had said: Sustained, disclosed R&D investment (Surat R&D/pilot-plant upgrade from FY23, new capability capex) converting into named new molecules with stated revenue potential every year — a Rs.40-50cr agro molecule and Rs.20-30cr Japanese patented product in FY25, ~13 new molecules in FY26 — plus co-development with global majors into semiconductor/advanced materials.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Capacity additions are framed as consolidating position and maximising the company's Kigali entitlement, and all three verticals grew 35-61% y-o-y, but no explicit market-share numbers or comparisons versus named competitors are given. (The long view had said: Clear evidence of winning share of global majors' supply chains (China+1 dual-sourcing, moving from transactional supplier to 'technology partner', 44% nine-month FY26 growth while peers cite a weak agchem cycle, 50-55 CDMO molecules), but management never quotes explicit market-share percentages or share versus named competitors.)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls revenue is described as order-book and campaign-based supply (POs, scale-up orders, repeat orders), with no licence fees, AMC, subscription or attach-rate discussion; working capital is positive (74 days) rather than customer-funded. (The long view had said: No discussion across the 16 calls of licence fees, AMC/service contracts, renewal or attach rates, or customer advances/deferred revenue as a funding source; revenue is order- and contract-based supply of molecules.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Sales are direct B2B to global innovators, agro majors and refrigerant OEM/blend customers; there is no prescriber/installer channel, no trade training programme mentioned in any of the four calls. (The long view had said: Nothing in the calls describes prescribers, installers or trade professionals influencing purchase; sales are direct B2B to global innovators and big pharma.)

*Unchanged: Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
