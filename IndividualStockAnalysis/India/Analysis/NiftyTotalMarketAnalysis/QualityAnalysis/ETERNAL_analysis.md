# Eternal (ETERNAL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Eternal (formerly Zomato) is an Indian consumer internet company that runs delivery apps: customers order restaurant food or everyday grocery and household items on their phone and a delivery rider brings it in minutes. It makes money mainly by taking a cut (a "take rate") of each order's value from restaurants, selling advertising to restaurants and brands on its apps, charging customers delivery fees and paid-membership (Gold) plans, and, in quick commerce, by earning a margin on goods sold out of its own network of small neighbourhood warehouses called dark stores. It operates in India, city by city — starting from the Delhi/NCR region and expanding across other cities including the south — with roughly 3,000 dark stores targeted by March 2026 and over 30 million monthly transacting users on the quick-commerce app alone. Management measures the business on gross/net order value (GOV/NOV), contribution per order and adjusted EBITDA, and is targeting about $1 billion of EBITDA by FY29.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Food delivery (Zomato)** — The original app that lets people order cooked meals from restaurants for home delivery, earning commissions, restaurant ad spend and customer delivery charges; it is the mature, cash-generating leg — it reached adjusted EBITDA breakeven back in FY23, was still growing order value about 20% a year in recent calls, and management targets a roughly 4-5% margin on order value.
- **Quick commerce (Blinkit)** — Ten-minute delivery of groceries and daily-needs products from company-run dark stores, acquired and pivoted into quick commerce from January 2022 after an initial ~$320 million investment budget; it is now the main growth engine and the focus of most analyst questions, with a guided ~60% order-value CAGR over three years, a 3,000-store target by March 2026, and a long-term margin goal raised to about 6% of order value.
- **Going-out / District** — A separate app and brand for dining-out and other going-out experiences (restaurant discovery, bookings, events), built to reuse traffic from the main app to keep customer-acquisition costs low; it is the smallest and newest leg, with management guiding only to profitability around FY30.
- **Bistro** — A newer, smaller venture discussed as Eternal's answer to lower-priced, affordability-focused food ordering (compared by analysts to a rival's cheaper delivery model); the calls treat it as an early-stage option rather than a material contributor.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

The strongest thing about Eternal is that it is visibly taking business away from everyone else, and it is doing so on the strength of service rather than price. The quality work scores its customer benefits highest of all seven areas, and the reason given is reliability: customers pick the platform because the assortment is there and the service is consistent, to the point that they will accept an order split in two rather than go elsewhere. That translates straight into the screen's single most convincing pattern, Market Share Gainers, rated a strong fit on disclosed, repeated evidence — food delivery order value compounding around 30% a year from FY20 to FY24 and still around 20% in FY26, and Blinkit moving from third or fourth place in southern markets to the leading position on order value. Both end markets are still expanding on their own, with food delivery having compounded at roughly 20-30% for years and quick commerce guided to grow about 60% a year over three years with no sign of users saturating. Management is the other pillar: the Corporate Culture pattern is also a strong fit, resting on promises that were actually kept — the 2022 profitability target, the cut in the Blinkit spending budget from $400m to $320m, and the 3,000-store plan reaffirmed in May 2026 — and the quality checks separately credit the same team for refusing discount-led growth, staying organic rather than buying growth, and spending on marketing when rivals were pulling back.

The awkward part is that the very things that make the growth story work are also what create the risks and drag down the financial scores. To own the customer experience end to end, Eternal pays for its own network — 3,000 company-funded dark stores guided by March 2026, its own warehousing, its own kitchens — which is what the screen recognises as forward integration and as a physical barrier that is genuinely expensive to copy. That same choice is why the capital returns look poor: every ₹100 of sales produces only ₹2 of operating profit, and every ₹100 of capital in the business produced ₹3 of operating cash this year, which is why Return on Capital is by far the weakest quality area. The second overlap is sharper. Quick commerce mostly sells packaged goods, staples and groceries that look the same wherever you buy them, so the same business that is winning share is also the one exposed to the Good Enough Goods risk, rated elevated, and management admits it has to match rivals' prices and keep pushing margin back into lower prices. That admission is exactly why Pricing Power is only a likely fit rather than a clear one: in food delivery the platform fee has been raised repeatedly while users and orders still grew, but in Blinkit order values are flat to falling and price matching is forced. The New Entrants risk is elevated for the same reason — well-funded rivals buying customers with subsidies, competitive intensity described as peaking by Q1FY27, and FY27 growth guidance already cut from 100% to 70-80%. The screen's own barrier check agrees: profit margins have swung by about 57 points, the infrastructure is a real barrier but the pricing behaviour around it is plainly irrational. Even the brand advantage cuts both ways — the cheap customer acquisition management celebrated in Q4FY26 happened because competitors briefly retreated, which is a benefit that can be withdrawn as easily as it arrived.

Set against that, the risk review is reassuring on the things that usually break consumer businesses. Four of the eight risk channels come out low: demand is driven by how often people order rather than by construction cycles or commodity prices, no single customer, restaurant or supplier can swing the numbers, the shift from physical shops to online is moving demand toward Eternal rather than away from it, and the categories are functional repeat purchases rather than anything fashionable, with a cohort's spending tripling over three years. Two channels sit on watch: the industry's format has already been reset once, and the cost base is exposed to gig-worker policy, with state minimum wage increases already cited as a real drag on Blinkit's costs by July 2026. Overall resilience is judged sound, and risk safety scores 72 — the best of the three inputs — against 59 for business quality and 56 for pattern fit, producing a combined 61 out of 100 and a Decent, three-star rating. The honest reading is a company with a genuine and improving position in growing markets, run by people who have done what they said they would, but whose economics are still thin, whose growth is lumpy from year to year despite averaging about 81% a year, and which is doing all of this in full public view — the quality work flags the opposite of an under-the-radar situation, with the largest global brokers on every call and the maximum amount of capital and competitive attention pointed at the sector. The rating hinges on whether the physical network eventually converts share leadership into real profit before competition and wage costs take that chance away.

### Every check, area by area

**Capital Allocation — Good (+0.75)** · 4 of 6 checks had evidence

- **Cost of working capital — Good** *(from the financial statements)*: About 2 days of cash stays stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers — a short, healthy cycle.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance) — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: Growth capex is high and rising — dark stores scaling toward 3,000 with ~INR 700cr capex in a single quarter and larger stores/warehousing — and management says these investments are already lifting margins (QC guidance raised to ~6%), though returns are still thin and unproven at scale.
  > "over time, we have increased the capex per store in our business and some of these investments are leading to increase in efficiency in the business" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Advertisement and promotion — Good** *(from the conference calls)*: A&P is deployed opportunistically and counter-cyclically for customer acquisition at low CAC rather than as sustained discounting, but it builds users more than durable brand equity and management refuses to break it out by business.
  > "We feel a lot of our competitors in the market have pulled back on this so we're seeing extremely low cost of customer acquisition" — *management, on an earnings call*
- **Merger and acquisition — Neutral** *(from the conference calls)*: The one major deal (Blinkit) was large and loss-making at acquisition — the opposite of a small bolt-on of an already-strong asset — but management budgeted it tightly, cut the guided investment from $400m to $320m, and it has since become the group's growth and profit engine.
  > "we wanted to now update that overall budget and guidance from $400 million down to about $320 million" — *management, on an earnings call*

**Return on Capital — Weak (-1.33)** · 3 of 3 checks had evidence

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

**Overall:** the company partly passes the foundation every multibagger shares (steady cash + high returns on capital + growth), 1 of 3 testable checks passing, and fits 4 of the 11 patterns: Market Share Gainers, Corporate Culture, Pricing Power, Innovation Dominance.

### Market Share Gainers — STRONG FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence and the numbers agree (1 of 1 check computable supporting).

**What the calls show:** Persistent, disclosed share gain across the four years: food delivery GOV compounding ~30% FY20-FY24 and still ~20% in FY26, Blinkit going from number three or four in southern markets to leading player by NOV, share taken from modern retail and e-commerce, with 60% NOV CAGR guided to FY29 — and management repeatedly refuses to buy share with subsidies.

> "Now, we are the leading player by NOV in most of these markets and we strongly feel that discount-led growth is not sustainable" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Sales compounding at 81% while margins held or improved (-93% → 2%) — growth that was WON, not bought with price cuts.

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** Promises made on early calls were delivered — the Aug 2022 Zomato adjusted-EBITDA breakeven target and the cut in the Blinkit investment budget from $400m to $320m, then the 3,000-store guidance reaffirmed in May 2026 ('we remain firmly on track'); management owns failures publicly (the southern-market discounting approach that 'didn't work'), declines to give metrics it cannot defend, and refuses growth it deems unhealthy.

> "our initial approach in those markets was like typical e-commerce thinking... That didn't work for us initially in some markets in the south" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Pricing Power — LIKELY FIT
*"Raises prices, keeps the customers"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and every one of the 2 computable checks supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** In food delivery they have raised platform fees consistently while MTUs and order growth accelerated (May 2026, Jul 2026) and take rates have risen since Aug 2022 without volume loss; but Blinkit shows the opposite — flat-to-falling NAOV, price matching forced by competitors, and management saying they do not expect NAOV to grow.

> "increase the overall revenue per order by increasing the platform fee and then channel that revenue to select cohort of customers" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹45 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ✅ Operating margin moved from about -93% (early years) to 2% (recent years) — the widening-profitability signature of pricing power / lock-in.

### Innovation Dominance — LIKELY FIT
*"Out-inventing the competition"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and every one of the 2 computable checks supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** A steady cadence of named new businesses with real revenue impact — Blinkit pivot (2022), Gold, District (2024), Bistro (2026) — and category/assortment expansion cited as a core growth block; but there is no R&D disclosure, no share-of-industry-R&D framing, and gross margins are thin rather than innovation-funded.

> "we're planning to launch this as a separate app and brand. We want to take the advantage of the traffic that we have on the Zomato app" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹45 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ✅ High retained margin (45% of sales) alongside 81% yearly growth — the profitable-innovation flywheel: margins fund the spending that drives the growth.

### Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) — PARTIAL
*"Sells once, earns forever"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (2 of 3 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Zomato Gold is a paid subscription that management says covers ~50-55% of food-delivery GOV (Aug 2024 call), and it demonstrably reduces customer delivery charges — but there is no discussion of attach rates, renewals, deferred revenue or customer advances as a funding source, and the core business is transactional commission/take-rate revenue, not licence or AMC streams.

> "almost half of our GOV is from Gold members... we are ballpark in the same 50-55% zone today on the Zomato Gold membership base" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Only about 2 days of cash is tied up in the trade cycle.
- ✅ Capital spending is about 3% of sales — growth needs little capital.
- ❌ Operating cash was positive in 3 of 7 years — not fully consistent.

### Friendly Middleman — PARTIAL
*"The expert sells it for you"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls are silent and the numbers are mixed (1 of 2 checks supporting).

- ❌ Operating margin of 2% — too thin for a protected franchise.
- ✅ Operating margin moved from about -93% (early years) to 2% (recent years) — the widening-profitability signature of pricing power / lock-in.

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 1 check supporting).

**What the calls show:** Management explicitly rejects discount-led price competition as a trap and instead frames price reduction as a scale dividend funded by improving unit economics and higher capex-per-store efficiency (Jul 2026), plus cost discipline on fixed costs (Aug 2022) — but there is no itemised cost-culture disclosure or no-frills positioning, and margins are being built up rather than deliberately kept thin.

> "prices can come off with scale, but then that's a choice that we make to pass on the benefits of scale to customers rather than trying to compete on price with someone else" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹45 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 1.3× a year — not the high-turnover, low-margin machine this pattern needs.

### Brand Strength — PARTIAL
*"The name itself sells"*

## What to watch

- Whether the FY27 growth guidance, already cut from 100% to 70-80% under the New Entrants risk, holds or gets cut again — that is the clearest test of whether the Market Share Gainers pattern survives peak competitive intensity.
- Whether Blinkit's flat-to-falling order values and forced price matching reverse — until they do, Pricing Power stays a split verdict and the elevated Good Enough Goods risk stays live.
- Whether the 3% cash return on capital and ₹2 operating profit per ₹100 of sales improve as the 3,000 owned dark stores mature — the Forward Integrators and Cost to Replicate patterns only pay off if the capital they consume eventually earns a return.
- Whether margins stop swinging by roughly 57 points a year, which is what drives the negative Barriers to Entry and Rationality check and the weak Persistence of Growth score despite an 81% average growth rate.
- Whether gig-worker wage and social-security rules spread beyond the state minimum wage increases already hitting Blinkit's costs, which would move Government Dependency off watch and squeeze the already thin margin further.
