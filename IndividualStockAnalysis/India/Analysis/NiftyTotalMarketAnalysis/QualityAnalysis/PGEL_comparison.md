**Checks that improved:**

- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Weak → Neutral. Now: PGEL ships to brands rather than owning distribution, and its logistics were shown to be fragile when a truck shortage stranded ~₹120cr of finished goods at the plant. | The long view had said: Distribution sits explicitly with the brand customers, not PGEL, and the FY26 truck shortage that stranded Rs.120cr of finished goods at the plant shows logistics reach is a vulnerability rather than an advantage.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Weak. Now: Some stickiness exists via platform investment and anchor-customer wallet share, but switching costs are evidently weak given order cancellations and continuous OEM price renegotiation. | The long view had said: Real but modest switching costs exist via co-developed ODM platforms and qualification, evidenced by zero client losses in five years and rising wallet share, yet brands retain in-sourcing capacity and annually renegotiate price.
- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Neutral. Now: Genuinely candid at the start of the year ('we got caught off guard', early guidance cut), but credibility eroded when they insisted on the guidance in February and then missed FY26 PAT badly (₹193.6cr vs ₹300–310cr guided), explained almost entirely by external shocks. | The long view had said: Unusually candid throughout — admitting the fully-automatic washing machine push disappointed as early as FY23, and in FY26 quantifying each self-inflicted and external hit (Rs.300cr LPG-related production loss, Rs.120cr truck shortage, Rs.120cr PBT impact) rather than blurring them.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Weak. Now: Technology is largely sourced rather than owned — POS entry depends on a PAX Global agreement and the compressor venture is gated on Chinese government clearance; no R&D intensity or proprietary edge is claimed. | The long view had said: Some genuine multi-product platform capability (AC, washer, cooler, TV ODM platforms; now compressors and refrigerant) but management itself frames the edge as cost first and product second, and no R&D intensity is ever disclosed to support a deep technology moat.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: No monopoly-like hold on the customer's mind — brands cancelled 50–70% of planned orders across June–August at will and there is no aftermarket or service annuity. | The long view had said: PGEL is the largest Indian AC contract manufacturer with sticky anchor clients, but it holds no monopoly-like position in any end-customer's mind and has no aftermarket/services annuity — the brand owns the customer relationship.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: The customer base is correctly corporate (brands buying reliability and TCO) and PGEL is growing wallet share within anchor accounts, but the required lock-in is missing given mass order cancellation and annual price renegotiation. | The long view had said: Customers are purely corporate brand owners and the offer is correctly matched to them — total-cost leadership plus reliability plus ODM platform lock-in and backward integration secured by anchor-customer commitments — rather than any misplaced retail-style intangible.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: Management claims deepening trust with brand partners, but the assurance was materially breached in Q4 when LPG shortages and truck unavailability cost ~₹420cr of customer deliveries. | The long view had said: Clients do buy reliability and quality alongside price — management argues stickiness rests on more than pricing and anchor customers have pre-committed to the new compressor and refrigerant facilities — but assurance was dented when the March LPG and trucking failures cost roughly Rs.420cr of customer deliveries.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: New platforms and categories are being added (washing machine 'new platforms', POS devices, refrigerators), but these come via partner tie-ups and licensed platforms rather than disclosed in-house R&D spend or efficiency. | The long view had said: R&D consistently converts into new ODM platforms that win clients — energy-rating-optimised split AC platforms in FY23, new washing machine, cooler and TV platforms later, and fully-automatic/front-load platforms in FY26 — with ODM already 80% of AC product sales, though R&D spend/intensity is never quantified and remains applied engineering rather than deep science.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 2 weakened, 8 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The model is genuinely asset-turn driven — fixed asset turnover held at 5–6x and ROCE at 25.2% (Q1) even in a bad year — and management repeatedly talks about optimising overheads and costs, but FY26 shows the fragility: ROCE fell to 18.6% by Q3 and PAT fell 33% for the year, and there is no itemised, hard-to-copy cost culture described. (The long view had said: Cost leadership is the stated core moat in every phase — Nov-2024 'cost leadership while striving for product leadership', Feb-2026 repeats it, and May-2026 details an itemised cost programme (lease rationalisation, molding consolidation at Salarpur, SAP, low-cost automation) alongside high turns (net fixed asset turnover >6x, ROCE 18.6%) on thin ~8% EBITDA margins.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Nothing in the FY26 calls describes a barrier a funded entrant could not rebuild — the advantages cited are land, plants and capex (INR700–750 crores in FY26, INR1,000 crores MoUs in Maharashtra and Andhra Pradesh), which is exactly the replicable kind, and the year's inability to hold price or margin against OEM customers demonstrates the absence of a durable moat. (The long view had said: Real barriers exist — five years of zero client attrition, multi-plant scale near ports, ODM platform libraries and now compressor/refrigerant backward integration anchored by a committed customer — but the underlying business is commoditised assembly where brands can and do in-source, and the FY26 margin collapse showed the advantage is cost-based rather than structurally un-replicable by a well-funded entrant.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Market Share Gainers (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Brand Strength (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: As a contract manufacturer PGEL's volumes are set by a small set of brand customers — June-August plans were cancelled 50-70% 'across most of the clients', receivables stretched because customers were themselves overstocked, and management frames growth as gaining 'wallet share within our anchor customers'. Category concentration is also acute, with RAC at 66-68% of revenue in the peak quarters. (The long view had said: Both ends are concentrated: the two largest new investments — the Sri City refrigerant plant and the Supa rotary compressor plant — are underwritten by a single unnamed 'anchor customer', and revenue depends on a handful of brands whose channel-filling decisions swung Q3FY26 and Q4FY26 sharply. On the supply side management stated critical components (compressors, motors, copper tubing, aluminium foil, controllers) must be imported from China with no domestic alternative, and Chinese New Year timing forces months of pre-buying.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: PGEL sells into brands that own the consumer relationship, so it has little pricing power: management said pricing pressure was expected and factored into the cut guidance, OEM customers were renegotiating prices, and roughly 250bps of Q4 gross margin from commodity and currency inflation could not be passed through, with full-year EBITDA falling to Rs 442 crore from Rs 519 crore. No specific cheaper challenger is named in the calls, which bounds the read. (The long view had said: PGEL sells an unbranded manufacturing service where the intermediary — the brand — controls the choice, the end consumer has zero awareness of PGEL, and the product is defended on cost rather than differentiation; analysts openly ask whether refusing to concede price will cost share to other OEMs, and FY26 showed the pricing power is thin (only 10-15% of a much larger cost increase passed through, 250bps gross margin hit). EBITDA margins run ~8% with electronics as low as 2-4%.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The BEE energy-rating transition made existing inventory hard to sell and forced production preponement into December 2025, showing that a specification change can strand the installed product format; offsetting this, the innovation cadence discussed is incremental and additive (new washing-machine platforms, POS devices with PAX Global, a compressor JV, refrigerators). (The long view had said: Across 14 calls the innovation discussed is incremental and value-adding — new ODM split-AC platforms optimised for revised energy ratings, fully-automatic top-load and front-load washer platforms, SAP migration, low-cost automation — with no evidence of a platform shift making PGEL's installed base legacy. BEE rating transitions shift timing of demand, not the technology base.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
