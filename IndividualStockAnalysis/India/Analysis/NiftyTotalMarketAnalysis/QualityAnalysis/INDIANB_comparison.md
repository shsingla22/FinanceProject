- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Weak → Good. Now: Genuine captive pockets exist — a Rs 1.27 trillion jewel-loan franchise growing 28% in its southern heartland, plus embedded government mandates (fintech solutions for 22 state departments, 1,000+ non-SNA accounts) that carry real switching costs. | The long view had said: Regional density in Tamil Nadu exists but confers no pricing protection — the bank must exit or reprice loans to keep margin and no bank can lead on rates.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Weak → Neutral. Now: Licensing gives high formal entry barriers, but pricing rationality is poor — yields on advances and NIMs compressed all year and deposit costs stay elevated because of competition for funds. | The long view had said: Licensing limits new entrants, but pricing behaviour is irrational on both sides — cut-throat loan pricing and bulk-deposit/CD rates spiking to 7.7-8.0%.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: Strong headline results are materially cycle- and treasury-aided rather than purely skill-driven, and management itself flags the tailwinds fading, so the record does not yet separate skill from favourable conditions. | The long view had said: Much of the record profit is cycle-driven — benign asset quality, written-off recoveries and treasury gains that management itself concedes are now fading — so results overstate durable skill.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: The benefit mix is reasonably matched — convenience and jewel/retail products for retail customers, and reliability plus embedded workflow lock-in (SNA/government accounts, e-BG, ULI lending) for corporate and MSME clients. | The long view had said: The retail/agri tilt (RAM 66%) is sensibly matched to relationship and convenience benefits and CRM deepening, but neither retail intangibles nor corporate switching-cost lock-in is evident.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Sustained multi-quarter tenacity on the hardest problems — SMA cut from 18.06% (Mar'25) to 4.69% and repeated CASA initiatives — but as a government-owned bank with a short current MD tenure, independence is structurally constrained. | The long view had said: Strategy (RAM focus, digital, margin discipline) survived the MD transition from S L Jain to Binod Kumar, but tenure is short and government-set, and priorities include PSLC/government-directed lending, limiting true independence.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Neutral. Now: Expansion is incremental and domestic only — agribusiness cells and micro-branches — with no credible new geography or overseas growth story in the calls. | The long view had said: Measured expansion — 300-branch three-year plan, new mid-corporate centres in Tamil Nadu, overseas book growing 27% with Sri Lanka/Singapore presence and USD1.5-2bn FCNR(B)/ECB plans — but it is network extension, not premium-brand travel.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 3 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 2 could not be compared.

*Foundation test: FAIL on the long view, FAIL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Cost to Replicate**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: There is a real licence-and-franchise barrier — a ~39% CASA base, Rs 7.9 trillion deposits and a branch network a new entrant cannot rebuild quickly — but the calls articulate no durable edge, and management itself flags that the float is structurally eroding and that CASA must be defended with new product launches and salary-account pushes. (The long view had said: Nothing described would stop a funded competitor: products are identically priced across banks, the deposit franchise is admitted to be a challenge (CASA below the 40% target for years), and the digital stack is built with 160+ third-party fintech partners any peer can hire.)

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Within the 12-month window the delivery-vs-promise record is good: GNPA guidance was raised mid-year (<3% to <2%) and beaten at 1.98%, FY26 recovery came in at Rs 6,651 crore against Rs 5,500-6,500 crore guidance, AUC recovery surpassed Rs 2,000 crore, and provisioning is conservative (Rs 310 crore West Asia buffer with 'no stress' seen, Rs 1,000 crore floating ECL provision). Management openly owned the one miss. Judged only 'partial' because the MD has been in post since FY2025 and PSU leadership tenure is short by design — no multi-year record is available here. (The long view had said: Across 17 calls the bank set explicit guidance and beat it — Aug'22 GNPA guidance of ~7% versus 8.13% then, delivered 1.86% by FY27Q1; FY25 deposit/advance/NIM/recovery guidance all met; FY26 recovery of Rs.6,651 crore against Rs.5,500-6,500 crore guided — while owning misses (cost-to-income) and refusing credit for flattering optics, plus conservative pre-emptive provisioning (Rs.310 crore West Asia, Rs.1,000 crore floating ECL) ahead of regulation.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No share numbers are cited in any of the four calls, and management explicitly guides growth (11-13% advances, 9-11% deposits) at or below peers cited by the analyst at 17-18%, prioritising NIM over share. Corporate book was deliberately shrunk in low-yielding advances and NBFC exposure cut by Rs 8,000 crore. (The long view had said: Consistent above-system growth in chosen niches without buying it — jewel loans up ~28% to Rs.1.27 trillion off 650 gold-loan branches, RAM up 15%, advances 13-14% — while GNPA fell 3.95%→1.86% and NBFC exposure was deliberately shrunk; but no explicit market-share numbers or share-versus-competitor framing is ever given.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

*Not comparable: Brand Strength (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view); Forward Integrators (NOT ASSESSED → NOT ASSESSED — could not be assessed on either view).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Unknown on the long view, Unknown on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Credit growth is explicitly tied to the macro cycle and treasury income swings quarter to quarter, and management pre-provisioned Rs 310 crore against a geopolitical shock and flagged tariff risk; but the book is operations-linked lending (RAM 66%, jewel loans, MSME working capital) rather than customer-capex equipment, and delivery has been steady with slippage falling each quarter. (The long view had said: Earnings across the 17 calls track the credit and rate cycle: GNPA fell from 8.13% (Q1FY23, with a Rs22,000cr COVID-restructured book, SREI/Future/sugar slippages) to 1.86% by Q1FY27, with credit cost down to 0.31% for FY26 — i.e. current profits sit at a benign point management itself names, while treasury income swings sharply with bond yields (Rs605cr to Rs105cr in FY23; FY27 plan assumes only Rs600-700cr) and NIM compressed from 3.53% to 3.10-3.25% guidance. Loan demand is explicitly tied to the macro cycle and the coming ECL transition (Rs3,000-3,500cr) adds a further cyclical charge.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Loans and deposits are largely undifferentiated, and the calls show price being the swing factor — the bank walks away from low-yielding corporate advances to defend NIM, yield on advances fell 18 bps sequentially, and NIM guidance was cut to 3.10–3.25% on cost-of-deposit pressure; the offset is a shift into segments where pricing is less commoditised. (The long view had said: The products are undifferentiated and the intermediary/price sets the choice: from 'corporate is very competitive pricing' in Aug 2022 to home loans stuck at 7.15-7.20% where 'no bank is taking the lead' in Jul 2026, the bank has no premium to defend and instead defends margin by exiting business — shedding Rs6,000cr of thinly priced loans in a single quarter and growing corporate only 3% in FY25. Origination increasingly runs through DSAs and aggregators, weakening any direct brand hold on the borrower.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The calls affirmatively show a broadening, granular base — RAM at 66% of advances, ~6 lakh MSME loans of Rs 48,000 crore, 3 lakh salary accounts added in a quarter, NBFC exposure cut by ~Rs 8,000 crore with 99% in A or better — with only occasional single-account lumpiness (two oscillating PSU accounts, one Rs 400 crore recovery) rather than dependence on any named relationship. (The long view had said: The loan book is deliberately granular (RAM 66%, ~6 lakh MSME loans sanctioned in FY26, 78% of agri in gold loans), yet single names still visibly move disclosed metrics: SREI Rs1,800cr and Future Group in FY23, two corporate SMA accounts of Rs263cr/Rs102cr in FY25, SMA-2 rising Rs190cr on one DCCO account and a single Rs400cr recovery in Q1FY27. Funding shows a bulk-deposit block of Rs1.61 lakh crore.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Technological Innovation (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Unknown on the long view (none of the stress checks could be computed.) vs Unknown on the one-year view (none of the stress checks could be computed.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
