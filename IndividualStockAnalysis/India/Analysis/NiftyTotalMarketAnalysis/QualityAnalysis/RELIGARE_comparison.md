### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 4 strengthened, 0 weakened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: The clearest pattern in all three calls: Care's retail health share rose from 11.4% to 11.6% of industry and 19.5% to 19.7% within SAHI, with growth explicitly benchmarked above industry (23.3% vs 16.5%; then 9% above industry), and profitability held through the gain (PBT INR539 crores FY26, mid-teens ROE for a fourth year) rather than share being bought with underwriting laxity. (The long view had said: Care's retail health share rises in every call — 11.2% of industry / 19.2% of SAHI (Nov 2025), 11.4% / 19.5% (Feb 2026), 11.6% / 19.7% (May 2026) — consistently outgrowing the market (Care 23.3% vs industry 16.5% in Q3; 9% above industry in FY26), and it is share won with profitability, not bought: profitable every year since FY19, mid-teens ROE for four consecutive years, PBT up 38% on n basis. Broking is the counterweight, losing traded clients (-5% for FY26).)
- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Health insurance premiums are inherently renewal-based and collected in advance — the float shows up as an investment book that crossed INR10,000 crores and yields 7.2-7.3% — and the 1/n accounting discussion shows multi-year policies billed upfront and recognised over time. But management never quantifies renewal/persistency rates or attach rates, so the recurring-stream quality is asserted rather than evidenced. (The long view had said: Health insurance renews annually and premium is billed in advance — the 1/n accounting discussion is explicitly about premium deferral, and the unearned float funds a INR10,944 crore investment book yielding 7.2-7.3% (May 2026). But across all three calls management never discloses renewal/persistency rates, attach rates or lapse ratios, so the recurring-stream quality is unevidenced.)
- **Brand Strength**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Care Health has scale and the #2 standalone-health position with rising share and a rating upgrade to AA-, but the calls describe the group's own heritage as something being repaired (fraud tag removal, corrective action plan, 'periods of scrutiny') rather than an asset, and there is no A&P spend, premium-pricing or adjacency-stretch evidence. (The long view had said: The Religare name carries the opposite of heritage value — RBI corrective action from 2018, a bank fraud tag until 2025, and management framing the whole exercise as 'rebuilding a group that reflects trust'. No A&P spend, premium pricing or brand-led adjacency is discussed on any of the three calls.)
- **Corporate Culture**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The last year shows genuine governance rebuilding — board reconstitution, promoter capital of INR750 crores plus open-market buying to 30.3%, return to quarterly calls after a long gap, and prompt disclosure of one-time labour-code and RFL write-down charges — but this is a one-year-old regime following a five-year record of chairman churn, a fraud tag and an RBI corrective action plan, so the delivery-versus-promise track record is not yet established. (The long view had said: The five-year record is one of governance failure and churn — funds 'misappropriated and siphoned' by erstwhile promoters, RBI corrective action from January 2018 to July 2025, a bank fraud tag, a contested chairmanship (Saluja FY21–FY25) and MD turnover, plus no investor call for years ('our return to this kind of interaction after a long gap'). The new Burman-led team is candid about the past and is rebuilding, but there is no track record yet of promises made in old calls delivered in later ones.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 4 eased, 0 worsened, 4 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Three of the four businesses — broking, SME lending and housing finance — ride capital-market and credit cycles, and management explicitly frames the outlook in cycle terms (Feb 2026: broking benefiting from 'strong retail participation and sustained domestic SIP inflows'; group positioned for 'the strengthening credit cycle'). The bounded part is that the dominant earner, Care Health, is renewal-driven insurance premium rather than capex-linked, and RFL is being restarted from a debt-free base rather than at a cycle peak. (The long view had said: The broking arm's earnings swing with market activity — FY26 revenue fell 2%, PBT fell 31%, average daily cash turnover fell 20% and unique traded clients fell 5%, while the industry activity ratio slid from 40% in 2022 to ~21% — and group profits also swung on mark-to-market and one-time items; the insurance business, however, is renewal/flow-like and grew through the period.)
- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: Across all three calls the technology discussion is incremental and value-adding — in-house claims adjudication, auto-adjudication cutting cashless TAT (30 mins in Nov 2025 to 87% within an hour by May 2026), daily agent commission payout, an in-house tech team — with no defensive discussion of a competitor platform, no legacy-format problem, and no analyst question about AI or platform disruption in the excerpts. (The long view had said: Broking is the exposed leg — management concedes it is 'repairing the business' and rebuilding core trading technology, mobile and AI initiatives from behind, in an industry reshaped by digital-first platforms; Care by contrast shows a steady small-scale innovation cadence rather than platform risk.)
- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Management itself flags that the insurance amendment bill passed in the December quarter enabling 100% FDI — a structural opening for well-capitalised foreign entrants into a segment where Care holds only 9.9% of private-player share. The exposure is bounded because no discounting or margin investment is described as a competitive response, and combined ratio actually improved 110bps YTD while share gained. (The long view had said: The insurance amendment bill enabling 100% FDI, flagged by management in the Feb 2026 call, opens the segment to well-capitalised foreign entrants, while in broking the falling activity ratio and shrinking traded-client base sit against digital-first, low-price competitors that management is answering with platform and product investment rather than pricing power.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Retail health is a lightly differentiated product where the intermediary largely controls the choice — the agency channel alone is ~40% of GWP — and the GST exemption has made affordability the industry's headline selling argument, precisely the setting where a cheaper-but-acceptable rival can take share. Care's defence is service metrics (97% settlement, 87% of cashless claims inside an hour) rather than a protected niche, and the claim ratio did worsen 2.7% YTD. (The long view had said: Both main businesses sell near-commoditised propositions where the intermediary steers the choice: Care's combined ratio at ~101.4% shows premiums priced to essentially no underwriting profit, and daily agent commission payouts are effectively a spend to hold intermediary loyalty in a market where the agent controls the customer's pick; broking faces the same commoditisation, with management pushing non-broking distribution income because brokerage itself is thin.)

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
