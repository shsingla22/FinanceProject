# Go Digit General (GODIGIT) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Go Digit General Insurance is an Indian general (non-life) insurance company, regulated by IRDAI and listed on the BSE and NSE, that sells policies covering vehicles, health and travel, property and business risks. Its customers range from individual retail buyers of car, two-wheeler and health cover to large corporates buying fire, marine, engineering and liability cover and group health for their own employees, reached mainly through a large network of distribution partners (66,000+ partners as of mid-2024, growing since) and a direct corporate team present across India, all run on an in-house digital platform with automated and predictive underwriting. It earns money in two ways: underwriting profit, the premium it keeps after paying claims and expenses (management tracks this as the combined ratio, around 105-107%), and investment income on the float it holds, an investment book of about INR23,000 crores by March 2026 that is mostly fixed income with a roughly 9.5% equity allocation. Gross written premium was close to INR11,300 crores in FY2026, with a full-year profit after tax of about INR179 crores in Q4 and an annual ROE of roughly 17.7%; it operates only in India.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Motor - Own Damage (OD)** — Covers damage to the customer's own vehicle; it is one of the two halves of motor and made up 37% of Digit's motor premium in Q1 FY26 versus 41% for the industry, and management has deliberately cut back stand-alone OD and older private-car business where prices and commissions no longer pay.
- **Motor - Third Party (TP)** — The legally required cover for injury or damage caused to others, priced by the regulator with no rate increase in five years; it is the larger half of Digit's motor book at 63% of motor premium, and management says rising wage-linked claim costs make aggressive TP growth unattractive.
- **Private car** — The biggest slice inside motor at about 41% of the motor book, closely tied to new car sales, which management said were weak in Q1 FY26.
- **Two-wheeler** — About 31% of the motor book and a fast-growing line (Q4 FY26 premium jumped to INR556 crores from INR365 crores); the long-tenure '1+5' policies push up the reported expense ratio because commission is booked upfront while premium earns over one year, but management considers it profitable and a good source of long-lasting investable funds.
- **Commercial vehicle** — Cover for trucks and other commercial vehicles, about 28% of motor in FY26 but shrinking by choice - it was 65% of the whole book four or five years ago and had fallen to roughly 23-24% of motor by Q1 FY27.
- **Health, travel and personal accident (PA)** — Retail and group medical, travel and accident cover; it grew 52% against the industry's 17% in Q1 FY25 and rose from 22% to 27% of gross premium, driven by retail personal-lines business.
- **Group health (employer-employee)** — Health cover companies buy for their own staff; Digit has deliberately de-grown here (minus 2% in Q1 FY25) because management calls the segment volatile and under-priced and expects pricing discipline to return.
- **Fire / property** — Cover for buildings, plant and corporate property, a strong growth area - up 40% against the industry's 17% in Q1 FY26, with Digit taking lead positions on very large corporate accounts, though it passes much of the risk to reinsurers (retention cut to 9% from 15%) to avoid concentration.
- **Marine, engineering and liability ('others')** — Other commercial covers for goods in transit, projects and legal liability, grouped with fire as the corporate book; grew 23% in Q1 FY26 and is supported by increased reinsurance treaty capacity from 1 April.
- **Crop** — Mentioned only as a line carrying different (corporate-style) commission rates than retail business, so it affects the reported expense ratio through mix.
- **Investments (AUM)** — The pool of premium float invested until claims are paid, grown from about INR17,773 crores in mid-2024 to roughly INR23,000 crores by March 2026, primarily fixed income with about 9.5% in equity; it is a core profit driver, though management says it refuses to book capital gains just to smooth quarterly numbers.
- **Reinsurance / retention** — Not a product but a lever management discusses constantly - the share of premium ceded to reinsurers, with net retention swinging from 76% to 65% in Q1 FY26 to lay off large fire risks, which worsens the optical combined ratio without changing economics.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## The verdict: Decent — 56 out of 100 ★★★☆☆

### The story in depth

Go Digit scores best on the people running it and on the way cash moves through the business. The management checks are the strongest part of the record — top marks for candor, for walking away from unprofitable business four years running, and for a willingness to be contrarian — and the multibagger screen agrees, marking Corporate Culture as the single clearest STRONG FIT on the back of an unchanged Goyal/Kohli/Khetan team across all nine calls, the same scorecard shown every quarter even when it flatters them less, and bad news volunteered rather than extracted. That culture sits on top of an unusually good cash engine: customers pay premium before claims are paid, so working capital is negative, and for every ₹100 of capital in the business it produced ₹40 of operating cash this year. The Recurring Revenue pattern is a LIKELY FIT for the same reason — AUM grew about ₹3,200 crore from operations in FY26, float leverage rose from 4.65x to 5x as promised, and ₹3,387 crore of long-term advance premium sits mostly in the 1+5 year two-wheeler book. That two-wheeler book is also what the framework counts as growth spending: commission is paid and expensed up front while premium and float come in over five years, so today's reported cost is tomorrow's float.

The weak side of the record is the industry itself, and almost every disappointing pattern traces back to it. This is a fragmented market with 30-plus insurers where Digit holds roughly 3.6% share, so the structure check scores the worst mark available and the screen returns NO FIT on both Pricing Power and Toll Roads — motor third-party rates have not moved in five years, fire rates are down 40-45%, and group health is won and lost on aggression. The risk review reaches the same place from the other direction, calling Good Enough Goods ELEVATED because a policy is a commoditised promise placed largely by intermediaries earning commission. Crucially, the price-setter here is often the regulator, which is why Government Dependency is the one HIGH RISK: third-party tariffs are fixed by IRDAI while wage-linked claims inflate every year, EOM rules pushed commissions up, and the 1/N circular changed how premium is recognised. Brand Strength, Low Price Plus and Innovation Dominance all land at PARTIAL for related reasons — the service scores are real (4.7/5 on Google, 4.9/5 on Facebook from 2.5 lakh reviews, customers up from 5.34 crore to 7.1 crore) and roughly two-thirds of policies are issued through APIs with about 140 new ones added in a single quarter, but expenses to GWP still rose from 30.9% to 31.4%, no premium price is charged, and combined ratios remain above 100%.

The most important connection is that the same discipline that earns the top management marks is what caps the growth score. Because Digit cannot set prices, its only lever is to refuse business — and it has, exiting motor third-party in FY25, group health and crop reinsurance inward, fire at soft rates, and commercial vehicles down 27%, all while holding solvency at 2.42-2.43x and doing no M&A. That is why Market Share Gainers is only a LIKELY FIT rather than a strong one: share gain was real and repeated through FY25-FY26 (22% GWP growth against industry 13%, fire up 34% against 13%) but by Q1 FY27 motor share fell from 6.25% to 5.6% and GWP fell 8% by choice, which is also why growth over the period averages about 35% a year but arrives lumpily. The same double-edge shows up with reinsurers: eight years of relationships are the one genuine barrier the Cost to Replicate pattern can name, delivering better treaty capacity and commissions in a soft market, and they are simultaneously the Stakeholder Concentration risk, since reinsurers carry around 80% of fire risk and treaty terms decide what Digit can write at all. Cyclicality sits on WATCH for the pricing cycle, while technology disruption, new entrants, changing preferences and fashion all score LOW — Digit is the digital-native player and demand is annual, functional and largely compulsory. Netting it out, the combined rating is 56 out of 100, a Decent three stars, built from 63 on business quality, 41 on multibagger fit and 62 on risk safety, with overall resilience judged SOUND: a well-run, cash-generative company operating in a structurally unrewarding market.

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** The clearest pattern in the record: unchanged Goyal/Kohli/Khetan team across all nine calls, the same KPI framework repeated every quarter even when it flatters them less, bad news volunteered (retention drop, own-damage loss ratio deterioration and the corrective actions taken), old promises delivered (leverage guided to ~5x post-IPO and reached 5x by FY26), solvency held at 2.17-2.43 against a 1.5 requirement, and refusal to harvest capital gains to dress a quarter.

*"Sells once, earns forever"*

### Cost to Replicate — PARTIAL
*"Could a newcomer copy this?"*

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** A large share of the book is priced or framed by the regulator: motor third-party tariffs are set by IRDAI and have not risen in five years while wage-linked claims inflate every year; EOM rules pushed commissions up; the 1/N circular changed premium recognition (INR303 crores unbooked); Ind AS/IFRS adoption and forthcoming RBC solvency norms are all regulator-driven.
