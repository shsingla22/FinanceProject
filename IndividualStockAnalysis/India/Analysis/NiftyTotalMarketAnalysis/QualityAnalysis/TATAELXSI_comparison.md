### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 6 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Management claims AI/fixed-bid delivery can convert into 'better pricing power', yet no price increase is ever quantified, growth in Q3 FY26 was explicitly volume-led, and Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin fell to 21.2% on onsite/subcontractor cost — customers are demanding productivity give-backs rather than accepting price rises. (The long view had said: FY23 calls cite price hikes secured from customers alongside volume growth, and in Apr 2026 management claims AI productivity translates into better pricing power; but the trajectory shows erosion — media/telecom clients pushing cost-takeout and GenAI efficiency pass-throughs, and EBITDA margin falling from ~33% (FY23) to 21.2% in Q1 FY27.)

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: LIKELY FIT → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: The recent calls contain no heritage, A&P spend, premium-pricing or brand-adjacency discussion; marquee client names (Terumo, Sky) are cited as references, which is not evidence of the brand pattern either way. (The long view had said: Tata parentage plus employer-brand and marquee ODC wins (Suzuki, Terumo) clearly help win logos, and Jul 2026 shows deliberate brand spend at specialised industry events, but there is no heritage/A&P/premium-pricing story of the consumer-brand kind and new logos contribute only 2–2.5% of revenue per quarter.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 2 worsened, 1 unchanged; 3 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Revenue is tied to customers' discretionary engineering/product-development programmes, which swung visibly in the last four quarters: a strategic OEM client's work streams were disrupted in Q2, healthcare fell 13.1% quarter-on-quarter on delayed awards and 'runoff from the end of some large regulatory programs', and Q1 FY27 flagged 'softness in Germany' amid a 'challenged macro environment'. Exposure is bounded by three verticals, multi-year annuity-style engagements and deliberate diversification into off-road, farm equipment and aerospace adjacencies. (The long view had said: Revenue is tied to customers' discretionary R&D/engineering budgets, and the FY25–FY27 calls show the full swing: 30% year-on-year growth and ~33% EBITDA margins in Jul 2022 collapsing to ~0-1% sequential growth and 21-24% EBITDA as automotive OEMs and Tier-1s cut spend, media/telecom consolidated, and healthcare deals stalled; utilization fell below 70% and fresher hiring was suspended.)
- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Across all four calls, revenue is described as coming from private OEMs, broadcasters, telcos and medtech firms (Terumo, Sky, a Tier-1 US telco) — no PSU tenders, tariffs, subsidies or regulated pricing determine the top line. Government touches only the cost line: a one-time exceptional item from India's new labour code and US visa delays requiring third-party contractors. (The long view had said: No revenue is set by a regulator, but policy visibly moves the P&L at two removes: EV demand rests on subsidy/tariff regimes with governments 'talking about putting up barriers for the Chinese OEMs', US visa restrictions forced costly subcontractor deployment hitting Q1 FY27 margins, and profitability has leaned on SEZ tax status that has since stepped down from 100% to 50% benefit.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The core business sells engineering effort, and GenAI directly attacks the billable-hours model — analysts questioned whether AI-led productivity and fixed-bid delivery are changing pricing power, and management conceded customers are 'demanding better efficiencies, productivity', i.e. passing productivity gains back in price. Management is responding at scale (DevStudio.ai, Neuron, ViTel, AnaTel, agent inventories, 'AI-native engineering organization'), but that is a defensive re-platforming of the delivery model itself, not incremental innovation. (The long view had said: The core model is billable engineering headcount, and GenAI is repeatedly raised by analysts as a threat to volumes and pricing; by Jul 2026 management concedes fresher hiring is being cut partly because of AI and that media/telecom clients are explicitly asking for GenAI-driven efficiency against fixed budgets. The auto vertical is simultaneously mid-platform-shift (ICE→EV→SDV), where TE's installed skill base must be rebuilt.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Results are visibly driven by a handful of relationships: Q3 growth was partly 'normalization of work streams... with a strategic OEM client that was impacted in the previous quarter', an analyst explicitly probed how much of the 7.3% transportation growth was the anchor customer, and management confirmed new logos contribute only 2%–2.5% of revenue with 77–78% of automotive revenue now concentrated in OEM customers. Healthcare's two consecutive misses were attributed to delayed awards from 'some of our key customers'. (The long view had said: Management states new logos contribute only ~2-2.5% of revenue in any quarter, so results ride on a small set of existing accounts and a handful of named large deals; a single media deal 'bumped up our overall growth in this segment', a top media customer's corporate restructuring was flagged as an open risk, and a customer's Chapter 11 forced receivable provisions in Q1 FY27.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Fashion Risk (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 9 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
