# ZF Commercial (ZFCVINDIA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

ZF Commercial Vehicle Control Systems India (formerly WABCO India, part of the global ZF Group) makes braking and control systems for commercial vehicles — mainly trucks, buses and trailers above 6 tonnes. Its products include anti-lock braking (ABS), electronic braking (EBS) and electronic stability control (ESC), air compressors and e-compressors, brake actuators, slack adjusters, air suspension, automated manual transmission parts, door control systems for buses, and driver-assistance and telematics offerings. It earns money by selling these parts directly to Indian vehicle manufacturers (OEMs), by selling spares and consumables through the aftermarket, by exporting goods and engineering services to ZF's operations and customers abroad, and by charging subscriptions for its connected-vehicle digital services. It is based in Chennai (registered office at Ambattur, with a newer plant at Oragadam), listed on the BSE and NSE, and its sales rise and fall with Indian commercial vehicle production.

**The parts of the business:**

- **OE Sales** — Selling braking and control systems straight to truck, bus and trailer manufacturers in India — the largest single piece of the business (about INR 430 crores in the June 2024 quarter), and management tracks it against industry vehicle production, claiming to grow faster than the market (17.6% OE growth in FY2025-26 versus 16.6% industry vehicle growth).
- **Aftermarket** — Selling replacement parts, OE spares (OES), e-compressor kits, filters, door control systems to bus body builders and even diesel exhaust fluid (DEF) to fleets and state transport units — a smaller but steadily growing stream, around INR 126 crores in the June 2024 quarter.
- **Exports – Goods** — Shipping components such as air disc brake levers, brake chambers, air supply units, clutch compressor assemblies and trailer actuators to ZF group companies and overseas customers (European buses, US electric trucks) — roughly INR 278 crores in the June 2024 quarter, making it comparable in size to domestic OE sales.
- **Exports – Services** — Providing engineering, design and technical support work out of India to ZF's global business, billed as service income — about INR 107 crores in the June 2024 quarter and growing around 16% year on year.
- **Digital Business** — Connected-vehicle and telematics services (including SCALAR EVO Pulse and Trailer Pulse) sold on subscription to fleets and state bodies — a small but fast-growing line, up nearly 32% year on year in the June 2024 quarter.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

ZF Commercial comes out of the 34-check quality framework as a genuinely strong business, with full marks on ten separate checks. It sells certainty rather than parts: braking, stability control and ADAS are safety-critical systems where reliability and regulatory compliance matter more than price, and that shows up in OEM excellence awards and a service-led aftermarket. It holds a leadership position in commercial-vehicle braking with an entrenched aftermarket and retrofitment franchise built on its own installed base, defended by high barriers — homologated safety electronics, deep localisation and capital. Management scores well too: growth has been entirely organic with no acquisitions and no leverage stress, the greenfield Oragadam plant was built and ramped counter-cyclically through the FY25 slowdown ahead of the FY26 upturn it now serves, and communication across all sixteen conference calls has been low-profile and engineering-led, with even the MD succession reported plainly. The weaker corners of the scorecard are return on capital (0.67) and capital allocation (0.8), which sit well below competitive advantage (1.33) and customer benefits (1.25) — the moat is clearer than the cash returns it generates.

Those same strengths explain exactly how far the multibagger screen goes, and where it stops. Only one of eleven patterns is a strong fit — Market Share Gainers — and it is the one the company evidences with hard numbers: Q3 FY26 sales up 28.1% against 20.6% growth in above-6-tonne CV production, FY26 OE sales up 17.6% against 16.6% for the industry, and share gains in e-compressors and EBS among independent bus makers. Innovation Dominance is a likely fit, resting directly on the R&D check: named new products appear in every single call across four years, from Hill Start Aid and T-EBS through the e-compressor, AMT and Actuator 4.0 to the exhaust brake assembly and hydraulic ESC. After that, eight patterns land only partially, and the reasons rhyme. Pricing Power is partial because price increases are real but read as recovering cost inflation in negotiation with OEMs rather than pricing at will — which is also why margins have barely moved (plus or minus 2.5 points) and why the barriers-to-entry check reads that flatness as rational pricing rather than weakness. Toll Roads is partial for the same reason: the product is regulation-anchored in a near-duopoly, but the pricing is not toll-like. Brand Strength is partial because the pull comes from technology and OEM approval rather than the WABCO/ZF name itself, and Forward Integrators is partial because the movement toward the customer stops at aftermarket channel and service infrastructure — 51 new distributor branches and 14 new service centres in FY26. Low Price Plus is an outright no fit: at roughly 19–20% EBITDA margins, localisation is pursued for cost targets and margin, not to hand savings back for volume.

The risk review lands on the flipside of the very traits that earn the high marks. The structural end-market growth check credits safety mandates — ESC from September 2025, AIS-113 trailer rules, anticipated ADAS and AEBS — as permanent secular expansion, but the same dependence appears as Government Dependency (watch): a large share of growth is created by regulation and public procurement rather than by the customer alone. The technology advantage and R&D strengths carry Technological Innovation (watch), because the shift to electric commercial vehicles threatens legacy pneumatic content such as air compressors and ABS, and the parent's sale of ADAS compute, camera and radar to Harman required an explicit clarification that the commercial-vehicle ADAS business was unaffected. Most importantly, the Market Share Gainers pattern and the two biggest risks are the same fact seen from different sides: outperforming CV production still means tracking it almost one-for-one — Q1 FY25 saw 3.5% production decline and 3.8% sales decline — which is Cyclicality (elevated); and winning share through named OEM programmes means results get explained by individual relationships, with one important customer's roughly 12% demand reduction moving a quarter and growth hinging on single projects at Volvo, DAF and Daimler, which is Stakeholder Concentration (high risk). The mitigation for both is the same aftermarket build-out that shows up in the convenience-benefits check and the Forward Integrators pattern — aftermarket and OES revenue of INR 584 crores in FY26, up 15.6%, across 6,000-plus retailers and 4,000-plus distributors. Fashion risk shows no signal at all, and overall resilience reads as sound. Netted out, business quality scores 76, multibagger fit 48 and risk safety 62, giving 64 out of 100 — a Decent, three-star rating: a high-quality, well-run franchise whose share gains and product cadence are real, but whose growth is rented from regulation and the CV cycle and concentrated in a handful of customer relationships.
