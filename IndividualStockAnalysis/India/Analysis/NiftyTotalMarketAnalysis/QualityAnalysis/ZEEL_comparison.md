### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 5 strengthened, 0 weakened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: On digital there is real evidence of price action without volume loss — the seven-language tailored plans plus a 'revised pricing strategy' delivered 73% Y-o-Y digital revenue growth in Q3 and 53% for FY26 alongside subscriber growth — but the far larger linear business shows the opposite, with ad revenue falling every quarter Y-o-Y (-11%, -9%, -4%) and no ability to price through the FMCG slowdown. (The long view had said: Real pricing action exists on the digital side — ZEE5 raised Rs 499→699 (40%) in 2022 and a 'revised pricing strategy' plus 7-language packs drove 73% year-on-year digital growth in Q3FY26 — but the core business shows the opposite: ad revenue declining year after year, and linear subscription pricing capped by the TRAI tariff order rather than set by the company.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Heritage and consistency are explicitly claimed — the '33rd anniversary of satellite television', a relaunched 'brand universe', 800mn monthly unique reach and seven channels leading their language markets — and the name stretches into adjacencies (Bullet micro-drama, KidZ, Ideabaaz, Zee Studios, Zee Music). But brand strength is not converting into premium pricing: advertising revenue declined for the full year and share gains required higher content spend. (The long view had said: Zee has heritage and a stretchable name across TV, ZEE5, Zee Music, Zee Studios, KidZ and Bullet, and sustains reach (800mn monthly unique, 28bn weekly impressions), but brand strength is not converting into premium pricing or revenue share — it remains a perpetual 'strong number 2' with falling advertising revenue.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: NO FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: There is a steady, named pipeline each quarter — Bullet micro-drama, KidZ on ZEE5, Ideabaaz, the Phantom Digital VFX investment, 120+ shows including 34 originals in FY26 — but this is content and format spend rather than disclosed, growing R&D, and it is funded from a thin ~10% EBITDA margin, not high gross margins; no share-of-industry-R&D or comparable metric is given. (The long view had said: There is no disclosed or growing R&D; the Bengaluru technology and innovation centre opened in Q1FY23 was largely dismantled by FY25 with staff called redundant, and Q3FY26 explains falling D&A by low capitalisation — the reverse of high margins funding outsized innovation spend.)
- **Corporate Culture**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Within this window management did deliver a specific promise — the ZEE5 breakeven guided at the start of FY26 was achieved, and the Q2 call openly flagged that content investment would hurt near-term margins — plus the balance sheet is conservative (INR 21.8bn cash/treasury, content inventory reduced). Against that, the CEO was absent from the Q3 call at short notice and no mistake is owned publicly; leadership is long-tenured but promoter-controlled, and the excerpts give no multi-year delivery record to judge. (The long view had said: The record shows repeated promises not delivered — Goenka's 2022 pledge that 'this year is the year' for market share recovery took until FY26, the Sony merger collapsed, fundraise proceeds were raised with deployment plans still 'work in progress', mass layoffs followed, and disclosure is guarded (ILT20 cost withheld, arbitration undisclosed, CEO repeatedly absent or leaving calls early); a Q4FY26 INR 4,084mn amortisation change/impairment further flatters prior-year comparability.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The articulable barrier management points to is depth across Indian language markets plus accumulated libraries — an 18,000-song music catalogue with 175mn YouTube subscribers, seven language-leading channels, 28bn weekly impressions — which takes time to assemble. But the calls also show the position is contestable: it remains the 'strong number 2', needed Free Dish to reach new households, and could not hold ad pricing, so a well-funded streaming rival is not obviously locked out. (The long view had said: No articulable barrier survives the replication test: the content library and language reach are being outspent by a merged Jio-Star, the tech investment was written down as redundant, and the company itself concedes it is 'a very small player' in music versus competitors — nothing here a well-funded rival could not rebuild.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 6 eased, 0 worsened, 2 unchanged. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Advertising — the core revenue line — swung with macro consumption in every one of the four calls (down 11% year-on-year in Q2, 9% year-on-year in Q3, 4% year-on-year in Q4 with March 'severely impacted' by the Middle East conflict), so revenue and margins move with the ad cycle rather than a commodity or customer-capex cycle; subscription revenue kept growing (+6%, +7%, +4%) through the same period, bounding the swing. (The long view had said: Across all four years shown, revenue and margins swing with the advertiser spending cycle rather than with anything ZEE controls — FMCG inflation and weak volumes in FY23, elections/sports in FY25, and the West Asia conflict freezing March 2026 spends; EBITDA margin moved from 12.8% (Q1FY23) to 6.9% (Q4FY26) with 300-800bps quarter-to-quarter swings, and ad revenue fell 9% year-on-year in Q3FY26 and ~12% over 9M FY26.)
- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → LOW. Why now: Across four calls no revenue, price or tariff is described as set by a regulator or subsidy — revenue comes from private advertisers, DPO/telco contracts and retail subscriptions; government appears only as a demand influence via GST reform and as an optional distribution route (Free Dish). (The long view had said: Linear subscription pricing is literally set by regulator: the TRAI tariff-order embargo suppressed subscription revenue for multiple years and NTO 3.0 later drove its recovery, while the government-owned DD FreeDish platform swung advertising revenue on both exit (Zee Anmol withdrawal, FY23) and re-entry (Feb FY26); GST cuts are cited as an ad-demand lever.)
- **New Entrants**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Structurally different formats — streaming, short-form/micro-drama apps and free-to-air Free Dish audiences — are reshaping viewing, and management's response has been to spend into them (content costs rose enough in Q2 to dent profitability) rather than defend the old format; no specific new-format rival is named defensively in the calls. (The long view had said: The Sony merger collapse left ZEE standalone against a consolidated Reliance-backed Star/Jio entity with telco bundling and free streaming economics; analysts pressed on resulting cost and pricing pressure, management answered defensively, and the FCCB fundraise was justified as war chest for 'the competitive dynamics of the sector which is undergoing change right now'.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Results are repeatedly explained by a narrow set of relationships: FMCG advertisers drive the ad line up or down in all four calls, DPO contract renewals drive broadcast subscription, and one 'revised pricing agreement with a telco player' materially helped Q3 digital revenue; no single named customer is disclosed as dominant, and management is actively widening the base. (The long view had said: Management concedes the advertising base is one category deep ('we are an FMCG-driven company'), distribution runs through a handful of DTH/cable operators whose non-payment forced large receivable write-downs (Dish Rs 5.8bn, Siti litigation), and the standout Q3FY26 digital jump was partly one deal — a 'revised pricing agreement with a telco player' — alongside an unresolved Star/Jio arbitration.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Viewing is migrating to digital and shorter formats — digital revenue grew 53% in FY26 while linear advertising declined year-on-year every quarter — and the whole portfolio is being repositioned toward younger, language-first, on-demand consumption; management insists the underlying TV base is not eroding. (The long view had said: The demand base is migrating off the company's core formats: management acknowledges theatrical audiences now simply wait for the streaming window rather than pay ticket prices, and the entire portfolio is being pivoted to digital, 7-language packs, short-form micro-drama and kids to follow viewers to new consumption habits.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Consumers have abundant free or near-free substitutes (YouTube, free-to-air), and the company's own moves point to weak pricing power at the low end — it went onto Free Dish to reach a new audience segment and broke subscriptions into cheaper tailored language plans rather than defending a single premium price point. (The long view had said: Cheap and free substitutes bound pricing at both ends — the free-to-air DD FreeDish decision was an explicit industry-wide trade of ad revenue to protect pay subscription, and ZEE5's growth in FY26 came from 'revised pricing' via cheaper single-language packs and a telco bundling deal rather than the Rs 499→699 price escalation attempted in FY23.)

*Unchanged: Technological Innovation (HIGH RISK); Fashion Risk (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
