### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view; 1 could not be compared. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: The phenobarbital franchise is the textbook case: SPARC's improved formulation competes against cheap, decades-old DESI products that prescribers keep using, and the company's remedy is to lobby the FDA to remove them rather than to win on the merits at the point of prescription. Elsewhere it must defend novel oral/topical mechanisms against established JAK inhibitors and generics. (The long view had said: The clearest live example is Sezaby: SPARC's clinically approved, benzyl-alcohol-free phenobarbital cannot capture the market while cheaper unapproved DESI formulations remain on sale, so the company is reduced to petitioning the FDA to remove them rather than winning on product merit. The same dynamic appears in CML, where genericisation of TKIs shrinks the perceived market for a differentiated new entrant.)

**That worsened:**

- **Government Dependency**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Nearly every near-term value event named in the Jan 2026 call is decided by a regulator or a court: the PRV monetisation (>$100m) turns on a DC district court ruling and appeal window under a scheme the government has already sunset, the phenobarbital exclusivity turns on an FDA Citizen Petition the agency keeps deferring, and PDP-716 received a complete response letter because of a vendor's OAI status. Fortunes are set by policy and agency discretion, not by customers. (The long view had said: Near-term cash and the value of the Sezaby franchise hinge on discretionary government action — a tradeable Pediatric Rare Disease Voucher whose statutory scheme was sunset and which the FDA denied until a district court overturned it, an unresolved Citizen Petition to force removal of competing DESI products, and a CRL on PDP-716 driven by an FDA OAI classification of a third-party vendor. Real and repeated, but bounded: the underlying value driver is science and partnering, not regulated tariffs or subsidy-funded end demand.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Cyclicality (QUANT FLAG).*

*Unchanged: Technological Innovation (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (HIGH RISK); Fashion Risk (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 15 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
