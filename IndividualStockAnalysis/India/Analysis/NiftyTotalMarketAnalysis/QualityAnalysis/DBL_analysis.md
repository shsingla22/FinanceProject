# Dilip Buildcon (DBL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Dilip Buildcon Limited (DBL) is an Indian construction and infrastructure contractor, headquartered in Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh, that builds public infrastructure — highways and roads, irrigation systems, water supply schemes, urban development works, metro rail, special bridges and tunnels — almost entirely for government customers such as the national highways ministry/NHAI and state governments (management says roughly 90% of revenue over 20 years has come from the central government and states other than its home state of MP). It makes money mainly by bidding competitively for these projects and getting paid to build them (EPC contracts, where the government pays for construction, and HAM contracts, where DBL also puts in some equity and is repaid over time), booking revenue as work is executed; order inflows were about INR18,548 crores in FY26 against a bid pipeline of over INR80,000 crores. Alongside construction, it runs a contract coal-mining business — operating mines for owners as a Mine Developer and Operator (MDO) — which produced about 28.7 million tonnes of coal in FY26 and is targeted at ~57 million tonnes by FY29. It also owns completed road assets which it transfers into infrastructure investment trusts (InvITs) to recycle cash, and management expects roughly three-quarters of profits to come from these long-life mining and asset businesses by FY29, versus one-quarter from construction.

**The parts of the business:**

- **EPC (engineering, procurement and construction)** — The core contracting business that physically builds roads, irrigation and water-supply schemes, urban infrastructure, metro, bridges and tunnels for government clients — historically the bulk of revenue (around INR10,000 crores a year in FY23 guidance), now described as a 'capital-efficient execution and incubation engine' that gives only 2-3 years of visibility and is expected to shrink to about a quarter of profits by FY29.
- **Roads / highways (within EPC)** — The original mainstay — highway construction for NHAI and state road agencies — deliberately reduced from 65% of order intake in FY20 to about 37% in FY24 as part of a diversification and risk-reduction strategy.
- **Irrigation, water supply and urban development (within EPC)** — The newer non-road civil verticals that management pushed into for diversification; in FY24 they made up the majority of new orders (42% irrigation, 35% water supply, 8% urban development), supported by programmes like the Jal Jeevan Mission.
- **Coal MDO / mining vertical** — Long-tenure contracts to develop and operate coal mines for their owners — mainly the Siarmal mine (about 22.35 million tonnes in FY26) and the Pachhwara mine (about 6.37 million tonnes) — totalling roughly 28.7 million tonnes in FY26, described as high-margin, low-investment-risk, with most capex already spent, and increasingly the company's central EBITDA and cash-flow engine with 25-55 years of revenue visibility.
- **Assets vertical — HAM road projects and InvITs** — Completed hybrid-annuity road projects that DBL part-owns and then sells or transfers into infrastructure investment trusts to release cash — 7 HAM assets already transferred, the rest to move in tranches through March 2027, with about INR1,600 crores of InvIT units held on the balance sheet (INR1,400 crores in the listed Anantam Highways InvIT and INR200 crores in Shrem InvIT), providing regular distributions and helping cut debt.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Dilip Buildcon builds roads, water, metro and mining projects, almost entirely for government customers, and the three reviews all land in the same place. The quality framework scores six of its seven areas negative, with only Growth mildly positive (+0.5) and Management neutral (0.0); the weakest is Industry Structure (-1.17). The one check that scores well is persistence of growth — sales have grown about 11% a year on average, lumpy year to year but inside the 10-15% band the framework prizes. The trouble is what that growth costs: every INR1 of assets produces only INR0.48 of sales against a INR3,600 crore gross block and INR7,082 crores of consolidated debt, about 115 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and after direct input costs only INR26 of every INR100 of sales is left. The combined rating is 29 out of 100 — Weak, one star — from 35 on business quality, 10 on multibagger fit and 40 on risk safety. Resilience, though, is judged SOUND: this is not a company in danger of falling over, it is a company that lacks the traits that make businesses compound.

The multibagger screen returns no clean matches at all — six patterns are PARTIAL and five are NO FIT — and almost every partial traces back to one and the same thing: the 'DBL 2.0' shift from one-off construction contracts into owned, long-life assets. Coal mine operating contracts of 25 to 55 years, INR1,400 crores of retained units in the listed Anantam Highways InvIT, and retained road concessions are what earn partial credit on Recurring Revenue, Forward Integrators and Cost to Replicate simultaneously. The same pivot drives the Market Share Gainers partial, where coal production scaled from 7mt in FY24 to 28.7mt in FY26 with a stated path to 8-9% of India's coal output, and management projects three-fourths of profit coming from these long-term assets by FY26. The cost side — headcount halved from 38,000 to 19,000, annual capex cut from roughly INR500 crores to INR100 crores, a debt-free equipment fleet — earns a partial on Low Price Plus, but the screen reads it as restructuring under a shrinking order book rather than a genuine scale advantage, and the quality framework scores that very same capex cut as a negative because growth spending is now behind the company while returns on the existing asset base stay modest at 13.8% core return on equity. The five outright NO FITs — Pricing Power, Brand Strength, Toll Roads, Friendly Middleman and Innovation Dominance — all reduce to a single fact repeated in different words: the work is won by submitting the lowest bid to a government buyer who is also the specifier.

The risk review then flags that same fact as the biggest hazard, which is the clearest example of one trait doing double duty. Essentially 100% of revenue comes from government counterparties, rated HIGH RISK, and the diversification management points to is across government segments and states rather than away from government at all. Lowest-price tendering shows up three times over: as the Oligopoly check scoring -2, as the Pricing Power pattern scoring NO FIT, and as an ELEVATED 'Good Enough Goods' risk where any adequately qualified contractor can undercut. Cyclicality is ELEVATED for the same reason growth is lumpy — FY26 standalone revenue fell 22.2% to INR7,005 crores with earnings down 18.7%, purely because order inflow in the prior two years was the lowest in seven or eight years. Most pointedly, the concentration that earns the patterns their credit is the concentration the risk review puts on WATCH: two coal mines, Siarmal and Pachhwara, with Pottangi to come, are described as the central profit and cash engine. Four risks — technology, new entrants, shifting consumer preferences and fashion — register only as quantitative flags with no evidence across 17 calls, which fits the quality framework's -2 on technology advantage: there is little proprietary to disrupt, and the differentiator offered is an owned equipment fleet, a capital edge rather than a technical one. Management's record sits in between: INR861 crores of debt was cut in FY24 against an INR800-1,000 crores target, but a net-debt-free promise for FY25 slipped, no dividend or buyback policy exists, and capital moved the other way through a INR533 crore warrant issue to Alpha.

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 1 check supporting).

**What the calls show:** There is genuine, itemised cost discipline — headcount halved from 38,000 to 19,000, annual capex cut from ~INR500 crores to ~INR100 crores, equipment fleet debt-free — but this reads as restructuring under a shrinking order book rather than a scale-driven low-price advantage; revenue fell 22% in FY26 while margins compressed.

### Forward Integrators — PARTIAL
*"Owns the shop, not just the product"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 2 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** DBL is moving downstream from pure contractor to owner-operator — retaining HAM assets, sponsoring the listed Anantam Highways InvIT (INR1,400 crores of units retained) and running coal mines as MDO rather than sub-contracting — but this is asset ownership funded off its own balance sheet, not franchising or D2C where third parties fund growth.

> "typically, EPC companies sell out whatever PPP project that they do... In our case, this time, we decided to hold those assets" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Operating margin moved from about 23% (early years) to 17% (recent years) — margins are ERODING, the opposite of pricing power.
- ❌ Operating margin of 20% but swinging ±5.5 points a year — too volatile for a protected franchise.

### Market Share Gainers — PARTIAL
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 1 check supporting).

**What the calls show:** Clear share gain in coal MDO — production scaled from 7mt (FY24) to 28.7mt (FY26) with a stated path to ~8-9% of India's coal output — but the core EPC business went backwards, with revenue down 20-22% in FY26 and a two-year order book described as the lowest in 7-8 years.

> "we remain on track to achieve coal production of around 57 million tons by FY29, which would represent approximately 8% to 9% of India's total coal output" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Corporate Culture — PARTIAL
*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and no computable check to cross-check.

**What the calls show:** Stable promoter-family leadership (Suryavanshi/Jain since FY21) and one clearly delivered promise — INR861 crores of debt cut in FY24 against an INR800-1,000 crores target — but the multi-year record shows repeated slippage: net-debt-free was promised for FY25-26 in May 2024, then pushed to FY28; FY26 revenue guidance of INR8,500 crores landed at INR7,005 crores and the INR1,500 crores net-debt target became INR2,100 crores, with misses attributed largely to external ordering.
