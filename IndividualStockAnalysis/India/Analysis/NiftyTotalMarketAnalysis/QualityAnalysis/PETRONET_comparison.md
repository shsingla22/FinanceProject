### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 8 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Toll Roads**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Petronet is literally a tolling business: it charges a regas fee that is a small fraction of the delivered gas cost while being indispensable to importers (GAIL, IOCL, BPCL, GSPC, Torrent, Deepak Fertilisers all route through Dahej), backed by use-or-pay contracts and evacuation/storage that rivals cannot match; Dahej ran at 90-94% utilisation through FY26 while nearby competing terminals could not ramp. (The long view had said: The business is an explicit tolling model: regas charge is a small fraction of the delivered LNG cost while the terminal is indispensable to the customer's gas chain, contracted on use-or-pay with annual escalation, in a rational few-player Indian terminal oligopoly — evidenced consistently from Aug 2022 (use-or-pay protection when Gazprom failed GAIL) through Feb/May 2026 (annual escalation taken, INR 630 cr CY22 dues eventually paid).)

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Across all four calls there is no price increase discussed; the new Deepak Fertilisers contract was signed at the same tariff as existing long-term deals, management markets itself on having the lowest charges, and volumes are visibly elastic to LNG price (Dahej utilisation fell to ~53% in March 2026 on a price spike to $25, recovering only as prices fell to $15-17). (The long view had said: Contractual 5% annual regas tariff escalation is taken every January at Dahej and April at Kochi without volume loss — conditional/locked-in pricing power — but it is formulaic rather than discretionary, and management concedes 'competitive tariffs' and volume incentives when negotiating the new 5 MMTPA capacity, so there is no evidence of raising price above contract to capture value.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Cost to Replicate (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (LIKELY FIT); Low Price Plus (LIKELY FIT); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (NO FIT); Innovation Dominance (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls throughput swings with LNG spot prices and end-sector demand — Q1 volumes fell to 220 TBTU vs 262 TBTU year-on-year on weak power/fertiliser offtake, and March 2026 Dahej utilisation collapsed to 53% when the Gulf crisis spiked prices to $25 before recovering as prices fell to $15-17; meanwhile the board approved ₹6,355 crore for Gopalpur plus a ₹12,000 crore rupee term loan and petchem capex while volumes were soft. Bounded because the core regas tariff is an operations-linked flow service under long-term take-or-pay contracts, with Dahej holding 90-94% utilisation and FY26 PAT (₹3,843 cr) roughly flat versus FY25 (₹3,926 cr). (The long view had said: Across all four years management explains volumes almost entirely through the LNG commodity price cycle — demand collapsed at $40-50/mmbtu spot in FY23, drove a 22% throughput jump when prices fell in FY24, and Dahej utilisation fell to 53% in March 2026 when the Gulf crisis spiked prices to $25; but this is a tolling/flow business (regas fee per TBTU, 5% annual tariff escalation, use-or-pay), so PBT has stayed in a narrow band (Rs 4,335cr FY23 → 4,757 → 5,275 → 5,158cr FY26) even as volumes swung.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Volumes and results are repeatedly attributed to a short list of named counterparties on both sides — offtake from GAIL, IOCL, BPCL, GSPC and Torrent, and supply overwhelmingly from QatarGas, whose disruption alone cut March utilisation to 53% and swung the balance sheet's receivables and payables by roughly ₹2,000 crore. Asset concentration compounds it: Dahej processed 201 of 219 TBTU in Q4, with Kochi still at sub-30% utilisation, and analysts questioned management on the Qatar contract extension and train status in successive calls. (The long view had said: Both ends of the chain are concentrated: capacity is held by exactly three offtakers ('They are only 3; GAIL, IOCL and BPCL'), who are also promoters, and supply rests on the single RasGas/QatarEnergy 7.5 MMTPA contract whose 2028 renewal dominated calls from 2022 onward. The dependence became visible in Q4 FY26 when Qatar volumes stopped and Dahej utilisation fell to 53% in March; use-or-pay disputes with the same three customers ran as provisions/reversals for four straight years.)
- **Technological Innovation**: NO SIGNAL → WATCH. Why now: The only innovation-related pressure surfaced in the calls is fuel substitution — analysts asked directly about slowing gas demand 'because of the competition from alternative fuels', and management concedes demand hinges on LNG being affordable versus alternate fuels. No platform-level disruption of the regas asset itself is discussed, and the infrastructure is long-lived, so exposure is real but bounded on the 12-month evidence. (The long view had said: No discussion across the 17 calls of competing technology platforms, substitution of the regas format, or analyst questions on disruption; capex debate is confined to conventional assets (tanks, jetty, PDH-PP, FSRU vs land-based).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (QUANT FLAG); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); New Entrants (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
