# Gravita India (GRAVITA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Gravita India is a recycler: it buys scrap — mainly used lead-acid batteries, plus aluminium, plastic and copper scrap — melts and refines it, and sells the recovered metals and plastic granules to industrial buyers, including battery makers and global OEMs (its Mundra lead is now LME brand-listed under 'GRAVITA M' and its Chittoor, Mundra and Phagi plants are MCX-empanelled). It makes money on the spread between scrap cost and finished-metal price, quoted internally as EBITDA per tonne (Q1 FY27: about INR24,181/t lead, INR25,175/t aluminium, INR10,197/t plastic, INR55,151/t copper), and pushes margins up by selling more 'value-added products' (63% of revenue in Q1 FY27) and by hedging metal prices back-to-back. It runs plants in India (Jaipur/Phagi, Mundra, Chittoor, Kathua being merged into Jaipur) and overseas (Sri Lanka, Ghana, Togo, a Romanian rubber plant via its Netherlands arm), with 12-plus facilities globally and sales into 70-plus countries; overseas operations were 42% of revenue and 76% of profit in Q1 FY23. Scale: quarterly revenue grew from about INR630 crores (Q1 FY23) to INR1,475 crores (Q1 FY27), with installed capacity of 4.97 lakh tonnes a year targeted to exceed 8 lakh tonnes by FY29.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Lead recycling** — Recycles used lead-acid batteries into lead metal and lead alloys — the company's original and by far largest business (42,151 tonnes in Q2 FY25 versus about 3,500 tonnes aluminium and 3,000 tonnes plastic), with management targeting non-lead to rise above 30% of the business.
- **Aluminium recycling** — Turns aluminium scrap into alloys such as ADC12; a much smaller volume line than lead but one of the faster-growing ones, guided at 35%-40% growth, and earning per-tonne margins similar to or above lead.
- **Plastic recycling** — Recovers plastic (largely from battery casings) into granules; the smallest of the older segments by volume and the lowest per-tonne margin, but grouped with aluminium as a high-growth vertical.
- **Copper recycling** — Newest large vertical, entered by acquiring Rashtriya Metal Industries plus a 29,400 tonne-per-annum Gujarat plant costing about INR160 crores; already meaningful, contributing INR376 crores of revenue in Q1 FY27 at only 50% utilisation and the highest per-tonne margin of any segment.
- **Rubber recycling** — A newer diversification, with a roughly 17,000 tonne-per-annum waste-rubber plant in Romania (Gravita's first European facility, about INR40 crores, 80% owned via its Netherlands subsidiary) and a first Indian rubber plant at Mundra.
- **Lithium-ion battery recycling** — Still early-stage — a pilot project targeted to be operational around H1 FY26 — and one of the new verticals the company's INR1,680 crore capex plan through FY29 is meant to fund.
- **Paper and steel recycling** — Planned future verticals mentioned in capex and Vision plans; no operating volumes or revenue disclosed in the calls.
- **Overseas business** — Plants and sourcing operations outside India (Sri Lanka, Ghana, Togo, Romania, plus the Netherlands holding arm) that sit close to scrap supply and earn better margins — 42% of revenue but 76% of profit in Q1 FY23.
- **Value-added products** — The higher-margin finished items (alloys and specialty grades) rather than plain refined metal; management tracks this as a share of revenue, up from 41% in Q1 FY23 to 63% in Q1 FY27 against an earlier goal of 50%.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Gravita India is a metal recycler whose strengths sit almost entirely in growth and competitive position, and whose weaknesses sit almost entirely in what that growth returns in cash. The quality framework scores Growth at 1.17 and Competitive Advantage at 1.0 — its two best areas — on the back of a genuine physical network: 12 facilities, presence in 70-plus countries, owned procurement yards near where scrap originates, and repeated executed expansion into Ghana, Togo, Sri Lanka, Mundra, a first European plant in Romania and now yards in the US. The end market is described as being in permanent expansion, with a 6-8% global growth rate in the core lead-acid battery market, and management is spending hard into it: growth capex has escalated from roughly ₹70-80 crore a year in FY23 to a stated ₹1,680 crore through FY29 to take capacity from 4.97 to 8 lakh tonnes, funded largely from internal accruals against a stated target of more than 25% return on capital employed. Against that, Return on Capital scores -0.33 and Industry Structure 0.17. The company currently converts every ₹100 of capital in the business into about ₹5 of operating cash, keeps only ₹20 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs, and leaves about 139 days of cash tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. The growth is real; the cash return on it has not yet shown up.

The pattern screen explains why the score is what it is, because the same traits that earn the two clear fits also rule out four other patterns outright. Only two of eleven patterns are likely fits — Low Price Plus and Market Share Gainers — and both rest on the same foundation. The edge is input cost, not selling price: proprietary overseas scrap sourcing in low-cost and tax-free zones, own yards being extended to the US to cut copper buying cost, solar installation, and consolidating the Kathua unit into Jaipur. That is precisely why Pricing Power is a clear non-fit — realisations are tied to the LME and fully hedged, and management guides to fixed earnings-per-kilogram bands (₹18-19 per kg on lead by FY25, ₹15-17 on aluminium and ₹10-12 on plastic in FY27) while attributing every margin surprise to arbitrage, volatility or scrap shortages rather than to its own pricing. It is the same fact behind the -1 score on intangible benefits: customers buy on price. Market Share Gainers rests on regulation — waste-management and extended-producer-responsibility rules, GST reverse charge and a 2% TDS on metal scrap pushing volume out of the unorganized sector, evidenced by domestic scrap availability growing 140% year on year — which is the same evidence behind the top scores on gain in market share, share donators and barriers to entry. Five further patterns come in as partial, generally because the ingredient exists but is not quantified: real business-to-business quality standing through LME brand listing for 'GRAVITA M' and MCX panel status, but no premium pricing; steady product-mix upgrading from 41% value-added revenue in Q1FY23 to 63% in Q1FY27, but no disclosed research spend; stable leadership and disciplined funding with net debt around ₹150 crore and an ICRA upgrade to AA, but capital allocation still only scores 0.75.

The risk review is the most reassuring of the three (risk safety 80, resilience sound, no risk rated high), but its five watch items are largely the mirror image of the strengths rather than separate problems. Government dependency is flagged precisely because the share-gain pattern leans on the same policy package, and cites the same 140% jump in domestic scrap availability as evidence — the engine and the exposure are one thing. Stakeholder concentration is the flip side of the global sourcing network: 63% of battery scrap was imported and 15-20% of imports route through the Gulf, so a Middle East disruption hit supply, and those long supply lines are consistent with the 139-day cash cycle. Good-enough-goods risk is the cost-driven Low Price Plus fit seen from the other side, since near-commodity recycled metal competes mainly on price. Cyclicality is flagged because results move with metal prices rather than volume — 42% revenue growth on just 4% volume growth in Q1FY27 — while roughly 100% back-to-back hedging removes both the upside and the downside. Technological innovation is flagged because the core vertical depends on lead-acid battery chemistry, with management conceding the point by committing to cut lead reliance from about 85% to 70-75% and adding lithium-ion and copper. Three risks are rated low, including new entrants, where compliance and capital requirements work in the incumbent's favour. Put together — 62 on business quality, 26 on multibagger fit, 80 on risk safety — the combined rating is 56 out of 100, or Decent at three stars: a well-positioned, structurally growing, low-risk cost operator whose thesis depends on regulation continuing and on the heavy capex finally converting into cash returns.

### Low Price Plus — LIKELY FIT
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and the only computable check supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** The whole edge is input cost, not price: proprietary overseas scrap sourcing in low-cost/tax-free zones, own procurement yards being extended to the US to cut copper buying cost, solar installation, and consolidating the Kathua unit into Jaipur for cost efficiency — high asset turns on ~10% EBITDA margins with ROCE >25% targeted. But it is a commodity price-taker, not a low-price-plus-differentiation consumer proposition.

> "we are just setting up our own yard procurement network. So it will, in fact, reduce the overall procurement cost for us" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹20 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 1.2× a year with 21% returns on capital — thin margins made up on volume, the low-price-winner profile.

### Market Share Gainers — LIKELY FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits and the only computable check supports the pattern.

**What the calls show:** Structural share gain is credible — formalisation under BWMR/EPR and reverse-charge GST shifts volume from unorganised to organised players, domestic scrap availability grew 140% y/y in Q2FY25, and capacity scales from ~5 to over 8 lakh MTPA — with genuine local-partner international expansion (Romania SPV 80/20, Ghana, Togo). But no explicit share percentages versus named competitors are ever given, and the 25% volume CAGR promised since 2022 has repeatedly not landed (7% in Q1FY23, 8% in Q2FY25, 4% in Q1FY27).

> "Gravita being a leader in this recycling business and have pan-India presence" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Sales compounding at 21% while margins held or improved (6% → 9%) — growth that was WON, not bought with price cuts.

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Revenue and margins are levered to LME metal prices and arbitrage windows rather than volume — Q1FY27 showed 42% revenue growth on just 4% volume growth, and management repeatedly attributes EBITDA/tonne swings to price fluctuation and India-vs-international arbitrage (Q2FY25 lead EBITDA/t +22%, aluminium +57% 'by leveraging arbitrage opportunities'). > "The company was able to increase these margins by leveraging arbitrage opportunities between international and domestic markets." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/consumable demand base (replacement lead-acid batteries, scrap feedstock) plus ~100% back-to-back hedging that strips out commodity price upside and downside; steady guided EBITDA/kg bands across cycles*

## What to watch

- Government Dependency risk / Market Share Gainers pattern: any dilution, delay or reversal of the waste-management and producer-responsibility rules, GST reverse charge or the 2% TDS on scrap would stall the shift of volume to organized players that drove the 140% year-on-year jump in domestic scrap availability.
- Return on Capital check versus Capex for Growth check: the ₹1,680 crore of spending through FY29 and the move from 4.97 to 8 lakh tonnes must lift the current 5% cash return on capital toward the stated 25%-plus ROCE target; if new capacity lands and the cash return stays near 5%, the growth is not paying for itself.
- Cost of Working Capital check / Stakeholder Concentration risk: the 139-day cash cycle alongside 63% imported battery scrap and 15-20% of imports routing through the Gulf — any further disruption on those lanes lengthens the cycle and ties up more capital.
- Cyclicality risk: whether volume growth starts catching up with revenue growth (42% revenue on 4% volume in Q1FY27) and whether earnings per kilogram hold inside the guided bands of ₹18-19 on lead, ₹15-17 on aluminium and ₹10-12 on plastic.
- Technological Innovation risk / Good Enough Goods risk: progress on cutting lead reliance from about 85% to 70-75% and on holding or raising the 63% value-added revenue mix — if both stall, the Low Price Plus cost edge is the only defence left.
