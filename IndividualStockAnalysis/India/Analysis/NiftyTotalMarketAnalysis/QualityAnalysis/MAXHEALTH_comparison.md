### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Entry barriers are demonstrably high — the company's own projects need environmental clearance, Consent-to-Establish, forest and building-plan approvals with 24-42 month gestation — and there is no evidence of discounting wars, though tariff-setters rather than competitors cap pricing. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±9.0 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Entry barriers are severe and evidenced repeatedly — environmental clearances, forest approvals, building-plan and fire NOCs, GRAP disruptions and multi-year build cycles — while pricing is rational with modest annual revisions of 2–2.5% rather than discounting.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹27 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹27 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±9.0 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Expansion is overwhelmingly organic brownfield/greenfield funded largely from operating cash with net debt/EBITDA held below 1x, alongside pruning of non-core assets and only small bolt-on M&A. | The long view had said: Leverage is prudent and expansion is largely self-funded (net debt/EBITDA consistently below 1, FCF of INR 1,541 crore in FY26), and it is mostly organic brownfield, though management does undertake regular sizeable acquisitions.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Recent quarters were depressed by identifiable transitory factors (weak vector-borne season, cashless stoppage, GST and chemo-drug changes) with a visible upturn ahead from CGHS tariff revision worth over INR 200 crore fully kicking in by April 2026 plus ~20% new capacity ramping. | The long view had said: The only cyclicality discussed is seasonal (weak vector-borne disease season) and regulatory (CGHS/insurer tariff resets), not a positioning near an identifiable cycle bottom.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Doctor attrition is disclosed at only ~1-1.5% and clinical/non-clinical talent was hired ahead of capacity commissioning, though no systematic succession or leadership-pipeline programme is described. | The long view had said: Evidence is about buying and replacing clinical teams rather than a systematic internal pipeline — a whole oncology team departed to a peer and was replaced by hiring another peer's team — with high churn in the disclosed senior-clinician roster.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Weak. Now: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹0.49 of sales — a fairly asset-heavy business. And from the calls: The model is structurally asset-heavy — roughly INR 10,538 crore of revenue against continuous multi-thousand-crore bed, land and building outlays with 24-40 month gestation — so despite a real regional moat, asset turn is low by design. | The long view had said: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹0.49 of sales — a fairly asset-heavy business. And from the calls: Hospitals are inherently asset-heavy, but Max is at the asset-light end via 50-year built-to-suit leases and brownfield-led expansion that management says only works because its ROCEs exceed the 8–8.5% yields paid to developers.
- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Weak quarters are explained specifically and unflatteringly (insurance cashless impasse, below-cost CGHS chemo pricing, pre-commissioning costs, receivables build-up) and the one-time INR 149 crore tax gain was stripped out voluntarily — offset by repeatedly slipping project dates (Gurgaon end-FY26 → H1 FY27 → end of year; Patparganj FY28 → FY29). | The long view had said: Consistently candid across years — excluded one-off COVID vaccination revenue for like-to-like comparison in FY23, itemised every negative in Q3 FY26 (payor mix, GST, pre-commissioning costs, seasonality), and openly owned project slippage.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Excellent → Good. Now: Demand is anchored on clinical reliability rather than price — management attributes the steady base to clinical capability, and occupancy stayed near optimal through a quarter of payor disruption and doctor rotation. | The long view had said: Classic assurance purchase — the ARPOB/mix uplift comes from high-acuity oncology, robotics, orthopedics and renal work where patients buy clinical certainty, and acquired units are re-rated purely by upgrading clinical programmes, doctors and equipment.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Neutral → Weak. Now: Growth is almost entirely volume/bed-led — ARPOB was flat at INR 78,000 → INR 77,900 across the year (1-3% network growth) — and mix actually deteriorated as oncology's share of in-patient revenue fell from 26% to 21% and payor mix shifted to institutional. | The long view had said: Growth has shifted from mix/price-led (ARPOB +7% in FY25 on oncology, robotics and tariff revisions) to largely volume-led — ARPOB grew only 3% in Q3 FY26 and was flat sequentially in Q4, with revenue growth driven by occupied bed days and new capacity.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: The mix is not optimised for either archetype — the retail/international side carries the brand premium, but the institutional (CGHS/insurer) book is where margin damage and receivables build-up occurred this year. | The long view had said: Benefit type is being consciously matched — the low-ARPOB institutional share was deliberately cut from 37% toward 15% in favour of self-pay, insurance and international retail patients who pay for brand and outcomes.
- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: Far from a stable oligopoly with powerless customers — payors demonstrated real negotiating power this year by suspending cashless services and by mandating CGHS drug prices below purchase cost, forcing product discontinuation. | The long view had said: The provider market is concentrated among a few large chains, but the paying customer is often an institution with real negotiating power, as shown by the cashless-services stalemate with insurers and CGHS dictating below-cost drug pricing.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Calls are dense operational detail delivered by the same three executives each quarter with no self-promotion, but the CMD is a well-known figure and the stock is closely covered by HSBC, BofA and Motilal Oswal, so the evidence cuts both ways. | The long view had said: Calls are operationally dense and metric-driven with no self-promotion, and the CMD refuses to give forward-looking guidance numbers, though Abhay Soi is a reasonably visible promoter figure.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Headline results owe much to added beds, acquisitions and one-offs rather than pure operating skill — network ARPOB is flat, Q3/Q4 growth halved to 10% and PAT included a INR 149 crore merger tax benefit — so the record should be discounted for capacity effects even though like-for-like EBITDA/bed did rise 7%. | The long view had said: Results are separable from skill because management discloses the mechanics — like-to-like reporting excluding new hospitals and one-offs, and explicit quantification of regulatory windfalls (INR 200 crore CGHS gross, INR 140 crore net) rather than claiming operational credit.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 4 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 2 weakened, 7 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: The brand travels — international patients held at ~9% of hospital revenue growing 12-32% year-on-year despite airspace restrictions, existing units run at 78-79% occupancy, and management is extending the name into new geographies (Lucknow, Pune, Bhubaneswar) — but the FY26 calls carry no heritage story, no A&P discussion and no premium-price evidence, since ARPOB is flat. (The long view had said: The Max name is stretched into adjacencies (Max Lab, Max@Home) and onto acquired assets (Max Lucknow, Max Nagpur, Nanavati Max), and pulls 31% of revenue through owned digital channels with website traffic up 39–44% y/y; but no A&P spend, heritage story or explicit premium-vs-competitor evidence is given.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: The FY26 calls themselves itemise why a funded newcomer cannot catch up: prime metro land (Saket complex, Dwarka, a 10-acre parcel in central Bhubaneswar), environmental clearances, Consent-to-Establish, forest-department permissions, plus 24-42 month build cycles and multi-year clinician onboarding — Max's own projects took years despite an existing licence base and balance sheet. (The long view had said: Concrete replication barriers do surface — finite prime metro land (Saket, Dwarka, 10-acre Bhubaneswar), multi-year EC/forest/fire/DDA approval chains, 24–36 month build cycles, and developers signing 50-year built-to-suit leases only because of Max's balance sheet — but the moat is site- and clinician-based, and clinician mobility (the FY26 oncology team swap) shows well-funded rivals can and do contest it.)

**That weakened:**

- **Pricing Power**: LIKELY FIT → NO FIT. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls headline ARPOB was essentially flat (1% year-on-year in Q1 and Q2, 3% in Q3, Rs 77,900 in Q4 — below cost inflation, with annual increments cited as an EBITDA drag), and the only material tariff relief came from a regulator after a long wait (CGHS, ~Rs 200 crore), not from the company's own pricing; GST changes, payor-mix shift and dropped oncology drugs pushed price/mix down. (The long view had said: Annual tariff revisions of only 2–2.5% are taken without volume loss (occupancy rose 68%→74–77%), and ARPOB rose from Rs.66,000 to Rs.80,100 by FY25 — but that was mostly mix, and by FY26 ARPOB stalled at Rs.77,900 with management conceding 'medical inflation is in very low single digits' and CGHS/insurers imposing price cuts.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Growth is real and repeated — 22 consecutive quarters of year-on-year growth, OBDs up 26%/19%/7%/8% through FY26, ~20% brownfield capacity added, Jaypee integrated and Kalinga (Bhubaneswar) acquired for entry into Eastern India — but no explicit market-share numbers or comparisons versus named competitors appear in any of the four calls, so share gain is inferred from capacity rather than stated. (The long view had said: 22 consecutive quarters of y/y growth, bed capacity expanded ~20% in FY26 with entry into Mumbai, Lucknow, Nagpur, Mohali, Pune and Bhubaneswar, and acquired assets turned around fast — but management never cites a market-share number or names competitor share.)

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
