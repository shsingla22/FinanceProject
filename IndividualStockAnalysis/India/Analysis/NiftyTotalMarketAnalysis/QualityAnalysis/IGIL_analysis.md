# International Ge (IGIL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

IGI is an independent testing-and-grading lab for diamonds, jewellery and coloured stones: customers send in stones and finished jewellery, IGI's gemologists examine them and issue a certificate (a "report") stating the 4Cs — cut, colour, clarity, carat — plus, increasingly, origin and traceability. Its customers are the trade rather than the public: diamond manufacturers, wholesalers, jewellery brands and retail chains (management says it serves nine of India's ten largest jewellery chains and over 7,500 customers globally), who need a trusted third-party stamp so buyers and sellers can price and transact with confidence. It makes money on a per-report fee, so revenue is essentially report volume times average selling price — about 3.56 million reports at roughly INR 1,010 per report in the June 2026 quarter — a very high-margin model (EBITDA margin around 60%, PAT margin around 45%, and the company is debt-free). It is headquartered in India but operates globally, with 31 laboratories, 12 in-factory labs and 18 gemology schools across 10 countries; the Indian listed entity bought the IGI Belgium and Netherlands businesses in December 2024 for Rs 1,345 crore and added US-based American Gemological Laboratories in January 2026, and it claims over 50% share in India, ~33% of global diamond certification and ~65% of lab-grown diamond certification.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Lab-Grown Diamonds (LGD) - loose stones** — Grading and certifying man-made diamonds, described as the company's volume engine and the segment where it claims roughly 65% global share; it grew 24-35% year-on-year across the quarters discussed and management expects the growing supply and retail acceptance of lab-growns to keep widening the market for certification.
- **LGD Jewellery** — Certifying finished jewellery set with lab-grown diamonds — one of the fastest-growing lines, up 26% in Q3 CY25, 29% in the March 2026 quarter and 44% in the June 2026 quarter.
- **Natural Diamonds - loose stones** — The original business of grading mined diamonds, which management calls a "key strategic pillar" where it is pushing to win share through new customers and more wallet share; growth has cooled from 29% in Q3 CY25 and 18% over the 15-month period to 6% in the June 2026 quarter.
- **Natural Diamond Jewellery** — Certification of finished jewellery containing natural diamonds; the softest line recently (about 2% growth in the June 2026 quarter), which management attributes to volatile gold and silver prices.
- **Coloured Stones / Gemstones and other certification** — Certification of coloured gemstones, a newer and still small but rapidly expanding avenue that grew over 200% in the June 2026 quarter, driven mainly by the AGL acquisition; management frames it as a way to widen the addressable market beyond diamonds.
- **American Gemological Laboratories (AGL)** — A US coloured-stone certification business acquired in January 2026 that gives IGI direct access to the US market; it added about 3% to revenue growth and 2% to EBITDA growth in the June 2026 quarter, with plans to extend it into other geographies.
- **Overseas subsidiaries (Belgium / Netherlands holding structure)** — The international lab network bought into the Indian listed entity in December 2024 for Rs 1,345 crore; management says these subsidiaries grew in line with India in the June 2026 quarter and were used to open operations in Italy.
- **Delivery formats - IGI labs, in-factory labs and mobile labs** — Rather than a separate revenue line, this is how the certification service reaches customers — 31 own laboratories plus 12 labs placed inside customers' factories and mobile units, which management says tighten customer relationships and are a competitive advantage.
- **IGI School of Gemology** — 18 gemology schools that train people in the trade; management presents them not as a meaningful profit source but as a way to build the brand, open markets and bring in new customers.

### The story in depth

International Ge earns an overall grade of Strong, 72 out of 100, built from a business-quality score of 75, a multibagger-pattern fit of 69 and a risk-safety score of 72, with overall resilience judged sound. The core of the story is a certification business that charges a small fee for something the customer cannot sell without: a stone has no commercial identity or price until it is graded. That shows up in the strongest quality checks — no meaningful direct input costs, so nearly all of every ₹100 of sales is available for people, marketing and profit; ₹57 of every ₹100 of sales is operating profit; and the company generated ₹38 of operating cash for every ₹100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings, against a debt-free balance sheet reported to earn 48% return on capital group-wide and roughly 70% in India. The same facts drive the pattern screen: Cost to Replicate is a strong fit on 50 years of accumulated trust, scarce gemmological talent and 31 labs across 10 countries including labs placed inside customers' factories; Brand Strength is a strong fit on the 50th year of operations and the escalation from almost no advertising to the ICC Women's World Cup, IPL and Gujarat Titans sponsorships and the 'Heera Ki Asli Pehchan' consumer campaign; and Toll Roads is a likely fit because certification is a small but indispensable cost inside a near-oligopoly holding 33% of global diamond certification.

The single trait that most explains both the upside and the danger is leadership in lab-grown diamonds. A roughly 65% global share in lab-grown, more than 50% share in India and nine of the country's top ten jewellery chains as customers make Market Share Gainers a strong fit and support the Mini-monopolies check, where the certificate is what confers the product's identity. But lab-grown is also what management itself calls a new disruptor: reports from lab-grown moved from about 20% to well over 30% of the total within a few years and now drive most of the growth, which is precisely why Technological Innovation sits on watch. The same volume surge feeds the Good Enough Goods risk, described as the live structural question in the calls — as lab-grown volumes explode and the stones themselves get cheaper, analysts keep asking whether the price per report must eventually be given up. Management's answer so far has been to hold prices flat through the collapse in lab-grown stone prices and through tariffs, with average price per report up 2-5%; that is a defensive form of pricing power, and it is exactly why Pricing Power is rated only a likely fit rather than a strong one — across seven calls no price increase is ever described. The concentration cuts the same way: the top-ten-chain relationships that prove share gains are also why Stakeholder Concentration is on watch, with the Q4 FY26 lab-grown surge credited explicitly to added capacity at a handful of primary growers.

The weak spots are consistent rather than scattered. Capital Allocation and Growth are the lowest-scoring quality areas at 0.6, and Corporate Culture only partially fits despite guidance of about 15% revenue and 20% profit growth being set at the start of the year and met or beaten in every call since, and despite a debt-free balance sheet. Profit margins have swung by about seven percentage points a year, which cost points on both the profit-margin and the barriers-to-entry checks and suggests important costs sit outside management's control — the same swings the cyclicality risk describes, with natural-diamond jewellery falling 19% in Q4 FY26 and growing only 2% in Q1 FY27 as gold and silver prices spiked, and softness in Belgium and the US blamed on war and macro slowdown. Because demand ultimately rests on jewellery taste, Fashion Risk and Shifting Consumer Preferences are both on watch: buyers rotated from diamond jewellery toward gold in the last two quarters, and lab-grown reached about half of US bridal. Government Dependency is on watch too, though softened by roughly 75% of group revenue coming from India and by a 10-country footprint that lets capacity move if trade routes shift to Dubai, Hong Kong or Turkey. The one comfort is that New Entrants is rated low — no structurally different competitor is defended against anywhere in the seven calls, there is no discounting, and IGI is the one doing the acquiring, buying coloured-stone capability through AGL. Innovation Dominance and Forward Integrators stay at likely and partial for the same reason: real service-format innovation and physical moves toward the customer, but no disclosed research spend, no named product and no owned stores; Recurring Revenue and Low Price Plus do not apply at all.

## What to watch

- Price per report: the Good Enough Goods risk and the likely-fit Pricing Power pattern both hinge on prices staying flat-to-up (2-5%); any per-report price cut as lab-grown volumes rise would weaken both.
- Concentration: watch whether growth keeps depending on a few primary growers and the top-ten India chains, the trait behind both the Market Share Gainers pattern and the Stakeholder Concentration risk.
- Margin swings: the ±7-point annual movement that held back the profit-margin and barriers-to-entry checks — steadier margins would lift business quality, wider swings would confirm costs sit outside management's control.
- Mix shift: the Shifting Consumer Preferences and Cyclicality risks turn on whether buyers keep rotating from diamond jewellery to gold, after natural-diamond jewellery fell 19% in Q4 FY26 and grew just 2% in Q1 FY27.
- Spending discipline: Capital Allocation and Growth are the weakest quality areas at 0.6 and Corporate Culture only partially fits — watch whether heavy brand, AI and leadership spending converts into the guided ~15% revenue / 20% profit growth.
