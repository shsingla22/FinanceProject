### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 1. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Toll Roads**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The products are mission-critical grid equipment with standards/certification anchoring (NABL re-accreditation of the power transformer factory, CIGRE/IEEE participation, contribution to the National Energy Policy through India Energy Stack), but the tiny-share-of-customer-cost test is contradicted — a 6,000 MW HVDC link or 30 units of 765 kV transformers is the customer's headline capex, not a rounding item, and no 'customers don't switch once converted' language appears. (The long view had said: The product is HVDC links, converter and power transformers, GIS substations — a large capex line item for the customer, awarded through international competitive bidding and tariff-based competitive bidding (Kudus-Mumbai, Adani, Marinus Link), the opposite of an un-negotiated tiny-share-of-cost toll; no certification/standards-body anchoring of revenue is claimed.)

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Leadership is stable (N. Venu MD/CEO and Ajay Singh CFO through the whole 5-year record), the FY26 priority stated at the start of the year was reported as delivered at year-end, and disclosure is reasonably candid about softness — the Q1 renewables decline, the Q2 transmission and data-centre declines, the labour-code exceptional item, and a 'temporary industry slowdown' in Q4; what is missing is any explicit owning of a mistake or a lesson learned on these calls. (The long view had said: Same MD (N. Venu) and CFO (Ajay Singh) across all 16 calls; bad news was disclosed plainly in FY23 (Rs 2.1 cr PBT, semiconductor and forex hits itemised) rather than glossed; the FY24 promise of 'sustainable double-digit EBITDA by end of FY25' was delivered and exceeded (FY26 operational EBITDA 15.4%), the Jul-2022 Mumbai HVDC order was commissioned in FY26, and capital is being committed long-term (Rs 2,000 cr Karjan greenfield on top of prior plans, ~Rs 4,000 cr cumulative) on a debt-free balance sheet.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The last four quarters show record order intake (Rs 11,339 cr in Q1 FY26 including the 6,000 MW Bhadla-Fatehpur HVDC award), an all-time-high backlog of ~Rs 29,555 cr at March 2026, 73% year-on-year base-order growth ex-HVDC in Q3, and 59% export order growth in Q2 — but management never cites a market-share percentage or names competitors, so the gain is implied by absolute wins rather than measured. (The long view had said: Growing faster than the market is a claim made consistently from Jul 2022 through FY26 and backed by numbers — FY26 revenue +27.6%, backlog +53.6% to Rs 29,555 cr, exports at 25-27% of orders with wins in Australia, Canada, Portugal, Greece, Iraq and a 'global feeder factory' model — and crucially the share was not bought with profitability, since PBT margin doubled to 16.2% over the same period.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Cost to Replicate (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: Margins moved sharply the right way across the year (PBT roughly 4x year-on-year in Q2, +118% in Q3, Rs 443 crore in Q4) on what management calls high-margin order execution and product mix, and there is no discussion in any call of defending price against cheaper alternatives. The core scope — 800 kV HVDC, 765 kV transformers, converter transformers, thyristor valves — is complex and scale-protected rather than easily commoditised. (The long view had said: A meaningful part of the mix — transformers, switchgear, standard substations — is sold through tariff-based competitive bidding and public tenders where price, not brand, decides, and incoming domestic capacity puts that pricing under question; gross margin already moves with product mix (down to ~39.5% in Q3FY26). The premium is real but only partly protected.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Demand is explicitly tied to customers' capacity-expansion capex — transmission build-out, renewable projects, data centres, industrial capex — and order intake swings violently quarter to quarter (Q1 FY26 orders +365% on one HVDC award, then transmission orders -43% and data centre -50% in Q2, renewables -25% in Q1). Management itself acknowledged navigating a slowdown in the Mar-26 quarter, and elevated FY26 margins (PBT ~4x year-on-year in Q2, +118% in Q3) are being earned at what looks like a capex-cycle peak. (The long view had said: Revenue is tied almost entirely to customers' capex — HVDC links, transmission substations, renewable grid connections, rail/metro and data-centre build-outs — and the P&L has swung violently across the 16 calls: Q1FY23 PBT was Rs 2.1 crore on Rs 991 crore revenue with margins crushed by commodity, freight and semiconductor costs, while FY26 delivered 15.4% EBITDA and PBT up 166%; management itself attributes quarterly revenue declines to 'the cyclical pattern' and to order timing, and Q4FY26 acknowledges a 'temporary industry slowdown'.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
