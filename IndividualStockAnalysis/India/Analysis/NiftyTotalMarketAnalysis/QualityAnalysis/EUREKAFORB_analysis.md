# Eureka Forbes (EUREKAFORB) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Eureka Forbes is an India-focused consumer appliance company that sells home health-and-hygiene products — water purifiers under its well-known Aquaguard brand, vacuum cleaners (including robotic and cordless models), air purifiers and water softeners — mainly to Indian households, at prices ranging from about INR 6,499 at the entry level to INR 79,999 for its most premium purifier. It makes money in two ways: one-time sales of the machines themselves (the 'product business'), and a recurring 'service business' of annual maintenance contracts (AMCs), repairs and replacement filters and spares sold to its installed base of 8 million-plus customers through a nationwide service technician network. Products reach buyers through an omnichannel mix of direct sales agents, retail/modern trade, distributors and e-commerce, all within India. Full-year FY26 revenue was INR 2,710 crores (up 11.3%) with adjusted EBITDA of INR 332 crores at a 12.2% margin, up from a 6.3% margin in FY23 after a multi-year turnaround management calls 'Project Udaan'.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Water purifiers (Electric Water Purifiers / Aquaguard)** — Sells household drinking-water purifiers under the Aquaguard brand across economy to premium price points; this is the company's original and still largest category — management describes the business as having been 'a single category company' built on it — and says the category has grown at a 3-year CAGR of over 12% despite a soft patch in FY26.
- **Vacuum cleaners / convenient cleaning (including Robotics)** — Sells vacuum cleaners, with growth coming from robotic, premium upright and cordless models; it is the second-largest product line and robotics is repeatedly called out as delivering strong growth and expanding into organized retail.
- **Air purifiers** — Sells home air purifiers, pitched on worsening urban air quality; still small ('on a low base') but the fastest-growing line, up about 2.7x in FY26 and sold out at the end of Q3 FY26.
- **Water softeners** — A newer category of water-softening equipment for homes, reported as delivering steady healthy double-digit growth on the back of rising awareness and investment in distribution.
- **Service business (AMCs, filters and spares)** — The recurring-revenue arm — annual maintenance contracts, servicing visits by its technician network, and sales of replacement filters and spares — where AMC bookings grew in double digits in FY26; management calls the aftermarket filter opportunity large but slow to capture, and notes service carries a higher margin mix than products.

### The story in depth

Eureka Forbes comes out of the three reviews rated Strong — 69 out of 100, or four stars, built from 65 on business quality, 72 on multibagger fit and 72 on risk safety, with overall resilience judged sound. The single strongest thread running through all three is management. The quality framework scores Management highest of any area at 1.5, with full marks for candor, disciplined stewardship and long-term tenacity, and the multibagger screen independently calls Corporate Culture a strong fit for exactly the same reasons: promises made on the first call in 2023 were kept, margins rose from 6.3% in FY23 to 12.2% in FY26 across three straight years of expansion, INR193 crore of net debt became INR443 crore of net cash, and free cash flow stayed above 100% of profit. Candor is not a soft point here either — management owned the missed Q3FY26 forecast and volunteered unflattering facts such as annual-maintenance-contract price dilution, coming FY27 gross-margin headwinds, and that the filters push will take years to change customer behaviour. The same discipline shows up as counter-cyclical spending: advertising and promotion was stepped up 40% in Q2FY25, 23.3% in Q3FY26 and 13.2% for FY26, straight through a demand slowdown, and that spend converted into category creation, share gain and margin expansion at the same time.

The commercial engine is brand plus service reach, and the two reinforce each other. The 40-year-old Aquaguard brand carrying rising advertising spend is what the screen calls a strong fit on Brand Strength, and it is being stretched into adjacencies — air purifiers up 2.7x in FY26, water softeners, robotics. Underneath it sits the company's hardest-to-copy asset: a nationwide service network plus direct sales and full omnichannel reach, in a category where installation and after-sales service are unavoidable. That network scores full marks on both distribution advantage and convenience benefits — 80% of customer interactions digital, a 24-hour turnaround tightened to a two-hour slot promise with a four-hour model in pilot — and it is what makes Cost to Replicate and Forward Integrators register at all, even if only as partial fits since the products themselves are copyable and no owned-store or direct-to-consumer revenue figure is ever given. The same network and the 8 million-plus installed base produce the annuity: annual maintenance contracts billed in advance, growing double digits for four consecutive quarters into FY26, which the screen reads as a likely fit on recurring revenue. Demand behind all of it is genuinely expanding — water purifier penetration is only 5% nationally and 3% rural, air quality is worsening, health awareness is rising, and BIS rules are formalising the category in favour of organised players, which is also why government dependency scores as a low risk rather than a threat. Pricing power is real but narrow: realisation gains come from premiumisation and mix, such as the INR79,999 Arctic Blaze, not from list-price increases — management said flatly in Q2FY25 that it took no pricing, and the only broad 6–7% rise, in April 2026, was cost-driven.

The uncomfortable part is that several of these same strengths are the source of the biggest risks. The aftermarket that creates the annuity is also where the Good Enough Goods risk is rated elevated: independent market service technicians, not consumers, choose which filter goes into the purifier, consumer awareness of filter brands is low, and gray-market and parallel operators hold the volume — which is precisely why the Friendly Middleman pattern rates only partial, since the company is courting those same technicians it must displace. Likewise, the discretionary consumer purchase that makes the Toll Roads pattern a clear no-fit is the same characteristic that makes cyclicality elevated: subdued demand and an 8% revenue decline in Q4FY23, an unanticipated post-festive slowdown in Q3FY26, and FY27 input-cost and currency pressure ahead. The industry itself is the weakest scored area, with barriers to entry marked negative on both swinging margins of plus or minus 10.8 points and management's own admission that entry barriers are low and pricing behaviour is promotional; that connects directly to the New Entrants risk on watch — two new aggressive players in water purifiers, plus quick commerce and Urban Company circling the service model — and it is why Market Share Gainers rates only a likely fit, since gains are claimed repeatedly but never quantified. Technological innovation is also on watch after the vacuum category shifted format to robotics, now around two-thirds of that portfolio; the company has ridden the shift rather than led it, and Innovation Dominance stays a likely rather than strong fit because research spending is never disclosed. Finally, the numbers behind the story are the softest evidence in the file: return on capital scores negative at roughly 5 rupees of operating cash per 100 rupees of capital, growth persistence scores the worst mark in the framework because year-to-year growth is lumpy, and the growth area overall scores just 0.33. The verdict, in short, rests on management quality, brand and service reach in an expanding market — not yet on returns or steady compounding.
