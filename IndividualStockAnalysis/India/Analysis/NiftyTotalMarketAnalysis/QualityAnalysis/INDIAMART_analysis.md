# Indiamart Inter. (INDIAMART) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

IndiaMART InterMESH runs India's large online business-to-business marketplace, a website and app where buyers looking for products post enquiries and suppliers (mostly small and mid-sized Indian businesses) list their catalogues, so the platform's core job is matchmaking and discovery rather than trading goods itself. It makes money almost entirely by selling paid annual or monthly subscription packages to suppliers — tiered as Silver, Gold and Platinum — who pay to be listed and to receive buyer enquiries; buyers use the platform free, and management describes buyer-side income as indirect (via seller enquiries/RFQs and some advertising-style audience monetisation). Cash is collected upfront and recognised as revenue over the subscription period, which is why the calls track 'collections from customers' and a large 'deferred revenue' pool separately from reported revenue. The business is India-focused, and alongside the marketplace it owns BUSY Infotech, an accounting-software business that sells licences to small businesses; consolidated revenue was about Rs. 1,569 crores in FY26 (year to March 2026) with roughly 2.2 lakh paying suppliers.

**The parts of the business:**

- **IndiaMART standalone (the B2B marketplace / web services business)** — The core online marketplace matching buyers' enquiries with suppliers, monetised through Silver/Gold/Platinum supplier subscriptions — it is the overwhelming bulk of the group, contributing Rs. 402 crores of the Rs. 463 crores of consolidated collections in the June 2026 quarter, with about 26–27 million unique business enquiries a quarter.
- **Platinum and Gold subscribers** — The premium, higher-priced supplier tiers that management repeatedly calls the profit engine — roughly 50% of the paying customer base but more than 75% of revenue, with very low monthly churn (under 1% for Platinum, 1–1.5% for Gold).
- **Silver subscribers (monthly and annual)** — The entry-level, cheaper supplier packages that bring in volume but churn heavily (about 4% a month for Silver annual and 7% for Silver monthly), and are the main reason the total paying-supplier count has stalled and even declined in recent quarters.
- **BUSY Infotech (accounting software segment)** — A wholly-owned accounting-software subsidiary acquired in April 2022 that sells licences to small businesses — about 4.54 lakh cumulative licences sold by June 2026, with FY26 revenue of Rs. 119 crores and billing of Rs. 170 crores, so a small but fast-growing slice (well under a tenth) of group revenue.
- **Trust, verification and platform-quality services** — Features layered onto the marketplace to make matching safer — multi-layer seller KYC, TrustSEAL verification, supplier ratings and reviews, newer buyer-side verification and a Buyer Payment Protection Programme — described as central to retention rather than as a separate revenue line.
- **AI and automation on the platform** — AI embedded across discovery — standardised cataloguing, intelligent matchmaking, content moderation, and an agentic call-handling system said to handle over 1 lakh calls a day — presented as a cost and quality lever, not a standalone business.
- **IndiaMART Finance Limited (new subsidiary)** — A wholly-owned finance arm approved in the June 2026 quarter to facilitate short-term transaction credit for MSMEs, explicitly working with partner lenders rather than lending large amounts off IndiaMART's own balance sheet; still at the starting line with no revenue disclosed.
- **Minority investments (Finlite Technologies, IDfy/Baldor Technologies and similar)** — Stakes in adjacent digital businesses — 51% of Finlite Technologies alongside the BUSY deal, and a 10% stake in Baldor Technologies which runs IDfy, an identity/KYC and verification platform — small bets aligned with 'empowering businesses digitally' rather than material revenue contributors.
- **Treasury / cash portfolio** — A large investment book built from the subscription cash collected upfront — Rs. 3,553 crores of cash and treasury as of June 2026 — whose mark-to-market swings move 'other income' and reported net profit sharply from quarter to quarter, in both directions.
- **Buyer monetisation experiments** — Early-stage attempts to earn from the buyer side, including a paid buyer programme with value-added services being tested on the website and monetising buyer audience data through third-party advertising retargeting — management stresses these are experiments and it does not charge buyers per transaction.

### The story in depth

IndiaMART earns its Strong rating (67 out of 100, four stars) on the strength of how it takes money in and who runs it. Customers pay upfront for subscriptions, so the money collected ahead of service has grown from Rs. 961 crore in the June 2023 quarter to Rs. 2,014 crore in the June 2027 quarter, and collections run ahead of reported revenue — in effect the customers fund the business. That is why the Recurring Revenue pattern is a strong fit, and it is also the reason the Cyclicality risk, though marked elevated, comes with a real cushion: the product is a day-to-day trading expense for suppliers rather than a capital project, and the prepaid book gives visibility even when small-business demand softens. The founders have run the company since inception, there is no debt, there is a Rs. 3,553 crore cash pile, and management scores the maximum on both candor and stewardship — naming problems it has not solved, splitting out how much customer loss came from war versus price, and volunteering that recent margin gains came from spending less on winning customers rather than from getting better at the job. That candor is the same evidence behind the Corporate Culture pattern being a strong fit and behind the overall risk picture reading as sound.

The single most important thing to understand is that growth has changed shape, and one trait explains both a pattern and a risk. Revenue now comes almost entirely from charging existing customers more — average revenue per supplier up 8-9%, using pricing that varies by category and city and by moving suppliers up to Gold and Platinum — while the number of paying suppliers is flat to falling (down 1,200 in the March 2026 quarter and 1,850 in the June 2027 quarter). At the top of the customer base this looks excellent: Platinum suppliers pay roughly ten times what Silver suppliers pay, Gold and Platinum are about half the customers but over three-quarters of revenue, and they leave at under 1-1.5% a month with steady upgrades. That is the Pricing Power pattern (likely fit) and the part of Toll Roads that works. At the bottom, the same pricing push is the problem: Silver suppliers churn at about 7% a month, and the FY26 Silver price increase directly cost an estimated 1,000-1,500 new customers. So the Good Enough Goods risk and the Toll Roads pattern only rating partial are not separate observations — they are the low end of the exact strategy that makes the top end so profitable. The 29% cash return on capital and 34-35% margins are the reward for that choice; the shrinking customer count is its bill.

The weakest link sits on the buyer side, and three of the framework's negative or partial readings all point at it. Buyer numbers and business enquiries have been stuck at 26-27 million for years despite Rs. 7-8 crore a quarter of performance advertising — hence the negative mark on advertising and promotion, the only-partial rating on Brand Strength, and management's own framing that the core problem is a shortage of enquiries. Because that spend has been effectively one channel, it also feeds Stakeholder Concentration and Shifting Consumer Preferences on the risk side. The technology risk is the same weakness seen from another angle: the supplier-side database of 7.2 million suppliers and 86 million products is genuinely expensive to copy, but management concedes the demand side depends on Google, that Google is under pressure from ChatGPT, and that bot traffic has made traffic figures unreportable — so Cost to Replicate rates only partial for the same reason Technological Innovation rates elevated. Set against that, the market position is close to its own ceiling by management's admission at roughly half the serviceable supplier market, which pulls down both the market-share check and the growth and industry-structure scores, though the wider opportunity is real: 1.6 crore GST-registered businesses exist against 2.2 lakh paying suppliers. The honest picture is a high-quality, cash-generative, well-governed franchise whose profits are safe but whose growth engine is running on price rather than on new customers, with the buyer funnel as the thing that decides whether that can continue.

## What to watch

- Paying supplier count — after net losses of 1,200 and 1,850 in the last two reported quarters, a return to net additions would confirm the Pricing Power pattern; further declines would tilt it toward the Good Enough Goods risk.
- Silver-tier churn and gross additions — roughly 7% monthly churn at the entry tier is what keeps Toll Roads at partial; stabilising it, or a further price move that costs another 1,000-1,500 customers, moves the verdict either way.
- Buyer counts and unique business enquiries — flat at 26-27 million for years despite Rs. 7-8 crore a quarter of spend; a durable break upward would fix the negative advertising check and lift Brand Strength above partial.
- Dependence on Google for buyer discovery — management's own concession that Google is under pressure from ChatGPT and that traffic has become unreportable is the heart of the elevated Technological Innovation risk; progress on Meta, Instagram and other channels would ease both that and Stakeholder Concentration.
- Average revenue per supplier holding at 8-9% growth — this is now essentially the whole growth story given a flat-to-shrinking customer base; if realisation growth fades before customer counts recover, the Growth and Industry Structure scores have nothing left to lean on.
