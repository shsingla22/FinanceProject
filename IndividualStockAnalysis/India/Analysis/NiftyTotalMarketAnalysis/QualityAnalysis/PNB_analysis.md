# Punjab National Bank (PNB) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: FINANCIAL SERVICES*

Punjab National Bank is a large Indian government-linked commercial bank that takes deposits from the public and lends the money out. It gathers current and savings accounts (CASA) and term deposits from individuals and businesses — global deposits of about INR17.25 lakh crores as of June 2026 — and lends roughly INR12.73 lakh crores to home buyers, vehicle and personal borrowers, farmers, small businesses (MSME) and large corporates, keeping about 73-74% of its deposits deployed as loans. It earns mainly from the gap between the interest it charges on loans (yield on advances around 8.4%) and what it pays on deposits (around 5%), a spread expressed as net interest margin of roughly 2.5-3.2%; the rest comes from fees, treasury/trading gains on its government-bond portfolio, and recoveries on previously written-off bad loans. It operates through a branch network across India — 144 branches added in FY26 with 250 more planned, tilted toward the southern and western regions — plus a heavily used digital channel (PNB ONE app, internet/mobile banking, UPI) where over 95% of transactions now happen, and it also has some overseas ('global') business alongside its domestic book.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Deposits (CASA and term deposits)** — Current and savings accounts plus fixed deposits that fund the bank — global deposits reached INR17.25 lakh crores in June 2026, growing 8-9% a year, with the low-cost CASA share around 37% (down from 46% in 2022) and management describing CASA as the main challenge for the industry.
- **Retail credit** — Home loans, vehicle loans and personal loans to individuals; part of the 'RAM' growth engine, growing about 17-18% a year excluding IBPC, and repeatedly named one of the three priority growth segments.
- **MSME lending** — Loans to micro, small and medium enterprises, growing around 19-20% a year, now supported by 'Digi MSME Prime' end-to-end digital loans of up to INR10 crores.
- **Agriculture / priority-sector lending** — Farm and priority-sector credit growing about 16% a year; the third leg of the RAM book, which together with retail and MSME makes up over half of total credit (55.5% in mid-2024).
- **RAM (Retail, Agriculture, MSME) taken together** — Management's headline label for its targeted growth engine, tracked as a share of total credit — 55.46% as of June 2024 and rising — and the stated focus of branch expansion and digital lending.
- **Corporate / wholesale credit** — Large-ticket lending to companies, sanctioned in big pipelines — over INR4 lakh crores of corporate credit lines sanctioned in FY26 with INR1.18 lakh crores still undisbursed — and over 85% of externally rated exposures above INR25 crores are rated A or better.
- **Treasury / investments** — The bank's government-securities and investment book, which produces trading profit (INR573 crores in Q1 FY23) but also mark-to-market swings — a INR1,409 crore MTM loss in Q1 FY23 when bond yields rose — and is a swing factor in reported profit.
- **Fee and other income** — Non-interest income from banking services and charges — INR2,055 crores of fee income in Q1 FY23, up about 26% year on year — reported alongside treasury gains as 'other income'.
- **Recovery from written-off accounts** — A meaningful earnings stream from chasing money on loans already written off — INR15,501 crores of total recovery in FY26, about 2.4x the year's fresh slippages — significant enough that management quotes 'core operating profit' excluding it.
- **Digital banking** — Mobile and internet channels (PNB ONE 2.0 with 350+ features, PNB One BIZ for 3 lakh corporate customers, UPI, WhatsApp banking) that now carry over 95% of transactions and originate every third loan, with over INR20,873 crores digitally sanctioned in Q4 FY26 alone.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Punjab National Bank comes out of this work as a well-run bank with a weak engine, and the rating of 47 out of 100 (Mixed) is essentially that sentence turned into a number: 48 for business quality and 44 for risk safety. The strongest part of the record is the people running it. Management scores highest of any area (0.67), with candor marked a full 2 out of 2 — the previous MD, Goel, published a quantified scorecard and revised it publicly in both directions, and Chandra opened FY26 by naming his own misses on low-cost deposits and margin instead of burying them. That same behaviour is what the multibagger screen credits under Corporate Culture, where guidance was repeatedly beaten and then tightened (bad-loan guidance cut from 5% to 4%, credit cost from 1% to 0.5% in August 2024). Stewardship matches the talk: the bank refused expensive bulk deposits, voluntarily cut its own capital raise from Rs 7,500 crore to Rs 5,000 crore because it was already over-capitalised, and built Rs 2,435 crore of floating provisions ahead of the new expected-loss accounting rules. But note what culture is not doing here — it is producing honesty about weak economics rather than fixing them. Return on capital scores the worst possible -1.0, with return on assets of 0.89% for FY26 and a global margin of 2.47-2.50%, among the weaker spreads in the system.

The heart of the story is that PNB's biggest asset and its biggest problem are the same thing: an enormous, old, branch-based deposit franchise. The 132-year history, roughly 10,000 branches and 24 crore customers are genuinely expensive and slow for anyone to rebuild, which is why both Brand Strength and Cost to Replicate come out PARTIAL rather than absent. Yet the same records show that scale converting into nothing at the price line. Intangible benefits score -1 because the bank explicitly prices 'in line with the market' on both what it lends and what it pays, which is exactly why Pricing Power, Low Price Plus and Market Share Gainers are all marked NO FIT. The proof is the low-cost deposit share, which fell from 46.34% in mid-2022 to around 37% through FY26 — the one guidance line management kept missing. That single number simultaneously undercuts the brand pattern, drives the New Entrants risk (savers walking to higher-yielding alternatives), drives the Shifting Consumer Preferences risk, and feeds the Good Enough Goods risk, where the FY26-27 plan of shedding Rs 22,411 crore of loan participations and about Rs 35,000 crore of low-yielding corporate loans reads as conceding it cannot hold price. The legacy branch base is also the exposed flank in the Technological Innovation risk: the economics rest on physical infrastructure while payments and lending move to phones. Management's answer is adoption rather than defence — 95% of transactions digital, PNB ONE 2.0 with 350-plus features, WhatsApp banking up 77% to 1.09 crore users, over Rs 1 lakh crore of cumulative digital sanctions — which is why Innovation Dominance is also PARTIAL: real, measured output, but no formal research spend and no evidence it earns a better price.

Timing is the third thread, and it argues for patience rather than enthusiasm. The clean-up that produced today's healthy-looking numbers has largely already happened: bad loans down from 11.2% in June 2022 to 2.78%, credit cost from 2.46% to about 0.33%, and provision coverage at 97%. That is why cyclical market growth scores -1 — the bank sits near the top of its credit cycle, not the bottom, so there is little further relief to harvest while margins keep compressing. The Cyclicality risk is rated HIGH for the mirror-image reason: the same swings that helped can hurt, as in the June 2022 quarter when operating profit fell from Rs 6,400 crore to Rs 5,379 crore on a Rs 1,409 crore mark-to-market loss on government bonds. Sitting on top of that is Government Dependency, also HIGH, and the most uncomfortable finding in the pack — as a majority government-owned bank, nearly every profit driver is set elsewhere. Central bank rate cuts took the margin from 3.07% in June 2024 to 2.47% by the fourth quarter of FY26, FY26 margin guidance was cut from 2.8-2.9% to 2.6-2.7% after the December cut, and the new expected-loss rules carry a one-time hit of Rs 9,500-10,000 crore plus an ongoing 10-12 basis points a quarter. The genuine offsets are that the loan book is spread very thin rather than concentrated — the largest single default in the June 2024 quarter was Rs 89 crore and the fifth largest Rs 7 crore on a Rs 10 trillion book, which is why Stakeholder Concentration is rated LOW — and that the end market is still expanding, with retail, small-business and farm credit growing 16-20% and over 35% of customers under 30. Management is spending into that: 144 new branches in FY26, 250 more planned in the South and West, a new Bengaluru office, and an overseas non-resident deposit drive of USD 2.5 billion with USD 425 million already raised. Whether that spending buys back the cheap deposits it lost is the open question the records leave unresolved, which is why overall resilience is marked UNKNOWN.

## What to watch

- Low-cost deposit share: management calls ~37% 'stabilised' after the fall from 46.34% — a further slide would confirm the New Entrants and Shifting Consumer Preferences risks and finish off the Brand Strength pattern; a genuine recovery would be the single best argument against the -1.0 return-on-capital score.

- The expected-loss accounting transition: the Rs 9,500-10,000 crore one-time hit plus 10-12 basis points a quarter is the concrete test of whether the Rs 2,435 crore of floating provisions built under Disciplined Stewards was enough cushioning.
- Credit cost and bad loans off a very low base (0.33% and 2.78%, coverage 97%): any upturn validates both the -1 cyclical market growth check and the HIGH Cyclicality risk, since the records say the easy improvement is already banked.
- Whether the deliberate shrinkage — Rs 22,411 crore of loan participations and ~Rs 35,000 crore of low-yielding corporate loans — is replaced by better-priced lending from the 250 planned South/West branches and the digital build-out; if not, it confirms Good Enough Goods and Market Share Gainers as written, with deposit growth of 8.5% already lagging the system.
