**Checks that improved:**

- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹20 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹20 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±5.2 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.7 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing behaviour was rational under stress — tariffs were passed through in negotiated steps and absorbed by mix and efficiency rather than met with discounting to defend volume. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±5.2 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Vertical integration and R&D create real barriers in specialty grades, but the pending anti-dumping case against cheap Chinese and Korean imports shows the industry is not immune to new low-price entrants.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Explicit evidence of weak rivals losing ground — established distributors in the US and UK defected to Garware during the tariff year. | The long view had said: Weak competitors are actively donating share — established distributors and a full Middle East team defected from competitors, PVC shrink film producers are losing customers to PET, and peers in industrial films went negative while Garware grew.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: Customers stayed through a 50% tariff because supply continuity and product quality outranked price — the clearest possible demonstration that trust dominates the purchase decision. | The long view had said: Customers buy consistency — 99% UV rejection, in-house control of every component, and retention of market share through a 50% tariff shock — though management partly bought that loyalty by absorbing cost rather than charging for trust.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: A speciality films niche run without fanfare and largely export-driven, though a INR2,120 crore revenue base with LIC Mutual Fund on every call is no longer truly under the radar. | The long view had said: The specialty film niche is inherently unglamorous, but the stock is now well covered by institutional investors on the calls (LIC MF, ASK, Carnelian, InCred), so the under-the-radar advantage has largely eroded.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Excellent → Good. Now: TPU backward integration with 25% earmarked for next-generation films, plus PDLC and UV-printable launches, shows a real multi-product technical base, but no R&D intensity figures are given to establish depth. | The long view had said: The technology edge is deep and multi-product — full backward integration from PTA/MEG through resin, metallizing and lamination, now extending to in-house TPU with 25% earmarked for next-generation products, plus PDLC switchable and spectrally selective films competitors in India cannot match.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: With revenue roughly flat for the year, Q4's 29% EBITDA growth and 26.2% margin were explicitly attributed to improved realization and richer mix, and Q3 gross margins were defended by pushing high-end products. | The long view had said: Growth is explicitly mix- and price-led — CPD share rose to 71% with value-added products ~88% of exports, EBITDA margins expanded to 26.2% in Q4FY26, and even a 50% tariff was largely offset via product mix and price pass-through rather than volume.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. Now: The year produced a concrete new-product slate — TPU-based UV printable films, PDLC privacy films, graphic solutions — with 25% of the new TPU line earmarked for next-generation products, but no R&D spend or efficiency data is given. | The long view had said: A 50+ member R&D team has delivered a continuous stream of commercialised products across the period — matte and coloured PPF, Spectra Pro and DecoVista architectural films, floatable/PCR shrink films, and now PDLC switchable and TPU-based UV printable films — each traceable to revenue and mix gains.
- **Advertisement and promotion** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. Now: Marketing spend is rising deliberately (cited as a Q1 margin drag) and is showing brand traction — 18 lakh website visits, 8 crore Meta impressions, digital push in Middle East — though returns are still early-stage. | The long view had said: Sustained brand investment — BCG engaged for branding in FY23, marketing costs explicitly flagged as long-run investments in FY25, and by FY26 ~18 lakh annual website visits and 8 crore Meta impressions alongside 250+ application studios — has visibly converted into share gains (architectural growing ~100%, Middle East doubling).

Now: PPF grew 28% y-o-y and capacity was doubled to 600 LSF, and architectural film is described as expanding rapidly across all geographies — genuine secular categories, though sun control at ~50% of revenue remains weather- and auto-cycle sensitive. | The long view had said: End markets are in secular expansion — sun control films $6.7bn growing to $11.1bn by FY32, shrink films $4.7bn to $8bn with structural PVC-to-PET conversion, and Indian PPF penetration under 0.5% versus 10% in the US.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 6 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 5. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 3 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Friendly Middleman**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Sun control and paint protection films reach the end consumer only through installers, dealers and Garware Application Studios, and the company invests heavily in that channel (250+ studios in Q3 FY26, 11 new global studios, UAE subsidiary and partner onboarding); however the calls give no evidence of formal installer training programmes or professional switching costs. (The long view had said: Consistently across FY23–FY26 sales run through tinters, applicators, dealers and OEM showrooms who choose and install the film while the car/home owner pays, with explicit trade training programmes and a studio network built to lock in the channel (500+ applicators trained by 2023, 5,000+ tinters, 4 OEM tie-ups by FY26).)
- **Brand Strength**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Heritage and consistency are the stated core ('90 years of innovation, integrity and resilience'), with rising marketing spend, 18 lakh website visits, 8 crore Meta impressions and adjacency stretch into Garware Home Solutions and ceramic coatings — but FY26 shows share maintained rather than gained, and brand did not confer the ability to price through the tariff. (The long view had said: The 'Global' brand has 20+ years standing as a top-3 branded player in US/Europe, backed by 90 years of company heritage, 23 trademarks, sustained A&P (8 crore Meta impressions, 18 lakh website visits in FY26), and stretch into adjacencies — architectural, ceramic coatings, Graphic Solutions and the Garware Home Solutions D2C brand.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Named new launches each year with commercial intent — TPU-based UV printable films, PDLC specialty films, Graphic Solutions — plus a TPU line with 25% of capacity earmarked for next-generation products; but no R&D spend figure, R&D share of industry, or pipeline economics are disclosed in any of the four calls. (The long view had said: Named new products land every year with revenue impact — matte PPF and floatable pearl shrink (FY23), Spectra Pro/DecoVista and Colored PPF (FY25), TPU-based UV printable and PDLC switchable films (FY26) — supported by a 50+ member R&D team, full vertical integration and a TPU backward-integration line with 25% of capacity earmarked for new-generation products; rupee R&D spend is never disclosed, the one gap.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Forward Integrators (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Films and PPF are installed by intermediaries (dealers, distributors, applicators) who control the end customer's choice and can substitute cheaper product — the Nobel Biocare mechanism — and management's defence of realisation runs through deliberate premiumisation and heavy brand spend rather than a structural moat; roughly 25% of revenue is in commoditised industrial products, and margin was held in Q3 partly by pushing mix upmarket while passing tariff to distributors. (The long view had said: This is the structural Nobel-Biocare setup: an unbranded-to-the-end-user product chosen by an intermediary (tinters, applicators, dealers) facing cheap Chinese/Korean import substitutes, and the company absorbed most of the 50% tariff rather than pass it through — passing 'a very small fraction to end consumer' — which suggests the premium is defended by cost absorption, not pricing power.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Cyclicality (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
