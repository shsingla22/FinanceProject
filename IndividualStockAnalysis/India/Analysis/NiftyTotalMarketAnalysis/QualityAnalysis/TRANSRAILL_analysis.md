# Transrail Lighting (TRANSRAILL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Transrail Lighting is an Indian engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) contractor that has been building electricity transmission and distribution infrastructure for over 40 years — power lines, substations, underground cabling and distribution networks — mostly on a turnkey basis, where it designs, supplies and builds the whole project for a fixed contract. Unlike a pure contractor, it also owns the factories that make what it installs: lattice towers, conductors and poles, plus its own tower testing facility, which management calls being 'fully backward integrated'. Its customers are largely state and national power utilities and grid operators (Powergrid, RVNL) and private power and renewable developers (Adani, ReNew, L&T), with overseas work in Africa, SAARC, Latin America and the GCC often funded by multilateral development banks and backed by letters of credit for payment security. It books revenue as projects are executed against a large order book (about INR 16,361 crore including L1 at March 2026, over two years of work), and delivered FY26 revenue of INR 6,880 crore, up 30%, with an EBITDA margin of roughly 12%; work is split fairly evenly between India (~57%) and 58-plus countries abroad (~43%).

**The parts of the business:**

- **Power Transmission & Distribution (T&D)** — The core business — building high-voltage transmission lines (up to 765 kV and 800 kV HVDC), substations, underground cabling and distribution works in India and overseas; it is roughly 84-90% of revenue and about 90% of the order book, and management repeatedly calls it the primary focus.
- **In-house manufacturing (towers, conductors, poles, tower testing)** — Factories that make the lattice towers, conductors and monopoles used in its own projects (over 1,50,000 tonnes of towers and 4,000 km of conductors supplied in FY26); capacity is being roughly doubled under a phased capex programme (INR 325 crore Phase 1, plus INR 203 crore approved in May 2026), which management says helps it meet customer timelines.
- **Civil construction** — A selective, niche vertical doing bridges, cooling towers, tunnelling and elevated roads — for example the 199-metre natural draft cooling tower at Yadadri and an elevated road in Udaipur; small relative to T&D, ranking second or third in the revenue mix.
- **Poles and Lighting** — A small vertical with a wide and complex product range of poles, high masts and related structures — such as high mast and metal fencing supplied for India's first bullet train and an order from L&T; contributes a minor share of revenue.
- **Railways** — Railway electrification (426 track-km of overhead electrification won in 9M FY25, plus RVNL overhead electrification completed in FY26), traction substations and signalling and telecommunication work; the smallest of the four verticals in the revenue mix.
- **Solar EPC (new)** — A newly entered line described as part of the post-listing strategy, opened with a first international order for an 80 MW DC ground-mount solar PV project with associated substation; at an early stage and not yet a material part of the order book.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Transrail earns its "Decent" 55-out-of-100 rating (63 for business quality, 40 for multibagger fit, 58 for risk safety) on the strength of one clear engine: it is taking share in a market that is itself getting much bigger. The Structural end-market growth check points to more than 2,00,000 km of transmission lines and over INR 9,00,000 crore of investment envisaged over five years, and the company has converted that into 3x revenue in five years, +30% in FY26 to Rs.6,880 crore, and an order book that has moved from roughly INR 10,000 crore in FY24 to Rs.18,216 crore. That is why Market Share Gainers is a STRONG FIT. Sitting alongside it is the other STRONG FIT, Corporate Culture: FY26 was guided at 25%, raised to 27% and delivered at 30%, margin guidance of 11.5-12% came in at 11.92%, and the INR 325-327 crore Phase-1 growth capex was completed on schedule before a fresh INR 203 crore was approved. Two supporting facts make the growth cheaper than it looks — the Cost of working capital check finds cash comes back 147 days before suppliers need paying, so customers largely fund the work, and the Geographical expansion check counts 58 countries with new entries such as Eswatini, Nicaragua, Abu Dhabi, Tunisia, Djibouti and Botswana actually commissioned in the period.

The same traits that drive those patterns also cap them, which is why six of the eleven patterns are NO FIT and the Competitive Advantage and Industry Structure quality areas both score zero. Backward integration into towers, conductors and poles, plus design engineering that cuts steel weight, is the source of the PARTIAL Low Price Plus fit and it is also the stated defence against both the New Entrants and Good Enough Goods risks. But it is a cost advantage, not a price advantage: every order is won at competitive fixed-price tender against four to five bidders, so Pricing Power is NO FIT and EBITDA margin has drifted from 13.04% in 9M FY25 to 11.92% in FY26 with about 11% guided for FY27, as the company absorbs geopolitical cost escalation. The Pricing, Mix and Volume check says the same thing in a different way — growth is volume and execution led, not price or mix led. The 41-year record, POWERGRID A-grade plant certification, tower testing facility and multilateral prequalifications give PARTIAL fits on Brand Strength and Cost to Replicate, but they work as tender entry tickets rather than as a premium. And because every rupee of capital goes upstream into tower, conductor and equipment capacity, Forward Integrators is NO FIT; because revenue is 100% lumpy turnkey contract execution with no maintenance contracts or renewals mentioned across six calls, Recurring Revenue is NO FIT too.

The risk review reads as the mirror image of the growth story rather than a separate list, and resilience is judged SOUND overall. The single HIGH RISK, Government Dependency, is the flip side of the end-market strength: about 90% of the order book is transmission and distribution, the largest domestic customer is a PSU in Power Grid, and order visibility is explicitly tied to Union Budget allocations and Power Grid's capex. Cyclicality is ELEVATED for the same structural reason — revenue is entirely a function of customers' investment cycles — with the two-year-plus order book (book-to-bill around 2.5x) and margin-assertive bid selection as the buffers. Three risks sit at WATCH and each ties back to a pattern already discussed: Stakeholder Concentration reflects Power Grid running 20-plus concurrent jobs and a single Bangladesh contract that was about 14% of the order book with roughly INR 488 crore of receivables, now being run down to about 5%; Good Enough Goods reflects the standardised L1 tender product that also blocks Pricing Power; and New Entrants reflects well-funded Chinese and local contractors in Africa — precisely the geography that the international mix of 39-45% relies on to dilute government dependency. Technological Innovation is LOW, since the company adopts HTLS, monopoles and 800kV HVDC rather than being displaced by them, and Fashion Risk shows NO SIGNAL. The honest read: a well-run, share-gaining contractor with genuine cost and credential barriers, but no mechanism to lift prices and a demand base it does not control.
