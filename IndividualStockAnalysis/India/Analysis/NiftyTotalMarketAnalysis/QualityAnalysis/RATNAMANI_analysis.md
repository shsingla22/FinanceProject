# Ratnamani Metals (RATNAMANI) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Ratnamani Metals & Tubes makes steel pipes and tubes in India and sells them mainly to large project customers — oil and gas companies and refineries (IOCL, BPCL, HPCL, GSPL, city gas distribution networks), government water schemes such as Gujarat's Sauni project and canal-linking projects in Rajasthan, and general process/industrial plants. It works on an order-book basis: it wins tenders and project orders, manufactures pipe to specification, and books revenue as it executes, so management tracks 'orders on hand' (around Rs. 2,000-2,345 crores across the calls) as the main visibility measure. Revenue comes from two in-house divisions, Carbon Steel and Stainless Steel, plus two subsidiaries that make bearing rings and nuclear pipe spools; consolidated sales were about Rs. 5,100 crores in FY24, Rs. 5,186 crores in FY25 and Rs. 4,494 crores in FY26, with EBITDA margins guided at roughly 16-18% and the standalone business debt-free. It manufactures in India (Gujarat including the Kutch plant, and a newer Orissa plant) and sells both domestically and for export — around Rs. 455-700 crores of the order book is exports, going largely to the Middle East/GCC and Europe, with a stainless steel plant being set up in Saudi Arabia and almost no supply to the US because of tariffs and anti-dumping duties.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Carbon Steel division (line pipes and process pipes)** — Makes large welded steel pipe — ERW, spiral/HSAW, LSAW, plus coating and induction bends — for oil and gas transmission, city gas distribution and water pipeline projects; it is the bigger swing factor in results, with a Rs. 750 crore order intake in one quarter of FY26 (60% oil and gas, 40% water) and a drop in its volumes being the main reason FY26 sales fell.
- **Stainless Steel division (pipes and tubes)** — Makes stainless steel welded, seamless, cold-finish and hot-finish/hot-extruded pipes and tubes plus mother hollows and instrumentation tubes, with about 50,000 tons of capacity running at roughly 60-70% utilisation; order booking ran Rs. 120-150 crores a month in FY24, and management says its edge is high value-added extruded product rather than the crowded pierced-seamless market.
- **Ravi Technoforge Private Limited (RTL) — bearing rings** — A subsidiary making bearing rings, sold to both export and domestic customers; it grew from about Rs. 258 crores of revenue in FY24 to Rs. 377 crores in FY26 (up 33%), with EBITDA margins improving from about 10-11% to 12-13%.
- **Ratnamani Finow Spooling Solutions (RFSS) — pipe spooling** — A subsidiary that fabricates pipe spools (and hangers/support systems), currently entirely for nuclear power plants, with an order book around Rs. 500 crores; FY26 was its first full year, at Rs. 390 crores of revenue, and capacity is being expanded from about 1,500 tons to 3,000-4,000 tons.
- **Overseas entities (Ratnamani Trade EU, Saudi subsidiary)** — Selling and manufacturing arms abroad — a Switzerland-based trading entity now wholly owned after buying the remaining 40% stake, and a Saudi Arabian subsidiary that received its commercial registration and is setting up a stainless steel plant to serve the Saudi and GCC market.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Revenue is tied directly to customer capex — refinery/petrochemical expansion, oil & gas transmission lines, city gas distribution and water schemes — and management itself calls the business cyclic; the swing is visible in the numbers, from 'best ever performance third time in a row' in FY24 to Q4 FY26 standalone sales of INR893 crores against INR1,575 crores a year earlier, with consolidated FY26 revenue down to INR4,494 crores from INR5,186 crores and the dividend cut to conserve resources. Order booking, pricing per tonne and margins all move with steel prices and with the timing of Middle East / domestic project cycles.

> "Normally, we see it is a little cyclic business. So, sometimes there are huge projects and projects come together whereas sometimes you have a situation where, but a few projects are under planning." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Debt-free standalone balance sheet, EBITDA % held at 16-18% through the downturn via mix and cost control, and countercyclical investment (Orissa plant, Saudi plant, RFSS/RTL capacity) while demand is muted*
