### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged. Financial resilience: Unknown on the long view, Unknown on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: As a regulated bank, pricing and portfolio composition are directly set by policy: RBI's repo path drove the margin trajectory in every call, CRR changes contributed basis points to NIM, and new RBI gold loan guidelines forced an outright run-down of the Digi-biz sub-segment. This is regulatory, not subsidy- or government-contract dependency — there is no discussion of PSU tenders or government-order revenue. (The long view had said: Fortunes are directly set by the regulator across all 17 calls: repo-linked EBLR repricing at T+1 means RBI rate decisions move NIM mechanically (125bps of FY26 cuts flowed straight through), and RBI actions repeatedly forced business changes — CD-ratio moderation to 80%, higher risk weights on unsecured, the AIF circular, gold-loan book downsizing 'in line with recent regulatory guidelines', wage/pension settlements and the coming ECL transition.)
- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Across all four calls technology appears as a steady cadence of incremental, value-adding builds — FedOne corporate platform, 15+ FedMobile features in a quarter, CRM enhancements, a GenAI internal co-pilot in alpha, credit-underwriting automation — with an external partner commissioned to future-proof the IT architecture. No call shows a competitor platform being discussed defensively or the bank's own infrastructure described as legacy. (The long view had said: Management repeatedly frames distribution as shifting to digital — 'the first judgment is formed not in our offices, but in our pixels' — and the bank leans on fintech partnerships for account acquisition and co-lending, an area where it had to cut daily account opening from 13-14k to 4-5k to strip out waste and fraud. Change is real but the bank is adopting it incrementally, not defending a legacy format.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The only preference shift discussed is the channel migration to digital-first acquisition, and management addresses it head-on with a brand refresh, digital identity work and platform investment. There is no volume erosion attributed to changing customer habits; deposit and credit demand is functional and repeat. (The long view had said: The savings pool is structurally migrating out of bank deposits into capital markets, put to management as a long-term question in Jan 2024 and conceded as a real, persistent drag on CASA and cost of funds; a related channel shift moves customer acquisition from branches to screens.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
