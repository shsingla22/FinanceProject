**Checks that regressed:**

- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: Not a stable oligopoly for Indigo — it is a small player facing intense price competition in commoditised categories with industry-wide down-trading and dealer bargaining power evident. | The long view had said: The historic four-player oligopoly (Asian, Berger, Nerolac, Akzo) is being destabilised by well-capitalised entrants — Birla Opus, JSW at Rs 2,000cr, Astral — so structural stability is eroding even if Indigo claims no measurable impact.

Now: Long-horizon commitments held through a bad cycle — seventh consecutive year of IPL brand investment, multi-year painter health and skilling programmes, and continued dealer/tinting expansion despite flat sales. | The long view had said: Founder-CMD Hemant Jalan has run a consistent multi-year playbook — 13-14 years of elevated advertising, a multi-quarter Tier 1/2 town pivot he refused to abandon early, capex on a 4-5 year view — and independently refused to follow Birla Opus into a discount war.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Organic focus with no large M&A, self-funded plant capex continued through a demand downturn, and network/tinting-machine expansion sustained counter-cyclically through weak quarters. | The long view had said: Textbook discipline — wholly organic decorative-paint focus, all expansion funded by internal accruals with no leverage discussed, one small bolt-on, and capex committed through the downturn rather than pulled.

Now: The sector spent over a year in a demand trough and the calls show an identifiable upturn forming — Q1 flat, Q2 +3.5%, Q3 +3.5% with double-digit November/December growth and dealer stock liquidation. | The long view had said: Clearly positioned near a cyclical trough with an identifiable upturn — two years of muted paint demand, then dealer stock liquidation, record painter-token scanning, rate/tax cuts and three consecutive months of double-digit value growth from Nov'25.

Now: Apple Chemie is a small complementary construction-chemicals bolt-on whose margins were weak post-deal but where management-driven mix rationalisation has now produced 31.5% revenue growth and improved gross margins. | The long view had said: Apple Chemie is exactly the prescribed bolt-on — small, complementary (construction chemicals/waterproofing), growing 22-50% y-o-y with gross margins improving post-deal via mix optimisation and a new sealant plant.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 7 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 6 weakened, 5 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Seven straight years of IPL sponsorship and A&P 'far above the industry' point to real brand investment, but A&P has been cut from 7.4% of sales (FY24) to 6.4% (FY25) to 5.9% (9M FY26), and one-year top-line growth of 0.3-4.7% does not evidence brand-driven share gain plus premium pricing; the brand is also too young for a heritage story. (The long view had said: A&P has been 'disproportionately high' versus peers for over a decade, with Dhoni nationally and Mohanlal added for Kerala (28% of revenue), and management explicitly claims the fourth-largest TV ad spend in the industry; the brand has been stretched into waterproofing/construction chemicals (0% to ~7% of topline in two years) and the Apple Chemie subsidiary.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Distribution is expanding steadily through the window (active dealers 18,400 → 18,600 → 18,900 → 19,100; tinting machines 11,000 → 11,900; depot count to 55) and management claims outperformance on mix, but no market-share figure is ever given and FY25 standalone growth of 1.8% with flat volumes is not evidence of persistent share capture. (The long view had said: Share gain is the recurring theme: four consecutive quarters of industry outperformance by FY24 (18% consol growth vs <1% industry), sales doubling FY20-FY24 with PAT tripling, active dealers rising 18,000 to 19,100 and tinting machines 10,000 to 11,900 through the FY25-26 downturn — and gained without sacrificing margin, since gross and EBITDA margins rose over the same period.)
- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Founder-led with unusually stable leadership (Jalan as CMD and Humane as CFO across FY21-FY25) and voluntary category-level volume/value disclosure each quarter; management does own bad news, but the Jodhpur water-based plant promise slipped four times in four calls (Q3 FY26 → Q3/Q4 → late Q4 → June 2026), which cuts against a clean delivered-what-was-promised record. (The long view had said: Founder-CMD Hemant Jalan and the same COO/CFO/IR team run every call from 2022 to 2026, disclosure of volume and value growth by category is granular and consistent, guidance has been met (FY24 EBITDA margin 'almost exactly in line with our earlier guidance'), capex is funded entirely from internal accruals, and misses are owned openly — the FY23 admission of not outgrowing the industry and the FY26 admission on economy emulsions.)
- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The Indigo Colour Canvas experiential stores launched in Q1 FY26 with a 50-70 store target, 8 added in Q2 and steady expansion in Q3, are a genuine step toward the customer — but these sit at dealer counters, no store count share of sales, franchise royalty or D2C revenue was quantified in any of the four calls. (The long view had said: Only in FY26 did the company start its own branded experiential retail format (Indigo Colour Canvas, 8 added in Q2 FY26, expanding in Q3), alongside 55 depots and ~11,900 tinting machines; there is no franchise royalty, D2C revenue share or own-store share-of-sales disclosure, so forward integration is early-stage.)
- **Toll Roads**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Paint is a discretionary, visible, price-negotiated purchase, and management repeatedly flags competitive price pressure in putty; there is no certification, standards or 'tiny share of customer cost' language anywhere in the four calls. (The long view had said: No discussion of the product being a negligible share of customer cost, of certification/standards, or of regulatory/industry-body anchoring; the calls are consumer-demand and channel driven.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The strategy explicitly runs the other way — highest gross margin in the industry (45-47%) and deliberate premiumisation; cost management appears only as opex efficiency, not as a low-price-plus-scale model. (The long view had said: There are cost-culture traits — budget/economy-range launches, itemised A&P savings (7.7% of sales in FY23 to 5.9% in 9M FY26) and 'rigorous cost management initiatives' in Q3 FY26 — but the core model is industry-leading gross margins (45-49%) and premiumisation, not low price with high asset turns.)

*Unchanged: Friendly Middleman (STRONG FIT); Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 5 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **New Entrants**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Management repeatedly describes intense price competition in putty and an industry where peers are seeing down-trading and value growth lagging volume — the signature of capacity-led discounting — while Indigo's own top line has been flat to low-single-digit for four straight quarters and A&P spend was cut from 7.4% of sales in FY24 to 5.9% in 9M FY26. (The long view had said: A structurally different, deep-pocketed entrant (Birla Opus, alongside JSW Paints and Astral) reset industry economics from FY24 onward; analysts pressed management on it in call after call, entry coincided with six-to-seven quarters of negative industry growth, and the entrant launched with unprecedented trade discounts. Management's repeated 'no perceptible impact' framing is itself defensive and remains unproven while Indigo's own growth has stalled at low single digits.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No named customer, licensor or supplier drives results in any of the four calls; sales run through a broad and steadily widening dealer base with 55 depots, and the only concentration disclosed is geographic (Kerala) rather than a single relationship. (The long view had said: No single customer matters — the dealer base broadened from ~18,000 to over 19,100 with 55 depots — but geographic concentration is real, with Kerala at roughly 28% of revenue, prompting a state-specific brand ambassador; brand equity is also anchored on two celebrity relationships (Dhoni, Mohanlal) whose renewal is a discussed dependency.)

**That worsened:**

- **Government Dependency**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Revenue and pricing are not regulator-set, but policy touches the P&L directly — a one-time INR5.85 crore gratuity provision from the draft labour code hit Q3 FY26 PAT — and the Apple Chemie subsidiary's revenue is anchored to government infrastructure work (roads, metro projects), with solar installations also gated on regulatory approvals. (The long view had said: Revenue is 100% decorative retail through ~19,100 dealers with no PSU tenders, regulated tariffs or subsidy-linked pricing anywhere in the calls; government appears only as a macro demand variable (income-tax relief, RBI rate cuts, GST) and as a cost item (the INR5.85 crore gratuity provision from the draft labour code), and management explicitly notes paint got no GST benefit.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
