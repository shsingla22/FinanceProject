**Checks that improved:**

- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Good. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹11 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹11 is operating profit. - **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: Deals in the window are small complementary integration bolt-ons — HSCL for epoxy, 53% of DNV Global hardware, a INR175cr Gujarat saltworks backward integration, and a Teknor Apex JV — but they are frequent and no post-deal OPM improvement is yet demonstrated. | The long view had said: Deals are small and adjacent (Westlake stake buyout, Hindusthan Speciality Chemicals, Fenesta-related acquisitions, Teknor Apex 50% JV in Shriram Polytech), but post-deal margins have not improved — acquisition-related costs are repeatedly cited as a drag on Fenesta PBDIT.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: There is little halo to strip away — revenue growth traces to commissioned capacity and policy while PBDIT grew only 4%, so results are attributable to the cycle and capex rather than demonstrated managerial skill. | The long view had said: Results track exogenous variables far more than management skill — the 36% ROCE of FY23 came from an energy-driven ECU spike and the collapse to 13% from oversupply and dumping, with outcomes repeatedly attributed to power costs, freight, monsoon and government duty decisions.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Oligopoly (vs duopoly)** (Industry Structure): Weak → Poor. Now: Core markets are structurally oversupplied rather than oligopolistic — 6.5mn t of Indian caustic capacity running at 75-85%, H2O2 oversupplied with cheap Bangladeshi imports, and PVC swamped by Chinese resin — leaving customers with full negotiating power. | The long view had said: The end markets are fragmented and buyer-favourable — Indian caustic capacity crossed 6 mtpa with more coming online, hydrogen peroxide is structurally oversupplied with Bangladeshi dumping, and large alumina buyers negotiate contracts directly.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: This is a large listed diversified group with quarterly analyst calls, an IR agency, IFC sustainability-linked funding and heavily contested end-markets — the opposite of an ignored niche. | The long view had said: The businesses are the opposite of ignored — chlor-alkali, PVC, sugar/ethanol and urea are heavily capitalised, policy-watched sectors attracting continuous new capacity and import competition.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: Some reliability positioning exists in agri-inputs and the newly commissioned ECH ('product has been well accepted in the market'), but price, not trust, is plainly the deciding factor across the chemicals and vinyl book. | The long view had said: In seeds and farm solutions the customer is buying reliability — consistent field performance, yield stability and trusted genetics — and Fenesta's project/retail customers buy an assured branded system, though this covers only part of the portfolio.

Now: Geographic ambition is stated for ECH exports to the US and EU but remains at the network-building stage with no execution results reported. | The long view had said: Expansion is real but modest and commodity-led: caustic exports to Middle East, Africa and Southeast Asia grew, Bioseed international contributes, and Fenesta added fabrication units (Bhubaneswar, Hyderabad facade) across Indian geographies — not a premium brand travelling globally.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 3. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Fenesta is a genuine consumer brand being stretched into adjacent building-material categories (DNV hardware, aluminium extrusion), and Shriram Farm Solutions is described as consumer-facing, but no A&P spend, heritage narrative or premium pricing is quantified and Fenesta's margins are 'normalising' rather than expanding. (The long view had said: Fenesta is a real consumer brand crossing Rs 1,112 crore in FY26 (+28%) with sustained promotion spend, and SFS speaks of strengthening the Shriram brand and influencer-led campaigns — but brand has not delivered premium pricing, as Fenesta's PBDIT margin compressed every year from FY25 on product mix and aluminium cost pass-through lags.)
- **Corporate Culture**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Across the four calls the promoter-family management delivered what it said on schedule — ECH moved from trial runs (Jul '25) to commissioning (Oct '25) to full commissioning (Apr '26), Kota's 68MW green power went from 'as per schedule' to actual injection in May '26 — while disclosing bad news plainly (ADD not imposed, lower cane crush, UP ethanol levy) and keeping a conservative balance sheet. (The long view had said: Stable multi-generation family leadership (Ajay/Vikram/Ajit/Aditya Shriram present on every call from 2022 to 2026), conservative net debt (Rs 1,767 crore at Mar-26 against Rs 13,538 crore revenue), consistent dividends and ROCE reported each quarter including when it fell; but the Rs 3,500 crore capex slipped materially — epichlorohydrin was promised for 'early part of financial year 24' in Oct 2022 and only fully commissioned in April 2026 — and this slippage is not owned as a miss on the calls.)

**That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The four calls discuss volumes, capacity utilisation and project commissioning only; there is no mention of AMC/service revenue, attach rates, renewals, subscriptions or customer advances funding the business, even for Fenesta. (The long view had said: Across all 15 calls there is no discussion of AMCs, service contracts, subscriptions, attach rates, renewals or deferred revenue as a funding source; the evidence is silent on this pattern.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Cost to Replicate (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The bulk of the portfolio is undifferentiated commodity chemicals where the buyer has no brand preference and cheaper imported tonnes win outright — evident in caustic, chlorine, hydrogen peroxide and PVC realisations falling to import parity; Fenesta's margins are also 'normalising' on product-mix shifts even as volumes grow, consistent with mix moving toward cheaper offerings. (The long view had said: Most of the portfolio is undifferentiated commodity where the cheapest tonne wins — imported PVC at $600-620 versus a five-year average of $800-820 leaves no room to defend a premium — and at Fenesta, the one branded franchise, margins are compressing as the mix shifts to lower-margin facade/project work management admits it is pricing for entry rather than profit.)

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Across all four FY26 calls management frames demand and profitability in terms of global commodity prices and oversupply cycles — caustic soda FOB prices retracting from $500 to $450/MT, chlorine under pressure, PVC prices whipsawing from Chinese dumping to Middle-East-conflict spikes and back, and sugar swinging from a 4mn MT global deficit to a ~3mn MT surplus within 12 months; meanwhile the company is in a heavy capex expansion (ECH, aluminium/calcium chloride, aluminium extrusion, 68MW power, salt works, epoxy formulations) precisely into these soft markets. (The long view had said: Across all 15 calls management frames demand and earnings in commodity-price terms — ECU, PVC per-tonne prices, sugar realisations — and segment profits swing violently (Vinyl PBDIT +Rs.156cr Q2FY22 → -Rs.10cr Q2FY23 → +Rs.44cr Q1FY25; sugar PBDIT -57% y/y in Q1FY25), while a Rs.3,500cr capex cycle was committed at the FY23 price peak and ROCE fell from 36% (Sep'22) to 13% (Mar'26).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (LOW); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
