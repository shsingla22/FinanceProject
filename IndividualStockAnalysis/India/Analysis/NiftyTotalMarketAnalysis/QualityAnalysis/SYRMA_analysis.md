# Syrma SGS Tech. (SYRMA) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Syrma SGS is an Indian electronics manufacturing services (EMS) company: other companies design or specify a product, and Syrma builds the electronic assemblies and finished devices for them, buying and managing the whole component supply chain on their behalf. Beyond pure contract assembly it also does its own design work — what it calls ODM, roughly 17% of FY26 revenue, up from 12% — including a MedTech design centre and design contracts for global medical-device firms. It earns money on the manufacturing spread (gross margin roughly 16% to 27% depending on mix), so profitability swings with which end-market it is building for, and it is topped up by government PLI incentives on the telecom/consumer work. It sells to both Indian and overseas customers (exports were about 24-25% of FY26 revenue, ₹1,200 crore-plus, with 30 years of EU exporting and a plant in Stuttgart, Germany), operating mainly out of Indian plants including a Pune campus, with FY26 revenue of ₹4,857 crore and EBITDA of ₹545 crore.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Automotive** — Electronics that go into vehicles — described by management as one of the mainstays of the business, about 24-29% of revenue and order book, and growing 39% in FY26.
- **Industrial** — Electronic assemblies for industrial customers, including HVAC work and smart metering (expected to add ₹200 crore-plus of revenue in FY25); it was the single largest vertical at 31% of Q4 FY26 revenue.
- **Consumer** — High-volume, low-margin consumer work led by telecom products, which management deliberately caps (31% of FY26 revenue, kept below 35%) because it drags the overall margin down even though it carries PLI benefits.
- **Healthcare / MedTech** — Medical devices and their design, a small but fast-growing slice (about 5-7% of revenue and order book) that grew 36% in FY26 and is being built up through a dedicated MedTech design centre and a large global design contract.
- **IT and Railways** — The smallest of the main verticals (about 11% of the order book) but the fastest-growing — up 74% in FY26 and 182% year-on-year in Q4 — though it carries lower margins that diluted the quarterly EBITDA mix.
- **Exports** — Selling to overseas customers rather than a separate product line, but tracked as its own performance measure: ₹1,200 crore-plus in FY26 (up 41%), a stated ₹1,500 crore target for the next year, helped by a German plant and the newly signed India-EU FTA, and clouded by US tariff uncertainty.
- **Defence (Elcome)** — A newly acquired business consummated in December 2025 that adds a defence vertical, expected to be only about 5-6% of revenue but to contribute proportionally more to profit (it added roughly ₹55 crore of the FY26 EBITDA).
- **PCB manufacturing (new project)** — A greenfield printed-circuit-board plant under construction, with buildings due around June-July 2026 and trial production targeted for the December 2026-March 2027 quarter — not yet earning revenue, with further capacity to follow demand.

### The story in depth

Syrma SGS is a contract electronics manufacturer that has done what it said it would do. Across the record, management guided conservatively and then delivered: FY25 EBITDA guidance of INR310-325 crore against INR324 crore delivered, FY26 guided at INR400 crore against INR545 crore delivered, and exports guided at INR1,100 crore against INR1,200 crore delivered. Working capital was cut from 80 days to 63, and return on capital employed rose from 12.4% to 16.9% (20.1% adjusted for goodwill) even while heavy spending went into new SMT lines, the Pune and Bangalore campuses and an INR800 crore multilayer PCB plant — spending for growth rather than upkeep, and it paid off. Revenue grew from an INR1,733 crore run-rate in FY23 to INR4,857 crore in FY26, exports grew 41% in FY26 past INR1,200 crore, and the open order book stands at INR6,400 crore. The balance sheet supports all of this: net cash of INR467 crore, debt-to-equity of 0.1x and a credit rating upgrade to AA. This is why the delivery record scores as a strong fit on Corporate Culture and why the checks on candor, growth capex, market-share gains, geographic expansion and structural end-market growth all rate well — the underlying industry shift, electronics manufacturing relocating to India under China+1 with government PLI support, was named as the thesis back in 2022 and has been validated by four years of compounding revenue.

The trouble is that almost none of this converts into a durable moat, and the same facts that make the growth story work also explain why nine of the eleven multibagger patterns are a no-fit or only a partial fit. Margins improved from a 4.6% EBITDA level in Q1 FY25 to 11.3% in FY26 and gross margin from 22.6% to 25.6%, but the record attributes that entirely to mix — capping low-margin consumer work at around 31% while pushing automotive, industrial, healthcare, exports and design-led ODM work — and to operating leverage, never to raising prices. Input-cost inflation is passed through only after negotiation and with a lag, which is why Pricing Power is a clear no-fit. The business is bilateral contract manufacturing, so there is no network that strengthens with scale (scoring -2), no subscription or service revenue to fall back on, no consumer brand, no distribution channel of its own, and nothing in the record explaining why a well-funded newcomer could not copy the model: SMT lines are bought and commissioned in months, and cost and quality are described as basic hygiene rather than an edge. The design-led side is the most promising exception — ODM revenue roughly doubled its share from about 10% to 17%, with a MedTech design centre, a global medical-device design contract and wins in power and IoT — but research spending is never disclosed, so Innovation Dominance rates only a partial fit. The scores reflect this split: strong marks on growth (1.17) and management (1.0), negative marks on competitive advantage (-0.67) and industry structure (-0.33).

The risks are not separate from the strengths; in several cases they are the same fact seen from the other side. Government policy is the biggest one. PLI schemes in telecom, white goods and IT hardware are the structural tailwind that makes the growth check score well, and they are also embedded in the profit line, with PLI claims booked into EBITDA and defended as operating income — which is why government dependency rates high risk. The consumer vertical is a second example: the volume it brings has helped build scale, but it is openly commodity-grade work, gross margin fell to 16% when consumer reached 53% of the mix, and management now deliberately caps it — an admission that a large slice of the business competes on price. That is the Good Enough Goods risk and the reason the Low Price Plus pattern only partly fits. Customer concentration remains elevated, with the top five customers at 34% and the top ten at 47% of revenue, and single relationships have visibly moved the numbers, as when a planned slowdown at one customer caused the FY25 export softness. Results swing accordingly: Q1 FY26 revenue fell 19% year on year before rebounding 37% and 45%, with EBITDA margin ranging from 4.6% to 12.6% on mix alone. Against that, the company is genuinely resilient — four verticals, exports at about a quarter of revenue, net cash, and a willingness to walk away from the Ksolare joint venture — and technology obsolescence is a low risk because general-purpose SMT capacity is agnostic to which end-product wins. The combined rating of 47 out of 100, or Mixed, is the honest arithmetic of this: a well-run, fast-growing company with a proven management team operating in a business that so far shows little structural protection.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Across the whole 2022-2026 arc, government policy is embedded in the P&L: telecom, white-goods and IT-hardware PLI schemes drive both capex and the consumer vertical, PLI claims are booked into EBITDA and defended as operating income, and growth legs include smart metering (state discom programmes), railways, defence (Elcome) and an INR800 crore PCB project whose returns depend on incentives expected only from FY28. > "You see, the margin is 4.6% and not 3.6% because PLI is part of the business. The very fact that we have taken the consumer business is because of the PLI." — *management, on an earnings call*
