## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 31 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 5 improved, 6 regressed, 20 unchanged. 2 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them).

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Allocation | Neutral (+0.40) | Good (+0.80) | +0.40 |
| Return on Capital | Good (+1.00) | Good (+1.33) | +0.33 |
| Growth | Good (+0.67) | Neutral (+0.50) | -0.17 |
| Management | Good (+1.17) | Excellent (+1.50) | +0.33 |
| Industry Structure | Neutral (+0.00) | Neutral (-0.33) | -0.33 |
| Customer Benefits | Neutral (+0.50) | Neutral (+0.00) | -0.50 |
| Competitive Advantage | Neutral (+0.33) | Neutral (+0.00) | -0.33 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Good. Now: No new deals in the window; the two legacy bolt-ons are now improving — Unlimited South is performing and LimeRoad losses were cut ~70% — which fits the 'post-deal economics improve' test. | The long view had said: LimeRoad was a distressed loss-making startup, not an already-strong bolt-on; it depressed margins for years (INR10cr quarterly loss even two years in) and Unlimited required closing 19 stores, though both were eventually fixed.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: No formal R&D line, but sustained investment in design/product development, vendor-mill integration and AI/analytics is described as yielding fresher product and faster trend-to-store cycles. | The long view had said: No formal R&D; there is genuine product-development, design and AI/analytics investment, but no evidence of measurable long-term R&D efficiency.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Management explicitly refuses returns-dilutive growth, heavy discounting and indiscriminate store openings, and expansion is organic with no new M&A in the window. | The long view had said: Zero long-term debt, expansion financed by internal accruals, capped LimeRoad investment at 15-20% of EBITDA and pruned loss-making stores — though the LimeRoad deal itself was an inorganic detour.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Good. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹14 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±1.4 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹14 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±2.1 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control.
- **Halo effect (caution)** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: Results are moderate and management itself attributes swings to weather, festive timing and calendar rather than claiming skill, which makes the skill-versus-luck separation easier and the assessment reasonably halo-resistant. | The long view had said: The 6x PAT jump partly reflects a low COVID-era base, a consumption recovery and cost/marketing cuts, so results should not be read purely as managerial skill despite genuine LTL and inventory-productivity evidence.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Weak. Now: Price is primary rather than secondary — the customer is described as budget-sensitive and comparison-shopping on both price and quality, not buying certainty at a premium. | The long view had said: A deliberate multi-year quality push has converted into measurable trust (NPS >60, 4.4-star reviews across 5 lakh ratings), though price remains primary rather than secondary.
- **Gain in market share** (Growth): Excellent → Good. Now: Eleven consecutive quarters of positive LFL and 23% total / 9% LFL growth in Q1 FY27 evidence share capture, with a long runway from unorganized retail, though competitors are taking share too. | The long view had said: Small share of a fragmented, largely unorganized market with explicit and demonstrated share capture (23-24% growth, 11 straight quarters of LTL growth, 591 stores across 335 cities) and a long runway.
- **Capex for growth (not maintenance)** (Capital Allocation): Excellent → Good. Now: Capex is clearly growth-oriented — new store additions plus a stated good payback on new stores and encouraging new-store performance in the South — though scale and incremental ROIC are not quantified. | The long view had said: Capex is growth-led and rising (INR159cr FY26 to INR170-180cr guided) funded from internal accruals, with 92 new stores in FY26 and new stores delivering above-network throughput, i.e. high incremental returns.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: Regional density in tier-2/3 North and East gives some local advantage, but there are no switching costs and national players like Zudio compete in the same catchments. | The long view had said: Some entrenched local density in small towns where incremental competitor stores no longer dent existing sales, but apparel carries no switching costs or captive aftermarket.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Weak. Now: The Bharat value-retail niche is no longer ignored — national chains and fresh capital are actively targeting the same towns and vendors. | The long view had said: Tier 3/4 small-town value retail is genuinely underserved, but the segment is well covered by listed peers, brokers and incoming capital, so it is not ignored.
- **Network effects** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Weak. Now: The store-based value-retail model has no self-reinforcing two-sided network; the omni/LimeRoad platform is described as an efficiency and loss-reduction story, not a network that strengthens with scale. | The long view had said: LimeRoad is a two-sided marketplace now showing an online-offline omni loop with higher repeat rates, but it is subscale, only recently near break-even, and not self-reinforcing at scale.

*Unchanged: Share donators; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Mini-monopolies; People matter (talent development); Distribution advantage; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Barriers to entry and rationality; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Convenience benefits; Cost of working capital; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Structural end-market growth; Cyclical market growth; Geographical expansion; Technology advantage; Asset turn; Candor; Return on capital (headline); Out of limelight; Intangible benefits.*

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these — identical by construction): Persistence of growth (Neutral); Advertisement and promotion (Good).*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 3 weakened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Innovation Dominance**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Repeated investment in AI/analytics, agentic workflows, supply-chain and design integration is described across all four calls (LimeRoad losses cut ~70% in FY26), but there is no R&D disclosure, no named products with revenue impact, and gross margins are by design below peers — so it is operational technology adoption, not innovation-funded dominance. (The long view had said: No R&D spend is ever disclosed, there is no named-product pipeline with revenue impact, and gross margins are drifting down (34.5%, -80bps in Q1FY27; -100bps in Q4FY26) rather than funding outsized innovation — the tech work described is AI-for-operations (forecasting, replenishment, assortment), i.e. efficiency, not innovation-led dominance.)

**That weakened:**

- **Low Price Plus**: STRONG FIT → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Management explicitly frames the model as deliberately low-priced with below-peer gross margins offset by cost discipline and inventory turns (Jan 2026), reiterating in Jul 2026 that affordability and visible value are non-negotiable and that price laddering/architecture drives the proposition. (The long view had said: The whole model is low price plus acceptable quality at scale: the FY24-25 calls describe a deliberate downward price correction in the South, sustained cost obsession (marketing spend cut 44-59%, expenses +8% vs sales +24% in Q4FY26, +15% vs +23% in Q1FY27), asset-light debt-free balance sheet, and growth driven by volume/throughput rather than unit margin — culminating in PAT up 6x to INR124 crore in FY26.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Q1 FY27 showed 23% growth with 9% LFL and an 11th consecutive quarter of positive LFL, plus successful Southern expansion via Unlimited, but management concedes competition has increased and the unorganized-to-organized shift is being shared with rivals rather than citing any share number against named competitors. (The long view had said: Share gain is the explicit strategy and the delivery is visible across the record: 11 consecutive quarters of positive LTL, 92 stores opened in FY26 and 23-24% revenue growth with footfalls +39%, Unlimited South at 33% revenue growth, all while management notes premium and rival value retailers are struggling — and it is profitable share, with PAT at a record and no long-term debt.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Management itself describes well-funded value retailers opening stores in the same markets, chasing the same vendor base and creating vendor-side pressure — the claimed edges (site selection, sourcing discipline, vendor partnerships) are exactly what a funded copycat like Zudio is currently rebuilding. (The long view had said: The articulable barriers — early store density in underserved Tier 2-4 catchments, site-selection discipline, a low-cost supply chain and the omni customer base — are real but replicable: management concedes competitors and 'rational regional players' are opening stores in the same towns, and the defence offered is market saturation rather than something a funded newcomer could not rebuild.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 2 worsened, 5 unchanged. Financial resilience: Stressed on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Demand is consumption/flow-linked apparel rather than customer capex, but the last four calls repeatedly tie quarterly outcomes to monsoon, agri output/MSP, festival timing and now crude-linked polyester yarn inflation, so sales and gross margin swing with exogenous cycles. (The long view had said: Demand is a flow/consumption product (repeat apparel purchase tied to festivals, weddings, school holidays) rather than customer capex, but the calls repeatedly tie sales to agri output, monsoon, staple inflation and now crude-linked polyester yarn costs, and Q4 FY26 shows raw-material inflation feeding straight into cost of goods.)

**That worsened:**

- **New Entrants**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Across all four calls management repeatedly and defensively discusses accelerating value-format competition — Zudio named directly, with V-Mart conceding structurally lower gross margins and rivals guiding to aggressive store rollouts in coming quarters. (The long view had said: Management discusses new competition in every phase — online marketplaces prompting the 2022 LimeRoad deal, 'more players walking into this market' in FY25, an 'upswing in the competitive space' with rivals opening stores in FY26, and 'more rational regional players opening stores' in FY27 — and the South price-correction exercise was an explicit margin investment to meet it.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: The Nov 2025 call flags real supplier-side strain as rival value retailers chase the same vendor pool; no single named customer or licensor drives results, so exposure is on the sourcing side and bounded. (The long view had said: The base is structurally fragmented — 591 stores across 335 cities, ~30 million registered V-Mart customers plus 17 million LimeRoad users, and sourcing spread across many vendors, mills and a marketplace seller base rather than named licensors or anchor customers; no analyst in these calls asks about a single relationship.)

*Unchanged: Technological Innovation (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Fashion Risk (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Government Dependency (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Stressed on the long view (2 of the 3 stress checks are flagged — multiple warning lights at once.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
