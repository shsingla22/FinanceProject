# I R C T C (IRCTC) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

IRCTC is an Indian government-owned company (a 'Mini Ratna' Govt. of India enterprise) that runs the commercial passenger-facing side of Indian Railways: it operates the online platform where railway tickets are booked, supplies food and catering on trains and at stations, bottles and sells packaged drinking water under the 'Rail Neer' brand, and sells tour and travel packages. Its customers are ordinary Indian rail passengers and travellers, and it works alongside the rail ministry, which introduces the trains (such as Vande Bharat train sets) that IRCTC caters. It earns money mainly from convenience fees and other charges on tickets booked through its site and app, from catering revenue and licence fees paid by catering licensees on trains, from selling bottled water, and from selling tour packages. It operates in India, is listed on the BSE and NSE, and in FY2025-26 reported revenue from operations of about Rs. 5,215 crores, EBITDA of about Rs. 1,666 crores and profit after tax of about Rs. 1,393 crores, having grown from about Rs. 853 crores of revenue in a single quarter of FY2023.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Internet Ticketing** — Runs the online railway ticket-booking platform — management says roughly 89% of all reserved railway tickets in India are now booked through it — earning convenience fee and non-convenience revenue (Rs. 251 crores and Rs. 150 crores respectively in Q3 FY26 on about 14.64 lakh tickets booked per day); it is by far the most profitable segment with EBIT/EBITDA margins of roughly 76%-85%, contributing Rs. 1,536 crores of revenue in FY2025-26.
- **Catering** — Supplies meals and food service on trains (including Rajdhani, Shatabdi and Vande Bharat trains) and at stations, partly through licensees who pay licence fees and partly through its own departmental kitchens and e-catering; it is the largest segment by revenue at Rs. 2,399 crores in FY2025-26 but a low-margin one (EBIT margin often in the 8%-15% range), squeezed by 5% GST on mobile catering and by newer Vande Bharat billing that carries less licence fee.
- **Tourism (including State Teertha)** — Sells tour and travel packages, including pilgrimage tourism run for state governments; it lost money for nine straight quarters during the pandemic but has since become the fastest-growing segment, reaching Rs. 890 crores of revenue in FY2025-26 (up about 19% year-on-year with profit up about 36%) at EBITDA margins of roughly 16%-19%.
- **Rail Neer** — Manufactures and sells packaged drinking water under the Rail Neer brand from its own bottling plants, sold largely to rail passengers; it is the smallest segment at Rs. 391 crores of revenue in FY2025-26, growing only about 3% a year, with margins swinging between small losses and roughly 13%-19% depending on plant utilisation and material costs.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

IRCTC's whole story starts from one fact: it is the arm of Indian Railways legally designated to sell reserved train tickets online, serve food on trains and bottle Rail Neer water. The quality work scores that position at the top of the scale — a mini-monopoly with about 89% of all reserved tickets, a captive onboard water franchise, service reach across 1,265 catering units and 563 station projects that no rival could assemble, and barriers to entry so complete that no new competitor has appeared. The pattern screen reads the same fact three ways and calls all three a strong fit: a Toll Road, because a Rs.20-40 convenience fee is trivial to the passenger but is collected on nearly every reserved ticket at roughly an 85% margin; Cost to Replicate, because no amount of money buys the mandate; and Forward Integrator, because the company keeps taking ownership of the channel to the passenger — its own booking platform, its own water plants, direct-to-customer e-catering.

The financial record is what you would expect from that position. Sales have compounded at about 14% a year with unusually steady year-to-year growth, operating profit is Rs.32 on every Rs.100 of sales with margins moving only about 1.7 points either way, and the business earns a 29% cash return on the capital employed while carrying Rs.2,263 crores of net cash and no debt. Consumer habits have also moved in its favour: the shift from ticket counters to online booking, and from pantry meals to app-ordered food, are both migrations IRCTC captured rather than lost, with e-catering scaling from roughly 21,000 meals a day to over 1 lakh. Demand is consumption-linked rather than cyclical, and the risk review rates both cyclicality and shifting preferences as low. Overall the combined rating comes out at 68 of 100, or Strong.

The catch is that the same mandate that creates the moat also removes the levers. Because the Ministry of Railways sets prices, catering tariffs have not risen since 2019 and the convenience fee is unrevised, so the pattern screen rates Pricing Power a clear no-fit and the quality framework marks pricing, mix and volume at its worst score: growth comes almost entirely from more trains and more tickets, with the mix drifting toward lower-margin work. That leaves the company exposed on the cost side with no way to pass costs on, and it is already visible — as customers moved to UPI, from 33% to 51.7% of payments, management attributes the ticketing segment's slide from an 88% to a 76% operating margin directly to that shift. The risk review names government dependency and stakeholder concentration as high risks for the same underlying reason: one counterparty is simultaneously parent, regulator, sole channel, price-setter and litigation opponent, and it has already granted and then withdrawn the 2S booking window and imposed haulage charges retrospectively. Growth guidance itself depends on the ministry delivering 260 Vande Bharat train sets and station upgrades. Capital allocation and management score neutral, and the growth area scores slightly negative, which is what pulls an otherwise exceptional franchise down to Strong rather than higher.
