# Vaibhav Global (VAIBHAVGBL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Vaibhav Global (VGL) is a Jaipur-based company that sells low-priced fashion jewellery and lifestyle products directly to bargain-hunting retail consumers in the US, UK and Germany, mainly through its own TV shopping channels (Shop LC in the US, Shop TJC and Ideal World in the UK, Shop LC GmbH in Germany) and its websites, apps, OTT platforms, social/live-stream commerce and third-party marketplaces. It makes money by manufacturing and sourcing goods cheaply through its own vertically integrated supply chain spanning about 30 countries — including its own factories in India and, from FY26, jewellery casting in the US — and selling them at deep-discount 'value' prices, which still yields gross margins above 60% (63% in Q3 FY26) and EBITDA margins of roughly 10-11%. Growth is driven by a '4R' framework — widening TV reach (about 127 million TV households), new customer registrations, retention (around 38-40%) and repeat purchases (roughly 22-23 pieces per customer a year) — off a unique customer base of about 6.8 lakh. Full-year FY26 revenue was Rs 3,691 crore, up from Rs 628 crore in the June 2022 quarter alone, with the business generating record free cash flow of Rs 272 crore and sitting on net cash.

**The parts of the business:**

- **TV / broadcast retail (Shop LC US, Shop TJC and Ideal World UK, Shop LC GmbH Germany)** — Proprietary live TV shopping channels carried on cable, satellite, telco and over-the-air (OTA/Freeview/Freenet) platforms reaching about 127 million households; still the larger half of sales at Rs 589 crore in Q3 FY26 (roughly 58% of revenue).
- **Digital / e-commerce** — Sales through VGL's own websites and smartphone apps plus OTT, social and live-stream commerce and third-party marketplaces; grew to Rs 423 crore in Q3 FY26, about 42-44% of B2C sales, with management targeting 50% by end of FY27.
- **Geographies — US, UK, Germany** — The US (Shop LC) is the largest and most mature market growing low single digits; the UK combines the older TJC business (recently declining) with the acquired Ideal World channel (growing 12% and profitable); Germany is the newest market, growing 5-18% and only turning EBITDA-positive in FY26.
- **Vertically integrated manufacturing and sourcing** — In-house manufacturing in India (solar-powered), gemstone sourcing across roughly 30 countries and new US jewellery casting set up to dodge tariffs — this is what management repeatedly credits for gross margins staying above 60%; VGL also won a GJEPC award as India's largest exporter of cut and polished coloured gemstones in FY23.
- **In-house brands** — Own-label products that management pushes because they improve sourcing efficiency and pricing power; crossed 50% of B2C sales in FY26, a year ahead of target.
- **Product categories — jewellery, lifestyle products, lab-grown diamonds** — Jewellery remains the core, lifestyle (non-jewellery) products are about 35% of sales with a medium-term goal of 50%, and lab-grown diamonds are a newer, higher-priced line at roughly 11% of retail revenue with an average selling price near USD 250.
- **Budget Pay (instalment payments)** — An in-house EMI/instalment payment option that lets customers spread purchases over time; management said it accounted for 38% of B2C revenue in Q1 FY25.
- **Mindful Souls** — An acquired digital subscription-box style business that launches new products regularly; small but profitable and margin-accretive, with about 85,000 unique customers gained since acquisition.
- **Discontinued apparel manufacturing** — A low-margin apparel manufacturing operation the company exited during FY25, which had been dragging overall margin by about 25 basis points.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Vaibhav Global is a Jaipur-based jewellery and lifestyle manufacturer that owns the entire path from its own factories to the end shopper — proprietary TV channels on cable, over-the-air and Freeview, its own websites and apps, live-stream and social commerce, plus the bolt-on purchases of Ideal World and digital-first Mindful Souls. That ownership of the whole route to the customer is why the screen rates it a STRONG FIT for the Forward Integrators pattern, and it is the same structure that produces the best quality scores: every rupee of assets generates ₹1.45 of sales, the company keeps ₹65 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs, gross margins run 63-66%, and in-house brands passed half of consumer sales a year ahead of plan. The balance sheet backs this up — net cash of ₹296 crore in FY26 (up from ₹158 crore), a 24% return on capital employed, record free cash flow of ₹272 crore, a 15% cash return on capital, and sales compounding around 9% a year with unusual year-to-year consistency. Management behaviour matches: only small complementary acquisitions, both of which turned profitable (Ideal World growing at a double-digit rate and lifting UK profitability by 2.2 percentage points), consistent dividends, and continued spending through the downturn on Germany, over-the-air distribution and digital. Return on Capital (+0.67) is the strongest of the seven quality areas, with Management, Growth and Customer Benefits each at +0.5.

The awkward part is that the very traits behind those strengths also generate the risks. The deep-discount, low-cost model that earns a LIKELY FIT on Low Price Plus is precisely what makes the Good Enough Goods risk live: the company is itself the cheap attacker on branded jewellery retail, the product is not complex, customer awareness of its own brands has historically been low, and the same playbook can be run against it — which is what the New Entrants risk points to when it flags live social commerce and ultra-discount cross-border platforms, and what analysts are probing when they ask whether the 9-10% volume declines reflect competition rather than mix. Owning distribution cuts the same way: the owned channel is largely television, and management concedes cord-cutting, reach flat at about 127 million homes since 2022, falling TV volumes, and customer acquisition migrating to Meta, TikTok and Google. That migration shows up directly in the worst-scoring items — content, broadcasting and digital marketing spend compounding at roughly 23% against roughly 12% revenue growth, climbing from 11% of sales in FY20 to 20% and staying there, with management admitting the line gives no operating leverage. Pricing is the third example: gross margin has held above 60% and improved on focused pricing, higher average selling prices ($32 to $38 on digital) and lab-grown diamonds at about 11% of retail revenue, yet customers openly defer purchases when prices rise and volumes fell — so the Pricing Power pattern only reaches LIKELY FIT, and the growth is genuinely price and mix rather than more goods sold. Cost to Replicate stops at PARTIAL for a blunt reason: management itself says the manufacturing base needs almost no capital to copy, and the company has been exiting broadcast slots. The framework's own weakest areas — Capital Allocation at -0.4 and Competitive Advantage at -0.33, alongside a very long 189-day gap between paying suppliers and collecting from customers — line up with exactly these tensions.

Put together, the risk review reads as one elevated risk and six on watch, with overall resilience STRAINED. Cyclicality is the elevated one because demand is discretionary and macro-driven: revenue fell 8% in one quarter of FY23 as inflation hit spending and operating margin dropped from 14.4% to 7%, and in FY26 high precious metal prices squeezed it again. The offsets to that are structural rather than cyclical — around 680,000 to 706,000 individual customers, so no customer or supplier concentration, 38-40% retention, 22-23 pieces bought per customer per year, and 50-100 new products a day across a deliberately wide range, which is why Fashion Risk is the single LOW rating: nothing rests on one trend or one licence. Distribution, not customers, is where the concentration sits, in a handful of channel gatekeepers and slots. The company is arguably at the low point of its cycle with named levers ahead — India-UK and EU trade agreements, easing US-China tariffs, margins already turning up, Germany reaching breakeven, digital at 44% of consumer sales heading to 50%, lifestyle at 35% of sales targeting 50% — but tariffs added 4.89 percentage points to product costs in one quarter of FY26, forcing it to set up its own jewellery casting in the US, and management's promises have repeatedly slipped, which is why Corporate Culture rates only PARTIAL. That is the honest shape of the combined 58-out-of-100 'Decent' rating: 54 for business quality, 64 for pattern fit, 58 for risk safety. A cash-generative, asset-light, well-run business with a modest protective moat, whose next few years hinge on whether better prices and richer mix can keep outrunning falling volumes and a marketing bill that keeps growing faster than sales.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.3× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 82% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ⚠️ Returns on capital slid from about 20% to 15% — the business is earning less and less on the money invested in it.

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Demand is discretionary and macro-sensitive rather than capex- or commodity-cycle driven: revenue fell 8% year-on-year in Q1 FY23 as inflation hit discretionary spend and EBITDA margin collapsed from 14.4% to 7%, and in FY26 elevated precious metal prices and soft consumer confidence caused customers to defer purchases (US +1-3%, UK -1% to -2%). It is a consumer swing, not a customer-capex swing, and low-ticket repeat purchasing (22-23 pieces per customer per year, 38-40% retention) cushions it.

> "the high inflation in western economies constrained consumer to spend on discretionary items" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/repeat-purchase demand base — low ASP, 38-40% retention, ~23 pieces per customer per year; deep-discount value positioning management says works across economic cycles; countercyclical investment in Germany/OTA/digital through the FY23-24 downturn*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 2 of the last 11 years with swings of ±9 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 5% and 15% (±2.8 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 11% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 1% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Technological Innovation — WATCH
*"Out-invented by the industry"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** The core asset — TV airtime, channel positions and OTA households — is the format under pressure: management concedes cable 'cord-cutting', TV reach has been flat at ~127mn homes since 2022, TV volumes are declining, and customer acquisition has migrated to Meta/TikTok/Google where content and broadcasting cost has ratcheted from 11% of sales (FY20) to ~20% with management explicitly guiding no leverage from that line. The pivot is working (digital 44% of B2C, targeting 50%), but the industry's distribution layer is being reset around them.

> "the retail landscape continued to shift rapidly towards digital -first customer discovery with paid social media emerging as one of the most important channel" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Omnichannel pivot executing — digital at 44% of B2C heading to 50%, OTT/live-stream/social commerce traction, plus small-scale continuous innovation (AI in targeting, merchandising and HR productivity delivering 120bps employee-cost leverage)*

- ✅ Growth is holding up: 11% a year earlier, 8% recently — no structural stall.
- ✅ Operating margin (10%) remains near its historical peak (15%) — no collapse in the premium.

## What to watch

- Marketing spend as a share of sales — the 'Advertisement and promotion' check (-1) only reverses if the line stops growing at roughly twice the pace of revenue and falls back below 20% of sales.
