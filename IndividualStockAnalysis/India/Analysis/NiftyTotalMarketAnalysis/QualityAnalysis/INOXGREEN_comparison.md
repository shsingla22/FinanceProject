### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 5 strengthened, 2 weakened, 3 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: FAIL on the long view, FAIL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Inox Green is structurally a recurring-revenue O&M annuity attached to the front-end turbine sale by parent Inox Wind, with the installed base compounding every quarter (12.5 GW in Nov-25, 13.3 GW in Feb-26, 13+ GWp in Jun-26) and performance measured on contracted machine availability (~96.3–96.5%). What is missing across all four calls is any disclosure of attach rates, renewal rates, contract tenor, pricing per MW or advance billing/deferred revenue as a funding source — and a meaningful part of reported profit is 'other income', so the quality of the recurring stream is asserted rather than evidenced. (The long view had said: Core model is multi-year O&M annuities attached to each turbine sold by parent Inox Wind — 10-year average contracts with 5% annual escalation, ~Rs 8-10 lakh/MW/annum, ~50% EBITDA margins, portfolio compounding from 2.8GW (Dec 2022) to 13.3GW (FY26); management repeatedly frames it as 'multi-decadal' annuity revenue. Weaker on the customer-funding leg: first two years are free O&M (cash only from year three) and group working capital ran 200-210 days, so it is not a prepaid/negative-working-capital model.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: Not a single price increase, escalation clause or elasticity discussion appears in the Sep-25 to Jun-26 calls. Margin guidance is raised (18–19% to 20–22%) but management attributes it entirely to integration and cost/asset-structure changes, saying nothing about realisation per MW. (The long view had said: Conditional/aftermarket pricing power exists: contracts carry a built-in 5% annual escalation and management says it can renegotiate price at renewal on a locked-in installed base, with EBITDA margin held at ~50% across all twelve quarters. But 5% is roughly cost inflation rather than above it, and no explicit price increase without volume loss is ever demonstrated.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: No heritage story, no A&P spend, no distribution leverage or consistency promise is discussed; the customer-facing pull described is policy tailwinds (ALMM, GST cut, CERC hybridisation) and group captive demand, not brand. (The long view had said: What exists is group provenance and B2B credibility — 'backed by a very, very strong and dependable $5 billion Inox GFL group', 18 years in wind, marquee customers (Aditya Birla, Amplus, Jackson, KEC, CESC) — but there is no A&P spend, no consumer brand, no premium pricing attributed to the name, so this falls well short of brand-driven share plus price premium.)
- **Innovation Dominance**: PARTIAL → QUANT SIGNAL. Why now: No R&D spend, pipeline or named product is disclosed for the O&M business itself; the only product news — the 4.4 MW turbine — belongs to parent Inox Wind and has slipped, being 'expected within this calendar year' in both Feb-26 and Jun-26 without a launch. (The long view had said: Some named, revenue-linked incremental innovation — the 4.45MW 4X turbine launch at the parent, WTG overhauling and life-extension packages described as a growth stream, multi-brand servicing capability for non-Inox OEM fleets, agentic AI deployment — but no R&D spend is ever disclosed or quantified at Inox Green, and none of it is presented as an R&D-led dominance.)
- **Forward Integrators**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The group is a genuine forward integrator in structure — a turbine manufacturer that has moved downstream into EPC, O&M and now its own IPP (Inox Clean Energy) — and management explicitly frames this as a 'virtuous cycle' where roughly two-thirds of Inox Wind's next 4–5 years of execution comes from captive group demand. But this is integration into related-party demand rather than the pattern's own-store/franchise/D2C economics, and it raises rather than settles questions about arm's-length pricing. (The long view had said: The strategic direction across the 12 calls is the opposite: backward integration into cranes, transformers and power electronics, and a deliberate pivot from turnkey EPC to plain equipment supply (100% turnkey 24 months ago to 75-80% equipment supply) explicitly to shed customer-facing working capital — moving away from the customer, not toward them.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Portfolio expands visibly across the year (12.5 GW → 13.3 GW → 13+ GWp) with a stated ambition to be India's largest renewable O&M player, but the bulk of the gain is bought — a 6.5 GW acquisition of two companies still awaiting statutory approval and consolidation, plus solar assets from KEC and group company Inox Clean. No share percentage versus named competitors is ever given, and share added via acquisition and captive group flow is exactly the kind the pattern warns is worth less. (The long view had said: Persistent, explicit portfolio share gain across the whole period: 90MW to 3.05GW over a decade, 3.5GW in Nov 2024 against a 6GW FY26 target, and 13.3GWp by Feb 2026 including 6.5GW of acquired third-party OEM assets, with management claiming India's largest renewable O&M position. The caveat is that much of the step-up was bought via acquisitions and captive group volumes rather than won competitively, and the FY26 acquisitions were still not consolidated as of Jun 2026.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: No cost-culture evidence in the last four calls — no itemised savings, no no-frills positioning, no discussion of passing cost savings into price. Margin improvement is attributed to backward integration (cranes, transformers) and to depreciation removal via the substation demerger, not to a replicable low-cost operating culture. (The long view had said: Genuine and itemised cost culture — indigenising gearboxes and control systems with local vendors, in-housing cranes and transformers, the Vayuveer training programme to build cheaper skilled manpower, and now agentic AI on low-value job profiles — but the savings are described as margin accretion, never as price passed to customers, and there is no scale-plus-low-price proposition.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 3 worsened, 1 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Inox Green's own O&M revenue is annuity-like and operations-linked (machine availability ~96.3-96.5% on a 13+ GWp portfolio), which is a genuine flow-product mitigant, but its portfolio growth is entirely a function of the renewable capex cycle — parent Inox Wind's turbine execution and Inox Clean Energy's 3 GW/year build plan — and the Q3/Q4 FY26 calls show that cycle slipping via customer site-readiness delays and geopolitical logistics issues. (The long view had said: The core O&M business is a flow/operations product — 10-year contracts with 5% annual escalation, ~50% EBITDA margins and 96.4-96.5% machine availability sustained across FY23-FY26 — so revenue is annuity-like; but portfolio growth is entirely a derivative of wind capex additions, and the Feb 2026 call showed customer site-readiness delays deferring turbine offtake and forcing the group to abandon megawatt guidance.)
- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Management devotes call time to defensively rebutting the view that solar plus battery storage displaces wind, indicating a live platform-substitution debate around the asset base Inox Green services; separately the group is launching a 4.45 MW turbine, which progressively makes the older, smaller installed fleet under O&M legacy. (The long view had said: Wind O&M sits downstream of turbine technology shifts — the group is launching a new 4.45 MW platform while servicing an ageing legacy fleet, and an investor in Jun 2026 asked directly about wind's standing versus solar-plus-BESS as battery prices fall. The innovation the company itself does is small-scale and value-adding (indigenised gearboxes/control systems, agentic AI on low-value job profiles, WTG overhauling and life-extension packages), which is a mitigant rather than a threat.)

**That worsened:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Inox Green is 56% held by Inox Wind and its stated growth engine is the related-party group — portfolio additions from parent execution and from group IPP Inox Clean Energy — while on the parent side management states two-thirds of annual execution for the next 4-5 years will come from Inox Clean and CESC; a further 6.5 GW of the 13.3 GW portfolio comes from acquiring assets of just two companies. (The long view had said: Portfolio additions are structurally tied to related parties: contracts flow automatically from parent Inox Wind's commissionings ('married to us for the next 20 years or 25 years'), and by Jun 2026 management stated two-thirds of annual execution for the next 4-5 years will come from group company Inox Clean and CESC, with Inox Green explicitly positioned as the beneficiary of Inox Clean's 3 GW/year build-out. Growth visibility therefore rests on a handful of affiliated counterparties rather than a broad customer base.)
- **New Entrants**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Management discusses low-cost Chinese players importing components as a competitive threat requiring a policy remedy to create a level playing field, which is a structurally different competitor entering the domestic wind supply chain; the exposure is bounded because the ALMM/DCR notification currently blocks that entry. (The long view had said: The structurally different competitor in wind O&M is the independent service provider undercutting OEM-tied contracts; management's response since Feb 2023 has been to own that channel rather than defend against it — acquiring I-FOX at a 4x multiple, building multi-brand capability across 'almost all the OEMs in the market', and bidding for third-party and PSU O&M tenders. No discounting or margin investment is framed as a competitive response.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: NO SIGNAL → LOW. Why now: The demand base is shifting toward, not away from, this company's product — management cites the global shift to greener power, India's best-ever 6 GW wind addition in FY26 and an expected 8-10 GW annually, with renewables moving from infirm to firm power; no erosion of the underlying demand base is described in any of the four calls. (The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and the only computed fingerprint is clean.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Good Enough Goods (ELEVATED); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
