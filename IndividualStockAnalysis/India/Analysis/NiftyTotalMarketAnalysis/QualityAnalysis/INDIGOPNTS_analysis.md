# Indigo Paints (INDIGOPNTS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Indigo Paints is an Indian paint company that makes and sells decorative paints and related coatings for buildings — emulsions (interior and exterior wall paints), enamels and wood coatings, primers and distempers, and cement-based putty and cement paints — plus a growing range of waterproofing and construction-chemical products. It sells through a dealer network across India (over 19,100 active dealers, close to 11,900 tinting machines placed at dealer counters and 55 depots as of December 2025), rather than directly to end customers; dealers stock and resell to painters, contractors and households, and the company pushes demand by advertising heavily (MS Dhoni nationally, Mohanlal for Kerala), running influencer/painter engagement programs, and opening branded 'Indigo Color Canvas' display stores at select dealer counters. It earns money on the manufacturing spread — management repeatedly points to industry-leading gross margins (45–49%) driven by a basket of 'differentiated' products that face less competition — with revenue of about Rs. 1,306 crores in FY24 and roughly Rs. 980 crores over the first nine months of FY26 on a consolidated basis. Operations are India-only (Kerala is called its strongest state, with plants in Tamil Nadu, Jodhpur, Cochin and a head office in Pune), with exports only just being explored for its subsidiary.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Emulsions** — Water-based interior and exterior wall paints — described as the largest category by value, contributing almost half of value sales for any paint company, and where the premium end has recently grown faster than the mass end.
- **Enamels and wood coatings** — Solvent-based glossy paints for metal and wood surfaces; a meaningful category that grew fastest in Q3 FY26 (18.9% value, 20.2% volume) and is now served by a new solvent-based plant at Jodhpur.
- **Primers, distempers and others** — Cheaper base and budget wall coatings applied before or instead of emulsions; a smaller category that management has at times flagged as a weak spot and pushed with extra promotions.
- **Putty and cement paints (powder paints)** — Powder products, mainly wall putty used to smooth surfaces before painting, made at Jodhpur where capacity was recently doubled through a brownfield expansion; growth here has been slower in recent quarters.
- **Waterproofing and construction chemicals (Indigo Protect Plus)** — A newer product line sold through the same dealer network, growing 'at a phenomenal pace' from a mid-single-digit share of revenue in FY24 to close to 7% of the top line by Q3 FY26.
- **Apple Chemie (subsidiary)** — A separately branded construction-chemicals business, strongest in Maharashtra and expanding into southern and eastern India, with quarterly revenue of about Rs. 20 crores in Q3 FY26 (up 31.5% year on year), a new sealant plant at Nagpur, and exports being explored — small next to the parent but the fastest-growing piece.
- **Differentiated products** — Not a separate segment but how management describes its niche, low-competition offerings spread across the categories; these are credited with the company's best-in-industry gross margins and with the Q4 margin peak each year.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Indigo Paints comes out of the three reviews as a well-run, founder-led decorative paints business that earns a combined score of 77 out of 100, or Strong (four stars), built from 67 on business quality, a full 100 on multibagger pattern fit and 68 on risk safety, with overall resilience judged sound. The quality framework's best marks cluster around the people and the profit engine: management and growth both score highest, with founder-CMD Hemant Jalan running a consistent multi-year playbook alongside the same COO, CFO and IR team on every call from 2022 to 2026, and candour rated at the top of the scale because the company openly called its own FY23 topline growth 'disappointing', named the segment it mishandled and the fix, and discloses volume and value by category every quarter. That same discipline shows up in the numbers: ₹47 of every ₹100 of sales is kept after direct input costs, ₹18 of every ₹100 is operating profit with margins moving only about ±0.6 points a year, and capital in the business generated a 19% cash return. Expansion — the Tamil Nadu 50,000 KLPA greenfield, Jodhpur water-based (~₹270cr), solvent-based (₹40–45cr) and putty brownfield — has all been funded from internal accruals with no leverage discussed, and the one acquisition, Apple Chemie, is the textbook small bolt-on: complementary construction chemicals and waterproofing, growing 22–50% a year with margins improving after the deal.

The striking thing is how often a single trait is simultaneously the source of a strength and the source of a risk. The company sells through painters, contractors and dealers rather than to the end consumer — that is the whole basis of the Friendly Middleman pattern rated a strong fit, and the reason the Tier-1/Tier-2 town pivot of August 2022 was framed as 'extensive engagement with the influencer community'. But the eight-channel risk review flags exactly the same arrangement under Good Enough Goods: when the painter or dealer largely controls which brand gets used, end-consumer awareness stays low and the industry runs on trade discounts and incentives, so wins have to be repeatedly bought back. The same doubling happens with the brand. Advertising spend has been 'disproportionately high' versus peers for over a decade, with the claimed fourth-largest TV ad spend in the industry and celebrity ambassadors including a Kerala-specific one — a strong-fit Brand Strength pattern. Yet the Stakeholder Concentration risk points at the underside of that: Kerala alone is roughly 28% of revenue, which is precisely why a state-specific ambassador was needed, and brand equity rests on a small number of celebrity faces. Most importantly, the market-share story and the competitive threat are two readings of one fact. Share gain is the recurring theme — four straight quarters of beating the industry by FY24 (18% consolidated growth against under 1% for the industry), sales doubling and profit tripling between FY20 and FY24, active dealers up from 18,000 to 19,100 and tinting machines from 10,000 to 11,900 — but the Cost to Replicate pattern only rates partial, and New Entrants is the one channel rated high risk, because Birla Opus, JSW Paints and Astral have reset industry economics since FY24 and management itself describes its moat as time-limited against deep pockets. This is why competitive advantage is the only quality area to score negative while every other area is positive.

The cycle sits underneath all of it. The framework rates the company as clearly near a cyclical trough with an identifiable upturn — two muted years, dealer stock liquidation, record painter-token scanning, rate and tax cuts, and three consecutive months of double-digit movement — while the risk review keeps cyclicality on watch for the same period seen from the other side: value growth of 43.6% in Q1 FY23 fell to 1.9% in H1 FY26 after roughly six quarters of negative industry growth. The mitigating fact is that demand is repainting-led rather than a customer capex decision, with new construction described as a very small part of paint purchase, and that capex was committed through the downturn rather than pulled — though the growth-capex check scores only 1 rather than 2 because the incremental return on that capital is not yet proven, and the headline return on capital carries the same qualification. Three of the remaining tensions are worth naming plainly. First, advertising spend has been cut from 7.7% of sales in FY23 to 5.9% in 9M FY26 alongside 'rigorous cost management' — genuine cost discipline that feeds the partial Low Price Plus reading, but it is a reduction in the very spending that underpins the Brand Strength pattern. Second, innovation is rated only a likely fit rather than a strong one: differentiated products are the stated source of the industry's highest gross margins and launches are steady, but R&D spend is never disclosed. Third, the newer defences are early — the Indigo Colour Canvas retail format only began in FY26 with 8 stores added in Q2, which is why forward integration is a likely rather than strong fit, and there is no recurring revenue stream anywhere in the records. Set against that, four risk channels — technological innovation, government dependency, shifting consumer preferences and fashion risk — all come out low, because this is a pure retail decorative franchise selling a functional, repeat-purchase product with no tender exposure, no format obsolescence and no taste cycle. The verdict, in short: a disciplined, candid, share-gaining operator whose channel-and-brand model is both its engine and its exposure, priced against a genuinely well-capitalised new competitor.

## What to watch

- Whether gross margin holds near the 44.1%–47.1% range as Birla Opus, JSW Paints and Astral scale — the New Entrants channel is the only high-risk rating, and margin is the first place a discounting war would show.
- Whether volume growth converts into value growth as the cyclical upturn plays out — the Pricing, Mix and Volume check rests on value growing at roughly double volume, and any slide toward peers' down-trading would undercut both the Pricing Power pattern and the Cyclicality watch.
- Kerala's share of revenue moving away from roughly 28%, and progress in the 750 Tier-1/Tier-2 towns — the Stakeholder Concentration risk names geographic concentration as the live exposure, with the town push as the stated fix.
- Whether advertising spend keeps falling below 5.9% of sales — the Brand Strength pattern is built on 13–14 years of disproportionately high spend, so continued cuts trade the moat for near-term margin.
- Returns from the new capacity (Tamil Nadu, Jodhpur, putty) and the Indigo Colour Canvas store rollout — the growth-capex check scores 1 rather than 2 precisely because incremental returns are unproven, and Forward Integrators is only a likely fit until the own-format channel has real scale.
