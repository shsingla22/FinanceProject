# Star Cement (STARCEMENT) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Star Cement Limited is an Indian cement maker: it digs up limestone, burns it into clinker at its own plants in Meghalaya, then grinds that clinker into bagged cement at grinding units in places like Guwahati, Siliguri and (from early 2026) Silchar. It sells mainly PPC (blended) cement with a smaller share of OPC, plus small amounts of raw clinker, and makes money on the gap between the selling price per tonne and its cost of fuel, power, raw material and freight — management tracks the business almost entirely in tonnes sold and 'EBITDA per ton' (roughly INR1,000-1,900 per tonne across the calls). Its home market is India's Northeast, which supplies about three-quarters of volumes, with the rest sold 'outside Northeast' in West Bengal and Bihar. In FY26 it sold about 5.3 million tonnes of cement for revenue of about INR3,776 crore, EBITDA of about INR955 crore and profit after tax of about INR390 crore, and it is spending heavily on new capacity, including planned grinding units in Bihar and Haryana.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Cement sales in the Northeast** — The core business — bagged cement sold across the Northeastern states, running at roughly 9-11 lakh tonnes a quarter and about three-quarters of total volumes, where management says prices hold up better than elsewhere because rival plants face high fuel costs.
- **Cement sales outside Northeast** — Cement pushed into neighbouring eastern markets, chiefly West Bengal and Bihar (including from the Siliguri grinding unit), at roughly 3-5 lakh tonnes a quarter; it is the smaller, more price-competitive and more volatile piece, with utilisation at Siliguri quoted around 55-70%.
- **Clinker production and sale** — The upstream half of the business — clinker plants at Lumshnong, Meghalaya feed the company's own grinding units, and small surplus volumes (0.5-1.2 lakh tonnes a quarter recently) are sold outright; when its own clinker line had teething problems in FY25 the company had to buy clinker outside, which cost it about INR30 crore of EBITDA in one quarter.
- **Trade vs non-trade channel** — About 84-86% of cement goes through the trade channel (dealers and retail, the better-priced route) and the remaining 14-16% non-trade to bulk or institutional buyers.
- **Premium products** — A higher-priced grade within the cement mix that management tracks separately, quoted at about 9.2% of volumes in mid-2024.
- **Government incentives / subsidy income** — State incentives, mainly an Assam SGST refund benefit, booked as income and material to profits — about INR33-56 crore a quarter and INR184 crore in FY26, though management expects it to fall to roughly INR145-150 crore in FY27 after GST rate cuts.
- **Capacity expansion (capex)** — An ongoing build-out — a 3 MTPA clinker line and 2 MTPA of grinding in Meghalaya and Guwahati completed earlier, the Silchar plant commissioned in February 2026, and land and approvals underway for new grinding units in Bihar and Haryana, with capex guided at roughly INR600-700 crore in FY27 and about INR1,500 crore in FY28.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Star Cement's foundation is a protected home market. The quality framework gives its highest marks to a genuine regional quasi-monopoly in the Northeast — limestone access in Meghalaya, integrated clinker-to-grinding logistics, and a three-to-four year lead time before any new entrant can arrive. That position shows up in the economics: after direct input costs the company keeps ₹87 of every ₹100 of sales, ₹25 of every ₹100 is operating profit, and capital in the business earned a 20% cash return this year. Management gets credit for behaving conservatively alongside this — four years of purely organic growth funded from cash flow, net debt of only ₹200cr against FY26 EBITDA of ₹955cr, continued investment through the weak FY25 cycle, and unusually candid disclosure, including cutting its own volume guidance mid-year and itemising a ₹30cr cost caused by its own plant's technical failure. The combined rating lands at 51 out of 100, a 'Decent' three-star verdict, with resilience judged sound.

Only one of the eleven multibagger patterns is a likely fit, and it grows directly out of that protected position: Market Share Gainers. Northeast volumes rose from 6.48 to 11.27 lakh tons a quarter, the company grew 3% in Q1 FY25 while its market shrank 8%, and volumes scaled from a 4mt FY23 target to 5.3mt in FY26 with 10-12% guided ahead. Five further patterns register as partial, and each is partial for the same reason — the strength is real but bounded by geography or by disclosure. Pricing power is genuine (₹20 a bag more year-on-year in the Northeast, realisations up 2% in Q3 FY26 while peers reported declines) but conditional on the home market. Brand strength is real, with the premium ARC grade scaling from roughly 3.5-4% of volumes in April 2022 to 15.1% by Q4 FY26, yet advertising and promotion spend is never quantified. The cost culture is itemised and credible — diversified fuel, 12MW of waste-heat recovery, manpower run-rate cut from ₹52cr to ₹45cr, lead distance down from 230km to 220km — but Star sells at premium rather than low prices, so it is not a low-cost play. Four patterns do not fit at all: there is no innovation pipeline, no recurring-revenue mechanics, no dealer or mason prescriber programme, and no owned stores or direct-to-customer channel.

The risk review keeps returning to the very traits that create the strengths. The high profitability of the protected Northeast is precisely what draws mainland majors in: Dalmia has commissioned a large kiln, investors have asked directly about Shree Cement and Ambuja, and management's own answer — that entry takes three to four years — is both the description of the moat and a countdown on it. Government support works the same way. A large slice of profit is policy-granted, and incentives worth ₹184cr in FY26 were cut to a guided ₹145-150cr by a single central decision to move GST from 28% to 18%; the geographic push into Rajasthan, Haryana and Bihar dilutes that dependence but also takes the company outside the market where its pricing power was proven. Cyclicality is rated high risk with EBITDA per ton swinging from ₹1,410 to ₹1,018 to ₹1,871 across the period, which matches the quality framework's observation that margins have moved about ±5.3 points a year on costs management does not control — and this swing sits against a near-continuous capex programme running from roughly ₹2,000cr to a further ₹4,800cr. Two smaller strains compound it: about 177 days of cash is tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and management has admitted giving back margin as dealer schemes to push the premium product, in a category where dealers steer the end customer. The pattern of the whole record is a strong regional business whose best qualities and its main exposures are the same facts read from opposite sides.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Government Dependency, New Entrants, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Cement is a price-driven commodity where Star's earnings swing with regional prices, monsoon, elections and fuel costs — EBITDA/ton moved from INR1,410 (Q1FY23) to INR1,018 (Q1FY25) to INR1,871 (Q4FY26) — while the company runs a near-continuous capex cycle (INR2,000cr in FY23-24, then INR4,800cr for Rajasthan/Bihar/Umrangso through FY29) into an expansionary peak, exactly the over-investment pattern the channel warns about.

> "On per ton EBITDA front, it is INR1,738 during FY '26 as against INR1,245 per ton in FY '25" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Demand is construction/operations-linked rather than tied to a single customer's capex, trade share is high (78-86%), and expansion is largely funded from internal accruals with net debt only INR200cr*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 2 of the last 11 years with swings of ±12 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 16% and 32% (±5.3 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 36% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 9% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** A large slice of profit is policy-granted: freight subsidy, income-tax exemption sunset, and SGST/GST incentives worth INR184cr in FY26 — and a single central decision (GST cut from 28% to 18%) mechanically cut that to a guided INR145-150cr, the exact 'small policy change' mechanism; the growth plan further depends on ECs, state MOUs, land allotment, Coal India FSA and railway rake allocation.

> "the run rate for the subsidy that we experienced in Q3 is the result of the reduction in GST from 28% to 18%, which of course then reduces the overall subsidy amount" — *management, on an earnings call*
