# Jyoti CNC Auto. (JYOTICNC) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Jyoti CNC Automation builds and sells CNC (computer numerical control) metal-cutting machine tools — the precision machines that factories use to cut and shape metal parts — from its plants in Rajkot, Gujarat and Strasbourg, France. It sells these machines directly to manufacturers: aerospace and defence firms (ISRO, BrahMos, MBDA, Tata Sikorsky, large European aerospace players), auto and auto-component makers (Bharat Forge, Bosch), general engineering shops, electronics manufacturing (EMS) companies, and die-and-mould makers. It makes money by taking machine orders into an order book and recognising revenue as machines are built and delivered, with build cycles running 3-6 months for entry-level machines and up to 12-18 months for large complex ones; FY26 consolidated revenue was about INR 599 crores in Q4 and the closing order book was INR 4,732 crores. It is vertically integrated (own foundry, machine shop, sheet metal, paint, assembly), sells mainly in India through roughly 29 sales and service centres, and exports via subsidiaries in France, Germany, Canada and Turkey.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Entry-level machines (roughly up to INR 50 lakh)** — Lower-priced CNC machines with a 3-6 month build cycle, supplied mostly to EMS, automobile/auto-component and general engineering customers.
- **Mid-level machines (roughly INR 50 lakh to 2 crores)** — Mid-priced machines with a build cycle of around 9 months; management describes Jyoti as present across the full value chain of entry, mid and high-end products.
- **High-end machines, including 5-Axis and multi-tasking** — Large, complex high-precision machines with 12-18 month build cycles, aimed mainly at aerospace and defence; the 5-Axis multi-tasking machine was developed with IIT Chennai under a Department of Heavy Industries scheme, and these are described as the company's most significant technology milestone.
- **Huron (France subsidiary, acquired 2007)** — A French machine-tool maker at Strasbourg (46,000 sq m, 120-machine capacity, doubled in November 2025) that supplies precision 5-Axis machines to global aerospace and defence customers and acts as the group's technology hub; an export-control investigation there caused a INR 67 crore revenue deferment in Q4 FY26.
- **Aerospace and defence end-market** — The largest single end-market — 39-45% of revenue across FY25 and FY26 and about 38-41% of the order book (roughly INR 1,900 crores) — served with high-precision machining solutions.
- **Auto and auto components end-market** — Consistently around 23-28% of revenue and about 20% of the order book, including EV-oriented products such as the VST 160 machine launched in 2022.
- **General engineering end-market** — Around 20-23% of revenue and about 19% of the order book, and one of the two segments management says picked up fastest in FY26 order intake (31% of FY26 intake).
- **EMS (electronics manufacturing services) end-market** — A newer market entered with the purpose-built 'Tachyon' product developed from 2015-2021; it was about 16% of the March 2025 order book but around 4% by March 2026.
- **Die and mould and other sectors** — A small residual slice, roughly 2-5% of revenue and order book.
- **R&D — Leonardo Da Vinci Centre and software products** — An in-house R&D centre set up in 2008 with 140+ engineers that has produced 200-plus product variants plus the '7th SENSE' Industry 4.0 platform, the 'PreciProtect' AI collision-prevention system and the 'Huma' control panel.
- **Electronics / mechatronics build-out (new)** — A stated push beyond mechanical into in-house servo motors, servo drives, sensors, PLCs and controllers, intended to cut import dependence and later support industrial automation, robotics and semiconductor equipment — described as future strategy rather than current revenue.
- **Manufacturing capacity and expansion** — Two vertically integrated Rajkot plants (253,000 sq m) running above 100% utilisation at peak, with installed capacity of 6,000 machines a year being expanded to 16,000 by around September 2026 — management calls this the main driver of the next growth phase.

### The story in depth

Jyoti CNC is, on the evidence here, a genuinely good manufacturing business wrapped around a weak balance sheet. The strongest checks all point the same way: a founder-led company running since 1989 that scored top marks for research (the Leonardo Da Vinci centre with 140+ engineers, 200+ product variants across 44 verticals, seven launches at IMTEX 2025, a patented HUMA interface), for people (a training centre that put 325 people through in one quarter, senior managers with 20+ year tenures), and for its selling reach (29 company-owned service centres across 12 states, machines installed in 60+ countries, subsidiaries in France, Germany, Canada and Turkey with a US operation being set up). Those same facts are what drive the pattern screen's only two positive verdicts: Innovation Dominance is a STRONG FIT precisely because the R&D is cadenced and traceable to named products, and Forward Integrators is a PARTIAL fit because the company sells and services direct rather than through dealers. The end market is helpful too, with India ranked sixth globally at $3.9bn of machine-tool consumption and expected to reach the top three by 2030, and the company still only third at home in a market largely served by imports — revenue compounded 36% in FY25 and 20% in FY26 while it turned orders away for lack of capacity.

The problem is that several of these strengths are the same fact as the risks. The 18-month waiting list gives real pricing power on new orders — prices were raised from 1 May after a 2-3% rise in raw material costs, and margins moved from 22.5% in FY24 to 27% in FY25 and 26.8-31.9% in FY26 — but management concedes it cannot pass cost inflation through on orders already booked, which is exactly why the Toll Roads pattern is a NO FIT and why the Good Enough Goods risk sits on WATCH for the entry-level 0-50 lakh machines where the company's own stated proposition is being the cheapest. The policy tailwinds counted as a strength (Make in India, defence self-reliance, PLI, ISM 2.0) are the same budgets behind the HIGH Government Dependency risk, with roughly 38-45% of revenue and order book in aerospace and defence, ordnance tenders, HAL, ISRO, Brahmos and a direct French government order — though intake share from that segment has fallen from 55% in FY24 to 27% in FY26 as auto and general engineering grew. The deep R&D record sits alongside an admitted dependence on Siemens and FANUC motion control, which is why Cost to Replicate and Low Price Plus land only at PARTIAL and why Technological Innovation is a WATCH; the plan to build controllers in-house, worth 10-25% of machine cost, is both the ambition and the acknowledgement of the gap. And the EMS vertical, 12-16% of the order book for two years, rests on a handful of unnamed mobile contract manufacturers plus Tata Electronics whose delays have already stalled that business — the live illustration of the HIGH Cyclicality risk and the reason FY26 order book growth slowed to about 5.5%, holding Market Share Gainers to PARTIAL.

What pulls the overall rating down to Mixed — 46 out of 100, from 61 on business quality, 33 on pattern fit and 36 on risk safety, with resilience judged STRAINED — is the money side. Two checks score at the floor: for every ₹100 of capital in the business, shareholders' funds plus borrowings, it generated only ₹2 of operating cash this year, and about 359 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. Long build cycles keep inventory and receivables high, which is also why Recurring Revenue is a NO FIT — there is no customer-funded, prepaid element to the model and operating cash flow went negative. Into that the company is committing a large debt-funded expansion: India capacity from 6,000 to 16,000 machines for INR400-450 crores, INR300 crores of it from Union Bank, plus a doubling of Huron France. With utilisation already above 100% the spending is clearly for growth rather than upkeep, and it scored positively for that, but it stacks capacity risk on top of a cash cycle that is already long and an end market whose demand is set by other companies' capex plans rather than their day-to-day operations. Management credibility is a genuine offset here — the roughly 25% margin guidance was met or beaten, the FY26 promise to turn cash flow positive was delivered, and the Huron export-control investigation was disclosed rather than buried — but Capital Allocation still scores just 0.25 and Return on Capital -0.67 against Competitive Advantage at 1.5. The story is a strong operator with a real moat in service reach and engineering, currently earning too little on the capital it employs to be more than a Mixed verdict.

> "Nothing has been invested much on the last component manufacturing in the last 1.5 years there" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Order book diversified across 12 industry segments (aero/defence 38%, auto 20%, general engineering 19%, EMS 4%) and 15,000+ customers; 18-20 month order backlog gives revenue visibility*
