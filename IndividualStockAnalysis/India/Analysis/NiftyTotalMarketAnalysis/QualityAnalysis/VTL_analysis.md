# Vardhman Textiles Ltd. (VTL) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: TEXTILES*

Vardhman Textiles is an Indian textile manufacturer, headquartered in Ludhiana, Punjab, that has been operating since 1965. It buys raw cotton and man-made fibres (polyester, acrylic, cellulosic) and converts them into yarn and woven fabric, which it sells mainly to garment exporters, home-textile makers and clothing brands — both inside India and as exports. Its money is made on the spread between raw-material cost (largely Indian cotton, priced off the local candy rate and the New York cotton futures benchmark) and the price it can get for yarn and fabric, so profitability swings with cotton prices, capacity utilisation and export demand. Its letterhead lists yarns, fabrics, threads, garments, fibres and steels as the group's product lines, and management on these calls discusses mainly the yarn (spinning) and fabric businesses, with plants including a fabric site at Budhni and a new performance-fabrics unit.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Yarn / spinning** — Spins cotton and blended yarn on a large spindle base — the calls describe it as the core business, running at about 95% capacity utilisation in Q3 FY26, sold both domestically and exported (India as a whole ships roughly 100–120 million kg of yarn a month), with a push toward contamination-free and value-added yarns.
- **Fabric (woven fabric, incl. processing)** — Weaves and processes woven fabric for garment makers and brands, running around 89–90% utilisation; processed capacity was expanded from about 145 lakh metres a month to 185 lakh metres, with grey capacity unchanged at roughly 2,300–2,400 lakh metres a year.
- **Vardhman Performance Fabrics (technical/performance wear)** — A newly commissioned unit making performance-wear/technical textiles, adding about 15 lakh metres a month (taking total processed capacity to around 200 lakh metres a month); it was servicing select domestic orders as of Q3 FY26 and is meant to scale from the following financial year.
- **Other group product lines (threads, garments, fibres, steels)** — Listed on company letterheads alongside yarns and fabrics as products of the group, but the calls give no detail on their size or performance.

### The story in depth

The clearest strength in this company is the people running it and the way they talk about it. Management scores highest of all the quality areas (+0.83), and the Candor check earns a full +2 because the team keeps volunteering unflattering, specific facts — halting its own spindles, admitting margin recovery may not come, putting a number on its own cost disadvantage, and openly reversing its earlier position on garmenting. The Corporate Culture pattern is the single STRONG FIT in the whole multibagger screen for the same reason: the same five names (Neeraj Jain, Jhamb, Thapar, Bansal, Malhotra) appear on every call from 2022 to 2026, bad news is delivered plainly, and capex is sized conservatively. This matters most because of what it is paired with — the Cyclicality risk is HIGH, with cotton candy prices swinging from INR105,000 in July 2022 down to INR52,000 and back to INR68,000 by May 2026 and EBITDA margins falling from about 22% before, yet the company pushed roughly INR2,500 crore of modernisation capex through the downturn while 11-12 million industry spindles shut for good. Honest, long-tenured management spending into a bad cycle is the strongest single fact in this record.

The trouble is that almost everything else traces back to one underlying trait: the product is a commodity, and that trait shows up as both the reason a few patterns work and the reason most of them fail. Yarn and fabric are priced off New York futures and international benchmarks, and management says so repeatedly — expensive Indian cotton 'has to be borne by us', and price increases downstream arrive only with a two- to three-month lag against customer resistance. That is why Pricing Power, Toll Roads and Cost to Replicate are all NO FIT, why Mini-monopolies, Network effects, Oligopoly and switching costs each score -2, and why the Good Enough Goods risk is ELEVATED, with mills fed by cheaper Vietnamese or Brazilian cotton holding a structural advantage of about $0.03-0.08 per pound. The company's answer is to be the low-cost, efficient producer — the Low Price Plus pattern is a PARTIAL fit on the back of internal cost programmes, the modernisation capex, biomass boilers and green power. But that defence is itself exposed: the Government Dependency risk is HIGH because an 8% MSP hike makes Indian cotton structurally costlier, the Cotton Corporation absorbs 75-80% of arrivals as a monopolistic stockist with no transparent sale policy, the 11% import duty was withdrawn then reinstated, and US tariffs sit on top. In short, the cost position that is supposed to be the moat is set partly in Delhi rather than in the mills.

The growth story is real but modest and mostly handed to the company rather than won by it. The Share donators check scores +2 and Gain in market share +1 because weak, old, high-cost mills are closing and China Plus One is pushing global brands toward large organised Indian vendors — but the framework is explicit that this is survivorship and industry structure, not brand pull, which is why Market Share Gainers, Brand Strength, Innovation Dominance and Forward Integrators all land at PARTIAL rather than strong. Management itself calls the garment business 'very, very miniscule' even after reversing course to double capacity on the EU trade agreement. Geographical expansion (+1) is likewise a shift in where the goods are sold — EU, UK, Australia, Canada, plus new Chinese yarn demand — rather than a premium product travelling abroad, and it doubles as the partial answer to the Stakeholder Concentration WATCH, where Bangladesh alone took 25-30% of India's yarn exports and China later swung monthly Indian exports by about 20 million kilos. Underneath all of it, the business is heavy: every rupee of assets produces only INR0.71 of sales, about 259 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, and roughly INR3,700 crore of inventory and INR2,500 crore of capex are carried against roughly 15% EBITDA margins. That combination is why the financial resilience reads STRAINED and why the blended rating comes out at 35 out of 100 — Mixed, two stars: good people and a genuine cost effort running a commodity balance sheet in a policy-driven cycle.

## What to watch

- Cotton policy — any change to MSP, the import duty (withdrawn then reinstated) or CCI's stockpile sale policy would move the cost position that the Government Dependency HIGH RISK and the Low Price Plus pattern both hinge on.
- The 259-day cash cycle and INR3,700 crore inventory — shortening these would lift the Cost of working capital check (-2) and ease the STRAINED resilience; lengthening them further makes the next downturn harder to fund.
- Whether closed capacity stays closed — the Share donators (+2) and Market Share Gainers case assumes the 11-12 million shut spindles do not come back; management's own point that competitors return when margins improve is the test.
- Realisations versus cotton — if VTL ever prices above the international benchmark instead of following it, the Pricing Power NO FIT verdict and the Good Enough Goods ELEVATED risk would both need rewriting.
- Value-added and downstream mix — performance fabrics, contamination-free yarns and the garment expansion moving from 'very, very miniscule' to a measurable share of sales would upgrade the Forward Integrators and Innovation Dominance PARTIAL verdicts.
- Management continuity and candour — the same five names on every call and the willingness to report bad news is the STRONG FIT holding the rating up; departures or a shift to vaguer commentary would remove the highest-scoring area (Management, +0.83).
