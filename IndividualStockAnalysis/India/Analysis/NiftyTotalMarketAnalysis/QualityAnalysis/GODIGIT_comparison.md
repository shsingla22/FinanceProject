### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 3 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 2 weakened, 7 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Share was gained through FY26 — motor OD share of 6.2% described as the highest since inception, growth beating industry in OD, TP, fire and commercial lines — but in Q1 FY27 motor share was deliberately given back to 5.6%, so gains are not persistent; notably the retreat is the good kind (declining underpriced government health and CV business), not share bought with underwriting quality. (The long view had said: Share gain was real and repeated through FY25-FY26 (22% GWP growth vs industry 13% in Q1FY25; FY26 GDPI +16.2% vs 13%, fire +34% vs 13%), but it is not persistent — by Q1 FY27 motor share fell from 6.25% to 5.6% and GWP fell 8% as they deliberately exited underpriced CV, health and crop business. Consistent with the pattern's caveat that share bought with underwriting quality is worthless.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Nothing in these calls articulates why a funded newcomer could not catch up — Digit is itself an ~8-year-old entrant that reached 3.4% overall share, and management describes an industry where competitors are freely underpricing TP and commissions are being bid up. The stated edge is underwriting discipline and accounting conservatism, which is a management choice, not a replication barrier. (The long view had said: Some articulable barriers are named — eight years of reinsurer relationships that produced better treaty capacity and commissions in a soft market, plus the pricing flexibility that came with it ('very few companies other than Digit had the flexibility to charge a premium rate based on what they think is right') — but these are relationship and capital advantages a well-funded entrant could rebuild, as Digit itself did from 2016.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (LIKELY FIT); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Motor insurance is a low-differentiation product where the intermediary largely controls the choice and is paid by commission — management notes commissions have been pushed up by EOM rules while premium rates drifted down, and Digit's response is to exit business rather than defend a price premium, which is exactly the price-competition squeeze the channel enables. It is bounded because service/claims quality (consistently strong NPS) and scale in niches like two-wheeler still earn some preference. (The long view had said: An insurance policy is a commoditised promise sold largely through intermediaries whose commission — not the end customer's brand awareness — drives placement, exactly the Nobel Biocare mechanism. Across the calls Digit repeatedly walks away rather than defend price: TP in FY25, group health and crop reinsurance inward in FY26/27, standalone OD and non-new private car where 'the combination of commission and the premium rates do not justify writing business', with motor market share falling from 6.25% to 5.6%. Competitors' cheaper cover is proving good enough for the channel.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The book is granular retail — 8.1 crore customers since inception and close to 80,000 distribution partners — and no single customer, licensor, distributor or reinsurer is cited as driving results in any of the four calls; management demonstrated it can drop a INR254 crore relationship (government health) without material consequence. (The long view had said: Customer and distribution bases are very broad (7.1 crore customers, 66,000+ partners), so no customer concentration. The real dependency is upstream: reinsurers carry ~80% of fire risk and treaty capacity/commission terms determine what Digit can write in commercial lines, with the same treaty leader retained year after year — management devotes substantial airtime to protecting gross loss ratios so reinsurers 'do not put some restrictions on the treaty'.)

**That worsened:**

- **New Entrants**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Competitors are pricing aggressively into a segment where the regulated price has not moved for five years, and expense-of-management rules have let rivals bid commissions up; Digit responded by conceding volume rather than matching, giving up private-car standalone OD, non-new car and commercial-vehicle business and losing ~65bps of motor market share in Q1 FY27. This is intensified price competition from existing peers rather than a structurally new format entering, which caps the severity. (The long view had said: The calls surface no structurally different entrant resetting economics — the pricing aggression described is from established insurers (private players in group health, incumbents in TP), not a new format. The one D2C-disruption question was met with a reasoned argument on why direct-only economics are hard (GEICO under 15% US motor share after decades), and Digit is itself the digital-native entrant of this cycle.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Cyclicality (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 2 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 2 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 9 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
