### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 6. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Share gain is explicit and repeated every quarter — Ajanta outgrew the IPM in all four calls (10% vs 8%, 10% vs 8%, 11% vs 9%, 13% vs 10%), improved its IPM rank from 26 to 24, and gained share in existing US products — and it is volume-led rather than bought with price. (The long view had said: Share gain is quantified and persistent over the whole four-year window: IPM rank 29 to 28 (2022) to 24 (MAT March 2026), consistently beating IPM growth (13% vs 10%) and leading on volume and new introductions, plus explicit market-share gains in Africa and in existing US products — and it is gained alongside 33-34% ROCE, not bought with margin.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Gross margin is stable-high at 78-79% across all four quarters, but no price increase is ever cited as a growth driver — India growth is explicitly volume- and new-launch-led, and the one margin improvement discussed was attributed to mix and input costs, not price. (The long view had said: India shows genuine pricing contribution (7 of 22 points of Q1FY23 growth from price increases, with volume still growing) and the domestic ranking keeps improving, but the US business ran through 15-18% price erosion and by FY26 the company says freight and RM-PM inflation is absorbed in its own P&L rather than passed on — so pricing power is real domestically, absent elsewhere.)

**That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The four calls cover branded generic and US generic product sales only; there is no mention of licence fees, AMC/service contracts, subscriptions, attach rates, renewals or deferred revenue as a funding source. (The long view had said: Across all five calls management discusses only product sales by geography — no licence fees, AMC/service contracts, subscriptions, attach rates, renewals or deferred revenue as a funding source.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Friendly Middleman (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: US generics rose to 29% of FY26 revenue and is sold through a small set of distributors that management repeatedly describes in relationship terms, while the institutional vertical depends on a handful of procurement agencies; against that, no single customer is named as driving results and revenue is spread across four verticals (India 30%, Asia+Africa branded 38%, US 29%, institution 3%). (The long view had said: The fastest-growing segment (US, 29% of FY26 revenue, +49%) sells through a structurally concentrated distributor/consortium channel that management repeatedly credits for results, and the entire emerging-market GLP-1 opportunity rests on a single supplier, Biocon, for finished product across 26 countries; no single customer is named as a results driver and the revenue base is otherwise spread across four verticals and many countries.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The business is structurally in the good-enough arena — US generics (29% of revenue) competes on price and supply reliability rather than differentiation, and the India book includes a Rs. 188 crore trade generics segment where the prescriber, not the patient, controls the choice; margins have held (gross 78–79%, EBITDA 27–28% ex-forex) but FY27 gross margin guidance was trimmed to 77% ±1 from 78% ±1, and EBITDA growth of 12% in Q4 lagged 21% revenue growth. (The long view had said: This is the structural risk in the business model: US generics are the archetypal good-enough good and management disclosed 15-18% price erosion hitting US EBITDA in FY22-23, while in India the doctor — not the patient — controls the choice and Ajanta also runs a price-led trade generics line (Rs 188cr FY26); the defence is product selection and brand-building rather than any inherent barrier, and rising RM/freight costs are absorbed rather than passed on.)
- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No disruption threat is raised by management or analysts in any of the four calls; instead the record is one of steady small-scale innovation — R&D held at ~5% of revenue (Rs. 252 crore in FY26) funding 26 new products in India, 15 in Asia, 8 in Africa and 8 US launches over 15 months, which is incremental value-adding cadence rather than platform risk. (The long view had said: The GLP-1 platform shift bears directly on Ajanta's largest therapy areas (cardiology 36%, plus metabolic), and management has had to buy its way in via a Biocon supply tie-up for 26 countries rather than develop it; otherwise the innovation cadence is small-scale and additive (new launches, new therapies) with no discussion of installed base becoming legacy.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (QUANT FLAG); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 7 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
