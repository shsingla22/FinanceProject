### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 5 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 6. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 6 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Share gain is quantified in every one of the four calls — IQVIA growth of 10%/10%/11%/13% against IPM's 8%/8%/9%/10%, IPM rank improving from 26 to 24, plus 'gain in market share in few existing products' in the US — and it is won on volume and new launches, not on discounting, with 78% gross margins and 33% ROCE intact. (The long view had said: Share gain is quantified and persistent over the whole four-year window: IPM rank moved 29 → 28 (Jun 2022) → 24 (Mar 2026), fifth largest in covered market, growth consistently above IPM (13% vs 10% MAT Mar 2026, 2x IPM volume growth), plus explicit share gains in existing US products — and it is not bought with profitability, since ROCE stayed at 33-34% and EBITDA margin at ~27% throughout.)
- **Pricing Power**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Gross margin is stable-high at 78-79% through all four quarters and improved 200bps in Q1 on 'better product mix and favorable API prices', but no price increase is ever discussed and FY27 guidance is a notch lower at 77% ±1%, with growth attributed to volume and new launches rather than price. (The long view had said: India shows genuine pricing power — 7% of FY23 growth came from price increases alongside 6% volume growth, and gross margins have risen and held at 78-79% — but the US generics business has suffered 15-18% price erosion and in May 2026 management said freight and RM/PM inflation is 'absorbed by us in our P&L' rather than passed on, so pricing power is segment-specific, not company-wide.)

**That weakened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The four calls discuss product launches, field force and market growth only; there is no mention of licences, AMC/service contracts, subscriptions, attach rates, renewals or customer advances funding the business. (The long view had said: the calls are silent and the numbers are mixed (1 of 3 checks supporting).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Toll Roads (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Friendly Middleman (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Brand Strength (LIKELY FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 3 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: US generics rose from 24% to 29% of revenue during FY26 and is sold through a structurally concentrated US distributor/buyer channel that management repeatedly credits by name-category ('preferred partner of choice for our buyers'), while the institution vertical depends on a handful of procurement agencies; growth is also concentrated in 8 recent launches rather than a broad base. No single customer is named or questioned on renewal, and the India business rests on ~3,750 MRs and a five-therapy portfolio, which bounds the risk. (The long view had said: The US vertical — now 29% of revenue and the sole engine of FY26 growth (+49%) — sells through a concentrated US distributor/buying-group channel, which management itself frames as a relationship business ('preferred partner of choice for the distributors and customers'); the GLP-1 emerging-market opportunity rests on a single supplier, Biocon. Against this, India (3,750 MRs, top-25 IPM rank) and Asia/Africa branded businesses are structurally fragmented across doctors and chemists.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The economics rest on prescription intermediaries rather than end-patient brand awareness, and the company competes in segments where cheaper substitutes are structurally available — it runs its own trade generics line (Rs 188 crores FY26) alongside branded, and US generics is an inherently price-eroding business; management has now guided gross margin down to ~77% (+/-1%) for FY27 from 78% delivered. Exposure is bounded rather than high because margins have so far been defended by mix and new launches, not by discounting, and no call shows management defending a price premium under attack. (The long view had said: US generics (29% of revenue) is the archetypal good-enough good — undifferentiated molecules where the distributor, not the patient, chooses and price erosion is permanent, requiring a launch treadmill of 8 new products in 15 months just to grow. In India and Asia/Africa the intermediary (prescriber/chemist) similarly controls choice and trade generics is an explicitly unbranded Rs. 188 crore line. That said, gross margin has held at 77-79% and EBITDA at ~27% through FY26, and management explicitly refuses to chase margin expansion via price, investing in brand and field force instead.)
- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: No call in the window contains any discussion of platform shifts, disruptive formats, or analyst questions on AI/technology risk; instead R&D is held steady at ~5% of revenue (Rs 252 crores FY26) and converted into a continuous cadence of incremental launches — 26 new products in India, 15 in Asia, 8 in Africa, 8 US launches over 15 months. That is small-scale value-adding innovation, not a business built on a format that could be obsoleted. (The long view had said: GLP-1s are a genuine large-scale shift in cardio-metabolic treatment and Ajanta has no originator technology of its own — it accessed the molecule by in-licensing finished product from Biocon for 26 countries and is filing generic semaglutide from Q1 FY27, i.e. participating as a follower. Offsetting this is a steady small-scale innovation cadence (26 new products in India FY26, 8 US launches in 15 months, R&D at 5% of sales).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (ELEVATED); Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 8 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
