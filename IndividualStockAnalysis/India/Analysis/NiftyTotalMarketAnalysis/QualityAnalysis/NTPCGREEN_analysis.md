### The story in depth

NTPC Green Energy comes out of the three reviews as a Decent business, scoring 56 out of 100 — built from 50 on business quality, 34 on multibagger fit and 92 on risk safety. The single fact that explains most of the picture is that this is an asset-heavy operation: every ₹1 of assets produces only ₹0.05 of sales. In exchange for that heavy asset base, the company earns ₹87 of operating profit on every ₹100 of sales, and those margins have barely moved — about ±1.5 points a year. The quality framework reads that steadiness as a sign that costs are under control and that the industry itself is well behaved, scoring Industry Structure at +2 with the note that flat margins over the years are consistent with rational pricing and few new entrants.

The strengths and the weaknesses here are two sides of the same coin rather than separate findings. The heavy asset base and the fat, stable margin are exactly what makes the Toll Roads pattern a partial fit — a business that builds something once and collects steady income from it. But that same structure is what drags the numbers down elsewhere: asset turn scores -2 for being asset-heavy, and headline return on capital scores -1 because ₹100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings threw off only ₹5 of operating cash this year, a 5% cash return. Growth tells the same double-edged story. Sales have grown around 156% a year on average, but the pattern is lumpy from one year to the next, and the framework penalises that at -2 for lack of persistence rather than rewarding the headline rate. On top of that, roughly 78 days of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, which ties up more capital in a business that already needs a lot of it. Return on Capital as an area comes out slightly negative at -0.33 and Capital Allocation at a neutral 0.

The risk review is where the record is most reassuring and most incomplete at the same time. All eight channels came back with nothing listed, which is what produced the 92 risk-safety score and lifted the overall rating — a quarter of the final number rests on an absence of flagged problems. Yet the same review labels the company's resilience STRAINED, which sits awkwardly next to a near-perfect safety score and lines up better with the 5% cash return and the long working capital cycle than with a clean bill of health. The multibagger screen is similarly thin: only 34 out of 100, with three of the five patterns — Friendly Middleman, Brand Strength and Forward Integrators — resting on quantitative signals alone because no management conference-call judgement was available, Toll Roads only a partial fit, and Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) no fit at all. Several quality areas — Competitive Advantage, Customer Benefits and Management — have no score at all. So the honest reading is a steady, well-priced, capital-hungry business whose durability is asserted more by the stability of its margins than by evidence anyone has actually examined.
