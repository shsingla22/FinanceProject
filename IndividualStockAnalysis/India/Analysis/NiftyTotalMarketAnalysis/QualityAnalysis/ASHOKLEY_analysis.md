# Ashok Leyland Ltd. (ASHOKLEY) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: AUTOMOBILE*

Ashok Leyland is an Indian commercial-vehicle maker: it builds and sells trucks and buses — from small 2-4 tonne pickups and mini-trucks up to heavy haulage trucks, tippers, tractor-trailers and city/intercity buses — plus the diesel engines, spare parts and defence vehicles that go with them. Its customers are fleet owners, individual truck operators, state transport bodies and bus fleets, industrial engine buyers, government defence buyers and overseas distributors. It makes money mainly by manufacturing and selling vehicles through a dealer and service network (2,104 touch points at end-FY26) and by selling higher-margin spare parts, service and engines afterwards; the calls stress premium products, cost savings and better price realisation as the profit levers. It sells mostly in India, with export 'home markets' in the Gulf (GCC), Africa and SAARC and a push into ASEAN and Europe; FY26 revenue was about INR 44,007 crores with a 13% EBITDA margin and INR 3,914 crores of profit before exceptional items.

**The parts of the business:**

- **MHCV (medium & heavy commercial vehicles) — trucks** — The core business: heavy trucks, tippers, tractor-trailers and multi-axle vehicles (AVTR, HIPPO, TAURUS ranges), selling 105,905 units in FY26 at roughly a 30% domestic market share, with a stated medium-term goal of 35%.
- **MHCV — buses** — Medium and heavy buses for state transport and private operators, where the company says it is the market leader with a 34.1% share and 20,840 units in FY26, and is expanding bus body-building capacity toward 20,000 units a year.
- **LCV (light commercial vehicles)** — Smaller goods carriers like Dost, Bada Dost, Saathi and the export-focused Phoenix in the 2-4 tonne space; FY26 volume was a record 74,322 units at a 12.7% VAHAN market share, and management calls it a key growth and market-share-gain area.
- **Exports / international operations (IO)** — Sales of India-built and export-specific vehicles through distributors in the GCC, Africa and SAARC — its three 'home markets' outside India — reaching a record 18,082 units in FY26, up 18.5%, with ASEAN being built as a fourth home market.
- **Aftermarket / spare parts and service** — Selling spare parts and servicing vehicles through the dealer network; a steady non-vehicle revenue stream that grew about 9.5% in FY26 and is one of the 'non-CV' businesses management wants to grow substantially.
- **Power Solutions (engines)** — Sells diesel and other engines for non-vehicle uses such as gensets and industrial applications; revenue grew 16.4% in FY26 and 45% year-on-year in Q3 FY26.
- **Defence** — Supplies vehicles to defence customers, including through subsidiary ALDS; revenue rose about 20% in FY26 (84% in Q3), with management repeatedly describing the order book and tender pipeline as strong.
- **Switch Mobility (EV subsidiary)** — Makes electric buses and electric light commercial vehicles in India (and the E1 bus for Europe); it delivered 1,530 e-buses and 1,600 e-LCVs in FY26, claims leadership in both segments, turned net profitable, and had a 1,600-unit order book.
- **OHM Mobility (e-MaaS subsidiary)** — Operates electric buses as a service for city and GCC transport contracts rather than selling them outright, running a fleet of over 1,400 e-buses at healthy double-digit project returns.
- **Hinduja Leyland Finance and Hinduja Housing Finance** — Lending subsidiaries — HLF finances vehicle buyers (AUM about INR 59,000 crores, PAT INR 491 crores in FY26) and HHF does housing loans (AUM about INR 16,000 crores) — which both add profit and help customers buy Ashok Leyland's trucks; HLF is being reverse-merged into listed NDL Ventures to unlock value.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

## Section 2 — Does it look like a long-term winner? (11 multibagger patterns)

**Overall:** the company partly passes the foundation every multibagger shares (steady cash + high returns on capital + growth), 1 of 3 testable checks passing, and fits 1 of the 11 patterns: Corporate Culture.

### Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls confirm the pattern with clear, repeated evidence (no computable check applies — the calls decide).

**What the calls show:** Promises made on early calls were subsequently delivered: double-digit then mid-teen EBITDA guided in May 2023 → 13% for FY26 ('truly entered the teen bracket'); ~15,000 export target guided in Feb 2025 → 15,255 achieved; net debt of Rs.720 crore in FY22 → net cash Rs.5,899 crore by FY26; Switch guided to profitability and reached it. Bad news is disclosed plainly (labour-code charge, UK bus uncertainty, RAK production drop, gross margin compression).

> "With full year EBITDA margin at 13%, we have now truly entered the teen bracket" — *management, on an earnings call*

### Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) — PARTIAL
*"Sells once, earns forever"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (1 of 3 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Aftermarket/spare parts and Power Solutions are repeatedly called out as growing, higher-margin annuity streams off the installed base (spares +14% YoY in Q3FY25, aftermarket +9.5% for FY26), and the company runs negative operating working capital — but no attach rates, AMC share, renewal rates or deferred revenue-as-funding discussion ever appears.

> "Domestic aftermarket revenue for the quarter was up 11.2% Y-o-Y and higher 9.5% for the full year" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Customers effectively fund the business — cash arrives 26 days before suppliers are paid.
- ❌ Capital spending is about 6% of sales — growth is capital-hungry.
- ❌ Operating cash was positive in 6 of 12 years — not fully consistent.

### Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 1 check supporting).

**What the calls show:** There is a genuine, itemised and sustained cost culture — ~Rs.650 crore of cost reduction per year, admin overheads held at FY2016-17 levels, value engineering and e-sourcing offsetting commodity inflation — but it is deployed toward margin expansion and premiumisation, not toward passing savings to price.

> "we ended up with a good Rs. 650 plus crores of cost reduction each year" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹30 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 0.5× a year — not the high-turnover, low-margin machine this pattern needs.

### Pricing Power — PARTIAL
*"Raises prices, keeps the customers"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 2 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Premiumisation is a stated strategy and FY26 gross margin was defended despite commodity headwinds, but pricing is modest and contested — only ~1%-1.5% taken in FY26, none between October and March, and recovery attempted via discount reduction rather than list price.

> "there was a general notion that since GST has really led to strong growth... This may not be the right time to increase the prices" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹30 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — limited pricing headroom.
- ❌ Operating margin moved from about 12% (early years) to 10% (recent years) — margins are ERODING, the opposite of pricing power.

### Brand Strength — PARTIAL
*"The name itself sells"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show some of the traits, with mixed numbers (0 of 2 checks supporting).

**What the calls show:** Strong franchise in MHCV buses (34-38% share, sustained leadership) and a genuine brand position in export home markets built over decades, but no heritage/A&P narrative, no premium pricing evidence, and brand is not the stated growth lever.

> "Ashok Le yland is like a formidable brand in the CV business" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹30 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — limited pricing headroom.
- ❌ Operating margin of 6% — too thin for a protected franchise.

### Innovation Dominance — PARTIAL
*"Out-inventing the competition"*

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Government Dependency, Cyclicality, Technological Innovation, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows multiple financial stress signals — 2 of the 3 stress checks are flagged — multiple warning lights at once.

- ⚠️ Borrowings are now 4.5× shareholders' funds (up from 2.0×) — a leveraged balance sheet leaves little room when any of the risks above bites.

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Demand is repeatedly attributed to policy levers rather than autonomous demand — government infrastructure capex, the scrappage policy, JNNURM bus replacement, the Union Budget, and above all GST 2.0, which management calls the 'trigger' for the entire FY26 up-cycle; STU bus tenders, a fast-growing defence business (order book/tender wins, an MOU with state-owned PT Pindad), EV subsidy-dependent e-bus economics and diesel-price/duty moves compound this.

> "The GST reset provided the much-needed trigger for fresh CV replacement cycle to kick in" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Partial diversification away from policy-driven revenue: retail buyers are 55-60% of heavy-truck demand, exports across GCC/Africa/SAARC/ASEAN growing 18-30%, and a growing aftermarket/Power Solutions base*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Trucks are a capex-linked capital good and the calls are dominated by cycle talk across all three years — analysts open with 'the CV cycle health', FY25 Q2 saw a 12% YoY industry decline attributed to a slowdown in government capex and erratic rains, and FY26 volumes swung to +21-24% on a GST-driven replacement trigger; margins have oscillated violently (4.4% EBITDA in Q1 FY23 to 14.6% in Q4 FY26) with commodity prices (steel, PGM, copper, aluminium) repeatedly moving gross margin 50-100bps a quarter.

> "everyone's worried about the cyclicality of the business" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Growing non-CV flow businesses tied to customer operations rather than capex — aftermarket/spare parts (+9.5% FY26), Power Solutions (+16.4%), defence (+20%) — plus exports at a record 18,082 units and a net-cash balance sheet (INR5,899 crores) that funds investment through the cycle*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 3 of the last 11 years with swings of ±26 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.

- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 2% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 6%).
