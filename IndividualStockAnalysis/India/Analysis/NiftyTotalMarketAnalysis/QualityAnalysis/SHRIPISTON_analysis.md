# SPR Auto Technologie (SHRIPISTON) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

SPR Auto Technologies (formerly Shriram Pistons & Rings, NSE: SHRIPISTON) is an Indian auto-components manufacturer that has made engine parts since the 1960s — pistons, piston pins, piston rings and engine valves — sold under its SPR and Usha brands. It makes money by supplying these parts directly to vehicle makers (Tata, Maruti Suzuki, Mahindra, Bajaj, Hero, Honda, TVS, Ashok Leyland, John Deere, Cummins and others) and by selling replacement parts into the aftermarket, with the same parts also going to non-automotive uses like gensets, railways, defence, compressors and lawn mowers. About 81% of standalone revenue came from India and about 19% from exports to 45+ countries (FY23), and it builds product in plants in Uttar Pradesh (Ghaziabad, Bulandshahr), Rajasthan (Pathredi) and Madhya Pradesh (Pithampur), plus assembly centres near customer factories. Since 2023 it has bought its way into several non-engine businesses — EV motors, precision plastic moulding, and car interiors and lighting — so that by FY26 consolidated income reached INR 4,571 crores (up 25%) with EBITDA of INR 989 crores, and roughly 35% of revenue now comes from products that do not depend on the type of engine.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Legacy business (pistons, piston pins, piston rings, engine valves)** — The original and still-core operation making internal-combustion engine parts for Indian and global vehicle makers plus the aftermarket, backed by decades-old technology tie-ups with Kolbenschmidt, Honda Foundry, Riken and Fuji of Japan; it is the bulk of the company, with standalone total income of about INR 7,956 million in Q3 FY25 against consolidated INR 8,751 million, and it continues to get capacity expansion and the purchase of piston lines from Sunbeam for INR 280 million.
- **Antolin India (automotive interiors and lighting solutions)** — Three Indian entities of Spain's Grupo Antolin bought outright in January 2026 for EUR 159 million (about INR 16,700 million), making headliners, sunvisors, door panels and trims, floor consoles, dome and ambient lighting and touch panels for Tata Motors, Mahindra, Volkswagen, Toyota, Hyundai and Renault — described by management as the largest and most significant diversification step so far.
- **SPR TGPEL / SPR Takahata (precision injection moulded parts and moulds)** — Two acquired businesses making high-precision plastic injection moulds and moulded components for automotive, electrical, consumer goods and medical customers such as Continental, Motherson, Denso, Havells and Schaeffler, run from plants in Noida with a new Takahata facility being set up at Neemrana; management calls them complementary to each other and a key part of the powertrain-agnostic mix.
- **EMF Innovations (EMFi) — EV motors and controllers** — A 66% stake bought through subsidiary SPR Engenious in a young electric-vehicle technology company that designs and builds electric motors and controllers from 250 watts to 300 kilowatts for two- and three-wheelers, cars, buses and trucks; a new world-class plant at Coimbatore was inaugurated in November 2025 and is now producing, marking the company's entry into EV components.
- **SPR Engenious Limited (SEL)** — The wholly owned subsidiary that houses the Pithampur (Madhya Pradesh) plant and acts as the holding vehicle through which the diversification acquisitions such as EMFi were made.
- **Karna Intertech (tooling)** — A small acquisition made at the start of FY26 to strengthen in-house tool manufacturing, described as supporting the growth programmes of the other group businesses rather than as a standalone revenue driver.
- **Technology centre and exports** — A DSIR-recognised product development and engine-testing centre set up in 2014 that develops solutions for green hydrogen, flex fuel and natural gas engines, underpinning exports to over 45 countries to customers including Volkswagen, JLR, BMW, Caterpillar, Perkins, Kubota and Cummins — a stream that faced pressure in FY25 from weak European demand, freight and energy costs.

### The story in depth

SPR Auto Technologie comes out of all three reviews as a genuinely good business that has been run well for a long time, and the combined rating of 69 out of 100 (Strong, four stars) reflects that: 68 for business quality, 79 for how well it fits the multibagger patterns, and 60 for risk safety, with overall resilience judged Sound. The quality framework's best marks are for what the company does for its customers and for the durability of its position — customers are buying certainty on engine-critical parts, backed by decades-long supply agreements with vehicle makers, assembly units placed next to customer plants for just-in-time delivery, quality certifications, and more than 1,200 aftermarket service points from Assam to Kerala. Management scores well too, because it said what it would do over several years and then did it: operating margin was lifted from 12.4% in FY20 to roughly 22-23%, the five-year programme to exit loss-making lines was completed, and the Coimbatore electric-motor plant was inaugurated exactly as promised. Roughly 200 crore rupees of FY26 spending went into new capacity rather than mere upkeep, while returns on capital stayed high. The two strongest pattern fits follow directly from this: Market Share Gainers, because the company has grown faster than every end market quarter after quarter while margins rose rather than fell, and Corporate Culture, resting on long-tenured family and professional leadership, a conservative balance sheet, and uninterrupted dividends.

The interesting part is that the single decision behind much of the margin story also sits at the centre of the risk review. Margins improved because the company pruned its mix, raised productivity and let volume growth carry fixed costs — not because it raised prices. That is precisely why two patterns are marked No Fit: Pricing Power, since pricing is a formula-based pass-through of raw-material costs with about a quarter's lag, and Low Price Plus, since the company deliberately walks away from price-competitive business instead of winning on cheapness at scale. The same admission — that it exited product lines where cheaper rivals set the price — is the main evidence behind the Elevated 'Good Enough Goods' risk, sharpened by the fact that in the replacement market it is usually the mechanic, not the vehicle owner, who chooses which piston or ring set goes in. That mechanic relationship cuts both ways again: it is what makes the Friendly Middleman pattern a Likely Fit and gives the Recurring Revenue pattern its Partial standing, because fitment on new vehicles today creates replacement demand from a very large existing vehicle fleet tomorrow. Similarly, the barriers that support Toll Roads and Cost to Replicate — small-cost-but-critical parts, long customer approval cycles, and technology tie-ups dating back to 1972, 1978, 1989 and 1993 — are the same four foreign licensor relationships that produce the Stakeholder Concentration watch flag, even though the customer base itself is unusually broad across segments, 45-plus export countries and four subsidiary businesses.

The largest question is what the core franchise is attached to. Pistons, pins, rings and valves exist because engines exist, so the installed base that gives the business its annuity-like replacement stream is the same installed base that electrification erodes — which is why Technological Innovation is rated Elevated and Shifting Consumer Preferences is on watch. The company's answer is visible in the accounts rather than only in the commentary: electric motors and controllers with localisation approvals, precision plastics, interiors and lighting, plus hydrogen, flex-fuel and CNG piston work in advanced trials and hybrid engine programmes slated for 2029-30. That pivot is real, but it carries its own exposures — the electric-motor arena is populated by newer, structurally different competitors rather than the company's traditional peers, and whether its customers there qualify for subsidy depends on approvals and policy schemes, which is what keeps New Entrants and Government Dependency on watch. Two further cautions temper the headline numbers. The quality framework marks the company down for entering this period near a cyclical high rather than a low, with record industry volumes helped by tax changes, rate cuts and the strongest festive season, and the risk review flags the same cyclicality; and the framework's own weakest area scores are growth and return on capital, with sales growth averaging about 12% a year but arriving unevenly. Fashion risk is the one channel rated Low, since demand here is functional and replacement-driven rather than trend-led.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Technological Innovation, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.7× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ✅ About 69% of recent operating profit became cash — earnings are still backed by cash.
- ✅ Returns on capital improved from about 16% to 25% — the business is earning more on its capital, not less.

### Technological Innovation — ELEVATED
*"Out-invented by the industry"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 2 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** The core franchise — pistons, pins, rings, valves — is the ICE installed base, and electrification is exactly the large-scale platform shift that makes incumbent infrastructure legacy; management defends it on essentially every call ("all powertrain technologies will coexist", arithmetic that more ICE engines will be built in 2030 than today) and analysts repeatedly probe EV penetration and mix. The response has been to buy its way powertrain-agnostic, but ~65% of revenue is still powertrain-exposed.

> "we expect all the powertrain technologies to coexist... there will be more IC engines made in 2030 as compared to what it's made today" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Deliberate diversification — EMFI motors/controllers, Takahata and TGPEL precision plastics, Antolin interiors and lighting — taking powertrain-agnostic revenue to ~35% and "nearly 60% of our business... not directly impacted by powertrain"; plus incremental innovation in hydrogen, CNG/HCNG, flex-fuel and hybrid engine programmes for 2029-30*

- ✅ Growth is holding up: 4% a year earlier, 23% recently — no structural stall.
- ✅ Operating margin (20%) remains near its historical peak (21%) — no collapse in the premium.

### Good Enough Goods — ELEVATED
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 1 numeric fingerprint reinforces it.

**What the calls show:** Management explicitly exited product lines where cheaper competitors set the price — the good-enough dynamic operating on a mechanical part — and in the aftermarket the mechanic, not the vehicle owner, usually picks the piston or ring set, which is the Nobel Biocare intermediary problem. The counterweight is genuine technical content (ring coatings for Euro 6 and fuel efficiency, hydrogen and hybrid development) and licensor-backed OEM qualification.

> "for areas where we were getting priced out because of our competition being on a much lower price targets, we decided to actually exit those businesses" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Niche, spec-in engineering protection — OEM approval and coating/technology content rather than a commodity screw; SPR and Usha brand recognition with 1,200+ owned aftermarket touch points; margin discipline held at ~22-23% EBITDA through the pruning*

- ⚠️ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs fell from about 69% to 57% — pricing is being competed away at the gross level.
- ✅ Operating margin (20%) remains near its historical peak (21%) — no collapse in the premium.
- ✅ Operating margin held (about 16% then 20%) — no drift down under competition.

### Cyclicality — WATCH

## What to watch

- Whether margins hold once the cost-pruning programme is finished — the 'Independent, long-term and tenacious' check and the No Fit on Pricing Power together mean there is no price lever to fall back on.
- How much more business is given up to cheaper competitors — further line exits would push the Elevated 'Good Enough Goods' risk from a mix decision into genuine share loss.
- Growth rates once the current volume surge fades — the negative 'Cyclical market growth' check and the Cyclicality risk both say today's record industry numbers flatter the trend.
- Progress in the new businesses — electric motors and controllers, plastics, interiors and lighting — since they are the stated answer to the Elevated Technological Innovation risk and the Shifting Consumer Preferences watch, while facing New Entrants and subsidy-approval dependency.
- The four decades-old foreign technology tie-ups behind the core products — they underpin the Partial Cost to Replicate rating and are the reason Stakeholder Concentration is on watch despite a very broad customer base.
