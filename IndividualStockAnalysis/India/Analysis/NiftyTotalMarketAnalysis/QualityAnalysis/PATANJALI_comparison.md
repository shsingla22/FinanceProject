### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 29 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 11 improved, 4 regressed, 14 unchanged. 3 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them).

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Weak → Excellent. Now: The oil palm business rests on government-allocated exclusive cultivation zones totalling 6.63 lakh hectares with captive farmer sourcing — a genuine regional monopoly that earns ~21.6% EBITDA versus 2.58% in refining. | The long view had said: Switching costs are essentially nil across edible oil, biscuits and toothpaste, where consumers move on price and grammage between rival brands.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Weak → Good. Now: Focus in the window is organic — plantation build-out, distribution depth, hedging and cost optimization — with no new large M&A announced, though leverage is never discussed. | The long view had said: Growth has leaned on three sizeable related-party asset injections from the promoter — the latest at Rs.2,800 crores — rather than organic build, which is the opposite of the 'no big M&A, organic focus' test.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Poor → Neutral. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹4 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±1.1 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹4 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±12.9 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Poor → Neutral. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.1 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Barriers in the core business are low and pricing is not rational enough to hold economics — management itself caps expectations at a 2-4% EBITDA band and consumers readily switch oil types on price. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±12.9 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Barriers are low and pricing behaviour is not rational — GST pass-through triggered grammage and promotional battles, and management concedes high promotional intensity in its key HPC category.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Neutral. Now: A steady stream of launches (Date Almond Spread, Gond Katira, Yellow Mustard Oil, Slim Choice and Sea Buckthorn capsules, Apple drink) but no R&D spend disclosed and the innovation-heavy nutraceutical category shrank to just Rs.58 crores in FY26 after rationalization. | The long view had said: Innovation is claimed and launches are frequent (Gonyle, Dant Kanti Fresh, Sea Buckthorn capsules), but no R&D spend is ever disclosed and the flagship nutraceuticals franchise collapsed from ~Rs.400 crores of quarterly revenue in Q2FY23 to Rs.58 crores for all of FY26 — poor long-run R&D efficiency.
- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: Patanjali is a mass-market national brand with celebrity endorsement and heavy media presence — the opposite of an ignored niche. | The long view had said: The company is the opposite of under-the-radar — a heavily covered listed FMCG name with celebrity endorsement, national media presence and openly stated ambitions to dethrone Unilever.
- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Good. Now: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹2.14 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: Turnover is high (Rs.40,169 crores revenue) but two-thirds of it comes from asset-heavy, commodity edible oil crushing/refining explicitly guided to a 2-4% EBITDA band, so the asset turn is not paired with a protecting moat. | The long view had said: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹2.14 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: The business is anchored in asset-heavy crushing/refining plus a 1.1 lakh hectare plantation with a 7–25 year yield cycle, and the largest segment earns only 2–4% EBITDA with no protective moat.
- **Out of limelight** (Management): Poor → Weak. Now: The business is built around high-profile celebrity endorsement and influencer marketing rather than a low-key posture. | The long view had said: The chairman-figure is a national celebrity who personally addresses investors with promotional rhetoric, and the brands are fronted by film and cricket celebrities — the antithesis of low-profile management.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: Expansion is purely intra-India (strengthening southern reach, plantations across 12 states) with no international or premium-brand travel evidence in the window. | The long view had said: Geographic ambition is repeatedly voiced (exports, world markets) but over four years no export revenue is ever quantified and even the domestic South is still at the strategy stage.
- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Good → Excellent. Now: Distribution is explicitly the company's core competitive asset — 2 million-plus outlets, rapid outlet addition, omni-channel coverage, and cited as the reason it can outlast smaller players in volatile input markets. | The long view had said: Distribution is explicitly the company's core strength with over 2 million outlets and an omni-channel push, but the reach is comparable to rather than unmatched by large FMCG rivals, and the numbers quoted by management have been inconsistent.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Management explicitly identifies weak smaller and unorganized players as the source of share in a volatile cost environment, and the outlet and category growth rates support that transfer already happening. | The long view had said: Weak unorganised and smaller regional players are visibly ceding ground under commodity volatility and formalisation, giving the company a pool of share to take.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Good → Weak. Now: Growth is volume- and commodity-inflation-led rather than pricing/mix — GST cuts were passed on via higher grammage packs, management guided to 300-400bps volume gains, and low-margin staples growth explicitly diluted mix. | The long view had said: Growth is genuinely mix-led — FMCG is 27.6% of revenue but 61% of EBITDA — yet pricing power is weak, with GST cuts passed through via grammage and price cuts and oil growth explicitly planned on volume.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: The customer base is correctly retail and the ayurvedic ethos is the right intangible lever, but the intangible is not monetized as premium pricing, so the benefit-to-customer match is only partially executed. | The long view had said: The customer base is retail/mass and the benefit offered (Swadeshi-ayurvedic image plus affordability) is correctly matched to it, though the premium 'classes' extension is still unproven.
- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Weak. Now: After direct input costs, the company keeps ₹13 of every ₹100 of sales — thin pricing power; customers likely buy on price. And from the calls: Despite a strong brand image, there is no evidence of image-based pricing power — gross margin fell with COGS up 294bps year-on-year, price cuts were passed through, and company EBITDA margin is only 4.79%. | The long view had said: After direct input costs, the company keeps ₹13 of every ₹100 of sales — thin pricing power; customers likely buy on price. And from the calls: Real intangible pull exists in ayurvedic HPC (≈25% segment EBITDA, premium Kesh Kanti Saundarya traction), but company-level pricing power is weak — GST benefits were fully passed on and blended margins sit under 5%.
- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: The only M&A referenced is the HPC business integration — a large related-party transfer rather than a small bolt-on — though FY26 as its first full year showed 35.42% HPC growth; segment-level OPM improvement is not evidenced. | The long view had said: Three complementary slump-sale purchases (biscuits, foods, HPC) were integrated with growth and margin uplift — HPC EBITDA moved from ~18% to ~25% — but they are large related-party transfers from the promoter, not small arm's-length bolt-ons.

*Unchanged: Capex for growth (not maintenance); Return on capital (headline); Cost of working capital; Mini-monopolies; Advertisement and promotion; Convenience benefits; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Cyclical market growth; Halo effect (caution); Technology advantage; Gain in market share; Structural end-market growth; Candor; Oligopoly (vs duopoly).*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 2 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 2 weakened, 8 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Brand Strength**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Brand-building is real and working — branded oils rose to ~85% of edible oil sales in 9M FY26 with MS Dhoni endorsement, Doodh biscuits grew from Rs 1,000 crores to Rs 1,300 crores, and the Patanjali name is stretched into HPC, beverages and nutraceuticals — but the brand delivers volume and distribution reach rather than premium pricing, and the Q4 margin compression shows limited pricing protection. (The long view had said: Repeated, delivered evidence across all four excerpted calls: swadeshi/Ayurveda heritage stretched into foods, biscuits, dental, skin, hair and home care under one name; branded oils rose to ~75-85% of edible oil sales; dental care reached Rs.1,413cr in FY26 with sustained A&P behind named ambassadors (Dhoni, Tiger Shroff, Tamannaah).)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Distribution expanded by 0.2–0.25 million outlets over the last calendar year to over 2 million, and FMCG grew 38.93% year-on-year in Q3 FY26 against a sector the company itself calls disrupted — but no explicit market-share percentages or comparisons versus named competitors are given in any of the four calls. (The long view had said: Persistent, quantified share gain over the period: direct distribution expanded to over 2 million outlets (0.2-0.25m added in the last calendar year alone), HPC grew 38.9% and 35.4% y-o-y in Q3/Q4 FY26 against a category growing 3-5%, and dental care added Rs.116cr y-o-y in a single quarter — gained with rising, not sacrificed, margins (HPC EBITDA ~25% vs 18% at takeover).)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 4 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: Policy repeatedly drives the P&L in these calls — GST 2.0 caused a demand and trade-inventory disruption in Q2/Q3 and is credited with an expected 300–400bps volume uplift; the oil palm business rests on government-allocated land (6.63 lakh hectares granted) under Atmanirbhar Bharat, and a government advisory on edible oil consumption and welfare/intervention schemes are cited as demand and price factors. Exposure is bounded because revenue comes from retail consumers, not government contracts or a regulated tariff. (The long view had said: Government action repeatedly drives earnings: the September 2024 import-duty hike produced a ~22% one-off gain, GST 2.0 forced repricing and caused trade disruption in Sep-Oct 2025, wheat prices are capped by 'government's intervention schemes', rural demand rests on 'government welfare schemes', and the oil palm business depends on 6.63 lakh hectares of allocated land under the Atmanirbhar Bharat policy — with management flagging policy 'overreach' as a risk to staples in Jun 2026.)
- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → LOW. Why now: Quick commerce — the structurally different format that has hurt Indian traditional retail — is discussed as a channel the company is scaling into, not as a threat, and management argues crude-linked volatility disadvantages smaller and unorganized players relative to large national brands. (The long view had said: Management describes intense competitive entry pressure in its FMCG categories — toothpaste 'competition is fairly intense' in a category growing only 3-5%, and regional biscuit players holding Rs.4.5/9 price points against the leaders' Rs.5/10 — while quick commerce has reshaped the channel.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Management explicitly reports demand migrating within its core category — a shift toward mustard oil, and a government advisory pushing lower edible oil consumption outright — while the FMCG portfolio is being reworked toward health/wellness (nutraceuticals rationalised, Slim Choice and Sea Buckthorn capsules, fruit-based beverages). The shifts described so far are substitution within the basket rather than erosion of the demand base. (The long view had said: Demand mix is visibly migrating — a 'noticeable shift in consumer preference towards mustard oil' in Apr 2026, palm imports down 20% while soya rose 20.2%, a government advisory urging lower edible oil consumption, and rice/ghee volumes falling; the portfolio must keep pivoting with these shifts.)
- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Across all four calls no single customer, distributor or supplier is named as driving results; management instead stresses breadth — over 2 million retail outlets with 0.2–0.25 million added in the last calendar year, plus modern trade, e-commerce and multiple quick-commerce platforms. (The long view had said: The entire growth story rests on one relationship — successive slump-sale transfers of biscuits, foods and the INR2,800 crore HPC business from promoter Patanjali Ayurved, with the Patanjali brand and Swami Ramdev's endorsement owned outside the listed entity; the Jul 2024 meeting was effectively the promoter narrating the listed company's strategy.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Technological Innovation (QUANT FLAG).*

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
