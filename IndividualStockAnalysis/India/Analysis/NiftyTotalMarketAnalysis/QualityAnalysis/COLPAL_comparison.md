### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 8 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 7. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 2 weakened, 7 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PASS on the long view, PASS on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Friendly Middleman**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The May 2026 call makes the dental profession a core brand asset ('Dentists Recommend' tracked as a headline brand-health metric, plus tie-ups with the Trained Nurses Association and eight state governments), but the sell is overwhelmingly mass-consumer — Rs. 822 crores of brand investment and 'most advertised FMCG brand' — so the professional is an endorser, not the buyer-chooser the pattern describes. (The long view had said: The dentist channel is a genuine and repeated lever — IDA seal, Dentist FIRST platform (2022), 50,000 dentists giving free time in the Oral Health Movement (FY25), a dentist on 500 million packs and PerioGard prescriptions carried through the professional oral care channel with the Indian Society of Periodontology seal (FY26). But sales are overwhelmingly driven by mass consumer marketing (Rs.822 crore of advertising, 'if not the largest advertised FMCG brand'), so the professional is an endorser rather than the buyer.)
- **Low Price Plus**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: There is genuine cost obsession ('suck out every non-value adding cost', May 2025: margins held 'not because of price increase, but actually because of extensive and very sharp cost management'), but it funds 69-70% gross margins and a premiumisation strategy rather than low prices — the opposite of the low-price/low-margin/high-turns model. (The long view had said: There is a real, itemised cost culture — the 'funding the growth' programme finding efficiencies in every P&L line, with FY25 gross margin expansion explicitly cost-driven and recycled into product quality and advertising — but the economics are the opposite of the low-price model: ~70% gross margins and 32-34% EBITDA, with strategy pointed at premiumisation rather than passing savings to price.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Forward Integrators (LIKELY FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT).*

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (STRONG FIT); Corporate Culture (STRONG FIT); Cost to Replicate (STRONG FIT); Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (QUANT SIGNAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
