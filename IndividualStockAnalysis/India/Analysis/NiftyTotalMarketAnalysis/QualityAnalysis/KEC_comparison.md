**Checks that improved:**

- **Merger and acquisition** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Good. Now: No new M&A in the window, but management flagged the 15-year anniversary of the SAE bolt-on which is now delivering 36% growth with profitable revenue, evidencing a well-integrated complementary acquisition. | The long view had said: No new bolt-ons in the period, but the legacy SAE Brazil acquisition was a persistent drag requiring loss funding and high-cost debt repayment over years, and management signalled openness to inorganic moves.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Weak → Good. Now: Real niche technical capability in HVDC converter stations, GIS substations, KAVACH train protection and e-beam/elastomeric cables, though partly dependent on partners such as Hitachi and a JV. | The long view had said: Capability in HVDC, GIS and digital construction tools is real but largely licensed or partner-dependent, with no disclosed R&D intensity to sustain a technology edge.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: No captive aftermarket or switching costs — revenue is one-off project delivery, and the only recurrence is voluntary repeat ordering by clients. | The long view had said: Clients re-tender every project with essentially no switching costs, and even large data-centre opportunities are split across multiple contractors.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: Clients buy proven execution certainty — repeat orders from marquee clients and award of first-of-kind, largest-ever and safety-critical projects reflect trust in reliability over price. | The long view had said: Execution track record and pre-qualifications do buy credibility for complex 765kV/HVDC and Kavach work, but selection still runs through lowest-price tendering.
- **Network effects** (Competitive Advantage): Poor → Weak. Now: Project-based EPC with no two-sided platform dynamic; nothing in the calls suggests value compounds with each additional customer. | The long view had said: Nothing in the business model strengthens with scale in a self-reinforcing two-sided way; each project stands alone.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: Growth is essentially volume/order-book led at thin margins (EBITDA ~7%, PAT margin 2.6%), though mix is improving via a shift to T&D and fewer-but-larger orders. | The long view had said: Growth is volume/order-book led in a competitively bid business with EBITDA margins stuck around 7%; mix improvement (T&D share, larger orders, cables) is real but secondary and margin guidance was cut mid-year.
- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Weak → Neutral. Now: Organic focus with no big M&A and a genuinely disciplined stance on loss-making water receivables, offset by leverage that rose all year against repeated promises of normalisation. | The long view had said: Organic focus and no big M&A are positives, but repeated promises of working-capital and debt improvement from FY23 onwards were not delivered — debt roughly doubled over the four years.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Well-executed multi-geography expansion: largest-ever EPC order in UAE, largest substation order in Saudi, first entry into a new CIS country, Africa/CIS revival and clear North American uptick via SAE. | The long view had said: Credible, executed international expansion — Middle East ~27% of order book, first 380kV GIS in Saudi, revival in Africa/CIS, Australia tower supply and GCC entry in oil & gas, with international T&D inflows up 35%.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Weak → Neutral. Now: Customers are corporate/utility buyers where reliability is correctly the pitch, but there is no switching-cost lock-in and government water clients impose severe payment risk. | The long view had said: Customers are governments, utilities and corporates where TCO and reliability matter, but KEC has no switching-cost lock-in and remains exposed to slow-paying state clients such as in water projects.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Genuine technical barriers exist in HVDC converter stations and GIS substations, but the broader EPC market is open-tender with pricing pressure evident in 7% EBITDA margins. | The long view had said: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pre-qualifications create some barrier in HVDC/EHV, but civil, water and domestic oil & gas see heavy entry and price competition forcing KEC to withdraw from segments.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Poor → Weak. Now: Business is competitively tendered project EPC with no aftermarket or mind-share monopoly; every order is won in open bidding across many players. | The long view had said: No monopoly-like position anywhere; work is won project-by-project through open competitive tendering with no aftermarket annuity.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Candor** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Management consistently names what went wrong rather than only highlights — labour shortages, water payment delays, LPG/logistics disruption, claim settlement delays and the exact drivers of higher debt. | The long view had said: Consistently candid across four years — admits underinvestment and below-market margins in cables, flags misses openly, and declines to project confidence it does not have.

*Unchanged: Asset turn; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Out of limelight; Gain in market share; Profit margin; Cyclical market growth; Capex for growth (not maintenance); Halo effect (caution); Return on capital (headline); Intangible benefits; Distribution to shareholders; Cost of working capital; Structural end-market growth; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Distribution advantage; Convenience benefits; Security by obscurity.*

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 0 weakened, 10 unchanged.

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 2 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: KEC is a pure capex-linked EPC: revenue comes from utilities', private developers' and governments' transmission/civil/rail capacity programmes, not from customers' day-to-day operations, and results swing with commodity prices and order-cycle timing (strategic steel/aluminium inventory built on 'benign commodity prices', FY26 revenue growth decelerating from 19% in Q2 to 8% for the full year while EBITDA margin sits at a thin ~7%). Management is expanding tower capacity at Dubai, Jaipur, Jabalpur and Butibori into the current up-cycle, the classic expansionary-period pattern; a bulging order book (INR40,000 crores, 6-7 quarters visibility) is visibility, not immunity to a capex turn. (The long view had said: KEC's revenue is almost entirely tied to customers' capex programmes — utility/private TBCB transmission builds, metro and railway projects, real-estate and industrial construction — and to commodity input prices under ~50% fixed-price orders, which is exactly the customer-cyclicality plus commodity-pass-through combination. The FY23 calls show consolidated EBITDA collapsing to 5.1% on 'elevated commodity prices and logistic cost', and even in FY26 margins swing on water-project slowdowns, LPG/steel supplier shutdowns and claim settlements, with guidance cut mid-year from ~8% to '7% to 7.5%'.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: NO SIGNAL → LOW. Why now: The demand base is expanding rather than eroding, and KEC's portfolio is positioned on the side the shift is moving toward: electrification, record peak power demand, renewable evacuation, plus its own solar, wind and BESS bidding and a 500 MW solar project in Karnataka and Rajasthan. (The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and the only computed fingerprint is clean.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): New Entrants (WATCH).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Strained on the long view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.) vs Strained on the one-year view (1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
