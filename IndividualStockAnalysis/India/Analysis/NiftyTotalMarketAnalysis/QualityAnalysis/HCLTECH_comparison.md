Why now: Management claims a third consecutive year of the highest organic growth among scale players, and backs it with client-bucket migration — one added $100m client, eight added $50m clients, 28 added $1m clients, all organic — plus explicit wallet-share gains in top accounts and record Q1 bookings of $2.4bn; the gain looks organic rather than bought, though no absolute market-share percentages are given. (The long view had said: Management claims relative share gain repeatedly and backs it with organic client-category migration (one new $100m client, eight new $50m clients in FY26, all organic), but the claim is self-assessed rather than an explicit share number, and absolute growth has decelerated from 15.6% (Jul 2022) to 2.6% year-on-year (Jul 2026) with FY27 guided at 1-4%.)

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 1 unchanged; 4 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Q4 FY26 showed how quickly client discretionary budgets can swing revenue: two large US telecom clients cut discretionary digital and engineering spend, two SAP programs were discontinued, and March procurement decisions were delayed, pushing Q4 revenue down 3.3% quarter-on-quarter with management guiding the impact to persist through the calendar year. Offsetting this, a large annuity/managed-services base held up as expected, so the cyclicality sits in the discretionary layer rather than the whole book. (The long view had said: Revenue tracks client discretionary IT budgets rather than a commodity cycle, but the FY26 calls show real swings — tariff volatility, discretionary spend cut by two large US telecom clients, and FY27 guidance cut to 1-4% — while the annuity/managed-services base and cost-takeout demand act countercyclically (clients spend to save in downturns).)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: AI productivity is functioning as a good-enough substitute for billed effort: five of the top ten renewals came with increased ACV but the rest saw SOW declines attributed to AI-linked productivity, and management concedes 'AI deflation' embedded in net new TCV that kept FY26 bookings flat at $9.3bn. Headcount fell in three of the four quarters, consistent with price/effort compression in commoditising service lines. (The long view had said: The economics of the core services portfolio are being repriced downward by cheaper-equivalent delivery: contracts carry annual productivity give-backs that mechanically shrink revenue every Q1 (stated in both the Jul 2022 and Jul 2026 calls), management concedes AI deflation is depressing TCV, quantifies 2-3% portfolio-level deflation, and describes cost take-out and vendor consolidation as dominating the deal mix — the classic pattern of a good-enough substitute compressing a value-added service.)

**That worsened:**

- **Technological Innovation**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Management itself frames AI as deflationary to its existing revenue base across all four calls — renewal SOWs declining on AI-linked productivity, TCV deflation in bookings, and an explicit intent to disrupt its own revenue. The response is credible and large-scale (AI Force in 92 accounts, $171m quarterly Advanced AI revenue, 62% year-on-year), but the industry-level reset in how IT services are priced and delivered is exactly the large-scale innovation risk, not incremental innovation. (The long view had said: Management itself quantifies a large-scale platform shift: it says 40% of the industry is at risk of AI disruption and can shrink 3-5% CAGR, translating to 2-3% deflation on HCLTech's own portfolio, with the installed base of traditional app development, infra operations and customer support becoming legacy work. HCLTech is investing heavily to stay ahead (AI Force across 92 accounts, $620m annualised Advanced AI revenue, 38 patents), but that is a defensive re-tooling of the core delivery model, not a small-scale innovation cadence.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH); Government Dependency (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW); New Entrants (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 12 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
