# Oberoi Realty Ltd. (OBEROIRLTY) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: CONSTRUCTION*

Oberoi Realty is an Indian property developer that designs, builds and sells premium and luxury apartments, mostly in the Mumbai Metropolitan Region (Goregaon, Borivali, Mulund, Thane, Worli, and South Mumbai locations like Pedder Road, Malabar Hill and Tardeo), and has recently started outside Mumbai with a project in the NCR/Gurugram market. Most of its money comes from selling homes to individual buyers — ticket sizes range from roughly Rs. 2.5-5 crore apartments up to Rs. 50-60 crore luxury units, with per-square-foot prices in Worli quoted around Rs. 1,50,000 on carpet — and revenue is booked over the construction period using percentage-of-completion accounting, with project margins the finance director says run from about 43-44% to about 65%. Alongside selling homes, it keeps and rents out commercial property — office towers (Commerz I, II and III) and shopping malls (Oberoi Mall in Goregaon and Sky City Mall in Borivali) — which give it steady recurring rent, and it also has a hotel. It builds its future pipeline by continuously buying land, doing society redevelopment deals, slum-rehabilitation (SRA) projects and distressed-company acquisitions, and it outsources actual construction to contractors such as L&T.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Residential development (pre-sales)** — The main business — building and selling apartments in large integrated townships and standalone towers (Elysian in Goregaon, Sky City in Borivali, Oberoi Garden City Thane with Jardin and Forestville, Mulund, Three Sixty West in Worli, and now Three Sixty North in Gurugram); management cites Rs. 5,400 crores of sales in FY26 and analysts expect Rs. 10,000-12,000 crores in FY27 after the Gurugram launch, making this by far the largest earnings driver.
- **Annuity portfolio — offices (Commerz I, II and III)** — Office buildings the company owns and leases to corporate tenants rather than sells, running at near 100% combined occupancy (Commerz III reached 98% and attracted 'marquee tenants'), providing steady rental income.
- **Annuity portfolio — retail malls (Oberoi Mall, Sky City Mall)** — Shopping malls it owns and leases to retailers; the older Oberoi Mall in Goregaon is described as a mature asset, while Sky City Mall in Borivali is about twice its size (roughly 760,000 sq ft carpet / ~1.1 million sq ft leasable) and reached 82% occupancy in its first year, and management says the mall also lifts prices of the apartments built next to it.
- **Hospitality** — A hotel business — a Ritz-Carlton that is roughly 80-90% finished on interiors and targeted to open by the end of FY27, plus the acquisition of Horizon Hotel Private Limited through an NCLT resolution plan; discussed only occasionally and clearly much smaller than residential.
- **Business development (land, redevelopment and SRA)** — The pipeline-building arm that buys land parcels and signs joint development, society-redevelopment and slum-rehabilitation (SRA) deals — about 4 million sq ft of new development potential added across MMR in FY26 alone (Bandra East, Aram Nagar-Versova, Pedder Road, Malabar Hill, Nepean Sea Road), plus Thane, Alibaug (81 acres) and Gurugram — and it isn't a revenue line itself but determines what the company can launch in future years.
- **Commercial for sale (strata)** — An occasional variant where a commercial building, such as the Enigma project, is sold off floor-by-floor to buyers instead of being retained for rent; mentioned only as a possible option for one project, so it is a minor part of the business.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Oberoi Realty comes out of the three reviews as a company whose whole story runs through one thing: the brand and the delivery record attached to it. The quality framework gives full marks to assurance benefits, noting that customers buy delivery certainty over price, with a flight to quality in downturns and NCR buyers refusing refunds even amid litigation. That same trait is what the multibagger screen picks up twice over as its two strongest patterns: Pricing Power, where management describes planned price increases without losing volume (20% hikes in Goregaon, repricing in Thane and Borivali, Worli at Rs. 1,50,000 per sq ft carpet, justified by resale trading 20-25% above the company's own launch prices), and Brand Strength, which the screen says stretched into a brand-new geography when the Gurugram Three Sixty North debut drew over Rs. 80bn of gross bookings. The risk review then leans on the very same evidence in the opposite direction: New Entrants is rated LOW precisely because the calls show no discounting anywhere and repeated price rises, and Good Enough Goods is LOW because buyers choose the developer directly rather than through an intermediary, with the company's own resale market pricing above its launch prices. One trait is doing the work in all three records, which is a strength but also a concentration of the investment case.

The second thread is the land bank and the owned downstream assets, and here the same fact is a virtue in one record and a cost in another. The screen rates Forward Integrators a likely fit because the company keeps and operates its malls (Oberoi Mall, Sky City at 1.1mn sq ft leasable), Commerz offices, the JW Marriott and Ritz-Carlton hotels and an international school, and sells homes direct; Cost to Replicate is a partial fit on the strength of irreplaceable Mumbai land assembled over 40 years and 15-year integrated ecosystems a newcomer cannot rebuild. The quality framework scores growth capex highly for exactly this build-out, funded largely from internal accruals. But the same balance sheet is why asset turn scores at the bottom of the scale, with every Rs. 1 of assets producing only Rs. 0.24 of sales, and why headline return on capital is negative-scored at roughly Rs. 7 of operating cash per Rs. 100 of capital. It also feeds the single HIGH RISK finding, Cyclicality, where the reviewer flags aggressive land accumulation into a strong upcycle across FY24-FY27 (Thane, Nirmal Lifestyle, 81 acres at Alibaug, Gurugram, RLDA, multiple SRA and redevelopment sites). Owning the whole ecosystem is what makes the moat hard to copy and what makes the capital slow to come back.

The third thread is how the company sources that land, and it explains two of the three flagged risks. The framework awards full marks for share donators, listing the former Worli partner's fire sale, Nirmal Lifestyle's NCLT insolvency and the distressed IREO Gurugram asset as weak competitors converted into Oberoi land and market share, and credits geographical expansion from the Goregaon and Worli base into Thane, Mulund, Alibaug and NCR. The risk review reads the same transactions as exposure: Stakeholder Concentration is a WATCH because the Three Sixty West joint venture partner's fire sale and residual inventory overhung that project's sales from Aug 2023 through Jul 2026, and Government Dependency is a WATCH because the launch calendar is gated by state machinery, with NCLT resolution plans, SRA developer appointments at Bandra Reclamation and Borivali, an RLDA railway process and the IOD, CC, RERA and OC sequence all standing between a bought parcel and a saleable home. Set against that, the annuity portfolio is the stated silver lining on cyclicality, with Commerz and Oberoi Mall near 100% occupancy, Sky City Mall at 82% in year one, and virtually no debt on large ready inventory. The combined verdict is Strong at 70 out of 100, built as 45% x 60 on business quality, 30% x 82 on multibagger fit and 25% x 72 on risk safety, with resilience rated SOUND. The area scores show the same split, with customer benefits at 1.5 and capital allocation at 1.25 against return on capital at -0.67 and competitive advantage at -0.33. Note too that the framework scores security by obscurity at the bottom: this is a large listed developer covered by HSBC, CLSA, JP Morgan, Morgan Stanley, Kotak and Nomura in India's most-watched property market, so nothing here is undiscovered.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Residential development is a long-capital-cycle business and the trajectory across FY24–FY27 shows aggressive land accumulation into a strong upcycle (Thane 6.4 acres, Nirmal Lifestyle, 81 acres Alibaug, Gurugram, RLDA, multiple SRA/redevelopments — ~4 mn sq ft of BD announced in FY26 alone), with lumpy pre-sales and revenue (residential rev-rec fell from Rs.13-14bn to Rs.8.8bn in Q1FY27; Three Sixty West sold 10 units in FY26 vs 17 in FY25) and input-cost inflation now eating into budgeted contingencies.

> "costs have gone up. Energy cost has gone up, aluminium has gone up, glass has gone up, labor's become expensive" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Growing annuity/flow income (Commerz I/II/III and Oberoi Mall near 100% occupancy, Sky City Mall 82% in year one), virtually no debt on large ready inventory, and land payments largely deferred/revenue-linked (RLDA payable until 2038), plus countercyclical land buying — 'be it a bad market or a good market, we go and buy that'*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 3 of the last 11 years with swings of ±37 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 44% and 59% (±4.7 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 64% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 11% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

### Government Dependency — WATCH
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Pricing and demand are market-set, but the entire launch calendar is gated by state machinery — IOD/CC/RERA/OC sequencing, SRA developer appointments at Bandra Reclamation and Borivali, NCLT resolution plans (Nirmal Lifestyle, Horizon Hotel), an RLDA (railway land) project, land temporarily surrendered to the Metro at Worli, and a Supreme Court ruling gating the Versova/Sahara parcel and litigation on the Gurugram launch; slippage is repeatedly attributed to approvals, not demand.

> "the tail to launch a project has got a little longer , given one needs to take all approvals in place" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: No revenue is subsidy- or tariff-determined; government touchpoints are permissions and land sourcing rather than a policy-set price, and project diversity across MMR and NCR spreads any single approval risk*

## What to watch

- Any discounting or resale prices slipping below launch prices would undercut both the Pricing Power and Brand Strength patterns at once, and would also weaken the LOW ratings on New Entrants and Good Enough Goods that rest on the same evidence.
- Whether the pace of land buying (roughly 4mn sq ft of MMR business development, Thane, Alibaug, Gurugram, RLDA) continues if sales slow - this is the core of the HIGH RISK cyclicality finding.
- Whether rising annuity occupancy (Sky City Mall at 82%, Commerz III toward 98%) lifts the weak return-on-capital and asset-turn checks, currently Rs. 7 of operating cash per Rs. 100 of capital and Rs. 0.24 of sales per Rs. 1 of assets.
- Slippage in the approval chain flagged under Government Dependency - SRA appointments, NCLT resolutions, the RLDA process, IOD/CC/RERA/OC - since these gate the entire launch calendar.
- Whether NCR sales repeat beyond the Three Sixty North debut phase, and whether the Three Sixty West residual inventory overhang named under Stakeholder Concentration finally clears.
