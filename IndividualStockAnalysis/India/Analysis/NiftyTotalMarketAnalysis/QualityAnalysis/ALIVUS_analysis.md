# Alivus Life Sciences (ALIVUS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Alivus Life Sciences (formerly Glenmark Life Sciences, renamed after Nirma became its promoter in 2024) is an Indian company that manufactures active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) — the chemical raw materials that go into finished medicines — and sells them in bulk to drug makers rather than to patients. Its customers are generic pharma companies across India, Europe, Japan, Latin America, the US and other regulated markets, plus its former parent Glenmark Pharma, and it had about 900 customers and 176 unique molecules as of FY26. It makes money by selling these APIs (mostly for chronic conditions like heart and central-nervous-system disease, which are about two-thirds of revenue) and, to a smaller extent, by making molecules to order for other companies under contract (CDMO). Manufacturing sits at plants in Dahej, Ankleshwar and Solapur in India, with a new R&D centre being built at Taloja; FY26 revenue was Rs. 2,552 crores with EBITDA margins of 33.6% and no net debt.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Generic API business** — The core business — making and selling off-patent APIs to pharma customers worldwide, backed by 611 DMF/CEP regulatory filings as of March 2026; it is the overwhelming majority of revenue (roughly Rs. 535 crores of Rs. 588 crores in the Q1 FY25 quarter management broke out).
- **Non-GPL (external) business** — API sales to outside customers across Europe, Japan, LATAM, India and rest-of-world — the growth engine management keeps pointing to, up from 59% of total revenue in FY22 to 71% in FY26 and growing 13% in FY26.
- **GPL business** — API supply to Glenmark Pharma, the former parent — a large but flat and lumpy captive customer (it fell 41% year-on-year in one 2022 quarter and rebounded 18% quarter-on-quarter in mid-2024); management is deliberately reducing dependence on it, now down to about 29% of revenue.
- **CDMO** — Contract development and manufacturing — making molecules to a customer's own specification instead of a catalogue product; small (about Rs. 43 crores in the Q1 FY25 quarter, roughly 7% of that quarter's sales) and volatile, but it doubled quarter-on-quarter in Q3 FY26 as new projects started and is credited as one of the main drivers of the recent margin improvement.
- **High-potency API portfolio (including oncology)** — A pipeline of more complex, harder-to-make APIs such as cancer drugs, built in dedicated facilities — 28 products in development as of March 2026 (12 validated, 7 in advanced stages), described as addressing a $70 billion market and intended as the company's future higher-margin growth layer rather than a current revenue line.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*
