# Alivus Life Sciences (ALIVUS) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Alivus Life Sciences (formerly Glenmark Life Sciences, renamed after Nirma became its promoter in 2024) is an India-based maker of active pharmaceutical ingredients — the actual drug chemicals that go inside finished medicines — which it sells in bulk to pharmaceutical companies rather than to patients. Its money comes mainly from selling a broad catalogue of generic APIs (176 unique molecules to roughly 900 customers as of FY26), weighted toward chronic-disease therapies such as cardiovascular and central nervous system drugs, which are about two-thirds of sales; a smaller contract-manufacturing (CDMO) arm makes molecules to order for specific customers. It ships to regulated and semi-regulated markets including India, Europe, Japan, Latin America, North America and the rest of the world, backing this with 611 DMF/CEP regulatory filings globally, and manufactures at Indian plants in Ankleshwar, Dahej and Solapur with an R&D centre being built at Taloja. FY26 revenue was about Rs. 2,552 crores with EBITDA margins of 33.6%, and the company is net debt free.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Generic API business** — The core operation: making and selling off-patent bulk drug ingredients to generic drug makers worldwide — it is the overwhelming majority of revenue (roughly Rs. 535 crores of Rs. 588 crores in Q1 FY25), led by cardiovascular and CNS molecules, with chronic therapies at about 66-67% of the top line.
- **CDMO (contract development and manufacturing)** — Develops and manufactures molecules on contract for specific customers; small but strategically important — around Rs. 43 crores in Q1 FY25 (well under 10% of sales) — and after a weak patch it grew 100% quarter-on-quarter and 85% year-on-year in Q3 FY26 as new projects ramped up, which management credits as a key driver of record margins.
- **GPL business (sales to Glenmark Pharma)** — Captive supply of APIs to its former parent Glenmark Pharmaceuticals, historically a large single-customer dependency that management is deliberately shrinking — it fell from about 41% of revenue in FY22 to roughly 29% in FY26, and has been volatile (down 41% year-on-year in Q2 FY23, then recovering).
- **Non-GPL / external business** — All third-party customers outside Glenmark Pharma, across India, Europe, Japan, LATAM and rest of world; this is the growth engine and the stated priority, rising from 59% of revenue in FY22 to 71% in FY26 and growing 13% in FY26 versus 6.9% for the company overall.
- **High-potency API portfolio** — A development pipeline of complex, higher-value ingredients (including oncology products) — 28 products as of March 2026, of which 12 are validated and 7 in advanced development — pitched as a large future opportunity (management cites a $70 billion addressable market) rather than a meaningful revenue contributor today.

### The story in depth

Alivus earns its "Decent" 60-out-of-100 rating on the strength of how it is run rather than on what it sells. The quality work scores management highest (1.2) alongside growth and return on capital (1.0 each), and the evidence is consistent: net debt free in all 15 quarters, FY27's ₹540cr capex funded entirely from internal accruals, capacity expanded in deliberate phases from 1,198 KL toward a planned 2,690 KL at roughly 1.3-1.5x revenue-to-capex and about 30% return on capital, and no large acquisition attempted despite a growing cash pile. Candor scores 2 for the same reason it shows up as the single STRONG FIT pattern, Corporate Culture: the same leadership pair stayed in place through a change of promoter, and bad news arrives early and quantified — the PLI subsidy expiry pre-warned, the Gujarat Pollution Control Board closure notice volunteered in opening remarks, the ₹20cr Dahej fire loss and ₹31cr forex gain both put on the record. Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹31 is operating profit, and that figure has moved only about ±1.7 points a year.

The business moat and the business's biggest weakness are the same fact seen from two sides. Its 611 global DMF/CEP filings, FDA-inspected plants and multi-year customer validation cycles are what make Toll Roads a LIKELY FIT — a customer who has filed exhibit batches against an Alivus source cannot switch cheaply — and they are what make Cost to Replicate a real, if PARTIAL, barrier that a newcomer cannot assemble quickly. That same regulatory anchoring is also the source of the Government Dependency risk rated WATCH: the regulator that raises the wall also issued the closure notice that threatened production, and the PLI subsidy whose expiry visibly compressed gross margins from 55.5% to 51.1%. Where the filings stop working is on price. Management concedes 4-4.5% annual price erosion and calls it ordinary in this business, which is why both Pricing Power and Low Price Plus come back NO FIT and why Good Enough Goods sits at WATCH — generic APIs are chemically identical products bought by price-sensitive formulators. The company's answer is the Innovation Dominance pattern: R&D held at roughly 3-3.6% of sales, filings up from 532 to 611, 11 new products and a 28-product high-potency pipeline. The 350bps gross-margin expansion came from that mix shift and from new launches, not from charging more, which is exactly what the Pricing, Mix and Volume check records.

The one HIGH RISK is customer concentration, and it traces back to the same promoter history that produced the leadership stability the culture pattern praises. Former promoter Glenmark has been the single largest customer at roughly 26-33% of revenue throughout, and its swings alone have moved reported results — a 41% year-on-year decline in that business turned group growth negative in Q2 FY23. The repair work and the Market Share Gainers pattern are one and the same effort: outside business up from 59% of revenue in FY22 to 71% in FY26, growing faster than the company at 13% in FY26 and 16.1% in the nine months, 49 customers added to a base of about 900, and launches across Europe, China, LATAM and Russia. That pattern only rates PARTIAL because there are no share figures against named competitors, and the two patterns flagged Brand Strength and Friendly Middleman rest on numbers with nothing in the calls behind them — screening hints, not findings. The remaining risks — cyclicality, technological change, shifting preferences and fashion — all read LOW, helped by chronic therapies at 66-67% of revenue, and the destocking trough appears to be behind the company with CDMO revenue up 100% quarter-on-quarter in Q3 FY26. The lower quality-area scores of 0.5 for capital allocation, competitive advantage, industry structure and customer benefits, plus a resilience reading of UNKNOWN, are what hold this at 60 rather than higher: a well-run company with narrow moats, one large customer, and no ability to raise prices.
