### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 2 worsened, 4 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Technological Innovation**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Innovation discussed is incremental and value-adding — lifecycle extensions around mega brands (Digecaine, Digeraft, Digene stick pack), 35 launches in three years with 75 planned, plus digital as a sales-productivity and DTC tool. No competing technology platform or format obsolescence is raised defensively, and the branded-generic model is not being displaced. (The long view had said: Therapeutic-class obsolescence is a real, if slow, erosion channel that management itself names — doctors migrating from antacids to PPIs/H2RAs, and phenytoin (Eptoin, a mega-brand) being succeeded by next-generation molecules like brivaracetam; the company's answer is to buy back in via loss-of-exclusivity launches, i.e. it does not own the innovation.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The only preference movement described is channel-side (patients searching symptoms on Google, brick-and-mortar chains going online, e-pharmacies going physical) plus a post-COVID shift toward adult immunisation — both of which management is pivoting toward rather than being eroded by. The underlying disease-driven demand base is not shown to be shrinking. (The long view had said: Two demand bases are shifting under the franchise: patients are moving to online pharmacies and to self-medication (driving the RX-to-OTC switches of Digene, Cremaffin and prospectively Duphalac), and post-COVID vaccine fatigue plus reduced travel slowed the influenza and vertigo markets. Management is pivoting rather than being caught, but the pivot is a response, not a choice.)

**That worsened:**

- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The flagship women's-health brand went from a 60-year monopoly to competing with 41-42 entrants inside two years, and Abbott has visibly lost share in the gynaecology segment; management devotes a large defensive block of the call to explaining why it will still grow. Thyronorm likewise now faces 11 competitors, and new-format online pharmacies are entering the channel. (The long view had said: The clearest demonstrated damage in the record: Duphaston went from a 60-year monopoly to one of 42 brands in two years and lost share in the gynaecology segment, with management conceding it is now a share-of-voice game against rivals with field forces up to four times larger; separately, e-pharmacy discounters are a new-format channel management is joining defensively rather than leading.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: The Duphaston episode is a textbook good-enough attack: 41 challengers all importing the molecule from China, with Abbott defending its premium purely on provenance and quality of its Netherlands source — and losing share in the gynaecology segment where the prescriber has no strong reason to insist on the brand. Management also concedes it is 'consciously looking at reducing our gross to net' and improving distribution margin, i.e. managing price/channel pressure. (The long view had said: This is the Nobel Biocare setup: 41 cheaper dydrogesterone copies sourcing API from China have taken share from Duphaston while management defends its premium purely on quality provenance; the prescribing doctor and the discounting chemist — not the patient — control the choice, e-pharmacies push hefty discounts that risk prescription substitution, and an analyst directly raised retailers' private labels against the OTC portfolio, to which management answered only that it would cross that bridge later.)

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
