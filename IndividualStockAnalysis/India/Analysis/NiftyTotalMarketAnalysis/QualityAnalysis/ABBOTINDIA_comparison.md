## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 29 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 6 improved, 3 regressed, 20 unchanged. 3 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them).

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Distribution advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Good. Now: Reach is a genuine competitive asset — leadership in GI with a smaller team than rivals, digital coverage of uncovered doctors in uncovered geographies, and access to state/central programmes — though it is a strong reach, not one rivals structurally cannot replicate. | The long view had said: Distribution is competent and broadening (retail availability, online chains, extra-urban push) but is explicitly not a reach advantage — a Duphaston competitor fields roughly four times the sales force and 30% of geography is still uncovered.
- **Candor** (Management): Good → Excellent. Now: Management volunteers the bad news without spin — admits share loss in Duphaston after going from sole supplier to one of 41, calls the vaccine performance a 'blip', and discloses the exact volume/price split and NLEM exposure. | The long view had said: Openly owns the Duphaston share loss and the vaccine slowdown rather than spinning them, and the CFO self-corrects a misstated figure live, but refuses to disclose the Novo split or payout policy.
- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: After direct input costs, the company keeps ₹47 of every ₹100 of sales — pricing power customers are happy to pay for. And from the calls: Pricing power rests on trust in provenance and quality rather than molecule differentiation — the same dydrogesterone is sold at a premium against 41 China-sourced rivals purely on the Netherlands/Abbott origin story, and customers keep paying. | The long view had said: After direct input costs, the company keeps ₹47 of every ₹100 of sales — pricing power customers are happy to pay for. And from the calls: Brand and provenance sustain premium pricing — Netherlands-sourced Duphaston commanding continued prescriptions against China-sourced generics, and ~400bps of gross margin gain — but 26% of sales sit under NLEM price control.
- **Advertisement and promotion** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: A&P is deployed as category-building capital — MITA thyroid awareness, 25 lakh TSH tests a year, Fibroscan/Gutfit in GI, and a deliberate multi-year investment to create the post-menopausal category with Femoston — a formula that already produced Thyronorm and Duphalac. | The long view had said: Promotional spend is deployed into durable category-building programmes (MITA thyroid awareness, Vertigo Care, Gutfit, the ABCDE epilepsy video, DTC digital for OTC) with an explicit ROI lens, though total promo was deliberately held flat and re-mixed rather than raised.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Good → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹27 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0.7 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹27 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±4.9 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Mini-monopolies** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: Several brands occupy the category-defining slot in the prescriber's mind — Thyronorm at ~53% share with the widest SKU range from 12.5 to 200mg, Digeraft already #1 in its category — which is a mini-monopoly of mindshare rather than of law. | The long view had said: Mind-share leadership in defined categories it created — ~53% of thyroid, 40%+ in vertigo, Digene as the default antacid — but the Duphaston experience shows these positions are legally, not structurally, protected.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Disciplined stewards** (Management): Excellent → Good. Now: Stewardship is organic and cost-disciplined — growth from productivity rather than headcount, explicit focus on gross-to-net, distribution margin and PCPM, with counter-cyclical headcount added in GI where they lead — and no M&A or leverage adventures. | The long view had said: Purely organic growth focus, debt-free with a large cash buffer, explicit refusal to overpay for inorganic deals, and counter-cyclical headcount investment in the GI franchise while others chase share of voice.
- **People matter (talent development)** (Management): Good → Neutral. Now: Talent is named as a stated pillar with concrete programmes (diversity and inclusion, 'quality of voice', GI headcount ramp), but the five-year record shows a revolving door at the top — four MDs across FY2021-FY2025 plus CFO turnover — which is the opposite of a settled internal pipeline. | The long view had said: Talent pipeline is named as one of four personal KPIs with concrete programmes (diversity and inclusion, 'quality of voice' productivity development), though the management history shows repeated MD/CFO turnover.
- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Excellent → Good. Now: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.07 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: The model is structurally asset-light — product sourced from Abbott's Netherlands plant and partners rather than owned capacity, with growth coming from productivity rather than added resources — though the moat protecting it is brand-based and was shown to be breachable in Duphaston. | The long view had said: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.07 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: Asset-light model — only about a third of manufacturing is in-house, the rest third-party — combined with brand-led leadership positions (53% thyroid, 40%+ vertigo) that protect returns.

*Unchanged: Convenience benefits; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Pricing, Mix and Volume; Gain in market share; Return on capital (headline); Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs; Halo effect (caution); Barriers to entry and rationality; Research and development; Share donators; Structural end-market growth; Assurance benefits; Independent, long-term and tenacious; Technology advantage; Geographical expansion; Cyclical market growth; Customer types (retail vs corporate); Out of limelight; Cost of working capital; Security by obscurity.*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 1 worsened, 6 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **New Entrants**: ELEVATED → WATCH. **That worsened:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. (The long view had said: The Duphaston episode is the classic pattern — cheaper challengers all importing the same molecule from China, with Abbott left defending its premium on source quality (Netherlands manufacturing) while the prescribing doctor controls substitution; on the OTC side an analyst directly flagged retailers' private labels and online discounting, and management's answer was to secure shelf presence and 'cross the bridge when we come to it'.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
