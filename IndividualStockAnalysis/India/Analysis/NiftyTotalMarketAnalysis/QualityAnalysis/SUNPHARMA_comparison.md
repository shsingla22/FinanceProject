- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Mix is genuinely improving — gross margin rose from 79.3% to ~81% on better product mix as innovative overtook generics in the US — but Indian growth is explicitly volume and new-launch led while peers take price. | The long view had said: Management explicitly disavows price-led growth and grows on volume and new launches, which is the opposite of the framework's preference, though a favourable mix shift to Innovative Medicines has lifted gross margin to ~80%.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Good → Excellent. Now: R&D at 5.4% of sales with 38% directed to innovation is visibly converting into products — Ilumya reached US$796mn (+16.7%), UNLOXCYT launched, psoriatic-arthritis sBLA accepted, and Innovative Medicines crossed $1bn in the US for the first time. | The long view had said: R&D held steadily at ~6-7% of sales with innovative R&D rising from 21% (FY23) to 37-45% of the pot, delivering LEQSELVI first-cycle approval, UNLOXCYT, Ilumya PsA filing and semaglutide, but guidance has repeatedly been missed on 'timing' grounds and much of the innovative pipeline is in-licensed rather than discovered.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Ilumya is now commercialised across 35 markets including a new India launch, Emerging Markets grew 17.4% and Rest of World 17.7% in Q4, and Organon adds a 140-country footprint. | The long view had said: Ilumya has been rolled out from 35 to 40 countries with emerging-market and RoW growth of 13.6% and 14.4% for FY26, and Organon adds China, South Korea and ten new markets — credible but partly bought rather than built.
- **Assurance benefits** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: The semaglutide launch was positioned on reliability of supply and completeness of strengths rather than price, which is how customers buying certainty are served. | The long view had said: Sun sells reliability and is prescribers' most-prescribed company, but the multi-year unresolved Halol FDA remediation is a direct blemish on the assurance proposition.

Now: Reported results are flattered by forex gains (INR4,268mn in Q4) and milestone income while the US business actually declined for the full year, and the Ranbaxy analogy is being used to pre-justify Organon — so results should not be read as proof of skill. | The long view had said: Reported results are materially flattered by items outside operating skill — a Rs.4,268mn forex gain, episodic lenalidomide sales and lumpy milestone income — so headline delivery should be discounted before crediting management.

Now: Parts of the end market are secular — women's health is a $35bn market growing 6-10% and GLP-1s open a new metabolic segment — but the core Indian market's volume growth of only 1.2-1.6% shows the base market is not in strong structural expansion. | The long view had said: End markets cited are in durable secular expansion — the Indian pharma market at Rs.2,450bn growing steadily, women's health at $35bn growing 6-10%, and a $70bn biosimilar opportunity from $320bn of patent expiries by 2035.

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 3 worsened, 5 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: This is the live mechanism in the results: US generics keep losing to cheaper entrants each quarter, India growth comes from volume in a branded-generic market where the prescriber, not the patient, picks the molecule, and the Organon deal buys a $-billion block of off-patent established brands (Zetia, Cozaar, Hyzaar — 55% of Organon revenue) that management itself describes as having 'no growth' — the classic post-exclusivity squeeze by good-enough substitutes. (The long view had said: A large slice of the business is itself exposed to cheaper-substitute economics: US generics face persistent price erosion and share loss, Cequa competes against a generic Restasis, and in India the prescribing doctor — not the patient — controls brand choice in a market of interchangeable branded generics; management's answer has been to migrate value into products that are harder to copy.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: NO SIGNAL → LOW. Why now: Demand is prescriber-driven and need-based, not attitude-driven, and where treatment preference is shifting (weight management/GLP-1) management pivoted the portfolio into it, launching semaglutide across all strengths for both chronic weight management and type 2 diabetes in Q4. (The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and the only computed fingerprint is clean.)
- **Fashion Risk**: NO SIGNAL → LOW. Why now: No growth in the window is credited to a trend, celebrity or fashionable product; growth is repeat-prescription volume across all major therapy areas, with No. 1 prescription rankings across 11–14 doctor categories and a portfolio spread over hundreds of brands rather than one taste-dependent bet. (The long view had said: the judge read the calls and found nothing, and all 2 computed fingerprints are clean.)

*Unchanged: Technological Innovation (WATCH); Government Dependency (WATCH); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 3 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
