# Praj Industries (PRAJIND) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Praj Industries is a Pune-based engineering company that designs, builds and equips plants that turn crops, farm waste and other organic material into fuels and chemicals — mainly ethanol distilleries, compressed biogas (CBG) plants, and increasingly ethanol-to-jet sustainable aviation fuel (SAF). It sells to sugar mills, grain-based ethanol producers, oil marketing companies like IOCL, breweries, pharma companies and industrial customers, taking large lump-sum project and equipment orders that it books into an order backlog (Rs. 40.44 billion as of June 2024) and recognises as revenue as it executes; it also earns recurring income from servicing the more than 1,000 plants worldwide already running on its technology. Alongside bioenergy it runs an engineering arm that fabricates modular process equipment and skids (carbon capture, blue hydrogen, data-centre cooling) and water-treatment/zero-liquid-discharge systems, plus a high-purity systems business for pharma. Roughly two-thirds to three-quarters of sales come from India, with exports around a quarter, spanning the US, Brazil, Europe, Southeast Asia, Africa and Latin America.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Bioenergy — 1G ethanol (domestic and international)** — Its core and largest business, supplying the technology and plants that make ethanol from sugarcane syrup/molasses, grain and other starchy feedstock for India's petrol-blending programme and for overseas markets like Brazil and the US; bioenergy as a whole was 72% of revenue in Q1 FY25, though management says greenfield ethanol orders slowed sharply in FY26 and shifted toward brownfield efficiency upgrades, distillers corn oil (DCO) add-ons and ENA plants.
- **2G / advanced biofuels** — Plants that make ethanol from agricultural residue such as straw and stubble — the flagship being IOCL's Panipat plant unveiled in 2022 — described as strategically important and a door-opener in Europe, but still small and early-stage with commissioning and ramp-up challenges.
- **CBG (compressed biogas)** — Plants converting press mud, Napier grass, rice straw and other farm waste into biogas for vehicle and piped gas use; management calls it a slow but steadily building business, with a healthy enquiry pipeline and supportive government blending mandates but repeated delays in orders being finalised.
- **SAF / ethanol-to-jet** — Technology to convert ethanol into sustainable aviation fuel, currently at the demo-plant and basic-engineering-order stage for US and international customers; no meaningful revenue yet, but positioned as a major future driver once blending mandates arrive around 2027.
- **Services / Lifecycle Services** — Operations and maintenance, 'performance enhancer' upgrades and biogenic CO2 capture sold back into its installed base of over 1,000 plants; a steadily growing, higher-margin annuity-style stream feeding off decades of past project sales.
- **Engineering — ETCA / Praj GenX modular fabrication** — Builds modularised process equipment and skids at its Kandla and Mangalore facilities for carbon capture, blue hydrogen, SAF projects, traditional energy and now data-centre cooling; part of the 'engineering' bucket that was about 20% of Q1 FY25 revenue and 38% of that quarter's order intake, and which saw a sharp slowdown in FY26 before winning a first CCUS skid order from a global oil major.
- **Zero Liquid Discharge (ZLD) / water treatment** — Effluent treatment, water recycling and zero-liquid-discharge systems for industrial clients, with recent wins including a Rs.100 crore-plus integrated order from a metal major and new opportunities in semiconductors, batteries and solar manufacturing.
- **Brewery** — Supplies brewing plants and capacity expansions in India and Africa; a smaller, steady line, with a recent greenfield Indian brewery contract valued at over Rs.100 crores.
- **PHS / Praj HiPurity Systems** — High-purity water systems, modular process systems, large-capacity fermenters and precision fermentation equipment for pharmaceutical and biotech customers; the smallest reported segment at roughly 8-10% of revenue and order intake.
- **Biopolymers / RCM (research and commercialisation)** — An R&D-stage effort to make lactic acid and lactide for biodegradable plastics, run through a demonstration plant at Jejuri and university tie-ups; described as a milestone in technology terms rather than a current revenue contributor.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Praj Industries is a founder-led builder of ethanol, compressed biogas and related process plants that has done the hard part of engineering well and the commercial part poorly. The balance-sheet and stewardship checks are the clear strengths: customers pay in ahead of suppliers, so cash comes back eight days before it goes out and the business is effectively funded by its own customers; there is Rs 6.12bn of net cash; the strategy is purely organic with no acquisitions; and management kept spending on GenX and R&D right through a three-year earnings downturn. Reach has widened too, with exports going from 17% of revenue in Q2FY23 to 36% in FY26 and entries into Brazil, the US, Europe, Southeast Asia and Africa, though many international wins are feasibility or basic engineering rather than full plants. Against that, the framework scores Return on Capital at zero and Competitive Advantage, Growth and Management all around a third, which is the tension in the whole story: the company builds genuinely new things and holds a rare working-capital advantage, yet very little of it has turned into profit.

The patterns screen tells the same story from a different angle, and the two ideas it accepts are the two that also create the biggest risks. Innovation Dominance is a likely fit because Praj has produced a steady run of self-developed technologies — 2G cellulosic at IOCL Panipat, India's first indigenous lactic acid and lactide at Jejuri, CBG from press mud, Napier grass and rice straw, an ethanol-to-jet demonstration, Bio-IBA — and the R&D check credits exactly this. But that same pipeline is why Technological Innovation is flagged as an elevated risk: the winning pathway in the energy transition is unsettled, so the company must keep re-platforming, and the R&D check itself notes that turning these platforms into revenue and margin has been slow. Recurring Revenue is the other likely fit, resting on aftermarket and Lifecycle Services across an installed base of more than 1,000 plants that kept growing through the FY26 downturn — and that same installed base is named as the silver lining to the elevated Cyclicality risk. Four patterns come out only partial (Brand Strength, Market Share Gainers, Corporate Culture, Cost to Replicate), largely because the supporting numbers are never disclosed: no share figures, no segmental split, no sizing of the services business. Five patterns do not fit at all, and Pricing Power fails outright, with the evidence running the other way.

That missing pricing power is the hinge between the strengths and the risks. Margins have barely moved in either direction (±1.9 points), which the barriers check reads as rational pricing and few new entrants, and the New Entrants risk shows no signal at all — but the flip side is that technology leadership has bought no ability to charge more. Contracts are predominantly 100% fixed-price, so raw-material and site-cost escalation lands on Praj, margins were stuck below 8% in Q2FY23, and FY26 profit before tax fell to Rs 763mn from Rs 2,704mn while ticket sizes shrank toward smaller brownfield jobs. The demand side carries the other big exposure: end markets in biofuels, CBG, SAF and CCUS are in policy-backed secular expansion, but that is precisely why Government Dependency is rated high risk — the FY25 order slowdown came purely from a policy change, and the cyclical check reads the company as sitting near a trough after the EBP-20 build-out finished, with the next leg dependent on E22–E30 blends, E85/E100 retail, CBG mandates and a 2027 SAF mandate arriving. Customer concentration is low and overall resilience is judged sound, and management was candid about three years of stagnation while still declining to give the segmental numbers analysts ask for. Put together — 60 on business quality, 26 on multibagger fit, 56 on risk safety — the combined score is 49 out of 100, a Mixed two-star verdict: a technically strong, well-financed, honestly run company whose earnings depend on someone else's policy calendar and whose innovation has not yet earned a price premium.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

### Government Dependency — HIGH RISK
*"Fortunes decided in Delhi"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure; this risk has no numeric fingerprint to cross-check, so it is judged from the calls alone.

**What the calls show:** Virtually every call opens with policy: EBP-20/E20 mandates, Union Budget outlays, BIS E22-E100 notifications, CBG blending mandates, IRA 45(Z), import duties and SAF blending policy. The FY25 order slowdown was caused purely by policy — the withdrawal of Molasses B/syrup and FCI rice as permitted feedstocks — and management explicitly expects recovery only 'after announcement of higher blending mandates', with PSU/OMC counterparties (IOCL) prominent.

> "prospective customers await for a clear long-term direction to emerge on clearance to use Molasses B/Syrup as feedstocks and removal of embargo on issue of rice from FCI" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Partial diversification into non-policy demand — PHS/high-purity fermenters, ZLD for metals and semiconductors, brewery, and data-centre cooling modules*

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Praj sells ethanol/CBG plants tied directly to customer capex: the EBP-20 build-out drove revenue from Rs.532cr to Rs.876cr a quarter by FY23, and once blending capacity was met greenfield ordering collapsed — FY26 PBT fell to Rs.763mn from Rs.2,704mn and Q3FY26 PAT went negative, with management itself conceding 'three years of stagnation'. Execution cycles stretching on funding-starved customer projects and fixed-price contracts amplify the margin swing.

> "capacity that was required for blending the ethanol in petrol has been achieved and so there is a reduction in the number of plants for ethanol" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Growing operations-linked flow revenue — Lifecycle Services, performance enhancers, brownfield efficiency and DCO retrofits on an installed base of 1,000+ plants; countercyclical investment in the GenX facility through the downturn*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 3 of the last 11 years with swings of ±28 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.

## What to watch
