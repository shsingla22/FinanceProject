# Canara HSBC (CANHLIFE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Canara HSBC Life Insurance is an Indian life insurance company (IRDAI Regn No. 136), listed in FY26 on the NSE and BSE under the symbol CANHLIFE, that sells long-term life insurance policies to retail customers across India — including Tier-2, Tier-3 and Tier-4 towns — as well as group cover tied to bank loans. Its products span unit-linked savings plans (ULIPs) that invest customers' money in equity and debt funds, traditional par and non-par savings plans, annuity/retirement plans, and pure protection (term) cover, with small add-on 'riders' attached to boost protection. It earns money by collecting first-year and renewal premiums, charging fees such as fund management charges on unit-linked assets, and investing the resulting pool — assets under management were roughly Rs. 44,000 crores as of September 2025 — with profitability tracked as value of new business (Rs. 627 crores in FY26 at a 22.4% margin), profit after tax (Rs. 127 crores in FY26) and embedded value (Rs. 7,233 crores). It sells almost entirely through banks — mainly Canara Bank branches plus a partnership with HSBC — and ranked 9th among Indian private life insurers in FY26 with about a 2.6% share of the private market.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Linked business (ULIP)** — Unit-linked savings plans where the customer's premium is invested in market funds for wealth creation; the calls say it is roughly half the business (about 50-51% of the FY26 mix) and grew 23% year-on-year over the first nine months of FY26, though a fall in equity markets hurt embedded value through lower future fund management charges.
- **Traditional / non-linked business (par and non-par)** — Guaranteed-style savings policies that do not move with markets; management says the mix shifted sharply toward these in Q4 FY26 (81% of that quarter's sales versus 39% up to December 2025), with par and non-par together growing 15% year-on-year in FY26 and making up 28% of total APE.
- **Annuity (retirement) business** — Retirement products, with a stated focus on deferred annuity, that pay customers a regular income later in life; annuity APE grew 24% year-on-year in FY26 and is about 14-16% of the overall business, described as profitable and giving long-term earnings visibility.
- **Individual / retail protection** — Pure life cover sold to individual customers, growing from a small base but tripling quarter-on-quarter in Q3 FY26 as the GST exemption on life premiums made it cheaper; it is a key driver of margin improvement.
- **Group credit life** — Cover sold alongside loans that repays the borrower's outstanding loan if they die — a fast-growing, profitable segment, up over 40% year-on-year in H1 FY26, about 50% in Q3 FY26 and 40% in Q4 FY26; total protection (individual plus credit life) rose to 7% of FY26 business from 4% in FY25.
- **Riders attached to ULIPs** — Small add-on protection covers bolted onto unit-linked policies, attached to about 70% of eligible policies but only around 5% of unit-linked premium — small in money terms, but management repeatedly credits them with lifting ULIP margins.
- **Bancassurance distribution (Canara Bank and HSBC)** — The main sales engine: policies sold through Canara Bank branches, with deeper penetration into Tier-3 and Tier-4 markets using customer data analytics, plus an HSBC partnership that brings a niche, better-persistency customer segment.
- **Agency channel** — A newly launched own-sales-force channel started 1 October 2025, with about 500 distributors onboarded and Rs. 14 crores of APE collected in under six months; management targets roughly 5% of business from it over three years.
- **Alternate channels** — Distribution outside the two parent banks and agency, which grew 29% year-on-year and contributes about 9% of weighted premium income, with a stated goal of reaching 15% over the next three years.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Canara HSBC comes out of all three reviews with the same single explanation for why it works: it does not have to go find its customers. Around 70% of its annual premium comes through Canara Bank branches and another 14-15% through HSBC, with analytics pointing staff at customers most likely to buy. That one trait is what earns the top marks in the quality framework for distribution advantage and customer convenience, and it is also what makes four separate multibagger patterns fit strongly — the Friendly Middleman (an intermediary recommends, the customer pays), Market Share Gainers (individual premium growth of 19-20% against industry growth of 10%, private market share up 20 basis points to 2.6%, rank improving from 10th to 9th), Low Price Plus (expense ratio down 20.5% to 19% to 18.7%, or 17.5% before the GST and labour-code effects, with commissions below industry), and Recurring Revenue, where the renewal book has compounded 29%, 34% and 25% year-on-year while 13-month persistency climbed from 82.5% to roughly 86%. Management scores well too: guidance on premium growth, product mix and the GST drag was given and then met, mistakes were corrected on the call, growth has been funded organically with a modest INR250 crore of subordinated debt rather than share issuance, and solvency sits near 190%.

The uncomfortable part is that the very same dependence that produces those patterns is the largest business risk. Stakeholder concentration is rated elevated precisely because under 15% of business comes from anywhere other than the two promoter banks, and analysts pressed on both a possible change in HSBC's stake and the chance that LIC takes more of the shelf inside Canara Bank branches. The regulator's open-architecture push, which would let more insurers sell through those same branches, is a watch-level risk aimed squarely at that chokepoint — and management's answer, that LIC is already present with 'minuscule' share, is a defence of access rather than of product economics. The 'cost to replicate' and 'brand strength' patterns only partially fit for the same reason: the hard-to-copy asset is borrowed access to someone else's counter and a decade of low-cost operations, not a name customers seek out or pay more for. Pricing power is explicitly absent — management said the GST change permanently re-bases margins and cannot be recovered through price, and that gains from the yield curve will be passed back to customers. So margin defence rests entirely on cost discipline and product mix, which is also the honest reason the Low Price Plus pattern and the 'good enough goods' risk are two readings of one fact. The company's own answer to the concentration problem is the Forward Integrators pattern in its early stage: an agency channel launched 1 October 2025 across 104 branches with 500 distributors and Rs 14 crores of premium in under six months, and alternate channels at 9% of premium targeted at 15% over three years.

The third thread is government, which shows up as both the biggest tailwind and the highest-rated risk. Under-penetration, the GST exemption and the 'insurance for all by 2047' push give the top mark for structural end-market growth and underpin the low ratings on shifting consumer preferences and fashion risk — demand here is functional and contractual, evidenced by 99.6% claim settlement, an NPS of 76 rising to 80, and improving persistency in every cohort. But the same policy machinery is what reset the economics: the GST exemption removed input tax credit and permanently lowered value-of-new-business margin by roughly 185-225 basis points, the new labour code cost INR9 crores, and further items are pending. Cyclicality is elevated for a related reason — with roughly half to 60% of premium in unit-linked products, results move with equity markets, which cost about INR820 million of negative economic variance in FY26, though the growing renewal base and a demonstrated ability to shift mix (traditional products at 81% of fourth-quarter premium, protection from 4% to 7%, annuity premium up 24%) cushion it. That combination is why the numbers land where they do: strong pattern fit at 85 and sound resilience, pulled down by weaker quality marks on lumpy year-to-year growth despite a ~25% average and a headline return of only ₹7 of operating cash per ₹100 of capital, and by middling risk safety at 52 — blending to 70 out of 100, a Strong four-star rating rather than a top one.

## What to watch

- Stakeholder concentration: any change in HSBC's stake or a visible rise in LIC's share of the Canara Bank counter would hit ~85% of the premium base at once.
