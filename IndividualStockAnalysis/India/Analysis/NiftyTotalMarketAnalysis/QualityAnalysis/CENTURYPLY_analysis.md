# Century Plyboard (CENTURYPLY) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Century Plyboards (India) Limited is an Indian building-materials and interior-products manufacturer that makes and sells branded wood panels — plywood, decorative laminates, MDF (medium-density fibreboard) and particleboard — used in home and office interiors, furniture and fit-outs. It earns money by manufacturing these boards in its own plants and selling them through a dealer/distribution network to the trade (dealers, retailers and carpenters), supported by heavy brand-building spend such as IPL advertising, with brands including Century and the value brand 'Sainik'; a laminate line at Andhra is also aimed at exports. Operations are almost entirely in India, with plants at Hoshiarpur (Punjab), Andhra Pradesh (Badvel), Chennai, Kandla, Guwahati and Joka, plus a large new INR 1,100 crore project announced for Uttar Pradesh; overseas arms such as a Singapore subsidiary were sold off in April 2024. Consolidated revenue reached a record INR 1,492 crores in the March 2026 quarter (FY26 revenue up 19.2%, EBITDA INR 702 crores, PAT INR 268 crores), and management has stated a long-term ambition of INR 12,000 crores of revenue by FY31.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Plywood** — The core and biggest business — making and selling branded plywood (including premium and the mass-market 'Sainik' range) — at INR 710 crores of revenue in the Dec-2025 quarter it is roughly half of company sales, with EBITDA margins around 15-16% and capacity being expanded toward ~430,000 CBM plus new lines at Hoshiarpur, Chennai and UP.
- **Laminate** — Decorative laminate sheets used as surfacing for furniture and interiors, including compact and larger-size laminates for export from the newer Andhra plant; revenue was INR 183 crores in the Dec-2025 quarter, and margins recovered to 8.5% for FY26 from 5.2% a year earlier after a weak couple of years.
- **MDF (medium-density fibreboard)** — Engineered wood panels sold as a plywood alternative for furniture, made at Hoshiarpur and a large new South India plant at Badvel (Andhra), costing about INR 730 crores; it is the fastest-growing segment with FY26 revenue up 25.5% and margins of 12.7%, running at 80-85% utilisation with capacity heading toward roughly 625,000 cubic metres a year.
- **Particle board** — Lower-cost boards made from wood particles, largely sold as pre-laminated panels; the smallest board segment at INR 65 crores in the Dec-2025 quarter, it grew 38.2% in FY26 off a new continuous-process plant but is only just around breakeven on profits.
- **PBC board (Andhra)** — A newer board line started at the Andhra location in May 2024 that management said would add close to INR 100 crores of revenue within a year.
- **Century Ports** — A wholly owned subsidiary that began commercial operations in Q4 FY26 running the rejuvenated Khidderpore Docks (KPD-1 West) at Kolkata port — a diversification into port logistics and infrastructure that management expects to turn cash positive in Q1 FY27.

### The story in depth

Century Plyboard scores well on the things that are within its own control and poorly on the things that are not. The quality framework gives full marks to Growth, Management and Customer Benefits: sales have compounded at about 12% a year, the family leadership team has been stable across all 16 calls and has been willing to disclose bad news, and the company keeps about ₹49 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs. The multibagger screen agrees, calling Brand Strength and Corporate Culture strong fits and Market Share Gainers a likely fit, with plywood volumes up 14.9% in one quarter while the industry struggled, FY26 revenue up 19.2%, and only about 7% of a ₹20,000cr plywood market held so far. But Industry Structure and Return on Capital both score zero, and the risk review rates overall resilience as STRESSED. The combined rating of 53 out of 100 — Decent — is essentially the arithmetic of a good operator inside a hard industry.

The same three traits keep showing up on both sides of the ledger. First, brand spending: the deliberate, front-loaded advertising (the IPL campaign cost roughly 1.5% of the plywood margin in one quarter) is what earns the Brand Strength verdict and the repeated price increases — 2-3%, then 4% across FY25, then 7% in April 2026 — passed through without losing volume. Yet the risk review flags Good Enough Goods as HIGH RISK precisely because the buying decision sits with carpenters, contractors and dealers rather than the homeowner, growth is admitted to be concentrated in the cheaper non-premium tier, and MDF and particleboard have drifted toward commodities where the cheapest capacity sets the price. Second, the aggressive growth capex — roughly ₹730cr in Andhra MDF, an ₹1,100cr UP project, laminates, a continuous-press particleboard line, Hoshiarpur and Chennai — is what makes the share-gain and structural-growth stories credible, and it is also why returns on capital score zero and why ROCE has fallen. Third, the very capacity build-out that lets Century take share is what other players are doing too: unprecedented capacity addition pushed MDF and particleboard margins from 35-36% down to roughly 12-14%, which is why New Entrants and Cyclicality are both ELEVATED and why Cost to Replicate is only a partial fit — the moat held for plywood but a well-funded entrant compressed panel returns within a couple of years.

What holds the story together is that Century is positioned on both sides of the shift it is exposed to. Demand is moving from plywood toward engineered panels and from unbranded to branded, and because the company sells plywood, MDF, particleboard, laminates and pre-laminated product, that migration reads as a mix question rather than a threat — MDF grew 25.5% and particleboard 38.2% in FY26 while plywood still grew 15.6%, so Shifting Consumer Preferences and Fashion Risk both come out LOW, as does Stakeholder Concentration given the widening tier-2 and tier-3 dealer base and sourcing spread across India, Brazil and Vietnam. The weaker links are structural rather than operational: about 131 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers; MDF economics lean visibly on import barriers that management can only say it believes will stay; and process-technology shifts have already stranded owned assets, with the multi-daylight particleboard plant shut and being scrapped. The honest read is a well-run, well-branded company with a long runway against weak competition, whose returns currently depend on panel pricing recovering enough to justify the money already spent.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Good Enough Goods, Cyclicality, New Entrants. The balance sheet and cash engine shows multiple financial stress signals — 2 of the 3 stress checks are flagged — multiple warning lights at once.

- ✅ Borrowings are 0.7× shareholders' funds — the balance sheet is not the problem.
- ⚠️ Only about 43% of recent operating profit became cash (was 77%) — profits are getting stuck in paper, an early stress signal.
- ⚠️ Returns on capital slid from about 25% to 13% — the business is earning less and less on the money invested in it.

### Good Enough Goods — HIGH RISK
*"Cheaper rivals that are good enough"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 1 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Growth is admittedly concentrated in the cheaper non-premium tier while the premium tier stagnates, MDF and particleboard have become near-commodities where price is set by the cheapest capacity, and the purchase is intermediated by carpenters, contractors and dealers whom the company must court directly — precisely the Nobel Biocare mechanism of a low-brand-awareness end customer and an intermediary controlling choice.

> "generally you have seen that the premium segment growth is lower the non- premium segment growth is quite high" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Brand spend and dealer/contractor loyalty programmes sustain a plywood price premium at 15-16% EBITDA, and better raw-material linkages give a genuine cost edge over unorganized challengers*

- ✅ The share of each sale kept after direct input costs held near 48% (was 49%) — no gross-level price erosion.
- ✅ Operating margin (12%) remains near its historical peak (18%) — no collapse in the premium.
- ⚠️ Operating margin drifted down from about 17% to 12% — steady profit erosion, the mark of rising competitive pressure.

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear exposure, but none of the 3 computed fingerprints confirms it in the numbers yet — held one step below the top severity.

**What the calls show:** Segment economics swing violently with capacity and input cycles: MDF/particleboard printed 35-36% EBITDA margins in Q1FY23 on zero imports and no new capacity, then collapsed to ~12-14% by FY25-26 amid what management called 'unprecedented capacity addition'; MD himself frames the industry in steel/cement capacity-cycle terms and concedes recurring multi-year price fights, while timber, phenol and melamine costs oscillate through every year of the calls.

> "if you look at steel industry, it's not passing through a good phase, but lot of addition is coming... every 2 year or so, there will be a fight for 2, 3 years; and again 2 years will be nice" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Plywood is a branded, renovation/flow-linked product with 15%+ margins and stated downside protection ('it will not go too low'), and the company keeps investing counter-cyclically (Andhra, Hoshiarpur, UP, Orissa) while peers hold back*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 11 years with swings of ±13 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 11% and 18% (±2.5 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 9% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 22%).

**What the calls show:** Panel capacity from new and small players has reset pricing twice in the window — particleboard 'under pressure' from a number of small players in FY25, and MDF prices driven to where an analyst calculated the industry's capital employed earns less than a fixed deposit; management's stated response is to pre-empt entrants by building capacity first, which is itself a margin-investing response.

### Government Dependency — WATCH
