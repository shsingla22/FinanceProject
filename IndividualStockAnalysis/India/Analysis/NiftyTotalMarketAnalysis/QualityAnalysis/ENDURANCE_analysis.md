# Endurance Technologies Ltd. (ENDURANCE) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: AUTOMOBILE*

Endurance Technologies is an Indian auto-components maker that manufactures and sells parts that go into vehicles — mainly braking systems (disc brakes, brake discs, ABS), suspension (front forks and shock absorbers), transmission parts such as clutches, and aluminium die castings and alloy wheels. It sells these directly to vehicle manufacturers (OEMs) under supply contracts won through a request-for-quote process, so revenue comes from per-part supply on programs that ramp from a 'start of production' date to peak annual sales a few years later; its Indian customers are chiefly two-wheeler makers — Bajaj Auto (its largest and anchor customer), Hero MotoCorp, HMSI/Honda, TVS, Royal Enfield, Suzuki, Yamaha and EV players like Ather — plus three- and four-wheeler makers such as Tata Motors, Mahindra, Hyundai, Kia and Isuzu. Roughly three-quarters of group income comes from Indian operations and about a quarter from European operations (largely Italy/Germany, serving passenger-car makers with castings and tooling). Manufacturing is concentrated in Maharashtra (Waluj/Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar, Chakan-Pune, AURIC Shendra and Bidkin) with newer plants in Tamil Nadu (Chennai, Vallam), alongside European plants.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Braking systems (2W disc brake assemblies, discs, ABS)** — Makes disc brake assemblies, brake discs, master cylinders and calipers for two-wheelers, plus anti-lock braking systems; management calls it a key growth engine — capacity is being built toward 7.6 million disc brake assemblies and 8.6 million discs a year (including a new 3-million-unit Chennai plant), ABS capacity is being expanded from 0.64 million to 1.84 million units a year on expected regulations mandating ABS on smaller bikes, and the company is backward-integrating into in-house electronic control units to raise margins.
- **Suspension (front forks, inverted front forks, shock absorbers)** — Supplies conventional and premium 'inverted' front forks and rear mono-shocks for motorcycles and scooters, including high-end units developed with partner KTM AG and exported to KTM Austria; it is one of the biggest repeat sources of new order wins, with individual OEM programs worth Rs 250–1,650 million a year from HMSI, Hero MotoCorp, Suzuki and TVS.
- **Transmission / clutch assemblies** — Makes clutch assemblies for motorcycles and continuously variable transmissions (automatic clutches) for scooters, including a shift from cork-based to paper-based clutches and entry into 200cc-plus premium bike clutches using know-how from its Italian acquisitions; it is a smaller line than brakes and suspension and mainly a value-add/mix-improvement story.
- **Aluminium die casting and machining (including four-wheeler castings)** — Casts and fully machines aluminium parts for two-, three- and four-wheelers, increasingly finished parts rather than raw castings; this is the main way the company sells into car makers, with the new AURIC Shendra plant alone carrying cumulative orders worth about Rs 513 crore of peak annual sales (from a large US EV maker, Jaguar Land Rover, Yazaki and Valeo), plus Chakan work for Tata Motors and Mahindra growing 35–40% in a year and Chennai/Vallam plants serving Hyundai, Kia, Isuzu and Ather.
- **Alloy wheels (two-wheeler)** — A newer plant at AURIC Bidkin making aluminium alloy wheels for two-wheelers, which management says was 100% booked on capacity before it even started production in October 2025 — first for Bajaj Auto, with Royal Enfield, Suzuki, TVS, Yamaha and Ather following through FY27.
- **Driveshafts** — A newer, higher-technology product launched at Waluj that transmits engine torque to the wheels for three-wheelers, four-wheelers and some light commercial vehicles; management describes it as a proprietary, 'EV-agnostic' additional revenue stream, with orders from Bajaj, Mahindra and TVS.
- **Maxwell Energy Systems (electronics / Battery Management Systems)** — A subsidiary being bought out in stages (61.5% owned as of mid-2024, moving to 100% by FY27) that makes battery management systems and motor control units for electric two- and three-wheelers and battery packs, supplying over 70 OEMs and battery-pack makers across 15 countries; it had won about Rs 4,242 million of business since FY22 and was targeted to exceed Rs 2,500 million of sales in FY27.
- **Electric-vehicle business (group-wide)** — Not a separate factory so much as EV-specific orders across brakes, suspension, castings and electronics, plus a new battery-pack assembly plant near Pune starting production around March–April 2026; cumulative EV order wins were Rs 8,421 million as of Q1 FY25 (from HMSI, Ather, Bajaj, Hero, Mahindra, Tata, TVS and Aptiv) on top of Maxwell's own wins.
- **European operations (Endurance Overseas — Italy and Germany)** — Supplies aluminium castings and related tooling to European passenger-car makers, run under a separate CEO, and contributes roughly 24–25% of consolidated income; it has been bolted on to via Italian acquisitions — Gremica and Adler (2020, premium two-wheeler brakes and clutches), Frenotecnica (2022, brake friction materials with the 'Brenta' aftermarket brand) and Velcoli (fleet-efficiency software), intended to form a European centre of excellence for premium two-wheeler components and aftermarket sales.

### The story in depth

Endurance Technologies earns a 60-out-of-100 "Decent" rating, and the three analyses agree on why: the operating business is genuinely well run, but very little of what it does is hard for someone else to copy. On the quality side the numbers are steady and clean — ₹13 of operating profit per ₹100 of sales with margins moving only about ±1.4 points a year, a 23% cash return on the capital employed, and a rare working-capital position where cash comes back from customers six days before suppliers have to be paid, so customers effectively fund the business. Management is the other clear strength: promoter-MD Anurang Jain has led throughout, and the multi-year programmes promised back in 2022 — ABS scale-up, driveshafts, aluminium forging backward integration, inverted front forks — were actually delivered on roughly the stated timelines. That credibility is what makes the growth plan believable: capex is clearly for growth rather than upkeep, with four new plants (Chennai brakes, AURIC Shendra castings, AURIC Bidkin alloy wheels, Talegaon battery packs) and India capex up about a third year on year.

Out of eleven multibagger patterns only one is a clear fit — Market Share Gainers — and it is the same fact the quality framework scores highest: verifiable share capture beyond the anchor customer, with new content at HMSI, Hero, TVS, Suzuki, Royal Enfield, Kawasaki, Piaggio and Ather, cumulative non-Bajaj wins of ₹5,323 crores since FY22, and EV sales compounding at 71% a year against a 21% industry rate. Everything else is partial or absent, and the reason is a single structural trait that cuts both ways. Endurance wins business one vehicle platform at a time, from one OEM at a time, after long validation cycles. That is exactly what produces the share gains and what gives the Cost to Replicate pattern its partial credit — but because nothing about the offer improves as more customers join, network effects score the worst mark on the sheet (-2), and the whole Competitive Advantage area comes out at zero. The same trait explains the three outright misses: Toll Roads fails because OEMs negotiate hard over parts that are a meaningful slice of a two-wheeler's cost; Pricing Power fails because prices are set in OEM contracts with raw-material increases recovered only one to two quarters late, and management concedes EV prices fall as volumes rise; Forward Integrators fails because the strategy runs deliberately backward — forgings, braided hoses, ABS valves and now in-house ECUs — with the aftermarket still sold through third-party distributors.

The risk review is reassuring in level but not in kind: no risk is rated high, six sit at WATCH and two at LOW, and overall resilience is judged SOUND — yet the watch-list items are mostly the flip side of the strengths rather than separate problems. Being a validated supplier to powerful buyers with no end-consumer brand is what makes the Good Enough Goods risk live and what keeps standalone margins structurally modest at roughly 12.5–13.5% EBITDA and about 7% net; it is also why Brand Strength only scores partial. The Bajaj relationship is so central that every order-win figure across all sixteen calls is quoted excluding Bajaj — that disclosure habit is the clearest evidence both of the concentration risk and of the deliberate broadening that is fixing it. The strong Structural end-market growth score leans on the same policy decisions that create Government Dependency: the draft ABS mandate underpins a 1.2 million-unit capacity plan, and Maharashtra incentives flow straight into profit, though management says the ABS project proceeds regardless of the final rules. And the EV shift is simultaneously the biggest source of share gain and the Technological Innovation risk, since the electronics bets sit in a fast-moving segment management itself describes as growing slowly. Cyclicality is real — Q1 FY23 saw profits shrink on a 24% commodity swing — but the four new plants are being built through the cycle rather than into a peak. The honest read: a well-managed, cash-efficient, share-gaining supplier whose economics are set by its customers, which is why business quality scores 68 and risk safety 76 while multibagger fit scores only 33.

### Cyclicality — WATCH
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure while the numbers stay quiet.

**What the calls show:** Demand is tied to OEM two-wheeler/PV production volumes (an operations/flow linkage, not customer capex), but the calls show real cyclical damage transmitted through commodity inputs and auto-cycle swings: Q1 FY23 saw consolidated EBITDA de-grow 4.9% on a 24% revenue rise purely from aluminium (+41%) and steel (+20%) prices, and FY26 standalone EBITDA margin still sits at 12.6% versus a 20% topline rise, with pass-through lagging one to two quarters. Aluminium and steel are >80% of purchases, and India capex has scaled up sharply (₹600cr to ₹800cr, similar planned FY27) into an up-cycle.

> "We feel we lost at least 3% in RMC in the first quarter due to high metal prices" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/operations-linked demand (parts consumed per vehicle built, not customer capex programmes), plus a deliberately countercyclical build-out — four greenfield plants and aftermarket targeted at 10% of India sales — and a growing replacement/aftermarket book that is independent of OEM build rates.*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 2 of the last 11 years with swings of ±10 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 12% and 16% (±1.4 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ✅ Capital spending stayed near 8% of sales with no runaway expansion year (peak 10%).
