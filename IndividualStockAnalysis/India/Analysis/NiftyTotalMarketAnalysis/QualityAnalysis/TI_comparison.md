**Checks that improved:**

- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹18 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±0 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹18 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±14.7 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Excellent. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±0.0 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Licensing, excise registration and distribution create high structural entry barriers, but pricing rationality is compromised by state-mandated price cuts and aggressively priced MML in Maharashtra, plus incumbents crowding into brandy. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±14.7 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Licensing and label registration keep new entrants rare and ownership stable, but pricing rationality is imperfect — TI had to cut prices to match competition in Andhra, and a new state-created MML category shrank its Maharashtra prestige segment by 25%.
- **Geographical expansion** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Execution of geographic expansion is concrete and repeated — Monarch Legacy into 6 states plus Hyderabad Duty Free, Mansion House Whisky into the Northeast, West Bengal, Odisha, Telangana, Puducherry and Kerala, Spaceman exports, and a step-change to pan-India reach via Imperial Blue. | The long view had said: Expansion has been real and executed — from a 14-state, South-concentrated brandy player to pan-India via Imperial Blue, plus Assam whisky, exports to Middle East/Africa/SE Asia and Monarch in international markets — though the pan-India leap was bought rather than built.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: Distribution reach and availability are the company's stated core asset and the explicit rationale for the Spaceman and Imperial Blue arrangements — pan-India access across 27 states plus international markets that partners cannot replicate. | The long view had said: Distribution reach and availability are explicitly the company's chosen route to consumer access, used to launch Mansion House Whisky in Assam and to carry Spaceman's gin and vodka, now extended to a 200 million+ case opportunity.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: The company sits near the bottom of a state-policy cycle with visible upturn — Maharashtra's IMFL industry down ~20% post-MML but already growing sequentially, alongside Andhra's retail privatization and excise-duty reductions driving industry volumes. | The long view had said: Alcobev demand is not presented as cyclical; the swing factors are regulatory (Karnataka excise cut, AP route-to-market change, Maharashtra MML shrinking the prestige segment 25%, Telangana receivable delays) and cut in both directions with no identifiable cycle bottom.
- **Distribution to shareholders** (Capital Allocation): Weak → Neutral. Now: A token Re 1 per share dividend was declared while capital was raised, not returned — Rs 2,296 crore of preferential equity and warrants diluted shareholders to fund the deal, and no buyback policy exists. | The long view had said: Distribution policy is thin and dilution-heavy: repeated preferential issues (INR 311 crore, then INR 2,093 crore) with no rights issue for retail holders, no buybacks, and only a token first dividend of INR 1 per share in FY26.

- **Security by obscurity** (Industry Structure): Good → Weak. Now: TI is no longer under the radar — a EUR 413 million acquisition from Pernod Ricard, a Rs 2,296 crore institutional fundraise and heavy sell-side coverage on every call put it squarely in view of competitors and capital. | The long view had said: Brandy was a neglected, low-prestige niche that larger players and capital ignored, which is precisely where TI built leadership, though the Imperial Blue deal has now moved the company firmly into the spotlight.
- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Good → Neutral. Now: There is no R&D spend or intensity disclosed; product development shows up only as an NPD pipeline and new launches (Seven Islands Pure Malt, Monarch Legacy, Mansion House Whisky relaunch) whose commercial yield is not yet quantified. | The long view had said: No formal R&D line, but a consistent new-product pipeline has delivered commercially — Flandy (India's first premium flavoured brandy), Monarch Legacy luxury brandy, Mansion House Whisky and Seven Islands Pure Malt — with management still describing a live NPD pipeline.

Now: Headline results are substantially flattered by factors outside management skill — subsidy income, favourable excise-policy changes and Andhra retail privatization, plus one month/one quarter of acquired IB revenue — so the reported growth overstates demonstrated operating capability, even though adjusted figures are provided. | The long view had said: Results are partly flattered by factors outside management skill — government subsidy income materially inflating EBITDA and a Karnataka excise duty cut driving 20%+ volume growth — although management does candidly attribute the Karnataka gain to the government rather than itself.
- **Share donators** (Industry Structure): Excellent → Good. Now: There is an explicit pool of weak donors — local/regional players in Andhra ceding share to pan-India entrants — plus a divesting MNC whose neglected Imperial Blue brand TI believes it can restore, though the remaining competitors (USL, Radico) are strong. | The long view had said: TI's growth has come squarely from under-investing rivals — the brandy category was starved of marketing by incumbents, and Imperial Blue itself was a brand its multinational owner had left underinvested for four to five years before selling it.

- **Intangible benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Neutral. Now: After direct input costs, the company keeps ₹46 of every ₹100 of sales — pricing power customers are happy to pay for. And from the calls: Brand aspiration is being built deliberately (luxury launches, celebrity association, premiumization), but true pricing power is absent — realisations rose only ~2% and consumer prices are set by state excise policy, with EBITDA margins in the mid-teens. | The long view had said: After direct input costs, the company keeps ₹46 of every ₹100 of sales — pricing power customers are happy to pay for. And from the calls: Brand pull is demonstrable — secondary volumes grew 8% in Andhra even while primaries de-grew during the RTM transition — with gross margins around 50% and a successful premium/luxury ladder from Flandy to Monarch.

Now: Calls are substance-led with numbers rather than personality, but the profile has risen sharply through a headline Pernod Ricard deal, marquee institutional fundraising and celebrity brand endorsements. | The long view had said: Calls are substance-led and operationally detailed with no celebrity positioning, and management explicitly declines to talk about stock price, redirecting to operating performance.

**That strengthened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → STRONG FIT. Why now: Share numbers are given explicitly and rise through the year: Andhra exit share ~10% under the earlier regime to ~12%, 32% P&A share in Southern India in December 2025, and close to 40% in Q4 FY26, with gains stated as coming from named categories of competitors rather than from discounting. (The long view had said: Explicit, rising share numbers across the whole period and cited profitably: brandy share 14-15% to 20% over five years and IMFL share 2% (FY17) to 4.3% (FY23), Telangana 6.8% to 7.4%, southern IMFL 5.8% to 6.2%, then ~32% and ~40% P&A share in the South post-Imperial Blue — achieved alongside EBITDA growth and debt reduction, not bought with margin.)

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management set a TSMA-exit target in Q3 FY26 and reported delivery in Q4, and disclosed the April disruption and geopolitical input-cost pressure rather than hiding them; against that, the balance sheet flipped from Rs 1,086 crore net cash to Rs 1,911 crore net debt for the IB deal and the CFO seat has turned over repeatedly (Sirsat, Chaturvedi, Gupta, Mathur, then Choudhary). (The long view had said: Fourth-generation family business with a documented promise-to-delivery record: gross debt cut from ~Rs 1,200 crore (2019) to Rs 45 crore with net cash by Q3 FY25, the Q3 FY26 TSMA-exit commitment met in Q4, Prag capex delivered as guided; bad news is disclosed promptly (AP price cut, April integration disruption, Telangana receivables), though four CFOs in five years and a Rs 2,100 crore acquisition loan temper the conservatism claim.)

Why now: Nothing in the four FY26 calls touches licence fees, AMCs, subscriptions, attach rates or deferred revenue as a funding source; the business is one-time case sales of spirits. (The long view had said: No discussion of licence fees, AMC/service revenue, renewals, attach rates, installed base or customer advances funding the business; the only adjacent items are a Tamil Nadu royalty arrangement and a Spaceman 'usership agreement', neither framed as recurring revenue economics.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 0 worsened, 5 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The acquired Imperial Blue business depends on a single named supplier for its critical input — a long-term supply agreement with Chivas Brothers for the Concentrated Alcoholic Beverage — and on Pernod Ricard itself via the TSMA, whose staged exit already caused 'small business disruptions in the first couple of weeks of April' with 3 states still on TSMA into FY27. Revenue also remains southern-concentrated (~40% P&A share in South India), and state-corporation markets mean government bodies are the buying channel. (The long view had said: Real but narrowing: for most of the period ~85% of volumes came from South India with Andhra alone ~30% (so one state's RTM change swung group revenue), state corporations are effectively monopsony buyers (Telangana dues), and post-acquisition TI depends on Pernod Ricard's TSMA for operations in states still transitioning; the Imperial Blue deal materially broadened the geography and channel base.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: The consumer shift in play is toward premiumisation, and TI is on the right side of it — Monarch Legacy Edition, Seven Islands Pure Malt, the Spaceman gin/vodka/rum stake at 21.36%, and entry into semi-premium whisky, all while Mansion House Brandy crossed 10 million cases in FY26. There is a structural skew to brandy, a regional category, but the calls show diversification rather than an eroding demand base. (The long view had said: Management repeatedly acknowledges the demand base is migrating — brandy is a legacy 'grandfather's drink' skewed to South India while 10 million new legal-drinking-age consumers a year are 'not biased towards a particular category' — and has responded with a sustained portfolio pivot (Flandy, Monarch luxury brandy, Mansion House Whisky, Samsara gin, Amara vodka, Seven Islands malt, then Imperial Blue) rather than defending brandy alone.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Technological Innovation (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); New Entrants (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 4 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
