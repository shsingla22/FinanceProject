**Checks that improved:**

- **Research and development** (Capital Allocation): Neutral → Good. Now: R&D is visibly converting into shipped products and customers within the year — a fully in-house 650 kVA genset engine, CPCB IV+ range rollout, Euro V+ export engines and a first pilot batch of rare-earth-free motors — though R&D intensity/efficiency figures are not given. | The long view had said: R&D does yield products (fully in-house 650 kVA genset engine, CPCB-IV+ range, rare-earth-free motor pilot), but efficiency is questionable given the Rs 16 crore impairment of ePowertrain assets that never scaled.
- **Pricing, Mix and Volume** (Growth): Weak → Neutral. Now: Growth is described mainly as demand/volume-led (3W diesel engines, export volumes, EV VAHAN volumes) with mix and cost discipline contributing to margin, and the flagship EV launch is a value price point rather than a pricing-led move. | The long view had said: Growth is predominantly volume- and cost-driven, with the EV arm cutting prices aggressively into a price-led market and margin gains attributed to cost optimization rather than realization.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Neutral → Good. Now: The benefit set is broadly matched to the customer — reliability/certification and long-term programmes for OEM, institutional and defense buyers, and channel intimacy via mechanics/retailers for retail aftermarket buyers. | The long view had said: The B2B side is correctly matched (reliability/TCO plus aftermarket lock-in), but the B2C EV side lacks intangible pull and competes on price and subsidy, so the benefit-customer match is only half right.
- **Technology advantage** (Competitive Advantage): Neutral → Good. Now: Credible multi-product technical work — fuel-agnostic powertrains, in-house genset engine, 100% LFP transition and rare-earth-free motors now at customer pilot — though it is not a dominant edge over larger engine incumbents. | The long view had said: There is real in-house engineering depth (fully in-house 650 kVA genset engine, rare-earth-free motors, CPCB-IV+, green-fuel engines) but no demonstrated multi-product technology lead, and the ePowertrain write-off shows the edge is not reliably monetised.
- **Cyclical market growth** (Growth): Neutral → Good. Now: Management describes a reviving 3-wheeler/SCV cycle, improving agriculture and construction on a good monsoon, plus GST 2.0 and easing rates — an identifiable upturn ahead rather than a peak. | The long view had said: The diesel 3-wheeler cycle has already run from its 2022 pandemic trough to 48% year-on-year automotive growth in Q4 FY26, so the company now sits mid/late-cycle with fresh commodity cost pressure rather than near a bottom.
- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±1.6 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Emission-norm certifications (Euro V+, CPCB IV+, FM/UL) create real technical barriers in engines, but the EV portfolio competes in a price-sensitive mass segment where the flagship launch is anchored on an aggressive price point and warranty. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±4.8 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: The EV segment explicitly features price-led competition and low entry barriers (new entrants treating scooters like phone assembly), with subsidy-dependent economics rather than rational pricing.

- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Good → Neutral. Now: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.20 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: The aftermarket leg is explicitly asset-light with strong margins, but the core is engine/component manufacturing needing conveyor lines and capacity capex, and the moat is thin at ~4% genset share. | The long view had said: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.20 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: Core standalone and retail businesses are explicitly asset-light with ROCE over 30% and net-cash balance sheet, but the protecting moat is thin given commodity-cost pass-through pressure and crowded competition.

Now: Share gains are described but no weak or retreating competitors are identified as the source; gains come from network expansion and product enhancement. | The long view had said: The fragmented L3E/e-rickshaw base and unbranded regional players are explicit targets for share capture using Greaves' pan-India network, and regional share gains in Bihar/east India confirm donors exist.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 0 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 2. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 2 strengthened, 1 weakened, 8 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront)**: PARTIAL → LIKELY FIT. Why now: Q3 FY26 launched a nationwide retail AMC offering and reorganised sales-service-spares into one structure, with spares & service up 40% year-on-year (9M) and aftermarket up 35% for FY26; the Q4 INR 35 crore institutional order explicitly bundled long-term maintenance. But no attach rates, renewal rates, deferred revenue or customer-advance funding were ever quantified. (The long view had said: The aftermarket/service stream is real and accelerating — Feb 2026 and May 2026 calls report spares & service growing 40% and aftermarket 35% for FY26, a nationwide retail AMC launch, Greaves Care franchise service outlets and a Rs.35 crore order including long-term maintenance — but management never discusses attach rates, renewal rates, deferred revenue or advance billing as a funding source.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: PARTIAL → STRONG FIT. Why now: Across the window management repeatedly describes selling through mechanics and retailers rather than end users — 21,000+ mechanics on a loyalty programme with 170mn reward points scanned (Q1), a piloted Retailer Loyalty Program, mechanic upskilling/ESG training, and 350 retailers onboarded in Q4 with a roadmap to 3,000 by FY27. (The long view had said: Greaves Retail sells multi-brand parts through mechanics and franchisee workshops, and the Upahar loyalty app explicitly recruits the trade (7,000+ scanning mechanics by Q1 FY25, ~350 retailers onboarded in FY26 targeting 3,000), but there is no training-programme or prescriber lock-in narrative, and the EV business is sold direct to consumers.)

**That weakened:**

- **Toll Roads**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: The core products — engines, gensets, control cables — are a major share of the customer's bill of materials, not a sub-1% critical ingredient, and Greaves holds only ~4% genset share in a fragmented market rather than sitting in a rational oligopoly; certification language (CPCB IV+, Euro V+, FM/UL) is regulatory compliance the whole industry must meet, not a proprietary standard. (The long view had said: Excel Controlinkage fits the shape — a small-ticket, mission-critical control sub-assembly at ~28-30% margins — and the FY26 calls add FM/UL certified engines, a defence order and Stanley Black & Decker supplier recognition; but management never quantifies it as a tiny share of customer cost, and Excel's growth has been lumpy (flat quarters, softer exports, margin slippage below the 30% guide).)

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Forward Integrators (PARTIAL); Market Share Gainers (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Low Price Plus (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT); Cost to Replicate (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 4 eased, 0 worsened, 3 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Stressed on the long view, Stressed on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Government Dependency**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: A large share of recent growth is regulation-created rather than demand-created — CPCB IV+ genset norms, Euro V+ emission standards for export engines, GST 2.0 cited as the auto tailwind — and the EV arm operates in India's subsidy-shaped two/three-wheeler market, with defence and railway institutional orders adding direct government-counterparty revenue; this is policy sensitivity rather than a regulated-tariff business. (The long view had said: EV volumes and pricing depend directly on MHI subsidy schemes (FAME/EMPS), with an admitted period of 'blips' requiring management to work with the government to resolve eligibility, and analysts questioning profitability given 'lack of support from the PLI and government related scheme'. On the ICE side, genset demand is being 'steered' by CPCB-IV+ emission regulation, GST 2.0 is cited as an auto-demand driver, and a Labour Code provision hit Q3 FY26 profits; defence and institutional orders add further policy-linked revenue.)
- **New Entrants**: HIGH RISK → WATCH. Why now: The electric two- and three-wheeler market GEML competes in has been reset by structurally different EV-native entrants, and GEML remains a small player at 4.2% VAHAN share competing on aggressive price-and-warranty packaging (Rs 90,000 with 5-year/75,000 km warranty) rather than from a position of strength; the risk is real but the excerpts contain no defensive discussion of discounting pressure on the core engine business. (The long view had said: The EV two-wheeler market has been reset by structurally different, venture-funded, direct-to-consumer entrants; analysts pressed management on this from the very first call, and by FY26 management describes the market as price-led and crowded while Greaves holds only ~4-5% share as the sixth player. Aggressive repricing of Eltra City and heavy brand/dealer investment are explicitly competitive responses.)
- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: Demand across all three segments is explicitly framed against capex-driven and macro cycles — infrastructure spending, industrial expansion, mining, construction, monsoon, rural sentiment and interest rates — and Q3 FY26 showed industrial solutions decelerating to 3% growth while automotive swung to 48% in Q4, indicating meaningful volatility; however a fast-growing aftermarket/spares stream (operations-linked flow revenue) and a net-cash balance sheet funding countercyclical capex bound the exposure. (The long view had said: The CFO explicitly concedes seasonality/cyclicality in the standalone engine business, and commodity swings bookend the period — raw-material inflation and price hikes in FY23, then an input-cost spike again in Q4 FY26; genset demand is tied to infrastructure and industrial capex cycles. Offsetting this, the fastest-growing lines are operations-linked flow revenue (spares, service, AMC) rather than customer capex.)
- **Good Enough Goods**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The retail spares and small-engine businesses are functional, low-complexity products where the mechanic and retailer — not the end vehicle owner — choose the part, the classic setup for cheaper good-enough substitutes to take share; management's response has been to buy intermediary loyalty and to exit commoditised segments (two-wheeler parts, batteries, construction equipment spares) where it lacked a defensible premium. (The long view had said: In both key channels the intermediary controls the choice and cheaper substitutes are available: the three-wheeler spares market runs through mechanics — hence the Upahar loyalty programme signing 7,000 scanning mechanics — and in EVs management is openly defending a premium chemistry against low-price rivals. Excel's control cables are also being pushed up-market into assemblies precisely because standalone cables are commoditisable.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Fashion Risk (LOW).*

*Unchanged: Technological Innovation (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (WATCH).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 2 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
