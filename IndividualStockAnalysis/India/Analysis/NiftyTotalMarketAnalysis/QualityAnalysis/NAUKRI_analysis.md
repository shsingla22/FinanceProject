# Info Edge (India) Ltd. (NAUKRI) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

*Industry: IT*

Info Edge (India) Ltd. runs a group of Indian online classifieds and matching websites/apps — Naukri for jobs, 99acres for property, Jeevansathi for marriage matchmaking, and Shiksha for education — that connect one side looking (job seekers, home buyers, brides and grooms, students) with the side paying to be found (employers and recruitment consultants, property brokers and developers, private colleges and universities). Almost all the money comes from selling paid subscriptions, job postings, resume-database access, premium listings and paid memberships, which are billed upfront and then recognised as revenue over the contract period (the company reports both 'billings' and 'revenue' for this reason). Recruitment is overwhelmingly the profit engine: in FY26 standalone recruitment billings were about Rs. 2,374 crores of the company's Rs. 3,178 crores total, at a ~57% operating margin, while the other three businesses are much smaller and hover around break-even. It operates mainly in India, with a Middle East jobs site (NaukriGulf) and a small study-abroad offering, and sits on a large cash pile (about Rs. 4,963 crores at March 2026) from which it pays dividends and funds acquisitions.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Recruitment (Naukri)** — The core job-portal business, where employers and recruitment consultants pay for job listings, access to Naukri's resume database (~115 million resumes by Q4 FY26) and employer-branding products; it is by far the largest and most profitable part — roughly Rs. 2,374 crores of FY26 standalone billings at a ~57-59% operating margin and Rs. 1,513 crores of operating cash.
- **Naukri Jobseeker Services** — Paid services sold to job seekers themselves rather than employers; small but fast-growing, at Rs. 176 crores of FY26 billings (up 19%) with a 57% operating margin.
- **NaukriGulf** — The Middle East version of the jobs business; Rs. 126 crores of FY26 billings, up 16%, with a 35% operating margin, though growth slowed to 9% in Q4 on regional disturbances.
- **iimjobs / hirist / Naukri Fast Forward** — Niche and adjacent recruitment brands (senior-management and tech-specific job sites, plus resume-writing style services for candidates) that management calls healthy growers — for example iimjobs and hirist billings of Rs. 15.3 crores in Q1 FY23 and 25-29% growth rates across these verticals in Q1 FY25.
- **JobHai** — A blue-collar/entry-level jobs platform still run mostly on a free (freemium) model in selected cities; deliberately loss-making and in investment mode — excluding JobHai, recruitment margins would be about 61% instead of 58% in Q4 FY26.
- **AmbitionBox** — A company-reviews and salary-ratings site for job seekers, described as the largest such platform in India (6.29 million unique visitors in Q1 FY23); small in revenue, only recently monetised, and held as a strategic 5-6 year bet rather than a near-term earner.
- **Zwayam and DoSelect** — Acquired recruitment-software businesses (hiring workflow and candidate skill-assessment tools) sold alongside Naukri; small add-ons that lift reported recruitment billings by roughly Rs. 27 crores a quarter in FY26.
- **Real estate (99acres)** — A property listings marketplace where brokers, channel partners and developers pay to list and promote homes for sale, resale and rent plus commercial space; FY26 billings were Rs. 497 crores (up 10%) but it still ran an operating loss of Rs. 59 crores, while holding about half of category web traffic.
- **Matchmaking (Jeevansathi, with Aisle and Arike)** — Matrimonial and dating apps where users pay for premium membership features; Jeevansathi billed Rs. 142 crores in FY26 (up 28%) and, combined with the now wholly-owned Aisle (and its Malayalam app Arike), the portfolio is close to break-even after years of heavy marketing losses.
- **Education (Shiksha)** — A college and course discovery site that earns mainly by sending student enquiries/leads to private (not government) colleges and universities, plus a small study-abroad counselling business; billings were Rs. 119 crores in the first nine months of FY26, roughly break-even, and management flags a sharp AI-driven traffic drop that is pushing it to pivot toward counselling-led conversions.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Info Edge earns a Strong rating (77 out of 100, four stars) built on one engine and three brands that hang off it. Naukri is the default resume database in the Indian recruiter's mind - about 115 million resumes, more than 120,000 paying customers, and operating margins of 57-59% in recruitment that held up even through a hiring downturn. The quality framework scores Network Effects, Mini-monopolies and Intangible Benefits at the top mark for exactly this reason: more resumes attract more recruiters, which attracts more resumes, and the product itself has almost no direct input cost, so nearly all of every Rs.100 of sales is left over for people, marketing and profit. The same facts drive four of the strongest pattern fits - Cost to Replicate (115 million resumes, 20,000-plus added daily, decades of two-sided behaviour data), Brand Strength, Market Share Gainers (99acres web traffic share moving 44% to 46% to 49%, and 52% in April, with resale supply share past 50%; Shiksha traffic share at an all-time-high 51%; AmbitionBox overtaking its leading competitor; Jeevansathi at 45% profile share in Hindi markets) and Recurring Revenue billed upfront, where subscription contracts are paid before the service is delivered and deferred sales revenue (Rs.825 crore in one quarter, up 63% year-on-year) works as a float funded by customers. That upfront billing is also why only about one day of cash sits stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. The customer cash then funds the next product, which is the Innovation Dominance pattern: AmbitionBox, JobHai, Naukri 360, Naukri Minis, Talent Pulse, PremiumX, 99Shorts and AI-Rex, the last already at 1,000 clients and 30,000 mandates.

The uncomfortable part is that several of these strengths are the risks seen from the other side. Upfront subscription billing makes cash collection excellent, but it also means the reported numbers move with corporate hiring appetite - recruitment billings grew 64.6% year-on-year in one early quarter and then slowed to 7-9% across the following years on cautious hiring, tariff-related uncertainty and geopolitical headwinds, which is why Cyclicality is rated Elevated. Free web traffic is what made the classifieds and education brands cheap to grow, and it is exactly the dependency that hurt: Shiksha holds a record 51% traffic share, one of the best share statistics in the whole record, yet its billings fell 13% year-on-year as job seekers and students shifted discovery from search engines to AI assistants and Google referral traffic dried up, forcing a pivot. That single event is the evidence behind two Elevated or Watch risks at once - Technological Innovation and the channel side of Stakeholder Concentration - even though customer concentration itself is genuinely low across 120,000-plus clients. Premium pricing is the third double edge. It supports the 57-60% recruitment margins and is why the Low Price Plus pattern is a clear No Fit, but it also leaves the company selling a premium database into a market with cheaper and free alternatives, including its own free products (JobHai, free chat on Jeevansathi) and matrimony rivals discounting harder than usual - the Good Enough Goods risk. And competitor entry, rather than resetting industry economics, mostly forces defensive spending: 99acres losses were driven by advertising against well-funded new entrants. That is the likely reason Industry Structure is the second-weakest quality area at 0.67, and why Pricing Power is only a Likely Fit - recent billings growth of 9-11% came from volume and mix, not price, in a soft hiring market.

The single worst score in the framework is headline return on capital at -2: for every Rs.100 of shareholders' funds and borrowings in the business, only about Rs.3 of operating cash was generated this year, and the Return on Capital area comes in negative at -0.67. This sits oddly next to 57-59% margins, and the explanation runs straight back to a trait the framework rewards elsewhere. The company is debt-free with net cash that rose from Rs.3,439 crore to Rs.4,963 crore, and it has held Zomato since 2010 and PolicyBazaar since 2008, run a deliberate 12-plus-2 year fund, and framed JobHai as a five-to-six year play - behaviour scored at the top mark under Independent, Long-term and Tenacious and central to the Corporate Culture pattern fit. The same balance sheet that makes the business almost impossible to break also makes the headline capital return look poor, and it is the cushion that lets management keep spending through downcycles (the Capex for Growth check, covering JobHai, AmbitionBox, the 99acres platform and AI infrastructure) instead of cutting. Candor scores at the top mark too - refusing to forecast 99acres losses, voluntarily disclosing a Rs.20.5 crore provision reversal that did not have to be disclosed, and calling a weak quarter 'a reset' rather than dressing it up. Net of all this, resilience is Sound and the arithmetic is honest about the shape: full marks on multibagger pattern fit (100), a middling 69 on business quality dragged down by the capital-return score, and 64 on risk safety reflecting the two Elevated risks. Growth has averaged about 15% a year and is lumpy year to year - right inside the steady band the framework prizes - so the case is a durable, cash-generative near-monopoly whose main uncertainties are when hiring turns and whether AI changes how people find jobs, homes and courses.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Technological Innovation. The balance sheet and cash engine shows no financial stress — none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.

### Cyclicality — ELEVATED
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show real but bounded exposure, and 2 numeric fingerprints reinforce it.

**What the calls show:** Billings swing hard with the corporate hiring cycle — recruitment billings grew 64.6% year-on-year in Q1FY23, decelerated to 7-9% through FY24-FY26 on 'geopolitical headwinds, tariffs related uncertainty and a generally cautious hiring stance', with management itself framing these as periodic cycles; but demand is subscription/database spend out of customer opex rather than customer capex, and recruitment operating margins held in a narrow 54-59% band throughout, so the damage shows in growth, not in margins or capital destruction.

> "A key strength in navigating these cycles, which we go through periodically as a business, has been our strong cash generation and our healthy cash balance." — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Flow/operations-linked subscription product with large deferred revenue, 57-59% recruitment margins sustained through the downturn, Rs.4,963 crore net cash and countercyclical investment in 99acres, JobHai and AI while growth was soft*

- ✅ Sales fell in only 1 of the last 11 years with swings of ±17 points — no pronounced boom-and-bust signature in revenue.
- ⚠️ Operating margin has ranged from -27% to 36% (swinging ±17.8 points a year) — margins fluctuate a lot, the signature of a cyclical business.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 60% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 5% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

## What to watch

- Whether recruitment billings re-accelerate past the 7-9% range they slowed to - the Cyclicality risk is the main swing factor between a soft patch and a structural slowdown.
- Whether the Shiksha pivot stops the 13% year-on-year billings decline; if record 51% traffic share keeps failing to convert into revenue, the Technological Innovation risk is doing lasting damage rather than causing a one-off reset.
- Whether more verticals start losing search-driven traffic to AI assistants, which would turn the Google channel dependency under Stakeholder Concentration from a Shiksha problem into a company-wide one.
- Whether growth returns to being price-led rather than volume-and-mix-led; that is the test of whether Pricing Power moves from Likely Fit to a firm one, especially with matrimony rivals discounting (Good Enough Goods).
- Whether the Rs.4,963 crore cash pile is put to work or keeps growing - it is the direct cause of the -2 Return on Capital score, and any deployment or return of it changes the weakest part of the rating.
