## Step 2 — Where it improved and where it regressed

### Bucket 1 — Business quality (34-check framework)

Of the 34 quality checks, 28 could be genuinely re-tested on both views: 7 improved, 1 regressed, 20 unchanged. 4 checks carry the long view's verdict forward unchanged (the window could not re-test them). 1 was newly assessable in the recent view.

| Area | Long view | Last one year | Change |

| Return on Capital | Excellent (+1.67) | Excellent (+1.67) | +0.00 |
| Growth | Neutral (+0.33) | Neutral (+0.50) | +0.17 |
| Management | Neutral (+0.50) | Good (+0.67) | +0.17 |

**Checks that improved:**

- **Barriers to entry and rationality** (Industry Structure): Neutral → Good. Now: Profit margins have barely moved over the years (±2.1 points) — consistent with rational pricing and few new competitors. And from the calls: Pricing is managed rationally through tiered propositions rather than discounting ('superior consumer value at a price that is considered worth it across each price tier') with stable P&G ownership, but management itself flags a challenging competitive environment and had to launch a value-priced 3-blade to defend the mid-tier. | The long view had said: Profit margins swing by ±4.4 points — a hint of price wars, cost shocks, or new entrants disturbing the industry. And from the calls: Pricing behaviour remains rational with tiered value propositions rather than discounting, but new entrants are visibly appearing — competitive activity in the entry tier has stalled oral care growth and analysts flag rising competition in men's shaving.
- **Customer types (retail vs corporate)** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: The customer base is retail consumers and the benefit set is correctly matched to them — brand image, aspiration, packaging as first moment of truth, and communication tuned by segment from the Hindi heartland to Gen-Z campus programmes. | The long view had said: The customer base is purely retail and the benefit stack is correctly matched to it — brand image, aspiration and packaging cues rather than TCO arguments — across every price tier from Guard to Labs.
- **Convenience benefits** (Customer Benefits): Good → Excellent. Now: Availability is treated as a strategic vector with store coverage/assortment optimisation, proprietary digital-shelf search tools, quick-commerce activation and AI-based distributor ordering to keep product on shelf. | The long view had said: Accessibility is a deliberate, well-instrumented advantage spanning general trade, modern trade, e-commerce and quick commerce, backed by analytics-driven assortment and daily availability monitoring.
- **Structural end-market growth** (Growth): Good → Excellent. Now: Categories are structurally under-penetrated in India with an aspirational upgrade ladder (basic to 3-blade, manual to power oral care) that management is explicitly building the portfolio to capture. | The long view had said: The grooming and power-oral-care end markets show secular expansion — female grooming razors the fastest-growing sub-segment, Oral-B power business doubling in three years — but the core grooming category itself is only growing mid-single digits and is undergoing a beard-driven shift.
- **Independent, long-term and tenacious** (Management): Neutral → Good. Now: The same integrated growth strategy is articulated consistently across all three calls with visible follow-through in results, though as a P&G subsidiary the strategy is the parent's playbook rather than independently authored. | The long view had said: Long-term consistency of strategy over a decade is evident, but independence is limited — the local team defers entirely to the global parent on structural matters and cannot comment on P&G's announced restructuring.
- **Partial monopoly / broken competition / switching costs** (Industry Structure): Good → Excellent. Now: The razor-and-blade model is a captive aftermarket by construction — system-specific cartridges across Guard, Mach 3, Fusion 5, Labs and Venus, reinforced by handle warranties and refill-driven repeat purchase. | The long view had said: The razor-and-blade model creates a captive aftermarket (handles locking consumers into Gillette cartridges, Oral-B handles into its brush heads), but management concedes rising competitive activity shows switching costs are not absolute.
- **Profit margin** (Return on Capital): Good → Excellent. Now: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹27 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±2.1 points a year — very steady, a sign costs are under control. | The long view had said: Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹27 is operating profit. Margins have moved about ±4.4 points a year — sizeable swings, suggesting key costs are outside management's control.

**Checks that regressed:**

- **Asset turn** (Return on Capital): Excellent → Good. Now: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.19 of sales — an asset-light business. | The long view had said: Every ₹1 of assets produces ₹1.19 of sales — an asset-light business. And from the calls: Sales grew 1.5x while profit rose 6-8x over ten years with ROE up 4-5x on an explicitly asset-light, productivity-funded model, protected by the Gillette brand's market leadership in blades and razors.

*Unchanged: Distribution advantage; Mini-monopolies; Disciplined stewards; People matter (talent development); Advertisement and promotion; Gain in market share; Technology advantage; Candor; Share donators; Assurance benefits; Intangible benefits; Security by obscurity; Pricing, Mix and Volume; Cyclical market growth; Halo effect (caution); Out of limelight; Oligopoly (vs duopoly); Return on capital (headline); Research and development; Cost of working capital.*

**Newly assessable in the recent view:**

- **Network effects** — Weak: Nothing in the business model is two-sided or self-reinforcing with scale; the moat described is brand, product and distribution, with no user network of any kind mentioned across the three calls.

### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 8 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 4 weakened, 7 unchanged.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The delivery record is consistent — sales up 6%, 12% and 8% with PAT up 16%, 40% and 23% across the three calls, and the Jun 2026 call claims five straight years of top- and bottom-line growth under MD V. Kumar, who has been in seat since FY2024 — but the calls are scripted presentations with pre-submitted questions, no mistakes are owned, and the chairman seat has turned over repeatedly (Das, Gupta, Dua exited; Duggal and Machado current). (The long view had said: The record shows promises turning into delivery across the window — Power Oral Care doubling in three years as flagged, five consecutive years of top- and bottom-line growth, ~8 consecutive quarters of balanced growth, 30+ years of unbroken dividends, and long-tenured MD V. Kumar through the whole period. Bad news is acknowledged rather than buried (slow oral care, soft urban demand, the P&G global restructuring), though the pre-submitted-question format and refusal to disclose segment mix limit transparency.)
- **Cost to Replicate**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Some replication barriers are concrete and named — 115 years of blade technology, patented irritation-defence bar, facial adaptive technology, Criss Cross bristle technology, and P&G's distribution and supply-chain scale (60% fewer supply touchpoints, AI-based distributor ordering) — but management itself flags 'a challenging operating and competitive environment' and never articulates why a well-funded entrant could not compete in Indian grooming. (The long view had said: A concrete replication barrier is articulable: 115 years of accumulated blade technology (FlexBall, anti-friction platinum coatings, Criss Cross bristles, iO), decades of Indian household penetration and distribution depth (2 million on-shelf availability data checks daily, AI-driven distributor replenishment), all funded by P&G's global R&D — none of which a well-funded entrant rebuilds in a few years. The FY24 oral-care entry-tier share loss shows the moat is stronger in blades than in manual toothbrushes.)
- **Friendly Middleman**: LIKELY FIT → NO FIT. Why now: Demand is created by mass consumer communication, celebrity endorsement (Abhishek Sharma) and retail/digital-shelf execution, not by a prescribing professional; the only professional angle is Oral-B using 'expert led voices' as a media tactic in the Jun 2026 call, with no trade training programmes or channel-professional lock-in described. (The long view had said: Oral-B leans on professional endorsement ('world's no1 dentist recommended toothbrush brand', Jun 2026 'a credible voice is usually that of a professional' and 'expert led voices'), but the bulk of the business is consumer-marketing-driven grooming sold at retail, with no trade training programmes or channel-professional lock-in described.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management repeatedly claims strengthened market leadership and cites household-penetration proxies — '5 million more consumers who now rely on Guard' (Jun 2025) and Oral-B Sensitive as a fastest-growing segment (Jun 2026) — but no explicit market-share percentages, trend line, or comparison against named competitors is given in any of the three calls. (The long view had said: Share leadership and gains are claimed in all three calls — 'highest ever shares' (Sep 2024), 'strengthened our leadership' (Jun 2025), 'market leader in the Blades and Razors segment, and we have grown year on year' (Jun 2026) — backed by penetration proof points (5m new Guard users in a year, 20m over three years) rather than bought with margin, since profit grew 16%, 40% and 23% alongside. Caveat: exact share percentages are never disclosed, and oral care lost ground to entry-tier competition in FY24.)

*Unchanged: Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (STRONG FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (LIKELY FIT); Pricing Power (LIKELY FIT); Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Forward Integrators (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 2 eased, 1 worsened, 4 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The Jun 2026 call flags retail consolidation and the rise of a few exponentially growing platforms — a narrowing of the channel base that shifts bargaining power toward large e-commerce/quick-commerce retailers — though no single named customer, licensor or supplier is cited as driving results in any call. (The long view had said: The entire franchise is licensed brands and technology from parent P&G, with strategy, portfolio and supply mix set globally — the Jun-2025 call had to deflect analyst questions on P&G's global restructuring with 'market-level plans are yet to be developed', showing the local entity's dependence on decisions it does not control; offsetting this, the customer/channel base is broad (traditional trade, e-commerce, quick commerce) with no named customer driving results.)
- **Shifting Consumer Preferences**: ELEVATED → WATCH. Why now: The Jun 2026 call is explicit that inflation has 'significantly altered Indian consumer psychology' toward disciplined, value-conscious spending and that shopping habits are migrating to new online formats — the demand base's channel and value expectations are moving even as the franchise executes well. (The long view had said: The core demand base — frequency of wet shaving — is eroding as Indian men shift to beards and stubble, a theme analysts raise in every one of the three calls and management concedes is accelerating, culminating in the Jun-2026 admission that the category is in a 'tectonic shift'; growth has had to be manufactured by re-jobbing razors as styling tools and adding trimmers rather than by category volume alone.)

**That worsened:**

- **Good Enough Goods**: WATCH → HIGH RISK. Why now: Across all three calls management persistently defends its price premium through the 'value' vector 'at every price tier', and by Jun 2026 is explicitly framing its new launch against cheaper substitutes — men 'moving beyond good enough solutions' — while inflation-hit consumers turn value-disciplined; razors and manual toothbrushes are simple, low-complexity products where challengers can undercut profitably. (The long view had said: Razors and toothbrushes are simple, low-complexity products where cheaper challengers can earn decent margins — management admits entry-tier competition stalled oral care, repeatedly frames value defence ('consumers are more value conscious', 'superior consumer value at a price that is considered worth it across each price tier'), and launched Guard 3in1 explicitly for men 'moving beyond good enough' at an accessible price; unlike the Nobel Biocare pattern, though, the consumer chooses the brand directly with high awareness and no intermediary captures the margin.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Government Dependency (LOW).*

*Unchanged: New Entrants (WATCH); Cyclicality (LOW); Technological Innovation (LOW); Fashion Risk (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
