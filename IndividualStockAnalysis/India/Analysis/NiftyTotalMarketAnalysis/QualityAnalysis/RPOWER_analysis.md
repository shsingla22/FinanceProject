### The story in depth

Reliance Power scores 41 out of 100 — a Mixed, two-star rating — and the arithmetic behind that number tells you where the company stands. The three inputs pull in different directions: business quality came in at 38, multibagger fit at just 10, and risk safety at 84. The single fact that shapes the business-quality picture most is how much money is tied up in the assets. Every ₹1 of assets produces only ₹0.18 of sales, which is a fairly asset-heavy way to earn a living, and the 'Asset turn' check is the weakest of all at -2. The company does keep a healthy slice of each sale: ₹31 of every ₹100 of sales is operating profit. But a fat margin on a small amount of sales per rupee of assets still comes out modest at the end — for every ₹100 of capital in the business, counting both shareholders' funds and borrowings, it generated ₹9 of operating cash this year, a 9% cash return. Growth adds little: sales have grown about 1% a year on average and the growth is lumpy from one year to the next.

The same trait shows up twice, once as a weakness and once as a risk, and it is worth naming plainly. Profit margins have moved by about ±9.9 points a year. In the quality framework, that swing is what drags the 'Barriers to entry and rationality' check to -1 and pushes the whole Industry Structure area to -1 — wide margin swings hint at price wars, cost shocks or new entrants unsettling the industry, and they suggest key costs sit outside management's control. In the risk review, that very same instability is what lights up Cyclicality, which carries three numeric fingerprints, the most of any risk raised. New Entrants, Fashion Risk and Good Enough Goods each carry one fingerprint. Alongside this, about 63 days of cash stays stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, a long cycle that ties up capital in a business that already needs a lot of it.

The thinnest part of this file is evidence about the company's own words. Every one of the eleven multibagger patterns tested was judged with the note that no conference-call judgement was available — Recurring Revenue came out a PARTIAL fit and the rest, including Pricing Power, Brand Strength, Toll Roads and Market Share Gainers, were NO FIT. That same silence explains why three whole quality areas — Competitive Advantage, Customer Benefits and Management — have no score at all, and why all four risks are marked QUANT FLAG rather than confirmed: the numbers raise the question, the calls answer neither way, and numbers alone are only ever a flag to investigate. So the low multibagger score of 10 is as much an absence of evidence as evidence of absence. It also means the strongest input to the rating — the risk safety score of 84, which is what lifts the total to 41 — rests partly on the fact that nothing was confirmed against the company, only flagged. Resilience is judged SOUND, so the near-term footing looks steady; the open questions are about what the business earns and how stable those earnings are, not whether it can stand up.
