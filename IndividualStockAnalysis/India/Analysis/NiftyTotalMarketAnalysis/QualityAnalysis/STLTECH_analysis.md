# Sterlite Tech (STLTECH) — The Analyst's Report

## About the business

Sterlite Technologies (STL) is an India-based maker of the physical 'plumbing' that carries internet traffic: it starts from ultra-pure silicon, makes glass preforms, draws optical fibre, builds fibre-optic and specialty cables, and adds the connectivity products that join networks together — what management calls the journey 'from glass to gigabit.' Its customers are telecom operators, data centre builders, government-backed 'citizen networks' (such as BharatNet in India and BEAD-funded projects in the US) and large enterprises, and it earns money by manufacturing and selling this hardware, increasingly bundled as complete connectivity solutions, plus network services and IT work through separate arms. Demand is driven by three build-out cycles management cites repeatedly — fibre-to-the-home/premises, AI data centres and 5G/6G — with North America, Europe, India and Asia-Pacific (excluding China) as the focus markets and 10-plus factories worldwide. By FY26–FY27 it describes itself as India's No.1 end-to-end optical manufacturer with roughly 8–9% share of the global optical-fibre-cable market outside China, and order intake has grown sharply, from INR 3,672 crore in FY25 to INR 7,687 crore in FY26 and INR 13,100 crore in the June 2026 quarter alone.

**The parts of the business:**

- **Optical Networking Business (optical connectivity — fibre, optical fibre cable, specialty cables and connectivity products)** — The core business: it makes and sells optical fibre, cables and the connectors/hardware that link them, and the calls treat it as the main engine — the company's stated goal is to be a global top-3 optical player, and it says it has moved from about 5% global share (ex-China) in FY20 to 8–9% by FY26–FY27, including roughly 21% in Europe and 14% in North America at its FY22–FY23 peak commentary.
- **Data centre / AI-DC suite (branded 'Neuralis')** — A fast-growing sub-line selling fibre and connectivity built specifically for data centres and AI workloads, including India's first hollow-core fibre cable; management set a target of about 25% of revenue from data-centre products in the medium term and says large North American data-centre wins were a big part of the record FY26 order book.
- **Global Services business** — A network-building and services arm that designs and deploys networks for customers; the calls describe efforts to globalise it, improve the project mix for better profitability, and complete a demerger of the services business (mentioned as in progress in the FY25 call and absent from the FY26–FY27 descriptions of the company).
- **STL Digital** — A separate IT/digital services unit being scaled up through investment in new technology and domain skills while keeping it profitable; it is described as the smallest and newest of the three business lines and is not mentioned in the later FY26–FY27 calls.

*Described strictly from management's own words on the earnings calls.*

### The story in depth

Sterlite Tech comes out of the three reviews as a genuinely improving business sitting in a genuinely difficult industry, which is why the combined grade lands at Mixed — 39 out of 100, from 55 on business quality, 25 on multibagger fit and 28 on risk safety — with overall resilience marked as strained. The clear strengths are all on the demand and execution side: the end market is in real multi-year expansion across home broadband, 5G/6G and AI data centres as copper hits its bandwidth limits (top score), and the company has built a credible overseas footprint over five years, taking North America from almost nothing to 54% of revenue with its own South Carolina plant plus 25-40% from the UK, Italy and Europe (also top score). Research spending of roughly Rs.50 crore a quarter, with patents rising from 740 to 785, has repeatedly turned into products that actually sell — Multiverse multi-core fibre, G.654.E with a first commercial order, hollow core fibre, Neuralis and CONCAT — and this is the single pattern in the eleven-pattern screen rated a strong fit, Innovation Dominance. Management also gets credit for cleaning up after over-extending into the downturn: Rs.769 crore of debt repaid in FY25, borrowings down to 1.3 times earnings and then a net cash position, the services arm demerged, no large acquisitions, and only about 16 days of cash tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers.

The awkward part is that most of these same strengths carry their own risk on the other side of the ledger, which is why five patterns scored only a partial fit and five no fit at all. The innovation record that earns the one strong pattern sits in an optical layer that is being redrawn quickly — 400G moving to 1.6T, hollow core, multi-core, G.654.E, pre-terminated assemblies — so today's conventional fibre and cable formats can become yesterday's product, and there has already been a European patent dispute with Fujikura; that is the Technological Innovation risk at elevated. The North American build-out that scores highest on quality is protected by anti-dumping duties and US 55% local-content rules rather than by anything structural, which shows up twice: as the New Entrants risk, where Chinese makers are about half of world demand on a structurally cheaper model and are held out by policy, and as the Government Dependency watch item alongside BharatNet, BEAD, UK broadband funding and state tenders. The end market's secular growth is spent by customers as network build-out budgets, and those swing violently — the FY24-25 stretch saw global cable consumption fall 7%, North America fall 12%, margins collapse to about 7% and market share slip from 9% back to 6% — so Cyclicality is the one risk rated high. That same round trip in share, recovering only to 8% in FY26 and 9% in FY27, is why Market Share Gainers is judged a partial fit rather than a real one, and why the persistence-of-growth check is negative: sales have averaged about 4% a year and arrive lumpily.

Read together, the picture is a company that competes on capability and cost rather than on any position that lets it set its own price. Pricing Power is a clear no fit — the price rises forecast in July 2022 never arrived, and the margin recovery from 7.6% to over 20% came from product mix, higher factory utilisation and cost cutting, not from charging more — which is the mirror image of the Good Enough Goods risk, where standard cable is close to a commodity and cheaper suppliers can win. Management pairing technology leadership with cost leadership in every call supports the partial fit on Low Price Plus and simultaneously confirms that exposure. The revenue itself is order-book and project driven (order book of Rs.11,200 crore in FY23 rising to Rs.18,618 crore in FY27) rather than subscription-like, so Recurring Revenue is no fit; the customer-facing deployment arm was demerged away, so Forward Integrators is no fit; and the products are a major purchase line for telcos and hyperscalers bought through tenders, so Toll Roads is no fit. Concentration is the flip side of the biggest piece of good news: the $1.1 billion multi-year hyperscaler deal is larger than all of FY26's order intake on its own, and the plan to take data centre plus enterprise to 50% of revenue in FY27 (from 1% in FY26) is exactly what is meant to reduce policy dependence — one relationship doing both jobs. Finally, the shareholder return picture is thin: no dividend policy or buybacks anywhere in the record, and a Rs.1,500 crore institutional share sale that diluted existing holders with proceeds mostly going to debt repayment. Scored by area, growth and customer benefit are strong, while competitive advantage and return on capital both come out at zero and industry structure is negative — the arithmetic behind a Mixed verdict rather than a poor or a good one.

## Section 3 — What could break it? (8 risk channels)

**Overall:** the risks worth attention are Cyclicality, Technological Innovation, Stakeholder Concentration, New Entrants, Good Enough Goods. The balance sheet and cash engine shows one financial stress signal — 1 of the 3 stress checks is flagged — one warning light, not yet a pattern.

### Cyclicality — HIGH RISK
*"Boom-and-bust earnings"*

**Why this verdict:** the calls show clear, repeated exposure AND 2 of 3 numeric fingerprints agree — that combination earns the top severity.

**What the calls show:** STL sells into customers' network build-out capex (telco FTTx, 5G, hyperscaler DC), and the 17 calls show violent swings: FY23 optimism about a 'multi-year network build cycle', then a FY24–25 downturn with global OFC consumption down 7% (North America -12%), share falling 8%→6%, consolidated EBITDA margin at 7.6% and PAT losses, followed by an FY26–27 boom with Q1FY27 revenue +87% year-on-year and EBITDA margin jumping from ~7% to 20%+. Input commodities (helium, germanium, polymer, copper/LME) add a second cyclical layer.

> "OFC consumption for 2023 declined by 7% globally this was led by almost 12% decline in North America market" — *management, on an earnings call*

*Silver lining: Countercyclical capacity and R&D investment through the FY24–25 trough (US South Carolina plant, data-centre portfolio) positioned it for the upturn*

- ⚠️ Sales actually FELL in 4 of the last 11 years — revenue moves in cycles, it does not compound steadily.
- ✅ Operating margin stayed between 10% and 23% (±4.8 points a year) — swings within the normal range, no extreme boom-and-bust profile.
- ⚠️ Capital spending spiked to 37% of sales in its heaviest year, versus a normal 9% — the over-investment-in-the-expansion pattern that starves future returns.

*"Out-invented by the industry"*

## What to watch

- Cyclicality (high risk) versus the cyclical-trough check: whether the upturn holds, with CRU's 8.2% demand growth for 2026 confirmed rather than revised down — a second down-leg would repeat the 7% EBITDA and 6% share outcome of FY24-25.
- Stakeholder Concentration (elevated): whether the $1.1 billion hyperscaler deal converts on schedule and whether data centre plus enterprise actually reaches the guided 50% of FY27 revenue, or the order book stays hostage to a handful of named accounts.
- New Entrants and Government Dependency together: any softening of anti-dumping duties or US 55% local-content rules would remove the main thing keeping ~50% of world capacity out of the premium markets that the top-scoring geographic expansion depends on.
- Pricing Power (no fit) and Good Enough Goods (elevated): whether the recovery to 20%-plus margins, revised up to 23%, survives once mix, utilisation and cost cuts are exhausted — the check on pricing, mix and volume flags the underlying volume and commodity exposure as unchanged.
- Persistence of growth (negative) and Market Share Gainers (partial): whether share holds above the 8-9% level for several consecutive years and revenue growth becomes steadier than the roughly 4% lumpy average, which is what would turn a round trip into a real gain.
- Distribution to shareholders (negative) and the Disciplined Stewards check: whether the net cash position produces a dividend or buyback policy, or leads to further dilution like the Rs.1,500 crore share sale.
