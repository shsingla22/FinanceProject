### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 7 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 4. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 0 strengthened, 3 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That weakened:**

- **Corporate Culture**: STRONG FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Management owns errors bluntly on the Jan 2026 call and leadership is stable and promoter-led (Vikas Oberoi Chairman and MD throughout), but the year's record shows repeated slippage against its own guidance — Gurugram, Adarsh Nagar and Ritz-Carlton all promised within FY26 and pushed out — which tempers the delivery-what-was-promised test. (The long view had said: Vikas Oberoi has led as Chairman/MD throughout with Saumil Daru as finance head across all 13 calls, the balance sheet is conservative ("virtually no debt" on ready inventory; land bought with deferred, revenue-linked payments), and promises made in early calls were delivered later — the Aug-2023 pledge of "good announcement hopefully in the next quarter" on BD became Nirmal Lifestyle, Alibaug, SRA, Aram Nagar and the South Bombay redevelopments, and the Borivali mall promised for pre-Diwali opened and hit 82% occupancy. Management also owns misses plainly and habitually builds guidance cushions, though launch timelines have repeatedly slipped (Adarsh Nagar guided "even faster than 12-18 months" in Jan-2025 was still unlaunched in Jul-2026) and three CEOs were appointed in FY2026 after one exit in FY2024.)
- **Forward Integrators**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: The company retains and operates malls, offices and now a Ritz-Carlton hotel rather than selling the assets, and is considering strata sale versus leasing for Enigma and RLDA — control of the end experience — but there is no franchising, D2C or own-store economics of the kind the pattern describes. (The long view had said: Oberoi retains and operates the downstream assets in its integrated Garden City model — owned malls (Oberoi Mall, Sky City 1.1mn sq ft), Commerz offices, JW Marriott and Ritz-Carlton hotels, an international school — and sells residential direct through its own experience centres/show apartments rather than via third parties; but there is no franchising, royalty or D2C-share disclosure, and the FY26 RLDA decision leans toward strata sale rather than holding.)
- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Evidence of gain is qualitative rather than numeric: a claimed near-monopoly on residual Worli inventory and a successful first entry into NCR, but no market-share percentages or comparisons versus named competitors are given in any of the four calls. (The long view had said: Share gain is asserted rather than quantified — no explicit share percentages are given in any call — but the FY26-FY27 record shows real expansion: Three Sixty West inventory consolidated into a self-described monopoly, ~4mn sq ft of MMR business development in FY26, and a successful first entry outside MMR into NCR with intent to make Gurugram bigger than Mumbai.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (QUANT SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Pricing Power (STRONG FIT); Brand Strength (STRONG FIT); Innovation Dominance (LIKELY FIT); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Low Price Plus (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 1 eased, 3 worsened, 4 unchanged. Financial resilience: Sound on the long view, Sound on the one-year view.

**That eased:**

- **Stakeholder Concentration**: WATCH → LOW. Why now: Revenue comes from a broad base of individual homebuyers rather than named counterparties, and management explicitly cites the width of the clientele at Elysian as the reason launches get absorbed; the annuity book is spread across multiple offices and malls with marquee, non-dominant tenants (Apple as one of many at Sky City). Land sourcing has been broadened across Bandra East, Aram Nagar, three South Bombay society redevelopments and NCR rather than resting on one relationship. (The long view had said: The end-customer base is thousands of retail buyers, but specific relationships have repeatedly moved results: the Three Sixty West JV partner's 'fire sale' and residual inventory overhung that project's sales from Aug 2023 through Jul 2026, leasing revenue steps are tied to a single anchor tenant at Commerz III, and L&T is the named contractor on the two largest new projects (Kolshet, Three Sixty North).)

**That worsened:**

- **New Entrants**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: Competition is same-format rather than structurally different, but it is visibly affecting results: management attributes the year-on-year decline in Three Sixty West pre-sales partly to competing supply in Worli, and Oberoi is itself the entrant in Gurugram against established local developers with a different sell-everything-upfront model. There is no sign of a discounter or new format resetting industry economics. (The long view had said: Analysts probe peers entering MMR and competitive intensity in business development, but the calls affirmatively show the opposite of economics being reset: no discounting anywhere, prices raised repeatedly in Goregaon, Borivali and Thane, and resale in its own projects trading 20-25% above primary; Oberoi is itself the new entrant in Gurugram, where the debut launch drew ~Rs.8,000 crore of bookings.)
- **Fashion Risk**: LOW → WATCH. Why now: An increasing share of the pipeline is uber-luxury product whose demand rests on the continued desirability of specific micro-markets and price points - Adarsh Nagar at Rs.50-60 crore tickets, Tardeo at Rs.12.5-15 crore, Peddar Road, Malabar Hill - and an analyst explicitly pressed on cannibalisation across these nearby luxury launches. Management's own answer concedes it is relying on the market having 'matured' to those numbers, which is a taste judgement that can move. (The long view had said: Demand is functional housing rather than taste-driven consumption, and the portfolio spans Rs.2.5-3 crore Thane/Mulund apartments to Rs.50-60 crore Adarsh Nagar rather than one trend bet; the only taste-like exposure is micro-market fashion in ultra-luxury Worli, where offtake did slow, and management leans on cult-brand language.)
- **Technological Innovation**: NO SIGNAL → LOW. Why now: Nothing in the four calls points to a technology or format threat to the business; the only innovation discussed is incremental product refinement - deck depths reworked from 8ft to 10ft to 12ft, podium ramps replaced with elevators at Peddar Road - which is small-scale, value-adding design iteration rather than a platform shift. No analyst raised disruption, and no installed base is described as becoming legacy. (The long view had said: Across all thirteen calls neither management nor analysts raise technology platforms, disruption or AI; the business is physical development where method changes are incremental.)

*Unchanged: Cyclicality (HIGH RISK); Government Dependency (WATCH); Shifting Consumer Preferences (LOW); Good Enough Goods (LOW).*

**Financial resilience:** Sound on the long view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.) vs Sound on the one-year view (none of the 3 stress checks (leverage, cash conversion, returns on capital) is flagged.)

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 5 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
