### Bucket 2 — Multibagger patterns (11 patterns)

The long view matches 1 of the 11 winning patterns; the one-year view matches 0. Genuinely re-tested pattern verdicts: 1 strengthened, 2 weakened, 7 unchanged; 1 carried forward unchanged from the long view.

*Foundation test: PARTIAL on the long view, PARTIAL on the one-year view.*

**That strengthened:**

- **Forward Integrators**: NO FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Skipper is a manufacturer that has moved downstream into EPC execution (25 active PGCIL projects, ~5,000 circuit km) and approved subsidiaries in three international regions plus retail distribution build-out in polymers — but there are no own stores, franchise royalties or D2C economics. (The long view had said: Skipper is integrated backwards (rolling mills, fasteners) not forwards; polymer goes through dealers and retailers with no own stores, franchising, royalties or D2C discussed in any of the 15 calls.)

**That weakened:**

- **Market Share Gainers**: LIKELY FIT → PARTIAL. Why now: Order inflows grew 24-33% y-o-y in a year management itself calls muted for industry ordering, exports rose 27-30%, and a first large North American utility order plus plant audits across five regions widen the addressable base — but no explicit market-share percentages versus named competitors are given. (The long view had said: Scale gains are evident — largest tower capacity in India expanding 300k to 450k tonnes (600k by FY28), record FY26 revenue of INR5,553 crores and order book of INR8,502 crores, plus a first North American utility order — but management gives no explicit market-share numbers, and the promised export share (50% of engineering revenue by FY23, 75% by FY24) collapsed to 10% of the order book by FY26.)
- **Brand Strength**: PARTIAL → NO FIT. Why now: Nothing in the last twelve months addresses heritage, A&P spend, consumer pull or premium pricing under a brand; the credibility discussed is technical qualification with utilities, not brand equity. (The long view had said: Management cites brand equity from the R&D centre and tower testing station and a sustained PVC brand-building push, but there is no heritage story, no disclosed A&P spend, and no evidence of premium pricing from the brand across the FY23-FY26 calls.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Friendly Middleman (PARTIAL).*

*Unchanged: Low Price Plus (PARTIAL); Innovation Dominance (PARTIAL); Corporate Culture (PARTIAL); Cost to Replicate (PARTIAL); Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) (NO FIT); Toll Roads (NO FIT); Pricing Power (NO FIT).*

### Bucket 3 — Risks (8 channels)

Genuinely re-tested risk verdicts: 0 eased, 1 worsened, 5 unchanged; 2 carried forward unchanged from the long view. Financial resilience: Strained on the long view, Strained on the one-year view.

**That worsened:**

- **Cyclicality**: ELEVATED → HIGH RISK. Why now: Revenue is almost entirely tied to customer capex — utility/PGCIL transmission line award programmes — and FY26 itself saw 'temporary moderation in ordering' with slower CEA bidding, while management admits 'peaks and troughs'; meanwhile the company is expanding capacity aggressively into the upcycle (450,000 tons by FY26, targeting 600,000 tons by FY28 with ~INR800 crores of capex), the classic expansionary over-investment setup. (The long view had said: Revenue is a pure derivative of customer capex — transmission tower/EPC orders from PGCIL, SEBs and global EPC contractors — and management itself concedes industry ordering swings year to year, with FY26 seeing 'temporary moderation in ordering' and FY27 revenue guidance cut to 15% from the standing 20-25% aspiration; the polymer arm separately took inventory losses when PVC prices collapsed in Q2 FY23. Meanwhile the company is investing hard into the up-cycle (300k → 450k → 600k tons by FY28), which is exactly the over-investment-at-peak pattern.)

*Carried forward from the long view unchanged (the window could not re-test these): Shifting Consumer Preferences (NO SIGNAL); Fashion Risk (NO SIGNAL).*

*Unchanged: Government Dependency (HIGH RISK); Stakeholder Concentration (WATCH); New Entrants (WATCH); Good Enough Goods (WATCH); Technological Innovation (LOW).*

## How this comparison was built

Both sides ran the same three analysis engines (BusinessAnalysis, MultibaggerPattern, QualityRisks) through their respective orchestrators:

- **Long view (full AnalystSkill):** statements over ~a decade, the whole call timeline — engines ran with_calls.
- **Last one year (RecentAnalystSkill):** latest fiscal year (prior year as baseline only), last 12 months of calls — engines ran 6 items carried from the full-history analysis, with_calls.

Comparison rules: only verdicts assessed on both views are counted; one-year-lens silences are listed but never scored as regressions; every movement line carries both sides' evidence so the WHY of the change is visible, not just the direction. Research tooling; not investment advice.
