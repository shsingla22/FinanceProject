### The story in depth

Prime Focus comes out as a Mixed business, scoring 43 out of 100 — built from 48 for business quality, just 10 for multibagger fit, and 72 for risk safety. The picture the numbers paint is of a company that earns a decent margin but has to put a lot of money on the table to do it. Out of every ₹100 of sales, ₹22 is operating profit, yet every ₹1 of assets generates only ₹0.34 of sales, and about 36 days of cash sits trapped between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. Put together, the business turns ₹100 of shareholders' funds plus borrowings into ₹13 of operating cash a year. That is why the Return on Capital area is the only one scoring below neutral (-0.33) and why the overall resilience reading is STRAINED: the healthy-looking margin is doing its work on a heavy, slow base of capital.

The single trait that shows up in the most places is instability. Profit margins have moved by about ±5.3 percentage points a year, and sales growth, while averaging roughly 8% a year, arrives lumpily rather than steadily. That one fact does double duty. On the quality side it holds down three separate checks — barriers to entry and rationality, profit margin, and persistence of growth all score a neutral 0, with the swings read as a sign that key costs sit outside management's control and that price competition or new entrants may be disturbing the industry. On the risk side, the same numerical pattern is what raises the flags: Cyclicality and New Entrants are both marked as QUANT FLAG, meaning the numbers alone were enough to warrant investigation. In other words, the wobble is not two separate problems; it is one problem seen from two directions.

The weakest score of the three is multibagger fit, at 10 out of 100, and the reason is as much about missing evidence as about the company. Only one of eleven patterns — Recurring Revenue with money collected upfront — registers even a partial fit, and every remaining pattern, including Pricing Power, Brand Strength and Market Share Gainers, is marked NO FIT with the same explanation: no conference-call judgement was available. The same gap runs through the risk review, where all five flags — Cyclicality, Technological Innovation, New Entrants, Shifting Consumer Preferences and Fashion Risk, the last with two numeric fingerprints — are numbers-only signals that the calls neither confirmed nor cleared. That is also why three quality areas (Competitive Advantage, Customer Benefits and Management) have no score at all. So the 72 for risk safety should be read as "nothing proven against it" rather than "nothing wrong", and the low pattern score as "not demonstrated" rather than "disproven". Management commentary is the missing ingredient that would move several of these readings at once.
