MECHANISM IN BANKS

In continuation of our letter nos. JKB/BS/F3652/2025/308 dated 05.02.2025 and 
JKB/BS/F3652/2026/271 dated 24.03.2026, wherein it was intimated that the GST authorities had

transactions comprising of two demand orders:  
1. Demand order dated February 04, 2025, from Joint Commissioner, Central GST 
Commissionerate, Jammu for GST liability of Rs. 81,30,66,42,768/ - (Rupees Eight Thousand 
One Hundred Thirty Crores Sixty -Six Lacs Forty -Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Sixty -

2. Demand order dated March 23, 2026, from Additional Commissioner, Central GST 
Commissionerate, Jammu for GST liability of Rs.200,20,80,009.00 (Rupees Two Hundred 
Crores Twenty Lakh Eighty Thousand and Nine Only) with interest as applicable and penalty 
of Rs.200,20,80,009.00 (Rupees Two Hundred Crores Twenty Lakh Eighty Thousand and Nine 
Only).  
 
 
The Bank challenged the aforesaid proceedings before the Hon'ble High Court of Jammu & Kashmir 
and Ladakh and obtained interim protection in respect of the two demand orders.  
 
In furtherance to same, we are providing the following update;  
The Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC), through circular No: 266/12/2026 -GST 
dated 28th October, 2026 has clarified that the notional amount/interest recorded in the Books of 
Accounts against the activity of making a loan or an advance unde r the Fund Transfer Price 
mechanism is covered by the definition of “interest” for the purpose of exemption under 
Notification No. 12/2017 -Central Tax (Rate) dated 28.06.2017. The relevant text of the said circular 
is reproduced hereunder;  
6. Clarification regarding GST treatment of Fund Transfer Pricing (FTP) mechanism in 
banks, i.e., whether GST is applicable on the notional interest recorded in the Books 
of Accounts by Head Office, when funds are notionally transferred from one branch to 
another, through the Head office.  
6.1 Representation has been received seeking clarification regarding applicability of GST on 
the notional interest recorded in the Books of Accounts by the banks under the Fund 
Transfer Pricing (FTP) mechanism, wherein funds are notionally transferred between 
different branches of a bank through its Head Office. FTP is an internal funds allocation 
and performance measurement mechanism used by banks for evaluating the performance 
of different branches or business units.

6.2 It has been observed that in certain cases, demands have been raised on such notional 
amount/interest on the ground that FTP represents a financial service provided by the 
Head Office to its branches since the Head Office and its branches are distinct persons 
under section 25(4) of the CGST Act.  
 
6.3 The matter has been examined. Under the FTP mechanism, the Head Office notionally 
pools funds from deposit -mobilising branches and allocates such funds to lending branches 
at an internally determined price linked to the relevant product or tenure. Such  notional 
amount represents the cost of funds and is akin to interest. Further, the definition of 
"interest" in Notification No. 12/2017 -Central Tax (Rate) covers interest payable in any 
manner in respect of moneys borrowed or debt incurred.  
 
6.4 Therefore, on recommendations of the 57th meeting of GST Council, it is hereby clarified 
that the notional amount/interest recorded in the Books of Accounts against the activity 
of making a loan or an advance under the Fund Transfer Pricing mechanism is covered by 
the definition of "interest" for the purpose of exemption under Notification No. 12/2017 -
Central Tax (Rate) dated 28.06.2017.”  
The Bank has consistently maintained that the relevant book entries represent notional interest in 
nature, arising from the internal FTP mechanism adopted for banking operati ons, and that such 
amounts are in nature if interest which is exempt from GST.  
This stance of the Bank has now been validated through the aforementioned circular issued by 
CBIC.  
In view of the aforesaid clarification, the bank shall pursue the matter with appropriate authorities 
for quashing of above demand orders, in accordance with the applicable provisions.
