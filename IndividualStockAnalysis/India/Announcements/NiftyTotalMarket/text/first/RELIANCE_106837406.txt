Quarterly Performance (2Q FY27 vs 2Q FY26) 
 Gross Revenue  increased by 27.8% Y-o-Y to ₹ 362,268 crore ($ 37.8 billion). 
o JPL revenue increased by 11.3% Y-o-Y driven by continued subscriber additions, ARPU increase 
and healthy growth in digital services. 
o RRVL revenue grew 6.4% Y-o-Y to ₹ 91,716 crore, with performance impacted by the festive 
calendar shift to Q3. 
o Oil to Chemicals (O2C) revenue increased by 44.2% Y-o-Y led by sharp increase in crude and 
product prices. Crude price for the quarter increased 40.6%, whereas transportation fuel prices 
were up 50-78% Y-o-Y. Prices for most of the downstream chemical products were up 25-35% 
Y-o-Y, driven by strong feedstock prices. 
o Oil and Gas segment revenue was stable Y-o-Y. KGD6 gas production as well as price realization 
was lower, which was offset by higher condensate price realization. Further, improved volumes 
and price realization for CBM supported revenues. 
 EBITDA increased by 23.9% Y-o-Y to ₹ 62,409 crore ($ 6.5 billion). 
o JPL EBITDA increased by 16.6% Y-o-Y with strong revenue growth and margin expansion of 250 
bps Y-o-Y. 
o RRVL EBITDA decreased 8.6% Y-o-Y to ₹ 6,228 crore, with margin at 7.8%, down 120 bps Y-o-
Y, reflecting planned investment phase in Digital Commerce, input-cost inflation absorbed for 
customers, and the festive shift to Q3. 
o O2C EBITDA increased by 67.9% Y-o-Y with a steep rise in transportation fuel cracks and 
downstream petrochemical margins. Fuel cracks were up 3-3.7x in a very tight global refining 
environment. Polyester chain margins improved sharply by 33%, led by 15% higher PX delta and 
52% increase in PTA delta. Earnings were constrained by higher crude oil premiums, elevated 
freight rates, SAED related costs and under-recoveries in domestic fuel retail. 
o Oil and Gas segment EBITDA was lower by 2.0% on Y-o-Y basis on account of declining KG D6 
gas revenues and higher GoI levies. This was partly offset by favourable movement in exchange 
rate.

Commenting on the results, Mukesh D. Ambani, Chairman and Managing Director, Reliance 
Industries Limited said : “This week marked a historic moment in Reliance’s value creation journey with 
Jio’s record-setting listing on Indian bourses. With over 22x subscription, and strong listing appreciation, 
the issue has set multiple global benchmarks. JPL is now India’s second largest company. It demonstrates 
the depth of confidence investors place in Jio’s solid foundation and strong growth visibility. I am proud to 
witness the rejuvenation of “the equity cult” originally pioneered by our founder-chairman during the 1977 
Reliance IPO. 
During 2Q FY27, our businesses delivered a strong performance despite a complex operating 
environment. Commodity markets remained volatile while geopolitical tensions continued to weigh on 
global supply chains. Against this backdrop, the domestic orientation of our portfolio, combined with 
operational discipline and versatility of our integrated businesses, helped Reliance navigate external 
volatility effectively. 
Our O2C business delivered a particularly strong performance this quarter, led by tight fuel markets, 
improved downstream petrochemical deltas and firm domestic demand. Our ability to optimise feedstock 
sourcing and product placement along with operations across the value chain enabled us to improve 
margin capture. This performance underscores the strength and resilience of our O2C business. 
The Digital Services business continued its robust growth this quarter. Jio’s performance across mobility, 
home broadband and enterprise was strong, with healthy growth in earnings. Jio has built a proprietary 
technology stack and a growing portfolio of digital services, platforms and enterprise offerings that extend 
well beyond connectivity. The Jio IPO is a defining milestone. The faith placed in us by millions of our 
investors reinforces our commitment to building indigenous, world-class deep-tech solutions to deliver 
sustained value to customers and shareholders.  
Reliance Retail had a steady quarter, with growth sustained across all formats and channels. Consumer 
demand stayed healthy, supported by our extensive physical and digital presence and our continued focus 
on serving the diverse needs of Indian consumers. Our consumer products business kept its growth 
momentum, with the FMCG brand portfolio gaining further ground among Indian consumers. 
Our disciplined capital allocation has helped create businesses that provide sustainable cash-flows and 
will remain significant contributors to India’s energy independence and technology evolution.”
