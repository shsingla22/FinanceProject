Management Discussion and Analysis
Global Pharmaceutical Industry  
Last year witnessed the evolution and expansion of the life sciences 
innovation ecosystem as the industry adapted to a complex 
and dynamic landscape shaped by geopolitical, technological 
and social uncertainties. At the same time, innovative science 
continued to advance, as evidenced by an increasing proportion 
of novel therapeutic modalities in clinical trials and by innovative 
products receiving regulatory approval and reaching patients 
— with multiple important Cell and Gene Therapy (CAGT) drugs 
having achieved success, alongside new options for historically 
challenging indications.
The industry is also undergoing significant regulatory and trade 
shifts that are still underway, focusing on price transparency and 
increasing competition in prescription drug markets. The Inflation 
Reduction Act (IRA) allows the Centres for Medicare and Medicaid 
Services to negotiate drug prices, potentially reducing industry-
wide EBITDA by $50 billion to $70 billion by 20281. The recent 
executive order signed by President Trump to end the IRA ‘Pill 
Penalty’ is aimed to remove the current discrepancy between small 
molecules and biologics. While the executive order does not specify 
the actions the Secretary and Congress must take to address the 
pill penalty, its implementation can help reduce the impact of drug 
price negotiations.
Further complicating the landscape, the Department of Health 
and Human Services (HHS) plans a 20000-position workforce 
reduction, including over 3500 USFDA roles2. This could delay 
drug approvals, increase regulatory uncertainty and raise costs for 
advanced modalities. These challenges emphasize the need for life 
sciences companies to enhance efficiency and cost management 
while fostering innovation and collaboration to sustain growth in a 
complex regulatory and pricing environment.While the pharmaceutical industry continues to face considerable 
challenges, ongoing advancements in technology and data 
integration point to a shift toward a more connected, efficient and 
patient-centric healthcare ecosystem—rather than one defined 
solely by cost pressures and operational constraints. Pairing rapidly 
evolving technologies with optimized portfolios and therapeutic 
innovations can potentially improve efficiency of Research & 
Development (R&D) by reducing drug development timeline 
and increasing probability of success, an important objective for 
biopharmaceutical companies.
Pharma’s focus has shifted towards evolving portfolios driven by 
scientific and technological breakthroughs. Innovations in novel 
modalities, newer mechanisms of action and therapies targeting 
unmet needs have reshaped pipelines. Companies are adopting 
to an ‘always be launching’ mindset alongside pursuing major 
acquisitions to navigate a challenging environment. Despite political 
uncertainties in the US, the industry remains cautiously optimistic 
about Mergers and Acquisitions (M&A) following a subdued 2024.
M&A continue to be a fundamental strategy for pharmaceutical 
companies seeking to diversify their portfolios, access new 
technologies and expand their market presence. As patents expire, 
companies are increasingly targeting biotech companies with 
promising late-stage assets. There is a clear shift towards acquiring 
assets in rapidly expanding fields, with oncology, immunology 
and rare diseases receiving particular attention. M&A activity is 
expected to remain robust, with small to mid-sized transactions 
anticipated to dominate the landscape.

Overall, partnerships in biopharma rose to 771 in 2024, with 
oncology assets featured in 39% of deals—up from 35% in 
20235. Neurology/psychiatry and immunology followed closely, 
maintaining their rankings from the previous year with 107 and 
76 deals, respectively5. Venture investments continued to focus 
heavily on clinical-stage companies, with biologics—spanning 
antibodies, conjugate and nucleic acid-based therapies—
dominating both investment and licensing activities. Bispecific 
antibody therapies boosted biologics deal values as well. Standout 
deals included the Daiichi Sankyo - Merck for a Phase 2 ADC 
program, valued at up to $22 billion, with $4 billion provided upfront5. Small molecules also gained traction, supported by 
increased deal activity and investment flows.
As the industry progresses into 2025, the stagnancy observed 
in funding and deals might overshadow the upcoming year as 
there is selectivity in the investors’ approach. Achieving clinical 
milestones will play a pivotal role in maintaining investors’ trust 
amidst a competitive funding landscape. Naturally, companies 
and assets backed by a positive and robust data set of preferably 
advanced stage assets will remain well-positioned for funds.
Despite persistent uncertainties, the global pharmaceutical 
industry is estimated to grow at a Compound Annual Growth 
Rate (CAGR) of 6.12% from 2025 to 20304. One of the key drivers 
of growth is launch of new drugs, globally, 65 Novel Active 
Substances (NAS) were launched in 2024, down from 80 in 2023 
but still surpassing pre-pandemic levels5.

Oncology R&D emphasized targeted therapies, with 35% of trials 
involving novel modalities like CAGT, ADCs and multi-specific 
antibodies5. Multi-specific antibody trials represented 17% of 
haematological and 11% of solid tumour trials in 2024, reflecting a 
growing focus on addressing multiple targets with single therapies5.
China’s influence on global pharmaceutical R&D continues to 
expand, with Chinese companies now involved in at least 20% 
of the industry’s clinical pipeline5. The country is particularly 
prominent in next-generation modalities, accounting for over half 
of the global clinical pipeline in ADCs, bispecific antibodies and 
CAR-T therapies. Western biopharma companies are increasingly 
seeking in-licensing opportunities from China which is reflecting 
the country’s growing importance, both as an innovation hub 
and a commercial market. Chinese innovative drugs have entered 
global markets since July 2021, with seven of these being licensed 
out to international partners. As new technologies advance and 
the focus sharpens on addressing clinical needs, China’s R&D 
efforts are poised to make significant impact in development of 
innovative therapeutics.
Biopharma Trends 
The biotechnology industry in 2025 stands at a pivotal junction 
where scientific advancement meets practical implementation. 
The convergence of Artificial Intelligence (AI), precision medicine 
and novel therapeutic modalities presents unparalleled 
possibilities for innovation and growth. Many of these trends 
have been developing over time, but their convergence in 2025 is 
driving unprecedented innovation and change. Some of the trends 
that are expected to lay the ground for exponential growth in the 
years to come include the following.
1.   The evolution of AI: A consistent trend reshaping the 
biotech industry
 It is difficult these days to talk about biotech without 
mentioning the revolutionary role that AI is playing in the 
industry. Advancements in AI are expected to continue in 
2025, with the technology likely to become more mainstream. 
From drug discovery and design—where AI accelerates the creation of novel structures—to the ability to analyze a drug 
candidate’s profile across multiple parameters and access 
to databases of both patients and healthy volunteers, the 
wealth of information now available to developers early in 
the process is enabling more efficient and potentially faster 
drug development.
2.  The expansion of gene therapy field
 With the potential to cure numerous genetic disorders by 
introducing, modifying, or repairing genetic material within 
a patient’s cells, gene therapy has been a hot topic in the 
biotech industry for some time now and is continuing to 
evolve. In years past, logistical and commercial challenges 
have been holding back therapies, however, with improved 
data as well as more advanced technology, the industry 
has a better understanding of what works and what needs 
to be improved. The overall gene therapy field will likely 
see growth when it comes to viral and non-viral delivery 
methods. As gene therapy expands, the demand for safer, 
more effective delivery systems will drive innovations in 
these areas and advances in synthetic biology and delivery 
technologies are leading to more refined, customizable 
solutions, ultimately providing more precise and versatile 
options for genetic correction.
3.   GLP-1 drugs: Still an attractive proposition for biotech
 Since their approval, GLP-1 drugs have been a topic of 
interest in the biotech industry. Despite the competition, 
start-ups and big pharma are still looking to create a better 
version of this drug class. GLP-1 receptor agonists have sent 
shockwaves through life sciences, initially as weight loss 
therapy but now showing promise across a broad spectrum 
of diseases beyond weight loss and glycaemic control. Apart 
from their established roles in diabetes management and 
weight loss, GLP-1 therapies are pioneering new therapeutic 
frontiers, including cardiovascular disease prevention, sleep 
apnoea treatment and potential applications in cancer, 
addiction and neurodegenerative diseases. This expansion 
is driving significant investment and research across the
