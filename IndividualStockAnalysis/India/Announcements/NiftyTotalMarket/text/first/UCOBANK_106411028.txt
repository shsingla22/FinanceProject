India’s economic momentum remains resilient, with both industry and services sector sustaining strong growth despite
global trade uncertainties and geopolitical tensions. Prudent macroeconomic management, proactive policy
interventions,and sustainedstructural reforms have supported this stability.
The International Monetary Fund (IMF) in its Oct-25 World Economic Output Report has said the Indian economy is
projected to grow at 6.2 percent in 2025-26. The Reserve Bank of India has raised the overall growth forecasts for the
fiscalyear 2025-26 at 6.8 percent in the Oct-25 MPC meeting.
India's foreign exchange reserve as of 3rd Oct-25 stood at USD 700 Billion, which is sufficient for 11 months of
merchandise imports or 95.4 percent of the country’s outstanding external debt as of end-Mar-25. The cumulative
exports (merchandise & services) during April-August 2025 is estimated at USD 349 Billion, as compared to USD 329 Billion
in April-August 2024, a growth of 6.18 percent.
India’s current account deficit narrowed to 0.2 percent of GDP in Q1FY26 from 0.9 percent a year ago. This was
supported by strong services and remittances of USD 35 Billion, keeping India the world’s largest recipient of private
remittances.
The IIP growth rate for Aug-25 over the corresponding period of previous year is 4.0 percent. The growth rates of the
three sectors, Mining, Manufacturing and Electricity for Aug-25 over Aug-24 are 6.0 percent, 3.8 percent and 4.1
percent respectively.
There is a sharp decline in headline inflation and food inflation in Sep-25 in comparison to Aug-25. The factors
supporting the ongoing disinflation and improved outlook for FY26 include impact of GST rate cuts, benign food prices,
and adequate foodgrainstocks.
