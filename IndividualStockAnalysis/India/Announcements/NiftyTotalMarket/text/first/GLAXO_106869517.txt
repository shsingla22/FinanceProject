GSK Launches CDSCO -Approved BLENREP  for Treatm ent 
of Relapsed /Refractory Multiple Myeloma  
 
▪ BLENREP is approved in India for relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma as combination 
therapy in adult patients who have received at least one prior therapy1. 
▪ Multiple myeloma is the second most common blood cancer globall y2,3, with more than 196,000 
new cases  reported globally  in 20242 including  17,529 new cases reported in India4. It is 
generally considered treatable but not curable.5  
▪ BLENREP’s launch marks the introduction of GSK's third precision oncology therapy in India, 
reinforcing its commitment to improving access to advanced cancer care in areas of unmet 
needs . 
▪ The therapy is b acked by robust clinical evidence from the DREAMM -76 and  
DREAMM -87,8 Phase III trials, with progression -free survival nearly tripling  that of the current 
standard of care .6,7,8 
 
Mumbai, 24th November  2026: GlaxoSmithKline Pharmaceuticals Limited (GSK India) announced the 
launch of BLENREP (belantamab mafodotin) in India as combination therapy for adult patients with 
relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma who have received at least one prior line of therapy1.  
As the world's first approved antibody -drug conjugate (ADC) targeting  
B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA) for multiple myeloma9, BLENREP introduces a new treatment approach 
for Indian patients and expands access to an innovative therapy backed by robust evidence from the 
pivotal DREAMM clinical programme.  
Multiple myeloma is a significant global health challenge, with GLOBOCAN  data reporting  more than 
196,000  new cases globally  in 20242. In India, multiple myeloma accounts for 1. 1% of all new cancer 
cases4, although rates of multiple myeloma are expected to increase  in the future  due to improving life 
expectancy, better healthcare and rapid urbanisation.10

Approved for Public Release  The challenge is compounded by the distinct realities of multiple myeloma care in India . Patients are 
diagnosed a decade earlier compared with Western population s11 and because the disease is 
characterised by repeated relapse12, there is a need for treatment s that can deliver durable disease 
control .12,13 At the same time, access to specialised medical  infrastructure can vary significantly across  
cities and regions  in India14,15 requir ing treatment options that can be administered across a range of 
oncology care settings. BLENREP -containing regimens address these needs by demonstrating significant 
and durable disease control across a broad range of patients  with relapsed or refractory multiple 
myeloma .6,7,8,16  
BLENREP  is supported by extensive  evidence from the DREAMM (D riving Excellence in Approaches to 
Multiple Myeloma) clinical programme, including the pivotal DREAMM -76 and DREAMM -87,8 Phase III 
studies. Across both trials, BLENREP -containing regimens , in second -line and later treatment of relapsed 
or refractory multiple myeloma,  demonstrated clinically meaningful improvements in progression -free 
survival and encouraging overall survival outcomes .  
In DREAMM -7, treatment with a BLENREP -containing regimen  [BLENREP combined with bortezomib plus 
dexamethasone (BorDex)] achieved a median progression -free survival (PFS) of 36.6 months, compared 
with 13.4 months for the daratumumab -based comparator regimen  [daratumumab plus BorDex] , with a 
59% reduction in risk  of progression or death  compared to the comparator regime n.6 In DREAMM -8, a 
BLENREP -containing regimen [BLENREP  (belantamab mafodotin), combined with pomalidomide plus 
dexamethasone (PomDex)], achieved a median PFS of 32.6 months compared with  12.5 months for the 
pomalidomide -based comparator regimen  [bortezomib plus PomDex], with a 51% reduction in the risk of 
progression or death  compared to the comparator regime n.7,8

About BLENREP  
BLENREP  is a monoclonal ADC (antibody -drug conjugate) comprising a humanised BCMA (B -cell 
maturation antigen) conjugated to the cytotoxic agent monomethyl auristatin F via a non -cleavable linker. 
The drug linker technology is licensed from Seagen Inc.; the monoclonal antibody is produced using 
POTELLIGENT Technology licensed from BioWa Inc., a member of the Kyowa Kirin Group .16 
BLENREP is engineered to target myeloma cells through a differentiated mechanism of action .2019,20 As 
an anti -BCMA ADC therapy, B LENREP  works two ways to both detect and attack multiple myeloma cells 
and consists of two parts – an immunotherapy/antibody called belantamab – joined to a potent anti -cancer 
drug called mafodotin.19,20 Unlike other BCMA -targeting therapies, the BLENREP  mechanism of action 
avoids T -cell exhaustion while effectively harnessing the immune system for deep and more durable 
responses .19,20,24,25,26,27,28,29 
BLENREP is approved by leading regulatory authorities worldwide, including the US FDA30, UK’s  MHR A16 
and European Medicines Agency31.

GSK in Oncology  
According to the World Health Organisation, cancer is a leading cause of death worldwide, accounting for 
nearly 10 million deaths in 2024, or nearly one in six deaths.32 These realities highlight a clear and urgent 
need for greater innovation. Cancer puts a substantial burden on healthcare systems and global 
economies. It is estimated that between 2020 and 2050, cancers will cost the global economy ~ USD 25.2 
trillion .33 
At GSK, we believe that getting ahead of disease is the best investment – for patients, communities, health 
systems, and economies. GSK’s  ambition in Oncology is to help increase overall quality of life, maximise 
survival and change the course of disease . Beyond haematological and gynaecological cancers , GSK is 
focusing on lung and gastrointestinal cancers, and other solid tumours, as well as accelerating priority 
programmes including B7H3 and B7H4 targeting ADCs.   
In India, GSK is currently conducting 15 oncology clinical trials, including studies evaluating  dostarlimab  
in head and neck cancer and colon cancer. The company is also advancing trials for  B7H3  ADC  for lung 
and prostate cancer  and B7H4 ADC  for gynaecological cancers.34
