Q: Can you walk us through the key highlights from the quarter?  
A: 
Business momentum remained firm in Q2 FY27. Same
 -
store sales growth (SSSG) 
was 28% YoY for the quarter, despite the shift in the festive season, and 32.9% YoY for 
H1 FY27. Over the past year, a key focus area has been our entry
 -
level price points, 
where gold price inflation had dislocated our offering. Our redesigned range is now 
taking more shelf space in our stores, and this quarter we saw early signs of that 
effort paying off: new customers contributed a larger share of revenue than in the 
previous quar
 ter, and AOV moderated accordingly. Repeat customers contributed 
56% of revenue, up from 51% a year ago. While we are not targeting a specific mix, our 
focus remains on driving growth across both new and repeat customers.  
 
Q: Were there any new investments this quarter?  
A: 
Yes. The key investment this quarter was our new flagship store on Park Street, 
Kolkata. The 3,200 sq. ft. store showcases more than 2,000 unique designs. It also 
introduces a range of handcrafted and antique jewellery, a new addition to our 
portfolio. Traditional elements such as Kundan setting and antique finishes give the 
range its heritage character, while a modern sensibility keeps it relevant for today’s 
consumer.  
 
We continue to widen both what we offer and how consumers experience it. On the 
product side, we keep adding new categories, design languages and price points so 
that consumers can turn to BlueStone for a wider range of occasions and tastes. On

Q: How has distribution progressed this quarter?  
A: 
We had mentioned last quarter that store additions would not be linear through 
the year, and Q2 demonstrates that. This quarter, we front
 -
loaded our distribution 
roll
-
out ahead of the festive end of the year and added 27 stores. This took our store 
count to 379 as of September 2026. Our city coverage expanded by 19 cities YoY, 
with all seven cities entered this quarter being Tier 2 and Tier 3 markets. While the 
opportunity remains large, we continue to expand in a calibrated way, both by 
entering new cities  
and by adding density in existing ones.  
We remain on track with 
our stated distribution objectives for the year.

Q: A&P has grown modestly on an absolute basis YoY 
 – 
is there a conscious 
effort to cut back on A&P?  
A: 
As we had mentioned in our commentary last quarter, A&P spend will have some 
quarterly seasonality but, directionally, has operating leverage over the medium 
term. This quarter, A&P spend grew a modest 5.7% YoY and stood at 6.0% of sales. 
This reflects a combination of factors: scale benefits, the shift of the festive season 
into Q3 compared with last year, and the timing of our cricket sponsorships, which 
falls in 
 Q1. 
 
At a fundamental level, our approach to A&P remains very ROI
 -
driven and there 
aren’t any investment cutbacks. From an ATL perspective, the advertising property 
landscape has undergone a sea change over the past few years, and we focus on 
areas where we can drive ROI on our investments. Given how fragmented 
consumer attention now is, a step change in spend does not deliver a linear ROI. 
A&P investment will keep growing, and we will keep evaluating strategic, long
 -
term 
opportunities.
