HCLTech and Transport for NSW to explore AI -powered traffic insights for India  
 
NOIDA, India, Sept 03 , 2026 —H CLTe c h , a leading global technology company and Transport 
for NSW ( TfNSW )’s Sydney Coordinated Adaptive Traffic System (SCATS) have entered into a  
Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) to explore the advancement of AI -powered analytics 
capabilities for SCATS. The current SCATS tool enables users to interact with traffic, 
congestion and geospatial data using natural language queries and compare insights across 
selected time periods.  
 Developed by the NSW Government in 1975, SCATS is now installed in  over 200 cities, across 
more than 30 countries.  HCLTech will explore integrating Sarvam’s  multilingual foundation 
models with SCATS , enabling traffic operators, planners and engineers to query and analyze  
SCATS data in local Indian languages.  
 The collaboration will also explore ways to address local data sovereignty requirements for 
smart city infrastructure, including enabling cities to operate SCATS AI infrastructure on 
premises using private cloud environments.  
 
“SCATS is a great example of New South Wales innovation with an international impact,” said 
Chris Minns, Premier of New South Wales. “This collaboration with HCLTech will explore how 
AI can make sophisticated traffic information more accessible to the peop le managing 
growing cities in India. It is also another example of the strong technology and economic links 
between New South Wales and India.”  
 
“The world’s major cities are expanding at an unprecedented pace, creating new 
opportunities to reimagine how technology can manage increasingly complex transport 
networks ,” said Swapan Johri, President, Growth Markets, HCLTech. “By bringing together 
SCATS’ proven traffic management capabilities with HCLTech’s AI and engineering expertise, we want to put powerful traffic insights directly into the hands of operators. This coll aboration 
has the potential to combine Australian innovation with Indian technology and talent to help build smarter, more responsive cities.”
