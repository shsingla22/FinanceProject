 Holiday Travel Demand Diverges as Hotel Searches Rise and Flight Bookings Lag 
 
 Flight bookings for the 2026 winter holidays are down in almost every major region, while hotel searches are up for 
key European destinations and the US, according to data from RateGain and Sojern. 
 Travel interest remains strong, even as airfares dampen demand: hotel searches are up 19% for France, 15% for 
Portugal, and 12% for Italy, despite flights to those countries declining 9–17%. 
 Travellers are finding ways to get away without going far: Domestic flights within Portugal are up 46%, while domestic 
flights within Latin America countries have increased 11%. 
India, 25 November 2026:  RateGain Travel Technologies Limited (BSE: 543417, NSE: RATEGAIN), today 
unveiled the trends shaping the 2026 winter holiday travel season, with travellers continuing to plan festive 
trips against a backdrop of rising airfares and wider travel uncertainty.  
 
Based on data from RateGain, the leading AI-powered travel platform, and Sojern, a RateGain brand, which 
together draw on the world's largest travel intent dataset, flight bookings for the Christmas and New Year 
period are running behind last year in nearly every major market. However, hotel searches tell a different 
story: interest in holiday stays is up year-on-year among US travellers (+10%) and for destinations including 
France (+19%), Portugal (+15%), Italy (+12%), Spain (+8%), and the UK (+5%). 
 
“Travel is an incredibly dynamic industry, and this holiday season shows how quickly travellers adapt. Flight 
bookings are softer in many markets, but rising hotel searches tell us the desire to travel hasn’t gone 
anywhere,” said Bhanu Chopra, Founder & MD, RateGain.  “People are rethinking when, where and how 
they go, with shorter trips proving more resilient. For travel brands, these final weeks before the holidays 
are about reading those shifts in the data as they happen, and responding in real time to win over travellers 
who haven’t yet decided where to go.” 
 
The data suggests that travellers still want to get away for the holidays, but are thinking harder about how 
they get there.  Jet fuel prices have climbed sharply  since the conflict began at the end of February, and holiday 
fares have followed. Hopper reports that Christmas airfares are at a  ten-year high , while Ryanair has warned 
that European fares could rise by up to  20% next year  if fuel costs stay high. Capacity cuts and recent 
disruption are also shaping when and how people book. 
 
With between 17% and 25% of holiday flight bookings still to come across the markets analysed, the final 
weeks before Christmas and New Year remain an important window for hotels, airlines, and destinations to 
turn that interest into bookings. That window includes Travel Tuesday on 1 December. Sojern data shows it 
was the biggest day for US flight bookings in both 2024 and 2025, with bookings around 49% above a typical 
Tuesday, though this covers travel throughout the year rather than Christmas alone. 
  
Americans stay closer to home, but New York shines 
 
Flight bookings from the US for the winter holidays are down 5% year-on-year, yet hotel searches are up 
10%, perhaps suggesting Americans are still planning trips but leaning towards those that are easier and 
cheaper to reach by car. The data runs through 12 November and so predates the FAA's nationwide ground 
stop on 13 November. 
 Travel within the US is broadly holding steady (-2%), while international routes have softened more 
sharply: Europe (-14%), Canada (-13%), the Caribbean (-13%), Mexico (-12%), and Asia (-7%). 
 Mexico remains Americans' top international destination by far, with 12.5% of international flight 
bookings, followed by the UK (6.6%), Canada (5.0%), the Dominican Republic (4.0%) and Japan 
(3.8%).

 New York stands out as a festive favourite, with hotel searches for the city up 30%, even as flights are

Europe: hotel interest holds as flight bookings fall 
 
Flight bookings by European travellers are down 16% year-on-year, with hotel searches broadly flat (-2%). 
Bookings are lower to every region, including within Europe (-16%), North America (-17%), Latin America 
(-13%) and Asia (-11%). Spain remains the top destination for travel within Europe (14.6% of flight 
bookings), ahead of Germany (9.5%) and the UK (9.3%), while Thailand leads in Asia (20.7%) and Canada 
leads across the Americas (27.8%). 
 
Across Europe's biggest destinations, the same pattern repeats: fewer flights, more hotel searches. 
 
 France: The widest gap of any country analysed, with flights down 17% and hotel searches up 19%. 
Strasbourg, home to one of Europe's best-known Christmas markets, accounts for 7.7% of hotel 
searches, second only to Paris (76.1%). 
 Portugal:  Flights are down 9%, but hotel searches are up 15%, and domestic flights have jumped 
46%. Brazilians account for almost a third (30.5%) of hotel searches, and Madeira (30.9%) is second 
only to Lisbon (53.5%). 
 Italy: Flights are down 16% and hotel searches are up 12%. Americans are the largest source of both 
flight bookings (21.0%) and hotel searches (27.9%), with Rome drawing almost half (48.5%) of all 
hotel searches. 
 Spain: Flights are down 12% and hotel searches are up 8%, with domestic flights edging up 2%. 
German travellers make up 13.1% of flight bookings, second only to the Spanish, and winter sun is a 
draw, with Tenerife (11.3%) and Gran Canaria (5.2%) among the top hotel destinations. 
 UK: Flights are down 18%, and hotel searches are up 5%. Americans account for a quarter (24.6%) 
of flight bookings, while Edinburgh (6.4%) is the most searched UK hotel destination outside London 
with Hogmanay on the horizon. 
Saturday 26 December is the most searched hotel date across all five markets. Flight bookings peak on 
Wednesday 23 December for Italy, Spain and the UK, and on Saturday 19 December for France and Portugal. 
 
Middle East and Africa: conflict weighs on regional links 
The Middle East and Africa have seen the sharpest fall in holiday flight bookings of any region, amid the 
ongoing conflict.  
 Flight bookings from the Middle East and Africa into Europe and the wider region are down 25% 
year-on-year, with bookings from the region into the UK down 29%.

 European flight bookings into the region are also down 25%. The United Arab Emirates remains the

Latin America: Brazil bucks the trend as Mexico softens 
Across Latin America, travellers are also staying closer to home. Domestic flight bookings are up 11% year-
on-year and travel within the region is close to flat (-2%), while bookings from the US (-11%) and Europe (-
14%) have fallen. 
 Mexico is the only destination analysed where both flight bookings (-13%) and hotel searches (-12%) 
are down, with flights from the US and Latin America each down 12% and from Europe down 21%. 
 Americans still account for almost half (48.0%) of flight bookings to Mexico, followed by Canadians 
(19.5%). Cancún is the top Latin American destination for travellers from North America (21.8%) 
and from Europe (12.7%). 
 Brazil is the standout, with hotel searches up 22%, the strongest growth of any country analysed, 
while flight bookings are almost level with last year (-3%). 
 That strength is driven by Brazil's neighbours. Flight bookings from elsewhere in Latin America are 
up 29%, with Argentina the largest international source of hotel searches after the US (6.1%). 
 Brazil also celebrates on its own calendar. Its most searched hotel dates are 29 and 30 December, as 
travellers book in for New Year's Eve (Réveillon), compared with 26 December in most other 
markets. 
India: festive demand stays close to home as global interest grows 
India's festive season is following the same pattern seen elsewhere, with travellers staying closer to home. 
Hotel bookings to India made in October, ahead of Diwali in early November, rose 20% year-on-year, driven 
by domestic stays (+31%). International hotel bookings dipped slightly (-2%). That leaves room for growth 
as India steps into the spotlight, after Travel + Leisure named it its  Destination of the Year for 2027 . 
India is also on the map for holiday travellers further afield over Christmas: 
 It is the third most-booked Asian destination for Europeans travelling over the winter holidays 
(11.3%), behind Thailand and China. 
 Among Americans, India ranks ninth for international holiday flight bookings (2.9%), narrowly ahead 
of Italy (2.8%) and Spain (2.7%). 
Looking at the past year, Indian travellers continue to head to Europe. Flight bookings from India to Europe 
rose 5% for trips taken between October 2025 and September 2026, even as searches fell 18%. That makes 
India the fifth-largest source market for Europe from outside the region, with London taking 28% of 
bookings.
