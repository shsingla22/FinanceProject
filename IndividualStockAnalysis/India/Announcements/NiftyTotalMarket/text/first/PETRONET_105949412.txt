NEW DELHI : Union home and 
cooperation minister Amit Shah 
on Saturday said India was on 
course to achieving its target of 
20% ethanol blending by 2026 — 
sooner than the 2030 deadline set
by Prime Minister Narendra Modi.
Addressing an efficiency
awards ceremony of the National 
Federation of Cooperative Sugar 
Factories Limited, Shah said, com-
pared to zero diversion of sugar for
ethanol production in 2013-14, the
country now diverts about 4.5 mil-
lion tonnes of sugar for ethanol 
production.
Mixing petrol with ethanol is a
high-priority national programme
aimed at lessening the amount of 
oil India imports. Ethanol can be 
produced from sugarcane, rice, 
maize, and biomass. On June 5, 
2021, Prime Minister Narendra 
Modi announced the advancing of
the target year for 20% ethanol-
blending in petrol by 2030.
The minister said for every 50
billion litres of petrol sold, the 
requirement of ethanol would be 
10 billion litres.
He added: “We have been suc-
cessful in increasing the area of 
sugarcane production by about 
18% from five million hectares in 
2013-14 to over six million hectares
in just 10 years.”
Sugarcane production
increased by 40% from 352 million
tonnes to 491 million tonnes dur-
ing the same period, he said. 
According to the figures cited
by the Shah, the sugar industry’s 
average production of ethanol 
used to be 380 million litres earlier
and there was limited use of the 
product. Ethanol output has 
increased to 3.7 billion litres cur-
rently. “The direct benefit of all 
this has gone into the pockets of 
the farmers,” he said.
He added that ethanol blending
and output were being constantly
monitored by the PM through a 
group of ministers, who review it
