1. The present appeal has been filed by the Appellant i.e., Mr. Abhay Sapre 
who is Suspended Director of FTF Pharma Private Limited under Section 61 of 
the Insolvency and B ankruptcy Code, 2016 (‘Code’)  against the Order dated 
09.09.2026 ("Impugned Order")  passed by the Hon’ble National Company 
Law Tribunal, Ahmedabad Bench, Court - II (“Adjudicating Authority” ) in C.P. 
(IB) No. 346 (AHM) OF 2025] . 
Immacule Lifesciences Privat e Limited , who is an Operational Creditor  is 
the Respondent No.1 herein.  
Mr. Sunil Kumar Kedia , Interim Resolution Professional of FTF Pharma 
Private Limited (IBBI/IPA -001/IP -P00028/2016 -2017/10064 ) is the Respondent 
No.2 herein.  
2. The Appellant stated that the Corporate Debtor is a company incorporated 
under the Companies Act, 1956, in 2012 and is engaged primarily in 
pharmaceutical development and formulation research. After investing 
substantial time and resources in developing Ph ytonadione Injection, the 
Corporate Debtor intended to commercialise the product in the United States of 
 
Page 3 of 27 
 America by obtaining the requisite regulatory approval through an Abbreviated 
New Drug Application (“ANDA”). Since the Corporate Debtor was engaged in 
research activities rather than manufacturing, it approached the Operational 
Creditor, which was engaged in the manufacture of finished dosage products on 
a contractual basis.  
3. The Appellant submitted that, following negotiations, the Corporate 
Debtor and t he Operational Creditor executed a Technology Transfer Agreement 
(“TTA ”) dated 04.05.2022 for the technology transfer and development of the 
finished product, namely, Phytonadione Injection having a strength of 10 mg/ml, 
packaged in 1 ml amber glass vials,  with a commercial batch size of 50 litres and 
conversion costs of USD 2.8 per vial, excluding applicable GST. The 
consideration specified under the Agreement was Rs.3,35,00,000, excluding 
GST. The Appellant contended that the claims subsequently raised by  the 
Operational Creditor arose from this Agreement and could not be considered 
independently of the contractual obligations, deliverables and conditions 
governing the payment milestones.  
4. The Appellant stated that, under Clauses 4.1, 4.3 and 4.7 of the TTA , read 
with the relevant annexures, the parties were required to undertake the 
technology -transfer activities in accordance with the prescribed responsibility 
matrix and technology -transfer plan. The Operational Creditor was responsible 
for various technic al and logistical activities, including vendor qualification, 
procurement, analytical method validation, method transfer and qualification, and 
 
Page 4 of 27 
 the release of the API, excipients, packaging materials and exhibit batches, 
without any additional cost. Althou gh the Corporate Debtor could be required to 
reimburse certain expenses incurred by the Operational Creditor, such 
reimbursement was subject to the applicable contractual conditions, including 
prior written authorisation wherever stipulated. The Appellant contended that the 
Operational Creditor could not unilaterally incur expenditure or raise invoices 
contrary to the allocation of responsibilities under the TTA and subsequently treat 
such disputed amounts as an undisputed operational debt.  
5. The Appellant su bmitted that the TTA also prescribed specific conditions 
for the completion of payment milestones. Under Clause 6, read with Annexure 
IV and the relevant stability -study requirements, the entitlement to payment for 
Milestone 2 depended upon the timely comp letion of the prescribed three -month 
stability data for the exhibit batches, while Milestone 3 was linked to completion 
of the requisite six -month stability data for the prescribed temperature conditions. 
The Corporate Debtor contended that the completion and acceptance of the 
relevant deliverables were essential contractual prerequisites to the release of 
milestone payments. Therefore, merely raising invoices could not establish that 
the milestones had been completed or that the corresponding amounts had 
become unconditionally payable.
