Page 2 of 25 Letter from the Founders  
Dear Shareholders,  
Last quarter we told you  that  India has not bought its glasses yet. It is only fair to tell you what kind of company it will 
take to change that. We believe it needs four things this industry never had. It will be an engineering company first, 
because the hardest problems in eyewear, the eye test above all, will not be solved by hiring or spending more. It will 
own its supply chain end to end, to make a ₹500 pair profitably and give it the experience of a ₹50,000 one. It will build 
brands for everyone, from a first pair to the one someone has always wanted. And it will never believe its customers 
are satisfied enough; it will innovate continuously and keep surprising them. That is the company we are building . It is 
easier said than done: it has to push harder, think like founders and builders, and change as fast as the world.  
If one word holds all four, it is evolve. We named our new concept store  Lenskart  evolv for that reason. It opened in  
Worli,  Mumbai, on a belief that the future of eyewear will be shaped not just by what we make but by how people 
discove r it. It brings design, culture and technology into one immersive space, the first expression of what an eyewear 
destination, and the Lenskart brand, can be. Walk in, spend an hour, and you will get a flavo ur of the culture we are 
building and the bar we hold ourselves to: raising the standard, refusing the status quo, thinking from first principles.  
Now , the quarter. Revenue grew 32.5% YoY to ₹2,845 Cr, EBITDA grew to ₹624 Cr (₹392 Cr pre -IndAS, up 65.0%), and 
PAT grew 2.1x to ₹232 Cr. 80 lakh eye tests globally ; India SSSG 20.8%. H1 revenue: ₹5,559 Cr, up 33.0%, EBITDA ₹1,213 
Cr (₹753 Cr pre -IndAS), and PAT ₹461 Cr, 87% of full year of FY26. Over the trailing 12 months, revenue crossed ₹10,000 
Cr, EBITDA ₹2,200 Cr (₹1,300 Cr pre -IndAS) and PAT almost ₹800 Cr. Measure us over years, not quarters.   
We have said too little about Hustlr Club: a face scan on the app, a colo ur, and a complete pair, frame, lenses and 
warranty, for ₹500. Hundreds of thousands  did this quarte r. It is how we hope the next 100 million Indians find us.  A 
year ago, AI at Lenskart meant choosing store locations. Today it runs through the company: it screens every hire, 
trains every new Lenskartian before they meet a customer, answers our customer calls with no IVR and no queue, and 
decides which frame goes to which store. Thousands of  store associates start the day with Saathi, our self -learning 
AI assistant. B, our smart glasses, is in 500 stores and online; 90% of buyers took it with a prescription.  
What all this is for. In India, the next curve is 100 million pairs a year, from 32 million today. Affordability, access and 
awareness  stand in the way; Hustlr  Club answers the first, the eye test the other two, and 100 million pairs needs 80 
million tests. Remote optometry, now in 934 stores, let us open 94 Tier 2+ stores this quarter, a store a day. Our AI-
assisted  eye -test now has a name, Drysh: you do most of it yourself , with an optometrist supervising, so each stands 
behind many more tests. Hyderabad, built for 50 million pairs a year, is around twelve months away.  
In the world, International grew 29.5%, with a second straight quarter above 10% EBITDA (pre -IndAS) margin. We 
continue to build a global brand for the next generation of consumers. Meller opens its next experience store on Via 
del Corso, Rome, in November. Our brands are learning to speak culture: John Jacobs with Shantanu & Nikhil and 
Masaba; Sh unya , inspired by Indian music; and Meller  with  Diwali edition of its sunglasses for India.  
Where we were tested. Product margin held at 70.0% through a year of the Rupee falling against the RMB. Our teams 
answered with 72% more frames and 52% more lenses from our own plants; that absorbed the currency, and that 
resilience is what we are building . Marketing fell to 4.5% of India revenue, not all of it efficiency; we want to deploy 
more and are waiting for opportunities that build the brand for years, not a quarter. Holding our standard at a store a 
day is hard: NPS had dipped to 77.6 in June and is back at 79.6; that recovery, on a ship this size, is what we are 
proudest of.   
Last quarter we invited you to walk with us in Drishti ki Daud. You walke d 800 Mn steps, 15 times round the Earth, and 
30,000 underprivileged people got the glasses they needed. Thank you. Also this qua rter, NSE Sustainability Ratings

but not move off it. What we have is full clarity of purpose. Thank you for building the future  with us.  It is still Day Zero.

Page 5 of 25 Factory to Face: The Path to 25%  
Shareholders and analysts ask us one question more than any other: how does Lenskart reach a 25% EBITDA 
(pre -IndAS 116) margin? This section is our answer: why we believe the business is built for it, and why we are in 
no hurry to get there.  
Our margin comes from one thing above all: how much of the chain we own, factory to face.  
Seven things decide the profit on a pair of glasses: who owns the brand, who makes the frame, who makes the 
lens, who edges and fits the pair, who sells it, who does the R&D on materials, process and design, and how the 
whole operation is run. In a conventional process , as in most categories, the first five sit with five different 
companies, a brand, a frame maker, a lens maker, a fitter and a retailer, and each keeps a slice of the price.  
Each can only ever improve its own slice, and when a pair needs fixing, no one is answerable to the customer for 
the whole of it. The last two, the R&D that only pays when you own the whole chain, and the way the company 
itself is run, belong to no one in that chain.  
Lenskart chose to own all seven: the brands on the temple, owned or held under exclusive licence  for our 
markets; the frame, moulded in our own plants; the lens, surfaced and coated in our own labs; the fitting, done 
centrally; our own R&D in materials, process and design; the store, app and home eye test, with no third -party 
retailer between; and a company run on its own systems and data.  
We did not do it for margin. We did it so that one party answers to the customer for the whole pair, a single throat 
to choke, and so that what we learn from every pair goes into the next. It is also why a ₹500 pair and a ₹50,000 
pair come out of the same factory with the same quality  standards . But each of the seven is also a pool of profit, 
and owning all of them lets us work on the whole system rather than one slice.
