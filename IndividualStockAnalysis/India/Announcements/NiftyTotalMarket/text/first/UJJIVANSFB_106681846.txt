India’s growth is fundamentally driven by the economic ambitions 
of its people. Across the nation, a profound shift is being noticed—
families securing their futures through systematic savings and robust 
protection, entrepreneurs expanding their enterprises and farmers 
accessing productive capital to enhance yields. Today, priorities like home 
ownership, asset creation, education, and long-term planning are vital 
goals for a rapidly rising aspirational population. These evolving dynamics 
demand more than basic credit access; they require comprehensive 
banking solutions that support every stage of financial progression.
Ujjivan SFB is engineered precisely to address these shifting dynamics. 
The Bank serves customers across a full spectrum of savings, credit, 
investments, protection, and transaction banking.  The product 
offerings spans across micro-banking, housing, MSME, agriculture, gold 
loan, vehicle finance, retail liabilities, Trust, Association, Society and 
Club (TASC) and Retail Institution and Government Banking (RIGB). It’s 
distribution model seamlessly integrates physical, assisted, and digital 
channels, ensuring customers engage precisely at their level of financial 
familiarity. 
As modern India’s aspirations scale, Ujjivan SFB expands alongside them - 
ensuring every dream finds its home, and every aspiration finds its heart.Where Every Aspiration Finds Its Heart.

Dear Shareholders,
Ujjivan was founded with a clear and enduring purpose: to 
make financial services accessible, responsible and relevant 
for those who have traditionally remained underserved. FY 
2025–26 was a year of steady progress, one that required 
resilience and disciplined decision-making as we continued 
to strengthen our foundations and advance our mission of 
financial inclusion.
Operating Environment
The global environment remained uncertain through FY 
2025-26, shaped by geopolitical tensions and shifting 
trade dynamics. India, however, continued to demonstrate 
resilience: GDP growth for the year came in at 7.6%, 
anchored by domestic demand, government capital 
expenditure and policy continuity. Consumer Price Index 
(CPI) inflation declined sharply to 2.1%, well within the 
RBI’s target band of 2–6%. This allowed the Monetary 
Policy Committee to pivot decisively, cutting the repo rate by a cumulative 125 basis points during FY 2025-26, from 
6.50% to 5.25%, the steepest easing cycle in recent years. 
Lower interest rates and improved household cash flows 
had a direct bearing on our business. Repayment capacity 
improved, and demand strengthened across micro-banking 
and affordable housing, our two largest segments.
India’s banking sector continued to play an important role 
in supporting economic growth, expanding financial access 
and deepening inclusion across customer segments. During 
FY 2025-26, the industry benefited from healthy credit 
demand, improving liquidity conditions and accelerating 
digital adoption, while maintaining a strong focus on risk 
management and deposit mobilisation. The microfinance 
sector, which had experienced stress during FY 2024-25, 
began to stabilise as industry-led guardrails strengthened 
credit discipline and supported an improvement in 
collection trends. Demand across affordable housing, MSME 
lending and other secured retail segments also remained 
encouraging, creating opportunities for banks pursuing 
diversified and sustainable growth.India’s banking sector continued to

Performance Overview
FY 2025-26 was a year of stable operating performance 
alongside continued investment in long-term capabilities, 
a balance we view as essential to building a franchise that 
performs consistently across cycles. Building on the progress 
made in FY 2024-25, we continued to strengthen our balance 
sheet, diversify our portfolio and improve the quality of 
growth across the franchise.
Our total balance sheet increased from ₹47,689 Crores in 
FY 2024-25 to ₹57,541 Crores in FY 2025-26, underscoring 
the steady expansion of our franchise and the momentum 
across both assets and liabilities. Net Interest Income 
increased to ₹3,871 Crores in FY 2025-26 from ₹3,636 Crores 
in FY 2024-25, driven by growth in the loan portfolio and a 
steady Net Interest Margin (NIM). Pre-Provision Operating 
Profit improved to ₹1,710 Crores from ₹1,689 Crores in the 
previous year, supported by business growth, operating 
efficiencies and continued investments in productivity-
enhancing digital capabilities.
Profit After Tax stood at ₹693 Crores in FY 2025-26, 
compared with ₹726 Crores in FY 2024-25, reflecting 
elevated credit costs and higher provisioning requirements, 
particularly during the earlier part of the year amid stress 
in the microfinance sector. However, underlying operating 
performance improved progressively through the year, 
supported by portfolio diversification, improving collection 
trends and strengthening asset quality indicators.
Return on Assets stood at 1.4%, compared with 1.6% in FY 
2024-25, while Return on Equity stood at 10.9%, compared 
with 12.4% in the previous year. These metrics were 
impacted by elevated provisioning during the year and 
should be viewed in the context of our prudent approach 
to balance sheet strengthening and risk management. Net 
Interest Margin remained healthy at 8.1%, reflecting the 
resilience of our core lending franchise.
Net Worth increased to ₹6,816 Crores from ₹6,083 Crores 
in FY 2024-25, supported by sustained profitability and 
internal capital generation. Our Capital Adequacy Ratio 
remained robust at 21.14% for FY 2025-26, providing a 
strong foundation to support future growth, portfolio 
diversification and long-term value creation.
Strengthening the Deposit Franchise
Our total deposits grew from ₹37,630 Crores in FY 2024-25 
to ₹45,668 Crores in FY 2025-26, reflecting the resilience of 
our deposit franchise and our ability to attract and retain 
granular retail deposits in a competitive market. Retail term 
deposits and CASA together reached ₹31,955 Crores, and 
our CASA ratio improved to 28.6% from 25.5% in FY 2024-25. 
Liquidity remained comfortable, with a Liquidity Coverage 
Ratio of 142% as at March 2026. What is encouraging is 
not just the growth itself, but the quality of this growth. 
The steady build-up in retail deposits and CASA reflects increasing customer confidence and a more balanced funding 
mix. This provides us with greater stability in a dynamic 
interest rate environment and strengthens our ability to 
support asset growth in a calibrated manner. These trends, 
in our view, are a clear indicator of the trust our customers 
place in us and our continued efforts to build a sustainable 
and resilient funding profile.
Our Gross Loan Book grew by 27% to ₹40,655 Crores during 
the year, reflecting steady progress in expanding our 
lending product suite while remaining aligned with our risk 
philosophy.
An important aspect of this growth has been our continued 
shift towards secured lending. The secured portfolio grew 
by 44% to ₹20,079 Crores and now constitutes 49.4% of 
our overall loan book. We see this transition as central to 
building long-term stability and resilience, as it not only 
strengthens the quality of our assets but also brings greater 
balance to our portfolio over time.
We witnessed strong growth across multiple segments, 
with Affordable Housing growing 35% to ₹8,900 Crores, 
Micro Mortgages 118% to ₹1,577 Crores, MSME advances 
58% to ₹3,230 Crores, Gold Loans 292% to ₹769 Crores, 
Vehicle Finance 102% to ₹944 Crores and Agri Banking 
127% to ₹731 Crores. This broad-based expansion reflects 
the increasing diversification of our portfolio, with each of 
these segments contributing meaningfully to growth while 
reducing concentration risk and enhancing the granularity 
of our loan book. Our Financial Institutions Group portfolio 
stood at ₹3,000 Crores, further supporting diversification 
and providing an additional avenue for calibrated growth.
At the same time, our microfinance portfolio remained 
stable, with Group Loans at ₹14,696 Crores, representing 
36% of the total portfolio. We believe this reflects a 
measured and disciplined approach, where we continue to 
retain our core strengths in microfinance while consciously 
moderating concentration and building a more balanced 
portfolio mix.
Asset Quality and Risk Discipline
Asset quality improved during the year, with Gross NPA 
moderating to 2.3% and Net NPA to 0.4%, reflecting the 
effectiveness of our underwriting, monitoring and collection 
efforts. Our Provision Coverage Ratio was maintained at 
81%, reflecting what we believe is a prudent and forward-
looking approach, ensuring that we remain well-prepared 
to navigate potential uncertainties while protecting the 
strength of our balance sheet.
On the other hand, collection efficiency on our overall 
loan book improved to 98.3% in Q4 FY 2025-26. From our 
perspective, this improvement is particularly encouraging, 
as it reflects both better borrower behaviour and the 
effectiveness of our on-ground engagement and risk 
management practices.
