Hexaware Hosts Greater Manchester and West 
Yorkshire Mayoral Delegation, Deepens Partnership  with 
University of Leeds  
Part of the Great North Mayoral Delegation’s visit to India, discussions focused on Hexaware’s 
UK expansion and planned R&D centers in Leeds and Manchester  
October 23, 2026  – Hexaware Technologies (NSE: HEXT), an AI -ﬁrst digital and IT services 
company, hosted a delegation led by Tracy Brabin, Mayor of West Yorkshire, and Bev 
Craig, Mayor of Greater Manchester, as part of The Great North’s ﬁrst joint mayoral -led 
trade missio n to India  
Hexaware met with Tracy Brabin and delegates from West Yorkshire at its Mumbai office to discuss the company’s UK investment plans, regional employment, and opportunities 
for technology collaboration across the North of England.  
During the visit, Hexaware and the University of Leeds discussed their  agreement to 
strengthen industry –academic collaboration through research, knowledge sharing, and 
experiential learning opportunities for students.  
Earlier in the mission, on October 14 in Bengaluru, Hexaware leaders met Bev Craig, 
Mayor of Greater Manchester, together with representatives of the British Deputy High 
Commission and the Invest Manchester team, to discuss Hexaware’s investment in the 
city region.  
Hexaware announced that it will create hundreds of jobs and invest £9 million in a new 
Design and Innovation Centre at Manchester’s innovation district, SISTER, delivered in 
collaboration with the University of Manchester.  By establishing a base at SISTER , 
Hexaware will have access to Europe’s largest clinical academic campus on Manchester’s Oxford Road Corridor and a rapidly expanding digital and tech ecosystem 
within the city.  
The visits follow Hexaware’s June 2026 announcement of a £25 million UK expansion, 
expected to create approximately 1,200 jobs over three to ﬁve years across Manchester, Leeds, and Birmingham, including 420 in Manchester and 240 in Leeds. Plans include 
proposed R&D centers in Manchester and Leeds, with Newcastle also under 
consideration as a potential future location, given the region’s strong university talent pool and proximity to Scotland.  
 
 
 
 
 
Parameshwaran Iyer, Executive Vice President & Head – Europe, Hexaware , said, 
“The North of England is an important part of our UK growth plans. Our investment goes 
into developing technology capabilities  and recruiting graduates from the region’s 
universities,  training them to work with enterprise and public -sector customers. Our new 
agreement with the University of Leeds will strengthen this pipeline, giving us closer 
access to graduate talent as we grow our presence in the region. We see considerable potential in the talent and expertise available across these cities.”  
Tracy Brabin, Mayor of West Yorkshire , said, “ It was great to visit Hexaware's 
headquarters in Mumbai and hear ﬁrst -hand about the company's ambitious plans for 
growth in the UK. Their investment in Leeds and the creation of hundreds of new jobs is 
fantastic news for West Yorkshire and a strong vote of conﬁdence in our region. Trade 
missions like this give us the opportunity to meet decision -makers  directly, strengthen 
international relationships and explore how businesses like Hexaware can deepen their 
links with West Yorkshire to create a stronger, better -off region that works for all."   
Bev Craig, Mayor of Greater Manchester , said, “Greater Manchester has established 
strengths in healthcare, life sciences, and AI, supported by close cooperation between 
universities, researchers, and industry. We welcome Hexaware’s plans for Manchester 
and the opportunities they present for investment, innovation, and employment in these 
sectors.”  
Glenn McCauley, Head of Proposition Development at the University of Leeds, said, 
“We were delighted to join this Great North Mayoral Delegation to India and to discuss 
with Hexaware leadership and with the Mayor of West Yorkshire how the University of Leeds’ agreement with Hexaware can be a catalyst to drive economic growth in our 
region.”  
India and the North of England are connected above all through people, with more than 
81,000 people of Indian heritage living in Yorkshire and the Humber, and 50,000 people 
of Indian diaspora in Greater Manchester. Thousands of Indian students also choose 
universities in the two regions  every year. Increasingly, Indian companies are also among 
the region’s investors and employers. The India –UK Comprehensive Economic and Trade 
Agreement (CETA), in force since July 2026, alongside The Great North’s investment initiatives, gives that relati onship fresh momentum.  
Hexaware will continue discussions with regional partners on its proposed R&D facilities, 
graduate recruitment, and technology partnerships across the North of England, drawing 
on Leeds’  strengths in digital engineering, data science, robotics, ﬁntech, and 
technology- enabled care, and Manchester’s strengths in healthcare, life sciences, cyber, 
and AI.
