Rationale and key rating drivers 
Reaffirmation of ratings to long-term instruments and certificate of deposits of Bank of Barod a (BOB) continues to factor in 
majority ownership by Government of India (GOI) and expectation of continued support, giv en the systemic importance of BOB 
being one of the largest public sector banks (PSB) in India.  
 
Ratings also favourably factor in the bank’s established franchise with a pan -India branch network, which helps it garner deposits 
at competitive rates and its sizeable international presence (~19% of total advances as on March 31, 2026), which provides 
diversification to the advances profile. Ratings continue to derive strength from the bank’s comfortable capitalisation levels  and 
improvement in headline asset quality ratios, leading to lower credit costs, w hich has supported improved profitability. However, 
the bank’s ability to contain incremental slippages and maintain its asset qualit y remains a monitorable.  
 
CARE Ratings Limited (CareEdge Ratings) expects moderation in BOB’s profitability in FY27 affected largely by one -time provision 
of ₹5,680 crore related to out-of-court settlement with the joint administrators of NMC Health PLC, NMC Healthcare Limited and 
NMC Holding Limited.  
 
CareEdge Ratings has withdrawn ratings of ‘CARE AAA; Stable’ assigned to the Tier -II Bond (ISIN:  INE077A08098) with immediate

Detailed description of key rating drivers 
 
Key strengths 
Majority ownership and expected continued support from the GoI 
The GoI continues to be majority shareholder, holding 63.97% stake in BOB as on J une 30, 2026. The GoI has been supporting 
PSBs with regular capital infusions and steps to improve capitalisation, opera tional efficiency, and asset quality. Given the majority 
ownership of GoI and BOB’s systemic importance being one of the largest PSBs in the domesti c banking system, the bank is 
expected to receive timely adequate capital and operational support from the GoI , when required, and will remain a key rating 
sensitivity. 
 
Long track record of operations with an established franchise and sizeable international presence  
BOB has a long and established operational track record of over 11 deca des and is one of the largest nationalised banks in terms 
of assets, business, and outreach. Post amalgamation of Vijaya Bank a nd Dena Bank with BOB on April 01, 2019, pan-India 
geographical presence of BOB rose substantially, furthering its existing strong franc hise. The bank’s net advances stood at 
₹1,395,727 crore as on June 30, 2026, making it the second largest PSB in the country.  The bank had a network of 8,728 branches 
(80 international and 8,648 domestic branches), 9,538 ATMs and over 75,000 employee s as on March 31, 2026.  
 
The bank has a stable depositor base, which raises its deposits at co mpetitive rates. Indian banks have been facing a challenge 
in raising deposits, especially low-cost current account savings account ( CASA) due to shift of depositors to other investment 
avenues. In FY26 , the bank’s total deposits grew by ~1 2% from ₹1 ,472,035 crore as on March 31, 2025, to ₹1,648,487 crore as 
on March 31, 2026, which was marginally higher than the industry growth of ~11.5 %. The bank’s low -cost CASA deposits grew 
at a slower rate of ~10 % from ₹ 556,666 crore as on March 31, 2025, to ₹613,207 crore as on March 31, 2026. As a result, the 
proportion of global CASA deposits to total deposits fell to 37.20% as on March 31, 2026, compared to 37.82% for the 
corresponding date of previous year. 
 
Comfortable capitalisation levels supported by internal accruals 
BOB has maintained comfortable capitalisation levels with higher cushion over the minimum regula tory requirement in the last 
few years. The bank had raised equity of ₹4,500 crore through qualified institutional placement (QIP) in FY21, post w hich, the 
accretion to capital has been through internal accruals. 
The bank reported capital adequacy ratio (CAR) of 15.82% (PY: 1 7.19%), Tier-I CAR of 13.64% (PY: 14.79%), and Common 
Equity Tier-I (CET I) of 13.16% (PY: 13.78%) as on March 31, 2026, driven b y growth in risk weighted assets and bond 
redemptions, against the minimum regulatory requirement of 11.5% for CAR, 9.5% for Tier-I, a nd 8% for CET I ratio (including 
capital conservation buffer), respectively. Comfortable capital cushion enhances the banks’  ability to absorb asset quality pressures 
and support advance growth in the near term. The bank reported CAR of 16.30 % (Tier-I CAR: 14.41%) and CET I Ratio of 
13.90% as on June 30, 2026. 
 
CareEdge Ratings expects BOB to keep sufficient cushion over minimum regulatory req uirements in the medium term, in line with 
similar PSB peers. 
 
Diversified advances book with focus on retail 
BOB’s gross advances grew by ~16% (domestic: ~15% and international: ~24%) in FY26. The bank has  been focusing on retail, 
agriculture and micro, small and medium enterprise (MSME; RAM) segments, which toge ther constituted 47.25% of the global 
gross advances as on March 31, 2026, against 46.78% as on March 31, 2025, whereas the wholesale segment (corporate and 
others: 34.54% and international: 18.21%). Net advances growth for FY26 stoo d at 16.50%, which is above the banking industry 
average of 14.5%. 
 
Improvement in earnings in FY26 but profitability to moderate in FY27 
The interest income increased by ~4% in FY26 compared to previous year due to lower yield on advances primarily driven by 
rate cuts. The non-interest income decreased marginally by ~0.2% y-o -y due to decrease in recovery from written-off accounts

Key weaknesses 
Moderate but improving asset quality 
The bank has reported improvement in its asset quality parameters with a reduction in the gross NPAs (GNPA), primarily driven 
by higher loan growth, lower proportion of slippages, write offs and healthy rec overies (incl. upgrades). The bank has done 
provisioning resulting in reduction in NNPA ratio over the years. The bank has written off NPAs of ₹ 6,594 crore and recoveries 
and upgradation of ₹4, 481 crore in FY26 and slippages stood at ₹10,299 crore. The GNPA ratio improved to 1.89% as on March 
31, 2026, from 2.26% as on March 31, 2025, and stood at 1.99% as on June 30, 2026. The bank’s NNPA ratio improved to 0.45% 
as on March 31, 2026, against 0.58% as on March 31, 2025, respectively, and stood at 0.50% as on June 30, 2026.  
The bank’s provision coverage ratio (PCR), excluding technically written -off (TWO) accounts, stood at 76.66% as on March 31, 
2026, compared to 74.87% as on March 31, 2025, and 75.07% as on June 30, 2026. The bank’s NNPA to net worth ratio stood 
at 4.47% as on March 31, 2026. The bank’s stressed assets (GNPA + Gross Standard  Restructured Advances + Gross Security 
Receipts) constituted 2.76% of gross advances as on March 31, 2026, aga inst 2.27% as on March 31, 2025. 
Going forward, the bank expects the slippage ratio to be ~1.25%, which would help  it to maintain stable asset quality parameters. 
BOB’s ability  to contain slippages and maintain asset quality will be a key rating monitorab le. 
 
Liquidity : Strong 
The bank’s liquidity profile is supported by the bank’s strong retail and CASA depo sitor base. As on June 30, 2026, the asset 
liability maturity (ALM) profile had positive cumulative mismatches in the time buckets  up to six months. Liquidity coverage ratio 
and net stable funding ratio as on June 30, 2026, stood at ~127% and ~120%, respectively against the minimum regulatory 
requirement of 100%. Considering the bank’s stable franchise, it is expected to roll over its depos its. The bank had excess 
statutory liquidity ratio (SLR) of ₹45,888 (~3.3% in excess over regulatory requirement) as on June 30, 2026, which provid es 
adequate liquidity. The bank has access to borrowing from RBI’s liquidity ad justment facility (LAF) and marginal standing fac ility 
(MSF) and option to refinance from SIDBI, NHB, and NABARD, among others, and access  to call money markets.
