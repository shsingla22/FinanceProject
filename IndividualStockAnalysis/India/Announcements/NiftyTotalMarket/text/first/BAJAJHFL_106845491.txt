02 Half Yearly Report 2026-27Macroeconomic Environment
Geopolitical uncertainty and energy-market volatility continue to weigh on the global outlook, 
particularly for net energy-importing countries. At the same time, accelerating AI-led demand is 
supporting economies integrated with global technology supply chains. Against this backdrop, 
the International Monetary Fund (IMF) kept its 2027 global GDP growth forecast to 3.4% in Oct 
2026 World Economic Outlook (WEO) update.
India’s economy remains resilient despite global headwinds and shifting trade dynamics. Citing 
strong private consumption and services activity, the IMF raised its 2027 GDP growth forecast 
for India from 6.7% to 7.0%. The RBI’s July 2026 Monetary Policy projected real GDP growth of 
6.7% for FY27, supported by resilient domestic demand and investment activity; this was 
subsequently raised to 7.1% at the October 2026 MPC meeting. Separately, quarterly estimates 
released by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MOSPI) on 31 August 
placed Q1 real GDP growth at 7.8%, above the RBI’s estimate.
Inﬂation remains relatively benign despite external uncertainty. After staying below the 4% 
target for sixteen consecutive months, inﬂation rose to 4.4% in June 2026, 4.45% in July, 4.83% 
in August and 5.54% in September 2026, marking the fourth consecutive monthly increase. The 
RBI has projected CPI inﬂation of 5.2% for FY27. (Source: RBI Monetary Policy, MOSPI)
At its 7 October MPC meeting, the RBI raised the policy rate by 25 bps to 5.50% and changed its 
stance from ‘neutral’ to ‘calibrated tightening’.
India’s housing ﬁnance penetration remains low compared with developed economies, creating a 
multi-decade structural growth opportunity supported by favourable demographics, 
urbanization and policy measures. Competitive intensity has increased, particularly from banks 
in prime housing. Even so, the overall home loan portfolio reached ₹ 44.5 lakh crore in FY26, 
although year-on-year growth moderated from 13% in FY25 to 10.4% in FY26. (Source: CRISIL 
report)
Scheduled commercial banks and NBFCs/HFCs continue to report healthy balance sheets, asset 
quality and proﬁtability. Well-capitalised lenders with strong risk management, diversiﬁed 
funding and scalable technology platforms are expected to be the principal beneﬁciaries of 
India’s long-term housing ﬁnance growth.Industry Trends
As of 30 September 2026, your Company operated 223 branches across 182 locations in 20 
states and union territories. Its approved project ﬁnance (APF) network covered more than 
10,300 projects offering home loans. The Company also maintained one of the industry’s most 
granular developer ﬁnance portfolios, spanning 614 active developer relationships and 885 
projects, which provide an additional sourcing channel for retail home loans.
Asset quality remained healthy, with GNPA at 29 bps and NNPA at 12 bps as of 30 September 
2026, reﬂecting prudent underwriting and a strong risk management framework. BHFL 
continues to hold the highest possible credit ratings: AAA/stable for long-term debt and 
A1+/stable for short-term debt programmes from both CRISIL and India Ratings. These ratings 
support access to a range of borrowing instruments at an optimized cost.Operational Highlights
Considering geopolitical uncertainty, moderating home loan industry growth and sustained

Financial Performance Highlights of H1 FY27 vs H1 FY26
Scale
AUM increased 25% year on year, from ₹ 1,26,749 crore as of 30 September 2025 to ₹ 1,58,205  
crore as of 30 September 2026.
By product, AUM grew 22% year on year in home loans, 26% in LAP, 39% in LRD and 13% in DF. 
Growth in anchor products improved year on year, with home loan growth rising from 19% in 
H1 FY26 to 22% in H1 FY27.
The portfolio remained well diversiﬁed. As of 30 September 2026, the mix of HL, LAP, LRD, DF 
and Others was 53.9%, 10.3%, 24.0%, 10.7% and 1.1%, respectively.
Proﬁtability
Net interest income rose 10% year on year, from ₹ 1,843 crore in H1 FY26 to ₹ 2,023 crore in H1 
FY27. Net total income increased 16% to ₹ 2,429 crore from ₹ 2,100 crore.
Operating efficiency improved, with opex to NTI moderating by 110 bps year on year from 
20.4% in H1 FY26 to 19.3% in H1 FY27.
Pre-provisioning operating proﬁt grew 17% to ₹ 1,959 crore in H1 FY27, compared with ₹ 1,671 
crore in H1 FY26.
Proﬁt before tax increased 20% year on year, from ₹ 1,590 crore in H1 FY26 to ₹ 1,908 crore in 
H1 FY27.
Proﬁt after tax grew 20% to ₹ 1,472 crore in H1 FY27 from ₹ 1,226 crore in H1 FY26.
Annualised return on assets was 2.2%, while annualised return on equity stood at 12.6% for H1 
FY27.
03 Half Yearly Report 2026-27FY27 Outlook
Asset Quality
Asset quality remained resilient, with loan losses and provisions declining to ₹ 51 crore in H1 
FY27 compared with ₹ 81 crore in H1 FY26.
Gross NPA was 0.29% as of 30 September 2026, compared with 0.26% a year earlier. Net NPA 
remained unchanged from H1 FY26 at 0.12%.
The Company maintained a healthy provisioning coverage ratio of 59.8% on stage-3 assets as 
of 30 September 2026.
Capitalisation and Liquidity
The Company maintained comfortable capital adequacy, with CRAR at 21.24% as of 30 
September 2026 against the regulatory requirement of 15%. The Tier-I capital ratio was 
20.83%.
The leverage ratio rose to 6.0 times as of 30 September 2026 from 5.5 times a year earlier.
The borrowing mix remained diversiﬁed, with a higher share of money-market instruments. 
As  of 30 September 2026, money market instruments, bank borrowings and NHB reﬁnance 
accounted for 56%, 35% and 9% of borrowings, respectively. The Company held a liquidity 
surplus of ₹ 2,487 crore.
The Company continues to expand its distribution network to increase its share of home loan 
originations, which has risen to approximately 2.6% of industry originations.
Launched approximately two years ago, the ‘Sambhav’ housing initiative is broadening the 
Company’s reach beyond prime customers and providing a natural hedge against margin 
pressure in prime housing. Serving near-prime and affordable customers, the business has 
steadily scaled its disbursements, with the monthly run rate increasing from approximately 
₹ 350 crore in Q3 FY26 to more than ₹ 500 crore in Q2 FY27.
Digital initiatives continue to simplify the home loan journey and deliver a seamless, 
consistent customer experience. In September 2026, 96% of retail logins were onboarded 
digitally, and 96% of retail agreements were executed digitally through the Customer One 
portal.
The Company is also using AI across the customer lifecycle through voice agents for lead 
generation, sales-call intelligence, credit personal-discussion call intelligence, collateral 
intelligence and geo-analytics, an AI customer-assist platform, frontline hiring tools and an
