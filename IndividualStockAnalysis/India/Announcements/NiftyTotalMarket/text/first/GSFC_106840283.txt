Gandhinagar, November 2, 2026 
 
A meeting of the Board of Directors was held today to consider and approve the Financial 
Results for Q2/H1 2026-27. The Company delivered its ever-highest Q2 and H1 top-
lines, achieving total sales of Rs. 4711 Crores in Q2 2026-27, a YoY growth of 50% and 
Rs. 8293 Crores in H1 2026-27, a YoY growth of 56%.  However, the top-line growth was 
constrained by unprecedented rise in key raw material prices, driven by geopolitical 
pressures.  
 
In Q2 2026-27, the Fertilizer segment recorded a 55% YoY growth in sales value, 
increasing by Rs. 1,386 Crores, despite a 6% decline in sales volume from 6.08 LMT to 
5.69 LMT. The growth was primarily driven by higher DAP realization, with DAP revenue 
increasing by Rs. 1,659 Crores, supported by the Government's DAP Special Package to 
compensate for international price variations. However, sharp increases in key raw 
material costs, particularly Sulphur (+327%) and Natural Gas (+62%), significantly 
impacted margins, compressing EBIT of the Fertilizer segment to Rs. 17 Crores compared 
with Rs. 224 Crores in Q2 2025-26. Urea subsidy rates remain subject to revision following 
finalisation of the actual Pool Gas rate by FICC.GAIL, which has been pending since April 
2026. 
 
The Industrial Products segment continued its positive performance in Q2 2026-27, with 
sales increasing by Rs. 185 Crores, registering a 30% growth over Q2 2025-26. EBIT 
improved from Rs. 54 Crores to Rs. 66 Crores. The improvement was supported by better 
realizations across major products in line with market trends. Sales of Caprolactam and 
Nylon increased by Rs. 58 Crores and Rs. 60 Crores respectively, mainly due to higher 
realizations, while Anone sales increased by Rs. 36 Crores, driven by higher volumes. TGU 
sales also increased by Rs. 26 Crores, partly offset by a Rs. 5 Crores decline in Melamine 
sales. The Capro-Benzene spread improved significantly from US$ 512 per MT to US$ 849 
per MT.

Outlook:   
The country will enter the Rabi season with comfortable fertilizer inventory levels, the 
highest in the past three years, particularly for Urea and NPK grades, intensifying 
competitive pressures across the industry. The subdued Southwest Monsoon and lower 
reservoir levels, especially in Southern India, are likely to constrain fertilizer demand. The 
prevailing price distortion between DAP and NPK fertilizers continues to influence farmers’ 
purchase preferences, adversely affecting NPK offtake.  
 
The forthcoming NBS subsidy rates will play a pivotal role in partially mitigating elevated 
raw material costs and addressing price distortions. Concurrently, any easing of 
geopolitical tensions in the Middle East and Europe could soften international raw material 
prices and provide relief to industry margins.  
 
The Caprolactam–Benzene spread is expected to moderate in Q3 26-27 as regional supply 
normalizes, potentially impacting margins across the Caprolactam–Nylon value chain. 
Demand for Melamine and Technical Grade Urea is expected to remain steady, while HX 
Crystal exports may improve amid subdued domestic demand. Other Industrial Products 
are projected to maintain steady demand, delivering reliable baseline stability to the 
segment.
