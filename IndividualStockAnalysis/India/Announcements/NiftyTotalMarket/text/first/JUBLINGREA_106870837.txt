1. Rationale for Face Value Exercise Price of Tenure & Rating-linked Options and Yearly Target-
linked Performance Options
The Board believes that the value of awards under the ESOP Plan 2021 should be assessed in 
conjunction with the relevant performance(Company and Individual)and retention conditions, and not 
by reference to Exercise Price alone. The face value Exercise Price is intended to facilitate employee 
share ownership only upon satisfaction of the applicable vesting conditions, including the risk of 
forfeiture. 
performance conditions applicable to each award category. The Notice makes clear that discounted 
pricing is confined to specific award categories and does not confer an unconditional right to shares.
Importantly, the IiAS Voting Guidelines 2026-27, under Section 5.4 relating to Share-based 
Compensation Schemes, continue to recognise that stock options issued at an Exercise Price 
representing a discount exceeding 20% to the Market Price may be supported where vesting is linked 
to pre-defined performance targets, whether at the Company level, individual level or a combination 
thereof, subject to appropriate disclosures. Accordingly, the 20% discount threshold is not an absolute 
restriction under the IiAS Guidelines, and the applicable Exercise Price should be evaluated in 
conjunction with the performance-linked vesting conditions, both Company and Individual 
performance, prescribed under the Scheme.
The Scheme distinguishes between three categories of awards:
(i) Tenure and Rating-linked Options vest upon completion of three years, subject to continued 
service, the applicable appraisal rating, vesting matrix; and Company level and individual 
level performance matrix for vesting mentioned in the Notice
(ii) Yearly Target-linked Performance Options are linked to the prescribed annual performance 
targets; and 
(iii) Time-based Stock Options carry an Exercise Price not lower than the Market Price on the 
Grant Date and are subject to the applicable vesting conditions. 
The face value pricing is confined to the first and second categories. The grant, eligibility and vesting 
considerations should not be conflated: factors such as role criticality and future potential may inform 
grant selection, while vesting remains governed by the conditions applicable to the particular award. 
Accordingly, discounted Options do not confer an unconditional right to receive shares merely by 
passage of time.We request IiAS to evaluate the Exercise Price together with the vested conditions for 
each category, consistent with the exceptions in its Guidelines.
As disclosed in the Notice, the performance framework identifies the Company-level measures 
including revenue growth, EBITDA/EPS, profitability margins, cash flows, capital efficiency and 
ROCE in all categories of award, as well as individual measures such as role-based KPIs, strategic 
contribution and leadership effectiveness. A minimum individual appraisal rating of 'Solid' or 3 out of 
5 (or equivalent) applies in the latest performance cycle before the relevant Vesting Date. 
The Notice also expressly specifies Company-level weightage ranges of 20%-50% for employees below 
Senior Vice President level and 50%-80% for employees at Senior Vice President level and above. The 
Company's individual-rating threshold is material which recognise individual-level targets and do not 
insist upon corporate-level targets for every category of discounted grant.
The structure is designed to support retention and performance recognition while aligning employee 
rewards with long-term shareholder value creation. In particular, the Notice records the commercial 
rationale for face value pricing and the separate market-price mechanism applicable to Time-based 
Stock Options. 
We request IiAS to evaluate the relevant Exercise Price together with the conditions attached to each 
category of grant, consistent with the exceptions recognised in its own guidelines.
2. Performance Framework and Disclosures
The 2026 Amendment formally sets out a structured Company-level and Individual-level performance 
framework in all the categories of award including measures relating to revenue growth, profitability, 
EBITDA/EPS, cash flows, capital efficiency, ROCE, KPIs, strategic contribution and leadership 
effectiveness. The Notice stipulates a minimum individual performance rating of 'Solid' or 3 out of 5 
(or equivalent) in the latest appraisal cycle prior to vesting. The Company would also clarify that the 
underlying principle of linking vesting to performance is not newly introduced: since inception of the 
Scheme in 2021, it has followed a performance-linked vesting approach through NRC determinations 
and the relevant Grant Letters. Clause 9.2 of the existing Scheme already envisaged vesting by reference 
to performance or other criteria communicated in the Grant Letter. The 2026 Amendment therefore 
elaborates and publicly records the principles and safeguards underlying that established practice, while 
prescribing more detailed provisions for future grants; it should not be read as a wholesale change in 
the Company's vesting philosophy.
As set out in the Notice, achievement of the prescribed minimum individual rating is a vesting 
prerequisite. Theachievementof Company-level performance measuresis assessed in accordance with 
the relevant framework and prescribed thresholds. This distinction is significant: Tenure and Rating-
linked Options are subject to tenure and appraisal conditions and Company level and individual level 
performance matrix for vesting mentioned in the Notice, while Yearly Target-linked Performance 
Options are tied to the applicable annual performance measures. Both categories of award are subject 
The Notice also contains an express commitment under Clause 9.4.6 of the proposed amended Scheme 
to disclose in subsequent Annual Reports, on a post-vesting basis. The Company commits that all
Company level Performance Targets and corresponding level of achievement against such targets that

We specifically draw attention to Clause 9.4.3 (Company-level performance weightages), Clause 9.4.6

3. NRC Discretion in Exceptional Circumstances
The NRC's power to adjust vesting outcomes by up to 25% is not an unrestricted discretion
limited and conditional authority. Under Clause 9.4.2, the adjustment operates only after the prescribed 
minimum performance threshold is met, is limited to 25% of the Options otherwise eligible for vesting 
and cannot result in vesting of more than 100% of the Options granted to the relevant Participant. 
No Options shall vest where the minimum prescribed performance threshold is not achieved. Further, 
Clause 9.4.7 identifies exceptional circumstances, including material business and external disruptions, 
and addresses revisions to vesting conditions for future grants, subject to shareholder interests, 
documentation and applicable law. These separate powers should not be treated as a general authority 
to waive performance conditions.These provisions are intended to retain limited operational flexibility 
conditions.
The NRC is responsible for administering the Scheme and for recording and documenting relevant 
decisions in accordance with the Scheme and applicable SEBI Regulations. The exceptional-
circumstances mechanism seeks to retain limited operational flexibility without displacing the 
prescribed minimum vesting safeguards. Any additional public disclosure of a particular adjustment 
shall be madein accordance with the Scheme and applicable disclosure requirements.
Further, the discretion to adjust vesting outcomes by up to 25% under Clause 9.4.2 is distinct from the 
authority to modify performance parameters in exceptional circumstances under Clause 9.4.7. The 
former operates only upon achievement of the prescribed minimum performance thresholds, whereas 
the latter is intended to address exceptional circumstances affecting future grants, subject to the 
safeguards expressly prescribed under the Scheme. These provisions should therefore not be construed 
as conferring unrestricted discretion upon the NRC to modify or waive the performance conditions 
governing vesting. The Company notes that any expectations concerning the weightage of subsequently 
introduced or undisclosed performance measures are separate from the 25% limit on vesting 
adjustments.
4. Individual Grant Limits
The proposed increase in the maximum individual grant limit from 10 lakh Options to 15 lakh Options 
is an enabling ceiling, intended to accommodate appropriate long-term awards for employees in critical 
leadership and strategic roles. As the Notice expressly clarifies, the increased ceiling does not give any 
employee, including the Managing Director and CEO, an automatic entitlement to a grant of that size. 
Each future grant remains subject to NRC determination, the overall Scheme pool, performance 
considerations and applicable legal and shareholder approval requirements. The historical allocation of 
Options to the Managing Director and CEO should be distinguished from the proposed permissible 
maximum; the latter is not itself a further grant or a commitment to concentrate future awards in one 
person. The Managing Director & CEO during his remaining tenure would get approximately 1.6 lakh 
Option from increased individual limit.
Under the leadership of Mr. Deepak Jain, Managing Director & CEO, the Company has made progress 
in strengthening its specialty-led business portfolio, improving operational efficiencies and pursuing 
strategic initiatives aimed at long-term sustainable growth. The historical grants made to Mr. Jain should 
therefore be considered in the context of his leadership responsibilities, strategic contributions and role 
in driving the Company's long-term business objectives. Such grants, however, do not determine the 
intended allocation of the enhanced Option pool. Please note that the future grants will be determined 
by the NRC, subject to the Scheme pool, performance conditions and applicable approvals. Historical 
grants (including to the Managing Director & CEO) reflect role, responsibility and past contribution 
and do not predetermine future concentration of awards.
At the same time, the Company recognises that its sustained growth and performance are attributable 
to the collective efforts of employees across various levels and functions. The Scheme is not designed 
exclusively for any one individual; rather, it provides a framework for recognising, rewarding and 
retaining eligible employees, including senior management, critical talent and employees contributing 
to the Company's operational and strategic objectives. The proposed enhancement of the aggregate 
Option pool is intended to support this wider employee base, facilitate leadership development and 
succession planning, and address future talent retention requirements.
The additional pool of 10 lakh Options is proposed to support future talent retention, succession and 
strategic hiring requirements. The Notice discloses an indicative allocation of 5,00,000 Tenure and 
Rating-linked Options, 4,63,000 Time-based Stock Options and 37,000 Yearly Target-linked 
Performance Options, subject to the Scheme's reallocation safeguards. The 2026 Amendment also 
makes the performance framework more transparent by expressly recording criteria and reporting 
commitments previously implemented through the Scheme and grant documentation, rather than 
introducing performance linkage for the first time.
The indicative category-wise allocation of the incremental pool, as disclosed in the Notice and set out 
below, further reflects the Company's intention to support different employee reward and retention 
objectives. Such allocation is not an employee-wise distribution, and the proposed enhanced ceiling 
does not, of itself, indicate an intention to concentrate future grants in favour of the Managing Director 
& CEO or any other particular employee.
5. Extension to Employees of Unlisted Subsidiaries (Resolution No. 2)
We note that IiAS has expressly indicated its support, in principle, for extending ESOP benefits to 
employees of unlisted subsidiaries, and has linked its adverse recommendation on Resolution No. 2 
solely to its view on Resolution No. 1. The separate resolution covers eligible employees of the 
Company's unlisted subsidiaries in India and abroad, subject to the Scheme and applicable law, and 
does not create any option pool beyond the aggregate ceiling of 30 lakh Options sought under 
Resolution No. 1. The proposal is also consistent, in principle, with IiAS' stated guidelines supporting 
extension of stock option schemes to qualifying unlisted subsidiaries. We therefore request that 
Resolution No. 2 be reconsidered in light of the foregoing clarifications.
In view of the clarifications above, including the express provisions in the Notice, we request IiAS to 
reconsider the observations and reassess its recommendations on both proposed resolutions.
We request that these clarifications be taken into account in the voting advisory so that shareholders 
may consider the resolutions on the basis of the complete pricing, vesting and disclosure framework set 
out in the Notice.
