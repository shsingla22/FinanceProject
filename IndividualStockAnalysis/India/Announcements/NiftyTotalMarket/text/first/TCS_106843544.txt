TCS Enterprise AI Study: 84% say productivity is AI’s top benefit; just 10% let AI act 
without human oversight  
 
25-country global survey shows that security, risk and governance is the #1 factor that leaders are 
considering when deploying AI  
 
MUMBAI, November  04, 2026:  Eighty -four percent of executives surveyed rank productivity improvement 
as the leading source of value realized from AI investments, yet only 10% of global enterprises allow AI  agents  
to execute decisions without human approval across multiple functions or business areas, according to a new 
global study from Tata Consultancy Services  (TCS) (BSE: 532540, NSE: TCS), a global leader in IT services, 
consulting, and business solutions. The study also finds that just 9% of executives report AI agents capable of 
securely orchestrating workflows across enterprise applications . The findings  highlight the gap between the 
value  that enterprises are already deriving from AI -assisted work and their readiness to give AI agents greater 
autonomy and authority to act.  
 
The study, TCS E nterprise AI  Study: Intelligence that is Real,  highligh ts security as a defining factor in the next 
phase of enterprise AI. Security, risk, and governance considerations rank as the leading factor shaping how 
and where AI systems are developed and deployed, ahead of cost, speed, and ease of deployment.  That 
emphasis is becoming increasingly consequential as enterprises give AI greater access to applications, 
workflows and decision -making processes.  
 
The findings reflect the evolution toward a Human+AI operating model, where AI progresses from assisting 
employees to taking on greater responsibility and, where appropriate, operating with increasing levels of 
autonomy. As enterprises move along that cont inuum, deriving greater value from AI will depend on having 
the right security, governance and controls in place to determine where human oversight remains essential 
and where AI can be given greater authority to act.  
 
Amit Kapur, Chief AI and Services Transformation Officer, TCS, said , “We are seeing significant progress in 
scaling AI for enterprises. The shift is clearly in AI moving from assisting people to taking more autonomous 
action with deep access into an enterprise. Productivity is clearly where enterprises are seeing the greatest 
value from AI today, with 84% of executives ranking it as the leading source of value from their AI investments  
though 10% have also indicated that they have seen an uplift in revenue. What seems to be holding 
organisations back – and rightly so – are concerns around security, governance, and resilience. Deploying AI 
responsibly, with the right guardrails and enterp rise controls will be the big focus in the next stage of this 
journey.”  
 
Platform independence:  Executives are also increasingly focused on AI resilience and flexibility. Ninety 
percent of respondents say that model independence is an important factor in AI investment decisions , while 
34% identify AI sovereignty -related requirements involving control over data location, model choice, and 
compliance as a high or very high organi sational risk. The concern is particularly acute in Europe, where 43% 
of respondents cite AI sovereignty -related requirements as a high or very high organizational risk. At the 
same time,  only 7% of global respondents report having organization -wide provider -switching continuity 
plans, suggesting that concern about model dependence and sovereignty is advancing faster than operational 
preparedness.  
 
Other key global findings from the report  include:  
• AI economics are under review : 39% of enterprises said that they continue to assess  whether AI

initiatives generate sufficient value relative to token and compute costs.  
• Enterprise knowledge remains fragmented : 44%  of respondents  report that important business 
knowledge remains embedded within employees or fragmented documentation, potentially 
limiting AI's ability to consistently access enterprise context.  
• Open -source AI is gaining traction : 46%  report targeted deployment of open -source AI for 
selected use cases.  
• AI's workforce impact is becoming less predictable.  The proportion of leaders saying it is still 
too early to determine AI's effect on jobs rose from 4% in 2024 to 19% in 2026.  
• Productivity remains the leading source of AI value , followed by improvements in customer 
experience and the development of new products or services.  
 
Regional AI Priorities and Readiness Vary : The study also identified notable regional differences in enterprise 
AI adoption. North America showed particular strength across several enterprise AI foundations, with 15% 
of respondents reporting that AI is treated as a strategic enterprise capability. Europe showed a strong focus 
on AI sovereignty, with 43% citing sovereignty -related requirements as a high or very high organizational risk. 
Meanwhile, the UK and Ireland stood out on operational resilience, reporting comparatively strong levels of 
AI cont inuity planning and governance integration.  
 
Survey methodology : The study surveyed 1,333 senior business and technology leaders across 25 countries 
and 12 industries. Respondents included C -suite executives, divisional leaders, and senior vice presidents and 
vice presidents from organizations with annual revenues rangi ng from US$500 million to more than US$20 
billion.
