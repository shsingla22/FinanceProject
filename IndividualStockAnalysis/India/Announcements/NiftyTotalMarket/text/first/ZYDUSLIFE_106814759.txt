Zydus Therapeutics Presents New Post Hoc Analysis of Phase 3 EPICS-III Trial 
at ACG 2026 Showing Greater Biochemical Response With Investigational 
Saroglitazar Compared to Placebo Across Geographic Subgroups in PBC 
 
 Numerically higher biochemical response treatment difference was observed in the U.S. 
subgroup relative to the non-U.S. subgroup ( ∆ 60.7% vs. ∆ 41.6%) in patients with primary 
biliary cholangitis (PBC) 
 Findings build on the clinical profile of saroglitazar in patients with PBC who have inadequate 
response or intolerance to ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) 
 
Ahmedabad, India and Pennington, N.J. October 12, 2026  
 
Zydus Therapeutics  Inc. (“Zydus Therapeutics”), a wholly owned subsidiary of Zydus Lifesciences 
Limited, a global innovation-led healthcare company, today shared new analyses from the pivotal 
EPICS-III Phase 2(b)/3 clinical trial of saroglitazar in adult patients with PBC in a late-breaking oral 
presentation at the American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2026 Annual Meeting. Saroglitazar is 
an investigational product that has not been approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA); 
its safety and effectiveness have not been established.  
 
The presentation, delivered by Raj Vuppalanchi, MD, Professor of Medicine at Indiana University 
School of Medicine and Global Principal Investigator for the EPICS-III study, shared a post hoc 
exploratory analysis of geographic subgroups investigating whether treatment effects observed with 
saroglitazar are consistent across regions, with particular focus on U.S. versus non-U.S. patients. More 
details on the full results of the EPICS-III trial, a Phase 3 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled 
study evaluating saroglitazar in adult patients with PBC who had an inadequate response to or 
intolerance of UDCA, in which nearly thirty percent of patients were from the U.S., were provided in a 
press release earlier this year and were presented at the EASL Congress 2026. 
 
The U.S. population compared 28 patients treated with saroglitazar to 14 placebo-treated patients; the 
composite non-U.S. population (consisting of Argentina, Iceland, and Turkey) compared 69 patients 
treated with saroglitazar to 37 patients treated with placebo. In the results of this exploratory analysis, 
composite biochemical response rates were greater with saroglitazar than placebo in both U.S. (60.7% 
vs. 0%) and non-U.S. subgroup regions (55.1% vs. 13.5%). A larger unstratified treatment difference 
was observed between saroglitazar and placebo in the U.S. subgroup (∆ 60.7%) versus the non-U.S. 
subgroup (∆ 41.6%). ALP normalization treatment difference rates at Week 52 were not substantially 
different between the U.S. and non-U.S. subgroups (7.7% vs. 9.6%); ALP normalization was defined as 
ALP<116 U/L. One additional U.S. participant in the saroglitazar group did not achieve normalization

“These findings provide additional insight into the Phase 3 EPICS-III results and the biochemical 
responses observed among patients enrolled in different geographic regions,” said Raj Vuppalanchi. 
“While this was a post hoc analysis and the findings should be interpreted accordingly, we believe it is 
important to continue evaluating the EPICS-III data to better understand the results across the study 
population.” 
 
Zydus Therapeutics is developing saroglitazar for the treatment of PBC in combination with UDCA in 
adults who have had an inadequate response to UDCA, or as monotherapy in patients unable to tolerate 
UDCA. Saroglitazar is currently under review by the FDA. If approved, Zydus Therapeutics plans to 
launch saroglitazar in the United States by March 2027. 
 
Three additional non-U.S. participants in the saroglitazar group did not achieve normalization but had 
ALP values of 118, 119, and 120 U/L at Week 52. Composite biochemical response was defined as 
achieving the following at Week 52: ALP < 1.67 x ULN, ≥ 15% decrease in ALP from baseline, and 
total bilirubin ≤ ULN or direct bilirubin ≤ ULN in subjects with known Gilbert's syndrome. 
 
About Primary Biliary Cholangitis (PBC):  
PBC is a rare, progressive autoimmune disease that gradually destroys the bile ducts, resulting in an 
accumulation of bile in the liver, which can result in fibrosis, cirrhosis, the need for liver transplantation, 
or death. PBC is characterized by increases in biochemical markers, particularly alkaline phosphatase 
(ALP) and bilirubin. Clinical symptoms include pruritus (itching) and fatigue, both of which can be 
severe. 
 
About the EPICS-III Trial:  
EPICS-III is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, seamless Phase 2(b)/3 trial 
evaluating the efficacy and safety of saroglitazar in patients with PBC who had an inadequate response 
to or intolerance of UDCA. After optimal dose selection, the Phase 3 trial randomized 148 patients in a 
2:1 ratio to saroglitazar magnesium 1 mg (equivalent to 0.97 mg saroglitazar and 0.03 mg magnesium) 
or placebo.

About Saroglitazar:  
Saroglitazar is a novel Peroxisome Proliferator-Activated Receptor (PPAR) α/γ agonist, currently under 
investigation as a treatment for patients with PBC. Its distinct biological profile targets both bile acid 
toxicity and liver inflammation. Saroglitazar has received Orphan Drug Designation, Fast Track 
Designation, and Priority Review from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration for the treatment of PBC.
