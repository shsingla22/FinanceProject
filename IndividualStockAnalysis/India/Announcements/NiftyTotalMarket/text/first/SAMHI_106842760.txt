This report covers SAMHI Hotels Limited, a public limited company
listed on BSE and NSE with its registered office in Delhi Aerocity,
New Delhi, for 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026. All operations are in
India. As in the BRSR, the boundary is consolidated: SAMHI Hotels
Limited and the 17 subsidiaries in its consolidated financial
statements, listed on page 65. At year end this meant 31 hotels and
one corporate office across 11 states. Caspia Delhi was part of the
portfolio until its sale in August 2025: its April–August 2025 data
are included in the FY202526 figures, but it is not counted among
the 31 hotels at year end.
SAMHI owns or leases, and asset-manages, its hotels: at 31 March
2026, 18 were owned and 13 leased (five of them in mixed-use
developments). Established partners Marriott, IHG and Hyatt)
operate them. SAMHI directs capital, portfolio strategy, asset
performance and governance; operators run day-to-day
operations, guests and much of the property workforce. Upstream,
hotels buy food, linen, amenities, utilities and services, partly
through centralised procurement under a vendor Code of Conduct.
Downstream, operator brands and distribution connect the hotels
with guests.

FY202526 was a year of stronger performance and sharper
operational discipline. It was also a year in which sustainability
became more visible in capital allocation, engineering decisions,
renewable-energy procurement, workforce systems and Board
oversight. We expanded access to renewable power, reduced
combined Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions, invested in efficient
building systems and continued to strengthen the protections and
development opportunities available to our people.
There is important work ahead. Hospitality remains energy- and
water-intensive. About 21 of our 31 hotels are in regions where
groundwater is under pressure, which is why water stewardship
is a priority for us. We are now building the framework to
measure our value-chain emissions. Retaining talent, a challenge
across hospitality, is at the centre of our people strategy. This
first standalone report is therefore both an account of progress
and a commitment to better measurement.

At SAMHI Hotels, we believe that
building a successful business goes
hand in hand with caring for our
people, our communities and the
environment. As Chair of the CSR &
ESG Committee, I am encouraged by
the steps we are taking to embed
these values across our operations.
The Committee, chaired by and
made up entirely of Independent
Directors, oversees SAMHIʼs CSR
Policy and ESG performance on
behalf of the Board. It met on 10
February 2026 with full attendance.
This first standalone Sustainability
Report sets the baseline against
which we will be held to account.
Our environmental footprint is
concentrated in electricity. In
FY202526 our hotels used 247,491
GJ of energy, 20.3% of it renewable,
and emitted 36,505 tCO₂e across
Scope 1 and Scope 2, 92.5% of it
from purchased electricity. Water is
our most local challenge: about 21 of
our 31 hotels are in over-exploited or
critical groundwater regions.
Our 3,480 employees are the
business. Women make up 16.4% of
the workforce and 25% of
management, and every employee
received a performance review.
Turnover of 46.6% and seven
recordable injuries show where the
work is hardest.Our CSR focuses on education,
livelihoods and community wellbeing
near our hotels. In FY202526, our
CSR programmes reached around
930 people from vulnerable and
marginalised groups. We also
launched a project with Aarohi Bal
Sansar in Satoli, Uttarakhand, to help
the school expand from Class 10 to
Class 12, adding vocational training,
laboratories, a library and a
computer centre for students from
22 villages. An initial ₹3.1 mn has set
the work in motion. SAMHIʼs CSR
team is managing the project end to
end, and in FY202526 it completed
the site survey and appointed the
architects and project-management
team. Our hotels also ran donation
and ration drives, health and
nutrition programmes, safety
awareness sessions, a sponsored
hospitality course for women and
support for local vendors.
Sustainability is a continuing
journey. Scope 3 emissions and
supplier screening are still to be
built, and the Committee will review
progress on each. We remain
committed to learning, improving
and creating lasting value for all our
