 1. Purpose and reporting basis  
This management report distils the principal findings, open matters, business implications and immediate actions 
arising from the draft forensic report prepared by KPMG Česká republika, s.r.o. The summary is intended for Capillary 
Technologies India Limited management, Legal, Risk, Finance and Security stakeholders. It does not replace the detailed forensic report, evidence register or technical annexures.  
The report concerns four disputed payments totaling EUR 3,041,169.90 executed from Session M Czech Republic 
s.r.o. between 26 June 2026 and 2 July 2026. The investigation reviewed two forensic laptop images, mailbox and 
authentication records, communications, transaction documentation, Google Workspace- related logs, Google Takeout 
exports and open- source intelligence material.  
2. Executive incident synopsis  
The available evidence is most consistent with a multi -stage payment -diversion fraud based on executive and legal -
professional  impersonation, social engineering, a lookalike domain and off -channel communications. The 
communications presented a purported confidential acquisition transaction and were supported by an NDA, invoices, 
payment requests, beneficiary banking details and payment confirmations.  
The reviewed evidence does not support a finding that malware, endpoint compromise, corporate mailbox compromise, 
successful unauthorized access or traditional email spoofing caused the payments. Relevant emails were sent from an 
operational lookalike domain, law -ogier.co.uk, rather than through forged infrastructure purporting to be the legitimate 
ogier.com domain. The use of deepfake voice or video remains an allegation that could neither be substantiated nor 
refuted because original audio, video, telecommunications metadata and mobile- device artefacts were not available.

5. Management implications  
• Primary business risk is not presently evidenced as technical compromise of corporate endpoints or mailboxes. It is the 
ability of external personas to exploit authority, secrecy, urgency, non- standard channels and formal -looking documents 
to induce authorized payment activity.  
• The circumstances surrounding the disputed transactions suggest that confidential, time-sensitive transaction requests may warrant enhanced scrutiny and verification. Management may consider assessing whether existing beneficiary -
validation, callback, escalation, treasury, and approval processes provide sufficient assurance for high- value transactions 
involving newly introduced counterparties.
 
• The use of personal email accounts and WhatsApp reduced organizational visibility and limited the availability of native evidentiary records. Consideration should be given to implementing independent verification procedures for executive payment requests and transaction approvals, particularly where instructions are communicated through personal email 
accounts, voice or video calls, caller identification, profile images, or messaging platforms outside established corporate 
channels .
 
• Information about organizational roles, approval patterns and payment processes may have been obtained through public information, prior interaction, exposed documents or other sources. The present report does not establish the source of that knowledge or insider involvement.
 
• The absence of identified compromise is limited to the artefacts and records reviewed. It should not be presented as proof that compromise was impossible or never occurred.
 
6. Critical limitations for management reliance  
• No forensic acquisition of the personal mobile device(s) used for WhatsApp communications was undertaken.  
• No original audio/video, call -detail records, telecommunications metadata or native call artefacts were available; 
deepfake use was therefore not technically determined.  
• Complete VPN, network, proxy, firewall, packet -capture, memory and some endpoint -security backend telemetry were 
unavailable.  
• Complete bank -portal audit trails, maker -checker records, workflow logs and approval timestamps were unavailable; the 
end-to -end approval process was not independently validated.  
• Slack logs were unavailable; communications occurring solely on unavailable channels could not be assessed.  
• OSINT attribution is contextual and not proof of identity, ownership, control or responsibility.  
• The financial review established first -hop payments from supplied records, not onward movement, beneficial ownership 
or recovery status.  
• No attribution to a specific individual, group or threat actor was established.  
• No statutory audit, legal opinion, compliance certification or criminal -liability assessment was performed.  
• Absence of identified compromise is not definitive proof of absence; conclusions remain bounded by the evidence 
supplied and review period.  
7. Recommended management conclusion  
Subject to the limitations above, the evidence reviewed is most consistent with a payment -diversion fraud executed 
through executive and legal -professional impersonation, social engineering, a lookalike domain and off -channel 
communications. The available evidence does not support a technical -compromise explanation for the two reviewed 
endpoints or corporate accounts. The potential use of deepfake technology remains unverified due to the absence of 
native audio, video, and telecommunications evidence. Additionally, a definitive assessment of the effectiveness of 
payment -governance, beneficiary -validation, and approval -control processes could not be performed because the 
underlying workflow records, approval logs, and bank -portal audit evidence were not available for review.
