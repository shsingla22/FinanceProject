# Glossary

Definitions for the terms used across the framework, so an agent scores
consistently.

- **Growth capex vs maintenance capex** — *Growth* capex builds new
  capacity/revenue (a new plant, new stores); *maintenance* capex merely sustains
  existing capacity. Only growth capex should dominate, and only if its
  incremental ROIC is high. Reported capex does not split the two — infer from
  commentary.
- **ROIC / Return on Capital** — post-tax operating return ÷ invested capital.
  Driven by *asset turns × profit margin*.
- **CROCI (Cash Return on Cash Capital Invested)** — the preferred metric: post-tax
  **cash** return ÷ capital invested, **adjusted for accounting conventions such as
  goodwill**. The skill computes a proxy = CFO ÷ (Equity + Reserves + Borrowings)
  and flags that the goodwill adjustment is not applied.
- **Asset turn / asset intensity** — Sales ÷ Total Assets. High turn = asset-light
  = attractive, but needs an additional moat to protect returns.
- **Gross margin / OPM** — GM signals pricing power; high OPM *with* high GM signals
  competitive advantage. Large OPM swings signal key costs outside management
  control. GM is proxied as `100 − Material Cost %` when no direct COGS line exists.
- **Pricing / Mix / Volume** — the three revenue levers, in descending value:
  pricing power (raise price without cost) > mix (shift to value-added) > volume
  (costs, incl. working capital, scale with it).
- **Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)** — Debtor Days + Inventory Days − Days Payable.
  Lower (or negative) is better; money stuck in working capital lowers ROC.
- **CFROI** — cash-flow return on investment; higher CFROI tends to precede higher
  future earnings growth (more internal accruals to reinvest), which is the *link*
  the framework draws to growth persistence.
- **Bolt-on acquisition** — a small, complementary acquisition absorbed into the
  existing business; higher success rate than large transformational M&A.
- **Mini-monopoly** — a monopoly that exists in the customer's mind (brand) or in
  aftermarket/services (spares, upgrades), rather than a regulated legal monopoly.
- **Switching costs (razor-and-blade)** — a captive aftermarket where the upfront
  product is competitive but repeat/consumable/service revenue is locked in.
- **Assurance benefit** — the customer pays for certainty/reliability because
  failure is catastrophic (industrial gases, testing, baby food); price-insensitive.
- **Intangible benefit** — value from taste/image that eludes measurement; larger
  role at smaller ticket sizes; demand rises in expansions, steady in contractions.
- **Halo effect** — attributing skill to management because results were good;
  a bias the framework explicitly warns against.
- **Security by obscurity** — a humble, unglamorous niche that competitors and
  capital overlook (locks, lenses, fittings).
- **Share donator** — a weak industry participant (poor management/products,
  ignored division, sub-scale) from whom a strong player takes share.
- **Coverage** — fraction of the 34 parameters actually assessed for a company;
  reported alongside every score because a high score at low coverage is a weak
  claim.
