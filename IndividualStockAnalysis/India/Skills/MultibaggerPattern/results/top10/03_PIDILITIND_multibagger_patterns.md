## Friendly Middleman — STRONG FIT
*"The expert sells it for you" · Strong fit — confirmed by the calls and the numbers*

**What the calls show:** Across all four calls the demand is created by carpenters, painters, applicators, architects and structural consultants rather than end-consumer purchase decisions — May 2023 calls 'user connect' the secret sauce, Feb 2026 describes India as a DIFM market and a project route-to-market through professionals, with applicator education for Roff.

> "we have a very clear go-to-market route through the architects, structural consultants into the projects route" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Operating margin of 24% moving only ±2.6 points a year — the steady, protected profitability these patterns produce.
- ❌ Operating margin moved from about 22% (early years) to 23% (recent years) — no widening trend.

## Pricing Power — STRONG FIT
*"Raises prices, keeps the customers" · Strong fit — confirmed by the calls and the numbers*

**What the calls show:** Repeated, disclosed price action without volume loss: 8–10% weighted pricing in FY23, and in May 2026 a 5% April hike followed by 7–9% in May on Fevicol (12–15% total) while still guiding to raised UVG; premiumisation/upgradation is an explicit strategy and the 20–24% EBITDA corridor has been defended through both inflation and deflation cycles.

> "we have taken about a 5% increase in April, and we have followed it up with another 7% to 9%" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹55 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ❌ Operating margin moved from about 22% (early years) to 23% (recent years) — no widening trend.

## Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds" · Strong fit — confirmed by the calls and the numbers*

**What the calls show:** Orderly multi-year succession (Puri to Vats/Singh via a 'designate' year), a stable CFO, and consistent delivery of what was promised — the 20–24% EBITDA corridor promised in May 2023 was restored and held, and the FY26 ambition to lift UVG was met; bad news is volunteered early, including the 13.5% export decline, the INR47 crore wage-code charge and Nina's 16% fall, alongside a conservative balance sheet and ~70% payout.

## Cost to Replicate — STRONG FIT
*"Could a newcomer copy this?" · Strong fit — confirmed by the calls and the numbers*

**What the calls show:** The articulable barrier is decades of contractor/applicator relationships plus a distribution network that has already survived well-funded attackers — Henkel, 3M, Huntsman and the large paint companies have been in waterproofing over ten years without displacing leadership, and Pidilite's own distribution 'strength and clout' is what makes its new launches land.

> "we had all the multinationals, we had Henkel, 3M, Huntsman, all of them come in" — *management, on an earnings call*

## Innovation Dominance — LIKELY FIT
*"Out-inventing the competition" · Likely fit — good evidence, not yet conclusive*

**What the calls show:** A disciplined, repeated innovation architecture — two-thirds of growth from core, one-third from innovation, with the core:growth-and-pioneer mix shifted from 80:20 to 55:45 — and new bets (Haisha coatings, CollTech electronics adhesives, paints, NBFC pilots) each year; but R&D spend, share of industry R&D and an innovation turnover index are deliberately withheld.

> "2/3rd of our growth should come out of growing the core and 1/3rd should come out of innovation" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ High retained margin (55% of sales) alongside 11% yearly growth — the profitable-innovation flywheel: margins fund the spending that drives the growth.

## Market Share Gainers — LIKELY FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals" · Likely fit — good evidence, not yet conclusive*

**What the calls show:** Three years of volume growth well above the categories they serve — UVG rising from 9.3% in FY25 to 11.1% in FY26 with 15.3% in Q4 FY26 — sustained while paint majors, Henkel, 3M and Huntsman attacked waterproofing and adhesives, and won on volume/mix rather than price or credit; explicit share percentages are never disclosed, only inferred leadership.

> "we have got healthy double digit volume growth, that will suggest that the market is not grown at this rate" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Sales compounding at 11% a year — below the 12% pace the share-gainer pattern looks for.

## Low Price Plus — NO FIT
*"Cheapest — and still the best" · Does not fit this pattern*

**What the calls show:** Management explicitly manages to a price premium over competition and to premiumisation/upgradation, not to a low-cost, no-frills proposition; cost discipline shows up as operating leverage (costs +9.2% vs sales +15.3% in Q4 FY26) rather than as savings passed to price.

> "if our premium vis-a-vis other product (of competition) goes more than 15%, we will actually cut back on price" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹55 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 0.9× a year — not the high-turnover, low-margin machine this pattern needs.
