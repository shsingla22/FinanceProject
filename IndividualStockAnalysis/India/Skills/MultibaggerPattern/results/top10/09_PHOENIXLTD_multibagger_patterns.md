## Pricing Power — STRONG FIT
*"Raises prices, keeps the customers" · Strong fit — confirmed by the calls and the numbers*

**What the calls show:** Sustained, repeated price increases without volume loss across all four verticals: rentals up 13% y/y on flat GLA (Feb 2026), St. Regis ADR +11% while occupancy rose (Feb 2025), and residential realisation deliberately taken from Rs. 15-16k/sq ft in 2018 to over Rs. 29,000/sq ft by Feb 2026 — with retail consumption still growing 25%.

> "This reinforces our pricing power and the premium positioning of our hotel." — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹93 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ✅ Operating margin moved from about 46% (early years) to 57% (recent years) — the widening-profitability signature of pricing power / lock-in.

## Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds" · Strong fit — confirmed by the calls and the numbers*

**What the calls show:** Promoter-led with exceptionally long tenure (Shishir Shrivastava since 1999, Rashmi Sen 15+ years) and a delivered record: the May 2023 promise to take offices from 2 msf to ~5 msf was met by Nov 2025, FY23 consumption guidance of Rs. 9,000 cr was beaten, and net debt/EBITDA has been held near 1x while capex ran. Management also owns errors publicly — the Feb 2025 admission that office positioning was wrong — and concedes same-store growth has lagged its own earlier double-digit guidance.

> "I want to talk a little bit about course correction... we came to realize that it was very important to get the front office; Grade A; best-in-class positioning right" — *management, on an earnings call*


## Cost to Replicate — STRONG FIT
*"Could a newcomer copy this?" · Strong fit — confirmed by the calls and the numbers*

**What the calls show:** The barrier is concrete and time-based: finite prime urban land assembled over decades (the Bangalore residential land bought in 2008 now realising Rs. 29,000/sq ft at ~25% total cost), multi-year approval cycles, ~Rs. 700-1,750 cr annual capex, and a tenant roster that gives Phoenix first call on flagship store launches — a well-funded entrant could not replicate Lower Parel or Hebbal in a few years.

> "the land has been bought in 2008 and the construction has been completed around two years ago" — *management, on an earnings call*

## Low Price Plus — NO FIT
*"Cheapest — and still the best" · Does not fit this pattern*

**What the calls show:** Management explicitly rejects price-led volume: in Feb 2025 it refused to cut residential prices to lift velocity, holding Rs. 1,000 cr of inventory to protect positioning, and the retail strategy is premiumisation, not value pricing. Cost discipline exists (marketing down 15%, cost of debt to 7.62%) but is used to widen margin, not to cut price.

> "we are not seeing the benefit of dropping pricing to improve sales velocity" — *management, on an earnings call*
