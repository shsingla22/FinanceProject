# Hindustan Unilever Ltd. (HINDUNILVR) — Multibagger Pattern Analysis

**Rank 6 of 10** in the Nifty Total Market multibagger-pattern screen.

All 742 index companies were screened on the numbers; the 293 that passed (or nearly passed) the core multibagger foundation were then read by an AI judge across their conference-call history and management record. This company fits **4 of the 11 patterns strongly** — among the highest in the entire index. A second, independent AI reading of the same calls confirmed the presence of all 4 of these patterns. Every quote below was verified word-for-word against the transcripts.

---

**Patterns this company fits:** Brand Strength (*The name itself sells*); Innovation Dominance (*Out-inventing the competition*); Corporate Culture (*A culture that compounds*); Cost to Replicate (*Could a newcomer copy this?*); Friendly Middleman (*The expert sells it for you*); Pricing Power (*Raises prices, keeps the customers*); Forward Integrators (*Owns the shop, not just the product*); Market Share Gainers (*Winning customers from rivals*)

**Foundation test:** the company partly passes the core multibagger foundation (predictable cash + high returns on capital + growth), 2 of 3 checks passing:

- ✅ Generated positive operating cash in 12 of the last 12 years, converting about 83% of operating profit into cash over the last five.
- ✅ Median return on capital employed of 91% across 12 years — comfortably above the 15% quality bar.
- ❌ Sales grew about 7% a year over the period — modest growth.

## Brand Strength — STRONG FIT
*"The name itself sells" · Strong fit — confirmed by the calls and the numbers*

**What the calls show:** The whole trajectory is brand-led: heritage brands (Sunlight 'since its launch over 130 years ago', Horlicks, Lakme, Vim, Kissan), sustained A&P at ~10% of revenue with share of voice held above share of market in every call, a proprietary superiority framework, and successful adjacency stretches (Indulekha into soap, Kissan into chutneys, Vaseline past Rs.1,000 crore).

> "more than 80% of our turnover is unmissably superior when compared to competition" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹50 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ✅ Operating margin of 23% moving only ±2.5 points a year — the steady, protected profitability these patterns produce.

## Innovation Dominance — STRONG FIT
*"Out-inventing the competition" · Strong fit — confirmed by the calls and the numbers*

**What the calls show:** Every one of the 12 calls names a slate of new launches with attributed revenue impact, and innovation is increasingly science-anchored — niacinamide/zinc-peptide Dove ranges, polyglutamic acid TRESemmé, RhamnoTech bio-surfactant in Vim, Nutrimax in Horlicks Superfoods — backed by an India-dedicated R&D 'design and deploy' organisation created in FY26 plus Rs.2,000 crores capex for premium formats; absolute R&D spend is not disclosed, which is the one gap.

> "RhamnoTech, our proprietary bio-surfactant technology, delivers breakthrough grease cutting... thus creating a science-backed competitive advantage" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹50 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ❌ High retained margin (50% of sales) alongside 7% yearly growth — the flywheel is incomplete.

## Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds" · Strong fit — confirmed by the calls and the numbers*

**What the calls show:** Bad news is disclosed promptly and specifically (business winning 'temporarily dipped below 60%', flat volumes, Beauty & Wellbeing at 1%, negative mix, gratuity hit of Rs.113 crores called out rather than buried), guidance is kept and met (EBITDA 23-24% band held across all 12 calls; the FY26 promise of a second half better than first half delivered 7% USG), and capital is rotated deliberately via divestments and bolt-ons; the caveat is heavy churn at the top — three CEOs and two CFOs across the five-year record.

> "over the last year, our business winning percentage had temporarily dipped below 60%" — *management, on an earnings call*


## Cost to Replicate — STRONG FIT
*"Could a newcomer copy this?" · Strong fit — confirmed by the calls and the numbers*

**What the calls show:** The replication barrier is concrete and stated across calls: a direct-coverage general-trade network expanded by ~2 lakh outlets in FY26 alone plus a 9-million-outlet-scale distribution reach, century-old brand equity, and access to Unilever's global R&D, trademarks and procurement — a combination a well-funded entrant cannot rebuild in a few years, though regional challengers do re-enter mass segments whenever commodity costs fall.

> "we will continue to maintain an important and mutually interdependent relationship with Unilever and will benefit fully from Unilever's global science, R&D technology platforms, brand trademarks and central services" — *management, on an earnings call*


## Friendly Middleman — LIKELY FIT
*"The expert sells it for you" · Likely fit — good evidence, not yet conclusive*

**What the calls show:** Mostly a mass-marketed consumer business sold on brand advertising, but there is repeated, growing use of expert intermediaries — dermatologist co-creation for Dove, salon/professional partnerships and expert squads for TRESemmé, a 30,000-strong creator network, and Unilever Food Solutions selling through chefs — though the end consumer still chooses and pays, and no professional training programme or trade lock-in is described.

> "co-created with dermatologists... demand generation through salons and professional partnerships" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Operating margin of 23% moving only ±2.5 points a year — the steady, protected profitability these patterns produce.
- ✅ Operating margin moved from about 18% (early years) to 23% (recent years) — the widening-profitability signature of pricing power / lock-in.

## Pricing Power — LIKELY FIT
*"Raises prices, keeps the customers" · Likely fit — good evidence, not yet conclusive*

**What the calls show:** HUL shows conditional, cost-pass-through pricing rather than durable above-inflation pricing power: it took price cuts through FY24-25 deflation (price growth 'near flat or marginally negative'), and in FY25 pricing-led 2% USG came with flat volumes; premiumisation is the real mix lever, and by Mar 2026 calibrated increases in Fabric/Household Care are flagged as possibly costing volume.

> "we have implemented calibrated price increase across Fabric Wash and Household Care in June quarter as well. This may lead to some rebalancing between volume and price growth" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Keeps ₹50 of every ₹100 of sales after direct input costs — room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ✅ Operating margin moved from about 18% (early years) to 23% (recent years) — the widening-profitability signature of pricing power / lock-in.

## Forward Integrators — LIKELY FIT
*"Owns the shop, not just the product" · Likely fit — good evidence, not yet conclusive*

**What the calls show:** No own-store estate or franchising, but a clear and deliberate move toward the customer over the period: D2C named a 'critical part of our fewer, bigger bets agenda', acquisitions of D2C-native Minimalist and OZiva (Rs.1,100 crore ARR combined), e-commerce growing 25%+ in FY26, and a dedicated quick-commerce organisation with its own supply chain — still only ~3% of business.

> "Masstige and within that D2C is a critical part of our fewer, bigger bets agenda... building a strong D2C playbook that can be replicated across other brands" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Operating margin moved from about 18% (early years) to 23% (recent years) — the widening-profitability signature of pricing power / lock-in.
- ✅ Operating margin of 23% moving only ±2.5 points a year — the steady, protected profitability these patterns produce.

## Market Share Gainers — LIKELY FIT
*"Winning customers from rivals" · Likely fit — good evidence, not yet conclusive*

**What the calls show:** Share gain is reported every quarter and is profitable rather than bought — 75%+ of business winning share in Jul 2023, a candid dip below 60% through FY24, recovery and a switch to turnover-weighted share by Jan 2025, then highest-ever Home Care and powders share by FY26 — all while EBITDA margin held in the 23-24% band.

> "the segment has continued to gain market share and has achieved its highest ever market share, further strengthening our leadership position" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Sales compounding at 7% a year — below the 12% pace the share-gainer pattern looks for.

## Low Price Plus — PARTIAL
*"Cheapest — and still the best" · Partial fit — some traits present*

**What the calls show:** A genuine and repeatedly itemised savings culture runs through every call (net material inflation defined net of buying efficiencies, savings 'across the lines of P&L', global procurement leverage) and mass access price points like the Rs.99 Sun dishwash liquid and Rs.99 Surf excel pack, but the model is premium-margin (gross margin ~50%, EBITDA 23-24%) rather than low-margin/high-turns.

> "deftly managed by leveraging the strength of our global procurement operations... and sustained focus on generating savings across the lines of P&L" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ❌ Keeps ₹50 of every ₹100 of sales after input costs and turns its assets 0.8× a year — not the high-turnover, low-margin machine this pattern needs.

## Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) — NO FIT
*"Sells once, earns forever" · Does not fit this pattern*

**What the calls show:** Across all 12 calls HUL is a pure consumable-goods P&L — revenue is FMCG offtake through trade, with no license fees, AMC/service contracts, attach rates, renewals or deferred revenue discussed as a funding source; the one hardware-adjacent business with a consumables tail (Pureit water purifiers) was divested and booked as an exceptional gain in the Dec 2024 quarter.

> "we had announced the divestment of Pureit in June quarter '24, and we have now concluded this transaction" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Customers effectively fund the business — cash arrives 89 days before suppliers are paid.
- ✅ Capital spending is about 2% of sales — growth needs little capital.
- ✅ Operating cash was positive in 12 of 12 years — the reliable cash engine recurring-revenue businesses show.

## Toll Roads — NO FIT
*"Small cost, can't skip it" · Does not fit this pattern*

**What the calls show:** HUL sells discretionary household consumables directly to price-aware consumers; the calls repeatedly describe consumers titrating consumption, down-trading to small packs and to loose tea, and the company passing commodity relief through in price — the opposite of a tiny-share, non-negotiated critical input.

> "consumers are titrating consumption in the current macroeconomic scenario... small packs growing faster than other packs" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Operating margin 23%, swings of ±2.5 points, median return on capital 91% — the fat, calm, high-return economics of a business customers cannot skip.
- ✅ Operating margin of 23% moving only ±2.5 points a year — the steady, protected profitability these patterns produce.
