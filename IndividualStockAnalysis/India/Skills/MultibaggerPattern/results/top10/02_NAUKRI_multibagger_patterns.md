## Recurring Revenue (Product Upfront) — STRONG FIT
*"Sells once, earns forever" · Strong fit — confirmed by the calls and the numbers*

**What the calls show:** Naukri sells subscription database/job-posting contracts billed in advance: deferred sales revenue of Rs 1,018.5 cr (Mar-23, +24% YoY) and customer advances ~Rs 1,100 cr are discussed explicitly as customer-funded float, and cash from operations consistently exceeds reported profits (Rs 1,469 cr FY26 vs Rs 1,138 cr operating profit) with essentially no capital employed.

> "we have customer advances that money doesn't belong to us... around 1100 crores" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Only about 1 day of cash is tied up in the trade cycle.
- ✅ Capital spending is about 2% of sales — growth needs little capital.
- ✅ Operating cash was positive in 12 of 12 years — the reliable cash engine recurring-revenue businesses show.

## Pricing Power — STRONG FIT
*"Raises prices, keeps the customers" · Strong fit — confirmed by the calls and the numbers*

**What the calls show:** Across the 3-year arc management repeatedly raises price without losing volume — FY23 'discount reduction and price increases', FY25 'pricing also gets better', and by FY26 an explicit framework of 'hiring volume, our share of hiring and revenue per hire' with premium tiers (Naukri Top Tier, PremiumX) — while recruitment operating margins held 57-62% even through the FY24 IT hiring slump. Weaker in Jeevansathi, where competitor discounting is admitted to pressure pricing.

> "organic volume and pricing remains the foundation" — *management, on an earnings call*

- ✅ Essentially no direct input costs — typical of software and internet platforms — leaving ample room to fund brand, R&D and price leadership.
- ✅ Operating margin moved from about 5% (early years) to 32% (recent years) — the widening-profitability signature of pricing power / lock-in.

## Corporate Culture — STRONG FIT
*"A culture that compounds" · Strong fit — confirmed by the calls and the numbers*

**What the calls show:** Founder-promoters Bikhchandani and Oberoi have run the business throughout, own mistakes plainly on calls ('we had all overhired... caught napping'; 'we are not very happy with our performance' in new homes; the Q4FY26 99acres billings reset disclosed unprompted), keep a conservative cash-rich balance sheet through cycles, and delivered what they promised — Jeevansathi and 99acres losses were guided down and then actually reached breakeven, and the long-resisted payout was raised from 35% to 39% to 65% of PAT once cash needs were clear.

## Cost to Replicate — STRONG FIT
*"Could a newcomer copy this?" · Strong fit — confirmed by the calls and the numbers*

**What the calls show:** The articulable barrier is a 113-115mn resume database built since 1997 plus two-sided behavioural interaction data that a funded entrant cannot buy — management makes exactly this argument against the AI disintermediation thesis, and the FY26 numbers (11% billings growth at 59% margins through a weak hiring market) are consistent with it; the counter-example within the group is Shiksha, where AI-driven search change is already destroying traffic, showing the moat is platform-specific rather than group-wide.

> "This two-sided data depth ... has been built over the years and cannot be replicated quickly" — *management, on an earnings call*
