payment solutions are not viewed merely as fee-income generators, but as enablers

integrate the Bank into their day-to-day financial ecosystem, strengthening longterm relationships. This philosophy shapes the Bank’s product selection approach.

India’s banking sector is undergoing a
structural transformation shaped by rapid
digitization, evolving customer
expectations, expanding financial inclusion,
continued formalization of the economy,
and increasing competition from fintech and
digital-first platforms that are redefining
how banks engage with customers, deliver
services, and build long-term value.
Despite global uncertainties, liquidity pressures, and margin
challenges, the Indian economy remained resilient during
the year, supported by steady GDP growth, moderating
inflation, and sustained credit demand across retail, MSME,
and rural segments. At the heart of this momentum remains
India’s entrepreneurial ecosystem comprising traders,
self-employed professionals, small businesses, and farmers
reinforcing the need for banking models that combine digital
capability with trust, accessibility, and
relationship- led engagement.

MSME Credit Gap in India

Banking as a Service

India’s 7.47 crore+ MSMEs contribute significantly to
economic activity, accounting for 31.1% of GDP, ~35.4% of
manufacturing output, and employment for over 32.82 crore
people. While formalization has accelerated, with more than
7.9 crore enterprises registered under the Udyam and Udyam
Assist platforms as of March 2026, improving access to credit
and liquidity remains a key priority. Policy initiatives such as
the expansion of TReDS, enhanced credit guarantee coverage,
the ₹10,000 crore SME Growth Fund, and increased allocation
to the SRI Fund are aimed at strengthening financing access
and supporting the long-term growth of the MSME ecosystem.

Expanding CreditDeposit Divergence

Branch of the Future

India’s banking sector continued to record strong balance
sheet growth during FY 2025-26, with deposits increasing
13.5% and credit growth accelerating to 15.9% year-on-year,
supported by robust demand across industry and services
segments. As credit expansion outpaced deposit mobilization,
banks increasingly relied on higher-cost funding sources
such as bulk deposits and certificates of deposit, placing
pressure on Net Interest Margins (NIMs) and reinforcing the
need for disciplined liquidity and funding management.

Strengthening Asset Quality
India’s banking sector has witnessed a significant
improvement in asset quality, with the Gross NPA (GNPA)
ratio declining from 14.98% in CY 2018 to nearly 2.2%
by early 2026. The recovery has been supported by
regulatory reforms, stronger provisioning standards,
disciplined underwriting practices, and systemic cleanup measures such as the RBI’s Asset Quality Review
and the establishment of NARCL. Together, these
initiatives have strengthened balance sheet resilience and
improved transparency across the banking system.

Banking as a Service (BaaS) is reshaping the financial
ecosystem by enabling banks, fintechs, and NBFCs to deliver
integrated financial solutions through digital platforms.
Leveraging their regulatory strength, infrastructure, and
customer trust, banks are expanding distribution reach
and creating new revenue opportunities. The model is also
driving the growth of segment-focused offerings, including
specialized SME credit solutions and digital financial
tools for gig-economy workers, positioning BaaS as a key
enabler of India’s next phase of digital banking growth.

Physical branches are evolving beyond transaction-focused
outlets into relationship and advisory centers. As customers
increasingly expect seamless omnichannel experiences,
banks are repositioning branches to combine personalized
service with digital convenience. Backed by extensive urban
and rural networks, future-ready branches are expected to
strengthen customer engagement, support financial literacy,
and deepen trust-led relationships within local communities.

Growing Adoption
of Generative AI
Indian banks are increasingly deploying Generative
AI to strengthen customer engagement, improve
operational efficiency, and enhance risk management
capabilities. Applications such as multilingual virtual
assistants, AI-led underwriting models, and real-time
fraud monitoring are helping institutions deliver faster
and more responsive services across diverse customer
segments. Internally, GenAI tools are also enabling
employees to resolve queries more efficiently, generate
insights, and personalize customer interactions at scale.

Hyper-Personalized
Digital Lending
Digital lending in India continues to expand rapidly, supported
by real-time data analytics and alternate credit assessment
models. Growth in short-tenure, small-ticket loans has
widened access to formal credit, particularly among younger
borrowers and customers beyond metropolitan markets. At
the same time, the regulatory framework governing digital
lending has evolved significantly to strengthen transparency
and customer protection. Measures such as mandatory Key
Facts Statements (KFS), direct bank-to-customer fund flows,
and fintech self-regulatory mechanisms are helping build
greater accountability and trust within the ecosystem.

Embedded Finance and
Expanding Financial Inclusion
Financial services are becoming increasingly embedded
within everyday digital ecosystems, reducing the distinction
between banking and non-banking platforms. Consumers
can now access credit, insurance, payments, and savings
solutions directly through retail, mobility, travel, and
logistics applications. Supported by the expansion of
Digital Banking Units (DBUs) and broader digital public
infrastructure such as UPI and the e-Rupee, embedded
finance is accelerating formal financial adoption across
underserved and rural markets, while encouraging a
gradual shift toward digitally enabled financial behavior.

Climate Change and
Sustainability
Climate-related disruptions are increasingly influencing
global economic and business priorities, placing greater
responsibility on financial institutions to integrate ESG
and climate considerations into their decision-making
frameworks. Recognizing the growing implications of
climate risk, the RBI has emphasized the need for banks
to strengthen their approach toward both physical risks,
such as extreme weather events affecting agricultural
output and repayment capacity, and transition risks arising
from the shift toward a low-carbon economy. As a result,
climate resilience, sustainable financing, and responsible
risk assessment are becoming increasingly important
components of the banking sector’s long-term strategy.
