Together, the theme captures both our intent and

Roots of Responsibility
Nesco’s approach to sustainability did not arrive
with a compliance deadline or a single defining
moment. Its foundations were laid much earlier,
in the vision of our founders, who believed in
building an organisation that created long-term
value for its stakeholders and for the communities
around it. Over the decades, that philosophy has
found expression in different ways: our continued
commitment to manufacturing in India, to promoting
exhibitions, events and hospitality that support local
enterprise and employment and to using resources
responsibly. Our CSR initiatives, spanning education,
healthcare, infrastructure, community development
and entrepreneurship, have been an inseparable part
of this journey, not an adjunct to it.
As our business has evolved, so has our
understanding of sustainability. What began as
sound business practice has, over time, adopted
the language, structure and rigour of formal ESG
management: green energy adoption, LEED-certified
developments, a structured materiality assessment
and a dedicated ESG Roadmap 2030.

From Habit to
Governed Function
If I were to identify the real shift of this reporting
year, it would be this: sustainability at Nesco moved
from being an operational habit to a governed
function. We undertook a detailed re-assessment
of our material ESG topics, strengthened our ESG
reporting through a dedicated digital platform and
retained LEED Platinum certification for Towers 3 and
4, reflecting sustainability considerations embedded
in their design and operations. We also introduced
the ESG Roadmap 2030, translating long-standing
commitments into a structured, measurable
programme, while extending green power across
exhibitions and events and providing it to organisers
without any additional cost to customers. During the
year, we also received ISO 45001:2018 certification
for Total Facilities Management at our IT Park,
reinforcing our commitment to occupational health
and safety.

The Year in Numbers
Some milestones from this year are worth setting
down plainly. 94% of our energy consumption across
operations now comes from renewable sources,
with Towers 3 and 4 running on 100% renewable
power for their own operations. Our safety record
remains at zero lost-time injury frequency rate and
zero fatalities across all operations, an outcome of
sustained discipline rather than a single initiative.
Our entire workforce completed training in safety
and ESG-related areas this year and our policy
review coverage across the organisation stood at
100%, alongside zero whistleblower cases and zero
data breaches.
We retained our ISO 14001:2015 – Environmental
Management Systems (Gujarat – Vishnoli), ISO
45001:2018 – Occupational Health and Safety
Management Systems (Mumbai and Gujarat –
Karamsad and Vishnoli); ISO 9001:2015 – Quality
Management Systems (Gujarat – Karamsad and
Vishnoli) certifications across facilities. Our combined
Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions for the year stood
at 2,355.55 MT CO2e, a figure we intend to keep
reducing as our renewable transition widens. We
planted over 1,300 trees across our facilities.

Rationale for a
Standalone Disclosure
We were often asked, in the course of preparing this Report,
why Nesco chose to publish a standalone ESG disclosure
rather than folding our reporting into the BRSR, as many
companies of our size do. The BRSR is, by design, a
fixed-format compliance disclosure. It records what we have
done, but allows little room for the why and how behind
those decisions. A standalone Report also serves a different
audience than a compliance filing: investors evaluating
governance quality, multinational tenants operating
under their own ESG disclosure obligations and clients
who increasingly expect their partners to meet the same
standards they themselves are held to. We did not want
to limit ourselves to a compliance disclosure. We wanted
a Report through which investors, customers, employees
and communities could see Nesco as a responsible and
future-ready organisation.

The Investor’s Lens
I am sometimes asked how I would respond to an investor
who sees ESG reporting as more symbolic than substantive.
My view has stayed fairly consistent: look at the evidence
behind it. Towers 3 and 4 operate at 100% occupancy on
long-term leases with global tenants and carry a tenant NPS
of 94.80. LEED-certified buildings command better occupancy
and pricing from multinational tenants who carry their own
ESG mandates. Renewable sourcing insulates us from energy
price volatility in a way a purely compliance-driven approach
never would. I would see this less as a symbolic gesture and
more as risk-adjusted asset quality, which the evidence tends
to support.

What We Are Quietly Proud Of

The Road Ahead

Not every achievement in this Report will draw questions.
Some of the ones I am proudest of are those that rarely do.
Our zero LTIFR and zero fatalities record across operations
rarely makes headlines, but it reflects discipline sustained
over years, not a single safety campaign. Our waste circularity
programme, including treated wastewater reuse and the
conversion of organic waste into manure through expanded
organic waste converter capacity, is foundational work that
quietly reduces our footprint. Our full policy review coverage
and zero data breaches this year reflect a governance
discipline that rarely features in an investor call, yet underpins
the credibility of everything else in this report. And our CSR
delivery model, which combines direct spend with vetted
implementing partners and outcome tracking rather than
disbursement alone, is designed to ensure that our CSR spend
does more than simply get spent.

We are making steady progress towards integrating ESG into
our decision-making processes. Going forward, we intend to
embed ESG considerations more deeply into how we manage
risk and evaluate investments. Our renewable transition is
intended to extend across our full campus, well beyond the
current scope of Towers 3 and 4. Our emissions measurement
is expected to mature beyond Scope 1 and Scope 2 to include
Scope 3. Wayside Amenities, our new business vertical, is
intended to carry ESG considerations from inception rather
than retrofitting them later, the way Towers 3 and 4 once had
to. And we expect our reporting platform to mature from an
annual disclosure exercise into something closer to real-time
ESG performance tracking.

If I am honest about what has been most difficult in building
Nesco’s ESG programme, it has not been cost, or even data,
though both present real challenges. It has been mindset:
building a shared understanding of sustainability across a
genuinely diversified business, where Realty, Exhibitions,
Events, Foods and Engineering each operate with different
processes and different levels of maturity and where a new
venture such as Wayside Amenities is being built from a blank
slate. Clearly defining ownership and improving the quality and
consistency of disclosures among the people closest to each
process has taken longer than we initially expected.

Reading the Risks Right
Risk management continues to evolve. Integrating climate
and environmental risks into our enterprise risk register
across business units is a goal under our ESG Roadmap
2030. We aim to assess two categories: physical risks,
such as Mumbai’s monsoons and extreme weather and
transition risks arising from evolving regulations on
energy, emissions, water and waste. Our response is
structural, built around climate-resilient infrastructure
design, LEED Platinum-certified buildings and a continuing
shift to renewable energy across operations, with our
Risk Management Committee reviewing the framework
periodically and reporting key risks to the Board.

Where Responsibility Pays

Looking Ahead

From a financial standpoint, sustainability at Nesco appears
increasingly to support performance rather than merely
add to costs. Our renewable energy transition helps reduce
Scope 2 exposure and insulate operating costs from energy
price volatility. LEED Platinum certification has increasingly
supported leasing and pricing outcomes, reflected in strong
occupancy on long-term leases with global tenants and
consistently high satisfaction scores. Our safety record of zero
lost-time injuries and zero fatalities is also a factor tenants
weigh in venue selection.

Looking three to five years ahead, our largest
sustainability-linked commitments will follow our largest
growth projects: Tower 2, a large-format development
comprising IT Park and hospitality facilities designed with
sustainability built in from inception; the Hall Modernisation
programme at the Bombay Exhibition Center; and the
Wayside Amenities build-out across expressway corridors, a
greenfield vertical where ESG considerations are part of the
founding brief. For now, internal accruals remain the more
disciplined path for funding these developments.

Financing Nesco’s
Sustainable Future
At Nesco, we have long believed that financial discipline and
sustainable growth are not competing priorities, but the
same conversation viewed from different vantage points. As
Chief Financial Officer, I see our ESG commitments not as an
overlay to our financial strategy, but as a natural extension of
decisions that were embedded in the way we build.
Our growth engine, comprising Tower 2, Halls Modernisation
and the Wayside Amenities build-out, continues to be
funded entirely through internal accruals. This reflects
a deliberate, debt-free approach to our balance sheet.
Sustainability capital expenditure, whether renewable energy
transition or LEED-certified construction, sits within our core
infrastructure spend rather than a separately ring-fenced
budget, because we see responsible growth as closely tied to
how we do business, not something separate from it.

need for a large-scale exhibition venue and has since grown

broader live events landscape. Furthermore, with capabilities
spanning both on-campus and off-campus events, Nesco
Events combines creative and operational expertise with
the flexibility to deliver diverse, large-scale experiences for

The foundation of our ESG journey lies in a belief that has
guided us for over eight decades. Anchored in our founding
philosophy, ‘Believe,’ our core values call on us to act with
integrity, listen with intent, thrive through teamwork and,
importantly, give back to society while caring for our planet.
These values are not aspirational statements; they shape
the way we make decisions, conduct business and create
long-term value.
This philosophy is reinforced at the highest level of our
organisation. Our Chairman and Managing Director,
Mr. Krishna S. Patel, has consistently emphasised that
sustainable development extends beyond economic growth.

Scope 1 and 2 emissions

This commitment is reflected in our focus on developing
IGBC-certified infrastructure, transitioning major operations
towards green energy and strengthening systems to improve
waste and water management.
During FY 2025–26, we translated this philosophy into a
more structured ESG approach. We formally undertook our
materiality assessment and transitioned our ESG reporting to
a dedicated digital platform, establishing stronger processes
to measure, monitor and communicate our sustainability
priorities with greater transparency and accountability.

FY 2025-26 marked ESG’s shift at Nesco from informal practice to a
governed, tracked function. We reassessed our materiality priorities,
moved reporting onto a dedicated digital platform and launched
the Nesco 2030 ESG Roadmap with measurable KPIs across energy,
emissions, water, green infrastructure, social and governance. We
strengthened stakeholder engagement through CSAT and ESG
Engagement Surveys, expanded green power certificate initiatives
and initiated supply chain ESG assessments to extend sustainability
practices across our value chain. We are proud of 94% renewable
energy, zero LTIFR, zero fatalities and 100% workforce training.
Our focus now is to strengthen data accuracy, consistency and
accountability across business units while deepening stakeholder
engagement and supply chain sustainability.

Digitisation runs through our ESG efforts less as a standalone initiative
and more as the infrastructure that makes the rest of our strategy
trackable. The clearest marker of this shift is our move towards greater
digitisation, including our transition to a dedicated digital platform for
ESG reporting by our partners, Credibl, during the year, alongside the
largely digitalised training programmes. These initiatives formed part
of our broader efforts to formalise the materiality assessment and
strengthen ESG processes.
Alongside this, we initiated a structured supply chain engagement
programme through the above digital platform and launched a supplier
assessment programme covering ESG standards.
On the workforce side, AdrenalinMAX, our integrated HRMS, functions
as the backbone for our ESG-adjacent people processes: it enables
a structured, digital ticketing system through which employees can
raise grievances, replacing informal or undocumented channels with
a traceable process, while also supporting performance management
and tracking.

Shaping the Future Responsibly

Digitising ESG Data Management with Credibl
During FY 2025-26, we strengthened ESG data management by
transitioning from manual, Excel-based tracking to a standardised
digital platform. The centralised system enables consistent data
capture, defined responsibilities and clear ownership through a
RACI framework, improving coordination and traceability. It has also
standardised the calculation and validation of ESG metrics, reducing
inconsistencies and reliance on manual consolidation.

By digitising the ESG data lifecycle, we have
enhanced data quality, accountability and reporting
efficiency, while establishing a stronger foundation
for monitoring ESG performance and meeting
evolving disclosure and assurance requirements.

STAKEHOLDER ENGAGEMENT

Shaping the
future, together
Every business we run sits inside a web of relationships and none
of them is incidental to us. The people and institutions connected
to our business do not simply receive our decisions after we have
made them; they shape those decisions beforehand.
Engagement is where we hear expectations surface,
where we identify early warning signs of risk and where
the feedback loop that keeps our operations sharp
comes to life. This is not a ritual we perform for a report.
It is an integral part of how we operate. That web is
deliberately wide. It stretches from the people who
work across our own operations to the tenants who
occupy our IT parks, the customers who walk through
our venues, the partners who supply our businesses
and the communities that live alongside our campuses.
Each relationship carries its own expectations and we
adapt our approach to fit them rather than the other
way around.
