Dear Shareholders,
Over the last few quarters, we reset our business and rebuilt it on a more sustainable model, which we are
now growing. The main headline of Q2 FY27 is that demand is no longer our constraint; supply is. The
constraint is not our manufacturing capacity but our supply chain's ramp-up, which we are scaling rapidly.
The dealer transition has progressed strongly, and the EV macro is robust. We stopped orders from our
D2C stores on 1 November, and they will largely close by December. Gross margin was stable at about
29.9% despite higher commodity costs. Operating expenses were ₹369 crore, down from ₹390 crore in
Q1 (excluding a one-time non-cash reversal of ₹57 crore in Q1), and will keep declining as our front-end
network transitions fully to the dealer model.
The QIP and rights issue strengthened our balance sheet, and we have deleveraged significantly. Total
borrowings came down from ₹3,132 crore in December 2024 to ₹1,819 crore in September 2026.
Specifically Auto borrowings reduced from ₹2,422 crore to ₹337 crore. With low Auto debt, a healthy
gross margin and cost structure, and a much-improved working capital cycle under the dealer model, we
expect Auto to generate free cash flow in the near term.
In Cell, we achieved SCOD on time for 6 GWh, and the Government extended our ACC PLI to CY2031.
Production is ramping, our cells are going into our own vehicles at a growing scale, and we are on track for
Shakti and other launches in the coming quarters. The cell macro is also strong, with government focus on
components and lithium refining, and growing storage deployments with higher localisation requirements.
Through this transformation, our balance sheet is much more stable, our cost base is structurally and
meaningfully lower, we are not demand constrained, and we have two long-term growth engines: Auto and
Cell.

Auto Business
Our Auto business enters the second half of FY27 with strong dealer momentum. Our first dealers went live
on Janmashthmi, 4th September, and by Diwali, 8th November we had appointed 255 dealer partners
operating over 300 stores. Wholesales are ramping and dealers are reordering, which has strengthened
the flywheel. Demand is not our constraint; the constraint is the ramp-up of our supply chain.
Auto borrowings have come down from ₹2,422 crore in December 2024 to ₹337 crore in September 2026,
so the business now carries minimal debt. Our working capital cycle has also improved under the dealer
model, as dealers pay for vehicles when they order and hold stock in their own stores.
Operating expenses have continued to reduce and will further reduce by about ₹50 crore as we shut our
company store network. Gross margin was stable from Q1 to Q2 despite rising commodity prices. Over Q3

Our product leadership remains strong. In Q2, we announced the S1Z, which will reach customers in Q4

existing products, which should reduce our bill of materials cost. As dealers ramp up, demand for our
premium scooters and motorcycles is rising meaningfully, and we expect the mix to shift toward both over
the next couple of quarters.
The EV industry continues to grow. In Q2, E2W industry volumes were 599,317 units, up 82.7% year on year,
and EV penetration of the two-wheeler market reached 11.2%. We expect this growth to continue. With
encouraging early trends in markets where dealers have matured, we are confident of regaining market
share, and of the Auto business generating free cash flow in the near term.

Dealer execution
The first dealer stores opened on 4th September. In the two months since, the network has scaled to 255
partners and 319 stores as of 8th November. We plan to reach about 450 stores by December, and 700 by
March 2027.

We have shut most of our company stores, and all new production now goes to dealers. Through October
and November, inventory in the dealer channel built up, and registrations were a little slower in October.
Dealer sell through is strong and improving, with channel inventory currently only about 15 days. In most of
the stores that opened earliest, inventory is actually less than a week. In fact, in many markets where our
dealers opened early and have stabilised, our market share is now well above where it was before the
transition.

Supply chain ramp-up
Production was broadly flat for several months and is now rising month by month; we expect to produce
about 25,000 vehicles in December.

Demand is ahead of supply, and we can sell every vehicle we produce. About 5,000 vehicles with paid
orders in September had their deliveries delayed by a week, pushing them into October, because of
charger unavailability. After the transition to dealers, we are now running a paid order backlog which we
intend to quickly extinguish as we ramp production. The constraint is the ramp-up of our supply chain, not
our own manufacturing capacity, as our plant can build well above current output. We are working closely
with our supplier partners to scale their capacity, and our output will step up month by month.

Cell Business
We achieved SCOD for 6 GWh of cell capacity ahead of schedule, and we are now ramping production and
yields. The Government has extended our ACC PLI to CY2031. As we are already ahead of our PLI
milestones, we expect PLI incentives to start coming in from next quarter.
We invested in this business early, and the demand and production ecosystem in India is now maturing.
EVs are growing, and OEMs, both Indian and foreign companies selling in India, increasingly want Indian
cells; we are in discussions with many of them. Energy storage demand has risen sharply, in both grid and
home. The Government has introduced a minimum 40% domestic content requirement for grid-scale
storage under its recently approved storage scheme, which turns into demand for domestic cells. It has
also approved a lithium refining scheme and is working on incentives for cell components.
In this context, we remain the only Indian company to have commercialised cell manufacturing and
technology. With commercial-scale capacity, our own technology, captive demand from our own vehicles
and the benefit of the PLI, we are well placed to capture this demand, and we see Cell as a strong
near-term growth area.
With the Gigafactory now commissioned, investors naturally want to understand how the Cell business will
scale and translate its technology into competitive economics. The questions we most often receive are:
what are our yields; what is our plan, and the timelines, to integrate our own cells into our vehicles, for both
NMC and LFP; what is our capacity utilisation now and over the next few quarters; how our cost compares
with buying cells from China, and how that moves with scale; when the cell business reaches EBITDA
break-even; and when will Shakti demand grow.
With the Gigafactory now commissioned, our first focus is meeting our own automotive requirements,
which will be roughly 30% NMC and 70% LFP. Given the growth in our Auto business, it should consume
about 2–3 GWh in FY28. NMC yields are in the 70-75% today. We expect them to reach 80-85% by the
end of this quarter (Q3 FY27) and around 90% next quarter (Q4 FY27). Our LFP technology is ready, and
we are starting to ramp LFP production this quarter. We expect LFP yields to reach around 70% by the end

At a yield of about 70%, using our own cells breaks even with buying cells from outside. At 85% yield, we
save about 10–15% over buying from outside. This is at the cell gross margin level, before the cell
business's fixed and operating costs; those also break even versus buying from outside at about 2–3 GWh
of consumption. Our raw material buying costs will fall as consumption scales from today's low levels to
2–3 GWh over the next few quarters. Capacity beyond this 2–3 GWh will go to Shakti. Shakti Gen 2 is
ready and will launch in Q4 FY27, and we expect it to grow into the remaining capacity over FY28.
