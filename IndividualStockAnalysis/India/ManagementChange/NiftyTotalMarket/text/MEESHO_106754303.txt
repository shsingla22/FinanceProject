Our Ecosystem
Our Mission
Our mission is to democratise internet commerce for everyone.
For us, that means removing the barriers that have
historically limited participation in e-commerce. We
believe access to e-commerce should not be determined
by income levels, geography, language, or technical
expertise. A consumer in a rural village should have the
same depth of choice as one in the large city, rather than
being limited to whatever the local market happens to
stock. A business owner should be able to reach
customers across India without significant upfront
investment. A logistics entrepreneur or content creator
should be able to participate in the growth of e-commerce
regardless of scale. Each barrier we remove brings more
people into the market, and over time that grows the
market itself.

Technology is the foundation that makes this possible.
An accessible and affordable platform brings consumers,
sellers, logistics partners and content creators into a single
ecosystem where each participant strengthens the value
proposition for the others; a new seller widens the choice
a consumer sees, a new consumer enlarges the market
a seller can reach, and a denser logistics network makes
serving both cheaper and more reliable. Today, Meesho
connects over 264 million Annual Transacting Users
with more than 961k active sellers, supported by over
18k logistics partners, and 63k content creators across
India. Together, they form one of the largest e-commerce
ecosystems in the country and advance our mission of
democratising internet commerce for everyone.

Consumers
Consumers are the starting point of the
Meesho ecosystem.
As more users transact on the platform, sellers gain
access to a larger demand base, logistics partners benefit
from higher volumes, and content creators find greater
opportunities to engage and influence purchase decisions.
Meesho remains India’s most downloaded shopping
app, driven by our continued focus on accessibility and
affordability for Bharat. Our Annual Transacting User
base grew 33.0% YoY to 264 million in FY26, continuing a
multi-year trend of accelerating user growth. A significant
proportion of these users come from Tier 2+ markets,
many of whom are participating in e-commerce for the
first time.

Our technology-led discovery experience,
personalised recommendations, and content
commerce ecosystem help consumers find
relevant products, while our broad assortment
spanning unbranded products, regional brands,
and national brands through Meesho Mall ensures
wide choice across price points. Combined
with our focus on everyday low prices, this
makes online commerce more accessible to
Indians consumers.

Logistics Partners
We pioneered a zero-commission model that enables
sellers to participate in e-commerce without the
commission structures traditionally charged by online
marketplaces. Combined with low-cost order fulfilment,
this makes Meesho one of the most economical
platforms to sell on in India and lowers the barriers to
entrepreneurship for businesses of all sizes.

Our logistics ecosystem comprises first and last mile
delivery partners, sorting centres, truck operators, and
end-to-end logistics providers. As order volumes on the
platform continue to grow, logistics partners benefit from
access to a large and expanding pool of e-commerce
demand across India.
Orders on Meesho are fulfilled either through Valmo,
our proprietary technology platform that orchestrates
fulfilment across a multi-partner logistics network,
or directly through end-to-end logistics partners. By
aggregating demand and intelligently routing shipments
across multiple participants, Valmo improves asset
utilisation and enables efficient fulfilment at scale.

As more sellers join the platform, assortment
expands, consumer choice improves, and the
marketplace becomes increasingly relevant to a
wider set of shopping needs.
Importantly, Valmo lowers barriers to participation
by allowing logistics providers without end-toend capabilities to participate in the growth of
e-commerce. This expands earning opportunities
for a broader set of logistics entrepreneurs while
increasing network capacity, coverage, and
resilience for the platform.

As the logistics network grows, fulfilment

and service quality improves for both consumers
and sellers.

Content Creators

Our platform is built on multiple scaled self-reinforcing flywheels
orchestrating transactions amongst our four key stakeholders.

As shopping becomes increasingly discovery-led, content
has emerged as a powerful bridge between consumers
and products. Rather than searching for a specific item,
consumers are increasingly discovering products through
videos, recommendations, and demonstrations that help
build trust and purchase intent.

Commerce Flywheel
At the core of our platform is our commerce flywheel.
Consumers transact on Meesho because of the wide
assortment of products that are available at affordable
prices. As more consumers transact and order volumes
increase, more sellers are attracted to Meesho. This
encourages sellers to list more products on Meesho and
price their products competitively.

Logistics Flywheel

Meesho enables this shift through its growing
creator ecosystem. Content creators produce
short-form videos on both the Meesho app
and third-party platforms, helping consumers
discover, evaluate, and purchase products in a
more engaging and immersive manner. In turn,
creators are able to monetise their influence by
earning commissions on sales generated through
their content.

As order volumes on Meesho increase, it helps logistics
partners better utilise their capacity, improve the density
of fulfilment, and reduce the price of their services on
a per-order basis. On Valmo (the technology of which
is housed in our Company), logistics partners who may
not have been able to service e-commerce orders
because they did not have end-to-end capabilities, can
come together to jointly service e-commerce orders.
With reducing fulfilment costs, we are able to reduce
the average cost charged to sellers, enabling them
to price their products more competitively and list
lower-value products on Meesho, which in turn attracts
more consumers.

In FY26, we enabled 63,213 Active Content
Creators who generated over 1.4 million pieces of
order-generating content on a cumulative basis.
As our creator ecosystem expands, consumers
benefit from richer product discovery experiences
while sellers gain new channels to reach and
convert potential customers.

Content Commerce Flywheel
We have activated a content commerce flywheel that
enhances product discovery and engagement on
Meesho. Content creators are attracted to Meesho as
we offer them an avenue to monetise their creativity by
promoting our sellers’ products. With increasing content
volumes on Meesho, product discovery becomes better,
further increasing order volumes and reinforcing the
commerce flywheel.

CEO Letter
Dear Shareholders,
This year is close to my heart. It was our first year as a
public listed company.
While much attention naturally goes to metrics such
as users, orders, and NMV, I have always believed that
numbers are outputs. The real work is the platform
itself, and if the platform becomes more useful for
consumers, more productive for sellers, more efficient for
logistics partners , and more rewarding for creators, the
numbers follow.
By that measure, FY26 was one of our strongest years,
and it was also a year in which we chose to invest
heavily for the future. What began as a vision is now an
identity. Meesho stands for something distinct in Indian
e-commerce. We built a platform not only for those who
were already online, but also for those who had never
been. Our mission has always been simple: Democratise
internet commerce for everyone.
When people talk about the future of Indian e-commerce,
the conversation often centres on market size. We think
about it differently. The opportunity is not defined by
how many people already use e-commerce. It is defined
by how many barriers still remain between people and
e-commerce. Those barriers take many forms. A consumer
may not know how to navigate online shopping. A seller
may not know how to start selling online. A logistics
entrepreneur may not have the scale or know-how to
participate in e-commerce networks. A creator may not
have a way to monetise his creativity. Our experience over
the last decade has reinforced a simple belief: the market
expands in proportion to the barriers we remove.

Foundation of our Success
As we have grown, we have worked hard to preserve
the principles that carried us through our hardest
decisions. Our ‘Meesho Mantras’ are operating principles,
not posters.
Our ‘User First’ mantra sends teams to spend time with
consumers, sellers, and logistics partners before proposing
solutions through programmes such as ‘Listen or Die’.
‘Problem First Mindset’ shapes how we organise: we build
teams around the largest barriers our stakeholders face
rather than around products or functions. Whether the
challenge is improving trust, lowering the cost of trial,
increasing relevance, or simplifying onboarding, the focus
remains on solving the underlying problem rather than
optimising individual functions.
‘Lightspeed’, ‘Company over Team over Individual’, and
‘Extreme Ownership’ let small cross-functional pods own a
problem end-to-end and decide close to the user, which is
where most of our best work has come from. ‘Think Long
Term’ governs how we allocate capital. Whether investing
in technology, logistics capabilities, or user acquisition,
we evaluate opportunities through the lens of long-term
value creation rather than quarterly optics.
Technology is what lets us remove barriers at scale, and
increasingly it lets us remove ones we could not before.

For much of its history, e-commerce was built around a
simple assumption: consumers knew what they wanted.
They searched for it, compared options, and completed
a transaction. That model worked well in markets where
shopping was already organised around shopping
aisles. Much of India, however, has traditionally shopped
differently through recommendations: whether from a
shopkeeper, a family member, a friend, or a local market.
Meesho is built around this reality. Instead of requiring
consumers to learn new behaviours, we use technology
to make digital commerce feel more intuitive, more
conversational and more aligned with how people already
discover and evaluate products.
This shift is important because it expands participation.
Consumers who may not arrive with a specific purchase
intent can still discover relevant products, while firsttime online shoppers can navigate commerce through
recommendations, content, and conversational
experiences. As internet penetration increases and
technology continues to reduce barriers around discovery,
language, and trust, the addressable opportunity for
e-commerce expands.
The progress we made during FY26 reflects this.

Our endeavour is to find those barriers before anyone else
does and to remove them quickly. We do that through two
things: Culture and Technology.

Letter from CTO
The Real Barrier to Commerce

Making Commerce Conversational

When Vidit and I were working out what Meesho could
be, the problem we cared about was making e-commerce
accessible to people who had been historically left out.

That belief led to Vaani, our voice AI shopping agent. What
began as a small experiment quickly revealed something
important: when users could interact with commerce
in a way that felt natural, participation increased. More
than 1.5 million consumers engaged with Vaani in its first
month. Users who adopted the experience demonstrated
meaningfully higher conversion rates, while many firsttime shoppers who had previously abandoned the
platform were able to complete purchases successfully.

Over the last decade, we have come to believe that the
biggest barrier to participation in commerce is expertise
and not access to the internet. Consumers have to know
how to search, compare, and navigate digital platforms;
sellers have to master cataloguing, pricing, and advertising;
logistics providers are expected to build sophisticated
operational networks and technology to deliver
e-commerce parcels. Participation often requires learning
systems that were never designed for most people.

Personalisation at Scale

Technology is changing that.

The same idea extends far beyond voice.

For much of history, e-commerce rewarded expertise; now
software is making it increasingly optional, shifting from
giving tools to doing tasks for them.

Many consumers arrive not with a specific product in mind,
but with a need, a budget, or simply curiosity. Legacy
e-commerce assumes intent exists before discovery but
in offline shopping in India, intent has always followed
discovery. No two users are the same, so no two should
see the same shopping aisle. As our understanding of
users improves, the platform becomes increasingly
personalised to the individual.

The implications are large. When participation
becomes easier, more consumers transact online, more
entrepreneurs start selling, more logistics providers join
fulfillment networks, and more creators can monetise
influence. The market grows because the barriers fall, not
because the population changes.

Learning from Bharat
We saw this most clearly as we moved into smaller towns
and rural India.

Today, more than 75% of orders on Meesho originate from
personalised feeds rather than search. PRISM (Personalised
Ranking and Intent Signal Module), our recommendation
system, combines long-term user preferences with realtime behavioural signals to help consumers discover
products they may never have actively searched for.

Many first-time internet users were not struggling with the
products available on Meesho; what they struggled with was
the interface.
We often watched users hesitate after opening the app
- they knew what they wanted, but translating that into
search queries and filters didn’t come naturally. Offline
shopping is conversational: you describe what you need
and get recommendations. Digital commerce instead
expects users to learn an entirely new behaviour. We
became convinced that e-commerce had to adapt to them,
not the other way around.

16

Empowering Sellers through AI
The same shift is reaching sellers.
AI is increasingly powering core seller functions from
cataloguing, pricing, advertising, and demand forecasting
on their behalf. Through AI-powered cataloguing, demand
intelligence, and GenAI voice agents, we are taking
operational complexity off from sellers and allowing
them to focus on their products rather than on platform
mechanics. Lowering the expertise required to succeed
online is one of the most powerful ways to expand
participation in commerce.

Building for Indian Realities
Technology also has to fit the realities of India. Many of the
systems that power legacy e-commerce were designed
for environments very different from our own. Consider
addresses. In many parts of India, asking someone for
their address rarely results in a street name and house
number. Directions are often given relative to temples,
schools, markets, and local landmarks. For millions of
consumers, this works perfectly well in everyday life but
not for software.
Technology should adapt to how people actually live
rather than expecting people to adapt to technology.
GeoIndia LLM was built on that principle. Trained on
millions of delivery traces across thousands of pin codes,
it translates unstructured addresses into precise delivery
coordinates, improving routing accuracy and reducing
fulfilment friction. More broadly, it reflects how we
approach engineering here: start with the reality of the
user and build technology around it.

The Foundation
Beneath all this sits BharatML Stack, our in-house
AI platform. It powers intelligence across discovery,
advertising, fraud prevention, cataloguing, and customer
support while significantly reducing inference costs.
By standardising how machine learning systems are
developed and deployed, it allows teams to innovate
faster and scale intelligence across the platform..

We believe Free Cash Flow (FCF) is the most relevant
metric for evaluating Meesho because it captures what
ultimately matters: how much cash a business generates
after funding the investments required to grow.
Many consumer and commerce companies report EBITDA
as their headline metric while deploying significant
capital into warehouses, fulfilment centres, logistics
infrastructure, and inventory. Those investments consume
real cash, but much of that cash outflow sits below
EBITDA. As a result, businesses with very different capital
intensity can appear similar on an EBITDA basis, despite
having fundamentally different return profiles.
We judge ourselves by Free Cash Flow, it is the hardest
metric to flatter and the one that tells the real story.
It captures capital intensity, working capital, and
reinvestment together, the factors that ultimately drive
long-term value and ROIC. Comparing our EBITDA to nonplatform businesses is flawed, as it ignores fundamental
differences in ROIC profiles.

Our model is structurally built to generate cash efficiently.
As a consumer tech platform, we scale primarily by
leveraging existing capabilities rather than building new
ones keeping capex low. Our negative working capital
cycle means growth is funded by the platform’s natural
operations, not incremental capital. Unlike capitalintensive models, our investments show up in reported
results as they happen, not amortised over time.
This is the core difference between a platform and a linear
business. In a linear business, serving twice the demand
takes nearly twice the infrastructure - cost and capital rise
with volume, so profitability doesn’t structurally improve
with scale. In a platform business, each additional unit of
demand costs less to serve, converting more growth into
cash as it scales, true increasing returns to scale. Many
businesses call themselves platforms, but few actually
have these mechanics; most have digitised the customer
experience while keeping a cost base that still scales with
volume. Meesho is structurally one of the few in India that
does, and that’s what underpins our confidence in longterm cash generation.
