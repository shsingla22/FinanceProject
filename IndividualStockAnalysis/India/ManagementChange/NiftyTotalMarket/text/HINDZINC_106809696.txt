Hindustan Zinc continued to strengthen its position as a global
sustainability leader, retaining its Global No. 1 Ranking in the
S&P Global Corporate Sustainability Assessment (CSA) 2025
for the third consecutive year by advancing decarbonisation,
water stewardship, circular economy, climate governance,
and climate-related disclosure maturity. As one of the world’s
leading integrated zinc producers and a key supplier of metals
critical to the low-carbon transition, the Company recognises
that climate change is both a material business risk and a
strategic opportunity. Its approach therefore combines
risk mitigation, operational resilience, capital deployment,
low-carbon product innovation, and long-term business
transformation.
The Company’s climate ambition remains anchored in its
Science-based Targets initiative-aligned pathway: to reduce
Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions by 50% and Scope 3 emissions
by 25% by 2030, from the FY2020 baseline, and to achieve Net
Zero by 2050 or sooner.
Considering HZL’s planned expansion to reach 2 million tonnes
of metal capacity by 2030, achieving the SBTi near-term target
may be challenging from a Scope 1 and Scope 2 perspective
due to the associated increase in absolute energy demand
and emissions accounting complexity. However, HZL remains
firmly committed to achieving its SBTi-approved targets and
is actively addressing these transition challenges through a
robust decarbonisation roadmap. Key interventions include
shifting from the State Transmission Utility model to the Central
Transmission Utility model to improve power procurement
efficiency and reduce transmission losses, along with adding
990 MW of battery energy storage capacity to support green
power demand for both existing and expanded capacity. With
these measures, HZL is well placed to manage growth-related

emissions while continuing to progress toward its climate
commitments.
In FY2025-26, HZL reported combined Scope 1 and Scope 2
emissions of 5.02 million tCO2e and Scope 3 emissions of 1.47
million tCO2e. The year also saw a meaningful increase in
renewable power utilisation, with 892 MU of renewable power
consumed, contributing to an estimated 0.83 million tCO2e
reduction. The Pantnagar Metal Plant continued to operate on
100% renewable power.
Beyond operational decarbonisation, HZL further strengthened
its market positioning through low-carbon and sustainabilitylinked value creation. EcoZen, Asia’s first low-carbon zinc
product, continued to demonstrate how climate action
can create product differentiation and support customers’
downstream Scope 3 reduction goals. On water stewardship,
HZL remained 3.32x water positive, reduced freshwater use by
13% against the FY2020 baseline, and continued scaling treated
sewage water use and recycling. On circularity, the Company
generated significant value through reuse and beneficial
utilisation of waste streams, contributing both to environmental
outcomes and business performance.
Governance oversight of climate-related matters continued
through Board and management structures, including the
Sustainability & ESG Committee and enterprise-level risk
oversight processes. Climate-related issues are considered
in strategic planning, investment decisions, risk assessment,
and remuneration-linked sustainability performance. The
Company continues to improve the quality and consistency
of its climate disclosures. This report provides a clearer and
more comprehensive overview of its climate-related approach,
aligned with IFRS S2 and informed by leading CDP disclosure
practices.

ABOUT THIS REPORT
This Climate Action Report presents Hindustan Zinc Limited’s climate-related
governance, strategy, risk management, metrics, targets, and progress for FY2025-26.
The report is intended to provide investors, customers, lenders, employees, regulators,
communities, and other stakeholders with decision-useful information on how climaterelated risks and opportunities affect the Company’s business model, strategy, value
chain, financial planning, and long-term resilience.
This report is structured to align with the four pillars of IFRS
S2 Climate-related Disclosures—Governance, Strategy, Risk
Management, and Metrics & Targets—and is designed to embed
the climate change-related disclosure requirements beyond
governance oversight, transition planning, scenario analysis,
climate-related financial impacts, value chain engagement,
emissions performance, targets, internal carbon pricing, and

assurance, this report includes disclosures on organisational
boundaries, time horizons, substantive effect assessments,
and the processes used to identify and manage climate-related
dependencies, impacts, risks, and opportunities. It also presents
metrics that support an understanding of the Company’s
climate-related exposure and resilience.

Reporting
Period:
Reporting period: April 01, 2025 to March 31, 2026, aligned with
Hindustan Zinc Limited’s (HZL) financial reporting year.

Reporting
Boundary:
The reporting boundary for this Climate Action Report is aligned
with Hindustan Zinc Limited’s financial statement boundary and
remains unchanged from the boundary applied in the previous
reporting year. Accordingly, the report covers Hindustan
Zinc Limited’s consolidated operations and, where relevant,
upstream and downstream value-chain interfaces considered
for climate risk, opportunity and Scope 3 assessment.

Consolidation Approach for
Climate Data:
Greenhouse gas emissions and operational performance
indicators are reported using the operational control approach,
consistent with the previous reporting year. It has been retained
for FY2025-26, and accordingly the approach to Scope 1 and
Scope 2 accounting, as well as Scope 3 reporting and valuechain coverage, remains unchanged. The operational control
approach is applied to ensure that the organisation accountable
for emissions can effectively manage and reduce them, with
clear assignment of responsibility for emissions management
and mitigation efforts.

CLIMATE SCENARIO
ANALYSIS AND
STRATEGIC RESILIENCE
Hindustan Zinc’s climate strategy is designed to respond to the material risks and
opportunities identified through the process detailed below while supporting
long-term resilience and value creation. As a producer of zinc, lead and silver,
metals with relevance to renewable energy systems, corrosion protection, energy
storage and industrial transformation, the Company’s strategic approach rests on
two complementary propositions: first, that climate-related risks must be actively
managed across operations, infrastructure, supply chains and markets; and second,
that sustainability leadership, low-carbon product innovation and resource-efficient
operations can strengthen commercial resilience and long-term competitiveness in the
transition to a lower-carbon economy.
Climate scenario analysis remains an important input to HZL’s strategic planning and resilience assessment. The Company assesses climaterelated risks across both transition and physical pathways over short-, medium- and long-term horizons, where short term is up to 2 years,

TRANSITION CLIMATE RISKS
AND OPPORTUNITIES
In addition to physical climate hazards, HZL faces a distinct set of transition-related risks and opportunities arising from policy
change, technology evolution, energy-system decarbonisation, market expectations and stakeholder scrutiny. For a metals and
mining business, these transition dynamics can affect cost competitiveness, capital requirements, product demand, customer
relationships and long-term strategic positioning in different ways under different pathways. The assessment below therefore
considers how major transition issues may evolve for HZL across relevant sites and operations under the IEA STEPS and NZE
scenarios over medium- and longer-term horizons, while also highlighting where the same transition trend may create both risk and
opportunity depending on the speed of response and the strength of HZL’s low-carbon positioning.

1. Stated Policies Scenario (STEPS)
The Stated Policies Scenario (STEPS) is an exploratory roadmap
that models where the global energy system is actually heading
based on currently enacted laws, industrial targets, and firm
government plans rather than vague, aspirational promises.
Under this realistic outlook, global demand for coal, oil, and
natural gas all peak before or around 2030, driven by a rapid
acceleration of clean energy tech like electric vehicles and
solar power. However, because current global policies lack the
aggressive enforcement needed for a deep climate transition,
greenhouse gas emissions decline far too slowly after their peak,
setting the world on a trajectory toward an estimated global
temperature rise of roughly 2.5°C by 2100.

2. Net Zero Emissions by 2050
Scenario (NZE)
The Net Zero Emissions by 2050 Scenario (NZE) is a strict, goaloriented roadmap that works backward from a specific target
to outline a narrow but technically feasible pathway for limiting
long-term global warming to 1.5°C. To achieve net-zero CO2
emissions across the global energy sector by mid-century, this
pathway demands an immediate, unprecedented structural shift
where global fossil fuel demand plummets by 80% by 2050 and
no new unabated coal plants or oil and gas fields are approved.
It requires a massive surge in clean energy investment to triple
global renewable capacity by 2030, double energy efficiency
improvements, and rapidly deploy carbon-removal technologies
to correct a minor mid-century temperature overshoot.
