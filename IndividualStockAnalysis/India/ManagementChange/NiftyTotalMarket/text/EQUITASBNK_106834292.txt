Equitas Small Finance Bank

Resignation of Mr. P. N. Vasudevan, Managing Director & Chief Executive Officer

The Bank has received a letter from Mr. P. N. Vasudevan ( DIN: 01550885), Managing Director &
Chief
Executive Officer ( MD & CEO) and Key Managerial Personnel of the Bank, conveying his intention to
step
down from his position to pursue certain personal and social initiatives through which
he believes he can
contribute further to society. A copy of his resignation letter as required under Schedule III, Part A, Para A
(7C) of the SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015 is enclosed . The Board
of Directors, at its meeting held today, took note of the resignation and agreed with the same.

Mr . P. N . Vasudevan has been instrumental in the journey of Equitas since its inception as a microfinance
institution in 2007 and its subsequent transition into a Small Finance Bank in 2016. Under his leadership, the
Bank has navigated multiple phases of growth and transformation, thus laying a strong foundation for its
continued progress and success.
The Board placed on record its sincere appreciation for the exemplary leadership, vision, and invaluable
contribution made by Mr . P. N. Vasudevan towards the growth, development, and success of Equitas over
the years.

The Board has also requested Mr . P N Vasudevan to continue to discharge the responsibilities of MD & CEO
till his successor is in place, and has advised the Nomination and Remuneration Committee to identify and
recommend suitable candidates, both internal and external, for the Board 's consideration and approval. The
appointment of the selected candidate shall be subject to the approval of the Reserve Bank of India and such
other regulatory or statutory approvals as may be required .

Mr . P . N . Vasudevan has confirmed that he will continue to function as MD & CEO till his successor takes
over from him . The Board will ensure that the Bank continues to pursue its long term vision set out earlier,
and does not expect this transition to have any impact on the same .

My daughter is the co - promoter of an NBFC which has received its licence from RBI in
2023. This point has been disclosed as required, to the Board and to RBI under various

declarations. My being the MD of a bank and my daughter being the co - promoter of an
NBFC are within the regulatory framework of the Banking Regulations Act .
In my investor meetings over the past 3 years, I have been repeatedly asked about the
perceived conflict of interest . In reality there is no conflict of interest, since there is no
transaction at all between our bank and the said NBFC (except one short term deposit
about 21/2 years back, which was duly repaid on maturity) . There are no othertransactions
including any loans by the bankto the NBFC.
Inspite of my explaining the above to investors, the perception of conflict continues to
persist. I am told by investors that this ‘overhang’ is causing the stock to underperform,

impacting value creation for both shareholders and employees. Sometimes perceptions
are stronger than reality.
Under the circumstance, it is important that we remove the ‘ overhang’ arising out of the
perception issue. Accordingly, I would like to step down from the bank . I am sure that
with this ‘overhang’ removed, the stock should reflect the financial strength of the bank,
benefiting all stakeholders including employees.
