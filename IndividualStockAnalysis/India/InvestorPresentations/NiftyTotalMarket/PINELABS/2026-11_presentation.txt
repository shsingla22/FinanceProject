What’s on Investors’ minds? (1/5)
How has revenue growth performed this quarter? Do you expect this pace of growth to continue in H2?
Q2 FY27Q2 FY27 revenue grew 24% YoY reflecting broad -based momentum across both our platforms. Our confidence in sustaining this 
pace into H2 is anchored in the breadth of growth engines now contributing to the top line — merchant acquisition, deeper 
monetization of the installed base, newer products and use cases, and international expansion. 
DITP
•Our Mid -market  investments are delivering strong results, with DCPs growing 49%  YoY & monetization deepening beyond 
subscriptions through higher flow -based revenue. In Q2, we entered a new strategic partnership with a global payments 
technology player to scale Tap & Pay and UPI transactions, further strengthening the growth opportunity.
•Subscription revenue now contributes 27% of overall revenue, while 30% of our installed DCP base is now generating flow & 
affordability GTV. Strong DCP residency + omni channel payments is unlocking deeper monetization, as we move beyond 
subscription revenues to more GTV -linked revenues, with 29%+ of DITP* GTV now monetized vs. 18% a year ago through flow, 
affordability & online payments. Solutions such as DCC, Growthx , Scan & Go, Payment aggregation etc. are scaling rapidly.
•Affordability continues to scale and diversify with emerging channels and international increasingly becoming meaningful 
growth vectors. During the quarter, we have expanded multi -issuer affordability across leading electronics brands in Malaysia 
with 13 issuers already onboarded.
•Online Payments expanded through agentic commerce and cross -border payments, while Fintech Infrastructure continued to 
scale with transactions growing 33%+ YoY and SignalIQ  now processing 125,000+ loans monthly. 
IAP
• India business saw significant growth momentum with revenue growing 40%+ YoY. In Processing, large tenured clients continue 
to see strong growth momentum, validating the potential of prepaid solutions as a great engine for driving commerce. In 
distribution, we expanded rapidly across network and partners.
• Broadening prepaid opportunity -gaming continue to scale, with emerging growth avenues across employee benefits, refund 
wallets, entertainment gifting (4x GTV YoY), corporate rewards (GTV +70% YoY H1) & mobility gaining traction over a small bas e.
• International anchoring for scale — Six airlines live in H1 FY27 (highest -ever first half, with another marquee go -live in H2), plus a 
multi -year renewal with a large ANZ retail conglomerate — moving international revenue beyond transactional economics.
Together, these products are unlocking newer, higher -value revenue vectors & deepening our role as a fintech growth enabler. Q1

What’s on Investors’ minds? (2/5)
Adjusted EBITDA margin remained strong at ~19% in Q2 FY27. How does management see indirect costs, 
ongoing investments and margins evolving through H2 FY27?
Q2 FY27We are excited about the opportunities ahead, and we are consciously investing in the future of commerce. Building that 
future means making deliberate choices today. Even as we invest, Adjusted EBITDA margin remained strong at ~19%  in Q2 
FY27 with 27% YoY growth.
To build that future, we are rebalancing where we invest:
• Employee cost: leaner overall, reinvested in growth – While we continue to optimize the employee cost (employee cost 
has decreased from 37% to ~30% of revenue YoY ), we continue to invested in talent in 3 fast -growing areas: mid -market 
(DCPs up 49% YoY), international business development (SEA revenue up ~40% YoY) and towards product teams 
(SignalIQ , Switchboard, Agentic Payments).
• Data, cloud and technology: scaling the platform: Tech cost has and will continue to grow in line with topline growth 
as indicated by management earlier. We continue to invest in our tech Infra where cost remains at ~8% of revenue. The 
investment largely is directed towards:  a) New strategic projects — Agentic Commerce, Information Security, OMC 
business scaleup, International expansion, and our long -term engagement partnership with OpenAI. b) OEM -agnostic 
Terminal Management System c) AI adoption across internal platforms — telesales  effort reduced by ~60%, ~89% of code 
changes written by AI, and 60%+ of email queries responded to by AI. Each builds capability and compounds 
productivity.
• Other indirect costs: Costs grew due to two deliberate investments in future franchise value a) Brand build -out – Pine 
Labs is stepping up brand investments to position itself as the default commerce infrastructure for the global south. This 
includes the Global Fintech Fest, where we were a sponsor and showcased our flagship launches b) Deeper investments 
with our top merchants to ringfence relationships and expand wallet share across the full spectrum of use cases — gift 
cards, prepaid, affordability, wallets and newer categories — converting transactional engagements into multi -year 
franchise anchors. 
Our H1 investments in growth areas position us well; we remain committed to sustained investment in H2 as we see 
multiple growth opportunities emerging . These investments are calibrated to drive sustainable growth while maintaining 
operating margins . Strategic investment accelerates growth in our existing businesses and newer segments —mid-market, 
international markets, and UPI corridors —while enabling the rollout of ~10 lakh soundboxes over the medium term122155

What’s on Investors’ minds? (3/5)
While Adj EBITDA has grown by 27%, PAT increased ~ 6x. What is driving the improvement below Adjusted EBITDA? 
Q2 FY27Q2 FY27 extended the sharp improvement in bottom -line performance that began in Q1 FY27. PBT grew to ₹61 Cr (+450% 
YoY) and PAT grew to ₹39 Cr (+546% YoY) , reflecting operating leverage flowing through from Adjusted EBITDA, a 
moderating effective tax rate, and continued discipline on costs between EBITDA and PBT.
• Q2 FY27 ETR moderated to ~37%  (from 48% in Q1 FY27). The elevated ETR is a direct reflection of where we are in our 
investment cycle : several of our newer and international entities are currently loss -making as we invest to scale them, 
and their losses cannot be set off against profits in our India business for tax purposes. The consolidated ETR therefore 
reads higher than the underlying tax profile of the core franchise. As these entities scale toward profitability, and with 
Q3 seasonally being the strongest quarter for our profit -making entity  — we expect the consolidated ETR to taper 
gradually to ~30% by the exit of FY27 .
• Continued discipline on ESOP (₹23 Cr, –20% YoY) and finance costs (₹14 Cr, –33% YoY) , alongside a stable 
depreciation and amortization profile, will help maintain our profitability trajectory. 
Operating leverage and the moderating tax rate together position us well for continued step -up in PBT and PAT over the 
balance of FY27.

What’s on Investors’ minds? (5/5)
Following recent regulatory developments on MDR on UPI, how does management see the opportunity for Pine Labs, given Pine lab s platform
processes large volumes of UPI linked transactions in the premium merchant category? How is the platform positioned to moneti ze it?
Q2 FY27The MDR framework on UPI (P2M) creates meaningful upside for Pine Labs.
We’re uniquely positioned to capitalize on this opportunity. Today, 70%+ of our DCP transactions are UPI -led, and we’re processing ~ ₹20k Cr in monthly UPI GTV —growing at 
68% CAGR over the last two years . We expect to sustain this growth momentum and believe we are on track to process ~ ₹33 -34k Cr monthly GTV in FY28 
Our average UPI ticket size is ~₹1,400 as of Q2 FY27 with more than 65% of our UPI GTV driven by transactions above ₹2,000 . Combined with our stronghold in enterprise, mid -
market, and premium retail segments, where transactions above ₹2,000 are common, we are well positioned. 
Under NPCI’s proposed framework, a 0.4% MDR cap applies to payments above ₹2,000, and we primarily expect to earn our proport ion through our acquiring bank partners —a high -
margin revenue stream that scales with volumes.
Rollout has been deferred pending regulatory clarity, but our distribution, technology, and operations are ready to execute i mme diately once the final framework and 
implementation date are announced.Q5

Q2 FY27Term Description
Adjusted EBITDAAdjusted  EBITDA  which  is calculated  as EBITDA  less (i) other  income ; plus  (ii) impairment  of non-current  assets ; plus  
(iii) exceptional  items ; plus  (iv) employee  share  based  payment  expense ; plus  (v) foreign  exchange  loss (net) ; plus  
(vi) liabilities  and provisions  no longer  required  written  back ; plus  (vii) legal  and professional  expense  relating  to 
fund  raising,  acquisition  and restructuring ; plus  (viii)  employment  incentive  linked  to acquisitions  less (ix) liability  
written  back  on settlement  of purchase  consideration  payable .
Adjusted  EBITDA  does  not include  certain  components  of other  income,  namely  interest  income  under  the effective  
interest  method  on financial  assets  carried  at amortised  cost  on bank  deposits,  interest  income  under  the effective  
interest  method  on financial  assets  carried  at amortised  cost  on security  deposits,  interest  income  under  the 
effective  interest  method  on financial  assets  carried  at amortised  cost  on finance  lease,  interest  on income  tax 
refunds,  gain  on sale  of property,  plant  and equipment,  liability  written  back  on settlement  of purchase  
consideration  payable,  net gain  on lease  termination,  net gain  arising  on financial  assets  mandatorily  measured  at 
FVTPL  on fair valuation  income  on derivative  call option,  gain  on sale  of mutual  funds  and fair valuation  gain  of 
mutual  funds  and miscellaneous  income . EBITDA  is earnings  before  interest,  tax, depreciation  and amortisation  
expenses  which  is calculated  as profit/(loss)  for the period/year  plus  (i) tax expenses ; plus  (ii) finance  costs ; plus  
(iii) depreciation  and amortisation .
Adjusted EBITDA Margin Adjusted  EBITDA  Margin  is Adjusted  EBITDA  divided  by revenue  from  operations  for the year/period .
Flow, Affordability and Transaction Processing GTVAffordability,  Flow  and Transaction  Processing  GTV is defined  as the total  transaction  value  primarily  processed  for 
our Affordability  solutions,  Payment  Aggregation,  Dynamic  Currency  Conversion  (DCC)  and UPI offerings . This is a 
subset  of entire  Digital  Infrastructure  and Transaction  Platform  GTV.
Contribution MarginContribution  Margin  is calculated  by deducting  the transaction  and related  costs,  purchases  of stock -in-trade  and 
changes  in inventories  of stock -in-trade  (excluding  attributable  employee  benefits  expense,  finance  costs,  
depreciation  and amortisation  expenses,  impairment  of non-current  assets,  impairment  losses  on financial  assets  
and contract  assets  and other  expenses)  from  revenue  from  operations  for the period/year .
Contribution Margin as a percentage of revenue from 
operationsContribution  Margin  as a percentage  of revenue  from  operations  is Contribution  Margin  divided  by revenue  from  
operations  for the year/period .
“Digital check -out points” or “DCPs”Digital  check -out points  represent  the number  of live touchpoints  (at the end of the period)  at merchant  stores  
powered  by our platform .
